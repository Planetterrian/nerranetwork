One death sentence is set aside in Saudi Arabia, changing a Sri Lankan worker's fate.

What You Need to Know: Saudi Arabia's Supreme Court has set aside the death sentence of Sri Lankan migrant worker Anojan Sivarasa, 27, over a Facebook comment, Colombo's deputy foreign minister said. Four Syrian security personnel were killed in an ambush in Hama province, and a record 962,832 candidates have begun South Africa's matric examinations. Lead

Saudi Arabia overturns death sentence for man over Facebook comment: BBC

Saudi Arabia's Supreme Court has set aside the death sentence handed to Anojan Sivarasa, 27, a Sri Lankan migrant worker, over a comment on Facebook, Colombo's Deputy Foreign Minister Arun Hemachandra said. A Saudi appellate court sentenced him to death in September after convicting him of blasphemy against Islam and the Prophet Muhammad. Hemachandra said on Wednesday, in a Facebook post, that the top court had set the sentence aside, and he thanked the authorities and legal teams of both countries. Sivarasa was arrested in July: in late June, footage of a young Muslim man kicking a dog that was following Hindu pilgrims spread online and set off religious arguments; after another user posted derogatory remarks about a Hindu deity, Sivarasa replied in Tamil with an insult to the Prophet, then deleted the comment and posted an apology video, and a screenshot reached his employer and Saudi officials. The Al-Ahsa Criminal Court had first sentenced him to five years in prison and a fine of three million Saudi riyals, about 0.8 million dollars. Faizer Mustafa, a Sri Lankan lawmaker involved in the negotiations, confirmed to BBC Sinhala that the death sentence had been overturned and said they are reviewing the Supreme Court's latest ruling. The BBC reported it is not clear whether further proceedings will follow or whether Sivarasa will remain in detention, and his family had said they have had no direct contact with him since 28 September. Joey Shea, senior Saudi Arabia researcher at Human Rights Watch, said on Tuesday that Saudi authorities should end the use of abusive laws to hand out extraordinary sentences for speech-related offences, especially against migrant workers with limited protections. Source: bbc.co.uk Source: bbc.com

Across the Region

Four Syrian troops killed in ambush on Hama motorway: The National

Four Syrian security personnel were killed on Tuesday night when two patrols were ambushed in the countryside of Hama province. Nour Al Baba, spokesman for Syria's Interior Ministry, condemned the attacks on the Salamiya-Athriya motorway and said the crime would not deter the pursuit of those responsible. He said the killings would strengthen the security forces' resolve to protect civilians and safeguard lives. Internal security forces said they were continuing efforts to track down those involved. The road links Salamiya, east of Hama city, with Athriya, farther east toward the Syrian desert, on a corridor connecting central Syria with the east, in an area where armed groups and ISIS militants have carried out attacks. Source: thenationalnews.com

From AI tutors to maths clubs, learners find new routes to exam support: Daily Maverick

A record 962,832 candidates are sitting South Africa's 2026 National Senior Certificate examinations, which began on 13 October and conclude on 26 November. Basic Education Minister Siviwe Gwarube said at a briefing on 11 October that preparations had been completed. This year's cohort is 815,051 full-time candidates and 147,781 part-time candidates; the department has established about 7,100 examination centres and recruited more than 51,000 educators to mark scripts. Gwarube said provincial performance would be measured by Bachelor passes and distinctions, learner retention, and participation and performance in Mathematics, Physical Sciences, Accounting and Economics, and she confirmed the papers had been set, moderated, quality-assured and approved by Umalusi. With one-to-one matric lessons reported at up to 750 rand an hour, the Daily Maverick pointed to free routes including Saving Grace AI Tutor, a CAPS-aligned tool the school's head of educational technology, Charles Mukembe, said would be free for Grade 12 learners, and Siyavula, whose site is zero-rated on MTN and Cell C. Olico Maths Education, whose executive director Andrew Barrett said most learners the group works with are many years behind in mathematics, has run after-school programmes since 2008 and now tutors in person in the Western Cape, Gauteng and Limpopo. Source: dailymaverick.co.za

The Closer Look

‘Where’d the money go?’ — feisty NMB tannies slam ‘shoddy’ R2.4m Smartie Bridge upgrades: Daily Maverick

Residents of Nelson Mandela Bay are demanding to know how 2.4 million rand was spent on upgrades to the historic Wylde Bridge in Gqeberha after height restrictors were destroyed and heavy trucks kept crossing. Francis Jeffrey, of Swartkops, and Eileen Leander, of the Aloes community, told the Daily Maverick the work is a mess and a disaster. Jeffrey called the work unacceptable and asked where the money had gone, and whether the result looked like 2.4 million rand of work. Leander called it a laughing stock and said the municipality should come and clean up its mess.

The bridge links Amsterdamhoek and Swartkops and spans the Swartkops River. It opened in September 1879 and is one of the oldest bridges in the metro. A brightly coloured refurbishment in 2016 earned it the nickname Smartie Bridge. That refurbishment removed the original height restrictors and left the bridge open to oversized vehicles using it as a shortcut to industrial areas and truck stops. The structure has a five-tonne weight restriction. Residents had complained for years that heavy trucks were damaging it. The municipality has said the 2.4 million rand went to upgrading the approaches, roadworks and kerbing, and to traffic-calming measures and signage. Jeffrey and Leander say what is on the ground does not match that sum.

In February the Daily Maverick reported that heavy trucks had already damaged newly installed plastic height restrictors, and that kerbing had repeatedly been broken by vehicles forcing through the works. On a visit on Friday, 9 October, the plastic restrictors had been destroyed, and several trucks crossed despite the restrictions. Leander said traffic officials sometimes monitor the bridge but stay for only about 15 minutes, which she said did little to enforce the rules. She said the bridge vibrates when vehicles cross. She and Jeffrey fear it will take a disaster before the municipality treats the warnings as serious.

Democratic Alliance Ward 16 councillor Werner Senekal agreed the work was absolutely shoddy and said he could not see how 2.4 million rand had been spent on what had been completed. A motion he submitted, seeking an account of the spending and the municipality's plans to upgrade the bridge, went to the Municipal Public Accounts Committee and was accepted. He said there is a standing motion that a report must show how every rand was spent, and that the matter will be taken further in the new term, after the municipal election on 4 November. He said detailed answers would wait until then.

Leander said that while officials delay, the bridge is deteriorating, and that children from Aloes must use it each day to reach school in Swartkops. Children from Swartkops, she said, do not have to use the bridge for school, and Aloes children cannot be expected to walk the railway line. She said gaps in the railings have been open for a long time, so children walk in the middle of the road and move aside when a vehicle comes. She said she had been cautioned against trying to close the gaps with wire and chevron tape. A temporary closure during the roadworks lasted two weeks and, she said, let children cross without contending with traffic. Source: dailymaverick.co.za

UAE weather: Rainy weekend expected in east of country: The National

The National Centre of Meteorology says rain is expected in the east of the UAE over the weekend, with wet weather in the south-east on Wednesday and winds up to 50 kilometres an hour, and a weekend high of 41 degrees Celsius in Sweihan on Saturday. Source: thenationalnews.com

Aramco building struck, missing sailors' families grieve and Dubai unveils underground transport plans: The National

Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry says a strike on an Aramco building in Dhahran caused a fire and no casualties, as Yemen's Houthi rebels escalate their missile campaign against the kingdom. Source: thenationalnews.com

Shell to sell Nigeria’s domestic gas business to Vaaris Energy: Premium Times

Shell said on Tuesday it will sell Nigeria's domestic gas business to Vaaris Energy, describing the deal as part of a strategy to simplify its portfolio and concentrate investment where it is strongest. Source: premiumtimesng.com

UN official says ‘window of opportunity’ for peace in DR Congo: Al Jazeera

A United Nations official said there is a window of opportunity for peace in the Democratic Republic of Congo, praising progress in diplomacy even as the security and humanitarian situation worsens. Source: aljazeera.com

BOOK EXCERPT: Kitchens of the Karoo – how cooks of old mastered stone, sourdough and coffee: Daily Maverick

A Daily Maverick book excerpt says Karoo cooks once beat peach pips into clay floors and coated them with aloe juice to keep insects away, a flooring method still used at Elandsberg Wilderness Cottages in Tankwa Karoo National Park. Source: dailymaverick.co.za

The Region and the World

From Cairo to Tokyo: Can you guess these twin cities?: Al Jazeera

Haifa, Israel's third-largest city, may lose a twinning with the east London borough of Hackney that dates to 1968. Hackney is consulting residents until 14 October on whether to end the link. Al Jazeera reported that the Green Party-led council voted on 28 July to end the partnership in principle, citing its values and human rights commitments in the setting of Israel's war on Gaza. Councillors will make a final decision after the consultation and a review of the borough's twinning policy. Ending such a link is often symbolic: several UK councils suspended or ended ties with Russian cities after the 2022 invasion of Ukraine. Source: aljazeera.com

Both Sides: Should Sudanese groups join talks launched in army-held Khartoum?

More than three years into the war between the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces, a self-appointed committee of 19 Sudanese public figures, chaired by Ahfad University president Qasim Badri, is trying to prepare a national dialogue inside Sudan. The committee launched the effort in August after meeting army chief General Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, who also heads the Sovereignty Council, and calling for legal restrictions on opposition politicians to be lifted. The authorities then issued a decree to drop certain politically motivated charges against Sudanese abroad who return for dialogue, and to grant participants temporary legal protections.

The case for engaging, as a senior researcher at the Chr. Michelsen Institute argued in Al Jazeera, is that fighting has caused immense suffering and a military outcome alone cannot rebuild the state or reunify society. The researcher says previous mediation has faltered, a ceasefire and humanitarian access remain urgent, and preparations for dialogue can proceed alongside both. The committee says it is not seeking to negotiate Sudan's future or set the agenda of a national dialogue. It says its role is to create conditions for Sudanese political actors to meet one another directly. The researcher argues that dismissing the effort before it has shown its value would be premature, and that meeting al-Burhan was a practical necessity because he could answer the demand for legal protections, not in itself an endorsement of his authority.

The case against is made by Sumoud, the civilian coalition led by former prime minister Abdalla Hamdok, and by Ta'sis, an alliance that includes the Rapid Support Forces. They question the committee's independence. They warn that holding talks in army-controlled areas could legitimise al-Burhan's authority and entrench the country's divisions. The initiative was launched in Khartoum, which is under army control. The researcher writes that those concerns are legitimate: the members were not elected and do not represent every constituency, and if the effort becomes too closely associated with the army, other actors may treat it as an extension of al-Burhan's agenda rather than an independent facilitator. The researcher also writes that their absence matters, because the crisis is too deep to be addressed by any single political current, and that the committee still has to reach people outside army-held areas, including Sudanese displaced by the fighting, so they can take part freely and without intimidation. Source: aljazeera.com Source: aljazeera.com
