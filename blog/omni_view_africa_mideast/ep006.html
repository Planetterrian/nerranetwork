Lead

Kenyan President Ruto Calls Dangote Refinery an Engineering Masterpiece: UA.NEWS

Kenyan President William Ruto, visiting the Dangote refinery near Lagos with his wife, Rachel Ruto, called the facility a masterpiece of science, engineering and art, and said he knew Nigerians' entrepreneurial spirit but had not expected a project of that scale. He said the Kenyan government fully supports this goal of Dangote Group. Aliko Dangote, president and chief executive of Dangote Industries Limited, said that under the group's Vision 2030 strategy it plans to invest nearly $50 billion in Africa's industrialization, including a power plant in Lamu of about 1,000 megawatts, with 500 megawatts to be sold to the Kenyan government. Dangote Petroleum Refinery expects to begin construction of an oil refinery in coastal Lamu on September 30, with a planned capacity of 700,000 barrels a day, as its entry into the East African market and a contribution to the region's energy security. Estimates cited by the outlet put construction at three years and $15 billion to $16 billion, processing oil for Kenya, South Sudan, Uganda, Burundi and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. In August, presidential economic adviser David Ndii said Dangote Group had offered East African countries a 30 percent stake, with Kenya able to take 10 percent for $500 million and Ethiopia and Rwanda ready to join; Engineers India Limited, under India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas, is to consult on project management, engineering, procurement and construction management under a contract valued at $450 million. A mix of internal funds, an initial public offering and bonds is being considered to finance the project. Source: ua.news

Somalia parliament speaker plans to cut pay of absent lawmakers: Somali Guardian

Somalia's parliament speaker, Abdulkadir Mohamed Nur Jama, plans to cut the salaries of about 50 lawmakers for not regularly attending assembly meetings, Somali Stream reported on Sunday. He has ordered the parliament secretariat to give him the names of lawmakers often absent, and has ordered that they receive no stipends or salaries by September. If carried out, it would be the first time a speaker of the Somali parliament has made such an attempt, and the first move of this kind since his recent election. The plan would affect at least 50 lawmakers, among them ministers in the government of Prime Minister Hamza Abdi Barre. Source: somaliguardian.com

The Region and the World

VP Shettima Returns To Nigeria After Leading Delegation At UNGA: Channels Television

Vice President Kashim Shettima has returned to Nigeria after representing President Bola Ahmed Tinubu at the 81st Session of the United Nations General Assembly in New York, a statement on Monday by Stanley Nkwocha said; he left Abuja on September 20 to lead the delegation. He delivered Tinubu's national statement at the General Debate, renewing Nigeria's call for reform of the UN Security Council and demanding permanent African representation, fairer global financing, stronger climate support and deeper multilateral cooperation. At the Third High-Level Roundtable of the Africa Minerals Strategy Group, Tinubu urged African countries to move beyond raw mineral exports toward processing, manufacturing and value addition. At an education-financing event co-hosted by Nigeria and Italy, Shettima reaffirmed education as an investment in productivity and human capital and supported the Global Partnership for Education's $5 billion financing campaign; he also met Nigerians in the diaspora, the new leadership of the ECOWAS Commission, and UN Deputy Secretary-General Amina J. Mohammed, pressing Nigeria's positions on development financing, regional integration, economic cooperation, technology, peace and security. Source: channelstv.com

Both Sides: Whether Sudan and Ethiopia are sponsoring each other's armed opponents

The question is whether Sudan and Ethiopia should treat one another as sponsors of armed opponents, and whether that charge goes to official talks or stands as a sovereignty warning. Sudan's Armed Forces, in a statement late Sunday, reject the allegation that Khartoum backs Ethiopian opposition groups. Their strongest reason is the one they put first: Sudan, they say, stays committed to international law and to non-interference in other nations' affairs. The army says it will not permit any threat to its sovereignty or national security, and alleges that backing for the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces is still coming from Ethiopian soil, including training, supplies and military activity involving long-range and one-way attack drones. It calls on Addis Ababa to settle the differences through dialogue and official channels, in the interest of stability in both countries and the wider region. Ethiopian National Defense Force Chief of General Staff Field Marshal Birhanu Jula had accused Sudan, Egypt and other actors of supporting groups opposed to the Ethiopian government. His strongest reason is that external actors are backing an alliance of armed and political groups, and that the Tigray People's Liberation Front and Eritrea would pose a more limited threat without that outside support. His remarks came amid renewed tensions in northern Ethiopia, where clashes have intensified between federal forces and the TPLF and allied groups. Each side's statement treats cross-border help to armed groups as a security issue between the two capitals; neither accepts the other's account of who is receiving that help. The next step Sudan names is dialogue through official channels. Source: en.yenisafak.com

Middle East crude exports in September recovered to 12.8 million barrels a day, the highest since the war began. Source: finance.biggo.com
