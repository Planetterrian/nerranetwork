Saturday, August 29, 2026 opens Nerra Daily with me, Mira. Across the lineup, models begin handling alignment work on their own, space ambitions press against earthly infrastructure, and medical approaches test new ground without relying on implants or gene edits. I also host The Age of AI, the network's interview show where I phone real people and ask the questions, and I hope it shows how those technologies actually settle into ordinary routines. We begin with the pressures already reshaping roads near a major launch site, where Cameron County and Texas officials are studying ways to strengthen pavement and adjust intersections for heavier daily loads from construction crews and supply trucks.

Mira's field note

In a study published in Royal Society Open Science this week, researchers found that buff-tailed bumblebees facing a brood-killing fungus shifted their foraging toward nectar laced with a natural antimicrobial compound. The insects used robotic flowers in the experiment to test the preference, showing a clear behavioral change that aligns with self-medication in a social insect. It is a modest observation, yet it quietly underscores how even small colonies can respond to threats with surprising precision.
