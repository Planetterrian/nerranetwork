# Models & Agents

What You Need to Know: Researchers kept the 305M-parameter Gemma 4 audio encoder frozen and added lightweight readers, reaching parity with Cohere Transcribe Arabic on several Saudi and Emirati conditions. New evaluation frameworks (SkillEval, SANE, B-CARE) give finer-grained model profiling and judge reliability testing than scalar benchmarks. Open releases include MADARTB parallel dialect treebanks, Lift-Bench scoped instruction constraints, and CSD cross-tokenizer distillation code.

Top Story

Researchers tested the 305M-parameter audio encoder shared by Gemma 4 and EmbeddingGemma 2 on Saudi and Gulf Arabic speech without updating any encoder weights. A frozen CTC reader (22.6M parameters) trained over mixed encoder layers trailed Cohere Transcribe Arabic (2.07B) in word error rate but matched it on character error rate for five of six human-labelled conditions while running 3.4× faster on CPU with greedy decoding. Adding 3.1M low-rank adapters plus a 41.5M-parameter hybrid CTC/attention reader with sub-word language model joint decoding produced statistically indistinguishable WER on a public Saudi customer-service call (19.20 % vs 19.27 %) and clean Emirati speech, with significantly lower CER across all six conditions and better results than a 0.6B SADA-fine-tuned recogniser. The system ranked fourth of fifteen on a blind human-corrected call-centre sample, behind three commercial APIs and ahead of other self-hostable systems by point estimate, with an optimised joint decoder reaching 139× real time on one GPU. Code and public-set results are released. Source: arxiv.org

Model Updates

SkillEval: Learning Interpretable Ability Profiles of LLMs via Cognitive Diagnosis Models: arXiv NLP

SkillEval applies LLM meta-cognition to discover interpretable skills from questions then fits cognitive diagnosis models to estimate per-skill abilities across 3,811 LLMs and 9,523 items from MATH, BBH, GPQA, MuSR and IFEval. The resulting profiles are more calibrated than average scores, predict unseen-item performance better, and enable LLM routing that cuts inference cost over 60 % while improving accuracy plus efficient Computerized Adaptive Testing for new models. Source: arxiv.org

Where Do Legal Factors Live in LLMs?: arXiv NLP

All 26 CATO factors are linearly decodable in Qwen3-1.7B, Qwen3-8B and Gemma-2 2B; single-feature sparse readouts from sparse autoencoders cover the full catalog with best-layer test AUCs 0.86–1.00, and five selected features approach full-vector performance. The study reports 78 model-specific layer-feature coordinates. Source: arxiv.org

LIFT: Scoped Constraints for Evaluating and Training Instruction Following: arXiv NLP

Lift-Bench asks models to draft documents from topic and structured outline with constraints scoped to specific sections, supplying both verifiable and judged constraints plus a matched Lift-Train set. Frontier models struggle on harder subsets; training on Lift-Train reduces reward hacking compared with common IFTrain. Both benchmark and training data plus the generation pipeline are public. Source: arxiv.org

A Parallel Multi-Dialect Arabic Dependency Treebank: arXiv NLP

MADARTB annotates 5,000 sentences across Beirut, Cairo, Doha, Rabat and Tunis dialects plus MSA using the CATiB scheme. Global construction profiles are similar but local structures diverge; a supervised parser reaches 87.5 % LAS while the best LLM setup reaches 85.7 % LAS across all six varieties. Annotations and models are released. Source: arxiv.org

CSD: Common-Support Distillation for Cross-Tokenizer Knowledge Transfer: arXiv NLP

CSD builds an explicit or virtual common support at each aligned chunk and matches induced distributions with one objective, handling one-to-one and other alignments via the autoregressive chain rule plus residual events. Across Llama-3.2-1B and Gemma3-1B-PT students with Qwen and Phi teachers it outperforms prior cross-tokenizer methods by up to 1.8 points and beats continued pretraining and same-tokenizer distillation; on Llama-3.1-8B it improves average accuracy up to 4.2 points for base and 1.6 points for instruction-tuned students. Source: arxiv.org

norregel: a preregistered benchmark of dated numeric values in Norwegian law: arXiv NLP

The benchmark extracts 916 dated numeric values (NOK amounts, percentages, G-multiples, age limits) from Lovdata with full revision history so answers can be classed correct, outdated, wrong or uninterpretable. In the second preregistered run eight models were evaluated; support for the hypothesis that outdated answers rise after a model’s cutoff was not robust under the strictest sensitivity analysis. Source: arxiv.org

Agent & Tool Developments

Is Anyone Enforcing the Cartel? What Reinforcement Learning Teaches LLM Agents in Markets: arXiv NLP

An audit forces cartel members to cheat in counterfactual markets and measures whether RL-trained pricing and trading agents punish the deviation. Small RL-trained LLMs learn fixed rules in self-play, deterrence against punishing rivals, and matching against cheaters, but rarely discover costly price wars; only matching agents enforce thin collusion and only against large cuts, while Q-learners enforce only in pricing. Source: arxiv.org

Building AI Research Teams for Autonomous Discovery of Linear Attention Architectures: arXiv NLP

Persistent AI researchers with distinct disciplinary backgrounds, private histories and adaptive research allocation outperform GDN2 at 1.3B scale on language modeling, commonsense reasoning and retrieval tasks. Removing disciplinary backgrounds, private histories or adaptive allocation each produces markedly weaker discoveries. Source: arxiv.org

Let Agents Design How They Search: Automatic Interface Optimization for Agentic Search: arXiv NLP

SearchWeaver uses execution trajectories and task rewards to jointly revise corpus artifacts, executable tools and usage instructions. With GPT-5.4-nano the optimised interfaces raise answer accuracy from 71.6 % to 78.4 % on BrowseComp-Plus and average nDCG@10 from 45.7 to 55.0 across four BRIGHT subsets. Source: arxiv.org

MOSAIC: An Omni-Modal Benchmark for Long-Term Memory and Cross-Modal Reasoning in Conversational Agents: arXiv NLP

MOSAIC supplies 923 questions over 11–24 session persona-grounded histories spanning text, image, video and audio with targeted spatial, temporal and prosodic signals. Matched decomposition shows representation loss is modality-dependent, residual reading dominates even with gold evidence, and abstention remains weak. Source: arxiv.org

Practical & Community

KIRA: A Full-Stack Reading Screening System with Dual-Pathway Analysis for Children: arXiv NLP

KIRA adapts pretrained word and phoneme recognisers to children’s speech, cutting word error rate from 13.88 % to 8.84 % and phoneme error rate from 41.66 % to 23.00 %. It implements Word Reading and Oral Reading Fluency tasks with a teacher-facing web interface that presents fluency scores and phoneme-level feedback. Source: arxiv.org

SANE: Automated Scorer-Specific Metamorphic Testing for LLM Judges: arXiv NLP

SANE encodes behavioral requirements as reusable agent instructions, automatically derives scorer-specific perturbation recipes and expected score relations, then validates them with a scorer-independent framework. Across five domains it produces 3.7× as many validated test families as the Judge Reliability Harness at comparable validity and detects deficiencies in professionalism and toxicity where JRH finds none. Source: arxiv.org

LTEM: LLM-Friendly Table-Enhanced Markdown for Table Understanding and Generation: arXiv NLP

LTEM extends Markdown with explicit span and hierarchical-header encoding while remaining minimal. On five state-of-the-art LLMs it matches HTML accuracy with a better accuracy-token trade-off and produces far fewer invalid responses than LaTeX for table generation. Taxonomy, benchmark and specification are released. Source: arxiv.org

TrainBridge: Agentic NPU Training Optimization Guided by GPU Compilation Evidence: arXiv NLP

TrainBridge extracts optimization intents from GPU execution traces to guide NPU program rewrites and iterates with profiling. It delivers 1.7× average speedup across four workloads (LLM pretraining, image classification, vision-language) and nearly 2.0× across 27 Modded-NanoGPT speedrun records. Source: arxiv.org

Under the Hood: Common-Mode Offsets in Preference Optimization

Preference optimization losses operate on relative differences between chosen and rejected pairs, so any shared directional shift that moves both representations equally produces almost no gradient signal. That shared shift, the common-mode offset, accumulates across token positions and prompts because every update slightly nudges the entire hidden-state distribution in the same direction. Once the offset grows large enough it projects through the unembedding matrix and creates a single high-probability token that dominates sampling, producing the well-known vocabulary collapse. The offset is already visible in textbook DPO and SimPO runs that never reach collapse, which explains why ranking metrics can keep improving while generation quality silently degrades. Masking the common component out of the loss during training prevents the well from forming, at the cost of a small drop in the ranking objective. The practical takeaway is to monitor the norm of the mean-centered hidden-state shift on a small validation set after each epoch; when it exceeds roughly 0.3–0.4 of the typical per-token activation norm, generation quality is likely to degrade even if pairwise ranking scores remain high. Teams that already track output entropy or repetition rate can add this cheap hidden-state statistic without extra inference passes.

Things to Try This Week

  • Run the released Gemma 4 frozen-encoder code on your own Gulf Arabic audio to measure CPU latency versus a commercial API.
  • Apply SkillEval’s skill profiles to route queries between a fast 8B model and a larger reasoning model and track cost versus accuracy on your own benchmark.
  • Test SANE’s scorer-specific metamorphic tests on any LLM judge you maintain to surface hidden failure modes in professionalism or toxicity.
  • Experiment with CSD distillation when moving a Qwen teacher to a Llama or Gemma student tokenizer to retain cross-family performance.
  • Try LTEM-formatted tables in your RAG pipeline if you currently lose merged-cell or hierarchical-header structure with plain Markdown.

On the Horizon

  • Continued releases of scoped instruction-following benchmarks that separate main-task performance from constraint adherence.
  • Wider adoption of sparse-readout techniques for auditing internal representations of domain-specific concepts such as legal factors.
  • More agentic search interfaces that jointly optimize corpus artifacts, tools and instructions rather than fixing the interface by hand.
  • Growing use of preregistered numeric-value benchmarks for low-resource languages to test factual grounding and temporal consistency.

Les pertes de préférence lèvent le capot sur un décalage partagé de l'état caché que le classement ne pénalise jamais.

Ces pertes opèrent sur des différences relatives entre paires choisies et rejetées, donc tout décalage directionnel partagé qui déplace les deux représentations également ne produit presque aucun signal de gradient.

Ce décalage partagé, le décalage de mode commun, s'accumule à travers les positions de jetons et les consignes, parce que chaque mise à jour pousse légèrement toute la distribution d'état caché dans la même direction.

Une fois le décalage assez grand, il se projette à travers la matrice de déprojection et crée un seul jeton de haute probabilité qui domine l'échantillonnage, produisant un effondrement du vocabulaire.

Masquer la composante commune hors de la perte pendant l'entraînement empêche le puits de se former, au prix d'une petite baisse de l'objectif de classement.

Surveillez la norme du décalage d'état caché centré sur la moyenne, sur un petit jeu de validation après chaque époque ; quand elle dépasse à peu près zéro virgule trois à zéro virgule quatre de la norme d'activation typique par jeton, la qualité de génération risque de se dégrader même si les scores de classement par paires restent élevés.

Preference losses pop the hood on a shared hidden-state shift that ranking never penalizes. Those losses operate on relative differences between chosen and rejected pairs, so any shared directional shift that moves both representations equally produces almost no gradient signal.
