Google's Gemini 4 Neon ships with 1M context at $3 per million input tokens for enterprise reasoning tasks.

By Patrick November 19, 2026 Ep 239 6 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Listen in
Contents
Top Story Model Updates Agent & Tool Developments Practical & Community Also Today Under the Hood: Decision Models vs Generative LLMs on Bounded Tasks Things to Try This Week On the Horizon

What You Need to Know: Google released Gemini 4 Neon, the new mid-tier model in its Gemini 4 family, positioned between Carbon and Hydrogen with four effort levels and strong agentic coding results. Deutsche Telekom committed $110 million to Mistral while Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share. Builders should test Neon in Antigravity or the Gemini API this week for long-context agent runs and watch the December 1 pricing adjustments on the rest of the Gemini 4 line.

Top Story

Google introduced Gemini 4 Neon as the middle model in its Gemini 4 series, retiring Argon and targeting coding agents, knowledge work, and science workflows with a 1M-token context window. It carries $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output tokens, four effort levels, and a default 64K output that can reach 1M via background long decode. At high effort it matches Carbon on the Intelligence Index while posting 58.6 on the AA Coding Agent Index and 95.2% on GraphWalks long-context tasks. Neon runs only on Google-hosted infrastructure with closed weights and supports orchestration of parallel Hydrogen sub-agents inside Antigravity. The rollout reaches the paid Gemini API, AI Studio, Android Studio, Gemini for business, and Pro/Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app under the “Deeper analysis” label. Developers working on agentic coding or long-horizon science tasks should compare Neon directly against Carbon and Hydrogen on their own workloads before the December 1 price changes take effect. Source: blog.google

Model Updates

Deutsche Telekom’s Tech Fund Invests $110 Million in Mistral AI: WSJ

Deutsche Telekom’s tech fund placed $110 million into Mistral AI, adding to the French lab’s existing European backers. The investment supports continued development of Mistral’s open-weight and enterprise model families. Source: wsj.com

Anthropic Prices IPO at $83 Per Share: @AnthropicAI

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share, above the initial range, with Nasdaq trading expected to begin the following day. The filing covers Class A common stock and lists Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley as lead underwriters. Source: anthropic.com

New LoRA Adapter Lifts Qwen3.8 Coding Success From 39% to 65%: MarkTechPost

Hugging Face released Qwen3.8-27B-pi-adapted, an Apache-2.0 LoRA adapter trained with reinforcement learning for the pi coding agent. It raises SWE-bench Pro hard-51 pass@1 from 39.2% to 64.7% and Terminal-Bench 2.1 from 39.5% to 55.8%. Source: marktechpost.com

AI models deliver up to 14% chip design gains in AMD collaboration: ET CIO

OpenAI reported that its models produced up to 14% improvements in performance, power, and area on AMD’s flagship GPU IP relative to already-optimized baselines, with some changes already taped out. The same workflow delivered single-digit gains on AMD’s next-generation CPU blocks and reduced standard-cell area by 13% and 10% in the vector and matrix units of OpenAI’s custom Jalapeño inference chip. Source: cio.economictimes.indiatimes.com

Agent & Tool Developments

The Envoy agentic stack: Routing models, tokens, and MCP: Google Cloud

Google Cloud described an Envoy-based agentic stack that routes requests across models, manages token budgets, and supports the Model Context Protocol for tool calling. The approach aims to give operators fine-grained control over cost and latency in production agent deployments. Source: cloud.google.com

Daydream launches AI personal shopping agent: Axios

Daydream introduced an AI personal shopping agent that helps users discover and compare products across retailers. The agent focuses on preference matching and price tracking rather than completing purchases. Source: axios.com

Early look at Loop, new autonomous AI agent from Google: TestingCatalog AI News

Google is testing Loop, an autonomous agent capable of multi-step task execution inside the Gemini ecosystem. Early reports indicate it can maintain longer-running sessions than previous Gemini agent features. Source: testingcatalog.com

Cato Networks launches AgentSense to block harmful AI agent actions: Network World

Cato Networks released AgentSense, a security control that inspects and can block actions taken by AI agents before they reach enterprise resources. The product integrates with existing network and identity policies. Source: networkworld.com

Practical & Community

datasette-arrows 0.1a0: Simon Willison

Simon Willison published datasette-arrows 0.1a0, a plugin that adds Apache Arrow as a streaming export format inside Datasette. The release demonstrates the new register_output_renderer hook for custom streaming outputs without loading entire tables into memory. Source: simonwillison.net

Nutrient Data Extraction API: JSON Matching Your Schema With Per-Field Citations and Confidence: MarkTechPost

MarkTechPost covered a Nutrient Data Extraction API that converts PDFs, scans, and Office files into JSON matching a user-defined schema while attaching page, region, and match-label citations to every field. A Studio interface lets teams test extraction before writing code. Source: marktechpost.com

Code Effects Releases Version 7 with Secure AI Agents, Rule Governance, and Auditing for .NET: HackerNoon

Code Effects shipped version 7 of its .NET rules engine, adding secure AI agent integration, rule governance controls, and full auditing capabilities. The update targets enterprise teams that need auditable decision logic alongside agent workflows. Source: hackernoon.com

Microsoft launches cheaper, specialised AI models with flexible licensing: TechGig

Microsoft announced a new line of cheaper, domain-specialized models accompanied by more flexible licensing terms. The offering is positioned for enterprises that want lower per-token costs on narrower tasks. Source: techgig.com

Also Today

The Gemini app is now available on lower-memory Android devices.: Google AI Blog

Google extended the Gemini app to lower-memory Android devices, enabling Gemini Live and custom image creation on a wider range of phones. Source: blog.google

Microsoft brings AI inference closer to enterprise data with Windows Server 2028: Network World

Microsoft announced Windows Server 2028 features that bring AI inference closer to on-premises enterprise data. Source: networkworld.com

Understanding Cross-Modal Inconsistency through Representation Subspaces in Omni Models: arXiv NLP

A new arXiv paper analyzes how omni models produce inconsistent answers across modalities and isolates the representation subspaces responsible. Source: arxiv.org

Contract-Explicit Evaluation: From Auditing Learned Readers to Scoped Certificates: arXiv NLP

Another arXiv paper introduces a contract-explicit evaluation protocol that delivers distribution-free certificates for document-level relation extraction and LLM-judge leaderboards. Source: arxiv.org

Under the Hood: Decision Models vs Generative LLMs on Bounded Tasks

Decision models return a typed answer plus probability directly instead of generating free text, which removes the need for post-hoc parsing on classification and judging tasks. In contact-center workloads they match frontier LLMs on six classification problems while running at the lowest per-request cost among the API options tested. Their main weakness is abstention behavior: most models rarely select the explicit “no-answer” option, creating the largest gap versus the strongest generative baselines. On two judging tasks the same models lose accuracy sharply once parameter count drops, and they show higher position bias than generative judges. The paper’s eight real-world use cases demonstrate that routing high-confidence decision-model predictions automatically while sending uncertain cases to generative LLMs or humans yields the best accuracy-cost trade-off. Teams facing high-volume intent classification or compliance checking should therefore benchmark a decision model first on their exact label set before defaulting to a full generative call. Source: arxiv.org

Things to Try This Week

  • Test Gemini 4 Neon at high effort on a long-context coding or science task inside Google Antigravity and compare cost per task against Carbon.
  • Install datasette-arrows and export a large table as Arrow to see streaming performance without full memory load.
  • Run the new Qwen3.8-27B-pi-adapted LoRA on a SWE-bench-style repo task to measure the 25-point lift on hard instances.
  • Evaluate a decision model such as Gemini-Decision-1 on your contact-center intent labels to check abstention rates before routing production traffic.
  • Review the Envoy agentic stack configuration examples on Google Cloud if you need token-budget controls across multiple models.

On the Horizon

  • Anthropic shares are expected to begin trading on Nasdaq following the $83 IPO price.
  • Google continues rolling out Neon to additional Gemini app surfaces and business customers.
  • Microsoft’s Windows Server 2028 inference features move into preview for enterprise data-center operators.
  • More European funding rounds for open-weight labs are anticipated after the Deutsche Telekom–Mistral announcement.

Une interface Studio permet aux équipes de tester l'extraction avant d'écrire la moindre ligne de code.

Code Effects, rapporte HackerNoon, a livré la version sept de son moteur de règles .NET, avec des crochets pour des agents I-A sécurisés, une gouvernance sur les règles, et une piste d'audit complète.

Les équipes d'entreprise qui ont besoin d'une logique de décision auditable à côté des flux d'agents sont les utilisateurs visés.

Des modèles moins chers, spécialisés par domaine, sont arrivés de Microsoft, rapporte TechGig, avec des licences plus souples.

Les entreprises qui veulent des coûts par jeton plus bas sur des tâches plus étroites, c'est pour elles que cette gamme est faite.

Ailleurs, Google a étendu l'appli Gemini aux appareils Android à mémoire plus limitée, a indiqué le Google AI Blog, en activant Gemini Live et la création d'images personnalisées sur un plus large éventail de téléphones.

Microsoft a annoncé des fonctions de Windows Server deux mille vingt-huit, rapporte Network World, qui rapprochent l'inférence I-A des données d'entreprise sur site.

D'autres tours de table européens pour les labos à poids ouverts sont attendus dans le sillage de l'accord entre Deutsche Telekom et Mistral.

Les modèles de décision, constate un article sur arXiv, renvoient une réponse typée plus une probabilité, au lieu de générer du texte libre, et c'est là que je veux soulever le capot pour les tâches bien délimitées.

La classification et le jugement n'ont plus besoin d'un analyseur collé à la fin, parce que l'étiquette arrive déjà typée.

Sur des charges de centres de contact, ces modèles égalent les grands modèles de langage de frontière, ou L-L-M, sur six problèmes de classification, et ils le font au coût par requête le plus bas parmi les options d'A-P-I testées.

L'abstention, c'est le trou : la plupart d'entre eux ne choisissent presque jamais l'option explicite « pas de réponse », et c'est l'écart le plus large face aux meilleures bases génératives.

Sur deux tâches de jugement, la précision chute nettement dès que le nombre de paramètres baisse, et ils montrent un biais de position plus fort — ils s'appuient sur l'ordre des réponses — que les juges génératifs.

Huit cas d'usage réels dessinent une scission : accepter automatiquement les prédictions à haute confiance des modèles de décision, et envoyer le reste incertain vers des L-L-M génératifs ou vers un humain.

Ce routage a produit le meilleur compromis précision-coût de l'étude.

La classification d'intention à fort volume et le contrôle de conformité sont les charges où un modèle de décision est d'abord évalué sur le jeu d'étiquettes exact ; le jugement, les petits nombres de paramètres, et tout ce qui demande un « pas de réponse » propre passent encore par un appel génératif complet.

Classification and judging no longer need a parser glued on the end, because the label arrives already typed. In contact-center workloads those models match frontier large language models, or LLMs, on six classification problems, and they do it at the lowest per-request cost among the API options tested. Abstention is the hole: most of them almost never pick the explicit no-answer option, and that is the widest gap against the strongest generative baselines. On two judging tasks, accuracy falls sharply once parameter count drops, and they show higher position bias — they lean on answer order — than generative judges. Eight real-world use cases point to a split: automatically accept high-confidence decision-model predictions, and send the uncertain remainder to generative LLMs or to a human. That routing produced the best accuracy-cost trade-off in the study. High-volume intent classification and compliance checking are the workloads where a decision model gets benchmarked first on the exact label set; judging, small parameter counts, and anything that needs a clean no-answer still go to a full generative call.
