Tesla First Principles

Battery degradation curves rarely follow the smooth lines shown in marketing slides. Real-world LFP packs in Model Ys often drop several percent in the first 20,000 miles, then flatten sharply once the initial SEI layer stabilizes. That pattern appeared again in recent health-test data where fast-charging frequency did not produce continued linear loss.

The practical result is that early warranty claims may cluster while later ownership years see slower capacity fade. For Tesla this means service-center load and parts inventory planning can be front-loaded rather than spread evenly across the fleet. It also changes the residual-value math for used vehicles that reach the market after the steep portion of the curve has already passed.

Over time the same data set will influence how aggressively Tesla can warranty longer-range packs or offer battery upgrades. If the plateau holds across climates and charging habits, the company gains a clearer cost target for second-life applications and insurance products tied to remaining capacity.
