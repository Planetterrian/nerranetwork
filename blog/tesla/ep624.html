Tesla First Principles

The decision to reduce memory capacity on the AI5 and AI6 chips while preserving bandwidth reveals a deeper engineering priority. Physical AI systems must react in milliseconds to changing environments, so data movement speed matters more than storing larger models. On-chip SRAM already delivers far higher exchange rates than external DRAM even though its capacity remains small.

This approach trades peak model size for lower latency and reduced power draw. Larger models can improve decision quality yet increase both compute time and energy use, slowing the robot or vehicle when quick responses are essential. Tesla’s choice aligns with the reality that inference hardware for Optimus and unsupervised FSD will run continuously in the field rather than in controlled data centers.

The same constraint appears in supply-chain planning. Cutting capacity to seventy-two gigabytes on AI5 and one hundred forty-four gigabytes on AI6 lowers cost and improves yield at a time when memory remains scarce. The move supports higher production volumes for Optimus without waiting for the memory market to ease, showing how hardware limits directly shape manufacturing timelines.
