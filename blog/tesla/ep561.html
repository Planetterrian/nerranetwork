Tesla First Principles

Tesla's two new power purchase agreements in the Southwest lock in long-term clean-energy supply at utility scale. The contracts directly support growing electricity demand from vehicle factories, AI training clusters, and Megapack production lines rather than relying solely on grid purchases or renewable-energy certificates. This approach converts what would be variable operating costs into predictable, contracted capacity that can be monetized through virtual power plants or excess sales.

The structure matters because Tesla's energy margin profile differs from its vehicle business. Megapack and Powerwall deployments already generate higher gross margins than many car sales; securing dedicated generation behind those assets reduces input-price risk and improves the return profile of each installed unit. Rivals without equivalent vertical integration must buy power on spot markets or accept thinner project economics when they attempt similar storage offerings.

Over time the pattern suggests Tesla's energy segment can function as a margin stabilizer even when vehicle deliveries face regional softness. The limiting factor will be how quickly Megapack factory output and Powerwall attach rates can absorb the contracted electrons without creating new curtailment or interconnection bottlenecks.
