Tesla First Principles

Europe's incremental approvals for supervised FSD create a natural experiment in how regulators weigh demonstrated miles against the absence of unsupervised capability. Denmark's decision follows similar clearances in the Netherlands, Lithuania, and Estonia, each requiring localized safety data before drivers can activate the system. The pattern shows regulators accepting supervised operation as a distinct category rather than demanding full autonomy from day one.

That approach differs from the U.S. path where unsupervised robotaxi deployment remains the headline goal. In Europe the supervised label keeps the driver legally responsible, which lowers the regulatory bar but also caps the service model Tesla can offer. The distinction matters for gross margin because supervised features still require a paid driver or owner behind the wheel, while true unsupervised operation would shift the economics toward fleet utilization rates.

The open variable is whether the safety snapshots Tesla is now publishing will accelerate or slow further European expansion. If localized data continues to show lower intervention rates than human drivers, additional countries may follow the same supervised route. If the data instead highlights edge cases, regulators could extend review periods and delay revenue from software subscriptions in those markets.
