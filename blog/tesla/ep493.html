Tesla First Principles

Taking a step back from today's headlines, the arrival of Chinese-built Model 3 vehicles in Canada tests the assumption that local manufacturing always delivers faster delivery and lower logistics costs than importing from existing plants.

The concrete data point is the time between production in Shanghai and port arrival in Canada, which occurred in days rather than the weeks often required for cross-border plant-to-dealer movement inside North America. That speed comes from established high-volume lines in China already running at scale, while new capacity elsewhere would require additional ramp-up time and capital.

Tesla’s approach of using existing global production to serve adjacent markets therefore shortens the path to customer deliveries and preserves capital for other programs, even when political pressure favors building every market locally from scratch.
