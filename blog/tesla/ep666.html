  1. Tesla quietly removes the $41,990 Model Y AWD from its US configurator — r/teslamotors

    The base all-wheel-drive Model Y trim priced at $41,990 is no longer available to order in the United States.

Tesla First Principles

The shift from outright vehicle sales to a subscription-locked ownership model for the Cybercab introduces a structural change in how Tesla captures recurring revenue. Buyers pay $29,990 upfront yet must maintain a $299 monthly autonomy subscription that the vehicle physically requires to operate, adding $3,588 annually after the first free month. This arrangement converts a one-time hardware transaction into an ongoing service relationship whose price Tesla can adjust after notice. The first month of the subscription is included at no charge, but the agreement makes clear that the car cannot function without an active plan covering self-driving, connectivity, and certain maintenance items.

The same structure limits where and how the vehicle can be used. Owners may drive only inside geofenced areas already approved for Tesla’s robotaxi service and cannot add the car to ride-hailing fleets without company permission. Regulators are still evaluating whether the steering-wheel-free design meets federal motor vehicle safety standards, which means the subscription itself cannot guarantee nationwide usability until approvals arrive. Tesla has stated that availability will expand across the United States as those approvals are secured. Ashok Elluswamy noted that privately owned Cybercabs will operate without human supervision inside the same geographic zones used by the company’s commercial robotaxi fleet. The vehicle features a gold-coloured exterior, two passenger seats, and butterfly-style doors.

For Tesla the model reduces capital intensity by shifting some operating costs to owners while preserving control over software and maintenance. It also creates a direct link between vehicle utilization and subscription revenue that scales with regulatory expansion rather than with additional factory output. The approach mirrors earlier connectivity and Full Self-Driving subscriptions but now applies to the entire vehicle, raising the question of how price adjustments or service-area changes will affect long-term owner economics. Elon Musk has already flagged that the current $299 monthly fee is too high and should align closer to the roughly $115 cost of FSD plus connectivity on other models. A non-refundable $1,000 deposit is required to place an order, with first customer deliveries targeted for December 2026.
