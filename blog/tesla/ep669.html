  1. Tesla unveils Powercore solid-state transformer for data centers and Megapacks — Teslarati

    Tesla unveiled Powercore, a solid-state medium voltage transformer that also handles the jobs of an inverter and a circuit breaker in a single unit. The product is rated at 7.4 MW or 9 MVA of continuous output at 34.5 kV AC with grid-forming support for 10 MVA of peak power. Peak efficiency reaches 98.86 percent across a DC range of 800 to 2,000 volts. A built-in solid-state breaker clears faults in about 100 microseconds. The unit carries no flammable oil and supports modular servicing. First installations are planned for 2027 and the product is described as Made in America. Tesla rates Powercore at 7.4 MW of continuous output at 34.5 kV AC. The device fills a gap left open when Megapack 3 and Megablock were introduced in September 2025. Powercore appears to be the missing piece that groups four Megapack 3 units with a transformer and switchgear into a single medium voltage block. A typical battery or solar project today uses low voltage inverters to turn DC into AC then a large oil-filled transformer to step that power up to grid voltage. Solid-state units handle both steps with power electronics which means less equipment a smaller footprint and no flammable oil. Grid-forming capability lets it actively help hold voltage and frequency steady rather than simply following the grid. Lead times for conventional transformers have stretched into years as AI data centers and renewable projects crowd utility interconnection queues. Transformers have barely changed since the 1890s with copper coils in a tank of oil while batteries solar and compute all run on DC. Source: teslarati.com

Tesla First Principles

Solid-state power electronics have historically been limited to lower voltages and niche applications because heat cost and reliability constraints kept them out of medium-voltage grid connections. Tesla’s Powercore integrates the transformer inverter and breaker functions into one unit rated for 7.4 MW continuous output at 34.5 kV eliminating the separate oil-filled transformer and its associated losses that conventional Megablock architectures still carry. The 98.86 percent peak efficiency and 100-microsecond fault clearing time are direct results of moving the entire conversion chain onto silicon rather than copper coils in tanks of oil. Data-center operators and large solar-plus-storage projects currently face multi-year waits for conventional transformers while their DC-native loads continue to grow. By delivering grid-forming capability inside the same enclosure that steps voltage and manages faults Powercore removes an entire layer of equipment and the associated real-estate permitting and maintenance overhead. The 25-year warranty target and -40 °C to +55 °C operating range further reduce the lifetime cost curve that has kept solid-state solutions out of utility-scale deployments until now.

For Tesla Energy the product closes the loop between Megapack production and the downstream electrical balance-of-system that has historically been sourced from third parties. Capturing that margin while shortening project timelines gives the division a structural cost advantage precisely when AI-driven load growth is pushing interconnection queues to their limits. The same hardware also positions Tesla to sell into solar arrays and behind-the-meter storage without requiring customers to coordinate multiple vendors for the medium-voltage interface.

Tesla is advancing two lines at once, letting existing car owners connect vehicles to the network to make money while also selling dedicated driverless cars to individuals. Conditions for private cars to join the network, revenue sharing, and operation boundaries are almost all blank. Individual buyers have been informed that buying the car does not mean they can casually use it to run Robotaxi to make money. Heat, cost, and reliability are why solid-state power electronics stayed in lower-voltage niches instead of sitting on medium-voltage grid connections. Moving the entire conversion chain onto silicon rather than copper coils in tanks of oil strips out a separate transformer layer and the real-estate, permitting, and maintenance that layer drags with it. Conventional Megablock architectures still carry that separate oil-filled transformer and its losses. Tesla is targeting a twenty-five year warranty on this class of hardware. The operating range runs from minus forty degrees Celsius to plus fifty-five degrees Celsius. Those two figures pull down the lifetime cost curve that kept solid-state solutions out of utility-scale deployments until now. For Tesla Energy, the product closes the loop between Megapack production and the downstream electrical balance-of-system that has historically been sourced from third parties. Capturing that margin while shortening project timelines is the cost advantage, at the same moment AI-driven load growth is stacking up. The same hardware also lets Tesla sell into solar arrays and behind-the-meter storage without asking customers to coordinate multiple vendors for the medium-voltage interface. Before we go, next month’s holiday software update is the larger release Tesla is lining up behind this light drop. That covers it for today's Tesla developments.
