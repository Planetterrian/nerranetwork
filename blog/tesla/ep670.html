Top 12 News Items

  1. Tesla registered a record 200 Cybercabs in Texas in one day — Teslarati

    The batch arrived on the Texas DMV automated vehicle register at 3:45 p.m. CT on November 17 and included 200 Cybercabs plus one Model Y. The additions pushed Tesla’s Cybercab count past 750 registered units in the state and restored its lead over Waymo with 1,507 driverless vehicles against Waymo’s 1,321. Cybercabs now make up 51.3 percent of Tesla’s registered Texas fleet at 773 units versus 734 Model Ys. The register does not indicate where the vehicles will operate or confirm they are carrying riders. The timing aligns with Tesla’s stated plans to launch in Dallas next month. Model Y robotaxis have already run without drivers around the clock in Dallas since November 11. Tesla is shifting factory capacity toward the Cybercab, including winding down Cybertruck production at Giga Texas by the end of summer 2027. Source: teslarati.com

  2. Musk pledges higher Tesla output at China plant — Reuters

    Elon Musk said during his first public visit to Shenzhen that Tesla will continue to increase production in China. The Shanghai factory delivered 810,889 Model 3 and Model Y vehicles in the first 10 months of the year, up 21.4 percent year on year, with 98 percent of components sourced locally. The plant has an annual capacity of more than 950,000 vehicles. Musk described himself as a “superfan” of China and praised its architecture, natural landscapes, and railway system. He noted that greater cooperation between the US and China would benefit both countries. The factory supplies the Chinese market and exports to Europe, South Korea, and Australia. Domestic retail sales in China totaled 389,905 vehicles in the first 10 months, down 15 percent year on year, while exports reached 420,984 vehicles, up 101.28 percent. Source: reuters.com

  3. Tesla seeks tax break for $3.1 billion plant in Nueces County — KRIS 6 News Corpus Christi

    Tesla wants to build a $3.1 billion polysilicon plant in the Tuloso-Midway school district that would hire 515 workers by 2032 and pay average compensation plus benefits of $130,000. The company applied under a state program that would waive day-to-day school property taxes from 2028 to 2030 and halve them from 2031 to 2040. Even with the break, the district would still collect about $222 million in taxes from the plant between 2028 and 2065. The plant would sit along State Highway 44 between FM 1694 and County Road 61, with construction running from 2027 to 2030. Tesla says more than 90 percent of the world’s solar-grade polysilicon is currently made in China. The school board has been under a nondisclosure agreement with Tesla for about six months but has not yet approved any tax break. The Comptroller’s office will review the application and make a recommendation before the school board and Governor’s Office decide. Source: kristv.com

  4. Musk signals steering wheel, pedal-free Model Y robotaxi with seating for up to 7 — InsideEVs

    Elon Musk said at the New York Times DealBook Summit that Tesla plans an autonomous Model Y without steering wheel or pedals to handle the 10 percent of rider miles that require more than two seats. The vehicle would carry six or seven people and complement the two-seat Cybercab. Regular Model Ys with steering wheels will remain available for purchase. Musk noted that the Cybercab covers 90 percent of passenger miles, leaving the remaining 10 percent for multi-passenger trips such as family travel. The new Model Y variant would be designed from the factory without basic driving controls so passengers only ride. Tesla already operates Model Ys in its robotaxi fleet, but those retain steering wheels and pedals. No launch timing or pricing was disclosed for the autonomous version. Source: insideevs.com

  5. Tesla plans Optimus robot demonstration within the next two months — Futunn

    Elon Musk said an interactive video of the Optimus robot will be released within two months and will emphasize authenticity. The robot is expected to handle the full dirty-plate sequence of scraping food, washing, drying and putting items away safely in 12 to 18 months. No current humanoid can perform that sequence. Hands remain the primary bottleneck. First real-world work is targeted at heavy industry such as mining and refining on a 24/7 basis, with home use coming later. Musk stated that everyone will eventually want one or two Optimus units. Source: news.futunn.com

  6. Judge orders Manitoba to reveal who excluded Tesla from EV rebate program — CBC

    A judge ordered the Kinew government to disclose who decided to exclude Tesla from Manitoba’s EV rebate program and why. The ruling requires the province to release the relevant affidavits. Tesla had been barred from the program despite meeting the technical criteria for eligible vehicles. Source: cbc.ca

  7. Powerwall owners stabilize grid in 100+ VPP programs — Tesla Energy

    Powerwall owners are dispatching power to stabilize the grid through more than 100 Tesla virtual power plant programs worldwide. The systems activate automatically and quietly during outages without homeowners noticing the switch. Source: x.com

  8. Vehicle touchscreen improvement in latest software update (2026.44.1) — @TeslaCharging

    The update clusters charger pins on the map to show network coverage when zooming out and adds third-party chargers in Europe. The change improves visibility of charging options at different zoom levels. Source: x.com

  9. Tesla opens first Texas Semi Megacharger — Digitaltoday

    Tesla opened its first dedicated Semi Megacharger in Texas, though the annual target of 37 sites remains in doubt. The site supports the growing Semi fleet operating in the state. Source: digitaltoday.co.kr

  10. Tesla’s iPhone app switches to Google Maps in version 4.63.0 — Drive Tesla Canada

    The app now uses Google Maps instead of Apple Maps, adding new look, Summon satellite views and smoother site maps. The change affects navigation and location features within the Tesla app on iOS. Source: driveteslacanada.ca

  11. Tesla drops hint at imminent Cybercab launch in Dallas — Drive Tesla Canada

    Tesla posted a video showing more than 50 Cybercabs parked in a Dallas lot with additional units arriving on a car carrier. The footage suggests preparation for an upcoming launch in the city. Source: driveteslacanada.ca

  12. Canada may limit where Chinese EVs can drive over security concerns — Drive Tesla Canada

    Canadian officials are reviewing whether to restrict Chinese-made electric vehicles from certain roads and areas due to data security risks. The review follows broader concerns about data collection and transmission features in vehicles from China. Source: driveteslacanada.ca

Tesla First Principles

Tesla’s Shanghai factory delivered 810,889 vehicles in the first ten months of 2026 while sourcing 98 percent of its components locally and running at more than 950,000 units of annual capacity. That level of vertical integration inside one country reduces logistics cost and tariff exposure but also concentrates supply risk if trade rules shift. The same plant now exports more than half its output, showing how local content can support both domestic sales and global reach when volumes are high enough. Domestic retail sales in China fell 15 percent year on year while exports rose more than 100 percent, illustrating how the factory balances two markets from a single production base.

A $3.1 billion polysilicon plant proposed for Texas would employ 515 people at average compensation plus benefits of $130,000 and pay the local school district roughly $222 million in taxes over nearly four decades even after the requested abatements. Building the front end of the solar supply chain inside the United States changes the cost structure for Tesla Energy products by shortening the distance between raw material and finished Megapack or Powerwall. It also ties future margin expansion to the success of a single large industrial project whose tax treatment is still under review. The plant would produce the main material for solar cells at a time when more than 90 percent of global solar-grade polysilicon comes from China, giving Tesla a domestic alternative that could stabilize input prices for its energy storage and solar offerings.

When a company controls both the cell chemistry and the final assembly steps, the gross margin on energy storage can move with material prices and factory utilization rather than with third-party supplier contracts. The 33.1 percent energy gross margin reported for the third quarter already reflects some of that leverage. Extending the same logic to polysilicon adds another controllable variable, but only if the Texas facility reaches the scale and yield targets implied by the 515-worker headcount and the 2027–2030 construction window. The project would also create a new revenue and employment anchor in Nueces County, where the school district stands to collect substantial long-term tax revenue even after temporary relief.
