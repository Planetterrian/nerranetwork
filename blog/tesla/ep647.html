Top 12 News Items

  1. Tesla appears to be preparing to sell US-built Model 3s in Canada again: Electrek

    Tesla Canada updated the Model 3 Premium Rear-Wheel Drive page with 584 km range and 5.2-second 0-100 km/h time. Those match the Fremont-built Long Range RWD sold in Canada two years ago. The Shanghai-built version previously listed at $39,490 CAD offered 463 km and 6.2 seconds. The page now shows a curb weight of 1,745 kg and marketing copy promising up to 584 km EPA-estimated range. Canadian owners first spotted the change on X. The update comes while Shanghai-built orders remain paused pending new government quota expected in March 2027. Canada’s 25% counter-tariffs on US-made vehicles remain in effect. The US site rates the same Fremont car at 363 miles EPA range with 0-60 mph in 4.9 seconds starting at $42,490 USD. Tesla’s Canadian comparison page still lists the older Shanghai specs. In October 2024 the Fremont Long Range RWD qualified for the federal iZEV rebate at a $54,990 starting price. The new figures align exactly with the Model 3 Premium RWD Tesla sells in the US today. Source: electrek.co

  2. Ex-Tesla engineers raise $23m for space batteries: The Australian

    Former Tesla engineers raised $23 million in Sydney to develop batteries for space applications. The funding targets the growing space battery race. The round was announced on October 27. The engineers left Tesla to focus on specialized cells for orbital and deep-space use. Sydney investors backed the effort after reviewing early prototypes. The capital will support scaling production of radiation-hardened packs. No specific delivery timeline was disclosed in the announcement. Source: theaustralian.com.au

  3. Tesla Cybercab Inverter Tech to Debut in Model 3 This Year: Teslarati

    Head of Drive Unit Engineering Nick Verzini said the new inverter board uses far fewer electronic components and supports multiple suppliers. The board drops straight into a Model Y drive unit. It will enter Model 3 production this year. The design keeps the entire interface compatible with existing drive-unit architecture. Verzini confirmed the printed circuit board assembly and power stage will reach Model 3 lines before year end. The Cybercab drive unit also runs an 11:1 gear ratio instead of the roughly 9:1 ratio used on most current Teslas. The same components were developed to reduce supplier risk and simplify assembly across platforms. Source: x.com

  4. Tesla And Turo Have A New Way To Get People Hooked On FSD: InsideEVs

    Turo hosts can now link their Tesla account to confirm which cars have Full Self-Driving Supervised equipped. Tesla models that qualify for FSD but lack it installed become eligible for a one-month free trial. The revised interface launches October 28. Renters receive a Turo disclaimer that they must supervise the vehicle at all times. Turo says Tesla Model 3 and Model Y already rank as its third and fourth most-rented cars. The partnership lets hosts share data directly so Turo can verify FSD status without relying on owner descriptions. Hosts who add FSD through the trial can then offer the feature to renters immediately. Source: insideevs.com

  5. Pilot, Tesla open first 2 heavy-duty truck charging sites: Transport Topics

    Pilot and Tesla opened the first two heavy-duty truck charging sites. The locations mark the initial rollout of Tesla’s Semi-compatible chargers at Pilot facilities. Both sites are now operational for commercial electric trucks. The partnership focuses on high-power charging corridors for long-haul routes. No additional site count or timeline was released in the announcement. Source: ttnews.com

  6. Tesla expands Houston robotaxi zone tenfold but has just 34 vehicles: Digitaltoday

    Tesla expanded the Houston robotaxi zone from 25 to 254 square miles. The new area covers 1.3 million residents. The fleet currently stands at 34 vehicles. The geofence now reaches roughly five times the size of Waymo’s operating area in the same city. Expansion brings the total covered population to 1.3 million people. The shape of the new boundary resembles a rocket silhouette. Service remains limited to the small active fleet despite the larger mapped territory. Source: digitaltoday.co.kr

  7. Latvia Becomes 10th EU Country to Approve Tesla FSD: Not A Tesla App

    Latvia approved Tesla’s Full Self-Driving Supervised for public roads. Rollout begins soon and starts with HW4 vehicles running the European build of FSD v14. Latvia joins Lithuania and Estonia as the tenth EU member to approve the system independently. FSD is now approved in 18 countries worldwide. The EU-wide vote is scheduled for December. Germany has indicated it will support the measure if speed offset stays within 10 percent of posted limits. Slovakia became the ninth country to approve the software less than two weeks earlier. The Baltic approvals now form a contiguous regional block for early deployment. Source: notateslaapp.com

  8. Fort Bend County sweetens pot to land $10B Tesla solar plant: Yahoo Finance

    Fort Bend County offered a 30-year incentives package to attract Tesla’s proposed $10 billion solar panel facility. The package waives machinery taxes and inventory taxes for the first ten years. Tesla would still pay property taxes on structures. The deal extends another five years if employment exceeds current targets. The race for the plant has narrowed to two locations. Lamar Consolidated Independent School District has already added its own incentives. A Tesla site selection advisor told the Houston Chronicle the final decision sits between one Texas site and one out-of-state option. The county director of economic development confirmed the extended abatement terms. Source: finance.yahoo.com

  9. Tesla Designed The Cybercab To Use LFP Cells In The Future: InsideEVs

    Tesla designed the Cybercab battery pack to support cheaper LFP chemistry in the future. The current 88S6P pack uses 4680 cells in a steel enclosure. Each module is potted separately for individual replacement. Wireless battery management boards replace traditional wiring harnesses. New contactors eliminated the need for a pyro fuse. The pack received an A-minus internal grade compared with lower scores for earlier structural packs. Source: insideevs.com

  10. What Robotics Experts Think of Tesla’s Optimus Robot: IEEE Spectrum

    Robotics experts shared their assessments of Tesla’s Optimus robot. The discussion covered mechanical design, control systems, and potential deployment timelines. Experts noted both strengths in cost-focused engineering and remaining challenges in dexterity and reliability. No specific performance metrics were released in the coverage. Source: spectrum.ieee.org

Tesla First Principles

The Cybercab’s modular assembly approach changes the traditional relationship between factory output and market readiness. Instead of a single long final assembly line, the vehicle splits into front, rear, side and center modules that are built and tested in parallel before meeting at just 25 stations. That layout uses roughly half the floor space of a Model Y line and reaches 78 percent automation overall, with the framing line already at 100 percent. The result is a line that can already exceed current deployment rates without running every day. Engineers removed the paint shop entirely and coat only the side modules inside the molding machine. The battery pack uses an 88S6P configuration of 4680 cells in a steel enclosure with each module potted separately so a single failed module can be swapped. Wireless battery management boards replace traditional wiring harnesses and new contactors eliminate the pyro fuse.

This reversal moves the bottleneck from manufacturing to regulatory approval and city-by-city service agreements. When the factory can produce thousands of units per week in half the usual space, expansion depends on how quickly new markets can absorb vehicles rather than how quickly the plant can be enlarged. The design choice to delete the paint shop and coat only the side modules inside the molding machine further reduces both capital and operating costs that would otherwise scale with volume. Lars Moravy noted during the Munro tour that the line does not run every day because output already outpaces deployments. The same modular logic that lets the line outpace deployments today also means future capacity additions require less incremental floor space and fewer stations than conventional automotive plants. The 500,000-mile durability target built into the Cybercab further supports high-utilization economics once regulatory gates open.

For investors the implication is that capital intensity per additional vehicle falls once the first lines are running. That changes the cash-flow profile of any future robotaxi network relative to earlier vehicle programs that were constrained by production hell. Battery management moved to wireless boards and new contactors eliminated the pyro fuse, trimming both parts count and assembly time. These engineering decisions collectively lower the marginal cost of each additional unit once the initial lines are qualified.
