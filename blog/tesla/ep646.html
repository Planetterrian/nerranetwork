Top 12 News Items

  1. Barclays Upgrades Tesla to Overweight, Raises PT to $450: TSLAming

    Barclays lifted its rating from Equal Weight to Overweight and its price target from $370 to $450. The firm expects Tesla automotive revenue to return to growth in 2026 after two years of declines, with further gains in 2027 from FSD demand and higher exports out of Giga Shanghai. Energy storage is described as nearly sold out for 2027, with potential shipment growth above 50 percent driven by data-center customers. Robotaxi miles driven have accelerated sharply since the June 2025 launch, and FSD uptake exceeded 75 percent of new North American vehicles in Q3 2026. Barclays also flags a potential Tesla–SpaceX merger that would include a premium for Tesla shareholders. The upgrade rests on four concrete pillars: returning automotive growth, energy storage as a second engine, Robotaxi and FSD reaching an inflection, and the merger catalyst. Source: za.investing.com

  2. Tesla India Racks Up Rs 247 Crore Revenue In First Year Of Vehicle Sales: ETV Bharat

    Tesla recorded Rs 247 crore in revenue from vehicle sales in its first full year operating in India. The company sold 650 units during the first half of fiscal 2027. Those figures mark the initial measurable footprint after the brand entered the market with Model Y availability. Revenue and volume together give the first public snapshot of how quickly Tesla established dealer and delivery operations in the country. Source: etvbharat.com

  3. Tesla entered India with big plans. Do its latest results show profit?: India Today

    Tesla India posted its first-year financial results, showing revenue alongside questions about whether the unit reached profitability. The results cover Model Y sales and the broader vehicle revenue line reported for the initial operating period. Observers are now examining whether the early revenue translated into positive margins after accounting for import duties, logistics, and local overhead. Source: indiatoday.in

  4. Powerwall Free Nights vs Tesla $800 Credit in Texas: shattered.io

    A Dallas-Fort Worth homeowner with a 17 kW solar array and two Powerwalls modeled a year of meter data and found a free-overnight electricity plan could deliver roughly $717 in annual savings. The Tesla credit pays $400 per Powerwall per year but restricts how the batteries can be used. The homeowner noted 63 percent of current imports already occur between 9 p.m. and 6 a.m., so grid-charging the batteries overnight could cut daytime imports from 4,412 kWh to about 620 kWh. No confirmation yet exists that the plan will accept a 17 kW distributed-generation system or how deep daily cycling will affect six-year-old batteries. The comparison pits a fixed Tesla incentive against a retailer plan that shifts load timing, highlighting a concrete trade-off for existing Powerwall owners in Texas. Source: shattered.io Source: thecooldown.com

The coverage frames the company’s push across autonomous driving and humanoid robotics as a widening lead that other firms are now trying to close.   6. Tesla Wants to Earn More From AI Than From Cars: Mezha

Tesla First Principles

Energy storage now sits at the center of how Tesla monetizes its AI ambitions. Barclays reports the division is nearly sold out for 2027, with data-center demand cited as a primary driver and potential shipment growth above 50 percent. That backlog arrives at the same moment Tesla commits roughly 14 billion yuan to a 20,000-robot AI training cluster. The two facts together show storage is no longer a side business but the physical layer required to keep large training runs running at scale. The sold-out position gives the energy group multi-year revenue visibility that the automotive segment has lacked in recent years.

The economics differ from vehicle margins. A sold-out 2027 book gives visibility into multi-year revenue that vehicle sales have not delivered recently, while the training cluster investment is capital that must be recovered through higher-value AI services rather than car deliveries. Storage also carries different supply-chain constraints: cells, inverters, and site integration move on separate timelines from vehicle platforms. When demand from data centers crowds out other customers, Tesla gains pricing power but also faces allocation decisions that did not exist when storage was mainly residential. The capital intensity of the training cluster therefore rests on storage margins remaining stable enough to fund continued compute expansion.

This shift changes the capital allocation question. If energy storage can absorb the output of new cell lines and deliver steadier cash flow than automotive, the case for continued heavy spending on AI compute strengthens. The same factories that once chased vehicle volume targets now feed a storage business whose customers pay for uptime rather than range or acceleration. The result is a narrower but more durable margin profile that hinges on execution in grid-scale projects rather than on quarterly delivery numbers. Allocation choices between data-center Megapacks and residential Powerwalls will determine how quickly the energy segment can scale without straining cell supply.

Tesla’s energy and AI threads are pulling the business in new directions at once.
