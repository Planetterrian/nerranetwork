    2. Executives confirmed the next-generation Roadster will be built at the Texas facility alongside other models.     3. Tesla's timeline for scaling the Optimus robot has hit a manufacturing constraint that executives have not yet detailed publicly.     4. The number of vehicles operating in unsupervised or limited-supervision mode in Austin, Dallas, and Houston has declined.     5. Tesla is requiring Canadian customers to sign off on lower-range or feature variants before taking possession of ordered vehicles. The policy appears tied to inventory management and certification timing in the market. Buyers who decline the adjusted specs face further delays.     6. Internal trainers have flagged discrepancies between Tesla's published safety statistics and observed edge-case performance during training sessions. The concerns center on how rare events are captured and weighted in the data pipeline. This feedback loop directly influences the next iteration of the Full Self-Driving software stack. Regulators reviewing unsupervised deployment will likely examine these internal assessments. Source: Google News

  6. Dry cathode battery process confirmed to cut cell costs substantially: May 28, 2026, EVTech.News

    7. Elon Musk stated that the manufacturing shift to dry cathode electrodes will meaningfully lower battery pack expenses. The technique eliminates solvent recovery steps and reduces energy use during production. Tesla is scaling the process at both Giga Texas and Nevada facilities. Cost curves from this change will be tracked in upcoming earnings reports. Source: Google News

    8. Vehicle Engineering VP Lars Moravy noted that adding a third motor to the Model 3 would require significant chassis and thermal redesign work. The project remains secondary to Roadster development. Existing dual-motor hardware already delivers strong performance, so the tri-motor variant is not a near-term priority. Any launch would likely follow Roadster production ramp. Source: Google News

  8. Cybercab prototype observed operating in San Francisco: May 28, 2026, r/teslamotors

    9. A vehicle matching the Cybercab design was spotted on public roads in the city.   9. Tesla patents lens-cleaning system for cameras and lidar: May 27, 2026, Benzinga

    10. The filing describes an integrated mechanism to keep external sensors clear without manual intervention. Clean optics are critical for both Full Self-Driving and future robotaxi fleets operating in varied weather. The patent also covers potential use on Optimus vision systems. Implementation details and timeline remain undisclosed. Source: Google News

  10. Neuralink details meltable plastic sealing process for brain implant wires: May 28, 2026, TSLAming

    11. The company patented a PCTFE-based method that creates hermetic seals around thousands of ultra-thin polyimide ribbons. The technique avoids shattering traditional materials when drilling high-density feedthroughs. It also enables wireless charging and data transmission through the skull without external antennas. The process is already in use for the roughly 20 participants in current human trials. Source: x.com

  11. Cybertruck owners gather in West Hollywood for first owner meetup: May 28, 2026, autoevolution

    12. Dozens of Cybertruck drivers convened to compare vehicles and discuss real-world ownership experiences. The event highlighted early build-quality observations and accessory modifications. No Tesla executives attended, keeping the focus on customer feedback. Similar gatherings may become more common as delivery numbers grow in California. Source: Google News

  12. New Tesla Supercharger site planned for northeast Eugene: May 27, 2026, The Register-Guard

    13. Local planning documents show Tesla preparing to install chargers along a key corridor in the city. The addition will improve coverage between Portland and southern Oregon routes. Construction timing has not been announced. The site follows Tesla's ongoing expansion of the network in secondary markets. Source: Google News

Internal trainers have identified gaps between published safety numbers and the edge cases they observe during training sessions. These discrepancies affect how the system weights rare events in future software versions. The feedback directly shapes the path to unsupervised operation and will be scrutinized by regulators evaluating broader deployment. Tesla will need to close this internal confidence gap before scaling driverless miles. Source/Post: Google News

Tesla First Principles

Taking a step back from today's headlines, the detailed manufacturing process for Neuralink's brain implant wires challenges the assumption that high-channel-count neural interfaces must remain low-volume, hand-assembled devices.

The approach uses a fluoropolymer that melts and reflows under controlled heat and asymmetric pressure to create thousands of hermetic feedthroughs without fracturing the surrounding structure. This single step replaces multiple traditional materials and drilling operations that previously limited electrode density. The resulting assembly supports wireless power and data transfer while protecting the delicate polyimide ribbons and metallic traces inside.

If the same vertical-integration logic Tesla applies to vehicle and battery production extends to medical hardware, the bottleneck on scaling channel counts disappears. Production could shift from specialized clean-room craftsmanship toward automated lines capable of supporting tens of thousands of electrodes per implant. That shift would change both the economics of brain-computer interfaces and the timeline for broader clinical use.
