Top 12 News Items

  1. Tesla FSD Europe Fleet Surpasses 50 Million km

    2. Dutch owners logged over 10 million kilometers within a month of provisional approval in April. Lithuania, Estonia, Denmark, and Belgium added approvals through June, pushing the combined fleet past 50 million kilometers by July 14. The rapid jump from 20 million to 50 million kilometers in eight weeks shows owners averaging nearly 580,000 FSD kilometers daily across five countries. This dataset now covers narrow roads and heavy cyclist traffic that regulators in larger markets want to see before granting broader access. The supervised system remains Level 2, yet the volume of edge-case miles accumulated in under eight weeks supplies the exact evidence needed for Italy, Germany, and France to evaluate next-stage approvals.   2. ATC Logistics Deploys 20 Tesla Semis in LA Ports

    3. ATC Logistics will run 20 Tesla Semis at the LA and Long Beach ports starting early 2027. The trucks will charge at Forum Mobility’s new FM Santa Fe depot in Rancho Dominguez. The Semi’s 500-mile range and Megawatt Charging System are the key reasons cited for the choice over diesel. Three other fleets have already signed similar deals with Forum Mobility for the same routes. The program tests whether the long-range configuration can maintain high reliability on short, high-volume drayage cycles while cutting emissions enough to meet port zero-emission mandates. Source: x.com

  3. 7-Eleven Japan is adding Tesla Superchargers to its stores

    4. 7-Eleven Japan will install Superchargers at select locations across the country. The move expands Tesla’s charging network into high-traffic retail sites that already serve millions of daily customers. No timeline or number of sites was disclosed in the announcement. The partnership places chargers where convenience-store foot traffic is highest, potentially increasing utilization during evening and overnight hours when other sites see lower demand. Source: Google News

  4. Tesla makes the cut on California’s newest EV Rebate program

    5. California signed a $270 million EV rebate program into law that begins this summer. Tesla vehicles qualify under the new rules for first-time buyers. The program targets price reductions for qualifying electric vehicles sold in the state. Tesla First Principles

Battery supply chains have long been viewed as a single bottleneck that any manufacturer must either own or endure. Tesla’s decision to have Samsung SDI build a dedicated US line for energy storage cells shows the company treating stationary batteries as a separate production track rather than a side stream of vehicle cells. This separation lets each product chase its own cost curve and chemistry without forcing trade-offs in energy density or cycle life that vehicle packs demand.

The practical result is faster scaling of Megapack output while vehicle programs continue to absorb 4680 and LFP cells at their own pace. It also reduces the risk that a sudden surge in car demand crowds out grid projects that now contribute meaningful revenue and margin. Investors watching only vehicle margins miss how this dual-track approach changes the capital allocation picture.

Over time the split could matter more than vertical integration itself. A dedicated storage line lets Tesla negotiate raw-material contracts sized to predictable utility demand instead of the lumpier vehicle schedule. That predictability feeds back into lower financing costs for the factories that build the factories.
