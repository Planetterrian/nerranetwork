Top 12 News Items

  1. EU meeting on Tesla Assisted Driving moved to December 15

    The European Commission's calendar now lists the next TCMV meeting on Tesla Assisted Driving for December 15. The date remains marked draft and follows the group's pattern of Tuesday meetings. Tesla needs 15 of 27 EU countries representing at least 65 percent of the population to secure approval. Twelve countries already allow the system, with Italy the latest addition. Germany backed approval after the rename to Assisted Driving and the 10 percent speed-offset cap. If the December 15 vote passes, Finland's regulator estimates EU-wide effect in about three weeks. The agenda will settle whether the session brings a real vote or another round of talks. The October agenda went public 11 days before that meeting, so this one could appear in early December. If all 13 current supporters vote yes, Tesla sits at about 47 percent of the population. That leaves two more countries as the bare minimum, and they have to bring about 18 more points of population with them. France alone cannot get there at about 15 percent, but Spain and Poland together can at about 19 percent. Source: x.com

  2. GM Korea signs NACS deal with Tesla for future EVs

    GM Korea announced an agreement to equip its future electric vehicles sold in South Korea with North American Charging Standard ports. The deal extends the 2023 North American charging partnership. Owners of current CCS-equipped GM vehicles can use adapters for Tesla Superchargers. Tesla Korea operates more than 1,200 Supercharger stalls across 180 sites. Stellantis previously signed for Tesla charging access in Korea starting in 2027. GM is stepping away from Korea's own CCS1 standard for its future cars. The country made CCS1 its fast-charging standard in 2017, and GM's Korean EVs have used it until now. Local charging companies have been adding Tesla connectors to fast chargers at highway rest areas. Other brands have been able to use part of the network since November 2023 through an adapter that stays on the Supercharger handle. GM has not named the models, the launch date, or the price per charge yet. That puts Hyundai and Kia in an awkward spot at home because they already build Tesla-port EVs for American buyers but their cars in Korea still use CCS1. Source: x.com

  3. BMW Highway Assistant gains red-light stop-and-go

    An over-the-air update will let BMW's Highway & Commuting Assistant stop at red lights and drive off automatically when the light changes. The feature brings the system closer to Tesla's Full Self-Driving capability. Source: insideevs.com

  4. Eight automakers form IONNA to build rival charging network

    Eight automakers are developing the IONNA network to challenge Tesla's Supercharger dominance in the United States. Source: en.koreadaily.com

  5. SpaceX walks away from $738 million in federal broadband subsidies

    SpaceX declined the grants yet will still serve every location it won under the BEAD program at no cost to the program. The company cited differing contract terms and state reporting demands across all 50 states. New subscribers at covered locations receive the dish and router free of charge. SpaceX must still meet BEAD’s minimum speeds of 100 Mbps down and 20 Mbps up, pass the program’s performance testing, and file a report every six months. The deal drops BEAD’s financial audit, procurement, and reporting provisions. States also cannot levy penalties if SpaceX falls short, and their only remedy is to return those locations to the program for a new award. Holding its BEAD locations with a $0 award keeps other federal broadband money from flowing to competitors in those rural areas. Source: teslarati.com

  6. Analyst projects $4 billion annual Robotaxi revenue for Tesla

    Seaport Global initiated coverage with a price target implying 25 percent upside, citing an expected $4 billion in Robotaxi revenue. Source: benzinga.com

  7. Shanghai factory exports now exceed domestic sales

    Exports have overtaken domestic sales at Tesla's Shanghai plant, prompting a production pivot. Source: autonews.gasgoo.com

  8. Watters Garden Center adds Tesla solar and battery storage

    The Arizona garden center is installing solar panels paired with Tesla battery storage to reduce grid reliance. Source: prescottenews.com

  9. Cyberbeast price rises again in the United States

    Tesla increased the price of the Cyberbeast model for a second time. Source: driveteslacanada.ca

  10. New York bans subscriptions for certain built-in car features

    The new law prohibits recurring fees for hardware already installed at purchase but explicitly exempts software-dependent driver assistance systems. Tesla's FSD subscription model therefore appears unaffected. The ban takes effect February 21, 2027. Manufacturers and dealers face civil penalties of up to US$250 per point of sale for each violation. The legislation targets subscriptions for features that meet two conditions: their hardware must already be installed when the vehicle is purchased or leased, and they must work after activation without ongoing expense or support from the manufacturer, dealer, or another service provider. Heated seats are specifically named as an example. Navigation updates, infotainment, satellite radio, in-vehicle Wi-Fi, roadside assistance, and connected services relying on data networks are among the exclusions. The key exclusion is for software-dependent driver assistance or driver automation features. Tesla ended one-time FSD purchases in the U.S. earlier this year and now offers it only as a subscription currently priced at $99 per month with active driver supervision still required. Tesla announced in its Q3 earnings report last month that FSD has hit a record 1.74 million subscribers globally. The Cybercab Autonomy subscription costs $299 per month and includes self-driving software, connectivity, remote support, and services such as tire and windshield replacements. Source: notateslaapp.com

  11. Tesla Model 3 and Model Y battery replacements cost about $12,000

    Battery replacements for Model 3 and Model Y remain rare and cost roughly half what many owners expect. The figure comes from recent service data reviewed by The Cool Down. Source: thecooldown.com

  12. Tesla FSD Version 15 shows measurable improvement

    Barron's notes that the latest FSD release delivers clearer gains in handling and visualization. Those updates support continued subscriber growth and help underpin the stock's valuation case. Source: barrons.com

  2. FSD Supervised highlighted for life-changing potential - @Tesla

  3. Powershare Home Backup tested in Texas - Tesla Energy

Short Spot

Calls for change after woman seriously hurt from test-driving Tesla: WTOP News

A woman sustained serious injuries during a Tesla test drive in Arlington, Virginia. Local officials are now calling for regulatory or procedural changes to improve test-drive safety. The incident has prompted questions about oversight of customer demonstrations at dealerships. Dealers typically allow prospective buyers to operate vehicles on public roads without additional supervision beyond the sales associate in the passenger seat. Virginia state law currently treats these drives the same as any other test drive by a licensed driver. The call for change comes after the driver lost control and struck a fixed object. No details on speed or road conditions have been released by police. Source: wtop.com

Tesla First Principles

Charging standards have long been treated as national infrastructure decisions rather than competitive tools. When GM Korea committed to NACS ports on future models, it abandoned Korea's CCS1 mandate from 2017 and gained direct access to more than 1,200 Tesla Supercharger stalls across 180 sites. That single move shifts the economics for every other Korean automaker still locked into the older standard. Local operators in Korea have already begun adding Tesla connectors to highway fast chargers, and Stellantis secured adapter access starting in 2027. Once two major rivals commit capital to the Tesla network, the remaining holdouts face rising per-station costs and shrinking negotiating leverage. The network effect compounds because every new NACS vehicle increases utilization without Tesla bearing the vehicle-side cost.

The same pattern is repeating elsewhere. For Tesla the outcome is straightforward margin expansion. More vehicles using the same stalls raise revenue per site while the marginal cost of electricity and maintenance stays largely fixed. The company avoids the capital outlay that would be required to match a fragmented CCS1 network at scale. Over time the standard itself becomes the moat, not the hardware.

Hyundai and Kia already produce vehicles with NACS ports for the American market, yet their domestic Korean lineup remains on CCS1. That split creates a visible cost disadvantage at home once GM and Stellantis vehicles begin drawing from the same high-utilization sites. The data shows Tesla Korea's network was selected to add stalls at rest areas this year, further widening the gap in coverage density. Every additional NACS vehicle sold in Korea therefore tightens the loop: higher throughput per stall, lower average cost per kWh delivered, and stronger incentive for the next manufacturer to switch rather than duplicate infrastructure.
