Top 12 News Items

  1. Tesla Opens Cybercab Rides in Dallas, Its Second City - The Defiant

    Tesla opened public Cybercab rides in Dallas after the initial Austin launch. The service marks the second U.S. market where riders can book rides in the purpose-built robotaxi. The rollout gives regulators and passengers direct experience with the vehicle in mixed urban traffic. Dallas joins Austin as the only cities where the Cybercab operates for the public so far. Source: thedefiant.io

  2. Tesla signals expanded in-vehicle Starlink by end-2027 - Digitaltoday

    Elon Musk stated that built-in Starlink connectivity should reach all Tesla models toward the end of next year. The feature will support unsupervised FSD with high-bandwidth links for work and entertainment. Starlink remains unavailable in China because it is illegal there. Musk replied directly to an X user asking about embedded Starlink beyond the Cybercab. He noted the connection will matter for passengers needing reliable bandwidth on remote roads. All Cybercabs now operating in Dallas already carry the roof-integrated Starlink antenna. Tesla’s AI lead Ashok Elluswamy confirmed the antenna currently handles only fleet management, navigation, and customer service rather than passenger streaming. A patent for the built-in antenna was filed earlier. The design avoids aftermarket mounts and reduces weather-related service needs over the vehicle lifetime. Source: digitaltoday.co.kr

  3. Tesla Optimus Robot Launch Timeline Targets 2027 Scale - TechRepublic

    Tesla set a 2027 target for scaling Optimus robot production and deployment. The timeline covers both internal factory use and external sales. The company is actively recruiting engineers focused on motors, electronics, and related hardware. No weekly production volume target was released with the announcement. Source: techrepublic.com

  4. Tesla owner notes major Auto Wipers improvement on v15 - @Teslarati

    An owner reported that Auto Wipers on v15 no longer jump straight to maximum speed and stay there. The system now adjusts more naturally to changing rain intensity. The owner described the previous behavior as locking at the fastest setting regardless of conditions. The change appeared during the first real-world test of the updated software. Source: x.com

  5. FSD v15 shows improved parking spot orientation selection - @Teslarati

    Owners testing FSD v15 noted the system selects the correct parking orientation more reliably than v14. The car still does not let drivers choose pull-in versus back-in. One tester said the v14 version reached roughly 90 percent accuracy on orientation choice. No issues with the new version have been reported so far. Source: x.com

  6. Report Issues at Tesla Chargers via App - @TeslaCharging

    Tesla added new categories to the in-app “Report an Issue” tool for Superchargers, Megachargers, and Destination Chargers. Reports now route directly to the 24/7 operations team. The update aims to speed up fixes by giving the team more precise information about each problem. Owners can now flag a wider range of issues without leaving the vehicle. Source: x.com

  7. Frustrated owner wants to expand 16-panel solar array, but Tesla says no - The Cool Down

    A Tesla solar customer was told the company would not support adding panels to an existing 16-panel array. The owner wanted to increase output but received no path forward from Tesla. The denial leaves the customer without an official upgrade route even though roof space remains available. Tesla’s current process treats each installation as a standalone project rather than an expandable system. Source: thecooldown.com

  8. Musk Says Cybercab Would Beat Rival Robotaxis at the Same Fare - evxl.co

    Elon Musk stated the Cybercab can deliver lower operating costs than competing robotaxis while charging the same fare. The claim centers on vehicle design and fleet economics. No specific cost-per-mile figure was released with the statement. The comparison assumes rivals maintain similar pricing structures. Source: evxl.co

  9. Tesla wants to remove the steering wheel from its best-selling car - USA Today

    Tesla is developing a steering-wheel-free version of the Model Y for robotaxi use. The vehicle would seat up to seven and operate without manual controls. The change targets fleet operators who want maximum passenger capacity and no driver intervention. Production timing for the modified Model Y remains tied to broader robotaxi rollout plans. Source: usatoday.com

  10. Tesla Energy's Storm Watch mode charges Powerwall to 100% ahead of extreme weather - Tesla Energy

    Storm Watch mode automatically charges Powerwall units to full capacity before forecasted severe weather. The feature aims to keep homes powered during outages. The mode activates based on weather alerts and runs without manual input from the owner. It applies to existing Powerwall installations that receive the latest software update. Source: x.com

  11. Cancelling Tesla rebates was budget decision: Manitoba’s top bureaucrat - Winnipeg Free Press

    Manitoba’s top civil servant confirmed the province ended Tesla purchase rebates as a budget measure. The change affects new electric-vehicle incentives in the province. No replacement program was announced at the time of the statement. The decision applies across all EV brands, not only Tesla. Source: winnipegfreepress.com

Tesla First Principles

Tesla’s decision to embed Starlink antennas directly into the Cybercab roof points to a deeper shift in how connectivity is treated inside the vehicle. Instead of relying on external cellular networks that vary by location and carrier, the car carries its own high-bandwidth link from the start. That single hardware choice changes the assumptions behind unsupervised FSD, because the system no longer needs to operate in complete radio silence on remote roads. The antenna sits flush in the roof panel, visible as a square cutout on every Dallas Cybercab already in service. Musk’s reply placed volume availability for the rest of the Tesla lineup at the end of 2027, giving engineering teams roughly twelve months to finalize integration across Model 3, Model Y, and the Cybertruck.

The timeline Musk gave places volume availability at the end of 2027 across the wider Tesla lineup. By then the same roof-integrated panel will need to handle fleet-management data, navigation updates, passenger entertainment, and the high-resolution video streams required for remote oversight. Each of those streams competes for the same antenna surface and power budget, so the design must prioritize without creating single points of failure. China remains the explicit exception because Starlink service is blocked there, forcing a separate connectivity strategy for that market. Ashok Elluswamy noted the current Cybercab implementation uses the link only for operations and customer service, leaving passenger features for a later software step. The patent filing already covers the physical mounting, which removes the need for aftermarket brackets and reduces service calls tied to weather exposure.

From a cost standpoint, moving the antenna inside the body removes the need for aftermarket mounts and reduces weather-related service calls. It also locks buyers into Tesla’s ecosystem for the life of the vehicle, since the hardware is not designed for easy removal or third-party substitution. The business result is recurring connectivity revenue tied to the car itself rather than to a separate subscription the owner could cancel. Over a fleet of hundreds of thousands of vehicles, that shift turns a once-optional accessory into a structural margin driver. The same roof real estate that once held only a glass panel now becomes a permanent data pipe, changing both the bill of materials and the long-term service relationship.
