Top 12 News Items

  1. Tesla FSD Probe Escalates to Engineering Analysis Over 3.2M Vehicles, TSLAming

    2. U.S. safety regulators escalated their investigation into Tesla’s FSD to an Engineering Analysis, the final phase before a potential recall. The probe covers 3.2 million vehicles after nine low-visibility crashes in fog, dust, and glare that caused one fatality and one injury. Regulators set an August 12 deadline for Tesla to provide the internal document "Radar Saves Us," related emails, and raw test data on the OEDR system, with daily fines up to $27,874 possible for non-compliance. Tesla can resolve the matter via OTA update if it proves the software detects blinded cameras and alerts drivers or disengages safely. Investigators are also reviewing fifteen promotional claims and a 2024 change that weakened the "FSD Degraded" alert from red to yellow with the chime muted. This development continues the tracked FSD Unsupervised program arc last reviewed on July 21, where regulatory approval remains the key open question. Source: x.com

  2. Tesla Building Consumer Infrastructure for Optimus in Homes via App Code, TSLAming

    3. Tesla app code shows consent screens for collecting video, 3D spatial data, and tactile feedback from Optimus units operating in customer homes. The setup includes a Bluetooth robot key with device whitelist and dual-layer alerts that filter developer logs into simple customer notifications like low battery. This approach mirrors the FSD shadow fleet strategy to train the robot on real household variables such as carpet friction and fragile objects instead of relying solely on $48-per-hour motion-capture trainers in labs. It advances the Optimus program, last reviewed July 21, by moving from lab training toward early home deployments. Source: x.com

  3. San Antonio Zoning Approves Site for Tesla Robotaxi Fleet Depot, TSLAming

    4. San Antonio's Zoning Commission voted 8-2 on July 21 to recommend rezoning at 245 and 257 W. Josephine St. for a dedicated Robotaxi depot with 56 charging stalls. The site will serve only the autonomous fleet and not public Superchargers. With service already running in Austin, Dallas, and Houston, this prepares expansion in a neighboring market 80 miles from Austin. This fits the Cybercab / Robotaxi tracked program, where production start and regulatory approval remain open questions. Source: x.com

  4. Tesla App Makes Changing Vehicle Wrap Super Easy, @Teslarati

    5. The Tesla app now supports direct selection and application of custom vehicle wraps without dealer visits. Owners can preview and switch designs through the mobile interface. This feature extends the app's role as a unified control point across Tesla hardware. Source: x.com

  5. Tesla's Street Parking Prevents Dooring Incidents, @Teslarati

    6. Street Parking mode positions the vehicle to reduce the risk of dooring by cyclists and other traffic. The system uses sensors to guide alignment along the curb. It adds a practical safety layer to daily driving in urban areas. Source: x.com

  6. New Model 3 and Y Animations in 2026 Summer Update, @Teslarati

    7. The 2026 Summer Update introduces new animations for Model 3 and Model Y interfaces. These visual changes improve driver feedback during vehicle interactions. The update continues the pattern of software refinements to existing vehicle lines. Source: x.com

Tesla First Principles

Camera-only autonomy places the entire burden of hazard detection on software that must interpret photon counts under every lighting and weather condition. When regulators demand proof that the system recognizes its own blindness, the question shifts from sensor count to algorithmic self-awareness. Tesla's approach bets that neural networks trained on fleet data can outperform fixed hardware rules, yet the August 12 filing will test whether that bet has produced measurable safeguards.

The internal document titled "Radar Saves Us" now sits at the center of the inquiry because it may reveal whether engineers once quantified the exact failure modes that later appeared in the nine reported crashes. If the code can flag degraded visibility and either alert the driver or disengage within milliseconds, an over-the-air fix remains possible. Should the data fall short, the company faces a choice between reintroducing radar or accepting limits on where FSD can operate.

That outcome carries direct cost implications. A software resolution preserves the capital already spent on vision training and avoids retrofitting millions of vehicles. A hardware reversal would reset part of the 2021 decision and add expense at a moment when cash flow is already under watch from investors. The next data point is whether the August submission closes the case or extends it.
