Tesla First Principles

The human brain processes language and sensory input at roughly one hundred bits per second while modern AI systems move data at trillions of bits per second. That gap is not a temporary inconvenience. It is a structural mismatch that grows with every improvement in model scale and inference speed.

Neuralink's approach treats the disparity as an engineering problem rather than a philosophical one. By increasing the bandwidth of the neural interface, the system aims to let humans exchange information at rates closer to those of the machines they interact with. Early targets focus on restoring function for people with neurological conditions, yet the same hardware path could eventually support higher-throughput communication for healthy users.

If the bandwidth increase materializes, the practical effect would be faster iteration between human intent and machine output. Training loops for both Full Self-Driving and Optimus would shorten because human feedback could arrive at machine-native speeds. The cost curve for that feedback would drop from hours of annotation time to seconds of direct neural input, altering the economics of scaling both autonomy and robotics programs.
