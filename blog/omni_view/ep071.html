Top stories (5)

1) Israel and Lebanon agree new ceasefire as Trump scrambles to end Iran war

What happened (neutral): Israel and Lebanon have agreed to implement a ceasefire to end hostilities, with the deal contingent on Hezbollah ceasing fire and evacuating fighters south of the Litani River. Despite the agreement, Israel carried out multiple drone strikes in southern Lebanon. The Trump administration announced the deal after negotiations in Washington involving the US, Lebanon, and Israel.

Perspectives: Outlets including The Guardian frame the agreement as a fragile step that could remove one barrier to wider talks involving Iran, while noting Israel’s stated intent to maintain a presence in southern Lebanon. Lebanese President Joseph Aoun described the deal as a “last opportunity” after difficult negotiations that required US intervention. Both sides agree the ceasefire depends on Hezbollah’s compliance; they differ on whether Israeli operations can continue without violating the terms.

STEEL-MAN EACH SIDE: The strongest case for viewing the ceasefire as progress rests on the explicit conditions tying withdrawal to Hezbollah’s relocation, which could reduce cross-border attacks if enforced. The strongest case for skepticism rests on Israel’s immediate post-agreement strikes and its defence minister’s statement that forces will not withdraw or allow population returns. Advocates on each side concede that enforcement mechanisms remain untested.

Questions to consider:

  • What enforcement steps are outlined for monitoring Hezbollah’s withdrawal?
  • How do the terms compare to previous Israel-Lebanon understandings?
  • Which regional actors gain or lose leverage if the ceasefire holds?

Understanding the Issue: How political donations are disclosed and regulated in the UK

Most coverage of large political donations assumes the public instantly knows who is funding which party and why. In the UK, parties must report donations above a certain threshold to the Electoral Commission within set timeframes, after which the information becomes public. The system relies on self-reporting by parties rather than real-time independent verification, and cryptocurrency donations are treated under the same rules as other contributions once converted to pounds. Knowing this, when you hear claims about “record fundraising,” you can check whether the totals reflect many small donors or a few large ones by reviewing the commission’s published registers. Next time you see a headline about party funding, ask: what is the disclosure threshold, when will the next report appear, and does the source separate one-off gifts from recurring support?

Media-literacy note

Cross-reference donation figures with the Electoral Commission’s primary database rather than secondary summaries. Compare coverage across outlets with different editorial priorities to see which details each emphasizes. Primary documents such as official statements and regulatory filings remain the clearest starting point for verifying claims.
