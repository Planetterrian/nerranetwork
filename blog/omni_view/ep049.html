What happened (neutral): UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer is scheduled to hold talks with Health Secretary Wes Streeting as he battles to remain in office following ministerial resignations. Starmer has stated he intends to continue leading the government despite internal party challenges. The meeting comes before the King's Speech outlining new legislation.

Perspectives: Al Jazeera frames the encounter as a critical test of whether Starmer can retain control within his party while managing resignations and public pledges to govern. Some coverage highlights Streeting's potential role as a stabilising figure or alternative leader, reflecting concerns over party unity and electoral prospects. Others emphasise Starmer's determination to push forward with the legislative agenda, viewing the talks as an internal management step rather than an immediate handover. These views differ on whether the priority is short-term survival or longer-term party renewal.

Understanding the Issue: How Family Courts Assess Unregulated Expert Testimony

Most coverage of family-court decisions assumes that expert psychological reports carry formal professional oversight, yet many experts providing testimony operate without statutory regulation. In practice, courts rely on a mix of registered psychologists, counsellors and other practitioners whose qualifications are evaluated case-by-case rather than through a single licensing body. This structure developed historically to ensure timely access to specialist insight in urgent custody matters, but it leaves room for varying standards of training and methodology. When judges weigh these reports, they must balance the expert’s stated experience against the absence of mandatory peer review or continuing professional development requirements. Knowing this, readers can better evaluate claims that a particular outcome resulted from “expert advice” by asking whether the individual held recognised credentials or simply presented relevant experience. Next time you see reporting on a custody dispute involving psychological evidence, ask: what formal qualifications did the expert hold, was their methodology open to challenge, and what alternative sources of information were available to the court?

Media-literacy note

Cross-check court documents and official regulatory registers against news summaries, especially when stories involve expert testimony or institutional processes. Primary sources such as inquest transcripts or fund announcements often clarify details that secondary reporting condenses. Comparing coverage across outlets with different editorial priorities helps identify which facts are consistently reported and which remain open to interpretation.
