1) US and Iran exchange fresh strikes after helicopter downing

What happened (neutral): The United States carried out strikes on Iranian targets following the downing of a US Army helicopter. Iran responded with attacks on sites in Jordan, Kuwait, and Bahrain. Oil prices rose on concerns that renewed fighting could affect the Strait of Hormuz.

Perspectives: Outlets including The Guardian describe the back-and-forth as a rapid slide into escalation that both sides have previously managed to contain. CNBC reporting focuses on immediate market reactions and shipping risks. Both sides agree the initial trigger was the helicopter incident; they differ on whether the US response was proportionate or whether Iran’s retaliation crossed a new threshold.

STEEL-MAN EACH SIDE: The strongest case for the US position rests on the need to deter attacks on its forces and maintain credible deterrence after a direct loss of equipment and personnel risk. The strongest case for Iran’s position rests on the principle that any foreign military action on or near its territory requires a visible response to preserve domestic credibility and regional influence. Each side’s advocates note that further rounds risk drawing in additional actors and raising costs for their own populations.

Understanding the Issue: How US primary elections select party nominees

Most coverage of primary results treats them as simple popularity contests, yet the actual mechanism involves state-by-state rules that allocate delegates rather than a single national vote. Each state sets its own date, voting method, and delegate-allocation formula, which can be winner-take-all or proportional; candidates accumulate delegates across these separate contests until one reaches the required majority at the party convention. This structure means early states exert disproportionate influence because momentum and media attention compound after the first few results. Knowing this, when you hear claims that a single primary “decides” a nomination, you can check whether the delegate math actually supports that conclusion or whether later contests remain decisive. Next time you see a headline about primary night, ask: which state’s rules determined the delegate split, how many delegates are still unallocated, and what historical precedent exists for comebacks after early losses.

Media-literacy note

Compare the same event across at least two outlets with different ownership structures. Check whether primary documents such as court filings or company statements are linked or quoted directly. Note which facts are agreed upon and which remain attributed to one side only.
