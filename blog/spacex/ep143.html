Raptor 4 targets 300 tons of sea-level thrust at Raptor 3 mass, pushing Super Heavy toward 9,900 tons total.

By Patrick October 27, 2026 Ep 143 7 min read Listen to podcast

Top News

  1. Ship 42 completes single-engine static fire at Massey's ahead of Flight 15

    SpaceX conducted the test on the Starship upper stage at the Massey's test site. The firing supports preparations for the next integrated flight test. No issues were reported during the test sequence. The single-engine run follows earlier multi-engine work on the booster side of the stack and keeps Ship 42 on schedule for its role in Flight 15. Video of the test was released the same day. Source: youtube.com

  2. Raptor 4 aims for 300 tons sea-level thrust at unchanged engine mass

    Elon Musk stated Raptor 4 will deliver 300 metric tons of thrust at roughly the same mass as Raptor 3. Earlier versions progressed from 185 tons at 2,080 kg on Raptor 1 to 230 tons at 1,630 kg on Raptor 2. Raptor 3 reached operational ratings near 250 tons at 1,525 kg. The higher thrust-to-weight ratio reduces gravity losses during the initial ascent phase when the vehicle is heaviest. With 33 engines on Super Heavy the increment adds roughly 1,650 tons of liftoff thrust, bringing total booster output near 9,900 metric tons. The design keeps the same propellant load and tank structure so the performance gain flows directly into payload or acceleration. Source: teslarati.com

  3. Telesat Lightspeed constellation requires 14 Falcon 9 launches in 2026

    The Canadian operator's 225-satellite broadband network depends on SpaceX vehicles for deployment. Launches are scheduled through the end of the year from Vandenberg and Cape Canaveral. The contract underscores continued reliance on Falcon 9 even as Telesat works on its own next-generation system. Each mission will carry multiple satellites to low Earth orbit on the standard 70-degree inclination profile used for many commercial payloads. Source: tech-insider.org

  4. U.S. Air Force completes first space-delivery demonstration using SpaceX capsule

    The test showed cargo could reach a battlefield within one hour via orbital trajectory. The demonstration used a Dragon-derived vehicle and validated rapid reentry and recovery timelines. The Air Force Research Laboratory conducted the flight to prove the concept of hypersonic delivery from orbit. Recovery occurred at a designated site after the capsule completed its deorbit burn and atmospheric entry. Source: finance.biggo.com

  5. Cursor releases Agent SDK for building custom coding agents

    Developers can now define agents in TypeScript with Markdown prompts and deploy them on hosted or self-managed infrastructure. Built-in evaluation tools allow measurement of performance changes after each modification. The SDK turns the existing coding agent into a programmable interface that any developer can extend. Agents run on Cursor's infrastructure or on user hardware with the same evaluation harness. Source: startupfortune.com

  6. PeakDo introduces LinkPower Ultra battery for Starlink Mini with active cooling

    The new unit adds a built-in fan to maintain cell temperatures during high-draw operation. It targets portable and off-grid users who need extended runtime without external cooling. The battery is sized for the Starlink Mini terminal and includes thermal management that was absent in earlier portable packs. PeakDo positions the product for users in remote or mobile environments where passive cooling is insufficient. Source: notebookcheck.net

  7. EU outlines five-pronged plan to reduce reliance on Starlink

    The strategy includes support for rival satellite constellations and new regulatory requirements. Officials aim to diversify European connectivity options for government and commercial users. The plan also addresses spectrum allocation and data-routing rules that could affect non-European operators. European Commission documents describe the measures as a response to single-vendor concentration in the low-Earth-orbit broadband market. Source: english.nv.ua

  8. USSF-432 mission now targeted for November 1 launch from Vandenberg

    The classified payload will fly on a Falcon 9 from Space Launch Complex 4E. Booster B1093 is assigned to the flight. The mission marks another national-security launch from the California site in a year that has already seen record cadence. The booster has previously flown 17 missions, primarily supporting Starlink and commercial rideshares. Source: tradingview.com

Engineering Deep Dive

Higher sea-level thrust at constant engine mass directly improves the initial thrust-to-weight ratio of the full Starship stack. When the vehicle is still deep in the gravity well and dense atmosphere, every extra ton of thrust shortens the time spent fighting gravity losses that cannot be recovered later in the trajectory. Raptor 3 already reached roughly 250 tons at 1,525 kg; moving to 300 tons on Raptor 4 without adding mass raises the thrust-to-weight ratio by about 20 percent on each engine. With 33 engines on Super Heavy, that increment adds roughly 1,650 tons of additional liftoff thrust, pushing the booster’s total near 9,900 tons. The design choice also preserves the same propellant load and tank structure, so the performance gain flows straight into higher payload or faster acceleration off the pad. Lower gravity losses mean more of the propellant’s chemical energy reaches orbital velocity instead of being spent simply climbing out of Earth’s gravity well. The same mass budget that once supported 185 tons of thrust on Raptor 1 now supports 300 tons, illustrating how iterative materials and turbopump refinements compound across versions.

The improvement compounds because the engine mass stays fixed while output rises. Earlier Raptor versions traded mass reductions for thrust gains, but Raptor 4 holds the mass line established by Raptor 3 and simply extracts more work from the same turbomachinery and chamber. That decision avoids the need to redesign tank walls, structural interfaces, or the vehicle’s center of mass. The net result is a higher acceleration profile in the first 60 to 90 seconds of flight, when aerodynamic drag and gravity losses are highest. Because the engines are already qualified for the higher chamber pressures required, the upgrade path stays within existing manufacturing and test infrastructure at Starbase. The cumulative effect across 33 engines is the equivalent of adding several additional Raptor 3-class engines without any increase in vehicle dry mass or propellant volume.

Over multiple flights the thrust margin also provides operational flexibility. A higher thrust-to-weight ratio at liftoff allows the stack to carry additional propellant for longer burns or to accept a heavier payload while still meeting the same orbital insertion targets. It also shortens the time the vehicle spends in the high-dynamic-pressure region, reducing structural loads on the booster and the tower catch hardware. SpaceX has historically used such margins to increase flight rate rather than payload, and the Raptor 4 numbers suggest the same strategy will continue. The progression from 185 tons to 300 tons across four engine generations shows that the dominant constraint has shifted from raw chamber pressure to integrated vehicle-level performance, where every kilogram saved or every newton gained multiplies across the entire stack.
