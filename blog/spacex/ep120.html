Top News

  1. SpaceX shares first picture of Starlink V3 satellite deployment by Starship on debut orbital flight

    SpaceX released the first onboard image of the deployment sequence from one of the satellites. Twenty-six V3 satellites reached orbit twenty-five minutes after liftoff from Starbase. The satellites are expected to add twenty-six Tbps total capacity, roughly ten times the output of one Falcon 9 V2 mini launch. Ship 41 lost one Raptor vacuum engine early in ascent and extended the burn on the remaining five engines to reach a safe suborbital path. The booster relit thirty-one engines for boostback and eleven for the landing burn before splashdown in the Gulf. All twenty-six satellites established contact through laser links, ground stations, and user terminals. Source: starlust.org

  2. Starlink Beamed Live Views From Orbit During Starship Flight 14

    Starlink's network supplied live orbital imagery throughout the flight. Three V3 satellites carried dedicated cameras to image the upper-stage heat shield after deployment. Each V3 satellite masses approximately two thousand kilograms. The design targets one Tbps downlink and one hundred sixty Gbps uplink per satellite. Six four-hundred-Gbps inter-satellite lasers form the mesh routing backbone. Source: basenor.com

  3. Memphis Council Faces Final Vote on Data Center Pause as Rally Targets Musk

    The Memphis City Council will hold its final vote on a data-center development moratorium on October sixth. The pause would allow time to study water and energy impacts before approving additional hyperscale facilities. xAI's Colossus campus has drawn an average of eight hundred twelve thousand five hundred two gallons of water daily from the Memphis Sand Aquifer. A rally organized by Indivisible Memphis is scheduled for three p.m. ahead of the meeting. Source: hoodline.com

  4. Starlink launches Communities Program for passive income through internet sharing

    The beta program lets property owners install one Starlink kit and sell hour, day, week, or month passes to nearby users. Hosts handle placement, power, and basic upkeep while Starlink manages accounts, billing, and the satellite link. Passes cover one device for shorter periods and up to four devices for monthly access. Applications are open in multiple countries. Source: teslarati.com

  5. SpaceX to launch recon satellites from Vandenberg. Where to watch launch

    A Falcon 9 is scheduled to lift off from Vandenberg Space Force Base carrying reconnaissance satellites. Viewing sites include local beaches and designated public areas along the central coast. The launch window opens later today. Source: vcstar.com

  6. Amazon spends $1 billion to counter datacenter opposition as communities push back

    Amazon has allocated one billion dollars toward community outreach and mitigation efforts around proposed data-center sites. Local opposition groups continue to cite noise, water use, and emissions concerns in multiple states. Source: news.lavx.hu

  7. SpaceX Stock Crosses Key Level Amid Launch Milestones as Lockup Expirations Near

    SPCX closed at one hundred fifty-eight dollars and ninety-six cents after clearing the one hundred fifty-eight dollar resistance level. The stock has risen fifty-one point six percent from its yearly low. Two additional lock-up expirations are scheduled for October seventh and October twenty-sixth. Source: benzinga.com

The Counterpoint

One thing worth watching is the Memphis City Council vote on the data-center moratorium scheduled for October sixth. The measure would pause new approvals while the city studies aquifer drawdown and power demand. xAI's existing Colossus facility already consumes over eight hundred thousand gallons of water daily from the Memphis Sand Aquifer. Residents and environmental groups argue the pause is needed to set permanent zoning rules before further expansion. Source: hoodline.com

AI & Compute

Engineering Deep Dive

The engineering angle on the V3 satellites centers on trading mass for bandwidth density. Each satellite masses roughly two thousand kilograms, more than double earlier versions, yet delivers one Tbps downlink and one hundred sixty Gbps uplink. That tenfold capacity jump per satellite is achieved through six four-hundred-Gbps laser terminals and larger phased-array antennas. If raw silicon, solar cells, and propellant were the only costs, the marginal expense per satellite would drop sharply once Starship reaches routine flight rates. The current gap between that raw-material floor and the finished satellite price is the classic Idiot Index for space hardware; reuse of the Starship upper stage and on-orbit laser calibration are the two levers expected to close it. The three camera-equipped satellites already returning heat-shield imagery close an earlier data loop that previously required either ground tracking or post-flight recovery. With twenty-six Tbps added from a single launch, the constellation economics shift from adding many small satellites to fewer, higher-output nodes.
