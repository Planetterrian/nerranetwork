The Counterpoint

Dependence on a single launch provider drew fresh scrutiny after a Boeing-Lockheed joint venture rocket suffered a total mission failure. The loss highlights how U.S. military and national-security payloads now rely almost entirely on SpaceX vehicles for timely access to orbit. Any extended grounding of Falcon or Starship fleets would leave few near-term alternatives. Regulators and defense planners are now examining diversification timelines that previously assumed multiple operational providers. Source: visiontimes.com

Engineering Deep Dive

Stainless steel was chosen for Starship and Super Heavy after carbon-fiber prototypes proved too expensive and difficult to scale. At cryogenic temperatures the 300-series alloys gain significant strength while costing roughly $3 per kilogram versus nearly $200 per kilogram for carbon fiber once scrap is included. The material also tolerates brief excursions well above the 150 °C steady-state limit of aluminum or composites, simplifying thermal protection during reentry. Because the alloy is easy to weld and repair, teams can iterate tank sections and structural joints in days rather than weeks. That combination directly supports the rapid-build cadence needed for full reusability, where each recovered vehicle must be inspected and reflown with minimal downtime. The same properties that once made stainless steel seem old-fashioned now collapse both the raw-material Idiot Index and the labor hours required to turn sheet stock into flight-ready stages.

The switch also changes how heat-shield tiles attach and how tanks handle repeated pressure cycles. Carbon fiber required complex layup tooling and autoclave curing; stainless steel uses conventional rolling, cutting, and orbital welding equipment already common in pressure-vessel shops. When a tank section fails a proof test, crews can cut out the damaged area and reweld a replacement patch the same day. This repair loop shortens the time between a test anomaly and the next flight article by weeks. The lower raw cost further reduces the penalty for building multiple prototypes in parallel, allowing concurrent work on different structural variants without exhausting the materials budget.

Cryogenic strengthening adds another margin. At liquid-oxygen and liquid-methane temperatures the alloy’s yield strength rises, so wall thickness can be reduced while still meeting burst-pressure requirements. Less mass in the tank walls translates directly into more payload or more propellant for the same vehicle size. The design therefore carries less structural overhead into orbit, improving the mass fraction that matters most for reaching the performance targets set for lunar and Mars missions.

Нержавейка также переносит краткие скачки температуры заметно выше предела ста пятидесяти градусов Цельсия для установившегося режима у алюминия или композитов, такого тепла, которое уже создавало бы проблемы алюминиевому планеру в установившемся полёте, и это упрощает тепловую защиту при входе в атмосферу.

Углеволокно требовало сложной оснастки для выкладки и автоклавного отверждения, цикла в печи под давлением; нержавейка идёт обычной прокаткой, резкой и орбитальной сваркой, швом по окружности бака, уже привычной в цехах сосудов высокого давления.

Когда секция бака не проходит прочностное испытание, проверку давлением перед полётом, бригады могут вырезать повреждённый участок и вварить заплату в тот же день, и это сокращает паузу от аномалии на испытании до следующего лётного изделия на недели.

Дешёвый листовой прокат снижает штраф за сборку нескольких прототипов сразу, так что разные конструктивные варианты могут идти параллельно, не исчерпав бюджет на материалы, а эти циклы сварки и ремонта за дни, а не за недели — как раз то, что нужно возвращённой ступени, если её собираются осмотреть и снова отправить в полёт с малым простоем.

Starship's tanks and structure, from an engineering standpoint, are three-hundred-series stainless because carbon-fiber prototypes cost too much and would not scale. The alloy is about three dollars per kilogram, while carbon fiber is nearly two hundred dollars per kilogram once scrap is included, so two hundred divided by three puts the composite on the order of sixty-seven times the steel, the Idiot Index on raw material, how many times the finished part costs over what you started with. At liquid-oxygen and liquid-methane temperatures that alloy's yield strength, the stress where the metal starts to permanently bend, rises, so wall thickness can drop and still meet burst pressure, the load that would rupture the tank. Less mass in those walls is more payload or more propellant for the same vehicle, the mass fraction that lunar and Mars missions have to win. Stainless also tolerates brief temperature spikes well above the one hundred fifty degree Celsius steady-state limit of aluminum or composites, the kind of heat that would already trouble an aluminum airframe in steady flight, which simplifies thermal protection during reentry. Carbon fiber demanded complex layup tooling and autoclave curing, a pressurized oven cycle; stainless uses conventional rolling, cutting, and orbital welding, a weld run around the tank circumference, already common in pressure-vessel shops.
