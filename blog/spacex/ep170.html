Top News

  1. Dragon capsule completes CRS-35 mission with targeted research samples

    The unpiloted Dragon spacecraft splashed down in the Pacific Ocean off Southern California at 2:03 p.m. PST on November 21 after undocking from the ISS on November 20 at 11:05 a.m. CST while flying above western China. It completed a 29-hour return journey that included a deorbit burn, trunk jettison, nosecone closure, drogue and main parachute deployment, and a planned sonic boom during atmospheric entry. The capsule returned a 3D-bioprinted blood vessel construct for studies on vascular aging and cardiovascular disease, frozen tumor organoids treated on station that may improve cancer treatments and personalized medicine, frozen microbial samples to examine how antibiotic-resistant bacteria survive and evolve in space, and a lighting system tested for more than three years to support astronaut sleep and well-being on long-duration missions. On its outbound leg the spacecraft had delivered more than 6,300 pounds of supplies and scientific equipment plus the final pair of iROSA roll-out solar arrays that raised the station’s power generation capacity by roughly 30 percent from 160 kilowatts to around 215 kilowatts. Dragon remains the only current cargo vehicle capable of returning hardware and samples to Earth; other vehicles such as Northrop Grumman’s Cygnus and Japan’s HTV-X are designed to burn up on re-entry. Support personnel recovered the spacecraft loaded with station hardware and science experiments for analysis on the ground. Source: nasa.gov

  2. Falcon 9 lifts off on classified national security mission from California

    SpaceX launched a Falcon 9 carrying a classified payload for the U.S. Space Force from Vandenberg Space Force Base at 11:20 p.m. EST on November 20 after a one-day scrub. The booster, designated B1100 and flying its eleventh mission, landed on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You roughly eight and a half minutes after liftoff. The upper stage reached orbit and deployed an undisclosed number of satellites; public details will emerge only from satellite catalog entries rather than official announcements. The launch was the sixth in a series of similar “R” missions, each of which previously placed 23 objects in orbit according to independent catalogs. B1100 had previously flown two Space Force missions back to back 55 days apart with no intervening flights. The webcast ended after booster landing, consistent with standard practice for classified missions. Source: techtimes.com

  3. First chopstick-caught Starship upper stage slated for retirement

    SpaceX will retire the first Starship upper stage recovered by the tower catch arms rather than fly it again. The vehicle completed its flight test and recovery sequence last week at Starbase. Retirement allows detailed post-flight inspection of the heat shield, structure, and tanks before any decision on future reuse of caught stages. The decision follows the successful tower catch that Elon Musk described as the last major piece of the puzzle for orbital operations. Engineers will examine the recovered hardware to inform design iterations on subsequent vehicles. Source: space.com

AI & Compute

The move follows congressional committee scrutiny of those models in developer tools after the models powered portions of the editor’s earlier releases. Engineering Deep Dive

Stainless steel became the structural material for Starship and Super Heavy after carbon-fiber prototypes proved difficult to scale to nine-meter diameter. At that size the alloy forms and welds far more readily than composites, and its raw-material cost sits roughly fifteen to twenty times lower than equivalent titanium sheet. Cryogenic exposure to liquid oxygen and methane actually raises its yield strength by about fifty percent without the brittleness risk that titanium faces at those temperatures. During reentry the steel tolerates roughly eight hundred fifty degrees Celsius before significant strength loss, allowing thinner or fewer heat-shield tiles than a titanium primary structure would require. The same properties let engineers iterate prototypes in weeks rather than months, collapsing the gap between raw-material cost and finished vehicle price. That manufacturing speed and thermal margin together make rapid, repeated reuse of an entire one-hundred-twenty-meter stack feasible at the volumes Starship is designed to achieve. Historical parallels show similar material shifts, such as the move from exotic alloys to stainless in early Atlas tanks, where cost and fabricability drove the choice once performance margins proved adequate. The Idiot Index for a finished Starship stage therefore drops sharply because the dominant expense moves from exotic forming and joining to simple welding and heat treatment that can be performed at existing industrial sites. With the tower catch now demonstrated, the next constraint becomes how quickly the factory can turn out vehicles whose per-flight cost approaches the price of the propellant alone.

Девятиметровый масштаб — вот инженерная реальность, которая поставила крест на углепластиковых прототипах «Старшип» и поставила нержавеющую сталь и под «Старшип», и под «Супер Хеви».

Углепластик воевал с заводом на таком диаметре, потому что девятиметровая композитная обечайка требует оправок, автоклавов и бездефектной полимеризации, которые с каждым лишним метром становятся всё злее, тогда как лист нержавейки катают и сваривают на оснастке, которая у верфей уже есть.

Сырой лист к тому же стоит примерно в пятнадцать-двадцать раз дешевле сопоставимого титана, так что металл перестаёт быть экзотической строкой в смете и становится биржевым товаром, который берут плитами.

Охладите эту сталь в жидком кислороде и метане — и предел текучести вырастает примерно на пятьдесят процентов, так что та же стенка держит примерно в полтора раза большую исходную нагрузку, и делает это без риска хрупкости, с которым титан сталкивается при криогенной температуре.

На возвращении через атмосферу сталь всё ещё несёт нагрузку примерно при восьмистах пятидесяти градусах Цельсия, что позволяет сделать теплозащиту тоньше или обойтись меньшим числом плиток, чем потребовала бы титановая силовая конструкция, потому что сам планер выдерживает этот прогрев.
