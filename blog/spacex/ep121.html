Top News

  1. SpaceX plans to rename its AI unit to SpaceXSI.

    Musk confirmed the change on X after a user suggested switching from SpaceXAI. He replied “Yes, we will make that change” after posting that there should be “no more AI” and describing SpaceX as a “super intelligence company.” The move would mark the second rebrand in months following the absorption of xAI earlier this year. No timeline or formal corporate announcement has been issued. The rebrand follows an earlier shift from xAI to SpaceXAI after the standalone company was folded into SpaceX. Trademark filings from the prior change already covered satellite data centers, cloud computing, model training, and AI workload management. Source: coinpaper.com

  2. SpaceX aborted the countdown for 21 data transport satellites in the final minute.

    The Tranche 1 Transport Layer A mission for the Space Development Agency was scrubbed during automated startup. The Falcon 9 with booster B1103 on its sixth flight is now targeted for no earlier than Tuesday, Oct. 6, at 1:17 a.m. PDT from Vandenberg. The booster is planned to land at Landing Zone 4 for the 37th time at that site. The 21 satellites were built by Northrop Grumman and form the fourth production batch for the Proliferated Warfighter Space Architecture. The mission is one of 23 awarded under the National Security Space Launch Phase 2 contract. Source: spaceflightnow.com

  3. Starship Ship 43 is advancing through final ground-test preparations for Flight 16.

    Cryogenic proof testing for Ships 42 and 43 may begin within days after the Ship Thrust Simulator stand returned to Starbase. Ship 43 is stacked inside Mega Bay 2 and could become the first Starship to launch from LC-39A at Kennedy Space Center. A provisional no-earlier-than date of October 30 appears in an FAA scheduling document. The vehicle began stacking on August 20 and is now inside Mega Bay 2 at Starbase in South Texas. Flight 16 is currently assigned to the Starlink 30-1 mission, marking one of the first operational Starlink payloads on Starship. Source: basenor.com

  4. Reflection signed a $150 million-per-month compute rental agreement with SpaceX.

    The deal provides immediate access to Nvidia GB300 chips at Colossus 2 beginning July 1, 2026, and runs through 2029. Either party can exit with 90 days’ notice after the initial three months. The full term would total roughly $6.3 billion. The agreement gives Reflection access to servers inside the Colossus 2 data center. Reflection is preparing to release an open-weight model expected to compete with leading Chinese offerings. Source: fourweekmba.com

  5. Multiple Falcon 9-class competitors are now targeting first flights no earlier than 2027.

    Blue Origin is rebuilding Launch Complex 36 after a May anomaly and still aims to return New Glenn to flight by year-end. Relativity has hardware in integrated build for Terran R with a late-2026 Cape target that observers expect to slip. Rocket Lab’s Neutron window for 2026 is narrowing toward a 2027 debut. New Glenn is rated for roughly 45 tons to low Earth orbit in reusable configuration. Terran R targets 23.5 tons reusable and 33.5 tons expendable. Source: nextbigfuture.com

Engineering Deep Dive

The engineering reality behind the final-minute scrub centers on the Falcon 9’s automated startup sequence, which begins roughly 60 seconds before liftoff and hands primary control to the flight computer. At that point the vehicle checks propellant pressures, valve positions, and engine health in a rapid cascade that must all report nominal before the hold-down clamps release. A single out-of-tolerance reading—whether from a sensor glitch, a valve response time, or a ground-system handoff—triggers an immediate abort to protect the booster and the 21 Northrop Grumman satellites on board. The sequence is deliberately conservative because the same first-stage booster, B1103, is already on its sixth flight and will attempt its 37th landing at LZ-4 if the mission flies. That reuse-driven reliability requirement forces tighter margins on every ground check than an expendable vehicle would tolerate. The net effect is that a scrubbed attempt today simply shifts the same hardware stack to the next available window without consuming a flight article, preserving the cadence that has already delivered three missions in 13 hours earlier this week. The Falcon 9 first stage uses nine Merlin 1D engines that must reach stable chamber pressure within a narrow time window before the vehicle commits to flight. Each engine carries its own set of turbopump speed sensors and propellant valve position encoders that feed the flight computer in parallel. When any parameter deviates beyond pre-set limits the computer issues an immediate hold and the ground systems vent residual pressure. This layered check prevents a partial ignition or valve misalignment from propagating into a more serious anomaly once the vehicle is committed. The approach trades a small amount of schedule margin for the ability to reuse the same booster dozens of times without cumulative damage from marginal starts. Because the booster has already flown five prior missions the ground team can compare today’s sensor traces against the historical baseline for that specific vehicle, tightening the tolerance bands further. The result is a launch system that can absorb a late abort, recycle the countdown within 24 hours, and still meet the aggressive cadence demanded by both commercial and national-security customers.

La réalité technique derrière cette annulation à la dernière minute, c’est la séquence de démarrage automatique du Falcon 9, qui commence environ soixante secondes avant le décollage et confie le contrôle principal à l’ordinateur de vol.

À ce moment-là, le véhicule vérifie les pressions d’ergols, les positions des vannes et l’état des moteurs, dans une cascade rapide où tout doit remonter nominal avant que les pinces de retenue ne se libèrent.

Une seule mesure hors tolérance — qu’il s’agisse d’un dysfonctionnement de capteur, d’un temps de réponse de vanne, ou d’un transfert avec les systèmes sol — déclenche un arrêt immédiat, pour protéger le booster et les vingt et un satellites Northrop Grumman à bord.

La séquence est volontairement prudente, parce que le même booster de premier étage, B mille cent trois, en est déjà à son sixième vol et tentera son trente-septième atterrissage à la zone d’atterrissage quatre si la mission décolle.

Cette exigence de fiabilité liée à la réutilisation impose des marges plus serrées sur chaque vérification au sol qu’un véhicule consommable ne tolérerait.

Au final, une tentative annulée aujourd’hui ne fait que reporter le même empilement de matériel vers la prochaine fenêtre disponible, sans consommer un exemplaire de vol, et ça préserve la cadence qui a déjà permis trois missions en treize heures plus tôt cette semaine.

Le premier étage du Falcon 9 utilise neuf moteurs Merlin 1D, qui doivent atteindre une pression de chambre stable dans une fenêtre de temps très étroite avant que le véhicule ne s’engage en vol.

Chaque moteur emporte son propre jeu de capteurs de régime des turbopompes et de codeurs de position des vannes d’ergols, qui alimentent l’ordinateur de vol en parallèle.

Dès qu’un paramètre sort des limites prédéfinies, l’ordinateur ordonne une mise en attente immédiate et les systèmes sol purgent la pression résiduelle.

Cette vérification en couches empêche un allumage partiel ou un désalignement de vanne de se propager en anomalie plus grave une fois le véhicule engagé.

Cette approche échange un peu de marge de calendrier contre la capacité de réutiliser le même booster des dizaines de fois, sans dommages cumulés liés à des démarrages limites.

Comme le booster a déjà volé cinq missions auparavant, l’équipe sol peut comparer les traces capteurs d’aujourd’hui à la référence historique de ce véhicule précis, et resserrer encore les plages de tolérance.

Le résultat, c’est un système de lancement capable d’absorber un arrêt tardif, de recycler le compte à rebours en moins de vingt-quatre heures, et de tenir quand même la cadence agressive exigée à la fois par les clients commerciaux et ceux de la sécurité nationale.

Эта многослойная проверка не даёт частичному зажиганию или перекосу клапана перерасти в более серьёзную аномалию, когда аппарат уже пошёл в полёт.

Такой подход меняет небольшой запас по расписанию на возможность использовать один и тот же ускоритель десятки раз без накопления повреждений от пограничных запусков.

В результате получается система запуска, которая переносит позднюю отмену, перезапускает предстартовый отсчёт в течение двадцати четырёх часов и всё равно выдерживает жёсткий темп, которого требуют и коммерческие заказчики, и заказчики национальной безопасности.
