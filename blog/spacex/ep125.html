SpaceX spectrum purchase opens indoor phone coverage for Starlink Mobile against carriers.

By Patrick October 09, 2026 Ep 125 7 min read Listen to podcast
Listen in
Contents
Top News Community Buzz The Counterpoint AI & Compute Engineering Deep Dive Market Watch

Top News

  1. SpaceX acquires low-band spectrum to build nationwide mobile network

    SpaceX will buy up to 14 megahertz of paired spectrum in the 800 MHz range, subject to final regulatory approval. The company plans to combine this with its existing 2 GHz holdings and a terrestrial network to reach devices inside buildings. Elon Musk called the move a "very big deal." The announcement follows FCC approval for 15,000 next-generation direct-to-device satellites that deliver more than 100 times the bandwidth of current Starlink Mobile units. Shares of AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile fell around 7 percent on the news. The new frequencies target improved indoor penetration and wide-area coverage without extra ground towers in every gap. SpaceX intends to integrate the 800 MHz allocation into the V2 satellite payload design. Final regulatory clearance for the transaction remains pending after the FCC placed some spectrum requests on hold. Source: forbes.com Source: investors.com Source: reuters.com

  2. SpaceX outlines heavy-haul logistics route for Starbase components

    The company is planning a dedicated heavy-haul corridor to move oversized Starship hardware and ground-support equipment to the Texas launch site. The route would reduce reliance on public roads and waterways for oversized loads. Dedicated infrastructure would allow larger single-piece components to reach the production and launch areas without repeated disassembly. SpaceX expects the corridor to support higher flight rates by shortening transport times for boosters and ships. The plan targets both marine and land segments to handle the increasing mass of Starship V3 hardware. Source: heavyliftpfi.com

  3. American Airlines expands Starlink to all mainline narrowbody aircraft

    The carrier will install Starlink terminals across its Airbus narrowbody fleet after initial testing on select routes. The move follows earlier narrowbody trials and aims to provide high-speed connectivity on domestic flights. American Airlines plans to equip the entire narrowbody fleet rather than limiting service to widebodies. The phased rollout will begin with aircraft already operating on high-demand domestic segments. Terminal installation will use the same hardware validated in prior Starlink aviation tests. Source: au.pcmag.com

  4. SpaceX urges improved orbital coordination after close Starlink approaches

    Multiple recent conjunctions brought Starlink satellites within tens to hundreds of meters of other objects. The company stated the distances were "way too close to comfort" and called for better data sharing among operators. SpaceX highlighted that current coordination processes left insufficient margin during several events. The firm requested standardized ephemeris exchange formats to reduce response times. Improved protocols would apply across both commercial and government satellite fleets operating in low Earth orbit. Source: arstechnica.com

  5. X prepares bundled subscription covering X, Grok, and Cursor

    The platform is developing a single plan that combines access to X Premium features, Grok models, and the Cursor code editor. The bundle would streamline billing for users who rely on both the social platform and AI development tools. Subscribers would gain unified access without separate logins or payment methods. The offering targets developers who already use Grok within Cursor for code assistance. X expects the combined tier to increase retention across its AI and social products. Source: techweez.com

  6. Cursor’s Europe chief departs for OpenAI role

    Ismail Elmas, who led Cursor operations across Europe while previously working at SpaceX, is joining OpenAI. The move highlights competition for talent between AI coding tools and frontier model companies. Elmas had overseen European expansion of the Cursor editor that integrates multiple large language models. His departure follows a period of rapid growth for Anysphere, the company behind Cursor. OpenAI gains an executive familiar with both SpaceX engineering culture and AI developer tooling. Source: yellow.com

  7. SpaceX advances Louisiana coastal infrastructure plans

    The company filed details for a large wharf and internal road network to support Starship component transport and operations along the Louisiana coast. The project would create dedicated marine and land access for oversized hardware. The wharf would accommodate heavy-lift vessels delivering booster and ship sections directly to the site. Internal roads would connect the waterfront to assembly buildings without crossing public infrastructure. SpaceX designed the layout to handle the mass and dimensions of fully assembled Starship stages. Source: thecentersquare.com

  8. ARK analysts compare Rocket Lab Neutron payload capacity to Falcon 9

    Neutron is projected to lift 13,000 kilograms, approaching Falcon 9’s 17,000-kilogram reusable performance. ARK noted satellite operators face a launch-capacity shortage as constellations grow and internal SpaceX demand rises. The firm pointed to Starlink V3 and upcoming StarMind satellites as drivers of that shortage. Kepler has already booked a dedicated Neutron mission no earlier than 2028. ARK views the gap between planned and available launch capacity as an opening for additional providers. Source: tradingview.com

AI & Compute

Engineering Deep Dive

Low-band spectrum at 800 MHz propagates farther and penetrates buildings more effectively than the 2 GHz frequencies SpaceX already holds. Pairing the new 14 MHz allocation with existing holdings lets the same satellite beam reach indoor handsets without requiring additional ground infrastructure in every coverage gap. The 100-times bandwidth increase planned for the next-generation V2 satellites then multiplies capacity once the signal reaches the device. This combination trades some spectrum efficiency for simpler user hardware, removing the need for specialized terminals that earlier direct-to-cell systems required. The approach also sets up a hybrid network where satellites handle wide-area coverage while a terrestrial layer fills urban capacity, a design that reuses the same orbital assets for both consumer broadband and mobile backhaul. Execution now hinges on integrating the new frequencies into the satellite payload without raising mass or power draw beyond current Starship lift margins.

The 171-foot-long Ship 40 returned to Starbase after its Flight 13 splashdown in the Indian Ocean. Recovery teams succeeded in towing the intact upper stage through the Port of Brownsville channel despite earlier concerns that the operation might fail. Engineers removed several heatshield tiles from the vehicle and flew them again on Flight 14, providing direct physical evidence of tile durability after reentry heating. The landing burn on Flight 13 was performed in a profile that matches the final descent needed for a future tower catch attempt. Because the stage remained intact rather than exploding on contact with the water, SpaceX obtained data on tile attachment and thermal protection that camera-equipped Starlink V3 satellites alone could not supply. This physical inspection shortens the iteration loop for the heatshield redesign required before repeated booster and ship reuse becomes routine.

Starship upper-stage recovery changes the economics of the entire stack. When a ship survives splashdown, the cost per flight drops from the raw material price of a new vehicle to the marginal cost of inspection, tile replacement, and refurbishment. The gap between those two numbers is the Idiot Index for the upper stage. Flight 13 demonstrated that the current tile attachment method can survive one reentry without total loss, lowering that index. The next engineering step is to close the remaining distance to zero refurbishment time so that the same ship can fly again within days rather than months. Achieving that target requires both the tower catch hardware now under construction at Starbase and the orbital refueling demonstration still ahead in the flight test campaign.
