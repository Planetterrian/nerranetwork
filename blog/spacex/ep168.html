Orbital AI satellites launching on New Glenn in 2029 will test whether solar arrays can sustain rack-scale TPU workloads without terrestrial grid ties.

Top News

  1. Google selects New Glenn for first dedicated Project Suncatcher launch

    Google booked Blue Origin’s New Glenn rocket for the first heavy-lift launch under Project Suncatcher, scheduled for the first quarter of 2029. Multiple computing satellites will fly, each carrying Tensor Processing Unit capacity roughly equal to one data-center rack. The satellites will enter sun-synchronous orbit to evaluate whether continuous solar exposure can support power-intensive machine-learning operations in space. An earlier prototype, built with Planet Labs and launched on a SpaceX Falcon 9 in October, successfully booted its TPUs and ran the Gemma 4 model to generate and transmit a haiku. Blue Origin’s next New Glenn flight remains delayed until January 2027 following a May explosion that damaged the Florida launchpad. Source: silicon.co.uk Source: teslanorth.com

The Counterpoint

Lenders are attempting to block SpaceX from placing $40 billion worth of chips into orbit through financing covenants that predate the current orbital-compute expansion plans. The restriction arrives while Google, SpaceX, and Starcloud all advance concepts for data centers in space, raising the possibility that capital-market terms could slow deployment timelines. Resolution would require either renegotiated loan agreements or alternative financing that explicitly preserves launch rights for compute hardware.

Engineering Deep Dive

Orbital AI satellites move the power and thermal problem from terrestrial grid constraints and land-use permits to sunlight availability and radiator sizing in vacuum. A sun-synchronous orbit keeps solar arrays illuminated for most of each pass, removing the day-night cycle that forces large batteries on lower orbits and thereby reducing mass that would otherwise be devoted to energy storage. Each satellite carries roughly one data-center rack of TPU silicon, so the mass budget must accommodate both the processors and the radiators needed to reject the heat they generate when running machine-learning workloads. The earlier Falcon 9 prototype already proved the chips survive launch loads and can run a Gemma 4 model, confirming basic functionality before scaling. Scaling to a full rack per satellite therefore hinges on whether the added radiator area and pointing accuracy stay within New Glenn’s payload envelope without driving structural mass beyond the point where solar power remains net positive. If the radiators can be sized to match the TPU thermal load at the expected orbit beta angle, the architecture avoids the ground-based cost of land, permits, and grid interconnection that currently dominate terrestrial AI clusters. The same mass trade also determines whether multiple satellites can be stacked on a single New Glenn flight or whether each requires its own dedicated launch, directly affecting the economics of building an orbital compute network. Blue Origin’s delayed schedule to January 2027 for the next New Glenn flight sets the earliest possible full-scale test window, giving Google additional time to refine radiator coatings and thermal-control algorithms before the 2029 mission.

L’énergie et la chaleur, du point de vue de l’ingénierie, deviennent la contrainte de charge utile pour ces satellites du projet Suncatcher dès l’instant où ils quittent le pas de tir.

Une orbite terrestre basse typique de quatre-vingt-dix minutes passe de l’ordre de trente-cinq minutes dans l’ombre de la Terre, donc à peu près quarante pour cent de chaque révolution a besoin d’énergie stockée — trente-cinq divisé par quatre-vingt-dix, ça fait environ trente-neuf pour cent — et ce stockage, ce sont des batteries, de la masse que vous ne consacrez plus aux processeurs.

Une trajectoire qui reste ensoleillée pendant la plus grande partie d’une révolution permet aux panneaux de continuer à alimenter les puces, donc la masse des batteries peut diminuer.

Chaque satellite est censé emporter à peu près une baie de centre de données de silicium T P U, donc le budget de masse doit porter à la fois les processeurs et les radiateurs qui rejettent la chaleur sous charge d’apprentissage automatique.

Dans le vide, chaque watt part en infrarouge ; la puissance rayonnée croît avec la puissance quatrième de la température absolue, donc doubler la température en kelvin rayonne seize fois la puissance depuis la même surface — deux à la puissance quatre, ça fait seize — mais les puces doivent quand même rester dans leurs limites thermiques, ce qui impose de la surface à une température viable.

Si ces radiateurs correspondent à la charge T P U à l’angle bêta d’orbite prévu, l’angle entre le plan d’orbite et le Soleil, la conception s’affranchit du terrain, des permis et du raccordement au réseau.

Au sol, un ensemble fini coûte plusieurs fois le silicium brut — un indice de l’idiot calculé sur un coin de table, de plusieurs fois la puce et le métal — tandis qu’en orbite, les intrants baguette magique, ce sont les processeurs, la surface de cellules et le métal des radiateurs, et le multiplicateur, c’est la structure, le pointage et la masse de rejet thermique.

Ce compromis de masse décide si plusieurs satellites s’empilent dans l’enveloppe de charge utile de New Glenn, ou si chacun a besoin de son propre vol.

Le travail encore ouvert, ce sont les revêtements de radiateurs et les algorithmes de contrôle thermique qui maintiennent le chemin thermique stable sans pousser la structure au-delà du point où l’énergie solaire reste nette positive.

Orbital AI satellites launching on New Glenn in 2029 will test whether solar arrays can sustain rack-scale TPU workloads without terrestrial grid ties. This is SpaceX Daily, episode 168. Lenders are pressing chip-launch covenants, Vandenberg scrubbed a Falcon 9, and a Super Heavy booster aborted into the Gulf, then The Counterpoint and an Engineering Deep Dive. Google booked Blue Origin's New Glenn, Tesla North reports, for Project Suncatcher's first dedicated heavy-lift — a vehicle class sized to loft several computing satellites together — aimed at the first quarter. Several computing satellites are manifested, and they will enter a sun-synchronous orbit, one that holds a fixed local time of day, so the team can see whether unbroken sunlight can feed power-hungry machine-learning work. Planet Labs helped build an earlier prototype that flew on a SpaceX Falcon 9 in October, brought its chips up, ran the Gemma 4 model, and sent back a haiku. The next New Glenn still waits until January two thousand twenty-seven because a May explosion damaged the Florida pad, so that rocket has to return to flight well before the dedicated Suncatcher mission. Lenders are seeking loan terms, the Financial Times reports, that would stop SpaceX from putting an estimated forty billion dollars of chips into orbit. The push aims at plans to grow orbital compute on hardware that sits next to Starlink, processors meant to fly with the satellite-internet stack, and those terms sit inside financing agreements already on the books. Vandenberg Space Launch Complex 4 East lost Thursday evening's Falcon 9 attempt when SpaceX scrubbed, Spaceflight Now reports, and the company now aims at a four-hour window that opens at six thirty-two p.m. Pacific Standard Time, or PST, on Friday. Booster B eleven hundred will fly its eleventh mission and try for a landing on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You, the landing barge waiting downrange, which would be that ship's two hundred thirty-fifth recovery. The payload is U.S. Space Force satellites on a south-easterly trajectory. Recovery teams locked down the Gulf off Starbase, SpaceX said, after the Super Heavy booster aborted safely — cut the remaining flight and put the first stage in the water — and splashed down following Flight fifteen. Environmental Health and Safety staff checked the recovered hardware and found no chemical, biological, or toxicological hazard. Specialists went to Mexican territory after social-media claims of widespread debris, and ground partners found very little material and none clearly tied to SpaceX operations. The company called most of those online reports fabrications or deliberately misleading. Diablo's Torment II difficulty is now a mark Tesla AI has hit on the AI4 computer. Relativity Space completed the Terran R flight-one second-stage hot-fire campaign on the A2 stand at NASA's Stennis Space Center, NASASpaceflight reports, beginning with a ten-second firing in October, the first time a full stage had been on that stand since nineteen seventy. Teams then set the stage for a full mission-duty-cycle burn, a firing as long as a real flight needs, lasting more than four hundred seconds, including an igniter hot fire and a coast demonstration that mimics the unpowered stretch of a mission. The Aeon V engine ran that profile without holds after propellant and helium, the pressurant, were loaded, and it did so on the first attempt. Max Evans sat for a video on current Starship development status and the test plans still ahead. Elsewhere, Elon Musk posted that a widely shared Tesla China dance video is authentic footage and not AI-generated content. SciGuySpace observed, unconfirmed by the companies themselves, that several launch providers now appear to operate under unified structures. Worth keeping an eye on are loan covenants that predate SpaceX's current orbital-compute expansion, because those older terms are now being used to limit chip launches. That is a reasonable credit concern if money already lent was never scoped for a new hardware path, and Google, SpaceX, and Starcloud are all advancing space data-center concepts at the same time, so capital-market language could slow who actually flies. Clearing it would take renegotiated loan agreements or new financing that explicitly keeps launch rights for compute payloads. Power and heat, from an engineering standpoint, become the payload constraint for those Project Suncatcher satellites the moment they leave the pad. A typical ninety-minute low Earth orbit spends on the order of thirty-five minutes in Earth's shadow, so roughly forty percent of each pass needs stored energy — thirty-five divided by ninety is about thirty-nine percent — and that storage is batteries, mass you are no longer spending on processors. A path that stays sunlit for most of a revolution lets the arrays keep feeding the chips, so battery mass can shrink. Each satellite is meant to carry roughly one data-center rack of TPU silicon, so the mass budget has to hold both the processors and the radiators that reject heat under machine-learning load. In vacuum every watt leaves as infrared; radiated power scales with the fourth power of absolute temperature, so doubling temperature in kelvin radiates sixteen times the power from the same area — two to the fourth is sixteen — but the chips still have to live inside their thermal limits, which forces area at a survivable temperature. If those radiators match the TPU load at the expected orbit beta angle, the angle between the orbit plane and the sun, the design skips land, permits, and grid interconnection. On the ground a finished cluster costs many times the raw silicon — a back-of-envelope Idiot Index of several times chip and metal — while in orbit the magic-wand inputs are processors, cell area, and radiator metal, and the multiplier is structure, pointing, and heat-rejection mass. That mass trade decides whether several satellites stack inside New Glenn's payload envelope or each needs its own flight. The open work is radiator coatings and thermal-control algorithms that keep the heat path stable without driving structure past the point where solar power stays net positive.
