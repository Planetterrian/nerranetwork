SpaceX Daily

Starship’s six-engine static fire advances the thirteenth flight-test campaign by validating the full Raptor cluster ahead of orbital attempts.

Top News

  1. SpaceX fires up all 6 of Starship's engines ahead of 13th test flight (video) — Space

    2. SpaceX conducted a 60-second static fire of Starship’s six Raptor engines at the Massey test site. The test exercised the complete propulsion cluster on the vehicle slated for the next integrated flight. This step directly follows the prior booster and ship static-fire sequence and moves the program closer to demonstrating repeated full-duration burns. Observers will next watch for FAA clearance and the specific timeline for Ship 13’s launch attempt. Source: Google News

  2. SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket launches 24 Starlink satellites from California (video) — Space

    3. A Falcon 9 lifted off from Vandenberg carrying 24 Starlink satellites into low Earth orbit. The mission continued the rapid cadence of dedicated Starlink deployments from the West Coast pad. Booster recovery followed the standard RTLS profile. The flight added to the constellation’s density in the latest orbital shells. Source: Google News

  3. 260 Starlink Satellites Burn Up in Earth's Atmosphere As More Head for Fiery Ends — PCMag

    4. Two hundred sixty Starlink satellites have re-entered and burned up in the atmosphere so far this year. The deorbits follow the standard end-of-life procedure for satellites that reach the end of their operational window or experience anomalies. Additional units are already on trajectories that will produce similar re-entries in coming weeks. The numbers reflect the high launch and replacement rate required to maintain the constellation. Source: Google News

  4. Perovskite solar panel startup Verde Technologies shifts focus to space — SpaceNews

    5. Verde Technologies is redirecting its perovskite thin-film solar technology from terrestrial rooftops to orbital applications. The material’s low mass and high specific power make it a candidate for powering large satellite constellations and future orbital data centers. The company is now pursuing qualification for space environments instead of its original ground-based market. Early prototypes target the power demands of high-density satellite buses. Source: spacenews.com

  5. Amazon now has enough satellites to launch competitor to SpaceX’s Starlink — AOL.com

    6. Amazon has manufactured and prepared a sufficient number of Project Kuiper satellites to begin initial service. The constellation is positioned as a direct broadband competitor in the low-Earth-orbit market. Deployment will rely on multiple launch providers for the remaining satellites. The milestone marks the first time a second large LEO broadband system has reached operational scale. Source: Google News

  6. SpaceX Fourth of July weekend launch. Watch liftoff from Volusia County — Daytona Beach News-Journal

    7. A Falcon 9 launch from Florida is scheduled for the Fourth of July weekend and will be visible along the Volusia County coastline. The mission profile follows the standard East Coast trajectory used for Starlink or commercial payloads. Local viewing guides have been issued for residents east of the launch site. The timing aligns with the ongoing high launch cadence from Cape Canaveral. Source: Google News

  7. Starship 60-second static fire ahead of the thirteenth flight test — r/spacex

    8. The Starship vehicle completed a full-duration, six-engine static fire lasting 60 seconds at Starbase. The test exercised the integrated propulsion system under flight-like conditions. Data from the burn will feed into final vehicle checkout for the upcoming mission. Community discussion centers on whether the next flight will attempt new hardware or profile elements. Source: reddit.com

  8. Status update: xAI mid-year report — The Business Journals

    9. xAI released its mid-year operational summary covering compute deployment and model development progress. The report outlines expansion of training clusters and inference capacity tied to existing data-center builds. Hardware utilization metrics and planned additions through year-end are included. The update provides the first public checkpoint since the company’s early-2026 announcements. Source: Google News

Community Buzz

  1. Observers on the Space subreddit are tracking exact timestamps from the recent Starship static-fire video to compare chamber-pressure traces against earlier tests. Several users noted the absence of visible plume anomalies during the 60-second hold. Discussion also covers expected changes to the ship’s heat-shield tile layout for the next flight.
  2. Local Florida spotters shared photos of the Falcon 9 booster landing zone preparations at Cape Canaveral ahead of the holiday weekend launch. One post highlighted the rapid turnaround of the transporter-erector since the previous mission.
  3. Amateur radio operators reported successful reception of Starlink satellite beacons on the latest California launch, confirming deployment and initial orbit parameters. Signal strength data is being compiled for comparison with prior batches.
  4. Engineers in the r/Starlink community are discussing the implications of the reported satellite re-entries for constellation replenishment modeling. Several threads examine how the deorbit rate affects long-term slot availability in lower shells.
  5. Space enthusiasts posted time-lapse footage of the Starship test stand being reconfigured after the static fire, noting the speed of propellant-line inspections.

The Counterpoint

Amazon’s Kuiper constellation has now reached the satellite count needed to begin service, giving a second operator the hardware base to compete directly in the broadband market. The development challenges Starlink’s early-mover advantage in user terminals and ground infrastructure. Resolution will depend on launch cadence, spectrum coordination, and demonstrated subscriber uptake for both systems. Source: Google News

AI & Compute

xAI’s mid-year report details cluster expansion and inference capacity additions through the remainder of 2026, with hardware utilization metrics tied to ongoing data-center construction. The update shows continued scaling of training runs and serving infrastructure that will draw on satellite backhaul for distributed workloads. No specific orbital data-center hardware milestones were listed. Source: Google News

Engineering Deep Dive

The 60-second, six-engine static fire on Starship tests the full Raptor cluster under simultaneous high-thrust conditions that single-engine or partial-cluster tests cannot replicate. From first principles, the dominant constraint is maintaining uniform propellant flow and chamber pressure across all six engines while managing the combined acoustic and thermal loads on the thrust structure. A successful hold confirms that the propellant manifolds, turbopump synchronization, and gimbal actuators can deliver the required impulse without inducing destructive pogo or vortex shedding. This result lowers the risk that an early cutoff or thrust mismatch will occur during the boost phase of flight thirteen. The next measurable step is whether the same vehicle can repeat the burn profile after minimal refurbishment, directly addressing the open question of rapid reuse cadence.
