Top News

  1. Musk revives TSMC Terafab talks

    Reports indicate Elon Musk has restarted discussions with TSMC over a potential Terafab facility. The move comes after Friday’s sell-off and is tied to comments that capex efficiency remains greatly underestimated. SPCX traded higher in premarket on the news. TradingView noted the stock jumped overnight on the revived talks. Musk framed the efficiency gains as greatly underestimated in public remarks. The update follows ongoing production deployment of the bot inside Cursor. One reply from a developer stated that users not trying Cursor with Grok Bot are missing a clear UX advantage over Claude Code and Codex. Another user noted that model performance still favors Opus in some cases but Cursor’s interface leads overall. Musk emphasized the weekly cadence of changes drawn directly from user input. Source: x.com

  3. Musk adds two sites to Colossus 2

    Elon Musk named two new Colossus 2 clusters “Spam” and “Gun Range,” with the latter slated to come online by the end of November. The sites expand the Tennessee campus that already supports xAI training runs. Spam occupies a former Hormel storage facility while Gun Range sits on land once owned by local police. Musk walked the full site at midnight and again in the morning, reporting good progress by the SpaceX team and contractors. The expansion targets roughly 1.2 million Nvidia Blackwell GPUs by year-end. A separate federal judge ruling left an NAACP air-pollution lawsuit active after denying xAI’s motion to dismiss. Source: finance.biggo.com Source: app.dealroom.co Source: thenextweb.com

  4. SpaceX to Launch USSF-432 Tuesday

    SpaceX is scheduled to launch USSF-432 on Tuesday from Vandenberg, advancing a Space Force aircraft-tracking mesh network. The mission adds satellites that extend real-time tracking coverage for military aircraft. Earlier targeting had placed the flight on November 1 before a one-day slip. The payload supports national-security communications requirements distinct from commercial Starlink. Source: techtimes.com

  5. Falcon 9 Passes 700 Launches

    Falcon 9 has now completed more than 700 flights as SpaceX shifts focus toward Starship operations. The milestone reflects sustained booster reuse rates that keep marginal launch costs low. Most recent flights carried Starlink satellites or commercial payloads under the same first-stage recovery profile. The program continues to set cadence records while Starship infrastructure at Starbase receives priority upgrades. Source: shiftdelete.net

  6. SpaceX Launches schedule for November 2026

The schedule lists exact times and public stream links for each attempt. USSF-432 appears as the first national-security flight of the month. Additional Starlink deployments fill the remaining slots through the end of November. Source: shattered.io

  7. Is there a launch today?

    Florida Today lists two Falcon 9 missions and one Starship test flight window inside the next 30 days from Cape Canaveral and Kennedy Space Center. The calendar includes both commercial and government payloads. Viewers can track each attempt through standard public streams. Source: floridatoday.com

  8. Tennessee bets on data centers

    Tennessee officials have approved multiple large data-center projects even as analysts question long-term demand if the AI boom slows. The state’s power utility has already committed transmission upgrades to support the new loads. Local economic forecasts tie job growth directly to continued GPU-cluster construction at sites like Colossus 2. Source: tennessean.com

Engineering Deep Dive

Starship Block 3 stands 124 m tall with a 9 m diameter and targets more than 100 tonnes of reusable payload to low Earth orbit. The Super Heavy booster stretches 72 m, carries 3,650 t of propellant, and mounts 33 Raptor 3 engines that together produce 8,240 tf of thrust at sea level. The upper stage Ship measures 52 m, holds 1,600 t of propellant, and uses three sea-level plus three vacuum Raptors delivering 1,614 tf total thrust, with each sea-level Raptor 3 rated near 250 tf. These figures reflect a closed-cycle methalox design that routes both fuel and oxidizer through the turbopumps before combustion, raising chamber pressure and specific impulse while cutting parts count. The resulting thrust-to-weight and reuse margins allow the full stack to reach orbit on Flight 14 and deploy operational Starlink V3 satellites, a step that earlier blocks could not complete. Because the engines now require far less post-flight inspection, the architecture moves closer to airline-style turnaround times between flights.

Flight 14 demonstrated that the Block 3 configuration can sustain orbit long enough to release 26 Starlink V3 satellites, each designed for roughly 1 Tbps downlink capacity. The heat-shield tile layout on the Ship uses hexagonal elements that survived re-entry while preserving the ability to fly again. Super Heavy’s 33 Raptor 3 engines achieved the necessary thrust-to-weight ratio for booster return and tower catch on prior flights, proving the propellant load and engine count deliver the required delta-v margin. The common bulkhead between liquid oxygen and methane tanks on both stages reduces structural mass compared with separate tank designs, directly improving payload fraction. This mass saving compounds with the higher chamber pressure of the full-flow staged combustion cycle, allowing the same propellant load to deliver more total impulse than an equivalent open-cycle engine. The net effect is a vehicle that can place substantial mass into orbit and still return both stages for reuse without expending the hardware on every flight.

Raptor 3’s streamlined turbomachinery eliminated many external lines and valves visible on earlier versions, lowering both production cost and potential leak points. Each engine now produces approximately 35 percent more thrust than the Raptor 1 baseline while using fewer parts, shrinking the Idiot Index between raw material inputs and finished engine output. The design choice to run the entire propellant flow through the turbines before injection raises efficiency but demands tighter material tolerances and active cooling passages. Those tolerances are met through additive manufacturing and precision machining that SpaceX has scaled inside its own facilities. The resulting engine reliability supports the goal of rapid reuse, where a booster can be caught, inspected, and reflown with minimal turnaround. That reuse loop is what ultimately collapses the cost per kilogram to orbit below any expendable architecture.

Starship Block 3, c’est la réalité d’ingénierie derrière un empilement de cent vingt-quatre mètres, neuf mètres de diamètre, avec une cible de fret réutilisable en orbite terrestre basse au-dessus de cent tonnes.

Super Heavy, le premier étage, s’étend sur soixante-douze mètres et emporte trois mille six cent cinquante tonnes d’ergols. Trente-trois moteurs Raptor 3 produisent huit mille deux cent quarante tonnes de poussée au niveau de la mer.

Cet ensemble est une conception à moteur en panne tolérée : perdre un Raptor en montée, et les autres peuvent encore soulever une masse humide de plusieurs milliers de tonnes.

Ship, l’étage supérieur, mesure cinquante-deux mètres et contient mille six cents tonnes d’ergols. Il vole avec trois Raptor conçus pour le niveau de la mer et trois conçus pour le vide, mille six cent quatorze tonnes de poussée au total, chaque Raptor 3 de niveau de la mer autour de deux cent cinquante tonnes.

Un système méthalox en cycle fermé, méthane et oxygène liquide, pousse le carburant et le comburant d’abord à travers les turbopompes, puis dans la chambre. C’est la combustion étagée à flux complet : aucun échappement de turbine rejeté par-dessus bord, donc chaque kilogramme travaille dans la chambre.

Le cycle élève la pression de chambre et l’impulsion spécifique, la poussée par kilogramme d’ergol, tout en réduisant les pièces.

Le méthane reste liquide à des températures plus clémentes que l’hydrogène, ce qui limite le volume des réservoirs, et il ne laisse pas la suie de carbone que le kérosène laisse dans une chambre réutilisée.

Une cloison commune, une seule paroi entre les réservoirs d’oxygène et de méthane sur les deux étages, économise de la masse structurelle par rapport à des réservoirs séparés. À ce diamètre, un second dôme pèse de l’ordre de plusieurs tonnes qui deviennent de la fraction de charge utile au lieu de l’acier, et l’équation de la fusée punit toute masse sèche en trop tout le long jusqu’à l’orbite.

L’écart, ce n’est pas le nickel ni le méthane, qui sont des matières industrielles, mais l’usinage, les tolérances, et les passages de refroidissement actif qui empêchent une chambre à quelques centaines de bars de fondre.

Ces tolérances sont tenues avec la fabrication additive, l’impression métal, et l’usinage de précision que Space X a mis à l’échelle dans ses propres usines.

Moins de points de fuite externes, voilà pourquoi l’inspection après vol peut être bien plus légère, et c’est ce qui permet à un propulseur Super Heavy d’être attrapé, inspecté, et remis en vol, et à un Ship de revenir, plus près d’une rotation façon compagnie aérienne, au lieu de sacrifier le matériel.

La comptabilité baguette magique pour cet empilement, c’est le métal inox des réservoirs plus de l’ordre de cinq mille tonnes de méthane et d’oxygène industriels sur les deux étages.
