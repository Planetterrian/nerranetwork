Top News

  1. FCC Advances 482 MHz for Starlink Mobile

    The FCC voted Thursday to advance a proposal opening another 482 MHz of spectrum to satellite direct-to-phone service, raising the eligible bands from five to 13. The vote gives Starlink Mobile a wider potential path to compete with AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile, though no spectrum has been assigned yet. The carriers want 290 MHz of that total removed, while SpaceX is asking for more. The ruling arrives as the Starlink constellation stands at 13069 satellites launched, 11190 in orbit and 11176 working. Socrates lists 7 upcoming conjunctions involving Starlink satellites among its top-probability events. None rate as high risk, and 2 carry upstream numbers that are physically implausible, so treat those as stale or erroneous reports rather than real collision odds. Separately, 2 Starlink satellites have active reentry predictions as they decay toward planned disposal. The FCC adopted a notice of proposed rulemaking that would expand Supplemental Coverage from Space from five bands to 13. A companion item proposes auctioning 25 MHz in the 1675 to 1695 MHz and 2020 to 2025 MHz bands, usable for terrestrial service, direct-to-device service or both. Comments are due 60 days after the notice appears in the Federal Register. AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile, through CTIA, asked the commission to drop the 2.5 GHz and 3.45 GHz bands, which account for 290 MHz of the 482 MHz. T-Mobile argued satellite interference could eliminate up to 96% of a cell site’s uplink coverage area. SpaceX asked the FCC to add the 902 to 928 MHz band. The adopted text had not been released at the time of reporting, so it is not yet clear whether any bands changed before the vote. Source: keeptrack.space

  2. Cursor Discloses SSH Backdoor Attempt in Malicious Claude Code Extension

    Cursor disclosed a security incident involving a maliciously modified version of the Anthropic.claude-code extension in its VS Code extension Marketplace. The package was not developed by Anthropic and ran code attempting to install a persistent SSH backdoor, affecting at most 5,000 users. The malicious package, version 2.1.313, was available between about 2:27 a.m. PT and 1:35 p.m. PT on October 29. The extension collects host and user information including hostname, username, operating system, architecture, home directory path, environment variable names, working directories, the configured Git email address, and the machine’s public IP address. It attempts to add an attacker-controlled SSH key, enable the local SSH service, and open a reverse SSH tunnel, though the company said this step would fail outside of highly unusual circumstances in which Cursor was started with administrator privileges. The extension still adds the attacker’s SSH key, installs persistence, and sends host details to the attacker. For persistence, the payload installs a LaunchAgent on macOS or, on Windows, a scheduled task that attempts to run as SYSTEM with a Startup-script fallback, while background scripts retry the tunnel roughly every 60 seconds. The extension reports host details and backdoor status through HTTP POST requests to a /beacon endpoint on port 8099. Cursor removed the malicious version from the marketplace immediately upon detection and blocked further distribution, then shut down the entire marketplace as a precaution. The company audited access and confirmed no other extensions were tampered with, rotated its keys, rebuilt the marketplace, and brought it back online. Cursor worked with the cloud provider hosting the attacker’s server to take it offline, and the attacker server no longer functions, leaving the reverse shell and the host-detail POST non-functional. Source: pasqualepillitteri.it Source: business-standard.com Source: unite.ai

  3. Musk's SpaceX Offers to Buy 418 Homes Near Mississippi Data Center, With a Lawsuit Waiver Attached

    Real estate agents working on behalf of the business have been approaching property owners in Colonial Hills, a neighborhood in Southaven, with offers covering as many as 418 homes. The community sits roughly half a mile from the power plant that supplies electricity to the company's data centers in the area. The buyout carries a condition that sellers must agree not to join the class-action litigation over noise from the gas turbines that run the facility's GPUs. The subsidiary operating the Colossus II site, MZX, sent an open letter to residents saying it "requires property for its business purposes." Southaven Mayor Darren Musselwhite said the acquired homes would house SpaceX and xAI staff as the companies expand in the region. He described the program as serving several ends at once, providing residences for employees while acting as "a goodwill gesture from a good corporate citizen for those who perceive the industrial development near them as negative." The mayor also said the purchases are not meant to turn Colonial Hills into a company town. Musk has been staying in the neighborhood in an Airstream trailer four nights a week, personally overseeing the build-out of capacity needed to power the Grok chatbot. The dispute centers on the gas turbines behind the Colossus I and Colossus II sites, which residents say generate persistent noise and air pollution. The facilities have been criticized for installing turbines without permits; a former Duke Energy generating site nearby hosts dozens of temporary gas units supplying the data centers. The company has said it will remove all unpermitted turbines within a year as its 1.2-gigawatt power plant comes online. That commitment does not address the noise complaints. SpaceX and xAI have denied the allegations and say they have taken steps to limit the impact on the surrounding area, including adjusting power output and erecting sound-dampening walls. Engineering Deep Dive

Direct-to-cell service on Starlink requires closing the link budget gap between a phone’s modest transmit power and the satellite’s receiver sensitivity across hundreds of kilometers. Adding 482 MHz of spectrum directly multiplies the available bandwidth, which raises the achievable data rate for a given signal-to-noise ratio without demanding higher transmit power from the handset. The FCC decision expands eligible bands from five to thirteen, giving operators more options to avoid interference with terrestrial networks while selecting frequencies that suffer less atmospheric or foliage loss. Carriers have asked to remove 290 MHz in the 2.5 GHz and 3.45 GHz ranges, arguing satellite signals could wipe out up to 96 percent of a cell site’s uplink coverage, so the final band plan will determine how much spectrum Starlink Mobile can actually use without coordination overhead. Once assigned, the extra bandwidth also allows tighter frequency reuse across the constellation’s spot beams, raising overall system capacity for voice and low-rate IoT traffic that must compete with terrestrial 5G. Regulatory approval of the notice of proposed rulemaking therefore sets the practical limit on how quickly the service can scale beyond emergency messaging into routine mobile broadband.

The constellation currently operates with 13069 satellites launched and 11176 working, providing a dense enough mesh that additional spectrum can be partitioned into narrower spot beams without sacrificing coverage density. Each new megahertz of bandwidth can be reused across non-overlapping beams, so the effective capacity gain scales with both the raw spectrum addition and the number of simultaneous beams the phased-array antennas on the satellites can form. Because the satellites already carry inter-satellite laser links, traffic from a direct-to-cell user can be routed to the nearest ground station or handed off to another satellite without returning to the same beam, further multiplying the value of every allocated hertz. The 25 MHz companion auction in the 1675–1695 MHz and 2020–2025 MHz bands introduces a hybrid terrestrial-satellite option that could let carriers test shared use before full satellite deployment, reducing the coordination burden that currently limits uplink reliability. SpaceX’s request to add the 902–928 MHz band would place service in a lower-frequency segment that penetrates buildings and foliage more effectively than the higher bands under discussion, trading some bandwidth efficiency for improved link margin on handheld devices. The 60-day comment window after Federal Register publication will therefore decide not only which frequencies are available but also the interference-protection rules that determine whether the service can operate at scale inside existing cellular footprints.

The engineering constraint is ultimately power-limited rather than spectrum-limited: a typical smartphone radiates only a few hundred milliwatts, so every decibel of path loss or interference directly reduces the sustainable data rate. Wider contiguous spectrum lets the system employ wider carriers or more aggressive coding that extracts more bits per joule at the handset, offsetting the fundamental disadvantage versus a terrestrial base station only a few hundred meters away. The current 7 conjunction warnings and 2 active reentries tracked by Socrates illustrate that the constellation is already dense enough for frequent hand-offs; adding spectrum simply gives each hand-off more usable channels rather than requiring more satellites. Final band assignments will therefore dictate both the near-term service footprint and the long-term economics of scaling direct-to-cell to millions of unmodified phones.

Fermer une liaison du téléphone vers le satellite, du point de vue de l’ingénierie, c’est un combat contre la distance et les milliwatts, pas un manque de pylônes.

Un téléphone de poche n’envoie que quelques centaines de milliwatts d’énergie radio, alors qu’un site cellulaire terrestre se trouve à quelques centaines de mètres et peut tourner à quelques dizaines de watts.

L’affaiblissement en espace libre à quelques gigahertz sur des centaines de kilomètres, en calcul de coin de table, est de l’ordre de cent cinquante décibels, donc la puissance reçue fond d’un facteur d’environ dix puissance quinze.

Le bruit thermique dans une bande d’un hertz se situe près de moins cent soixante-quatorze décibels-milliwatts, un plancher de manuel, donc chaque décibel de plus d’affaiblissement entame les bits que vous pouvez faire passer.

La formule de Shannon dit que, dans le régime de rapport signal sur bruit modéré, davantage de hertz au même rapport fait monter le débit sans demander au combiné de crier plus fort.

Dans la limite extrêmement contrainte en puissance, la capacité tend vers un plafond fixé par la puissance reçue sur la densité de bruit, donc le spectre en plus aide davantage comme canaux plus propres et comme réutilisation des mêmes hertz dans des faisceaux ponctuels qui ne se recouvrent pas, autrement dit des empreintes radio étroites au sol.

Chaque nouveau mégahertz peut être peint en même temps sur une autre parcelle de Terre, donc la capacité du système croît à la fois avec la largeur de bande et avec le nombre de faisceaux simultanés que les antennes à réseau phasé du satellite peuvent former.

Parce que les satellites portent déjà des liaisons laser intersatellites, les bits d’un utilisateur en direct vers le téléphone peuvent quitter le faisceau de desserte et voyager sur le maillage jusqu’à une station sol, au lieu de se battre pour un chemin de retour sur la même fréquence.

Le chiffre baguette magique, c’est les hertz réutilisés d’un faisceau à l’autre plutôt que des milliwatts en plus dans votre poche, et l’indice de l’idiotie, c’est ce gouffre de quinze ordres de grandeur que le codage, le gain d’antenne et la réutilisation de fréquences doivent combler.

Des canaux contigus plus larges laissent le modem extraire davantage de bits par joule au combiné, le seul levier qui n’augmente pas la puissance d’émission du téléphone, tandis que les attributions à plus basse fréquence pénètrent les bâtiments et le feuillage avec moins de pertes.

Avant de nous quitter — la prochaine étape datée sur les bandes satellite vers téléphone, c’est la parution de l’avis au Federal Register, qui ouvre la période de commentaires.

Closing a phone-to-satellite link, from an engineering standpoint, is a fight against distance and milliwatts, not a shortage of towers. A handheld phone puts out only a few hundred milliwatts of radio energy, while a terrestrial cell site sits a few hundred meters away and can run a few tens of watts. Free-space path loss at a couple of gigahertz across hundreds of kilometers is, on a back-of-envelope figure, on the order of one hundred fifty decibels, so received power shrinks by a factor of about ten to the fifteen. Thermal noise in a one-hertz bandwidth sits near minus one hundred seventy-four dBm, a textbook floor, so every extra decibel of path loss eats into the bits you can push. Shannon's formula says that in the moderate signal-to-noise regime, more hertz at the same ratio raises the data rate without asking the handset to shout louder. In the extremely power-limited limit, capacity approaches a ceiling set by received power over noise density, so extra spectrum helps more as cleaner channels and as reuse of the same hertz in non-overlapping spot beams, meaning narrow radio footprints on the ground. Each new megahertz can be painted onto a different patch of Earth at the same time, so system capacity scales with both bandwidth and the number of simultaneous beams the satellite's phased-array antennas can form. Because the satellites already carry inter-satellite laser links, a direct-to-cell user's bits can leave the serving beam and ride the mesh to a ground station instead of fighting for a return path in the same frequency. The magic-wand number is hertz reused across beams rather than extra milliwatts in your pocket, and the Idiot Index is that fifteen-orders-of-magnitude gap that coding, antenna gain, and frequency reuse have to close.
