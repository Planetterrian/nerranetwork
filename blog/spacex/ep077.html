Top News

  1. SpaceX and a new Trump order that could rewrite the next decade of launches

    2. The order directs federal agencies to identify sites on federal land suitable for heavy and super-heavy launch capability. It responds to projected growth in launch demand that exceeds current pad capacity at existing ranges. SpaceX has already demonstrated rapid reuse on Falcon vehicles and is scaling Starship flight tests, both of which benefit from additional orbital-class pads. The policy also calls for streamlined environmental reviews that could shorten the timeline from site selection to first launch. Reports suggest the changes target both commercial and national-security missions. Existing ranges at Vandenberg and Cape Canaveral would gain redundancy from any new federal site approved under the directive. Source: teslarati.com

  2. SpaceX is hiring for natural gas trading to support energy needs

    3. The company posted roles focused on procuring and trading natural gas to meet power-generation requirements at its Texas facilities. Starbase and the planned Gigabay expansions rely on on-site generation for test stands and production lines. Natural-gas supply directly affects the ability to run multiple Raptor test cells and Starship static-fire campaigns in parallel. The hiring indicates SpaceX is internalizing fuel procurement rather than relying solely on third-party suppliers. On-site generation capacity must scale with the number of simultaneous engine tests and vehicle stacking operations. Source: businesstimes.com.sg

  3. Cape Canaveral studying impact of future Starship launches

    4. Local officials are evaluating infrastructure, noise, and environmental effects ahead of potential Starship operations from the Florida coast. The study examines road closures, public-safety zones, and compatibility with existing Falcon and Atlas launches. Starship's larger propellant load and higher thrust profile create different ground-impact footprints than current vehicles. Findings will inform whether new exclusion zones or mitigation measures are required. The assessment also reviews compatibility with ongoing Dragon crew and cargo rotations at the same range. Source: mynews13.com

  4. Trump's space transportation policy calls for new spaceport on federal land

    5. The administration states that existing ranges cannot accommodate the expected surge in heavy-lift missions. Officials noted the need for at least one additional site rated for super-heavy vehicles. The policy pairs new site development with updated licensing processes intended to reduce multi-year delays. SpaceX's current Starbase and Vandenberg operations would gain redundancy if a third federal site is approved. The directive explicitly references super-heavy launch capability as a requirement for the new location. Source: arstechnica.com

  5. SpaceX scales back plans for next Starship launch

    6. The company adjusted the test objectives for the upcoming integrated flight test, reducing the number of new hardware demonstrations. The change follows data review from the prior flight and focuses remaining risk on the booster catch hardware. Ship and booster vehicles remain in flow at Starbase, with the next static-fire campaign still scheduled before flight. The revised manifest keeps the program on track for an orbital attempt before year-end. Booster recovery hardware and Ship reentry systems continue parallel qualification testing. Source: spacenews.com

  6. NASA contracted SpaceX for $843 million to develop the U.S. Deorbit Vehicle

    7. The contract covers design and production of a vehicle to safely deorbit the 945,000-pound International Space Station after crew departure. SpaceX will adapt Dragon-derived systems to control the station's reentry corridor and limit debris footprint. The award adds a new high-mass, controlled-reentry mission to the company's manifest. Work will run in parallel with ongoing Crew Dragon and cargo rotations. The vehicle must manage the station's mass and attitude during the final deorbit burn sequence. Source: ecoticias.com

  7. Nvidia joins Starcloud’s $250 million orbital data center funding round

    8. The round values the orbital-compute startup at $2.3 billion post-money. Starcloud plans to place GPU clusters in low Earth orbit and use laser links for data return. Nvidia's participation signals hardware-level interest in space-based inference nodes that could later integrate with Starlink constellations. The funding supports prototype satellite builds and ground-segment testing. Laser crosslinks would enable tasking and results delivery through existing Starlink ground stations. Source: spacenews.com

  8. Orbital data center startup Starcloud valued at US$2.3 billion in latest funding

    9. The valuation reflects investor expectations for on-orbit AI workloads that avoid terrestrial power and cooling constraints. Starcloud's architecture relies on frequent launch cadence to replace short-lifetime compute nodes. The round includes participation from both traditional venture funds and semiconductor partners. Frequent replacement cycles would depend on reliable Starship or Falcon launch availability to maintain constellation capacity. Source: bnnbloomberg.ca

Engineering Deep Dive

SpaceX's decision to fabricate Starship's smallest structural brackets and fittings from the same 304L-derived stainless as the primary tanks reduces the number of material transitions that must be qualified for cryogenic and reentry environments. By keeping the alloy family consistent, the company avoids separate heat-treatment and passivation protocols that would otherwise multiply inspection steps on the production line. The raw-material cost floor for these parts is dominated by the price of nickel and chromium plus the energy required to roll and anneal sheet stock; once formed, the finished brackets cost several times that baseline because each still requires precision laser cutting, forming dies, and weld-prep machining. That Idiot Index gap shrinks when the same forming tools and weld schedules are reused across thousands of identical brackets per vehicle. The approach also simplifies the heat-shield attachment interface, because tile studs and brackets share the same coefficient of thermal expansion as the tank wall, reducing differential expansion loads during ascent heating and descent plasma exposure. Watching how quickly these small-part processes scale inside the Gigabay will indicate whether the overall vehicle production rate can match the targeted flight cadence. Consistent alloy selection further supports rapid iteration on bracket geometry without introducing new qualification campaigns for each design tweak.
