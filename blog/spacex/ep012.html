  2. Report: Kennedy Space Center not ready for era of super heavy rockets: Ars Technica

    3. SpaceX has informed NASA of plans to launch Starship every eight days from Kennedy. Current pad infrastructure, propellant storage, and road networks fall short of that cadence. The assessment highlights needed upgrades to deluge systems and lightning protection. No schedule for those upgrades has been released. Source: arstechnica.com

  3. SpaceX is building a factory for 1,000 Starships a year: Vozpopuli

    4. Construction targets an annual output of one thousand vehicles through assembly-line methods rather than traditional aerospace hand-build approaches. Community Buzz

LabPadre’s June 22 Starbase update shows continued work on the orbital launch mount and flame trench modifications ahead of the next Ship static fire. Observers note new cabling runs along the tower that may support additional sensor suites. The post also flags ongoing heat-shield tile application on Ship 39. No timeline for the next full stack rollout was given.

A Reddit thread discusses viewing angles for an upcoming Falcon 9 launch from Morro Bay. Posters compare sightlines to previous Vandenberg flights and debate whether the distance justifies staying put or driving south. Several users posted photos from prior events showing the plume visible over the hills.

Arizona residents posted early reactions to the nighttime Falcon 9 advisory, with some sharing apps that predict boom arrival times. Comments focus on whether the trajectory will produce audible events farther east than usual.

The Counterpoint

Kennedy Space Center infrastructure is not yet sized for the eight-day Starship cadence SpaceX has discussed with NASA. Propellant lines, road access, and lightning protection all require upgrades that have no announced schedule. The gap leaves open the question of whether Florida operations can support the rapid-reuse goals set for the vehicle. Resolution depends on coordinated NASA and SpaceX investment decisions still ahead.

AI & Compute

Elon Musk’s Terafab project targets placement of one terawatt of AI compute hardware in orbit aboard Starship vehicles. The concept would rely on Starship’s payload capacity and eventual refueling architecture to deliver and service the orbital clusters. Source: Google News

Reflection AI signed a computing-power agreement with SpaceX for access at the Colossus data center. The startup will receive capacity on NVIDIA GB300 chips housed in the Mississippi facility. Source: Google News

SpaceX confirmed a third large compute deal at Colossus, again supplying GB300 capacity to an external customer. Monthly payments under the new contract begin July 1. Source: teslarati.com

Engineering Deep Dive

Starfall’s reentry capsule trades the large pressurized volume of Dragon for a compact disk shape optimized solely for cargo return. The geometry reduces the heat-shield area that must survive peak heating while still providing enough internal space for manufactured optics or crystals. By shedding crew systems and life-support mass, the design lowers the propellant budget needed for de-orbit, directly attacking the cost per kilogram returned from orbit. If the first flight’s thermal-protection data hold, the same capsule could fly multiple times with only tile inspection between missions, collapsing the Idiot Index that currently multiplies raw-material cost by the price of a single-use vehicle. The next engineering milestone to watch is whether the recovery system can achieve land landing accuracy tight enough to avoid expensive at-sea retrieval.
