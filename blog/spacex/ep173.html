Top News

  1. Amazon accelerates Falcon 9 launches after Vulcan failure

    Amazon told Ars Technica it is moving its next Falcon 9 launch for the Amazon Leo constellation from 2027 to December, with additional flights to follow quickly. The shift follows the November 15 Vulcan failure that destroyed 40 Amazon Leo satellites and the ongoing grounding of ULA’s Vulcan. An Ariane 6 launch on Tuesday added 36 more satellites, bringing the fleet to 432. SpaceX has already flown three batches of 24 Amazon satellites on Falcon 9 and holds ten more reserved flights. Amazon originally booked 108 launches across Western rockets to deploy 3,232 satellites, with seventeen of those flights completed and one failure. Blue Origin’s New Glenn remains grounded since May after a pad explosion, leaving Falcon 9 and Ariane 6 as the only active vehicles for Amazon hardware. Source: keeptrack.space Source: arstechnica.com

  2. SpaceX launches USSF-266 classified payload from Vandenberg

    A Falcon 9 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base carrying a classified reconnaissance payload for the U.S. Space Force. The first stage, on its 29th flight, landed on the Of Course I Still Love You droneship. The mission had been scheduled for the prior day and lifted off after propellant loading began under favorable weather. SpaceX posted video of the ascent and confirmed the booster touched down on the droneship. Source: spacex.com Source: x.com Source: space.com

  3. STARLINK-2558 drops 85 km in 89 days

    The satellite now sits 150 km below its assigned working shell after losing 85 km of altitude over 89 days. Nine Starlink satellites currently carry active reentry predictions, though none appear on the high-risk conjunction list. No Starlink satellites show in the current SOCRATES top-probability conjunction list. Source: keeptrack.space

  4. SpaceX forgoes up to $738 million in BEAD subsidies

    SpaceX will still serve every rural location awarded under the Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment program at no cost to the program after declining the federal funds. Reports place the forgone amount between $636 million and $738 million. The company gave up the subsidies but keeps the awarded service locations. Source: teslarati.com

  5. xAI expects Colossus wastewater plant restart in January

    The Memphis facility is now projected to resume operations in January after the earlier pause. Local officials are tracking the timeline for the recycling plant tied to the growing compute campus. Source: neoteo.com

  6. Northrop confirms additional Falcon 9 flight for Cygnus after NG-25

    Northrop Grumman has booked another SpaceX launch for a Cygnus cargo mission following the NG-25 flight. The new mission will continue the station resupply cadence using Falcon 9. Source: teslanorth.com

  7. SpaceX plans Sunday Falcon 9 launch of holiday supplies to ISS

    The company is preparing a Falcon 9 mission from Florida to deliver holiday meals and other cargo to the International Space Station. The flight is listed for this Sunday. Source: talkoftitusville.com

  8. Goldman Sachs projects sustained U.S. data-center power and gas demand through 2028

    The bank expects the buildout to keep lifting electricity and natural-gas consumption as new facilities come online. The forecast covers the period through the end of the decade. Source: idahostatesman.com

Engineering Deep Dive

Falcon 9’s first-stage recovery relies on a single Merlin engine performing what engineers call a hoverslam. The engine cannot throttle below roughly 40 percent without losing the ability to adjust thrust, so the booster must begin its landing burn at 40–70 percent throttle while still moving fast enough for the remaining impulse to bring velocity to zero at the exact moment of touchdown. This single-engine constraint forces a precise, continuous throttle profile with no option to drop to a lower setting mid-burn.

Super Heavy takes a different path. It lights thirteen Raptors to cancel most of its velocity, then reduces to the center three engines for the final approach. Each of those three can still modulate between roughly 40 percent and full thrust, giving the vehicle a near-hover capability the tower arms require for capture. The staged burn therefore trades engine count for finer control authority at the moment of contact.

The difference shows up in hardware choices. Falcon 9 keeps landing legs because a single-engine hoverslam leaves little margin for position error. Super Heavy deletes the legs entirely, saving mass that would otherwise be carried to orbit on every flight. The trade-off is that the catch mechanism must now absorb the final positioning task that legs once handled.

Sur le front de l’intelligence artificielle, l’installation de turbines à gaz de X-A-I à Memphis est citée comme un modèle de centre de données d’intelligence artificielle hors réseau qui s’affranchit des contraintes classiques des services publics, note Crypto Briefing, afin que les ensembles de calcul de classe Colossus puissent monter en puissance pendant que le réseau électrique rattrape son retard.

La récupération du premier étage du Falcon 9, c’est l’angle technique d’un seul Merlin qui exécute ce que les ingénieurs appellent un atterrissage en percée.

Le moteur perd la capacité de moduler s’il descend sous environ quarante pour cent de régime, donc le booster doit allumer la combustion d’atterrissage déjà entre quarante et soixante-dix pour cent alors qu’il va encore vite, et l’impulsion qu’il reste doit ramener la vitesse à zéro à l’instant exact du toucher.

Ce plancher à un seul moteur, voilà pourquoi le profil doit être continu : il n’y a pas de régime plus bas vers lequel descendre au milieu de la combustion.

Si la poussée minimale pousse encore plus fort que le poids du booster, on ne peut pas faire du vol stationnaire ; on ne peut que percuter, en faisant correspondre l’énergie restante à la distance restante pour que les deux tombent à zéro ensemble.

Le Super Heavy n’essaie pas de faire ça avec un seul moteur ; il allume treize Raptor pour annuler l’essentiel de la vitesse, puis bascule sur les trois moteurs centraux pour l’approche finale.

Chacun de ces trois module encore entre environ quarante pour cent et la pleine poussée, ce qui donne au booster Super Heavy un quasi-vol stationnaire dont les bras de la tour ont besoin pour la capture.

Passer de treize moteurs à trois, c’est de l’ordre d’une réduction de poussée par quatre, le frein grossier, puis le trio restant assure le contrôle fin.

Trop de moteurs à la fin garderaient même un plancher à quarante pour cent trop fort pour un vol stationnaire ; trois moteurs ramènent ce plancher dans la plage avec laquelle les bras peuvent travailler.

Trois moteurs peuvent aussi être régulés un peu inégalement, donc le booster Super Heavy peut corriger une légère inclinaison qu’un seul Merlin, qui pousse uniquement le long de son propre axe, ne peut pas corriger.

Le Falcon 9 conserve donc ses pieds d’atterrissage, parce qu’un atterrissage en percée à un seul moteur laisse peu de marge si l’on n’est pas au bon endroit, tandis que le Super Heavy supprime les pieds et économise la masse qui, autrement, irait en orbite à chaque fois.

Le matériel de capture doit désormais assurer le dernier bout de positionnement que les pieds assuraient.

Спасение первой ступени Falcon 9 — это инженерный сюжет про один Merlin, который делает то, что инженеры называют жёстким гашением скорости у самой земли.

Двигатель теряет возможность регулировать тягу, если уходит ниже примерно сорока процентов, поэтому ускоритель обязан зажигать посадочный прожиг уже на сорока–семидесяти процентах, пока ещё летит быстро, и оставшийся импульс должен обнулить скорость ровно в миг касания.

Этот нижний порог одного двигателя — вот почему профиль должен быть непрерывным: посередине прожига некуда сбросить тягу ниже.

Если минимальная тяга всё ещё сильнее, чем весит ускоритель, зависать нельзя; можно только ударить, совместив остаток энергии с остатком расстояния так, чтобы оба пришли к нулю вместе.

Super Heavy так одним двигателем не работает: он зажигает тринадцать Raptor, чтобы снять большую часть скорости, затем переходит на три центральных двигателя на финальном участке.

Каждый из этих трёх по-прежнему регулируется примерно от сорока процентов до полной тяги, и это даёт ускорителю Super Heavy почти зависание, которое нужно стрелам башни для захвата.

Переход с тринадцати двигателей на три — это примерно четырёхкратное снижение тяги, грубый тормоз, а оставшаяся тройка берёт тонкое управление.

Слишком много двигателей в конце оставило бы даже сорокапроцентный минимум слишком мощным для зависания; три двигателя заводят этот минимум в диапазон, с которым могут работать стрелы.

Три двигателя можно чуть-чуть дросселировать неравномерно, так что ускоритель Super Heavy может поправить небольшой крен, который один Merlin, толкающий только вдоль своей оси, исправить не может.

Поэтому Falcon 9 сохраняет посадочные опоры: однодвигательное гашение у земли оставляет мало запаса, если ты не там, где надо, а Super Heavy убирает опоры и экономит массу, которая иначе каждый раз улетала бы на орбиту.

Falcon 9 first-stage recovery is the engineering angle on a single Merlin performing what engineers call a hoverslam. The engine loses the ability to modulate if it goes below roughly forty percent throttle, so the booster has to light the landing burn already at forty to seventy percent while it is still going fast, and whatever impulse is left has to bring speed to zero at the exact instant of touchdown. That single-engine floor is why the profile has to be continuous: there is no lower setting to drop to in the middle of the burn. If the minimum throttle still pushes harder than the booster weighs, you cannot hover; you can only slam, matching leftover energy against leftover distance so both hit zero together. Super Heavy does not try to do that with one engine; it lights thirteen Raptors to cancel most of the speed, then cuts to the three center engines for the final approach. Each of those three still modulates between roughly forty percent and full thrust, which gives the Super Heavy booster a near-hover the tower arms need for capture. Going from thirteen engines down to three is on the order of a fourfold cut in thrust, the coarse brake, and then the remaining trio handles the fine control. Too many engines at the end would keep even a forty percent floor too strong for a hover; three engines bring that floor into the band the arms can work with. Three engines can also be throttled a little unequally, so the Super Heavy booster can correct a small lean that a single Merlin, thrusting only along its own axis, cannot. Falcon 9 therefore keeps landing legs, because a one-engine hoverslam leaves little margin for being in the wrong place, while Super Heavy deletes the legs and saves the mass that would otherwise fly to orbit every time. The catch hardware now has to do the last bit of positioning the legs used to do. And that's a wrap on today's SpaceX developments.
