    The flight lofted 27 Starlink satellites into orbit.   3. SpaceX opened a commercial storefront for its Starfall microgravity capsule

    The new page at spacex.com/starfall lists up to 1,000 kg payload capacity, 1 kW power, and continuous Ethernet connectivity via Starlink. Missions target biomedical organoid growth, uniform protein crystals for drug development, and semiconductor materials processing. The capsule is a disk about 10.2 feet wide and 2.5 feet tall that can return 2,200 pounds of cargo. Three commercial Starfall missions were signed in the third quarter. The service launches on Starship to provide weeks of continuous microgravity instead of seconds from drop towers or parabolic flights. Source: teslarati.com

  4. Astranis upgraded its fourth Falcon 9 contract to a dedicated 2028 mission

    The flight will carry the entire six-satellite Block 4 production run directly into geostationary orbit using an Impulse Helios upper stage. This brings Astranis’ total fleet to 16 satellites and expands capacity for commercial and government customers. The amendment gives Astranis full payload capacity on the mission. The satellites will support communications and space domain awareness missions. Source: cyprusshippingnews.com

  5. SpaceX is exploring a deal with Boost Mobile to expand Starlink Mobile service

    Talks center on either acquiring the carrier or accessing its existing network infrastructure. The move would accelerate direct-to-cell coverage for mobile users. Source: grafa.com

  6. Russia is concealing anti-Starlink jammers inside parking lots and trailers along the front lines

    Ukrainian drone expert Serhii Beskrestnov identified Volna, Kupol, and Garant systems hidden in civilian-style locations. The placements aim to disrupt Starlink terminals supporting Ukrainian operations. Source: businessinsider.com

  7. A viral video of a Starship launch is not an Iranian ICBM test

    Three technical proofs distinguish the footage as a U.S. Starship flight rather than a foreign ballistic missile. The clip originated from a SpaceX test campaign. Source: shattered.io

  8. Eight launches are scheduled worldwide this week across four countries

    Rocket Lab’s Electron completed its 99th mission on November 2 from New Zealand, delivering the QPS-SAR-20 satellite. China plans four flights including a Kinetica 2 on November 3 from Jiuquan, while South Korea’s Mir vehicle is set for November 4 from the ADD Offshore platform. Two U.S. Space Force missions are slated for Falcon 9 from Vandenberg Space Force Base beginning November 5. Source: nasaspaceflight.com

Engineering Deep Dive

The Starfall capsule’s disk geometry directly addresses the dominant heat-flux and structural loads of reentry while preserving a large internal volume for microgravity experiments. A flat, low-aspect-ratio shape spreads the stagnation-point heating over a wider area than a traditional blunt cone, lowering peak heat flux per square meter and allowing simpler tile layouts on the windward face. With a stated diameter of roughly 3.1 meters and height of 0.76 meters, the vehicle can return 1,000 kg while still fitting inside Starship’s payload bay without requiring an extended fairing. Continuous 1 kW power and Starlink Ethernet during the orbital phase eliminate the usual post-splashdown data-recovery delay, letting researchers monitor crystal growth or organoid development in real time and adjust parameters mid-mission. Because the capsule returns intact rather than burning up, the same hardware can fly repeatedly, collapsing the per-kilogram cost of microgravity access from the thousands of dollars typical of parabolic flights or drop towers toward the marginal cost of propellant and integration only. That reuse loop is the same first-principles lever that made Falcon 9 economics viable: recover the largest single hardware element and fly it again before its thermal-protection or structural margins are exhausted.

The power and data architecture inside Starfall further reduces the effective cost per experiment by keeping the payload online for the entire mission duration. Instead of waiting for physical recovery and ground processing, investigators can stream data through the Ethernet link and Starlink constellation, enabling closed-loop control of temperature, agitation, or nutrient flow while the crystals or organoids are still forming. This capability turns a passive return vehicle into an active orbital laboratory whose marginal operating cost is dominated by the electricity draw of the 1 kW bus and the incremental propellant mass needed to carry the extra hardware. When the same capsule flies multiple times, the amortized cost of the thermal-protection system and structure drops sharply, exactly as booster reuse drove down Falcon 9 marginal launch cost once flight rates exceeded a handful per year.

From a manufacturing standpoint, the disk form factor also simplifies integration with Starship’s payload bay. The 10.2-foot diameter and 2.5-foot height allow the capsule to sit flat on a standard payload adapter without custom cradles or fairing extensions, cutting both integration time and the risk of damage during stacking. Three commercial missions already signed in the third quarter indicate that paying customers see the same economic signal: once the vehicle is recovered and reflown, the dominant remaining expense is the Starship propellant and the small incremental mass of the capsule itself. That cost structure is what makes routine microgravity manufacturing plausible at scale rather than a one-off demonstration.
