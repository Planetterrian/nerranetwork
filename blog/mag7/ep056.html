More than 50,000 CUDA cores define Nvidia’s Rubin HPC for science, shipping in 2028.

By Patrick November 17, 2026 Ep 56 9 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top News Company Desk Also Today The Counterpoint The Thread: A desk, a partner cloud, a chip raise On the Calendar The Tape

What You Need to Know: Nvidia used Supercomputing 2026 to unveil Rubin HPC, a GPU it says carries more than 50,000 CUDA cores and will ship in 2028 for scientific simulation. The current Rubin tops out at 28,672 CUDA cores. A Commerce Department license rule on remote chip access, a new Nvidia board committee, and an Apple memory contract sit beside that roadmap.

Top News

Nvidia unveils Rubin HPC with more than 50,000 CUDA cores: AI Weekly

Nvidia used Supercomputing 2026 to tee up Rubin HPC, a GPU with more than 50,000 CUDA cores that it says will accelerate agentic workflows and simulation for scientists, engineers, and analysts working with large data sets. The current Rubin tops out at 28,672 CUDA cores. Nvidia is framing the part as a balanced design, pairing more compute with faster node-to-node movement over NVLink 7, which retains the 3.6 TB/s of per-GPU bandwidth already shipping on NVLink 6. Tom’s Hardware reports the design likely reuses a four-die layout similar to Rubin Ultra, linked by NVLink-C2C. Nvidia says Rubin HPC will arrive sometime in 2028. Ian Buck, vice president of hyperscale and HPC, said faster simulation means faster discovery. Source: aiweekly.co

US requires licenses for some Nvidia chip sales, and curbs remote access: Bloomberg Technology

The Trump administration moved to restrict foreign countries from giving Chinese companies remote access to advanced AI chips and servers, amid scrutiny of semiconductor export controls following an alleged Nvidia espionage scandal. Several Southeast Asian nations, along with Brazil, will need a US government license to purchase advanced AI chips from Nvidia and other American manufacturers, according to an interim rule released by the US Commerce Department. Source: bloomberg.com · bloomberg.com

Nvidia’s board adds an export-control committee: NVIDIA Newsroom

Nvidia’s board established a standing National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee for board-level oversight of national security policies, export-control compliance, and global trade risk management. Director Suzanne Nora Johnson will chair it, with directors Stephen C. Neal and John O. Dabiri as members. The committee will meet at least quarterly, including with management and in executive session, and will retain independent outside experts in law, national security, and trade compliance. The existing National Security Team and Global Trade Compliance Team will report to the committee and to interim CEO Colette Kress on day-to-day operations. Chairman Harvey Jones said Nvidia has a responsibility to help safeguard national security while supporting continued innovation. The Financial Times reports the move comes as the company faces criticism in Washington over its ambition to increase revenues in China, after the US moved to cut off China’s access to Nvidia’s most powerful AI chips. Source: nvidianews.nvidia.com · ft.com

Apple nears a four-to-five-year DRAM deal with Samsung: MacRumors

Apple is close to signing a long-term DRAM supply agreement with Samsung, Korea’s Maeil Business Newspaper reported. The deal would run for four to five years from the first half of 2027, set a minimum supply volume, and leave prices to be finalized later based on market conditions. Apple usually favors short-term contracts and presses suppliers on price. It recently signed a similar deal with SK hynix on supplier-favorable terms as a worsening memory shortage pushes up component costs amid the AI data-center buildout. Source: macrumors.com

Blackstone takes 75 percent of an AWS data-center portfolio: Telecompaper

Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust agreed to acquire a 75 percent majority interest in two joint ventures holding data-center assets leased to Amazon Web Services, its first investment since listing on the New York Stock Exchange. The transaction values the portfolio at approximately $5 billion. It covers 13 newly constructed powered-shell buildings across two AWS campuses in the United States, representing 647 MW of IT capacity. Source: telecompaper.com

Company Desk

Google opens a sovereign-AI accelerator for Europe and Israel: Google Cloud

Google Cloud opened applications for the Google for Startups Accelerator: Sovereign AI, an equity-free, 15-week hybrid program in Europe and Israel for late-seed to post-Series A startups building AI for European regulatory environments. The blog describes artificial intelligence as an estimated €1.2 trillion economic opportunity for Europe, and points founders at EU AI Act, NIS2, and DORA requirements plus national frameworks such as SecNumCloud. Defense applications are excluded. The cohort Google wants is aimed at government and public services, healthcare and life sciences, regulated finance, and civilian cybersecurity. Applications close on January 15, 2027. Source: cloud.google.com

Amazon’s $17 billion Ohio AI data-centre bet draws voter backlash: Financial Times Technology

Community fights over Amazon data-center plans are not new; the Financial Times now attaches them to a $17 billion Ohio AI data-centre bet threatened by voter backlash. The paper says voter revolts across the US threaten billions in AI investment as communities turn against Big Tech’s expansion. Source: ft.com

Meta tightens Instagram recommendations in India: Meta Newsroom

Meta said it has strengthened the default protections behind what Instagram recommends in India, to reduce unwanted content, guided by what users told the company they want from recommendations. Bloomberg reported that Meta removed more explicit content from Instagram in India after a series of lapses, under tougher scrutiny in a country of over half a billion users. The Times of India reported that the government has flagged risks from recommendation systems. Source: about.fb.com · bloomberg.com · timesofindia.indiatimes.com

Also Today

Amazon to invest $7 billion in renewable energy in India by 2030: About Amazon

Amazon said it will invest $7 billion in renewable energy in India by 2030 and announced four new wind and solar projects, including its largest wind farm contracted in Asia to date. Source: aboutamazon.com

Amazon sets a 65 percent electricity carbon-intensity goal: About Amazon

Amazon set a science-aligned interim target to cut electricity carbon intensity 65 percent by 2030, describing itself as a leading corporate buyer of carbon-free energy. Source: aboutamazon.com

Gemini history can stay off the training pipeline: Android Central

Android Central reports that Gemini users can now keep chat history without feeding Google’s training pipeline. Source: androidcentral.com

Gemini Skills gain files as Gems retire: Android Authority

Google is adding file attachments and sharing to Gemini Skills as Gems retire starting today. Source: androidauthority.com

Gemini adds 66 regional voices: Beebom Gadgets

Google is adding 66 new regional voices to Gemini across Android and iOS. Source: gadgets.beebom.com

DeepMind strategy chief Jagjit Sekhon joins Anthropic: TradingView

Google DeepMind strategy chief Jagjit Sekhon is joining Anthropic, TradingView reported. Source: tradingview.com

Nvidia’s robotics chief calls OpenAI’s Astra a data breakthrough: The Information

Deepu Talla, Nvidia’s robotics chief, told The Information that OpenAI’s Astra model, released in September, has made it 100 times faster to create some kinds of data for training robotic AI models and could help automate putting robots to work in factories. Source: theinformation.com

The Counterpoint

Core count meets a 3 percent benchmark: AI Weekly

Rubin HPC’s headline core count is the claim that has to survive procurement math. AI Weekly, citing the HPCG benchmark, says top supercomputers achieve only 3 percent of theoretical peak on sparse workloads, the benchmark most relevant to Rubin HPC’s target uses, and that two procurement cycles sit between this tease and silicon in 2028. The Vera CPU rack — 192 CPUs, about 300 TB of memory, and 1 PB of storage — arrives in early 2027, a full year before Rubin HPC ships. Source: aiweekly.co

The Thread: A desk, a partner cloud, a chip raise

Nvidia, Alphabet, and Meta are each trying to place advanced compute where a shared queue is not the default. Nvidia’s DGX Spark HPC Developer Kit, already on sale at SC26, links four Spark units into 512 GB of memory and 20 TB of NVMe. Each node is a GB10 Grace Blackwell Superchip with 128 GB of LPDDR5X, 6,144 CUDA cores, up to 1 PFLOP of FP4, a 140-watt thermal design power, and 273 GB/s of bandwidth. Twenty Arm cores split into 10 Cortex-X925 cores at up to 4 GHz and 10 Cortex-A725 cores at up to 2.8 GHz. ConnectX-7 joins each box to two neighbors in a ring, so the kit needs no separate switch. Nvidia says NVFP4 lets those four nodes run models of up to 700 billion parameters, a class it illustrates with DeepSeek V4.1. The Rubin Lab at the University of California, Berkeley, Nvidia says, received genomic results 15 times sooner than a workflow stuck in an 11-hour cluster queue. Berkeley’s Hartwig Group fine-tuned a 120-billion-parameter-class chemistry model on a single Spark using NVFP4.

The shared problem is who holds the machine, not who posts the largest core count. At the same conference, cuPhoton.xBeam cut Event Horizon Telescope analysis from one month to 8 minutes. KTH Royal Institute used Nemotron for Science to repair 81 percent of faulty molecular-dynamics simulations overnight; the toolkit includes Nvidia’s Nemotron 3.5 Super VL open model and the LitmusBench v0.2 dataset. TCBP-JOBS cut a design step from 12 weeks to 45 minutes. In Brussels, Google said select Gemini models are coming to Google Cloud Dedicated, operated by an independent local partner on isolated infrastructure. Select Gemini models already carry a commitment to process customer data in-region in the EU, and Gemini is already available with Google Distributed Cloud. In France, S3NS has made managed model serving generally available on PREMI3NS, so teams can deploy Mistral and Google open models, or their own, inside an environment S3NS operates. Confidential External Key Management is in preview, designed so customers or a local partner hold root keys outside Google while key operations run in a hardware-attested confidential-computing environment. A Telefónica partnership in Spain, also in preview, expands who can hold those keys. A Data Boundary framework for Switzerland is available today. A framework for the EU’s DORA act is planned for financial services.

What changes is the path, not a single delivered campus. A lab can develop on the four-node ring with Nvidia’s HPC SDK — GPU-accelerated C, C++, and Fortran plus MPI — then scale to larger Nvidia supercomputers; LAMMPS and NAMD are confirmed on the four-unit kit, and the community reports that GROMACS, OpenFOAM, and WRF also run. A European hospital or finance team can aim Gemini at a partner-operated cloud while key control is still partly a preview and Switzerland already has a mapped framework. Meta’s route is a custom memory program: a Korean outlet reports Samsung is developing HBM4E for Meta’s AI chips on a 2nm base die. Cassava Technologies is seeking to raise as much as $1 billion to buy Nvidia chips for African AI data centers, people familiar with the matter told Bloomberg — a raise being sought, not a cluster switched on. The kit is the piece already on sale, through partners including Acer, Asus, Dell, Gigabyte, H3C, HP, Lenovo, MSI, and xFusion, and through the Nvidia Marketplace.
