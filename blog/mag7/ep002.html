Hundreds of thousands of merchants can now offer Prime on their own sites at no extra cost.

By Patrick September 24, 2026 Ep 2 5 min read Listen to podcast View summaries

MAG 7 Daily

Hundreds of thousands of merchants can now offer Prime on their own sites at no extra cost.

What You Need to Know: Hundreds of thousands of merchants on Amazon’s U.S. fulfillment network can now turn on fast, free Prime delivery on their own websites at no added cost to them or their shoppers. Google is rolling Gemini audio models out to developers the same day its DeepMind chief says Gemini 4 is in refinement. Apple priced a two-phone number handoff, and Meta put a pocket Muse device and a hundred-gram headset on a launch path.

Top News

Merchants switch on Prime delivery on their own sites: About Amazon

Merchants using Amazon Multichannel Fulfillment in the U.S. can now offer fast, free Prime delivery on their own websites at no additional cost to them or their shoppers. Hundreds of thousands of merchants can activate it in minutes, with no changes to checkout, payments, or store policies. Through the new MCF Preferred Pricing Program, merchants can save up to 25% on fulfillment fees across their own websites, marketplaces, and social channels, with no contracts required. Source: aboutamazon.com

Gemini Audio models roll out to Studio, Notebook, and the API: Google

Google said new Gemini Audio models are rolling out across Google AI Studio, Gemini Notebook, and the Gemini API. The linked post is titled around Gemini 3.8 text-to-speech. Google said the models are coming soon to Google Vids and Gemini Enterprise. Source: x.com

iPhone Handoff gets a monthly price and a short carrier list: MacRumors

iPhone Handoff in iOS 27 lets the same number sit on two iPhones, active on only one at a time, and it is separate from the existing Handoff that continues an app on another Apple device. Calls, voicemail, messages — including iMessage, RCS, SMS, and MMS — and Find My location move to the phone that is unlocked, with a “Switched to this iPhone” confirmation. T-Mobile in the United States is charging customers 5 per month; Deutsche Telekom is charging €6.95 per month, but only on plans that do not already include multi-SIM, while MagentaMobil L includes one multi-SIM and XL includes two. The feature is available through T-Mobile and Telekom; Verizon and EE will support it later this year, and AT&T has not committed, with a spokesperson pointing to existing eSIM activation and transfer options. Both phones need iOS 27 or later, Wi-Fi, the same Apple Account, Messages in iCloud, and FaceTime and iMessage on that account. Stopping the service means contacting the carrier; deleting an eSIM may not stop the charge. Source: macrumors.com

DeepMind chief says Gemini 4 is in refinement: The Verge

Koray Kavukcuoglu, in his first media appearance as leader of Google’s DeepMind division, told The Information that Gemini 4 is in its refinement stage. The Verge reports Google is nearing the launch after trailing rival developers on flagship AI releases. Source: theverge.com

Apple sets an early-next-week fix for iPhone 18 Pro reboots: 9to5Mac

Apple confirmed to 9to5Mac that a software update is coming early next week for iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max phones that freeze and reboot after a failed Face ID attempt. 9to5Mac had reported the failures on Monday and read them as a software glitch rather than a hardware fault. Source: 9to5mac.com

Company Desk

Muse stays free and adds Walmart, Sephora, and Best Buy shopping: Benzinga

After Meta’s test of a human concierge for Muse, Benzinga reports Muse stays free and adds shopping with Walmart, Sephora, and Best Buy. The shopping additions are the new commercial step on the agent. Source: benzinga.com

AI factories come online as Egypt’s institute participation grows: NVIDIA Newsroom

NVIDIA said AI factories are coming online across Africa, giving developers and startups more local computing. More than 85,000 developers across the continent have trained through the NVIDIA Deep Learning Institute, and participation in Egypt grew more than 10x in one year. NVIDIA pointed to Egypt startups using that talent and infrastructure in Arabic speech, healthcare, robotics, and financial services. Source: x.com

The Thread: A hundred-gram headset against a shelved pendant

A hundred grams is the weight MacRumors gives Meta’s new VR glasses, five times lighter than the Meta Quest 3, with full-color passthrough and a 5K display. The same report says Apple has shelved plans to launch an AI pendant, while Mark Zuckerberg used a “one more thing” slot at Connect to unveil Muse Charm, a puck about the size of an Apple Watch with a tamagotchi-like avatar for talking to Muse. Zuckerberg said Meta packed the real-time voice and avatar stack into something that fits on a keychain, started by a tap on a fingerprint sensor in the corner, without unlocking a phone or opening an app, and that Charm is aimed at sale by the holiday season, with more to share later this year. The VR glasses are set to launch in the spring at a starting price of 1,300. Meta says they offer cinema-grade clarity with Dolby Vision and Dolby Atmos, up to three hours of continuous high-resolution media playback on a single charge, every game available for Quest headsets, Xbox Cloud Gaming, and a virtual keyboard and trackpad on a desk when connected to a PC or Mac. For context, MacRumors notes an M5 Apple Vision Pro weighs 750–800 grams depending on configuration. An audio-only Ray-Ban model, presented against the “pervert glasses” label, supports media playback and calls and runs for up to 12 hours, while the charging case adds up to 48 hours. The refreshed camera Ray-Ban Meta is slimmer, adds a button that activates Meta AI, and is said to have nine hours of battery versus eight on the second-generation model, plus a six-microphone array Meta says can cut out over 90 percent of background noise. The 12MP camera supports 3K video, with Dolby Atmos spatial audio support coming soon, and prices start at 449. An FDA-cleared hearing enhancement is due later this year, as part of a Meta One subscription or a one-time cost of 149, with a 30-day free trial. Source: macrumors.com
