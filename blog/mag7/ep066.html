Apple must produce 3,200 documents a judge sampled from 122,000 Epic says it hid.

By Patrick November 27, 2026 Ep 66 7 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Follow MAG 7 Daily Apple Podcasts RSS
Listen to this episode
Contents

What You Need to Know: A federal judge ordered Apple to produce a random sample of 3,200 documents for Epic Games to review, from more than 122,000 files Epic says Apple withheld in the dispute over proposed App Store commissions. Google will require many independent Android developers to publish a phone number and address worldwide under the EU Digital Services Act. Dutch groups have asked a court to halt sales of Meta’s AI glasses over the risk of secret recording.

Top News

A judge orders Apple to produce 3,200 documents for Epic: 9to5Mac

A federal judge ordered Apple to produce a random sample of 3,200 documents for Epic Games to review. Epic alleges Apple improperly withheld more than 122,000 documents related to its proposed App Store commissions. The order is a sample for that review, not a ruling on the commissions themselves. Source: 9to5mac.com

Google will publish many Android developers’ phone numbers and addresses: The Verge

From next year, many independent Android app developers will have to share a phone number and an address publicly. Google said the change is meant to meet European Union rules under the Digital Services Act, and that the contact details will be published worldwide, not only in Europe. Independent developers have raised privacy worries about that worldwide disclosure. Source: theverge.com

Dutch groups ask a court to halt Meta AI glasses sales: The Business of Fashion

Dutch groups have asked a court to halt sales of Meta’s AI glasses on privacy grounds. A related report says the court is to rule on a bid to ban those sales over the risk of secret recording. No ban is in place on the facts reported today. Source: businessoffashion.com Source: finance.biggo.com

US smartphone shipments fell 9% as Apple’s share rose to 57%: Techmeme

Counterpoint Research, via Techmeme, reported that US smartphone shipments fell 9% year over year in the third quarter as memory costs hit Android phone makers, especially at the low end. Apple’s share rose from 50% to 57%. Samsung’s share held at 25%. Source: x.com

Company Desk

Schneider Electric connects open automation to AWS: ARC Advisory Group

Schneider Electric announced a solution that integrates its Open Software-Defined Automation with Amazon Web Services, introduced at SPS 2026 in Nuremberg. Delivered through EcoStruxure Automation Expert, the software is decoupled from the underlying hardware so engineers can reuse automation applications across compatible computers. Locally executed function blocks connect to AI applications built with Amazon Bedrock and Amazon SageMaker, and AWS IoT Greengrass handles deployment and management across industrial assets and plants. The design is meant to cut reliance on gateways, protocol converters, and custom interfaces while keeping local processing for time-critical control. Schneider said potential uses include predictive maintenance, energy management, and operational optimization, and the step follows its collaboration with HPE and its EcoStruxure Foxboro software-defined automation. Source: arcweb.com Source: marketscale.com

Nvidia reinstates Jensen Huang as chief executive: Bloomberg Technology

Bloomberg reported that Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive. The Financial Times reported that a letter from Huang was dropped from the company’s messaging on ethics, and that employees, analysts, and legal experts are questioning whether management structure and governance will be reformed. Source: bloomberg.com Source: ft.com

Also Today

Microsoft is deprecating the Telnet client in Windows: PC Gamer

Microsoft said it is deprecating the Telnet client in Windows. Source: pcgamer.com

Copilot Vision is generally available: BigGo Finance

Microsoft’s Copilot Vision, which lets users talk to the AI by sharing a screen, is now generally available. Source: finance.biggo.com

No Windows 12 yet, a Windows boss says: Windows Latest

A Windows boss said there is no Windows 12 yet because Microsoft must prove its AI is useful and “earn permission,” after users pushed back on Copilot in Windows 11. Source: windowslatest.com

AWS adds virtual iPhones to its cloud: The Register

AWS added virtual iPhones to its cloud. Source: theregister.com

Groupement Mousquetaires taps Anthropic and AWS: ESM Magazine

Groupement Mousquetaires is tapping Anthropic and AWS for an AI roll-out. Source: esmmagazine.com

Westcon-Comstor becomes an authorised AWS distributor in sub-Saharan Africa: Telecompaper

Westcon-Comstor became an authorised AWS distributor for sub-Saharan Africa. Source: telecompaper.com

The Counterpoint

The sample is not a finding: 9to5Mac

The order Epic won is a random sample, not a judgment that Apple’s proposed App Store commissions were documented improperly, and not an order to turn over the rest of the file. Whether those papers show what Epic alleges is the question the sample still has to answer. No article today carries a ruling on that merits question. Source: 9to5mac.com

The Thread: Cash, land, and the partner bill

Foreign investors sold a record net $33.1 billion of Taiwanese stocks in November, topping October’s record outflow, with AI valuation fears and a broader tech sell-off as the backdrop Bloomberg reported through Techmeme. In the same set of constraints, vertical data centers are being proposed as the option where land is limited, including in Taiwan, and Kansas City recently rejected a 20-story tower, the New York Times reported through Techmeme. Neither item names a tenant or a seller among the seven. They name the market and the permit around the buildings the build needs.

Meta’s cash is the company version of that squeeze. Kalkine Media reported that capital expenditures, mostly for AI data centers and servers, exceeded cash generated from operations and pushed free cash flow negative for the first time in many years. Meta raised the low end of its full-year capital-spending forecast and paused share repurchases. The board approved a new class of publicly listed, non-voting shares, effective early next year, which the company said may be used for capital raising, employee compensation, and acquisitions. Kalkine said some investors view that class as a way to fund the AI investments without diluting existing voting control. The same report said a large legal charge tied to a settlement with state attorneys general over youth mental health and social media litigation pulled reported operating income down, that average price per ad and ad impressions both rose, that daily users across the family of apps continued to grow, and that Meta launched Muse and Muse Wonder as its first frontier models. Management guided fourth-quarter revenue roughly in line with expectations, expects Reality Labs losses to decline compared with last year, and said it plans to keep investing aggressively next year. Headcount declined meaningfully from a year earlier. Meta also disclosed very large future spending commitments tied to data centers and computing capacity. Separate from that bill, iAfrica reported a $700,000 AI grant and a learning hub covering four African countries.

Amazon is on the selling side of the same build. ClickHouse expanded its AWS strategic collaboration with a $1.5 billion-plus agreement to scale a unified enterprise AI data stack, AI Insider reported. Westcon-Comstor broke a $100 million AWS Marketplace barrier while pursuing a one-stop-shop goal, IT Channel Oxygen reported. Alphabet’s Waymo, in a smaller physical incident, said egg attacks took seven cars out of service after a Denver man allegedly blocked them with cones, the Times of India reported.

What the companies share is not a joint contract. Meta is spending ahead of the cash its operations produce, and it has created a non-voting share class the company says can raise capital, pay employees, or fund acquisitions. Amazon is where a data-stack vendor and a distributor are putting enterprise commitments, in a named agreement and in marketplace volume. The Taiwan selling is not attributed to either firm; it is a record exit from the market that sits under the hardware story, framed as valuation fear. Waymo’s sidelined cars are a deployed fleet taken offline by a person, not a capex line.

The consequence splits by who has to write the next check. Meta can try to fund the build without giving up votes, if it uses the new class as described, while the legal charge already cut reported operating income and buybacks are paused. Amazon’s partner deals turn enterprise AI demand into contracted marketplace and collaboration volume rather than an uncommitted plan. The African grant and hub do not substitute for the data-center commitments; they are a separate, far smaller program. The robotaxi incident does not change either bill; it shows cars already in service can be pulled off the road without a regulatory order. The next dated step a source names is the non-voting share class, effective early next year.
