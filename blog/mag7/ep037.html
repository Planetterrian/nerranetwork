Gemini 4 Carbon scores 59, ahead of Claude and GPT-6, for cyber teams first.

By Patrick October 29, 2026 Ep 37 7 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top News Company Desk Also Today The Counterpoint The Thread: Two cloud lines, one September quarter On the Calendar The Tape

What You Need to Know: Google’s Gemini 4 Carbon posts 59 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0, just ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, and the first copies go to cyber defenders rather than the open Gemini app. Apple’s first touchscreen MacBook Pro is on a November 6 launch, and Amazon put five new Alexa devices on sale the same week it confirmed device-unit job cuts.

Top News

Google's Gemini 4 Carbon promises a leap in coding and cyber defence: Engadget

Google released Gemini 4 Carbon on top of Gemini 4 Argon, which it launched two weeks earlier, and Koray Kavukcuoglu, SVP at Google DeepMind and chief AI architect at Google, called Carbon the company’s strongest model, with state-of-the-art cybersecurity and long-horizon work. Access starts this week with selected cyber-defence teams, then trusted partners through the Fairwind programme, while Google says it is in the US government’s voluntary pre-release testing process; Argon is already in the Gemini app and AI search mode, and no wide Carbon date was set. Google says Carbon scored 59 on version 5.0 of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 at 58 and GPT-6 Astra at 57, up from Argon’s 50. On tests Google cited, Carbon scored 88.2 percent on SEC-bench Pro, 54.8 percent on ExploitGym and 71.0 percent on CWE-bench, with secure-code success more than double Argon’s and the Chrome team reporting patch quality more than ten times that of another lab’s large frontier model. Introductory pricing matches Argon at 2 dollars per million input tokens and 10 dollars per million output tokens, cached input 95 percent below the standard input rate, and Basic Tutorials reported those rates later rise to 4 dollars and 20 dollars. Source: engadget.com · itbrief.asia · basic-tutorials.com

Apple unveils touchscreen MacBook Pro: Bangkok Post

Apple unveiled the MacBook Pro with ProLED XDR Display and Touch, its first touchscreen Mac and the first Mac with OLED, in 14-inch and 16-inch models with Dynamic Island, tandem OLED and a touch sensor built into the panel. Apple lists up to 2,000 nits peak HDR and SDR brightness, a 10,000,000:1 contrast ratio, a 120Hz refresh that can drop to 10Hz, P3 and Adobe RGB coverage, up to 24 hours of battery life, a 1.36–1.81 kg design, M5 Pro and M5 Max, up to 128GB of unified memory, up to 16TB of storage, a six-speaker system with 50 percent deeper bass, Thunderbolt 5 and a 12MP Centre Stage camera. NewsCord put the 14-inch at 2,899 dollars, the 16-inch at 3,399 dollars and a fully loaded configuration at 14,549 dollars; The Information said the latest MacBook Pro without a touchscreen starts at 1,999 dollars, and MacRumors said the OLED models carry a 400-dollar premium over comparable mini-LED models. NewsCord said pre-orders open October 30 and the machines launch November 6. Johny Srouji called the machine a monumental step forward for the Mac, John Ternus introduced it after taking over from Tim Cook last month, and NewsCord recalled Steve Jobs calling touchscreen Macs ergonomically terrible — “your arm wants to fall off” — and Tim Cook comparing a laptop-tablet combination to a refrigerator and a toaster. Source: bangkokpost.com · newscord.org · theinformation.com · macrumors.com

Amazon launches Alexa gadgets and cuts device jobs: Silicon UK

Amazon on Wednesday launched five devices for its updated Alexa service: Echo Show 16, a 16-inch display with built-in Fire TV and a night-vision camera, from 499.99 dollars; a refreshed Echo Studio at 249.99 dollars with a camera; an Echo Dot at 99.99 dollars with a smart-home hub and Wi-Fi extender; Echo Time at 199.99 dollars, which uses sleep sensing to time a wake-up without a wearable; and Alexa Photo Frame from 199.99 dollars, syncing pictures from Amazon Photos. Amazon confirmed to Silicon UK that it cut a “small number” of roles and said the cuts would improve efficiency; a Reuters report, citing an unnamed person familiar with the matter, put the devices-and-services reduction at up to 250 people, mostly in software development, after about 1,000 core-retail cuts earlier this month. Marketscreener set the nearby Apple hardware at 299 dollars for HomeView and 149 dollars for HomePod mini 2, both on the latest Siri, and noted OpenAI is developing its own hardware. Source: silicon.co.uk · marketscreener.com

Company Desk

Microsoft pulls Copilot as AI on smart TVs fails to take off: FlatpanelsHD

FlatpanelsHD reported that Microsoft is pulling Copilot after AI on smart TVs failed to take off. KuCoin reported that LG is discontinuing the Microsoft Copilot AI service on its TVs. Source: flatpanelshd.com · kucoin.com

Also Today

Russia now requires Apple to store user communications: 9to5Mac

Bloomberg, as reported by 9to5Mac, says Apple and Microsoft are now under Russian rules that require companies to store users’ communications and hand them to security agencies if requested. Source: 9to5mac.com

Apple and OpenAI trade-secrets lawsuit now has a trial date: 9to5Mac

Judge Edward Davila set the schedule, including a trial date, in Apple’s trade-secrets case against two former employees, OpenAI and io Products. Source: 9to5mac.com

Meta delays update amid concern about scraping websites for AI training: PCMag

PCMag reported that Meta delayed an update amid concern about scraping websites for potential AI training. Source: pcmag.com

Nvidia unveils DSX POD server racks: TradingView

Nvidia unveiled DSX POD server racks intended to speed AI factory buildouts. Source: tradingview.com

Google DeepMind and Google Cloud deploy AI across Singapore’s health system: TradingView

Google DeepMind and Google Cloud are deploying AI across Singapore’s health system. Source: tradingview.com

The Counterpoint

First on the composite index, second or worse on several jobs that buyers actually run: BigGo Finance

BigGo Finance’s read of the same launch cuts the “near sweep” claim: paired with Google’s Antigravity tool, Carbon scored 59 on an agentic-coding index, short of Claude Code with Opus 5.5 at 62, and on FrontierSWE 2, GPT-6 Astra led at 65.5 percent against Carbon’s 61.2 percent. On the Vals Index, covering finance, coding, legal and tax tasks, Carbon ranked second at 70.36 percent, behind Sonnet 5.5 at 70.85 percent, and on WebDev Arena it placed fourth. The three headline cyber scores, including 54.8 percent on a 10-hour ExploitGym test, were measured without safety guardrails enabled, so the defensive chain Google is selling was not scored in the configuration customers would actually be allowed to run. Source: finance.biggo.com

The Thread: Two cloud lines, one September quarter

Google Cloud revenue more than doubled in the September quarter, to over 30 billion dollars, and that unit’s operating income tripled to 11.12 billion dollars. Azure revenue in the same period was 33 billion dollars, up 48 percent. Yesterday the open question on this show was still the growth rate; those two lines are the answer, and they leave the long-time number-three cloud business a few billion dollars from Azure.

Alphabet reported a 25 percent rise in company revenue to 127.58 billion dollars, slightly short of Wall Street forecasts, The Information wrote. Free cash flow turned negative by more than 9 billion dollars, against positive free cash flow of more than 24 billion dollars a year earlier. Search advertising grew 12 percent to 63.35 billion dollars, the softest growth in that business since the second quarter of 2025. Sundar Pichai said year-over-year search-query growth is the highest in five years, and he credited AI investment. Philipp Schindler told analysts that strong growth in AI Mode queries weighed on the result because monetization there is lower: Google is not showing as many ads in AI Mode as it does in ordinary search.

The cloud comparison is not a pure hosting match. Google Cloud’s revenue includes tensor-processing-unit systems that customers install in their own data centres. It did call those sales a meaningful contributor to revenue growth. Thomas Kurian, Google Cloud’s chief executive, said the company is seeing extraordinary demand, and executives told analysts to expect TPU sales to count for even more of Google Cloud next year.

Microsoft’s September-quarter revenue rose 19 percent to 92.3 billion dollars, one percentage point faster than the growth rate reported for the prior quarter, a figure Digitimes also carried. Microsoft 365 Commercial Cloud, the line that includes Copilot and the broader Office suite, rose 17 percent to 27.7 billion dollars, a slight acceleration from the June quarter. What Microsoft did not repeat was the paid Copilot seat count it had published before: 30 million at the end of June, up from 15 million at the end of 2025, each paying 30 dollars a month on top of the application fees. Satya Nadella said the number of customers paying for usage-based Copilot features grew 75 percent in the September quarter. CFO Amy Hood said average revenue per user rose because of that pay-as-you-go use. Late last month Microsoft folded Copilot features into one application, in part to steer people toward agents and usage billing, including tools set against Meta’s Muse.

Both companies are funding the same kind of build, and the quarter shows different proofs. Google’s cash flow went negative while cloud operating income tripled, and part of the cloud line is now chip hardware leaving the building. Microsoft kept commercial-cloud growth in the teens and stopped publishing the seat count that had been the simple evidence subscriptions were absorbing the spend. The next marker Kurian gave analysts is next year, when TPU sales are expected to weigh more on Google Cloud. Source: theinformation.com · digitimes.com

On the Calendar

NewsCord said pre-orders for the touchscreen MacBook Pro open on October 30, with the official launch on November 6. Source: newscord.org
