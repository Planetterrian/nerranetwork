A four-week DeepMind proof found no new flaws in Signal code behind WhatsApp.

By Patrick November 10, 2026 Ep 49 9 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Listen to this episode

What You Need to Know: Google DeepMind used a new framework, AlphaProof Code, to formally verify key cryptographic guarantees in the Signal protocol in four weeks, and the check found no new vulnerabilities in the sections examined. Meta is extending AI age estimation worldwide on Instagram so suspected teens land in Teen Accounts. A British Columbia judge certified a class action over Apple’s search deal with Google.

Top News

A New AI Tool From Google DeepMind Proved the Signal Protocol Is Secure: WIRED

Google DeepMind researchers used agentic AI and a framework called AlphaProof Code to formally verify a set of the Signal protocol’s key cryptographic guarantees, in a proof-of-concept that ran four weeks. The work is further independent validation of cryptography that protects communications on Signal, Google Messages, and WhatsApp. The analysis found no new vulnerabilities in the portions examined. Run against older Signal versions that contain known, long-fixed vulnerabilities, AlphaProof Code identified them and showed those versions could not be formally verified. The researchers are releasing the specification and proof they developed. Signal chief technology officer Ehren Kret said the approach enabled a significant expansion beyond prior formal verification of smaller portions of code. Source: wired.com · mezha.net

Meta Expands AI Age-Estimation Tech to Protect Teens on Instagram: Analytics India Magazine

Meta is expanding AI age estimation globally on Instagram to find accounts it suspects belong to teenagers, including people who registered with an adult birthday, and to place them in Teen Accounts. Teen Accounts limit who can contact a teen, what content they see, and how much time they spend; Meta says the global step will bring millions more teenagers under those protections. The company is continuing the rollout on Facebook and plans to introduce similar safeguards globally on Threads later this year. In Australia, Meta has started using Apple’s age-range signal: when a parent sets up a phone, the app store can confirm an age range and pass it to a developer when the teen downloads an app. This month, parents in the United States start getting an Instagram notification on how Meta places teens in age-appropriate protections, linking to an article by Larry Magid, CEO of ConnectSafely. Meta said the expansion builds on an agreement with 52 attorneys general across US states, territories, and the District of Columbia, and it called on TikTok and YouTube to adopt similar age-identification measures. Source: analyticsindiamag.com · medianama.com

Canadian court certifies class action against Apple over Google search deal: 9to5Mac

A British Columbia Supreme Court judge has certified a class action against Apple. The allegations are that Apple’s search agreement with Google stifled competition and inflated advertising prices. Source: 9to5mac.com

Moonshot Rents Nvidia’s Top AI Chips in China, Documents Show: Bloomberg Technology

Moonshot AI accessed a trove of Nvidia’s most advanced chips this summer, according to documents filed with Beijing regulators. Bloomberg’s account highlights the Chinese lab’s reliance on American hardware despite US export restrictions. Source: bloomberg.com

Company Desk

Sunlife Brings Google Workspace with Gemini to Asia Advisors: Fintech Singapore

Sun Life is rolling out Google Workspace with Gemini to advisors in Hong Kong, Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, and Vietnam, with Gemini inside Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Drive, and Google Meet. Frankie Leung, chief agency officer for Asia at Sun Life, said the tools help advisors spend less time on routine tasks and more time with clients. The rollout follows Sun Life’s 2021 offer of Google Workspace to advisors across Asia. Haydn Sallmann, director of Google Workspace for Asia Pacific, said embedding Gemini in daily workflows is meant to cut administrative overhead. Source: fintechnews.sg · techinasia.com

Hackers Use Qubole AWS Credentials To Breach Customer Cloud Environments: Dataconomy

Hackers used Qubole AWS credentials to breach customer cloud environments, Dataconomy reported. SC Media describes the incident as a cross-account access attack aimed at Qubole customers. Source: dataconomy.com · scworld.com

Here's What Apple Fixed in iOS 27.2.1 and macOS 27.2.1: MacRumors

Apple released iOS 27.2.1, iPadOS 27.2.1, and macOS 27.2.1 with fixes for a handful of security vulnerabilities. An iMessage bug could let a remote attacker join a group chat and receive its messages, and another could let users see messages sent before they joined. A malicious Bluetooth device could pair without user interaction, and a separate bug could let a nearby attacker corrupt process memory. Several bugs could let an attacker bypass the rate limit on AirPlay pairing-code attempts. Apple credits itself on four of the vulnerabilities. The Mac update also addresses the eMudhra certificate vulnerability, adding protections for command-line tools. Source: macrumors.com

Muse Meta AI agent price leaks online ahead of launch in India, details here: India Today

A report in The Hindu, carried by India Today, says Meta’s Muse agent is headed to India with a Power plan at Rs 1,799 and a Max plan at Rs 8,999. In the United States those tiers are monthly, at $20 and $100. On Meta’s global site, Power includes 500 million Muse tokens a week and Max includes 3 billion. Muse, which takes actions inside apps including WhatsApp, passed 10 million downloads within two months of availability in the United States, Canada, Japan, and Singapore. From India, signup still goes to a waitlist, and Muse cannot independently make card transactions for Indian users because it is not integrated with domestic payment gateways. Source: indiatoday.in · firstpost.com

Nvidia Unveils GR00T 2 Robot Brain and Isaac Lab 3.0, With Foxconn and Siemens Testing Industrial Rollout: BigGo Finance

Nvidia unveiled GR00T 2, described as a next robot brain with a 95 percent success rate from five demonstrations, along with Isaac Lab 3.0. BigGo Finance says Foxconn and Siemens are testing an industrial rollout, and MLQ.ai says the model learns new skills from human video and launched at CoRL. Source: finance.biggo.com · mlq.ai

Also Today

Hunter is killing Google Home and Alexa voice commands and automations in favor of Matter: Android Authority

Hunter is ending Google Home and Alexa voice commands and automations in favor of Matter. Source: androidauthority.com

Google Lists Gemini 4 Carbon at $4/$20 in Its Cloud Catalog: FourWeekMBA

Google has listed Gemini 4 Carbon in its cloud catalog at $4 and $20. Source: fourweekmba.com

Apple announces investment in Türkiye with 5 developer academies, 2 centers: Türkiye Today

Apple announced an investment in Türkiye built around five developer academies and two centers, which The Apple Post counts as seven new technology facilities. Source: turkiyetoday.com · theapplepost.com

Apple files China registration for Apple Intelligence on iPad, Mac: digitimes

Apple Intelligence cleared another China roadblock, Digital Trends reports, and Digitimes says Apple filed a China registration for the feature on iPad and Mac. Source: digitimes.com · digitaltrends.com

November 2026 Microsoft Office Updates Brings 7 Mew Copilot Features: Geeky Gadgets

Geeky Gadgets reports that November’s Microsoft Office updates bring seven new Copilot features. Source: geeky-gadgets.com

Meta's AI Agent "Muse" Expands to Apple Watch and Wear OS, Enabling Voice Commands From the Wrist: BigGo Finance

Meta’s Muse agent is expanding to Apple Watch and Wear OS so it can take voice commands from the wrist. Source: finance.biggo.com

Nvidia’s Next Headache: New Push For Export Controls in Wake of Spy Scandal: The Information

In late October, White House officials shared feedback with Congressional staff on export-control measures under consideration for the National Defense Authorization Act, and Commerce staff have renewed a push on lithography-tooling rules and on limits meant to stop Chinese companies from remotely accessing advanced chips such as Nvidia’s. Source: theinformation.com

How Jensen Huang’s Yearslong Ties to Alice Huang Enabled Nvidia Chip Sales to China: The Information

The Information now puts a figure on the chip path tied to Alice Huang: at least $7.5 billion in Nvidia chips since 2023, through a network centered on Shanghai Century Litong, founded in June 2023 and on Nvidia’s preferred cloud-partner list by that October. Source: theinformation.com

AWS Nitro Sandboxes: 1 VM per EKS Pod, $0 Extra [2026]: shattered.io

Shattered.io reports AWS Nitro Sandboxes that place one virtual machine on each EKS pod at no extra charge. Source: shattered.io

The Counterpoint

The proof stops at the sections they chose, and the cost is unpublished: WIRED

DeepMind did not formally verify the entire Signal mobile app, and Swarat Chaudhuri, a DeepMind research scientist, told WIRED there is a long distance before the method scales to all software. Google declined to share the cost of the four-week analysis and declined to comment on whether it plans a commercial formal-verification product. Chaudhuri said the team does not want a setup in which AI writes both the goals and the proofs and then grades itself. Source: wired.com

The Thread: Credits for the clinic, three clouds in the waiting room

Amazon Web Services is putting $100 million of artificial intelligence, agentic services, and cloud infrastructure into a five-year Health Impact Initiative for global health nonprofits, government agencies, research institutions, healthcare delivery systems, and health-tech firms working with underserved populations. The line continues the Health Equity Initiative AWS launched in 2021, which distributed $60 million across more than 400 awardees. With other AWS health social-impact programs, the company has supported more than 600 organizations in more than 80 countries. An independent evaluation of 83 past Health Equity Initiative awardees found that 60 percent named cloud hosting and compute costs as the single largest barrier to modernization where they operate, and ranked promotional credits as their highest-priority operational need. The new fund is written for more than hosting. HIT Consultant lists autonomous patient-outreach bots, automated call-center triage, serverless predictive analytics, and ambient workflow tools meant to extend constrained clinical staff. Three domains set the priority: expanding access to quality health services, women’s and children’s health research and innovation, and global nutrition. Dr. Rowland Illing, chief medical officer and director of healthcare and life sciences at AWS, said agents and AI are revolutionizing health services but too often do not reach the communities that need them most.

HIT Consultant places Microsoft and Google in the same contest, on different products. Google Cloud is framed around generative healthcare search through Vertex AI. Microsoft Azure is framed around an OpenAI integration inside Epic. AWS, in this account, is using the credits to seat HealthOmics, Amazon Bedrock, and Amazon Comprehend Medical inside research institutes and health NGOs while those groups are still choosing a stack. The piece’s stickiness claim is plain: once data pipelines and agents sit on one cloud, moving them is costly. The shared constraint is staffing. Safety-net hospitals, federally qualified health centers, and rural clinics cannot close shifts with signing bonuses. For those sites, an autonomous scheduling agent, a voice-based multilingual intake coordinator, or an automated triage bot is described as how continuous operations stay possible, and the grant is what pays the compute so the software does not eat operating cash. Proposals are audited weekly against projected lives impacted, computational feasibility, and implementation roadmaps. Organizations are expected to price the build in the AWS Pricing Calculator so an award becomes a production deployment rather than an open research note.

What shifts is who owns the default health agent in organizations that never sign a commercial cloud contract on their own. A group that stands up outreach and triage on Amazon’s credits is a harder later sale for Microsoft, even where Azure already sits inside the Epic record, and a harder later sale for Google, even where search is the clinical front door. Amazon gets a path into care networks that do not show up in a standard cloud forecast. Microsoft and Google keep the wedges this account assigns them — the record-system model and healthcare search — but they are not funding this round. The filter that decides who actually ships is that weekly audit: a plan that cannot show a live deployment does not stay on the credit line. Source: hitconsultant.net
