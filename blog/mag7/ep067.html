OpenAI countersued Apple on Friday over supplier threats and stolen device secrets.

By Patrick November 28, 2026 Ep 67 8 min read Listen to podcast
Follow MAG 7 Daily Apple Podcasts RSS
Listen to this episode

What You Need to Know: OpenAI countersued Apple on Friday over supplier threats and stolen device secrets, filing in a California court after Apple sued in July. Chile's competition authority has filed an App Store case that 9to5Mac says could mean an eighty-million-dollar fine. Waystar is connecting claims and eligibility tools to Gemini for Healthcare, and an Ontario tribunal found Amazon's encrypted-technology hiring rule discriminatory.

Top News

OpenAI countersues Apple, alleging trade-secret theft and supplier threats: Financial Times

OpenAI filed Friday in a California court, accusing Apple of stealing trade secrets and blocking access to hardware suppliers in an effort to "compete unfairly by any means available." The Financial Times reports that OpenAI accuses Apple of "coercing and threatening" suppliers that work with both companies. Bloomberg reports the complaint also alleges unfair employment agreements meant to keep talent from leaving. The hardware work follows last year's acquisition of Jony Ive's io firm. Apple's July suit names OpenAI, io Products, and former Apple employees Chang Liu and Tang Tan, who previously led iPhone and Apple Watch product design. OpenAI says an employee who returned to Apple in October sent more than 100 confidential files on unreleased hardware, including device images, engineering data, and supplier information, to personal accounts; Apple said then that its investigation had not located OpenAI confidential information. Apple has sought a preliminary injunction and won expanded access to evidence. The original case is scheduled for trial on July 19, 2027. Source: ft.com Source: bloomberg.com Source: 9to5mac.com

Apple faces potential $80 million fine and App Store overhaul in Chile: 9to5Mac

Chile's national competition authority, Fiscalía Nacional Económica, has filed an antitrust lawsuit accusing Apple of abusing a dominant position in iOS app distribution and in-app purchases. 9to5Mac reports the case as a potential eighty-million-dollar fine and an App Store overhaul. Source: 9to5mac.com

Waystar integrates revenue cycle intelligence with Gemini for Healthcare: Digital Health News

Waystar is extending a multi-year Google Cloud collaboration by putting AltitudeAI claims analytics and insurance-eligibility checks inside Gemini for Healthcare, the workspace unveiled on November 10, 2026. Providers can query claims and payer-payment data, and start real-time eligibility checks, in natural language inside a governed workspace, using open standards and Waystar's Model Context Protocol connector. The connector lets Gemini agents trigger standardized revenue-cycle actions, not only retrieve data, so front-office staff can run eligibility checks without switching applications. Waystar says early-adopter providers cut time spent analyzing complex claims and payer-payment datasets by up to 75 percent with its conversational analytics. Chief executive Matt Hawkins said advancing a more autonomous revenue cycle "requires trusted intelligence, deep connectivity, and the ability to take action." Source: digitalhealthnews.com

Amazon discriminated against Iranian job candidate under global hiring policy, Ontario tribunal rules: GeekWire

An Ontario tribunal ruled that Amazon discriminated against a Toronto engineer because of his Iranian citizenship. GeekWire reports that Amazon's global policy barred some Iranian citizens from jobs involving access to its encrypted technology. Source: geekwire.com

Company Desk

New Google ad takes a dig at ChatGPT: ThePrint

Google's Instagram Reels ads in India invent an assistant called Chatty AI that keeps asking for payment, then set it against Gemini. In the clips, a live camera video call and unlimited image uploads are described as free on Gemini, and viewers are told they can transfer history from other bots. Sensor Tower figures cited by ThePrint put ChatGPT at 330 million monthly users in India in May 2026, against Gemini's 229 million. ChatGPT launched in November 2022, months before Google introduced Bard, later renamed Gemini. Source: theprint.in

The Alexa Tablet 12 Pro replaces the Fire line with a midrange Android slate: 9to5Google

Amazon has ended the Fire tablet line and replaced it with Alexa Tablets. The 12 Pro review unit uses a MediaTek Dimensity 8400 with 8GB of RAM and 128GB of UFS 3.1 storage; a 256GB model with UFS 4.0 and 16GB of RAM is offered. The base list price is 500 dollars, nanomatte is a 50-dollar upgrade, and the 256GB nanomatte model is 650 dollars. 9to5Google says it has been on sale for as low as 350 dollars. Digg, citing 9to5Toys, lists both 128GB versions at 400 dollars for Black Friday, 100 dollars off the standard model and 150 dollars off nanomatte. This model is unsubsidized: unlike the Alexa Tablet 8 and 11, it does not charge 20 dollars to remove lock-screen ads. Digg also lists the Tablet 8 from 130 dollars and the Tablet 11 from 200 dollars. Source: 9to5google.com Source: digg.com

Meta faces Australia's Digital Duty of Care law and fresh EU AI scrutiny: Simply Wall St

Australia passed its Digital Duty of Care law in late November 2026, adding direct monitoring requirements and heavy fines for content-moderation failures at Meta and other large platforms. European regulators are urging broader AI Act enforcement and specific investigations into Meta's handling of gender-based risks, including ads linked to AI "nudifier" tools. Source: simplywall.st

Trump and Vance backed Huang's return over open-source AI: The Information

With Jensen Huang already back as Nvidia's chief executive, The Information reports that President Trump and Vice President Vance backed that return partly because they see him as critical to protecting open-source AI. Their stated concern is that closed-model companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic could become too powerful. Source: theinformation.com

Also Today

Azure AI Gateway reaches general availability as a standalone service: InfoQ

Azure AI Gateway is now generally available as a standalone service. Source: infoq.com

AWS introduces centralized workforce access to IAM roles across accounts: InfoQ

Amazon Web Services has introduced centralized workforce access to IAM roles across accounts. Source: infoq.com

Microsoft offers companies $7,500 in credits to put Copilot to work: Observer

Satya Nadella is offering companies 7,500 dollars in credits to put Copilot to work. Source: observer.com

The Counterpoint

Apple says the countersuit distracts from employees who took its secrets: 9to5Mac

Apple told 9to5Mac the case is about OpenAI employees "deliberately stealing, accessing, and using" secret information on unreleased technologies, processes, and products, not inadvertent retention. It called the counterclaims "a transparent attempt to distract from the substance of Apple's lawsuit, in which OpenAI has already been ordered to stop using Apple's confidential information." Source: 9to5mac.com

The Thread: The run rate beside the old cloud

Twenty-five billion dollars is the annualized run rate Andy Jassy says Amazon's AI business has already passed, three years in. Crypto Briefing, citing remarks on November 17, 2026, puts early Amazon Web Services at an annualized run rate of about fifty-seven million dollars after its own first three years, the gap he calls 440 times. Those accounts say AWS posted 46 billion dollars of revenue in the third quarter of 2026, up 39.3 percent year over year, its fastest growth in nearly five years, which annualizes to a run rate of roughly 184 billion dollars. A contracted backlog of 578 billion dollars, he said, gives visibility on demand through 2029. A large amount of AI compute is already reserved for 2027, with portions extending into 2028 and 2029. On the third-quarter 2026 earnings call he projected that AWS could exceed 1 trillion dollars in annual revenue within five to ten years. In March 2026 he had projected at least 600 billion dollars within a decade; roughly seven months later both the target and the clock had moved. He extended the same 1-trillion-dollar ambition to physical stores. Cash capital expenditure for 2026 is approximately 220 billion dollars, most of it aimed at AWS-related work, and he expects a substantial increase in 2027. He has also indicated AWS is expected to grow by over 40 percent in both 2027 and 2028.

The comparison is Amazon measuring a new line against its own origin story. Companies training and running models need computing power, and many would rather rent it than build it; the reports call AWS one of the largest landlords in that market. The early cloud business started from nothing in a market that barely existed. The AI line launched on an already enormous customer base and a global data-center footprint, so a faster multiple is not a repeat of the same starting conditions. Jassy's phrase for the market is "incredibly early innings." He argues that spending now should eventually become stronger free cash flow and a better return on invested capital once new data centers are fully monetized. An annualized run rate, the reports note, projects current revenue across a year. It measures momentum. It is not a booked annual total. No company, those reports say, has had even one division at the trillion-dollar scale he is now describing twice.

What changes is the size of the destination he is willing to say out loud: a cloud line and a stores line each aimed at a scale no division has reached, paid for by a capital plan he says is mostly for the cloud and set to rise again. The risk left on the table is demand. The backlog and the reservations pay off only if customers keep the capacity they have already spoken for, and if the new buildings do not sit idle. The checks inside his own account are the two growth years he indicated and the capital step he expects after this year's bill. Source: cryptobriefing.com Source: cryptobriefing.com

On the Calendar

MacRumors reports a red HomePod mini 2, with a (RED) box label, has been spotted in stores and is expected to be released by Tuesday, December 1, 2026, World AIDS Day, alongside up to four other (RED) products next week, including silver-and-white AirPods Max 2 with a red Digital Crown, a red AirPods Pro wrist strap, a red Apple Watch sport band, and potentially a red AirTag 2. Source: macrumors.com

Apple says its Westfield Chermside store in Brisbane, closed for renovation since late April, reopens Friday, December 11, 2026, at 9 a.m. local time, with an updated design and an Apple Pickup station. Source: macrumors.com
