5,248 Nvidia employees joined a petition past 100,000 names to reinstate Jensen Huang.

By Patrick November 15, 2026 Ep 54 7 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top News Company Desk Also Today The Counterpoint The Thread: A capacity upgrade, then a thundering herd On the Calendar The Tape

What You Need to Know: A petition to reinstate Jensen Huang passed 100,000 signatures on Saturday, including 5,248 people who said they are current Nvidia employees, and by Saturday afternoon the count was above 115,000. Italy’s data-protection authority fined Meta €805 million ($887 million) for accessing Android users’ gallery photos and videos without explicit consent. Apple Intelligence is approved for iPad and Mac in China ahead of a December rollout, and Firmus is seeking $3 billion privately with prepayments from Nvidia and Meta.

Top News

Thousands of Nvidia staff sign to bring Huang back: The Information

A petition urging Nvidia’s board to reinstate Jensen Huang passed 100,000 signatures on Saturday, The Information reported, including 5,248 people who said they are current employees. Signers named there include Elon Musk, Epic Games’ Tim Sweeney and Y Combinator’s Garry Tan. By Saturday afternoon TechCrunch put the total above 115,000, with the petition site listing 5,965 current employees, 1,108 former employees and 63,063 shareholders, plus Replit CEO Amjad Masad among notable supporters. The text says signers are concerned Huang was removed without meaningful due process, demands that the board bring him back while an independent investigation establishes the facts, and says he deserves due process, not mob justice. Huang resigned Thursday, four days after confessing to an affair with a chip executive suspected by U.S. intelligence of being a Chinese spy, and he has agreed to testify before Congress. Nvidia named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO while it searches for a permanent replacement. Source: theinformation.com Source: techcrunch.com

Italy fines Meta €805 million over Android galleries: Bloomberg

The Italian Data Protection Authority fined Meta Platforms €805 million ($887 million) for accessing photos and videos in Android users’ galleries without their explicit consent, Bloomberg reported. Reuters carried the same fine as $887 million over Android users’ data access. Source: bloomberg.com Source: reuters.com

Apple Intelligence clears China’s hurdle for iPad and Mac: Softonic

Apple Intelligence is approved for iPad and Mac in China, ahead of a December rollout. Source: en.softonic.com

Google explores AI ad-tool deals and pays Hightouch: The Information

Google’s corporate development team has explored acquiring AI tools for creating ads, and the company is paying startup Hightouch to move advertisers onto its customer data tools, The Information reported. The moves are aimed at making ads in AI features more useful as advertisers complain they cannot see how those ads perform. Source: x.com

Firmus seeks $3 billion after its IPO collapse, with Nvidia and Meta prepaying: Bloomberg

Firmus Grid is planning a $3 billion private fundraising that will include new equity and debt, plus prepayments locked in from customers including its backer Nvidia and Meta, the Australian Financial Review reported, according to Bloomberg. The raise follows an IPO collapse. Source: bloomberg.com

Company Desk

Gemini glasses start at $395: Nacionale

Google’s Gemini glasses have a starting price of $395, the new commercial term on a product whose face-hiding limits for Gemini were already set. A sale date is on the calendar below. Source: nacionale.com

Apple gives iOS 28 and macOS 28 longer coding stretches: MacRumors

Apple has changed how it builds iOS 28, macOS 28 and the matching updates, Bloomberg’s Mark Gurman reported via MacRumors. Each yearly release is still split into three stages, but coding in each stage runs six weeks instead of four, and the convergence period shrinks from four weeks to two, under new CEO John Ternus. Source: macrumors.com

Nvidia’s security chief left in August: TechCrunch

Nvidia’s security chief quietly left in August, TechCrunch reported, ahead of Jensen Huang’s resignation over relationships that include an executive at a company suspected of funneling chips to China. A former intelligence officer told the outlet this is the kind of leak that runs through everyday conversations, which most corporate security teams are poorly equipped to catch. Source: techcrunch.com

Also Today

TSMC plans a $500 million fund for the next chip winners: Techmeme

TSMC plans a new $500 million fund for chip startups, suppliers and other upstream partners, and said it also wants to support “the future’s Nvidia,” Nikkei Asia reported. Source: x.com

DeepSeek’s $92 billion round drew chip-access pitches: The Information

The Information reported that DeepSeek’s round, at a $92 billion valuation, drew Tencent, CATL and companies pitched as a major source of black-market Nvidia chips, with Alice Huang’s companies investing while promising those chips. Source: theinformation.com

Asian governments check chip supply after Huang’s exit: TechCrunch

Some Asian governments moved to confirm chip supply commitments after Jensen Huang’s exit, while others are monitoring local projects or addressing ongoing investigations, TechCrunch reported. Source: techcrunch.com

AWS warns of an MCP Gateway Registry flaw: GBHackers

AWS warned of an MCP Gateway Registry vulnerability that could let attackers reach internal network services. Source: gbhackers.com

AWS releases SGLang-Neuron for Trainium: CASRAI

AWS released SGLang-Neuron for deploying frontier AI models on Trainium. Source: casrai.org

AWS introduces UAEF for closed-loop agent evaluation: CASRAI

AWS introduced UAEF, a framework-agnostic system for closed-loop evaluation of AI agents. Source: casrai.org

AWS rolls out a Nitro boost: The Stack

AWS rolled out a Nitro boost for its runtime. Source: thestack.technology

Siri AI reaches older Apple TV 4K boxes: MacRumors

tvOS 27.2.1, out Friday, brings Siri AI to second- and third-generation Apple TV 4K boxes. Source: macrumors.com

The Counterpoint

A restored Huang still faces adversaries Nvidia made: Financial Times

The petition treats reinstatement as the remedy. A Financial Times account of the post-Huang company says Nvidia has only itself to blame for a long list of adversaries, and the column circulated by Techmeme argues the company must reshuffle its leadership, reset its Washington ties, and accept that even the most valuable U.S. company cannot dictate U.S. foreign policy. On that telling, a board vote to bring Huang back does not clear the policy problem the adversaries are using. Source: ft.com Source: x.com

The Thread: A capacity upgrade, then a thundering herd

Microsoft’s Microsoft 365 failure lasted about 30 hours, and the company now says the break was introduced during an earlier network capacity upgrade, then showed up after a more recent network change. GeekWire, reading Microsoft’s incident log, puts the disruption at about 30 hours across more than 70 countries. TechXplore puts products offline in at least 74 countries and territories in the Americas, Europe, Asia and Africa. The services named were Outlook, Teams and Copilot. Microsoft called it a technical issue, not cyber and not related to AI or agents, and on Saturday said the remaining customers and regions had service again.

Charles Lamanna, executive vice president of Copilot, Agents and Platform, said cascading failures and thundering herds at the network perimeter made recovery harder than normal. In his account a thundering herd is huge numbers of devices reconnecting at once and overwhelming systems as they return. Microsoft told customers a preliminary post-incident report would come within two business days and a final report within five. He said the write-up would include a step-by-step timeline and many repair items, and that the company would be very specific.

The load on that perimeter is a January number Microsoft reported: more than 450 million paid commercial Microsoft 365 seats. GeekWire’s reading is roughly one seat for every 18 people on Earth. Unity Health Toronto declared a Code Grey and said patient care continued. Pennsylvania lawmakers went about 18 hours without email. A Colorado jail could not take online bond payments. Business Upturn, citing Nikkei, said securities firms and asset managers in Japan fell back on fax machines when trade-confirmation email stopped. New York’s Emergency Management office warned of delays reaching city agencies. In Singapore some government systems, including email, were disrupted. In London the UK Supreme Court was not receiving email.

It was at least the fourth significant outage of this kind this year. A January outage lasted nearly 10 hours. In July a bug in Microsoft’s automated network maintenance system disrupted Azure and Microsoft 365 for hours. On August 31 an Exchange Online outage also hit Teams, OneDrive and SharePoint. GeekWire sets those failures next to earlier incidents at Amazon Web Services: a second cloud operator, the same class of customer dependence. Friday’s FAA ground stop was not part of the Microsoft incident. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no connection, and Microsoft said it had no indication the ground stop involved any Microsoft service. Federal officials blamed aging FAA telecom gear.

What the two companies share is the reconnect problem, not this week’s fault. The incident account gives Amazon no role in the upgrade. Customers still treat both perimeters as one risk, because a network change at either can drop mail, chat and an assistant together, then worsen as devices rush back. Microsoft owes the specific repair list its Copilot executive already promised. Amazon inherits the comparison buyers will use the next time its own change window fails. Source: geekwire.com Source: techxplore.com

On the Calendar

Apple’s Support Community discussion forums close permanently on November 20, and they are already read-only. Source: macrumors.com

Google’s Gemini glasses go on sale December 10, starting at $395. Source: nacionale.com
