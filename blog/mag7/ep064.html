A day after returning, Huang told Nvidia staff he broke the code he wrote.

By Patrick November 25, 2026 Ep 64 9 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Follow MAG 7 Daily Apple Podcasts RSS
Listen to this episode
Contents

What You Need to Know: Jensen Huang used Nvidia’s first all-hands since his reinstatement to admit he had flouted the conduct code he largely wrote. He told staff he is in therapy, thanked the administration for intelligence that framed the national-security risk of his return as low, and is headed for a public Senate Banking hearing. The same day, DeepMind published an open benchmark for open models, and Meta’s agent started taking Instagram messages.

Top News

Huang apologizes to Nvidia staff after returning as CEO: Bloomberg Technology

Jensen Huang apologized at an all-hands in Santa Clara and acknowledged violating Nvidia’s code of conduct. TechCrunch, citing CNBC, reported a remorseful tone and the line “I hurt all of you.” He called it the hardest company meeting he had ever done, and said the hardest meeting of all had been with his wife. He admitted he had flouted a code he largely devised, said the code remains in effect, and, as Wired reported his statement, said a violation does not mean the company walks someone out the door. He told staff he is in therapy and grateful for a second chance, downplayed President Trump’s role even though the president reportedly called the board, and thanked the administration for quick access to intelligence that, The Wall Street Journal reported, helped the board judge the national-security risk of reinstating him as low. He is also scheduled for closed House hearings before the Intelligence Committee, the Foreign Affairs Committee, and the China Select Committee. Source: bloomberg.com

Source: techcrunch.com

Google DeepMind releases gBench for open models: BigGo Finance

Google DeepMind open-sourced gBench, a benchmark framework for open-weight large language models and vision-language models, including its Gemma family. It ships as a Python package installable with pip, with source and documentation on GitHub under the Apache License 2.0, and it needs an OpenAI-compatible API endpoint; operation is confirmed on vLLM and Ollama, and it can start and stop a local vLLM server. Evaluations sit in three tiers: academic and domain coverage across six areas with 86 suites, a 16-item Golden Set Smoke Test that separates chat-template defects from weak model capability, and agent tests through GemmaClaw and OpenClaw that check multi-turn tool calling, session memory, and plug-in routing, plus stress tests of response times and error rates. The Google Gemma account on X announced the release on November 23. Separately, Google’s Android Bench 2.0, which includes closed models, had Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.2 in first place and Gemini 4 Carbon in eighth at release. The write-up notes that publishing suites raises the risk that models are tuned to the test. Source: finance.biggo.com

Meta’s Muse AI agent comes to Instagram DMs: MacRumors

Meta expanded Muse inside Instagram so the agent can sort saved posts into collections or travel itineraries and build grocery lists from recipes. Users in the United States and Canada see an option to let Muse organize a post when they save it. Direct-message chat with Muse, which also shows up as a side conversation in the Muse app, is rolling out in the United States, Canada, Mexico, Japan, and Singapore, and Muse will not change anything or take an action without permission. Source: macrumors.com

Google Messages gets Gemini Tasks: Android Authority

The latest Google Messages beta routes the existing Gemini button into Gemini Channels, then into an in-app conversation that runs on RCS Business Messaging. Gemini can text first with price-drop alerts, package updates, subscription reminders, and a daily briefing, and the thread still takes ordinary questions plus photos and files. Tasks is available to free users in the United States, Japan, South Korea, Canada, Australia, and India, but the Messages integration is only in the United States and requires Tasks to be switched on in the Gemini app. Source: androidauthority.com

Source: 9to5google.com

Microsoft’s cloud outage exposed a Code Red overhaul: Ars Technica

After a disruption that Moneycontrol described as a 24-hour outage hitting Exchange Online, Teams, and Copilot, Ars Technica reported that internal memos show a Code Red overhaul and a recovery target of one hour. The memos frame the incident as an engineering fog of war. Source: arstechnica.com

Source: moneycontrol.com

Company Desk

Google and the KBO form a search partnership: The Korea Times

Google said Wednesday it is teaming with the Korea Baseball Organization so Gemini and Google Search show up on the league’s YouTube channel and Instagram and in live broadcasts of official matches. Google will help fans look up athletes and past results, and the two sides plan collaborations through next season. Source: koreatimes.co.kr

AWS Lambda doubles its timeout to 30 minutes: InfoQ

AWS Lambda’s maximum timeout is now 30 minutes, double the 15-minute cap that had stood for eight years. The change is a platform limit on how long a single function invocation may run. Source: infoq.com

Tidal bans AI music, while Apple Music sticks to labels: MacRumors

Tidal said it will block tracks generated entirely with AI from December 9, deleting about 29 million songs after 43 percent of uploads in the last 90 days were flagged as AI-generated, and it expects to reject 80,000 to 85,000 songs a day, with AI tracks “completely gone” at the start of 2027. Lead Robert Andersen said 96 percent of AI songs get zero plays. Apple has not banned AI songs; it relies on labels and distributors for a “Made With AI” tag, and Apple Music vice president Oliver Schusser said in April that more than a third of monthly uploads are fully AI-generated while AI music was under 0.5 percent of listening. Source: macrumors.com

Also Today

Asia data centers sound out US clients to replace China demand: Bloomberg Technology

Data centers across Asia are trying to sell spare computing power to US buyers after the Trump administration tightened a loophole that had given Chinese tech firms access to Nvidia’s most advanced chips. Source: bloomberg.com

AWS Lambda gets GPUs at EC2 price plus a 9.75 percent fee: shattered.io

GPU inference on Lambda is priced at the EC2 rate plus a 9.75 percent fee. Source: shattered.io

AWS CFO John Felton on Amazon’s $220 billion AI spending: Business Insider

Business Insider reported that AWS chief financial officer John Felton laid out the thinking behind Amazon’s $220 billion AI spending strategy. Source: businessinsider.com

GOSH launches an Agentic Hospital project with AWS and Hyland: Telecompaper

Great Ormond Street Hospital launched an Agentic Hospital project with AWS and Hyland. Source: telecompaper.com

Walmart lets you shop and pay in Meta’s AI: Superpower Daily

Walmart said shoppers can set spending limits and require approval inside Meta’s AI, and that prices and membership benefits carry over. Source: superpowerdaily.com

Nvidia still faces key-man risk in Huang: Bloomberg Technology

University of Colorado law professor Ann Lipton said Huang had about 60 people reporting to him in his prior CEO role and that Nvidia still lacks a succession plan. Source: bloomberg.com

Trump reportedly says pardoning Jensen Huang is an option: TechCrunch

TechCrunch reported that President Trump has called a pardon for Huang an option he does not think will be necessary, while Huang still faces questions about Alice Huang, an executive with alleged ties to Chinese intelligence who previously worked at a firm accused of illegally diverting Nvidia chips to China. Source: techcrunch.com

AmCham hosts Korea-US AI talks with Apple, AWS and Qualcomm: The Korea Herald

The American Chamber of Commerce in Korea hosted Korea-US AI talks that included Apple, AWS, and Qualcomm. Source: koreaherald.com

The Counterpoint

Yale’s Sonnenfeld: the return is a detriment, not a rescue: TechCrunch

The apology asks staff and Washington to treat Huang as fit to lead again. Yale’s Jeffrey Sonnenfeld warns that the return markets cheered on Monday could be a giant mistake, exposing directors to what he called historic personal liability from a growing pile of lawsuits, and he asks whether the country is celebrating a company that has helped put advanced chips in adversaries’ hands. Source: techcrunch.com

The Thread: A frame on the face, a car on the site

Meta’s Oakley Meta HSTN puts a 3K Ultra HD camera in the frame and rates the pair IPX4 for water. The Vanguard sibling uses a 12-megapixel ultra-wide camera with a 122-degree field of view, high-decibel open-ear speakers, wind-noise reduction, and an IP67 rating against water and dust. EssilorLuxottica and Meta are putting Ray-Ban Meta Generation 3, in Wayfarer, Aviator, and Zena, and Oakley Meta, in HSTN and Vanguard, into South Africa through selected optical partners, the Oakley store, and online. Ray-Ban Meta still does hands-free capture, open-ear audio, calls, translation, and messages, with clear and sun lenses including Transitions, polarised, and blue-light filters, plus unrestricted prescriptions. Everyday wearers get optical-first Blayzer Optics Generation 2 and Scriber Optics Generation 2. Vanguard works with cycling helmets and hats and ties into Strava and Garmin so a run or ride can be tracked in real time. Select HSTN pairs use Oakley PRIZM lenses. Karen Gold, marketing director at EssilorLuxottica, said the range opens a new way for South Africans to see, share, and interact. The companies called Ray-Ban Meta the world’s leading AI glasses after millions of units sold worldwide. That is a wearable that captures, listens, and answers on the street, sold as eyewear rather than as a phone accessory.

Amazon is opening a different kind of storefront, and it is not in the same country. Amazon Autos, the car marketplace that has run in the United States since December 2024, is making the United Kingdom its first market outside the US. In the US it already operates in more than 150 cities and lists vehicles from 35 brands through participating dealers. UK shoppers are meant to browse live dealer inventory, see upfront pricing, get an instant trade-in valuation, and reach a local dealer on Amazon.co.uk, including finance and lease comparisons for new, used, and certified pre-owned cars. Hyundai is the first brand at the UK launch, with the IONIQ 3, IONIQ 5, i20, and KONA. Vertu Motors will list used vehicles. More brands and dealers are supposed to be named before the service opens. The US product already lets a buyer compare price and specification and start financing without standing on a lot. The UK version copies that path rather than inventing a new one.

What the two launches share is a retail surface that used to belong to a shop or a dealer, now sitting on a Mag Seven account the customer already has. Meta is selling capture and an assistant as a pair of glasses, through an eyewear partner, into a country that did not have the category. Amazon is selling the car-buying steps it already runs in 150 American cities, through Hyundai and Vertu, into a country that did not have Autos. Neither company is waiting on the other’s hardware. Meta does not need Amazon’s marketplace to put a camera on a face, and Amazon does not need Meta’s glasses to list an IONIQ. The dependency is the habit: a logged-in account that can hear a question, hold a saved post, or hold a trade-in number. For Meta, South Africa is a distribution test of whether Generation 3 styles and the two Oakley performance frames travel outside the markets that already bought millions of units. For Amazon, the UK is the first proof that a dealer marketplace built for 35 brands can cross a border without a new buying flow. The consequence for each is operational, not a shared chip. Meta’s retail partners have to fit prescriptions, PRIZM lenses, and helmets. Amazon’s dealers have to keep inventory, pricing, and trade-in values live on Amazon.co.uk. A shopper in Johannesburg gets an assistant on their face. A shopper in Britain gets a lot that lives on a website they already use for everything else. Source: glamour.co.za

Source: kz.kursiv.media

Source: aboutamazon.com
