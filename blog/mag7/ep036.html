Two-minute simulated worlds with live audio now let Genie 4 testers direct scenes.

By Patrick October 28, 2026 Ep 36 9 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode

What You Need to Know: Google DeepMind's Genie 4, released in research preview, lets trusted testers run interactive worlds and change them while they run, with sound generated in the same pass. Apple has unveiled an M6 iMac from $1,499, with deliveries on November 6, and the FIA has named AWS its AI and cloud partner from club racing through Formula 1. OpenAI plans to buy Google's chips, and a Meta AI director has left to build a workspace aimed at Office and Google Workspace.

Top News

Google DeepMind Releases Genie 4 World Model With Real-Time Editing and Audio: AI Insider

Google DeepMind has released Genie 4 in research preview for trusted testers. Users steer with keyboard controls and plain-language prompts, including mid-simulation changes to weather, camera, people and objects, and the model generates ambient sound, music and dialogue with the picture. MarkTechPost reports scene consistency of up to two minutes, double Genie 3, which it says ran at 720p and 24 frames per second with visual memory of about one minute after an August 2025 announcement. Gemini Omni anchors look and behavior; Gemini 3.8 Flash breaks a goal into steps, checks frames and replans if the user intervenes, and in one demo Gemini 4 Argon split a checkers task into steps that Genie 4 simulated with Apptronik's Apollo humanoid. Research director Tejas Kulkarni said future world models will be hybrids, with Gemini writing code and text while Genie simulates reality. All Genie 4 video and audio will carry SynthID watermarks; a waitlist is open, Google AI Ultra subscribers are due to get it in Project Genie, and early-access Gemini API and Google AI Studio partners follow, with The Hindu listing uses from creative direction and historical scenes to simulation after internal safety evaluations. Source: theaiinsider.tech

Source: marktechpost.com

Source: thehindu.com

Apple Unveils M6 iMac With Faster AI Performance, Starts at $1,499: Techeconomy

Apple has unveiled a 24-inch iMac with the M6 chip, starting at $1,499, with pre-orders from Wednesday, October 28, in 35 countries and regions and deliveries from November 6. Apple says on-device AI is up to five times the M4 iMac, graphics up to twice the M4 model, performance up to 18 times the most popular Intel-based iMac, and LM Studio prompt processing up to 12 times the M1 iMac, with memory bandwidth of 170GB per second and SSD speeds up to twice the prior generation. A 10-core CPU and 10-core GPU model starts at $1,499; a 12-core CPU and 12-core GPU model, two more cores than the previous generation, starts at $1,699, with education pricing of $1,449 and $1,599. The 4.5K Retina display is rated at 500 nits in seven colors, with Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6, macOS 27, a 12-megapixel Centre Stage camera, up to four Thunderbolt 4 ports and support for two 6K external displays. A 14-inch MacBook Pro with M6 starts at $1,999 and is due November 6, and 9to5Mac reports that iMac and that MacBook Pro keep the previous generation's prices. MacRumors says those pre-orders open at 10:10 a.m. Pacific Time, and Bloomberg, via MacRumors, expects still-unannounced OLED touchscreen MacBook Pros to be announced at today's "Pro and tell" event in Los Angeles, with expected prices from $2,899 for 14-inch and at least $3,399 for 16-inch; the already unveiled OLED iPad mini, from $699, opens for pre-order on the same clock. Source: techeconomy.ng

Source: macrumors.com

Source: 9to5mac.com

FIA Partners with AWS to Drive Innovation Across Global Motor Sport: Federation Internationale de l'Automobile

The FIA has named Amazon Web Services its official partner for artificial intelligence, cloud computing, machine learning and cloud infrastructure, from club racing through Formula 1. AWS will help shape technical rules for the next generation of Formula 1 cars; the FIA already runs aerodynamic simulations on AWS high-performance computing so engineers can test airflow without building parts, and AWS will give technical teams AI tools for rules on safety, performance and sustainability. The deal extends the Digital Licensing Platform AWS developed and hosts, which The Next Web says launched in 2025, now reaching more national sporting authorities with more languages, FIA University links and licence renewals, with event management for volunteers and officials planned next. More business applications are set to move to AWS, work the FIA ties to doubling global motor sport participation, and AWS becomes an official partner of the FIA Awards and the FIA Sustainable Innovation Series. The agreement is separate from AWS's deal with Formula 1's commercial rights holder, cloud provider since 2018. FIA president Mohammed Ben Sulayem said the partnership can make it simpler for drivers, officials and volunteers to take part. Source: fia.com

Source: thenextweb.com

OpenAI Plans to Buy Google's Chips as Part of Its AI Build-Out: Bloomberg Technology

OpenAI plans to buy artificial intelligence chips from Alphabet's Google, Bloomberg and The Information report, each citing a person familiar with the matter. Financial terms and other details of the tensor processing unit purchase could not be learned. The Information says OpenAI has been one of Nvidia's most valuable customers, and that Nvidia has invested tens of billions of dollars in the startup. Source: bloomberg.com

Source: theinformation.com

Meta AI director Rohit Patel quits after eight years to take on Microsoft Office and Google Workspace: Indian Television Dot Com

Rohit Patel, a director at Meta Superintelligence Labs, is leaving after eight years to found Ndoc, aimed at replacing Microsoft Office and Google Workspace with one AI-native workspace. Three tabs split the work: Stage for decks, dashboards and mini apps, Narrative for analysis and citations, and Model for datasets and Python scripts, and users can ask the assistant to explain any script or formula. Ndoc is built on fine-tuned open-source models, keeps context across people, documents, teams and companies, and Patel plans to open-source a .ndoc.html format, which he tied to leaving rather than seeking consensus from hundreds of people at Meta. The startup has a team of three, has not raised outside funding, is in an enterprise pilot and limited alpha, and has connectors to import data from existing apps, with a beta expected in the coming weeks and a full launch in 2027. The exit follows Joel Kaplan, Andrew Tulloch and Emily Dalton Smith; OpenAI launched Space, a shared workspace inside ChatGPT, in September. Source: indiantelevision.com

Source: theaiinsider.tech

Nvidia-Backed Firm Launches $3.1 Billion GPU Loan in Market: Bloomberg Technology

Zankore, an Indonesian artificial intelligence infrastructure platform backed by Nvidia, is syndicating a $3.1 billion loan to the broader market, Bloomberg reports, citing people familiar with the matter. The loan adds to a flurry of financing for Asia's AI buildout. Source: bloomberg.com

The Counterpoint

A few minutes, and no ground truth: Trending Topics

DeepMind's own limits, as MarkTechPost lists them, cut against reading Genie 4 as a place to train agents for long: interaction lasts a few minutes, not an extended session, and visual detail and object permanence drift in longer runs, after complex prompt edits, or during navigation, with real-time audio accuracy still unsolved. Trending Topics cites AI writer Ben Dickson, who argues a generated world has no ground truth to check against, so errors in the model could compound when robots are trained in it. The two-minute consistency mark is double Genie 3's visual memory, not a solved session. Source: trendingtopics.eu

Source: marktechpost.com

The Thread: A prospectus, a reseller, and next year's TPU revenue

The Information reports that Anthropic's IPO prospectus records a plan to spend $114.5 billion with Google Cloud, and the filing does not split out how much of that sum is tensor processing unit compute. The same prospectus estimates a further $125 billion to $150 billion on TPUs with Google and other providers, and $161.2 billion for TPU compute with Broadcom, which co-designs the chips with Google. TPUs began as chips Google used only internally, starting more than a decade ago. Outside customers first reached them through Google Cloud in 2018.

Oracle is the new reseller in that stack. The Information says Google has said Oracle will be the first major cloud provider besides Google itself to offer TPUs to customers from Oracle's own data centers. Google began recognizing revenue from selling TPUs into outside data centers last quarter. Chief financial officer Anat Ashkenazi said on that earnings call that only a small portion of revenue from those agreements showed up in the quarter, and that the vast majority kicks in next year.

John Blackledge of TD Cowen, in a report The Information cites from earlier this month, estimates TPU sales of $9.4 billion in the second half of this year, about 13 percent of the roughly $70 billion he forecasts for Google Cloud in that half. By 2028 he thinks TPUs could be a $138 billion business, 34 percent of his Google Cloud revenue estimate for that year. Analysts, on average, expect Nvidia to report $692 billion of revenue in the fiscal year ending January 2028. The Information notes that Blackledge's 2028 TPU figure would still be a fraction of that Nvidia total. Those are his estimate and the analysts' average, not figures Google or Nvidia have booked.

Meta is already a tenant on the same chip. The Information says it reported earlier this year that Meta struck a deal to rent TPUs from Google. Alphabet therefore has three commercial paths in one piece of reporting: a cloud commitment from Anthropic at prospectus scale, a sale channel through other companies' data centers with most of that revenue still ahead, and an existing rental with Meta. Nvidia's side of the comparison is the analyst revenue number, not a new chip announcement in this article. Oracle's role is to put Google silicon in a second cloud's buildings. The next dated marker the source names is next year, when Ashkenazi said the vast majority of outside-data-center TPU agreement revenue is due to be recognized. Source: theinformation.com
