Moonshot scrapped a $150 million Nvidia chip rental after a new U.S. rule.

By Patrick November 19, 2026 Ep 58 9 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top News Company Desk Also Today The Counterpoint The Thread: Scripted aliens, two robot suppliers On the Calendar The Tape

What You Need to Know: Moonshot AI scrapped a $150 million rental of Nvidia Blackwell chips in an Indonesian data center after the U.S. restricted remote access to advanced chips. Google shipped Gemini 4 Neon for coding and long tasks, and New York, Los Angeles and Chicago are weighing a break with Google Search. Amazon opened a shared cache for self-hosted models, and Disney put AWS inside three Star Wars robots.

Top News

China’s Moonshot AI Scraps $150 Million Deal to Access Nvidia Chips in Indonesia: The Information

Moonshot AI, the Beijing developer of the Kimi models, has scrapped a $150 million deal to reach Nvidia Blackwell chips through a data center in Indonesia, after the U.S. this week announced a rule restricting Chinese companies from accessing advanced chips remotely, two people with direct knowledge told The Information. Moonshot had signed with a Southeast Asian neocloud to rent a sizable cluster of Blackwell Ultra B300 chips, with access starting as early as next year. The B300 is barred from sale to China. Remote rental from data centers outside China had been the workaround; the Commerce Department rule released this week closed it. Source: theinformation.com

Gemini 4 Neon: Google's new $3 AI model for coding: DNE Africa

After shipping Gemini 4 Hydrogen for speed, Google on Wednesday released Gemini 4 Neon, placed between Hydrogen and Carbon and aimed at coding, analysis, science and long agent tasks. Google priced it at $3 per million input tokens, $0.15 per million cached inputs and $15 per million output tokens, with a context window of up to 1 million tokens across text, PDFs, charts, audio and video, and four thinking levels. Google said Neon scored 61 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, third overall, and ranked first on that firm’s coding-agent index; InfoWorld reported a coding-agent score of 58.6, plus 65.2 percent on Terminal-Bench 4.1, and dev.ua reported SWE-sweep rising from 12.8 percent in a base shell to 39.0 percent in Antigravity without a model change. Google said a task at the default high setting costs 32 percent less than on Gemini 4 Carbon, and quoted Glean saying Neon matched Carbon’s quality at three times the speed. It replaces Argon for Google AI Pro, $19.99 a month in the U.S.; free users and AI Plus, at $4.99 a month, do not get it, and DNE Africa said Google’s benchmarks have not been independently replicated. Source: africa.dailynewsegypt.com · dev.ua · infoworld.com · notebookcheck.net · digitaltrends.com

The nation’s largest school districts consider ditching Google Search: The Verge

New York, Los Angeles and Chicago schools are considering alternatives to Google after the company said that, starting next year, schools cannot turn off Gemini-powered features without also losing Search, The Verge reported, citing The New York Times. Chris Phillips, Google’s vice president of education, told the Times that “Search and AI are deeply integrated,” and that schools that turn off Google’s AI features “will not have access to Search.” New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Senator Bernie Sanders responded harshly, The Verge said. Source: theverge.com · gizmodo.com

Announcing distributed KV caching for self-hosted LLMs with Amazon ElastiCache: Amazon Web Services

Amazon said distributed KV caching is generally available on ElastiCache for Valkey, a shared cache beside a GPU fleet that stores key-value state computed during prefill. Amazon said it cuts self-hosted inference cost by up to 40 percent and time to first token by up to 80 percent. Existing engines, including vLLM, SGLang and TensorRT-LLM, connect through the open-source LMCache connector with a configuration change and no code changes. In Amazon’s account of tests on B300 GPUs, prefill takes seconds for a 32K-token prompt, tens of seconds for 100K tokens and minutes past 250K; coding-agent traces from SemiAnalysis and TraceLab show more than 95 percent of prompt tokens sit in prefixes already processed. Source: aws.amazon.com

At All Hands, Nvidia Interim CEO Tried To Reassure Employees, Prevent Talent Loss: The Information

At Nvidia’s all-hands on Wednesday, interim CEO Colette Kress tried to steady employees and limit a talent exodus, people present told The Information. She said they can expect pay raises and more equity at the end of this year. Source: theinformation.com

Company Desk

You turned off Gemini history? Google still keeps everything for two months: Korben

Korben reported that turning Gemini history off does not stop Google keeping the chats for two months. Tech Times reported that AI Mode temporary threads have launched with no stated retention, and that Gemini chats are now kept two months. Source: korben.info · techtimes.com

Full Fine-Tuning on SageMaker AI Serverless Model Customization: Amazon Web Services

Amazon said SageMaker AI Serverless Model Customization now supports full fine-tuning, updating model weights without GPU provisioning, cluster setup or a distributed-training configuration. On a Qwen3-4B model and a code-generation set of 110K instruction pairs, with both methods given the same prompts, targets and evaluation code, Amazon reported full fine-tuning at 90.0 percent LLM-as-judge semantic correctness once training passed 100K examples, and said low-rank adaptation plateaus because the update itself is rank-deficient. Source: aws.amazon.com

Meta Platforms Suspends Plan to Issue Non-Voting Shares: Financial Times

Meta has suspended a plan to issue non-voting shares, the Financial Times and MarketScreener reported. Under the deal, Mark Zuckerberg would have been able to sell down his stake without reducing his control, the Financial Times said. Source: ft.com · marketscreener.com

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella explains why he asked 60K employees to quit: TechCrunch

Satya Nadella explained why, in October, he offered buyouts to 60,000 employees rather than cutting through another mass layoff. TechCrunch said the short answer is AI, but not in the way a reader might expect. Source: techcrunch.com

Also Today

Forum, a Dedicated App for Facebook Groups, Is Now Available in Indonesia: Meta Newsroom

Meta said Forum, a dedicated app for Facebook Groups, is now available in Indonesia. Source: about.fb.com

Meta to Hire New India Policy Chief to Repair Frayed Ties: Bloomberg

Meta is scouting for a senior public-policy executive in India to mend a fractured relationship with the government of one of its biggest growth markets, Bloomberg reported. Source: bloomberg.com

Ex-Google Engineer May Face New Charges Over Bets on Gemini 3.0: Bloomberg

Bloomberg reported that an ex-Google engineer may face new charges over bets on Gemini 3.0. Source: bloomberg.com

AWS Lambda adds GPU support for serverless inference: Tech in Asia

AWS Lambda has added GPU support for serverless inference, Tech in Asia reported. Source: techinasia.com

Microsoft Announces Windows Server 2028 With Security-By-Default, Kerberos and Hardened LSASS: NewsCord

Microsoft announced Windows Server 2028 with security-by-default, Kerberos and a hardened LSASS, NewsCord reported. Source: newscord.org

Amidst fears of AI cyberattacks, Satya Nadella says lots of critical infrastructure is ‘unpatchable’: TechCrunch

Satya Nadella told TechCrunch that lots of critical infrastructure is unpatchable, downplaying a recent Outlook outage and saying AI is creating both openings and problems for security. Source: techcrunch.com

US tech outages blamed on rush to fix bugs found by AI: Financial Times

The Financial Times reported experts blaming a rush to patch flaws found by AI for instability at Microsoft, JPMorgan and others. Source: ft.com

AWS Adds Training Validation and Hung-Job Detection to HyperPod: Unite.AI

AWS added training validation and hung-job detection to HyperPod, Unite.AI reported. Source: unite.ai

Exa boosts embedding throughput by 50% to index billions of documents on AWS AI chips: Amazon Web Services

Exa said embedding throughput rose 50 percent on AWS Inferentia2 and Trainium2 while it indexes billions of documents, including a re-embed of 30 billion. Source: aws.amazon.com

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella remains confident in Nvidia after Huang’s exit: TechCrunch

Satya Nadella told TechCrunch he remains confident in Colette Kress as Nvidia’s interim chief executive. Source: techcrunch.com

The Counterpoint

Commerce is still clearing an older Nvidia chip for China: The Information

The Indonesia cancellation does not close every Nvidia path into China. Jeffrey Kessler, who oversees the Bureau of Industry and Security, has pressed staff to speed approvals of Nvidia H200 sales to China, in some cases over objections from other departments, three people told The Information. The H200, launched in 2023 and a generation behind Blackwell, was approved for sale to China in December 2025 by President Donald Trump; Nvidia agreed to give the U.S. government a 25 percent cut of those sales. Direct H200 exports are being hurried through while remote access to Blackwell is what the new rule cut off. Source: theinformation.com

The Thread: Scripted aliens, two robot suppliers

In 1963 Disney opened the Enchanted Tiki Room on audio-animatronics locked to one script. On Wednesday Disney and Amazon described three Anzellan robots — Vilo Vele, Kado Bre’a and Jibu Bre’a — that react to guests, to the workshop and to each other, with the cloud side on AWS.

Kyle Laughlin, senior vice president of Walt Disney Imagineering research and development, technology and engineering, told The Hollywood Reporter the Amazon work goes back more than a decade, from Disney+ into daily Imagineering. The species was in The Rise of Skywalker and in the 2026 film The Mandalorian and Grogu. BigGo Finance said these three were created with Lucasfilm for this project, and that the build took just a few months. Vilo leads. Kado is a droid mechanic who wants attention. Jibu, his twin, is shy. They mix English — Galactic Basic, in the story — with Anzellan, and they pick from a large bank of lines recorded in advance.

Concealed cameras and microphones read the scene. Samira Panah Bakhtiar, AWS general manager for telecommunications, media, entertainment, games, advertising and sports, said each figure tracks three streams at once: the guests in front of it, the workshop, and the other two characters. A fast model handles an instant move, such as eye contact. Larger models shape a longer exchange. Imagineers wrote every action and every line. The model chooses inside that script and does not write new words on the spot. AWS engineers used the coding tool Kiro to turn the ideas into software, and people approved the code. Imagineers also used Amazon Bedrock, which can call foundation models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta by task. Bakhtiar said the cloud tools shortened the path from concept to a working system by orders of magnitude compared with not using them.

Nobody drives the figures from backstage. What they do changes with how many people are present and with whether those people are children or adults. A birthday badge or a particular Star Wars shirt can become the topic. Laughlin said guests want meetings that feel genuine, and that today’s audio-animatronics are scripted in how that meeting happens. BigGo said the three are expected at a Disney park after the conference showing.

Disney is not doing the robot work with one supplier. BigGo reported a robotics partnership with Nvidia, including Olaf from Frozen as a walking robot at Disneyland Paris and at Hong Kong Disneyland, and a guide robot under consideration for a Disneyland parking lot. Laughlin described characters from a single franchise moving and talking together, and he indicated the pattern is meant to spread past Star Wars.

Amazon is supplying the cloud, Kiro and the model switcher for lines a person still wrote. Nvidia already has walking characters in two Disney parks, and a parking-lot guide still on the table. Meta is not building the bodies, but its models are on the Bedrock menu, so the reasoning step need not be an Amazon model. The guest hears a recorded line. The new piece is which company’s software picks it, in the room, with no one behind the curtain. Source: hollywoodreporter.com · finance.biggo.com

On the Calendar

The three Anzellans make their public debut at AWS re:Invent in Las Vegas starting November 30, BigGo reported. Source: finance.biggo.com

On December 1, Google’s API prices for Gemini 4 Carbon and Argon rise to $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens, from $2 and $10 through November 30, Notebookcheck reported from Google’s pricing page. Source: notebookcheck.net
