YouTube is preparing to pay three billion dollars to settle school addiction claims.

By Patrick November 21, 2026 Ep 60 9 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents

What You Need to Know: YouTube is preparing to pay $3 billion to thousands of school districts over claims that its addictive design harmed kids, in a deal Bloomberg sources described to The Verge as agreed in principle, with new safeguards for teens. The Securities and Exchange Commission is probing Nvidia’s accounting, on top of the difficulties that followed Jensen Huang’s resignation. Apple is putting daily limits on server-side Siri AI and Apple Intelligence in iOS 27.3, with higher allowances tied to iCloud+ plans.

Top News

YouTube prepares a $3 billion school-district settlement over addictive design: The Verge

YouTube is preparing to pay $3 billion to thousands of school districts to settle claims that its addictive design harmed kids, The Verge reported, citing Bloomberg. Sources told Bloomberg that YouTube reached the agreement in principle, and that the deal would also include new safeguards for teens. The settlement would let Google avoid a wave of cases. Source: theverge.com

SEC opens an accounting probe as Nvidia deals with Huang’s exit: Financial Times

The Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia’s accounting, the Financial Times reported. The paper said the probe adds to difficulties facing the chip group after Jensen Huang’s resignation. Source: ft.com

iOS 27.3 meters Siri AI and Apple Intelligence, then sells a higher cap: Mashable

Apple is putting daily limits on server-side Siri AI and Apple Intelligence features with iOS 27.3, while basic Siri requests keep working after the advanced allowance is used up. Mashable, citing Apple, said the capped categories are separate: image tools including Clean Up, Extend, Spatial Reframing and Image Playground; AFM Cloud and AFM Cloud Pro models in Shortcuts; and third-party apps that use Apple Foundation Models in Private Cloud Compute. Limits sit on the Apple Account and apply across iPhone, Mac and Apple TV. A limit prompt reads “Daily Request Limit Reached” and “You can continue with basic requests,” with a button to raise the cap through iCloud+. Apple’s published multipliers, as Mashable laid them out: the 50GB plan at $0.99 a month adds nothing; 200GB at $2.99 is up to 2x; 2TB at $9.99 is up to 5x; 6TB at $29.99 is up to 10x; 12TB at $59.99 is up to 20x. People already on an eligible plan get the increase inside that subscription. Limits reset at about midnight in the user’s time zone; travelers reset around midnight in the central time zone of the Apple Account’s country or region; a Family Sharing group resets around midnight in the plan purchaser’s time zone; someone not signed in resets about 24 hours after first use of that category that day. Unused allowance does not roll over. Source: mashable.com

Google drops the desktop Search button and pins Gemini to the homepage: Startup Fortune

Google began rolling out a redesigned desktop homepage on Thursday, November 19, 2026, Robby Stein, VP of Search, said on X. The Google Search button beside I’m Feeling Lucky is gone. Under the box sit Create images, Ask about files and Brainstorm; shattered.io identified the image tool as Nano Banana. A collapsible sidebar holds Gemini chat history and tasks, a New chat control, and, per Engadget’s account in Startup Fortune, AI and ordinary search history, with each entry reopening in AI Mode. Pressing Enter still runs an ordinary search. Stein said: “People are coming to Search with more questions that aren’t just one and done, and in testing, this sidebar made a big difference for making progress and continuing to explore the web.” The rollout is global, though 9to5Google noted availability may vary by account and device. Stein’s announcement covers the desktop homepage, not phone users or the Google app. Earlier server-side tests, including one for signed-out users, let file analysis and brainstorming work without an account while image generation needed a sign-in. Source: startupfortune.com

Google and Penn State describe a memory log so one model’s harness can teach the next: eu.36kr.com

A paper from Google and Pennsylvania State University, reported by 36kr, proposes Continual Recursive Self-Improvement built on Self-Improvement Memory. The memory is a structured log of harness changes — prompts, tools and interception rules — not a rewrite of model weights. Each change stores a rationale and the code touched, predicted outcomes, observed outcomes, and the evaluation context of model and dataset. The account says a harness evolved on Claude Opus scored lower on Gemini than Gemini’s own starting harness, because the new model kept the result and lost the reason. In the cross-model test described, a harness evolved by Claude Opus 5.5, packaged with that memory, was handed to Gemini 3.8 Flash to keep optimizing. Source: eu.36kr.com

Company Desk

AWS Quick builds a business flow from a chat and runs several agents on one prompt: Amazon Web Services

AWS Quick’s new release lets someone describe a process in chat and have Quick build a flow that orchestrates agents, skills and tools with access to structured data, knowledge and apps. Deterministic steps are there to make workflows consistent and verifiable, including longer-running workflows and large data volumes. The updated web app puts attention items and a daily briefing on the home screen, adds desktop agents and skills, and keeps work running after the tab closes. Multi-agent orchestration has several specialized agents work one request at the same time and combine what they find into one response. Quick can connect to Meta ads and Amplitude. Skills can be saved from the web app. Configurable content-safety and sensitive-information controls apply to user prompts. Language support adds Japanese, German, Chinese in Simplified and Traditional, and Vietnamese. In the finance example, a flow drafts a Microsoft Excel summary to a shared Space and tells the team in Slack that the draft is ready. Source: aws.amazon.com

Microsoft retires To Do and points work accounts at Planner: GeekWire

Microsoft is retiring the To Do app, moving work and school users to Planner and leaving personal-account users to pick another app. GeekWire reported that users vented on X and LinkedIn as Todoist, TickTick and Superlist pitched themselves as replacements. Source: geekwire.com

Also Today

Meta reconsiders its 2030 net-zero goal amid data-center emissions: Ars Technica

Ars Technica reported that Meta is re-evaluating its 2030 sustainability goals amid record emissions from AI data centers. Source: arstechnica.com

A U.S. startup sues Apple in India over tap-to-share contacts: TechCrunch

A U.S. startup is seeking $162 million in damages and an injunction that would affect iPhone and Apple Watch imports to India, over a tap-to-share contacts feature. Source: techcrunch.com

iOS 27 puts one phone number on two iPhones: 9to5Mac

iOS 27 lets two iPhones keep the same phone number through iPhone Handoff. Source: 9to5mac.com

Google is shutting down the Nest app and Assistant on home devices: USA Today

Google is shutting down the Nest app and Google Assistant for home devices. Source: usatoday.com

Alabama’s high court dismisses a privacy suit against Meta: AL.com

The Alabama Supreme Court dismissed a privacy lawsuit against Facebook’s parent company. Source: al.com

Siri Recap on the newest watches summarizes talk and then drops the audio: MacRumors

In the watchOS 27.3 beta, Siri Recap on Series 12 and Ultra 4 listens to nearby conversation, writes a short summary, and discards the audio, with no speaker labels and no original recording to check against. Source: macrumors.com

Google’s AI radio tool is meant to help Watch Duty cover all 50 states: Google

Google pointed to a new AI radio tool as the latest step in a multi-year collaboration to help Watch Duty scale its mission across all 50 states. Source: blog.google

Duplex can now place business calls from Gemini: Google Research

Google Research published how Duplex evolved so it can also call businesses for a user inside Gemini. Source: research.google

Yahoo reports Google bought Spirit Airlines data to train Gemini: Yahoo

Yahoo reported that Google bought Spirit Airlines employee and customer data to train its Gemini AI tool. Source: yahoo.com

The Counterpoint

Three billion dollars is still an in-principle number: The Verge

The figure that has to become a filing is the same $3 billion. Bloomberg’s sources, as The Verge reported them, said YouTube reached an agreement in principle, not that a court has entered a judgment or that the districts have been paid. Until that principle is a completed settlement, the wave of cases the deal is meant to avoid is still the live docket, and the teen safeguards travel with the unsigned deal rather than with a ruling. Source: theverge.com

The Thread: The cart, and the door that shuts

Google’s November 20, 2026 post put grocery ordering inside Search and the Gemini app for users in the United States, naming Albertsons, Safeway, Shaw’s and Target. Albertsons, Safeway and Shaw’s are banners of Albertsons Companies. In the published Target example, a request for a quick brunch returns product cards, and the first bakery card is Favorite Day Petite Butter Croissants at $6.49, sold by Target, rated 4.8 stars from 32 reviews. A follow-up that begins with @Target asks for two bagels and cream cheese, strawberries, croissants, cold brew and juice, for pickup. The confirmation adds two Thomas Everything Bagels in an 18oz six-count pack at $3.99 each, quantity two, $7.98, and two Philadelphia Original cream cheese tubs at $4.19 each, quantity two, $8.38, plus Fresh Strawberries, Butter Croissants, Good & Gather Cold Brew Coffee and Tropicana Pure Premium pulp-free orange juice, for pickup at a store named Nashville West in Nashville, Tennessee. The cart panel shows a subtotal of $37.49 for 8 items. Google says the apps help build an order and check out, powered by the Universal Commerce Protocol, the open standard it linked to a January announcement. The same post says Experian, already live on the Gemini app, is rolling into Search so U.S. users can check a credit rating and shop for a personal loan; that H&R Block can calculate taxes and estimate refunds from Search; and that links to Angi and Thumbtack accounts in Search arrive in the coming weeks, for jobs from a leaky faucet to last-minute decorations.

Amazon is closing the other door on its own store. Mezha and The Register reported that Amazon is blocking shopping bots on its main site while AWS Marketplace opens to agent purchases. Google is letting a conversation assemble a retailer’s cart inside Search. Amazon is keeping outside agents off the flagship site and allowing agent buying on the cloud marketplace instead.

The shared dependency is the last step of a purchase: who is allowed to turn a suggestion into an order, and on whose system the cart lives. Google’s protocol is the pipe it wants agents and merchants to share, dropped onto the page people already open, next to a chat sidebar that keeps the thread. Amazon’s block keeps third-party agents away from the catalogue, the reviews and the fulfillment on the main site, and points permitted agent spending at infrastructure the company already bills as cloud. One assistant is being invited into the grocer’s cart. The other company’s store is being reserved for shoppers Amazon is willing to serve itself.

For Alphabet, a holiday list, a credit check, a tax estimate and, within weeks, a home-services booking can start in Search, so the retailer’s app is no longer the only front door to its own inventory. For Amazon, an outside shopping agent that cannot enter the main site has to stop, hand the task to a person, or buy through the marketplace path AWS is opening. The next marker Google actually dated in prose, not on a calendar, is those Angi and Thumbtack links, described as arriving in the coming weeks. Source: ppc.land Source: mezha.net Source: theregister.com

Some cookies keep the site working. Before you choose, Google gets only basic, cookie-free signals (for example, that a page loaded), with no cookies or IDs that identify you. With your OK you can also turn on: Analytics, which uses Google Analytics cookies to show us which pages and episodes are useful, and Ad measurement, which uses Google Ads to measure sign-ups from our ads. When you sign up from a Nerra Personal page, Ad measurement also sends Google a coded (hashed) version of your email. Change your mind any time from Cookie settings at the bottom of every page.
