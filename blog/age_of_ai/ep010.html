Roddy de la Garza

Broker /Author / Area Leader, Roddy de la Garza Real Estate Group @ EPIQUE

A veteran real estate broker who trains AI on brokerage systems weighs what judgment, intuition, and human connection he refuses to delegate while seeking spiritual balance through daily mindwork.

In their own words

A Harlingen, Texas native and University of Texas at Austin graduate, Roddy grew up in a family brokerage spanning more than 50 years—measuring houses and hanging signs by age eight. His own sales journey began with $25,000 duplexes and eventually reached $4 million homes in Beverly Hills: a trajectory he never imagined, built by saying yes to every opportunity. Those opportunities carried him through real estate FinTech, media, brokerage leadership, home staging, publishing, and more than $400 million in real estate sales. He has led and supported over 8,000 agents across California and Texas while remaining close to the work: clients, contracts, culture, and the messy middle where leadership actually happens.

What you need to know

Roddy de la Garza said the most surprising thing he has learned while training AI on brokerage tasks is that the models still cannot read the psychological “feel” of a property or talk a client off an emotional cliff, so he refuses to delegate any conversation involving debt, divorce, or death.

What we talked about
  • He staged a West Hollywood condo to feel like “coming home” instead of “someone’s left-behind furniture,” producing the only sale above list price in the building in two years and closing in 14 days.
  • After his father’s death, Roddy created the daily devotional Above Asking so agents could practice gratitude and boundaries without buying another coach or portal.
  • He replicated the seasonal split he knew growing up in Texas’s Rio Grande Valley—year-round residents and winter Texans—by dividing his own practice between Hollywood and Palm Springs.
  • When he experimented with an AI receptionist, callers hung up rather than leave messages; he dropped the tool because it created “cold distance” and lost direct human connection.
  • On his 22-person team he assigns most of his own volume to others and adjusts splits for tenured agents, finding that “the more I’ve given away, the more I’ve gotten in return.”
  • After solving a client’s request for non-gendered title language on a married couple’s deed, he required the solution to be taught to two other team members so the knowledge spread.
