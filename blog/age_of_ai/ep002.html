The Age of AI · Episode 2

Dan Perra

Co-Founder Nerra Network and Boeing 737 Captain, Nerra Network

A podcast network operator who pushes aviation's hard-won rules on spotting subtle automation errors and preserving human skill must square them against his own AI-written shows that already released a factual error under lighter oversight.

Listen to the conversation
September 17, 2026 ·24 min ·Read the transcript

Automation has been on the other side of the controls from me for my whole working life. I fly the 737 across North America and Europe, where automation is mature, heavily regulated, and still has two qualified humans accountable for everything it does. I've been an early beta tester for Tesla FSD, where almost none of that scaffolding exists and I signed up anyway. And I co-run the Nerra Network with Pat, sixteen-odd podcasts written and voiced almost entirely by AI — so I'm also the one deploying it at people rather than receiving it. I work with Pat nearly every day on where the network goes next, and I listen to almost every episode across the English shows to work out what's landing and what isn't.

What you need to know

A Boeing 737 captain who treats every cockpit decision as non-negotiable still lets AI-written podcast episodes containing factual errors keep running, calling the contrast between the two worlds “somewhat humorous.” Dan Perra describes aviation automation as a tool that never thinks for itself and always requires layered human oversight, while the Nerra Network’s lighter process accepts source-driven mistakes and moves on. The result is an interview that keeps circling the same practical question: how much of the cockpit discipline can, or should, travel into daily AI production.

What we talked about
  • Aviation’s two-pilot rule and triple-redundancy standard keep the autopilot strictly under human command; Dan notes the system “does absolutely no thinking for itself whatsoever,” unlike the Nerra workflow.
  • After an episode aired with a factual error, the network’s check was simply a discussion; the episode stayed up because “we do let it run.”
  • Dan frames the error not as process failure but as reflection of available source material, then shifts focus to refining inputs rather than halting release.
  • He draws a hard line between domains: “primary goal is a safe flight every time” in the cockpit versus “get a good episode and you know hope it turns out” for the network.
  • The same person who built a meal-planning app that worked for him but not his wife now listens to every Nerra episode himself to catch when the AI “starts going astray.”
  • Patrick Novak prompted the conversation toward career-long use of AI tools; Dan responded that any future single-pilot long-haul concept would still need backups capable of handling every edge case the aircraft might encounter.
