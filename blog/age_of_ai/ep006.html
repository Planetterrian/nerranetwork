The Age of AI

How a founder embedding AI agents into enterprise software delivery multiplies senior judgment across programs while preserving human gates, and what that shift does to accountability, quality, and the wider engineering workforce.

Episode 6 · September 21, 2026

What You Need to Know: Matt Davis described the most surprising effect of embedding AI agents in enterprise software delivery as the way senior judgment scales across many programs at once, while human decision points remain fixed at key gates. He explained that this arrangement changes how accountability and quality are managed, and it alters demand for different engineering roles. Patrick Novak joined parts of the discussion to clarify implications for teams.

About Matt Davis

Co-founder, Critical Propulsion

https://criticalpropulsion.com/who-we-are/team

What we talked about

  • Davis noted that AI agents now draft and review large sections of code and test plans, yet “the human still owns the final gate on every release.”
  • He observed that quality metrics improved because agents surface inconsistencies faster than manual reviews alone, but only when the senior engineer’s criteria are explicitly encoded.
  • On accountability, Davis stated that responsibility stays with the named individual who approves the output, even when most of the material was generated by agents.
  • He described a shift in hiring: fewer junior coders are needed for routine tasks, while demand rises for engineers who can define precise rules and audit agent behavior.
  • Davis said the model works only when the organization treats the agents as “force multipliers for existing judgment, not replacements for it.”
  • He warned that without clear human gates, teams lose traceability and begin to over-trust agent suggestions on edge cases.

Matt Davis is Co-founder at Critical Propulsion.
