The Age of AI · Episode 17

Luke Miler

Founder of DCNY

A subscription engineering founder shows how pairing senior developers with AI agents at unlimited volume turns project friction into always-on delivery, and which bottlenecks still demand human oversight.

In their own words

Luke Miler is the founder of DCNY, which combines experienced engineers and AI coding agents. He started building with computers in post-communist Poland and learned programming, design, music, and visuals in the demoscene. He grew a one-person agency into a team of more than 50, then sold it to a local private equity fund. DCNY reached seven figures in recurring revenue in its first year.

What you need to know

Luke Miler revealed that AI agents now produce 95% of DCNY's code, with humans handling only the first 15% of scoping and the final 5% of review and iteration. This subscription service converts senior engineering capacity into an always-available resource that removes traditional hiring friction while keeping human judgment in charge of direction, priorities, and customer orchestration. The result is faster experimentation for clients and a hiring filter that values product sense over raw coding speed.

What we talked about
  • Luke traced the original bottleneck to the difficulty of finding and retaining strong engineers quickly, whether through agencies, full-time hires, or platforms like Upwork, which led him to design DCNY as a simple subscription that delivers capacity in minutes.
  • The first remembered client was an agency needing parallel work on client websites and e-commerce builds, treated initially as a vendor but quickly becoming an embedded team extension.
  • Hiring now favors candidates who have built their own side projects, because those demonstrate the product-management decisions agents cannot yet make.
  • Luke ends the vetting process with an in-person dinner or drinks test, drawn from Tim Minchin, to observe how candidates treat service staff and confirm cultural fit.
  • The 1585 workflow keeps humans responsible for the opening 15% of requirements and the closing 5% of edits while agents execute the middle 80%, with human oversight expanding only when clients introduce frequent changes.
  • Clients are shifting from committing to a single idea for years toward shipping and measuring multiple versions quickly, since additional capacity no longer requires per-project justification.
  • Luke's current bet is that token prices will fall sharply within twelve months, so the team optimizes for customer value rather than token spend today.
