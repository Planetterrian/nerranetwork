The Age of AI · Episode 9

Sheldon Poon

CEO Drive Marketing, Drive Marketing

In performance marketing agencies, AI delivers returns not through creative generation but by enforcing the tracking discipline and attribution fixes most competitors neglect, turning platform algorithms into reliable revenue engines.

I have been a Teacher, Dev Op, Software Developer, and AI expert. We use Data and train AI algorithms to run ads that out perform our competition 2:1. in 2025, we had $1.4M under management and generated $21M+ for our clients our secret is that we have figured out how to combine proven marketing strategy (that's over 100yrs old) and cutting edge AI and Data technology. this combination has proven across industries to work every time. it's not for everyone because it's a lot of work to get right but when it works, it pays back $10 for every dollar spent on Meta or Google

What you need to know

Sheldon Poon says the biggest platforms' predictive AI only delivers reliable returns once humans manually fix the tracking and attribution pipelines that most setups neglect. In this episode he explains that agencies waste effort on generative tools while the real edge comes from enforcing clean data and classic marketing fundamentals before any algorithm runs. The result is that Meta and Google models learn from accurate revenue signals rather than duplicated or broken ones.

What we talked about
  • Human developers still handle all tracking fixes because every e-commerce site and ad platform combination carries unique nuances that current automation cannot resolve reliably.
  • Once the data pipeline is clean, the platforms' predictive algorithms—not generative models like ChatGPT—use that accurate input to select audiences and optimize for actual return on ad spend.
  • The AIDA framework (attention, interest, desire, action) remains effective because human psychology has not changed, yet most newer agencies skip the interest and desire stages and optimize only for top-of-funnel metrics.
  • Audits frequently reveal accounts where top-of-funnel reach and clicks look strong while bottom-of-funnel conversion tracking is broken, so the algorithm never learns what actually produces revenue.
  • Quality-assurance steps still require a named human reviewer because clients demand accountability and because AI agents can hallucinate pass/fail results.
  • Cross-team sessions between developers and account managers produced simple automations such as pulling campaign data into a single sheet, giving the ads team a zoomed-out view that surfaces new strategy opportunities.
  • Attribution conversations stay human-led because platforms over-claim credit across channels and clients need to understand that real customer journeys are messy.
