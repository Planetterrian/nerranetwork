Models & Agents for Beginners

Midjourney just went from drawing cats to building a real medical scanner.

An AI company famous for generating images is now making hardware that scans your whole body with sound waves. This matters because it shows how fast AI ideas are jumping from screens into the physical world around us. We'll also explore a simple way to build websites with AI help and check a couple of quick updates on self-driving cars and public opinion.

The Big Story

Midjourney, the company that made it easy for anyone to type a sentence and get a picture back, just showed off its first piece of actual hardware. Instead of another image tool, they built a full-body scanner that uses rings of sensors and sound waves to create detailed pictures inside your body. Think of it like turning the same clever pattern-spotting that makes pretty art into something that can look for health issues without needing a big hospital machine. The scanner creates a vertical slice as you stand inside the ring, giving doctors or even everyday users a quick, non-invasive view. This shift matters because AI tools are no longer stuck on your screen—they're starting to touch real things like health and daily life. For a student curious about medicine or a parent thinking about future check-ups, it hints that scans could one day happen at a local spa-like spot instead of only in clinics. It also raises the question of how companies that started with creative fun might help solve practical problems like reducing strain on medical systems. If this direction works, it could make proactive health checks feel more normal and less scary. Right now there is no public demo you can try, but the announcement itself is worth watching because it shows one company's surprising next step from pictures to physical tools. Source: theverge.com

Explain Like I'm 14

You know how when you're playing a city-building game, you place roads and buildings one piece at a time and suddenly the whole town feels alive and connected? Now imagine the game gets much smarter: instead of you placing every single road, the game starts guessing the best next piece based on everything already built, and it keeps doing that over and over until a full working system appears. Then picture swapping the roads for sound waves bouncing through a body—the system predicts the clearest path for each wave, fills in the blanks, and builds a complete inner picture without anyone drawing it by hand. And that's basically what the new Midjourney scanner is doing when it turns simple sensor readings into a full-body view. It takes the same step-by-step prediction trick that makes image generators work and applies it to real-world signals. So next time someone says "ultrasound hardware from an AI company," you can tell them—it is basically a city-builder that works with sound instead of pixels. Not so scary, right?

Cool Stuff & Try This

Build a whole website by chatting with AI

A free template pack on GitHub lets you point any AI chatbot at a folder of ready-made designs and say exactly what kind of site you want. The AI looks at the templates, asks you a couple of quick questions about colors or style, then builds a working frontend page for you. It is perfect if you have ever wanted a cool online portfolio, fan site, or project page but do not know how to code. Go to the GitHub link, download the WebTemplate folder, then open ChatGPT, Claude, or any similar chatbot and type: "Make me a site about my favorite game using the templates in this folder." The chatbot will scan the designs and create something you can view in your browser right away. No coding needed—just chat and watch it appear. Source: reddit.com

See 20 different AI models build the same harbor town

A simple online gallery lets you watch how twenty different AI systems each create a 3D harbor that grows from ancient times all the way to the future. You can click through the versions, pause on the ones you like, and even control the camera to explore the town. It is a fun way to compare how creative different AIs can be when given the exact same request. Head to ai-harbor-town-gallery.netlify.app on your phone or laptop and just start clicking through the gallery—no sign-up required. Source: x.com

Quick Bits

Waymo pulls back over 3,800 robotaxis

The self-driving taxi company is fixing a software glitch that sometimes made cars try to drive onto closed freeway construction zones. It is a reminder that even advanced AI cars still need careful testing before they can handle every real-world surprise. Source: engadget.com

Most Americans say AI is moving too fast

A new poll found that 63 percent of people think the technology is advancing quicker than feels comfortable, even though more of us are using chatbots than ever before. It is interesting to see that excitement and caution are growing at the same time. Source: theverge.com
