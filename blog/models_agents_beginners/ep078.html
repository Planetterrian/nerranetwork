ChatGPT just added a simple way to schedule tasks so your AI helper can check things for you while you sleep.

Models & Agents for Beginners

ChatGPT just added a simple way to schedule tasks so your AI helper can check things for you while you sleep.

OpenAI upgraded ChatGPT so it can run tasks on a schedule and only alert you when something actually changes. This moves the chatbot one step closer to being a true personal assistant you can trust with everyday jobs. Today we’ll look at how that works, peek inside a system that uses seven AIs like a tiny newsroom, try the new Siri, and check one country’s decision about AI in schools.

The Big Story

OpenAI added a new “Scheduled” page inside ChatGPT that shows every task you’ve asked it to run later. Instead of typing the same request every day, you can now tell ChatGPT to check something at a set time and only message you if the answer is different. Think of it like setting an alarm on your phone, except the alarm is an AI that quietly watches the web or your connected apps and only wakes you up when something worth knowing happens. The old “Pulse” feature that did something similar is being retired, so this new page becomes the single place to manage everything. For students this could mean asking ChatGPT to watch for new scholarship deadlines or changes to a sports schedule and text you only when something moves. For anyone who follows a hobby like music releases or game updates, it means less time refreshing pages and more time doing the thing you actually enjoy. The change matters because it turns ChatGPT from a tool you have to remember to open into something that can quietly work in the background. You can already try it if you have ChatGPT Plus: open the sidebar, click the new Scheduled page, and set a simple repeating task like “check for new episodes of my favorite podcast and tell me only if one drops.” The system will handle the rest and send an alert only when it finds something new. Source: the-decoder.com

Explain Like I'm 14

You know how when you’re working on a big group project at school, one person researches, another writes the slides, and someone else checks the facts so the whole thing actually makes sense? Now imagine seven different AIs doing exactly that job together. One AI acts like the lead reporter and turns a plain spreadsheet of numbers into a full news story. Another AI searches the web for extra facts. A third AI draws the charts. A fourth AI double-checks every sentence and adds links so readers can verify the information. The whole team works like a tiny newsroom that never gets tired. When the system finishes, 93 percent of the statements it makes have clickable sources, and in one test most readers actually preferred the AI version over the human-written article. That’s basically what Data2Story does when it turns a boring CSV file into an interactive, fact-checked story. So next time someone says “multi-agent system,” you can picture seven AIs passing work back and forth the same way your project group does—just faster and without anyone forgetting their part. Source: the-decoder.com

Cool Stuff & Try This

Siri finally feels like a real helper

Apple’s updated Siri is now conversational and can actually stay helpful across a whole conversation instead of forgetting what you just said. It lives on your iPhone and can handle normal requests without you having to phrase things perfectly. If you have an iPhone, just hold the side button or say “Hey Siri” and try asking it to plan a simple route or remind you about something later in the day. The new version is designed to feel more like texting a helpful friend than barking commands at a robot. Source: wired.com

Norway is hitting pause on AI for the youngest kids

Starting late August, Norway will not allow generative AI tools in grades 1 through 7 so children can first master reading, writing, and math without shortcuts. Secondary schools can still use the tools, but only with teacher supervision. The rule is meant to protect basic learning skills before kids start relying on AI for everything. It’s a clear signal that some countries want to slow down and make sure the technology helps rather than replaces early education. Source: the-decoder.com

Quick Bits

More people are asking AI for news, but they still don’t fully trust it

A new report shows that 10 percent of people worldwide now check AI chatbots for news every week, up from 7 percent last year. Only 4 percent actually click through to the original source, which means most people are getting summaries without seeing the full story. Source: the-decoder.com

A new test shows even the smartest AIs still struggle with real work

Researchers built a benchmark that mimics actual knowledge-work tasks, and the best models could only fully solve 3 percent of them. The gap between “good at homework questions” and “good at messy real jobs” is still huge. Source: the-decoder.com
