Your smart speaker can now write and record an entire podcast on any topic you name.

Models & Agents for Beginners

Your smart speaker can now write and record an entire podcast on any topic you name.

Amazon just added a feature to Alexa Plus that lets it create full AI-generated podcasts from scratch. This turns a simple voice assistant into something that can research, script, and voice an audio show while you steer the topic. Today we break down how that works, explore the tech behind turning text into natural conversation, and share quick ways to experiment with similar creative AI tools yourself.

The Big Story

Amazon has updated its Alexa Plus assistant so it can now generate complete podcasts on almost any subject you request. You simply tell it a topic, and the system creates an overview of what two AI hosts will discuss, then lets you guide the direction of the conversation before it produces the finished audio episode.

Think of it like having a tiny production studio inside your speaker. Instead of just answering questions one at a time, Alexa now plans a whole back-and-forth discussion, writes lines for different “hosts,” and records them with natural-sounding voices. It’s a step beyond basic text-to-speech because the AI is building structure and flow, not just reading words.

This matters because podcasts are already how many teens and students learn about new ideas, from history to science to gaming. Being able to create one instantly means you could turn a homework topic into an audio summary to listen to while walking or doing chores. It also shows how AI is moving from quick answers to helping with longer creative projects.

For you personally, this could change how you study or explore hobbies. Need a quick explainer on a game update or a tricky math concept? Ask for a short podcast instead of reading another article. The feature is rolling out now to Alexa Plus users, so if you have an Echo device with the upgrade, try saying something like “Alexa, create a podcast about how video games are made” and see what it produces. Source: theverge.com

Explain Like I'm 14

You know how when you text a friend, your phone sometimes suggests the next word before you finish typing? Now imagine that same idea, but instead of guessing one word, the system guesses an entire back-and-forth conversation between two people.

First it reads your topic and decides what the two hosts should cover in order. Then it writes lines for each host that sound like real talk, including questions and replies that connect naturally. Next it picks different voices for each host and adds small pauses or changes in tone so it doesn’t sound robotic. Finally it stitches everything together into one audio file you can play like any podcast.

And that’s basically what Alexa is doing when it builds these AI-generated shows. It’s using the same word-prediction trick at a much larger scale to create the whole script and then turn that script into spoken audio. So next time someone says “AI-generated podcast,” you can tell them — it’s basically supercharged autocomplete that also knows how to talk to itself. Not so scary, right?

Cool Stuff & Try This

Make a pro-looking explainer video for under a dollar: Reddit (r/artificial)

A user shared a full workflow that combines Claude’s design tools with Eleven Labs for voice to create short animated videos that line up perfectly with audio. The trick is feeding the AI both the script and timing information so the visuals match the spoken words. Anyone who likes making YouTube-style explainers or school project videos should try this. Go to claude.ai, ask it to write a short script about something you’re learning, then use a free TTS site like Eleven Labs to record the audio. Feed the audio timestamps back into Claude to generate the matching video. Source: reddit.com

Watch Google’s big AI announcements live: Engadget

Google’s annual I/O keynote happens tomorrow and is expected to show off new AI features across Android and other products. You don’t need any special setup — just a browser or phone. Head to the official Google I/O website or YouTube channel on Tuesday morning and watch the livestream. Pause and try any new tools they demo right after the show ends. Source: engadget.com

Quick Bits

World’s first color lidar sensor for robots and cars: Live Science

Ouster released a new lidar that captures both 3D shape and full color at the same time. This lets self-driving cars and robots “see” the world more like humans do instead of just in flat point clouds. Source: livescience.com

Pope to release first major statement on AI: The Decoder

Pope Leo XIV will present an encyclical focused on artificial intelligence later this month, with an Anthropic co-founder invited to speak. It’s one of the highest-profile ethical discussions about AI happening right now. Source: the-decoder.com
