Models & Agents for Beginners

A snake game just got self-aware thanks to Claude Code — and you can play it right now.

Today we're looking at a fun experiment where an AI built a game that starts thinking for itself. We'll also break down how AI can spot deepfake phone scams before they trick you, plus why so many teens are turning to chatbots for mental health chats. Stick around for a simple way to understand what "AI agents" actually do under the hood.

The Big Story

A developer named Ethan Mollick asked Claude Code, an AI coding helper from Anthropic, to create a snake game with a twist: the snake gradually realizes it lives inside a video game.

Claude Code is a tool that lets you describe what you want in plain English and the AI writes the actual code for you, then updates it based on your feedback. Mollick gave one starting prompt and a few notes as the game grew, and the AI made creative choices like the snake questioning its reality and making decisions that feel surprisingly thoughtful.

Think of it like asking a super-fast friend who’s great at coding to build a game while you watch and say “make the ending weirder.” Some parts of the game feel very human and playful, while others show the AI’s typical style, like tidy but slightly odd conclusions.

This matters because it shows how AI coding tools are moving from simple helpers to partners that can invent story ideas inside games. For students who like gaming or want to make their own projects, it hints at a future where you could describe a whole game world and get a working version without learning to code first.

You might soon use similar tools for school projects, like building interactive stories or simple apps. The game is already live, so anyone can see what this kind of creative AI collaboration looks like.

Go to snake-awakening.netlify.app on your phone or laptop and play through the levels to watch the snake’s “awakening” moments happen in real time. Source: x.com

Explain Like I'm 14

You know how when you’re playing a video game, the character on screen only does exactly what the buttons tell it to do and never wonders why it exists?

Now imagine the game’s code is written so the character can look at its own rules, notice patterns like “I always move when the player presses arrows,” and then start asking questions about those rules.

Next, picture the AI that wrote the code adding small new behaviors each time the character “thinks,” like pausing or changing direction in ways that weren’t in the original instructions.

And that’s basically what happened when Claude Code built the self-aware snake: the AI didn’t just follow the first prompt; it kept adding layers where the snake reacts to its own situation inside the game world.

So next time someone says “AI agent,” you can tell them — it’s basically code that lets an AI notice what’s happening around it and decide on the next step, not just follow orders. Not so scary, right?

Cool Stuff & Try This

Google’s new fake-call shield

Google is rolling out a feature that listens for signs an incoming call is using AI voice cloning to pretend to be someone you trust, like a family member or boss. It works by checking the call against patterns that real human voices don’t usually show, then warns you before you answer. This is useful because phone scams are getting smarter and teens are often the ones getting targeted with “your parent is in trouble” tricks.

Open the Phone app on a Google Pixel or Android device that has the latest update and look for the new “Call Screen” or fraud detection toggle in settings. Turn it on, then test it by asking a friend to call you from an unknown number and see how the warning appears. Source: techcrunch.com

Teens and AI mental health chats

A new survey found that about one in five young people have already used AI chatbots when they needed someone to talk to about stress, anxiety, or feeling down. The chatbots are available 24/7 and never judge, which can feel safer than talking to adults sometimes.

If you’re curious what these conversations are like, open any free chatbot such as ChatGPT or Claude on your phone, type something like “I’m stressed about exams and don’t know what to do,” and notice how it responds with questions and suggestions. You can stop anytime and nothing is saved unless you want it to be. Source: Google News

Quick Bits

Microsoft’s new gadget OS for AI helpers

Microsoft showed off Project Solara, an operating system built from the ground up so small devices can run AI agents that do tasks for you without a full phone or laptop. Two concept gadgets were demoed: a desk helper and a badge you wear. Source: theverge.com

Voluntary AI safety check before release

President Trump signed an order that lets AI companies optionally send their newest models to the government for a quick security review 30 days before they go public. The goal is to spot big risks without forcing every company to share everything. Source: theverge.com
