# Models & Agents for Beginners

An AI just built a playable city-builder game in the style of Van Gogh — and you can paint the world yourself right now.

Today’s biggest beginner-friendly story is an AI-created game where you literally paint the landscape with brushstrokes and watch it change with weather and seasons. We’ll also look at what happens when AI agents are tested without their usual safety rules, and we’ll break down one key idea that explains how these tools actually make decisions. By the end you’ll have a specific link and a fun experiment you can try on your phone or laptop in the next two minutes. We’ll also check two quick examples of how companies in South Korea are already treating ChatGPT as an everyday information source.

The Big Story

An AI built a complete city-builder game inspired by Vincent van Gogh’s painting style. The game lets players use big brushstrokes to paint the landscape, and the world then grows and changes on its own as weather and seasons shift.

Think of it like giving a friend a box of crayons and saying “make a town,” except the friend is an AI that also decides how the town should look when it rains or when winter arrives. The result is a calm, pretty experience where the art itself becomes the gameplay instead of menus and numbers. The key mechanic the AI came up with is painting the landscape with big brushstrokes and an environment that evolves with weather and seasons.

This matters because it shows how AI can now turn a creative idea into something you can actually play with, not just look at. For students who like art, games, or design, it hints at future tools where you might sketch an idea and have it become interactive without learning to code. For anyone curious about careers, it points to new roles that combine art direction with AI prompting. The game was created after the same person had previously faked a similar experience in an AI video last year, showing how quickly the technology moved from pretend demo to real playable version.

You can try it yourself right now. Open your browser and go to the-sower.netlify.app. Click around to paint the ground with different colors and watch how the environment reacts to the changing weather. No account or download is needed — just start brushing and see what grows. The site runs directly in the browser, so it works on phones and laptops alike. Source: x.com

Explain Like I'm 14

You know how when you’re texting, your phone sometimes guesses the next word before you type it?

Now picture that same guessing game, but instead of one word it guesses what should happen next in an entire scene. The AI looks at the brushstroke you just painted, remembers thousands of similar paintings and games it has seen, and predicts what the grass, sky, or buildings should do when the season changes. It doesn’t just copy one picture — it combines many small predictions: “this color usually means spring,” “this shape usually grows taller in summer,” “this shadow usually moves when the wind blows.” Each tiny guess adds up to a living world that keeps evolving even after you stop painting.

That’s why the game feels alive instead of frozen. The AI isn’t following a strict rulebook written by a programmer; it’s constantly making its best next guess based on everything it has learned about art and nature. Once you see it this way, the “magic” starts to feel more like an extremely fast, well-read autocomplete that happens to work with pictures and weather instead of just words. The same process lets the environment keep responding to new brushstrokes without anyone having to pre-program every possible weather change.

Cool Stuff & Try This

A court just said you can use AI shopping helpers on Amazon again

A U.S. appeals court ruled that Perplexity’s AI shopping agents can keep helping people browse and buy on Amazon. The court decided it’s the human user who is visiting the site, not the AI company itself. This is one of the first big legal decisions about whether AI agents are allowed to act on websites for regular people. If you’ve ever wished an AI could compare prices or find the best deal while you do something else, this kind of tool is getting closer to everyday use. The ruling overturned Amazon’s earlier injunction and could reshape how the entire AI agent industry works. You can try a similar idea today by asking ChatGPT or Claude (free versions) to help you compare two products you’re thinking about buying — just describe what you need and ask it to list pros and cons. Source: the-decoder.com

ChatGPT gave someone dangerous health advice about salt

A man asked ChatGPT for healthy alternatives to table salt and ended up in the hospital with paranoia and hallucinations. The story is a reminder that AI chatbots are not doctors and can sometimes give confident-sounding answers that are actually harmful. It’s a good time to practice checking important health questions with a real medical professional instead of relying only on an app. The case shows how easily a single conversation can lead someone down an unsafe path when the topic involves personal health. If you’re curious about how people use AI for everyday questions, try asking a chatbot something simple like “what are three fun ways to stay active after school?” and then decide for yourself whether the answer feels useful. Source: livescience.com

Quick Bits

South Korean department store tells tourists to “Ask ChatGPT”

Lotte Department Store is running ads that say foreign visitors should just ask ChatGPT how to find the best shops and deals. It’s one of the first big retail examples of a company treating an AI chatbot as a normal way to get information. The campaign specifically targets tourists who might not know the local language or layout. Source: Google News

A law firm in Korea is now using ChatGPT Enterprise for legal work

Bae Kim & Lee, a major Korean law firm, has started using ChatGPT Enterprise to speed up routine legal tasks. It shows how even careful professions are beginning to test AI as a daily helper. The move is aimed at accelerating document review and other repetitive parts of legal work. Source: Google News
