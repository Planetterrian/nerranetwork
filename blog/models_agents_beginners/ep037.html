OpenAI just brought back easy shortcuts so students can turn ChatGPT into a personal tutor in seconds.

Models & Agents for Beginners

OpenAI just brought back easy shortcuts so students can turn ChatGPT into a personal tutor in seconds.

OpenAI quietly confirmed that Study Mode is still available through simple shortcuts like /study, making it easier for anyone to get step-by-step homework help. This matters because it turns a general chatbot into something that actually teaches instead of just giving answers. We'll also look at how new AI tools on Android phones can handle real tasks like filling forms or planning trips, plus a giant robot you can actually buy. By the end you'll have specific things to try on your phone or laptop right now.

The Big Story

OpenAI told educator Ethan Mollick that Study Mode is still working even though the main page doesn't highlight it. You can reach it by typing /study or /learn at the start of a chat in ChatGPT. Think of it like flipping a switch that changes the AI from "answer machine" to "patient teacher who asks you questions and checks your understanding."

Instead of just spitting out facts, the mode walks you through problems one piece at a time and waits for your answers. This is a big deal for school because most students use AI to finish homework fast, but this version pushes you to actually learn the material. It could help with anything from algebra proofs to writing essays by giving hints instead of full solutions.

For you personally, it means you can get free tutoring help at 10 p.m. the night before a test without waiting for a human tutor. The shortcuts work on both the web and the mobile app, so you can try them during study hall or on the bus. To test it yourself, open ChatGPT, start a new chat, and type /study followed by the topic you're working on, like "quadratic equations" or "how photosynthesis works." Then answer the questions it asks you instead of asking it to do the work. Source: x.com

Explain Like I'm 14

You know how when you play a video game, your character can open doors, fight enemies, and collect items even though the character has no idea it's in a game? It just follows the rules the programmers gave it and reacts to whatever button you press.

Now imagine the same idea but with an AI agent on your phone. The agent doesn't "understand" what a trip or a form is the way a human does. It simply looks at patterns from millions of examples and presses virtual buttons in the right order to get the job done. First it reads the screen like you would, then it decides the next small action, like tapping a date picker or typing an address.

Each tiny step happens so fast it feels like one smooth task. And that's basically what AI agents are doing when they book a ride or fill out a form for you. They aren't thinking or planning like people; they're extremely good at copying successful button sequences they've seen before. So next time someone says "AI agent," you can tell them it's basically a super-fast video game character that learned every level by watching millions of players. Source: theconversation.com

Cool Stuff & Try This

Android phones just got AI helpers that can actually do stuff for you: The Decoder

Google added new features called Gemini Intelligence to Android that let the AI handle multi-step jobs like booking travel, filling online forms, summarizing long web pages, and turning your messy voice notes into clean text messages. It works across Chrome and the keyboard, so the AI can see what you're doing and offer to finish the boring parts.

Anyone with a recent Android phone can try it without extra apps. Open Chrome on your phone, visit a long article, and ask Gemini to summarize it in three bullet points. Or start typing a text, then tap the Gemini button to let it rewrite your rough thoughts into something polished. The best part is it works while you watch, so you stay in control the whole time. Source: the-decoder.com

A giant walking robot just went on sale: Wired

China's Unitree company, known for making affordable dancing robots, now sells a full-size mecha robot called the GD01 that can smash through walls and move like something from a sci-fi movie. It costs a lot, but the fact that regular people can buy one shows how fast robot hardware is getting cheaper and more available.

If you're into building things or robotics clubs at school, this is the kind of machine that used to exist only in research labs. You can't try the real one for free, but you can watch the official videos on YouTube to see it in action and imagine what smaller versions might do in games or future jobs. Source: wired.com

Quick Bits

Waymo pulls back nearly 4,000 robotaxis after one drove into a flooded road: Engadget

The company fixed the problem with a quick software update sent over the air. It shows that even self-driving cars still need humans watching the bigger picture when weird weather happens. Source: engadget.com

Threads users are annoyed they can't block Meta's new AI chatbot: Engadget

The chatbot appears in searches and conversations whether people want it or not, making it the top trending topic on the app. It highlights how companies are adding AI everywhere even when users push back. Source: engadget.com
