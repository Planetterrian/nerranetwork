ChatGPT just learned how to turn your photos and prompts into real animated GIFs you can tweak frame by frame.

By Patrick November 05, 2026 Ep 218 5 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Listen to this episode
Contents
The Big Story Explain Like I'm 14 Cool Stuff & Try This Quick Bits

OpenAI rolled out a new image model that lets anyone create short looping animations without any video editing skills. This matters because it turns a tool you already use for homework or fun into something that feels like a tiny animation studio in your pocket. Today we’ll unpack how the new GIF feature works, explore why keeping characters looking the same across frames is surprisingly hard, and check out a few other quick AI moments worth knowing.

The Big Story

OpenAI just added the ability to create animated GIFs inside ChatGPT using its new Images 2.6 model. You type a prompt or upload a photo, and the system strings together several frames into a short looping animation you can then slow down, speed up, or edit one frame at a time. Think of it like giving your phone the power to make a tiny flipbook movie from a single sentence or picture. The model is good enough at keeping the same character recognizable across frames that the animations feel smooth instead of jumping around. This matters because creating even a few seconds of animation used to require expensive software and hours of work; now the same thing is a few taps away for anyone with a free or paid ChatGPT account. Students can turn a history report into a moving scene, artists can test quick ideas, and friends can send each other custom reaction GIFs they made themselves. The change also shows how fast image tools are moving from still pictures to moving ones, which could soon affect everything from school projects to social media posts. Right now the feature is rolling out to ChatGPT and Codex users on web, desktop, and mobile. OpenAI also released two new models for the API called GPT-Image-2.6 Flare for faster generation and Sunburst for higher quality, both running about 50 percent quicker than before. Users now create well over four billion images every week through ChatGPT and the API. The update also adds reference images so you can save a picture of yourself, a pet, or a character and reuse it across many new creations without uploading it again each time. To try it, open ChatGPT, type something like “make a gif of a cat dancing on a skateboard,” or upload a photo of your pet and ask it to animate the pet waving. You can then drag sliders to change speed or click individual frames to tweak them. Source: the-decoder.com

Explain Like I'm 14

You know how when you draw the same cartoon character on several pieces of paper to make a flipbook, it’s really easy for the character’s face or clothes to look slightly different on each page? Now imagine an AI trying to do the same thing, but instead of paper it’s creating every frame from scratch. The model has to remember exactly what the character looked like in the first frame—hair color, shirt pattern, even the way the shadows fall—and copy that look into every new frame without drifting. It does this by treating the first image like a strong reference that gets checked against each new one it generates, almost like a teacher glancing back at the original drawing while the student keeps sketching. When the system works well, the character stays consistent enough that your eye accepts it as the same person moving. When it fails, the character suddenly changes height or grows an extra finger, which breaks the illusion. The new model is better at this memory trick than older ones, which is why the GIFs feel more like real short animations instead of random pictures strung together. The consistency comes from the way the model was trained to treat the starting image as a fixed anchor rather than starting fresh each time, so small details like a jacket zipper or a pet’s ear shape survive from frame to frame. That same anchoring trick is also what lets you upload a reference photo once and keep using it in later prompts without the character drifting.

Cool Stuff & Try This

Take a quick survey and help researchers understand how people actually use AI

Researchers at the University of Toronto want to hear from everyday users about how they talk to chatbots for writing, advice, or searching. The survey is completely anonymous, takes up to 30 minutes, and needs no technical background at all. It’s a simple way to turn your own experiences into data that could shape how these tools improve. The questions focus on daily experiences with your favorite chatbot and your views on what it can and cannot do. Go to the link https://utorontosociology.ca1.qualtrics.com/jfe/form/SV_5u5mLg5Px1amWWy on your phone or laptop, answer the questions about your favorite chatbot, and you’re done. You may need a parent’s help if you’re under 18, but the whole thing is designed for regular people, not coders. Your answers stay private because no names or IP addresses are collected, so the researchers only see the patterns in how people use these tools. Source: reddit.com

Quick Bits

Only four Uber rides were booked by AI agents last quarter

During the busy Halloween week, Uber handled hundreds of millions of trips, yet just four of them came from third-party AI agents trying to order rides on someone’s behalf. The numbers for food delivery were a bit higher but still tiny compared with normal app use. Uber’s chief financial officer noted that the company handled around 320 million trips and orders across Uber and Uber Eats that week, with only about 500 Uber Eats orders placed by an AI agent. CEO Dara Khosrowshahi called the traction “very, very early and very limited,” adding that most people still prefer the speed of the regular app, which takes about 50 seconds to book a ride. It’s a reminder that while people talk a lot about AI agents doing tasks for us, most of us are still happily tapping buttons ourselves for now. Source: techcrunch.com

AT&T’s smart home security system now works with Google’s Gemini

If you have AT&T’s Connected Life security setup, Gemini for Home can now summarize what happened at your house, search through old video clips, and answer questions about events. It’s one of the first times a major security system has added this kind of AI helper directly into the app. The integration lets Gemini pull up specific moments from your camera history and explain them in plain language instead of making you scroll through hours of footage yourself. Source: theverge.com
