Models & Agents for Beginners

An AI that feels like texting a real friend just landed on your iPhone.

Sesame, a startup started by the people behind Oculus, just released a free iOS app that lets you talk naturally with AI agents. It skips the usual chatbot stiffness and aims for real back-and-forth conversation. Today we’ll explore how that works, check out a new design helper from Adobe you can try right now, and see a few other tools that turn everyday phones into AI playgrounds.

The Big Story

Sesame just launched its conversational AI app on iOS, bringing the kind of natural back-and-forth chat that usually only happens in science-fiction movies straight to your phone. Instead of typing short commands and getting robotic answers, you can speak and get responses that feel more like talking with an actual person. Think of it like the difference between sending a stiff email and having a quick voice note exchange with a friend who actually listens and replies in context. The app is designed so the AI remembers the flow of the conversation instead of treating every message like a brand-new question. This matters because most of us already spend hours on our phones chatting with friends or scrolling social media; an AI that joins that same natural flow could help with homework explanations, story ideas, or just practicing a language without feeling awkward. For students or anyone curious about future careers, it shows how AI is moving from “search engine that talks” to something closer to a helpful companion you can carry everywhere. You can download the Sesame app directly from the App Store and start a conversation right away—no special setup needed. Try asking it to explain a topic from school in a fun way or help brainstorm a creative project, and notice how the replies stay on track even when you change the subject. The experience is free to try, though you may need a parent’s help if you’re under the usual app-store age for accounts. Source: techcrunch.com

Explain Like I'm 14

You know how when you’re texting a friend, your phone sometimes guesses the next word before you even finish typing? Now imagine that same autocomplete could also remember everything you’ve said in the last few minutes and use that memory to guess not just one word, but a whole helpful reply. The AI listens to your voice or text, breaks it into small chunks it understands, checks what you talked about earlier in the same chat, and then builds an answer that feels like it’s actually paying attention. It keeps a short “conversation history” the same way you remember the last few messages in a group chat, so it doesn’t repeat itself or forget what you just asked. When the AI gets better at this, the replies stop feeling like random guesses and start feeling like talking with someone who knows the whole story so far. That’s basically what a conversational AI agent does when it holds a natural back-and-forth instead of resetting after every single message. So next time someone says “conversational AI,” you can tell them—it’s autocomplete that remembers the whole chat, not just the next word. Not so scary, right?

Cool Stuff & Try This

Adobe’s new AI design helper feels like having a patient intern on your laptop: Source Name

Adobe built a conversational AI that works inside its design tools and can take simple spoken or typed instructions to create or tweak images. It’s different from most AI image tools because it keeps the conversation going—you can say “make the background warmer” or “add more space on the left” and it remembers what you already made. This is exciting if you like art, posters, or school projects because it lets you experiment without starting from a blank page every time. Go to Adobe’s Firefly website or open the tool inside Photoshop, type a short description of something you want to design, then follow up with one change at a time to see how it builds on your idea. You may need a free Adobe account to try the full chat version. Source: theverge.com

Turn your iPhone into a private AI chatbot that runs completely on the device: Source Name

A new guide shows exactly how to install a small AI model that works offline on your iPhone so you can chat without sending anything to the internet. This keeps your conversations private and works even when you have no signal. It’s perfect for anyone who wants to experiment with AI without worrying about data or subscriptions. Open the Engadget article on your phone, follow the simple steps listed (they use free apps already in the App Store), and within a few minutes you can ask the local chatbot to write a short story or quiz you on vocabulary. No coding or extra hardware is required. Source: engadget.com

One person redesigned their whole apartment by chatting with Claude: Source Name

A Reddit user shared how they used Claude to plan every detail of a home renovation—from choosing styles to calculating exact measurements for furniture—so the contractors got precise instructions instead of vague ideas. The AI helped keep colors and shapes consistent across rooms and even suggested ways to hide cables. If you’ve ever rearranged your room or made a poster for school, this shows how the same kind of chat can handle bigger creative projects step by step. Open claude.ai on your phone or laptop, describe a space you want to improve (even if it’s just your desk setup), and ask it to suggest layouts or color combinations while you reply with what you like or don’t like. Source: reddit.com

Quick Bits

A sea-urchin robot with 20 legs can climb walls and move through trees

Scientists built a robot shaped like a sea urchin that uses its many flexible legs to grip different surfaces. The design could help future robots explore places that wheels or normal legs can’t reach. Source: livescience.com

Waymo’s new pale-blue robotaxi called Ojai is heading to California and Arizona roads

The self-driving cars will start picking up regular passengers soon, showing how AI is already moving people around cities without a human driver. Source: wired.com
