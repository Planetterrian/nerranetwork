Four in 100 GLP-1 stoppers had a heart event or died yearly, double those who continued, in observational records.

By Patrick November 12, 2026 Ep 9 12 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
The Week in Peptides Also Today Peptide Spotlight: Tirzepatide and Dual Agonists Worth Knowing Evidence Ledger

What You Need to Know: In observational records, about four in 100 people a year who stopped a GLP-1 had a non-fatal heart attack, stroke, or death, roughly double the rate among people who stayed on treatment. That comparison comes from Italian diabetes-clinic data, not from a trial that assigned people to stop. A separate regulatory action this week adds a rare optic-nerve warning to GLP-1 labels, which is a labeling change, not a new outcomes trial.

The Week in Peptides

Stopping GLP-1 drugs could increase heart attack risk for some, study says: Global News

An observational analysis in The Lancet Regional Health – Europe, of 80,302 people with type 2 diabetes and no established cardiovascular disease seen at 301 Italian clinics from 2004 to 2024, linked stopping a GLP-1 receptor agonist with about twice the combined rate of non-fatal heart attack, non-fatal stroke, or death. After propensity-score matching, each group had 28,713 people: the combined outcome occurred at 3.94 events per 100 person-years after discontinuation versus 1.96 among people who continued, a hazard ratio of 2.03, which is the more precise version of about four in 100 versus about two in 100. Death from any cause was 2.23 versus 0.48 events per 100 person-years, a hazard ratio of 4.81, and the association was stronger among people with a body mass index of 30 or higher. About 60 percent of starters stopped after about one year, while people who stayed on treatment did so for about three years. The study cannot show that stopping caused the extra events: it did not establish why people stopped, including gastrointestinal effects, cost, or illness, and some who stopped may already have been in poorer health. Cardiologist Christopher Labos told Global News the pattern is what you would expect if the drugs’ known heart protection is lost when treatment stops; a separate review of 63 trials and more than 9,000 people, published in the BMJ in January 2026, found weight regain averaging 0.4 kilograms a month after weight-loss drugs were stopped. Source: globalnews.ca · thesouthfirst.com

GLP-1 Weight Loss Drug Side Effects Include Permanent Blindness, Warns FDA: Timeline Daily

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has required a labeling change, a regulatory action rather than a new patient trial, warning that GLP-1 medicines may be associated with nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy, a rare sudden loss of blood flow to the optic nerve that can cause acute, painless vision loss in one eye, including permanent vision loss. Products named for that warning include Novo Nordisk’s Ozempic and Wegovy, Eli Lilly’s Mounjaro and Zepbound, and Sanofi’s Soliqua. The agency lists diabetes, smoking, hypertension, and hypercholesterolemia among NAION risk factors. Drug Topics, Ars Technica, and other outlets describe the same step as an added NAION risk warning across GLP-1 labels. These named products are approved medicines receiving a new warning; the warning does not measure how often NAION occurs, and it does not establish that every vision change on treatment is NAION. Source: timelinedaily.com · arstechnica.com · drugtopics.com

ERs see surge in GLP-1 side effects | What to know: WBAL-TV

This is a clinician’s report from emergency practice, not a counted study: MedStar Health emergency physician Miriam Fischer told WBAL-TV that patients arrive every day with GLP-1 side effects, and that emergency physicians are seeing more preventable visits as people also buy these drugs online and use them without medical supervision. She described stomach upset severe enough that people cannot keep down food or drink, plus dehydration, and secondary problems she named as biliary disease, gallbladder trouble, pancreatitis, and a higher chance of kidney stones when dehydration is severe. She said these peptides slow the digestive system, so mild nausea can occur, but they should not cause severe pain. She called the medicines useful and said patients need a doctor’s care; that is her clinical judgment, not a trial result. Source: wbaltv.com

Symptom improvement was not significantly associated with weight loss, dose, or treatment duration in women with lipoedema treated with tirzepatide: Metabolism Open

In a retrospective look at prospectively collected routine-care outcomes, 49 women with clinically diagnosed lipoedema treated with tirzepatide at one specialist clinic between January 2024 and May 2026 filled out the Lipedema Symptom Assessment Questionnaire before treatment and again at 3 to 18 months. Tirzepatide is approved for type 2 diabetes in the United States, Europe, and the UAE; this paper is not an approval trial for lipoedema, and it has no untreated control group. Percentage weight change was not significantly associated with percentage symptom change (r = 0.058, p = 0.692), and symptom change was also not significantly associated with dose intensity or treatment duration, while weight change was associated with dose intensity (r = −0.569) and duration (r = −0.611). Every participant improved by at least 30 percent on the questionnaire, and 93.9 percent improved by at least 50 percent. The authors state that the near-zero correlation and the small, uniformly improved sample do not prove symptoms moved independently of weight loss, and that the improvement cannot be attributed to tirzepatide. Source: europepmc.org

Baseline monocyte GIP receptor mRNA expression is associated with reductions in body weight and FIB-4 index after switching to tirzepatide in patients with type 2 diabetes: Diabetology International

In a single-center retrospective study of 90 people with type 2 diabetes who had already taken a GLP-1 receptor agonist for at least six months, higher baseline monocyte GIP-receptor mRNA tracked with larger drops in body weight and in the fibrosis-4 index over six months after a switch to tirzepatide. The weight and FIB-4 associations remained in multivariable models, and the FIB-4 association remained after further adjustment for percentage weight change; GIP-receptor mRNA was not associated with weight change during the prior GLP-1 period. FIB-4 is a calculated blood-and-age index, a biomarker rather than a biopsy or a clinical liver outcome. In exploratory ex vivo assays, native GIP suppressed lipopolysaccharide-induced interleukin-6 and tumor necrosis factor-alpha responses in monocytes. The authors call monocyte GIP-receptor expression an exploratory marker only: treatment specificity, a causal receptor mechanism, and regression of structural liver fibrosis were not established. Tirzepatide’s approval, as stated in a separate review, is for type 2 diabetes in the United States, Europe, and the UAE, not as a fibrosis drug. Source: europepmc.org

Peptide Spotlight: Tirzepatide and Dual Agonists

Tirzepatide is an acylated peptide engineered to activate two receptors, the glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide receptor and the GLP-1 receptor, and a 2022 review states it is the first such dual agonist approved for type 2 diabetes in the United States, Europe, and the UAE. Those receptors help mediate insulin secretion and also sit in brain regions that regulate food intake. The peptide is described as a 39-amino-acid synthetic sequence based on native GIP, modified so it can engage both receptors.

The dual-agonist idea came from human physiology, not from a marketing claim. A 2021 clinical review recounts that GIP alone, infused at supraphysiological levels, had looked inactive as an insulin releaser in people with type 2 diabetes, which is why GIP was long set aside as a glucose-lowering drug. The same review says emerging evidence showed that infusing GLP-1 and GIP together produced a larger insulin response and a stronger suppression of glucagon than giving either hormone alone. That co-infusion finding is human experimental physiology summarized in a review, not one of the later phase 3 outcome trials. It also does not prove that the GIP half of tirzepatide is what adds benefit once both activities are built into a single weekly peptide.

The strongest human evidence in these sources is the SURPASS program. Five trials in people with type 2 diabetes found that tirzepatide reduced HbA1c by 1.24 to 2.58 percentage points and body weight by 5.4 to 11.7 kilograms, ranges the 2022 review calls unprecedented for a single agent. A sizable share of participants, 23.0 to 62.4 percent, reached an HbA1c target described in that paper. The same review says tirzepatide lowered HbA1c and body weight more than the selective GLP-1 receptor agonist semaglutide and more than titrated basal insulin. It also reports greater improvement in insulin sensitivity and in insulin secretory responses than with semaglutide, with lower prandial insulin and glucagon, similar reductions in appetite, and greater weight loss. Appetite falling by a similar amount while weight fell further is a clue that the extra weight loss is not explained only by “people ate less” in the way those trials measured appetite.

A systematic review and meta-analysis, searching through 27 October 2021 and pooling seven randomized trials of at least 12 weeks in 6,609 adults with type 2 diabetes, found dose-dependent superiority on HbA1c against every comparator class it tested. Mean differences versus placebo ran from 1.62 to 2.06 percentage points, versus other GLP-1 receptor agonists from 0.29 to 0.92, and versus basal insulin regimens from 0.70 to 1.09. Extra weight loss versus GLP-1 receptor agonists ran from 1.68 kilograms at the lowest tirzepatide dose in that analysis to 7.16 kilograms at the highest. At the time of that review, tirzepatide was still described as under review for marketing approval; the 2022 paper is the source that states approval in the United States, Europe, and the UAE. The older meta-analysis remains useful for the pooled trial comparisons, not for current legal status.

Cardiovascular findings in the 2022 review are safety evidence with a thin event count, not a completed outcomes proof. Events were adjudicated across the program. MACE-4 — nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, cardiovascular death, and hospital admission for angina — tended to be lower over up to about two years, but the number of events was low. In a meta-analysis of the program, no analyzed cardiovascular outcome had a hazard ratio above 1.0 against pooled comparators, and the upper bounds of the confidence intervals for MACE were under 1.3, which met a conventional definition of cardiovascular safety. A hazard ratio that has not crossed above 1, with few events, does not establish that tirzepatide prevents heart attacks.

Harms in these trials look like the harms already known for GLP-1 receptor agonists. The 2022 review names nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and constipation, more common at higher doses. The meta-analysis found hypoglycaemia similar to placebo and lower than with basal insulin. Against placebo, nausea was more frequent, especially at the highest dose (odds ratio 5.60), along with vomiting (odds ratio 5.50) and diarrhoea (odds ratio 3.31). Gastrointestinal odds were generally similar to other GLP-1 receptor agonists, except diarrhoea at the middle dose (odds ratio 1.51). The highest dose led to more people stopping study medication because of adverse events, whatever the comparator. Serious adverse events and mortality were not increased in that pooled analysis.

What these papers do not establish is the part listeners most often hear asserted. The 2022 review says GIP reduces food intake and body weight in rodents, and that those effects have not been demonstrated in humans. It also says it remains to be shown that GIP-receptor agonism can improve insulin secretion in people with type 2 diabetes, a group earlier work had found unresponsive to GIP. So a larger drop in glucose and weight than with a GLP-1-only drug is a human trial result; a proven GIP mechanism for that gap is not. Follow-up in the cardiovascular summary is on the order of up to two years, with few events. These sources do not show what happens over a longer horizon, do not show benefit in conditions outside type 2 diabetes, and do not turn a biomarker association or an uncontrolled clinic series into evidence that the dual agonist caused those changes.

What it is: Tirzepatide is an acylated dual GIP and GLP-1 receptor agonist peptide, described as 39 amino acids based on native GIP.

Humans given it: The pooled analysis covers 6,609 adults in seven trials, and the SURPASS program studied people with type 2 diabetes.

Regulatory status: A 2022 review states it is approved for type 2 diabetes in the United States, Europe, and the UAE.

Risks: Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and constipation were more common at higher doses, and the highest dose had more discontinuations for adverse events.

What it does not show: These papers do not show that GIP-receptor agonism itself causes the extra benefit in humans, or that heart attacks are prevented.

Worth Knowing

An observational clinic comparison can show that heart events clustered among people who stopped a GLP-1, and still leave open the possibility that illness or other reasons for stopping, not the stop itself, drove the difference.

A required label warning is a regulatory judgment that a rare optic-nerve event belongs on GLP-1 prescribing information, which is a different kind of evidence from a trial that counts how often the event happens.

In randomized trials in thousands of adults with type 2 diabetes, a dual GIP/GLP-1 peptide lowered glucose and weight more than GLP-1-only comparators and basal insulin, while the human contribution of the GIP receptor remains unproven.

In a small uncontrolled clinic series, lipoedema symptom scores improved without tracking weight loss, and that pattern cannot be credited to tirzepatide until a controlled study says so.

Evidence Ledger

  • Tirzepatide, an acylated dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist, is described as approved for type 2 diabetes in the United States, Europe, and the UAE; in five SURPASS trials it lowered HbA1c by 1.24 to 2.58 percentage points and weight by 5.4 to 11.7 kilograms, more than semaglutide or titrated basal insulin, with gastrointestinal effects and cardiovascular safety but few events. Evidence level: human randomized trials summarized in a review. Source: europepmc.org
  • Seven randomized trials in 6,609 adults found dose-dependent HbA1c and weight advantages for tirzepatide versus placebo, GLP-1 receptor agonists, and basal insulin, with more nausea, vomiting, and diarrhoea than placebo and more discontinuations at the highest dose. Evidence level: systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized trials. Source: europepmc.org
  • Tirzepatide is a 39-amino-acid peptide based on native GIP; co-infusion of GLP-1 and GIP had looked synergistic on insulin release and glucagon suppression, while GIP alone had looked inactive in type 2 diabetes. Evidence level: narrative review of preclinical work and phase 1 and 2 trials, plus earlier human infusion observations. Source: europepmc.org

Education only: these findings are for understanding a claim, not a reason to start, stop, or change a medicine.

It also says nobody has yet shown that turning on the GIP receptor can improve insulin secretion in type 2 diabetes, a group earlier work found unresponsive to GIP. A larger drop in glucose and weight than with a GLP-1-only drug is a human trial result. A proven GIP explanation for that gap is not. Cardiovascular follow-up in these sources runs up to about two years, with few events. The papers do not show a longer horizon, do not show benefit outside type 2 diabetes, and do not convert a biomarker link or an uncontrolled clinic series into proof that the dual agonist caused those changes. One thing worth knowing from the clinic records is that heart events clustered among people who had stopped, while illness or other reasons for stopping can still explain the gap. A required eye warning is a regulator deciding a rare optic-nerve event belongs on the label, which is not the same as a trial that counts the event. In randomized trials in thousands of adults with type 2 diabetes, the dual peptide lowered glucose and weight more than GLP-1-only drugs and basal insulin, and the human share of that gap that belongs to GIP is still unproven. In a small clinic series without a control group, lipoedema scores improved without tracking weight loss, and that improvement cannot be credited to the drug until a controlled study says so.
