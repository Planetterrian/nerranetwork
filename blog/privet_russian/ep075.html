Word of the Day

Word of the Day

окно

ok-NO

window

Example: Я смотрю в окно. (Ya smot-RYU v ok-NO.) — I am looking out the window.

Memory hook: Imagine your window as the “eye” of the house — it lets light and views in just like an eye.

Repeat after me: ok-NO… ok-NO.

  • Russian (Cyrillic): дверь

Transliteration: dvyerr’

English: door

Example sentence: Закрой дверь, пожалуйста.

Example translation: Close the door, please.

Memory hook: It sounds a bit like “dver” — think of a heavy wooden door that “dver” you push to enter.

  • Russian (Cyrillic): стол

Transliteration: stol

English: table

Example sentence: Книга лежит на столе.

Example translation: The book is lying on the table.

Memory hook: “Stol” sounds like “stool” but means the flat surface you eat or work on.

  • Russian (Cyrillic): стул

Transliteration: stool

English: chair

Example sentence: Сиди на стуле.

Example translation: Sit on the chair.

Memory hook: It rhymes with “stool” — perfect for the thing you sit on!

  • Russian (Cyrillic): кровать

Transliteration: kro-VAT’

English: bed

Example sentence: Я сплю в кровати.

Example translation: I sleep in the bed.

Memory hook: “Kro-vat” — picture a cozy “crib” or cot where you “vat” (rest) at night.

  • Russian (Cyrillic): лампа

Transliteration: LAM-pa

English: lamp

Example sentence: Лампа светит в комнате.

Example translation: The lamp shines in the room.

Memory hook: Same as English “lamp” — easy to remember!

  • Russian (Cyrillic): пол

Transliteration: pol

English: floor

Example sentence: Кот лежит на полу.

Example translation: The cat is lying on the floor.

Memory hook: “Pol” sounds like “pole” but means the ground under your feet at home.

  • Russian (Cyrillic): потолок

Transliteration: po-to-LOK

English: ceiling

Example sentence: Потолок белый.

Example translation: The ceiling is white.

Memory hook: “Po-to-lok” — think of the “top” of the room that “locks” the walls together.

  • Russian (Cyrillic): ванная

Transliteration: va-NNA-ya

English: bathroom

Example sentence: Я моюсь в ванной.

Example translation: I wash in the bathroom.

Memory hook: “Va-nna-ya” — like a “van” full of water for bathing.

Grammar Spotlight:

In Russian, we use the preposition “в” (v) to mean “in” when talking about being inside a room or space. It works like English “in the kitchen” but the noun often changes its ending. Examples: Я в комнате (I am in the room), Кот в ванной (The cat is in the bathroom), Мы в доме (We are in the house). Unlike English, the noun takes a special ending called the prepositional case, but for beginners just listen for the “e” or “и” sound at the end.

Word Origins — Deep Dive: окно

This word started in ancient Slavic as “oko,” the old word for “eye,” because a window is literally the eye of the house that lets you see outside. It travelled through Old Russian unchanged in spirit and arrived in modern form as окно centuries ago. But be careful — it looks nothing like the English “window,” yet both words grew from the same idea of an opening to look through. Here’s a memory trick: picture your window as a giant friendly eye on the wall of your home.

Cultural Corner:

In many Russian apartments you will see heavy wooden doors and thick curtains over the windows to keep the long winters out. Russians often say “дом — это крепость,” meaning “home is a fortress,” showing how important a warm, safe home feels after cold months outside.

Practice Challenge:

For kids: Draw a simple picture of a room and label the window, door, and table in Russian, then say each word out loud. For adults: Walk through your own home and name three objects using today’s words — “Это стол,” “Это окно,” “Это дверь.”
