The feeds profit from making people feel powerless. This show starts from the opposite conviction: individuals are not powerless. Two friends—an airline pilot who thinks in checklists and redundancy and a physical chemist turned science-fiction novelist who chases mechanisms and second-order effects—built the Nerra Network (Novak + Perra) as an independent, ad-free collection of daily shows produced end-to-end by the two of them. Dan Perra and Patrick Novak step in front of the microphone here to review the most consequential science and technology developments, not to recite them, but to analyze what they actually mean, who should act on them, and where the real leverage sits for ordinary people. The audience is builders, creators, and contributors—people already trying to move the needle and people who want to start. Each episode delivers one mindset principle, ten minutes of consequential verified progress with honest numbers, one concrete weekly action called The Lever, and proof the approach works through the Do Positive Dispatch of listener results. The entire network runs without ads, sponsors, or paywalls because the goal is a club, not a media property. Patrick Novak, Vancouver-based with a BSc in Chemistry from the University of Alberta, spent nearly twenty years in environmental analytical chemistry as VP and co-owner of CARO Analytical Services, which became British Columbia’s leading environmental lab and stood up COVID PCR testing during the public-health crisis while pioneering cannabis testing after legalization. He now runs Planetterrian Ventures, an incubator whose projects include Bill Saved for fighting billing disputes, Avvizo for daily check-in calls to seniors, Loans Expert for mortgage comparisons, and Lilwords, a word game built in Unity over two years, plus a food-tech venture turning brewery spent yeast and soybeans into carbon-negative vegan protein. Dan Perra, a WestJet captain living in Collingwood, Ontario, with two daughters, drives a Tesla Model 3 and Model Y and recently installed solar on his house. Think Positive

Tony Robbins has long emphasized that motivation follows action rather than the other way around. When people wait for the right feeling before beginning, the feeling rarely arrives; when they begin anyway, the state often shifts as a result. The same pattern appears in behavioral activation research: small, scheduled actions create momentum that later supports larger effort. The hosts see this principle at work in every builder story they cover. Today’s mental rep is to pick one task you have been postponing and start it for five minutes without waiting to feel motivated.

The anchor discussion begins with the First Principles Daily observation that the real price of artificial light has fallen roughly ten thousandfold since whale-oil lamps because each generation redesigned the conversion step itself rather than simply scaling the previous technology. The single most surprising number is that cumulative redesign—from combustion to semiconductor—delivered the bulk of the gain, not cheaper fuel or cheaper electricity. Open questions the hosts will argue: how far does this redesign-the-loss-term pattern stretch into other domains such as food, transport, or computation; what would a builder listening today actually change in their own work if they applied the same lens; and where does the pattern break when physics or regulation imposes hard limits that incremental redesign cannot overcome.

The network tour covers three sibling shows. First Principles Daily tracks the reasons a thing should cost less, one example per day. Fascinating Frontiers delivers the day’s space and science wonders with a cosmic deep dive. Planetterrian Daily focuses on science, longevity, and health research that changes how listeners live.

Take the pledge on the show page at nerranetwork.com and send this episode to one person who has been doomscrolling. It costs about one minute of your time. The return is the first step from passive consumption to active club membership. If even one percent of listeners do it, the zero-listener debut becomes a functioning club within days. The main barrier is the feeling that one share cannot matter; the fix is to treat the share as the concrete first action rather than waiting for evidence it will scale.
