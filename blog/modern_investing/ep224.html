Strategy Spotlight

Blended-yield ETF construction inside a TFSA lets investors combine dividend and REIT exposure to generate monthly tax-free cash flow without picking individual stocks. XEI tracks the S&P/TSX Composite High Dividend Index at a 3.5% distribution yield while ZRE follows an equal-weight Canadian REIT basket at 4.7%, producing a 4.1% blended yield when held together. The Motley Fool Canada article notes that XEI provides exposure to a basket of Canadian dividend stocks through a single ticker, eliminating the need to recheck and rebalance individual holdings for regular TFSA contributors. ZRE supplies real-estate income through an equal-weight approach that gives each REIT similar allocation at rebalancing, reducing dependence on the largest holdings. An investor opens a TFSA at a platform such as Questrade or Wealthsimple, contributes the required capital, buys equal dollar amounts of each ETF, and sets the account to reinvest or withdraw distributions monthly. For a $200 monthly tax-free target, the 4.1% blended yield requires roughly $58,500 in total capital because $2,400 in annual distributions divided by 0.041 produces that figure. The approach has delivered steady income in sideways or modestly rising Canadian markets but can lag when broader equity rotation favors growth stocks over yield names, as seen in recent sessions where financials and REITs underperformed tech. To implement, screen for monthly payers in the brokerage interface, confirm the current yields on the ETF fact sheets, and size the position so the blended payout meets the target monthly amount after the 4.1% yield calculation. Source: fool.ca

Investor Education: REIT Distribution Taxation Inside Registered Accounts

You hold ZRE in your TFSA and receive the 4.7% monthly distribution, expecting the full amount to compound tax-free as advertised. The mechanism works because REIT distributions flow through as return of capital, income, or capital gains, and inside a TFSA none of those categories triggers immediate tax, unlike a non-registered account where return-of-capital reduces adjusted cost base and can create future capital-gains exposure. The BMO Equal Weight REITs Index ETF pays monthly distributions that, as of the time of writing, work out to a yield of 4.7%, and those payments retain their character even when sheltered. A pro habit is to download the annual tax-information package each REIT or ETF publishes in February and confirm the split before filing, rather than assuming every dollar is pure income. The common misconception is that all REIT payouts behave like bond interest; the fix is to treat them as a three-part allocation and track the capital component separately even inside registered accounts so you understand the true economic return. Investors who skip this step may later misjudge the after-tax economics if they ever move the holding outside the TFSA or need to calculate adjusted cost base for any reason.

Досрочное исполнение не моделируется, позиция идёт до закрытия пятой сессии или до стопа — что наступит раньше, а слушатели, которым нужна недостающая цифра премии, снимут её сами через минуту.
