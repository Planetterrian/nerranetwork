Sector rotation into defense and industrial names is gaining traction as European governments increase procurement spending. Saab’s second-quarter results beat expectations on both sales and orders, demonstrating how rising defense budgets can create sustained backlog visibility for contractors. Investors can implement this by screening for companies with order-backlog growth above 15% year-over-year and defense-revenue exposure above 40%, then confirming the setup with a same-day volume check against the 20-day average. The approach has historically delivered excess returns during multi-year rearmament cycles but carries elevated geopolitical-premium risk if budgets are later cut. Investor Education: Canadian Discount Brokerages Compared

Imagine you want to buy 200 shares of an industrial name trading on both the TSX and NYSE right after the open. Your order fills at the ask on Wealthsimple Trade but you notice the same order would have cost an extra $4.95 on Questrade because the U.S. leg triggered a commission. Wealthsimple Trade offers commission-free trades inside registered accounts but limits advanced order types and has no margin capability. Questrade charges $4.95–$9.95 per trade yet provides free ETF purchases and solid mobile charting for active investors. Interactive Brokers posts the lowest margin rates (currently around 5.8% for CAD balances over $100k) and full access to 150+ global exchanges, but its platform complexity and data-feed fees make it better suited for traders managing multiple accounts. Bank-owned platforms such as TD Direct Investing and RBC Direct integrate seamlessly with chequing accounts yet carry higher commissions ($6.95–$9.95) and slower execution on U.S. names. The biggest mistake retail investors make is choosing a brokerage solely on advertised commission rates without checking whether it supports the exact markets and order types their strategy requires. Instead, match the platform to your account type and trade frequency: Wealthsimple for simple TFSA/RESP buy-and-hold, Questrade for occasional ETF and stock trades, and Interactive Brokers once you need international access or margin.
