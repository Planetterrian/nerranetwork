Strategy Spotlight

Analyst rating changes paired with revenue-growth data can highlight names where fundamentals already support the upgrade. Erste Group raised Spotify to Buy on strong revenue growth, giving investors a concrete signal to check quarterly filings for the same metric. Pull the latest earnings release in your brokerage platform, compare year-over-year revenue growth to the prior two quarters, and confirm the upgrade came after at least one positive revision. The method works when revenue acceleration precedes the rating move; it fails when the upgrade arrives after the stock has already run. In past cycles, upgrades without revenue confirmation produced flat or negative three-month returns once sector rotation shifted. Run the check inside a TFSA or RRSP to avoid tax drag on any short-term position. Erste Group also resumed coverage on Johnson & Johnson with a Buy rating tied to its growth outlook, showing the same revenue-first filter can be applied across sectors. Investors who applied the filter to Spotify last quarter would have seen the revenue line accelerate before the rating change appeared. The sequence matters because rating moves that follow revenue beats tend to attract follow-on institutional flows rather than front-running noise. Track the exact revenue figure reported in the most recent 10-Q or 10-K and note whether it exceeds the two-quarter average by at least 5 percent before acting on the upgrade. Source: investing.com

Investor Education: How Lock-Up Expirations Create Selling Pressure

You bought shares of a newly public AI company on the first trading day after its debut and watched the price hold steady for two weeks before sliding. Lock-up agreements prevent insiders from selling for 180 days after the IPO, so the first wave of sales begins on the expiration date listed in the prospectus. Anthropic’s expirations start December 15, which means roughly 180 days of restricted supply ends then and additional tranches follow over subsequent months. Professionals set calendar alerts 30 days before each scheduled release and reduce position size in advance rather than waiting for volume to spike. The common misconception is that an IPO debut price already reflects all future selling; in reality the lock-up schedule creates a predictable supply overhang that only appears once the restriction lifts. The article notes that Anthropic could see a flood of selling over the next few months as multiple expiration windows open sequentially. Investors who ignore the calendar often find themselves competing against large blocks that hit the tape on the exact date the lock-up lifts. Setting a position-size reduction rule 30 days ahead converts that known supply event into a planned exit rather than an unexpected drop. The same pattern has repeated across prior high-profile AI and tech listings where the first post-lock-up session produced above-average volume and below-average returns for holders who stayed fully sized.

Ignore the calendar and you are competing against large blocks that hit the tape on the exact date the lock-up lifts. The same pattern has repeated across prior high-profile AI and tech listings, where the first post-lock-up session produced above-average volume and below-average returns for holders who stayed fully sized. A position-size reduction rule thirty days ahead turns that known supply event into a planned exit rather than an unexpected drop — the debut print is not a finished sale schedule. Models & Agents covers the AI side daily. Open your brokerage screener, set a filter for NASDAQ tech names with today's volume above the twenty-session average, and flag any that also appear on an analyst-upgrade list. Review the top three results for five minutes each, and run the revenue-first check on any upgrade that shows up before you even think about size.
