Strategy Spotlight

Cash-secured puts function as paid limit orders that compensate you for waiting to buy a stock you already want to own. In the OptionsPlay Growth Lab example, a ranked list of names shows the put strike, premium collected, and probability of assignment so you can compare the effective purchase price against your target entry. You open the position by selling the put and posting cash equal to the strike times 100 shares; if the stock stays above the strike you keep the premium as income and repeat the process, while assignment simply delivers the shares at your chosen net cost. Implementation starts in any options-approved brokerage by scanning the chain for 30- to 45-day expiries where implied volatility sits above the 20-day average, then confirming the premium covers at least 1 percent of the strike. The approach worked well in the 2022-2023 rate-hike period when elevated volatility produced richer premiums, yet it fails when a sudden gap down leaves you assigned shares well below your original target and forces an immediate unrealized loss. Canadian investors can run the same screen inside Questrade or Interactive Brokers while staying inside TFSA or RRSP contribution room because the cash collateral counts toward the account’s settled-cash balance. The platform displays the ranked list directly in the Growth Lab interface so users can filter by probability of profit and annualized return before placing the order. One concrete step is to select a name trading near your target entry price, sell the 30-day put at the strike one standard deviation below the current price, and record the premium received as a percentage of the strike. This method turns waiting time into income while enforcing discipline on the entry price you are willing to pay. Source: x.com

Investor Education: Order Execution Mechanics on Volatile Days

You place a market buy for 200 shares of a TSX-listed name at the open on a day when the S&P/TSX Composite is already down 268 points and the bid-ask spread instantly widens from 3 cents to 18 cents within the first two minutes. The mechanism at work is that market makers pull quotes or widen them to protect against adverse selection when order flow spikes and inventory risk rises, so your fill lands several cents worse than the last printed price even though the underlying index move was only 0.8 percent. A pro habit that limits the damage is to enter the order as a limit at the mid-point of the current spread and refresh it every 30 seconds rather than accepting the first market fill, which on high-volume days can save 10 to 20 basis points per round turn. The common misconception is that a market order always executes at the displayed quote; the fix is to check the level-2 ladder and size the limit order no larger than the visible depth at your target price before hitting submit. On days when the TSX falls more than 250 points, liquidity providers reduce their posted size at the best bid and offer to manage risk, which directly widens the spread and increases the cost of immediate execution. Investors who place market orders in the first minutes of trading on such days often see effective prices 15 to 25 cents away from the previous close even when the index move itself is modest. Checking the depth of market before entry reveals whether your intended size can be filled at the displayed level or whether a limit order at mid-spread is required to avoid slippage. The level-2 ladder also shows whether large sellers are stacked at the offer, giving a clearer picture of near-term pressure than the last traded price alone.

Tools & Techniques

Wealthsimple Fractional Share Cleanup

Investors can sell an entire fractional position and repurchase a whole-share amount in one transaction inside the TFSA without triggering a contribution-room issue, provided the net cash movement stays within the account. The feature removes the drag of odd-lot holdings that complicate ACB tracking and superficial-loss calculations when rebalancing. Users access it directly in the app’s trade ticket by selecting “sell all” then entering the exact whole-share quantity on the buy side. The Reddit discussion notes that some fractional amounts cannot be offset by buying the missing piece because of minimum trade sizes, making the sell-and-rebuy route the cleanest method for keeping every holding in whole shares. This approach avoids repeated small trades that could draw attention under superficial-loss rules while preserving the full contribution room already used by the original purchase. Source: reddit.com

CFTC Equity Perps Approval

The CFTC’s Tuesday approval lets registered exchanges offer perpetual futures on individual stocks for the first time, subject to dual CFTC-SEC oversight. Canadian investors can monitor the new instruments through Interactive Brokers or similar cross-border platforms to gauge leveraged single-name sentiment without holding the underlying shares. The structure is still limited to exchanges registered with both agencies, so availability will roll out gradually. The StockMKTNewz post emphasizes that equity perps fall under both the CFTC and SEC, meaning only dual-registered venues can list them and that the product marks the first time perpetual futures on single stocks can trade on US-regulated exchanges. This development gives Canadian investors an additional lens on sentiment for names such as PLTR without needing to own the shares outright inside a margin account. Source: x.com
