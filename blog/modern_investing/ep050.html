Strategy Spotlight

Nvidia’s record quarter included a 2,400% dividend increase alongside continued acceleration in its core data-center business. This combination shows how a single name can deliver both growth and rising cash returns to shareholders when capital returns are scaled with free-cash-flow growth. The move highlights a repeatable approach: screen for companies that pair rapid revenue expansion with accelerating shareholder distributions rather than hoarding cash. Investors can implement this by pulling quarterly free-cash-flow trends and dividend-growth rates in a screener, then comparing payout ratios against sector peers. The pattern has worked best in technology and semiconductor names during multi-year AI buildouts, but it carries risk when growth slows and the market reprices high multiples. Always verify the sustainability of the payout increase against the underlying earnings trajectory before adding exposure. Source: fool.com

Investor Education: How to Do a Quarterly Portfolio Review

Imagine you bought CNR last quarter when the rail sector rotated out of favor and your position now sits 12% below your entry. Between your purchase and today the account shows a time-weighted return of –4.2% while money-weighted return is –3.8% because you added fresh TFSA contribution room mid-quarter. The difference matters for tax planning: realized losses can be harvested before the superficial-loss window closes, while unrealized losses stay in the account for future recovery. Professionals always run the actual-versus-target allocation check first, then review position sizes against a 5% single-name cap and calculate fees paid year-to-date. They also track the dividend-income trend line separately from price return because compounding inside an RRSP or TFSA accelerates when payouts grow. The biggest mistake retail investors make is skipping the realized-versus-unrealized split and missing tax-loss opportunities. Instead, always run a simple checklist at quarter-end: rebalance if any sector exceeds target by more than 3%, harvest losses where appropriate, and decide whether new capital goes to existing holdings or fresh ideas.

Tools & Techniques

Wealthsimple Kids & Business Accounts: Source

Wealthsimple is expanding its banking platform to include accounts for children and teenagers plus dedicated business offerings. This gives Canadian families a single low-cost interface to manage both personal and next-generation savings inside TFSA, RRSP, and FHSA wrappers. Parents can set up automated transfers and track long-term compounding without opening separate brokerages. The feature is available now to existing Wealthsimple clients at no extra cost. Source: cbc.ca

Polymarket–Nasdaq Private Market Partnership: Source

Polymarket has partnered with Nasdaq Private Market to bring regulated prediction-market data into institutional workflows. Individual investors gain indirect access to crowd-sourced probability estimates on policy and earnings outcomes that can inform options positioning or sector rotation decisions. The integration is live and can be monitored through public Polymarket dashboards. Source: x.com

AI Candidate Screening Tools: Source

Companies are increasingly deploying AI to screen job applications before human review, which is shifting the skills that appear on winning resumes. Investors can use the same class of tools to scan earnings transcripts or regulatory filings for recurring language that signals management confidence or risk. Several free and low-cost platforms now offer similar natural-language processing features that retail investors can apply to 10-Ks and conference-call transcripts. Source: x.com

Quick Hits

China Cracks Down on Cross-Border Brokers

China’s securities regulator plans penalties for Futu, Tiger Brokers, and Longbridge for operating without mainland licenses. The escalation raises compliance costs for platforms serving Chinese clients and could slow user growth for these names.

Action: Trim exposure to Chinese-exposed fintech names if they break below recent support on the daily chart. Source: bloomberg.com

Advance Auto Parts Earnings Surprise

Advance Auto Parts delivered a strong Q1 beat that sent shares sharply higher. The move shows how even mature retailers can re-rate quickly when cost controls and same-store sales exceed expectations.

Action: Add AAP to a watchlist and consider a small position only after a pullback to the 20-day moving average. Source: fool.com

Fed Floats “Skinny” Payment Accounts

The Federal Reserve proposed a limited payment-account framework for fintech and crypto firms while pausing Tier 3 applications. The change could lower barriers for compliant platforms while increasing scrutiny on others.

Action: Hold CAD cash and avoid adding new fintech or crypto exchange exposure until the final rule is published. Source: cointelegraph.com

Warsh Swearing-In Friday

Kevin Warsh’s swearing-in as Fed chair is set for 11 a.m. Friday at the White House. Markets will immediately test whether the new chair can balance tariff-driven inflation pressures against the political mandate to cut rates.

Action: Keep duration short in bond holdings and wait for the post-swearing-in reaction before extending fixed-income exposure. Source: x.com
