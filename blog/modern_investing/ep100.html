Strategy Spotlight

Energy exposure through producers rather than broad commodity ETFs can capture short-term price spikes when geopolitical shocks hit supply routes like the Strait of Hormuz. Today’s nearly 6% oil jump after the U.S. revoked Iran’s oil-sale license shows how quickly margins expand for upstream names when inventories tighten. Implement this by screening TSX-listed energy producers for free-cash-flow yields above 8% and debt-to-equity below 0.5 using your broker’s screener, then size positions at 3-5% of TFSA capital. The approach worked best in 2022 when similar Strait tensions lifted Canadian energy names 40%+ over six weeks, but it carries the risk of rapid reversals once diplomatic talks resume. Pair any entry with a same-day volume check against the 20-day average to avoid false breakouts. Source: bnnbloomberg.ca

Investor Education: Tax-Loss Harvesting in Canada: The Strategy Guide

Imagine you bought XIU last month at $38.50 and it now trades at $36.80 after the broader market dip; selling locks in a $1.70 per-unit capital loss that can offset gains elsewhere in your TFSA or RRSP. The superficial loss rule blocks you from repurchasing the identical ETF for 30 days, so the pro move is swapping into a near-identical but distinct product such as XIC for that window before switching back. Most retail investors miss that the 2024 inclusion-rate increase to 50% on gains above $250,000 makes harvesting losses even more valuable because every dollar of loss now saves 25 cents in tax instead of 20. Losses carry forward indefinitely and can be applied against future gains or even up to $3,000 of ordinary income per year. The T5008 slip your broker issues in February reports proceeds but not your adjusted cost base, so you must track ACB yourself or use software to avoid CRA disputes. The biggest mistake with tax-loss harvesting is selling and immediately buying the same ticker. Instead, always map a 30-day substitute pair in advance using your brokerage’s ETF comparison tool.
