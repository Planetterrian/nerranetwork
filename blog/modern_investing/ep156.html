This creates a classic over-optimism setup where investors should apply a valuation screen first, then layer a same-day volume check before adding exposure. The approach works best in late-cycle environments when policy assumptions drive multiples higher than fundamentals support. Investor Education: Crypto as a Portfolio Allocation: A Risk-Managed Approach

Imagine you added a 3 percent position in a Canadian Bitcoin ETF last month after reading about institutional flows. Your order filled at the market price, but what actually happened behind the scenes was a brief liquidity gap that widened the bid-ask spread from 4 cents to 18 cents in the first 15 minutes of trading. Canadian investors can access BTCX or ETHX on the TSX with the same TFSA contribution room as any equity, yet the tax treatment differs because crypto gains are treated as capital gains with the new inclusion rate applying above the annual threshold. Professionals always check the ETF's premium or discount to net asset value before entry and compare it against the previous five-day average. Bitcoin versus altcoin risk profiles differ sharply because Bitcoin benefits from institutional custody solutions while many altcoins face higher regulatory and liquidity risks. Self-custody wallets reduce exchange counterparty exposure but introduce key-loss risk that exchange-held positions avoid. The regulatory landscape in Canada continues to evolve through CSA guidance on stablecoins and trading platforms, which can affect listing eligibility for new products. The biggest misconception is treating crypto like a high-beta tech stock; the one-sentence fix is to size any allocation between 1 and 5 percent of total portfolio value and rebalance only when it drifts more than 2 percentage points from target.
