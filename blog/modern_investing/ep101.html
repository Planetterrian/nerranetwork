Strategy Spotlight

Schwab’s warning that the era of easy index gains is ending points directly to a volatility-adjusted allocation approach. This strategy involves screening for stocks or sectors with depressed multiples that can buffer against macro shocks rather than chasing pure momentum. In today’s environment of more frequent geopolitical events and supply disruptions, the tactic starts with pulling a list of names trading below their 200-day average on elevated volume. Investors can implement it on Questrade or Interactive Brokers by setting alerts for volume above the 20-day average combined with forward P/E below sector medians. The approach worked best during 2022’s rate-hike cycle when value and defensive holdings outperformed broad indices by wide margins, but it carries the risk of prolonged underperformance if growth re-accelerates. Always pair the screen with same-day volume confirmation to avoid false rebounds. Source: marketwatch.com

Investor Education: Real Estate Investing in Canada Beyond Your Home

Imagine you added a REIT position to your TFSA last month expecting steady distributions while home prices stalled. Your order filled cleanly, yet the actual economics depend on whether the trust uses mortgage debt, property-level leverage, or both. Publicly traded REITs such as RioCan or Canadian Apartment REIT trade with daily liquidity but distribute most taxable income, creating immediate tax drag inside non-registered accounts. Mortgage investment corporations offer higher yields through private lending yet carry liquidity risk and different tax treatment as interest income. The professionals always check the distribution composition and debt-to-gross-book-value ratio before allocating, because a 60% payout ratio funded by new unit issuance is not the same as one backed by rising rental cash flow. The biggest mistake with real-estate exposure is treating every vehicle as equivalent to owning physical property. Instead, always match the structure to your account type and time horizon.
