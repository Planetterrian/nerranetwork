Strategy Spotlight

The core-satellite ETF approach builds a low-cost broad-market core with tactical satellite positions that target specific themes or sectors. Today's market conditions with mixed rate signals and sector rotation make this structure relevant because the core provides stability while satellites capture opportunities like the TSX miners that lifted the index. A Canadian investor can implement it in a TFSA by allocating seventy percent to XIC as the core for broad Canadian exposure and thirty percent to sector ETFs such as those tracking utilities or technology. Historical data shows this method performed best during periods of sector leadership shifts when rebalancing occurred quarterly rather than monthly to control transaction costs. Risks include over-concentration in satellites if the core allocation drifts below sixty percent. Use a platform like Wealthsimple or Questrade to set automatic rebalancing alerts when any satellite exceeds its target weight by five percent. Track performance against the TSX Composite to confirm the satellites are adding alpha without increasing overall volatility beyond the core alone. The XIC ETF itself trades with tight spreads that support frequent small adjustments inside registered accounts without eroding returns through wide bid-ask gaps. Sector satellites can be sized in fixed dollar amounts rather than percentages so that rebalancing triggers only when the core drifts more than five percent from its target weight. Quarterly rebalancing has historically kept transaction costs below the alpha generated by timely sector exposure during rotation periods. The approach separates the stable Canadian market beta from opportunistic bets on themes such as utilities or technology that may outperform during specific policy cycles. Source: seekingalpha.com

Investor Education: Core-Satellite ETF Portfolio Strategy

Imagine you bought XIC last week when the TSX rallied on mining gains and your order filled at the market price. The mechanism that actually determines your cost is the ETF's creation-redemption process which keeps the price within one or two basis points of net asset value even during volatile sessions. Most retail investors do not realize that holding the core in a registered account like a TFSA avoids annual capital gains distributions that can erode returns in non-registered accounts. The pro tip is to set satellite positions at fixed dollar amounts rather than percentages so rebalancing happens only when the core drifts more than five percent from target. The common misconception is that frequent satellite trading improves returns when the real edge comes from keeping the core allocation stable and letting the satellites run until they hit a predefined exit rule such as a twenty percent gain or a sector rotation signal. The creation-redemption arbitrage keeps tracking error low enough that even a five percent satellite allocation can be added or removed without moving the overall portfolio beta outside its intended range. Registered-account placement of the core also shields the investor from the annual tax drag that appears when the same ETF sits in a taxable brokerage account during high-distribution years. Fixed-dollar satellite sizing removes the need for constant percentage calculations and instead triggers a review only when the core weight moves beyond the five percent drift threshold. Exit rules tied to absolute gains or sector signals prevent the common error of holding a satellite too long after its original catalyst has faded.
