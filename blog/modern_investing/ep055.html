Strategy Spotlight

Bank earnings releases paired with dividend increases create a repeatable income-plus-growth screen that works well inside tax-advantaged accounts. Today's results from TD, RBC, and CIBC show profits rising between 25 percent year-over-year at RBC and solid beats at the others, with all three lifting payouts. The strategy is to filter TSX-listed banks for quarter-over-quarter dividend growth above 5 percent and payout ratios below 60 percent, then size positions at 3-5 percent of TFSA capital. This approach historically outperforms in late-cycle environments when rates stabilize because the yield provides a buffer while capital returns compound. Use Questrade or Wealthsimple screeners to pull the last four quarters of dividend history and cross-check against the most recent earnings release for confirmation. The main risk is sector concentration, so cap total financials exposure at 15 percent even when the screen lights up green. Source: bnnbloomberg.ca

Investor Education: Sector Rotation: Reading the Business Cycle

Imagine you bought a consumer discretionary ETF last week when inflation data first printed hotter than expected. Your order filled at the open, but what actually happened was a rapid rotation out of growth names into defensives as the yield curve steepened and PMI readings softened. The mechanism is straightforward: during expansion, cyclicals like industrials and consumer discretionary historically outperform by 4-8 percent annualized; at the peak, staples and utilities take the lead because earnings visibility matters more than revenue growth. Professionals always check the 10-year minus 2-year yield spread and the ISM new orders index before reallocating, because those two indicators lead sector moves by one to two quarters. The biggest mistake with sector rotation is treating it as a timing tool instead of a risk overlay. Instead, always keep a core 60 percent in broad market ETFs and use only 20-30 percent satellite sleeves that you rebalance when the yield curve or PMI crosses its 200-day average.
