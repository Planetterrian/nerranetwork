Strategy Spotlight

Valuation discipline pairs depressed multiples with same-day volume confirmation to buffer against momentum rotations. Today's session showed financials outperforming while tech lagged, creating setups where lower multiples can cushion downside if volume supports the move. Investors can implement this by screening TSX financial names trading below sector-average P/E, then checking the 20-day volume average on the same day before entry. This approach has historically provided stability during growth-index drawdowns when momentum names rotate out. Risks include false rebounds if volume fails to confirm, turning a buffer into a trap. Investor Education: Dividend Withholding in TFSAs

Imagine you added a U.S. dividend ETF to your TFSA last quarter expecting full compounding. Your brokerage credited the dividend at the announced amount, but the actual deposit was 15% lower because the IRS withheld tax at source before the shares reached your account. The mechanism works through the qualified intermediary system: U.S. payers route dividends through Canadian custodians who apply the treaty rate automatically for registered accounts, yet the 15% still reduces the reinvestable sum each quarter. Most retail investors miss that growth-oriented U.S. names with low or zero dividends minimize this drag compared with high-yield payers. The pro tip is to run a quick screen for dividend yield below 1.5% before adding any U.S. equity to a TFSA, then compare the after-withholding yield against an equivalent Canadian-listed ETF. The concrete misconception to avoid is treating the headline dividend as the net amount you will actually compound.
