Investor Education: Account Prioritization Mechanics

A Canadian investor with $10,000 available today must decide whether that cash enters the TFSA, RRSP, or FHSA first because each account carries different contribution-room rules and tax treatment on withdrawals. The TFSA grows tax-free with no tax on withdrawal, the RRSP delivers an immediate tax refund but taxes withdrawals as income, and the FHSA combines both benefits only for first-time home buyers. The correct order depends on current marginal tax rate, expected future tax rate, and whether home ownership is a near-term goal. Placing funds in the wrong account first can leave thousands in lost tax savings over a decade. The practical check is to model the after-tax outcome of each account choice using the investor's own tax bracket before transferring the cash. Contribution room in a TFSA carries forward indefinitely, while RRSP room resets each year based on earned income, and FHSA room is capped at a lifetime limit of $40,000. A higher current tax rate favors the RRSP for the immediate deduction, whereas a lower expected future rate favors the TFSA for tax-free growth. The Motley Fool Canada article outlines a simple priority sequence that begins with the FHSA if home purchase is planned within 15 years, followed by the TFSA for flexibility, then the RRSP for the tax refund. Dividend payers such as Scotiabank fit inside any of these accounts, but the tax credit on eligible Canadian dividends provides an extra advantage only in non-registered accounts. Modeling a $10,000 contribution at a 40 percent marginal rate shows an immediate $4,000 refund in the RRSP versus zero in the TFSA, yet the TFSA preserves full access without clawback on future income-tested benefits. The article notes that the TFSA offers the most flexibility for any future need while the RRSP provides the largest upfront tax saving when income is high. Investors should also consider that FHSA contributions can be transferred tax-free to an RRSP if home purchase plans change. The choice between accounts ultimately hinges on whether the investor values immediate tax relief, long-term tax-free growth, or the specific home-buying incentive. Running the numbers with current contribution room and projected tax brackets prevents costly sequencing errors that compound over decades. Source: fool.ca
