Strategy Spotlight

Passive-income ETFs built around high-quality dividend payers offer a mechanical way to compound without stock-picking risk. The approach works by holding a basket of companies with decades-long dividend-growth records, automatically reinvesting payouts inside a TFSA or RRSP to avoid immediate tax drag. In today’s environment of elevated rates and selective AI spending, these vehicles provide ballast when growth stocks rotate. Implementation is straightforward on Questrade or Wealthsimple: screen for ETFs with 10+ year payout histories and expense ratios below 0.25%, then dollar-cost average monthly. The strategy has delivered best results during sideways or modestly rising markets when yield plus modest capital appreciation outpaces cash; the main risk is sector concentration if the ETF tilts heavily into one industry. Always verify the current yield and distribution frequency before allocating more than 15% of a TFSA sleeve. Source: fool.com

Investor Education: ETF Dividend Reinvestment Mechanics

Imagine you bought a passive-income ETF last week at the market open after seeing the Motley Fool list; your 200-share order filled at the ask price of $42.18. What actually happened is the ETF’s net asset value was calculated at the previous close, but the market maker widened the bid-ask spread from 3 cents to 11 cents in the first 15 minutes because two large institutional sellers hit the tape, so your effective cost was 0.26% higher than the NAV. The pro tip most retail investors miss is that limit orders placed at the mid-point during the first 30 minutes of trading often get filled inside the spread once volume normalizes, whereas market orders pay the full widened spread. The biggest mistake with dividend ETFs is assuming the published yield already includes reinvestment friction; instead, always check the ETF’s distribution calendar and set a limit order at least one cent inside the current bid before the ex-dividend date to capture the full compounding effect without paying unnecessary spread.
