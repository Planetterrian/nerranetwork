Strategy Spotlight

Earnings volatility creates opportunities when investors separate reported beats from the quality of the underlying margin resilience. Today's Delta release shows how fuel-cost absorption can still produce an EPS beat when revenue growth outpaces the expense spike. Screen for carriers or transport operators where year-over-year revenue growth exceeds 8% and fuel-hedge disclosure appears in the MD&A. Use the company's 10-Q or earnings transcript to quantify the hedge ratio before entry. This approach worked best in 2022-2023 when oil swung 30%+ intra-year and only hedged operators preserved margins. The risk is sudden policy shifts that invalidate hedges, so limit position size to 3% of TFSA capital. Source: marketwatch.com

Investor Education: Crypto in a Traditional Portfolio: How Much and Why

Imagine you added 3% Bitcoin exposure via the Canadian-listed BTCX ETF to your TFSA last quarter when the S&P 500 dipped 4%. Your order filled at the market open, but the actual mechanics between click and fill included a 0.8% premium to spot plus the ETF's 0.45% management fee that compounds daily. Bitcoin's 0.15 correlation to the TSX Composite over the past 12 months improves the efficient frontier at 1-3% allocation, yet that correlation spiked above 0.6 during the March banking-stress window, erasing the diversification benefit exactly when it was needed. Professionals always cross-check the ETF's premium/discount to NAV on the exchange before buying and set a hard 5% portfolio cap so volatility cannot overwhelm the rest of the book. The biggest mistake with crypto allocation is treating the 1-5% band as a starting point rather than a ceiling. Instead, size the position so a 50% drawdown in the crypto sleeve produces no more than a 2.5% portfolio loss.
