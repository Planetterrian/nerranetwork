Strategy Spotlight

The ETF fee race leveling off reveals that investors are shifting priority from chasing the lowest expense ratios toward total cost of ownership, including liquidity, tracking error, and tax efficiency inside registered accounts. With the average ETF expense ratio now stable after years of compression, the marginal benefit of switching purely for five basis points has diminished while the cost of trading and potential capital-gains drag has not. Canadian investors can implement this by screening for ETFs with tight bid-ask spreads and high average daily volume rather than headline fees alone, then holding them inside a TFSA where tax drag is already eliminated. This approach worked best during the 2022-2024 rate-hike cycle when liquidity premiums widened dramatically on thinly traded thematic funds. The risk is over-paying for liquidity on products that rarely trade, so the practical filter is to require at least 500,000 shares in average daily volume before considering an ETF for anything beyond a core long-term holding. Investor Education: Technical Analysis: What the Charts Actually Tell You

Imagine you bought an energy ETF last week after oil spiked on geopolitical headlines and your order filled at the market open. The chart showed price sitting just above the 50-day moving average with RSI(14) at 58, yet the next two sessions erased the entire gain. What actually happened is that the 20-day average volume was only 60% of normal on the breakout day, so the move lacked the institutional participation needed to hold the level. Moving averages (especially the 50-day and 200-day) act as dynamic support and resistance zones rather than magic lines; a golden cross (50-day crossing above 200-day) has shown positive forward returns in broad indices over multi-year periods, but the same pattern on single stocks produces far more false signals. Volume confirmation remains the single most reliable filter—rising price on above-average volume increases the odds the move is real, while declining volume on a rally is a classic warning. RSI above 70 or below 30 flags overbought or oversold conditions on the 14-day setting, yet these readings frequently persist in strong trends, so treating them as automatic reversal triggers is a common retail error. MACD crossovers add momentum context but lag in fast markets. The honest limitation is that technical analysis improves entry and exit timing far more than it predicts which stocks will fundamentally outperform. The biggest mistake with technical analysis is treating chart patterns as predictive astrology. Instead, always require at least one confirming factor—volume surge, earnings catalyst, or sector rotation—before acting on a pure price pattern.

Tools & Techniques

Wealthsimple Trade Advanced Order Types: Wealthsimple

Wealthsimple now offers conditional orders and extended-hour trading for Canadian and U.S. equities inside registered accounts. This gives TFSA and RRSP users the ability to set bracket orders that automatically protect gains or limit losses without monitoring screens all day. Intermediate investors who already hold positions in volatile sectors should enable these tools to enforce discipline on weekly holds. Access is available at no extra cost within the standard Wealthsimple Trade app for users with at least $1,000 in assets. Source: bnnbloomberg.ca

Interactive Brokers TWS API for Custom Screeners: Interactive Brokers

The Trader Workstation API lets users build automated scans that combine volume, moving-average distance, and sector-relative strength in real time. This edge is most useful for investors running multiple weekly holds who want alerts when a name meets all three technical filters simultaneously. Pricing starts at the fixed monthly commission plan; the API itself is free once the account is approved. Source: reddit.com
