Strategy Spotlight

Sector rotation into defensive energy names works by shifting capital from high-beta growth stocks into producers with stable cash flows and lower correlation to broad equity declines. Today's Morgan Stanley upgrade of Galp highlights how European energy can act as a buffer when U.S. indices wobble on rate uncertainty. Implement it by screening for upgrades on platforms like Wealthsimple or Questrade, then allocating 5-10 percent of a TFSA to an ETF such as XEG while monitoring the 20-day volume average for confirmation. Investor Education: Asset Allocation — The Only Free Lunch in Investing

Imagine you bought XEQT last week when the TSX posted modest gains. Your order filled at the market price, but here's what actually happened between your click and that fill: the ETF's underlying mix of equities and bonds rebalanced internally as global equity weights shifted 0.4 percent that day. Modern Portfolio Theory shows that combining uncorrelated assets like Canadian equities, U.S. large-caps, and bonds moves you closer to the efficient frontier where expected return stays the same while volatility drops 15-20 percent. Strategic allocation sets long-term targets by risk tolerance, while tactical shifts of 5 percent or less let you respond to events like today's energy rotation without overtrading. Bonds still reduce drawdowns post-2022 because their negative correlation with stocks reappears once rate volatility subsides. Age-based rules like 60 percent equities at age 40 are oversimplified; instead, run a quick risk quiz on your broker's platform and set a 5 percent threshold for rebalancing. The biggest mistake with asset allocation is ignoring correlation drift after a strong equity year. Instead, always review your XBAL or VGRO holdings quarterly against target weights and rebalance only when any sleeve drifts beyond 5 percent.
