Investor Education: Tax-Loss Harvesting Mechanics

Imagine you bought an ETF tracking Canadian banks last spring and it now sits 12% below your cost base after rate-sensitive rotation. Your order to sell would trigger a capital loss, yet the superficial loss rule means you cannot repurchase the identical ETF for 30 days without the CRA denying the loss. The mechanism works by requiring a 30-day window on either side of the sale during which you must stay out of the exact security or an identical one, but you can immediately buy a similar-but-not-identical ETF—such as switching from XIU to XIC or a global financials ETF—to maintain market exposure while still claiming the loss. Professionals always verify the T5008 slip issued by their broker the following February to confirm the loss was properly reported before carrying it forward indefinitely against future gains. The capital-gains inclusion rate change that took effect in 2024 makes harvesting more valuable because only half of gains are taxable, so sheltering losses early compounds the benefit across multiple years. The common misconception is that you must wait until December to harvest; the fix is to scan positions quarterly and execute any harvest as soon as the 30-day clock can be satisfied without disrupting your target allocation. Losses realized this way remain available to offset gains in any future year, and the T5008 reporting requirement means investors should retain brokerage statements that show both the sale and the replacement purchase to defend the claim if audited.

Tools & Techniques

Theta Decay Efficiency Calculator

This simple spreadsheet or broker platform feature lets you compare daily premium collection across different option expirations so you avoid the trap of selling long-dated contracts that deliver almost zero theta per day. It gives investors an edge by quantifying income per day rather than total premium, directly supporting the rule of repeating monthly sales instead of one multi-year position. Any options trader with a standard brokerage account can build it in five minutes using the option chain data already provided by the platform. When you sell options, you want to collect theta decay as fast as possible, and most people focus on total income collected rather than income collected per day; a long-dated option collects close to zero premium per day, which means capital sits idle while monthly sales compound the same capital repeatedly. Source: x.com

TFSA Income Portfolio Builder

Motley Fool Canada's model allocates $30,000 across one ETF and two REITs to target more than $110 monthly in tax-free distributions. Canadian investors can replicate the structure inside any TFSA by selecting the specific holdings listed in the article and rebalancing annually to maintain the yield target while respecting contribution-room limits. The setup works because the chosen REITs and ETF together produce a blended yield that clears the $110 monthly threshold after fees, and the TFSA wrapper ensures every distribution remains tax-free even when the inclusion rate on capital gains rises. If volume sits above the twenty-day average, add it to a watchlist for potential tax-loss harvesting before year-end.
