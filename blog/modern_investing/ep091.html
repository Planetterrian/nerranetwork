Strategy Spotlight

Corporate bond issuance by U.S. tech giants offers a lower-volatility way to gain exposure to AI spending without riding equity drawdowns. When companies such as Microsoft and peers tap foreign bond markets to fund data-center buildouts, the resulting investment-grade paper typically carries spreads that compensate for duration risk while remaining senior to equity. Canadian investors can access these names through USD-denominated corporate bond ETFs held inside a TFSA, capturing yield while avoiding the 50% inclusion rate on equity capital gains. Implementation starts with screening for recent issuance on platforms that show real-time bond data, then sizing the position to 5-8% of fixed-income sleeve so that rising rates do not overwhelm the carry. This approach has historically performed best in mid-cycle expansions when earnings growth supports credit metrics but equity valuations already price in perfection. The main risk is spread widening if AI capex disappoints, which is why a hard stop at 3% below entry price on the ETF is required. Source: theglobeandmail.com

Investor Education: Passive Indexing vs Active Management

Imagine you bought an S&P 500 ETF last week when the index dipped 0.8% on geopolitical headlines; your fill came at the exact closing NAV because the ETF’s creation-redemption mechanism keeps tracking tight. The mechanism works because authorized participants arbitrage any premium or discount in real time, keeping expense ratios under 0.05% while delivering the index return minus that tiny fee. What most retail investors do not realize is that the same low-cost structure also prevents tax-loss harvesting opportunities that active managers can exploit inside the same account type. Professionals always check the SPIVA scorecard before deciding: over 85% of active large-cap funds have underperformed the S&P 500 over 15 years, yet the same data shows active small-cap and emerging-market managers beat their benchmarks roughly 40% of the time. The biggest mistake with the passive-versus-active debate is treating it as an all-or-nothing choice. Instead, always run a core-satellite test: keep 70% in low-cost index ETFs for market beta, then allocate the remaining 30% to active sleeves only where the SPIVA outperformance rate exceeds 50% and your own due diligence confirms repeatable edge.
