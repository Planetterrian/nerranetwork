Strategy Spotlight

The two-stage TFSA-RRSP arbitrage exploits the fact that RRSP contributions generate larger tax refunds when made in higher marginal brackets while TFSA room can be preserved for later lower-bracket years. With fast-growing incomes common among mid-career Canadians, the approach involves maxing RRSP contributions in peak-earning years then shifting future savings into the TFSA once income (and the refund value) drops. Implementation requires tracking adjusted cost base across both accounts and using a tax-planning spreadsheet or Wealthsimple Tax to model the refund differential each year. The strategy has historically added 1–2 percentage points of after-tax compounding for investors whose income crosses brackets multiple times over a decade. Risk lies in over-contributing to the RRSP and facing withdrawal taxes if cash needs arise before retirement. Source: financialpost.com

Investor Education: Technical Analysis Fundamentals for Modern Investors

Imagine you bought a TSX-listed industrial name last week after it bounced from the 200-day moving average on heavy volume. Your order filled at the ask, yet the next candle closed below that average because the 50-day line was already sloping downward. The mechanism here is simple: support levels only hold when volume exceeds the 20-day average and RSI (14) stays above 40; otherwise the bounce is a liquidity grab. Professionals always check the slope of the 50-day versus the 200-day before treating any test as valid. The biggest mistake retail traders make is treating every support touch as an automatic buy. Instead, always require both a volume spike and RSI confirmation before entering.
