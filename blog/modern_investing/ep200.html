Strategy Spotlight

Contributing TFSA room early is a simple planning tactic that locks in the full annual limit without waiting for December. With the 2026 contribution room at $7,000, moving the contribution forward reduces the chance of missing the deadline amid year-end cash-flow pressure. Implementation is straightforward on any brokerage platform: transfer the amount from a non-registered account or cash balance as soon as the new room is available on January 1. The approach has worked best in years when contribution-room changes or tax-year deadlines create last-minute rushes. The main risk is opportunity cost if the cash earns higher interest outside the TFSA, but that is usually small relative to the planning certainty gained. An earlier TFSA contribution can simplify your plan, even when the immediate tax saving is smaller than you expect. Investor Education: The Canadian Home Bias Problem

An investor who keeps 40% of a TFSA in TSX-listed financials and energy names because they feel familiar with the companies is effectively doubling down on the same economic exposures already embedded in their job and housing. The mechanism that creates the gap is simple: Canada represents roughly 3% of global equity market capitalization, yet many portfolios hold 30-50% in Canadian stocks, producing heavy concentration in two sectors that together make up about half the TSX. A pro habit that narrows the gap is to run a quick geographic allocation report in the brokerage once a quarter and compare the Canadian weight against the MSCI World ex-Canada benchmark. The common misconception is that familiarity with domestic names automatically justifies the overweight; the fix is to treat home bias as an explicit, sized decision rather than the default. Keeping forty percent of a TFSA in TSX-listed financials and energy names because the companies feel familiar is not diversification. It is doubling down on the same economic exposures already sitting in a Canadian job and a Canadian house. That overweight lands in two sectors that together make up about half the TSX, so the home book is a financials-and-energy book with a maple-leaf label. Run a geographic allocation report in the brokerage once a quarter and compare the Canadian weight against the MSCI World ex-Canada benchmark, which is the unglamorous habit that makes the overweight visible. The common misconception is that familiarity with domestic names automatically justifies that concentration. The fix is to treat home bias as an explicit, sized decision rather than the default setting of a TFSA. Size it on purpose if you want the dividend stream and the energy tape.
