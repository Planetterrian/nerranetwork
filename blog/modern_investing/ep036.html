Strategy Spotlight

The 2019 repo ruckus demonstrated that reserves drifting lower in a calm macro setting can still trigger sharp spikes in short-term funding rates without any active quantitative tightening. Today's market conditions make this lesson relevant because some observers assume the Fed can simply offset slower rate cuts by adjusting balance-sheet runoff at the margin. In practice, the nonlinearity of reserve demand and the fragility of money-market plumbing mean the two tools are not clean substitutes. To implement this insight, track weekly reserve levels and repo-rate prints alongside the Fed's balance-sheet schedule using data from the New York Fed's website or your broker's macro dashboard. The strategy worked best historically when reserves approached levels that stressed dealer balance sheets, as seen in September 2019. The main risk is overestimating how quickly the Fed can pivot the balance sheet without market stress. Treat the funds rate and balance sheet as distinct policy levers rather than interchangeable knobs. Source: x.com

Investor Education: Asset Allocation: The Only Free Lunch in Investing

Imagine you bought a globally diversified ETF like XEQT last week when equity markets dipped on Fed commentary. Your order filled at the prevailing price, but what actually happened between your click and the execution was a rebalancing flow across stocks, bonds, and international holdings that reduced single-asset volatility without lowering expected long-term return. Modern Portfolio Theory shows that combining assets with low correlations can move your portfolio closer to the efficient frontier, where risk is minimized for any given return target. Strategic allocation sets long-term weights based on your risk tolerance, while tactical shifts respond to short-term signals such as valuation spreads or sector momentum. Bonds still serve as ballast even after 2022's drawdowns because they provide negative correlation during equity stress, though the magnitude has varied. Age-based rules like "100 minus age in stocks" oversimplify because they ignore personal factors such as pension income or TFSA room. Rebalance using threshold triggers (for example, when any asset class drifts more than 5% from target) rather than rigid calendar dates to avoid unnecessary transaction costs inside registered accounts. The biggest misconception is treating asset allocation as a one-time decision. Instead, review your XBAL or VGRO holdings quarterly against your written risk tolerance statement and adjust only when thresholds are breached.
