Ten Grey County councillors will not return after the term's last meeting.

By Mira November 20, 2026 Ep 9 7 min read Listen to podcast

What You Need to Know: Ten Grey County councillors will not return after the term's last meeting. Warden Andrea Matrosovs closed the 2023-2026 term on November 12 with service pins, a Rockwood Terrace rebuild she said is 75 per cent complete, and two new paramedic bases. The week also includes a serious school-bus crash near Stayner with no students aboard, Collingwood's overnight parking ban until April, and a new Blue Mountain ski-week package.

The Week's Stories

Grey County warden proud of accomplishments of outgoing council: Sarnia News Today

Grey County Council closed its 2023-2026 term on November 12 and bid farewell to ten members who are not returning, Sarnia News Today reported. Bronze pins went to Warden Andrea Matrosovs, Ross Kentner and Dane Nielsen after four years, to Peter Bordignon after five, and to Terry McKay and Tom Hutchinson after eight; silver pins went to Sue Paterson, Ian Boddy and Scott Mackey after twelve years and to Brian Milne after sixteen. In her closing address, Matrosovs said that behind every decision there are heads, hands and hearts, and she pointed to Rockwood Terrace, 75 per cent complete ahead of a planned 2027 opening, and to Lee Manor as one of 17 Ontario long-term care homes to receive provincial dementia-care funding. She said the Durham paramedic base opened in September 2025 and Feversham in October 2026, and that paramedics met or exceeded most response-time targets even with a 9 per cent increase in calls. She also cited Colour It Home, a 10-year housing and homelessness strategy introduced in April 2026 after more than 1,000 additional nights of shelter, and a road-transfer agreement set to take legal effect on June 1, 2027. Matrosovs thanked Deputy Mayor Peter Bordignon and Deputy Warden Brian Milne, and said work with the Chippewas of Nawash, the Chippewas of Saugeen and the Six Nations of the Grand River had built a foundation for the next term. Source: sarnianewstoday.ca

60-year-old bus driver seriously injured after crash with transport truck near Stayner: CTV News

A 60-year-old school bus driver was seriously injured in a crash with a transport truck near Stayner, and the Simcoe Muskoka Catholic District School Board said no students were on the bus, CTV News reported. OPP were called just before 7 a.m. to Highway 26 between Centre Line Road and 3-4 Sideroad, and the bus driver was taken to hospital, Barrie 360 reported. Simcoe.com later reported the Stayner roadway had reopened. Source: ctvnews.ca Source: barrie360.com Source: simcoe.com

No overnight street parking allowed until April: CollingwoodToday

Collingwood's winter parking restrictions are in place, CollingwoodToday reported. No overnight street parking is allowed until April. Source: collingwoodtoday.ca

Blue Mountain Brings The All-Inclusive Ski Week Back To Ontario, And Every Guest Goes Home With A Season Pass: The Hype Magazine

Blue Mountain Resort, in its 85th anniversary season, announced on November 18 an All-Inclusive Ski Week starting at $1,299 per adult, The Hype Magazine reported. The resort says the package saves more than $500 per adult against full retail and bundles three nights, eight meals, daily lift tickets, two days of group lessons, evening activities and a 5×7 Pass for the rest of the winter. Sessions run Monday to Thursday, January 18 to 21 and February 15 to 18, 2027, and the February session begins on Family Day. Pricing is in Canadian dollars for two adults in a double-occupancy studio before HST and the 4 per cent municipal accommodation tax: adults 18 and over start at $1,299, a youth add-on for ages 13 to 17 is $999, and a child add-on for ages 7 to 12 is $734, with at least one adult package per room, booking at least seven days ahead, and no combining with other offers. Jennifer Allan-Cummings, hospitality marketing manager, said the package takes the stress of planning off the table so guests can spend more time on the slopes and make new friends. The 5×7 Pass covers skiing and riding Monday to Friday from 9 a.m. to 9 p.m. and weekend evenings from 3:30 p.m. to 9 p.m., through a projected close in late March 2027, subject to weather, with blackout windows on select peak dates; deeparrival reported the 85th winter also adds a lift safety scanner and snowmaking. Source: thehypemagazine.com Source: deeparrival.com

Grey County Snowplows Hit The Road With Names Chosen By Residents: The Meaford Independent

Residents have named all 23 Grey County snowplows for the 2026/2027 winter after more than 650 suggestions and two weeks of voting on Connect Grey, the platform that launched October 9, The Meaford Independent reported. Ideas were in until October 22 and voting ran until November 6. Leading names include Blizzard Wizard, Agnes MacPlow, Betty Whiteout, Clearopathtra, Dolly Plowton and Plowy McPlowFace, and communications manager Rob Hatten also pointed drivers to Blizzard of Oz and Darth Blader. Hatten said crews who maintain 835 kilometres of two-lane highway across nine municipalities cleared an average of 460 centimetres of snow last winter and applied 36,000 tonnes of pre-treated salt and winter sand. He asked drivers to be patient and give plows room, because there is always a person behind the wheel. Source: themeafordindependent.ca Source: sarnianewstoday.ca

The Closer Look

Ice Rescue Recognition For Grey County EMS: Bayshore Broadcasting News Centre

Grey County paramedics are being recognized for their part in a 2026 ice rescue in Owen Sound, Bayshore Broadcasting reported. At last week's council meeting, Grey Bruce OPP Staff Sergeant Henry Thalen praised everyone involved. On behalf of the OPP, each Grey County paramedic who responded received a letter of commendation. Kyle Stewart, wellness and support manager for Grey County Paramedic Services, put the incident in front of council.

Stewart said that on March 8 a rapidly evolving, life-threatening emergency unfolded in Georgian Bay. Two large sections of ice broke away from shore and carried 23 ice fishers approximately 2 kilometres, in two different directions, out into the bay. As conditions deteriorated, the ice continued to fracture. A multi-agency response followed, involving paramedics, fire services, the OPP, Ornge, dispatch and other supporting partners. Through that coordination, Stewart said, all 23 people were brought safely to shore. Some patients suffered minor injuries and hypothermia. All recovered.

Stewart told council this was an extraordinary and highly complex incident that brought emergency service partners from across the region together. He said he had been present for part of the response and had seen the professionalism, teamwork and commitment. He called the morning a reminder of how quickly conditions can change, and of the critical role coordinated emergency response plays in protecting communities on the bay.

CTV News described the same operation as Canada's largest ice rescue. Bayshore named the Grey County paramedics tied to the commendations: Mike Larocque, Amanda Nicholson, Nate Downing, Meghan Clouatre, Andy Brown, Paula Trombley, Stacy Chandler, Andrew Rogers, Alex O'Donohoe, Laura Winer, Andy Millman, Joe Draper and Mark Urquhart.

The 23 fishers are the people the morning changed first: they were carried about 2 kilometres offshore on breaking ice and still reached shore, some with hypothermia, all of them recovered. The letters of commendation change the record for the named paramedics, whose work that day is now formally recognized by the OPP. Stewart's account to council is also a warning for anyone who goes onto Georgian Bay ice. Ice that starts at the shore can separate and travel. Source: bayshorebroadcasting.ca Source: ctvnews.ca
