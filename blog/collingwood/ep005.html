The Week's Stories

Collingwood CAO Sonya Skinner Receives National Public Service Award: Barrie 360

Collingwood chief administrative officer Sonya Skinner is one of two winners of the 2026 Institute of Public Administration of Canada Established Leader Award, which Barrie 360 describes as one of Canada's top public-service honours. The award was presented at IPAC's National Public Service Symposium in Ottawa on October 7, and Skinner was chosen from a national shortlist of seven. She has been Collingwood's CAO since 2020, after more than two decades in executive and board roles that included the Grey Sauble Conservation Authority and the Ontario Public Service, spanning municipal administration, environmental regulation, transportation, and information technology. Since joining the town she has led work on governance, financial sustainability, and community trust. Skinner said it is a great honour, that she is grateful, and that she is proud to work with dedicated colleagues and community leaders. Town officials said the award also reflects leadership across the organization, noting several municipal employees have been recognized this year. Source: barrie360.com Source: collingwoodtoday.ca

Wasaga Beach Resident Charged in Child Exploitation Investigation: Barrie 360

The Ontario Provincial Police Child Sexual Exploitation Unit, with help from Peel Regional Police, executed a search warrant at a Wasaga Beach home on June 23 and seized several electronic devices. Police say the accused was arrested on September 12. The accused is charged with two counts of possession of child sexual abuse and exploitation material, one count of making that material, one count of accessing it, and one count of voyeurism. The accused is scheduled to appear in court on October 27. Source: barrie360.com Source: ctvnews.ca

NVCA honours Lynn Dollin for nearly 20 years of source water work: CollingwoodToday

The Nottawasaga Valley Conservation Authority has named the boardroom in the John Hix Administrative Centre the Lynn Dollin Boardroom. CollingwoodToday reports the naming recognizes nearly 20 years of leadership and service on the South Georgian Bay Lake Simcoe Source Protection Committee. BarrieToday identifies Dollin as the mayor of Innisfil. Source: collingwoodtoday.ca Source: barrietoday.com

The Closer Look

Grey County Paramedic Chief earns provincial leadership award: CKNX News Today

Grey County paramedic chief Kevin McNab is the sole recipient of the 2026 Richard Armstrong Leadership Award. The Ontario Association of Paramedic Chiefs presented it at the association's annual gathering in Niagara Falls earlier this month. Grey County council recognized him at its meeting on Thursday, October 22. The Owen Sound Current reports that the county says he was the only recipient this year. The award recognizes leadership, innovation, and contributions to paramedicine in Ontario.

McNab has worked for Grey County for more than 30 years, including nine years as paramedic chief. A county media release says he guided Grey County Paramedic Services toward a standout standard for community-based paramedicine, medical innovation, and seamless care delivery. He took the local Community Paramedicine Program from an initial trial to a service the county calls essential, one that aids hundreds of people managing chronic conditions. Community paramedics provide that care in patients' homes, outside emergency calls. He obtained provincial grants so the program could continue without adding costs for municipal taxpayers. He has also worked on paramedic training, Ontario-wide clinical guidelines, and health strategies the county credits with better patient care in Grey and across the province. During the COVID-19 pandemic, that leadership supported localized testing and vaccination outreach for high-risk residents. The Owen Sound Current says the service delivered in-home testing and vaccinations.

Grey County Warden Andrea Matrosovs said McNab's leadership has had a profound impact on healthcare in Grey County and beyond. She said he is never satisfied with the status quo and looks for new ways to improve care, support paramedics, and strengthen the healthcare system. She said his willingness to innovate, collaborate, and advocate has made Grey County a leader in community paramedicine, and that the county is proud of the provincial award. McNab said he is honoured and shares the recognition with the entire Grey County Paramedic Services team. He thanked county council for its support and leadership. He said the work is built on collaboration, dedication, and a shared commitment to the best possible care for residents. The county ties the honour to more than 160 paramedics and support personnel who care for the community every day, and says his career has developed emergency medical care and health outcomes for residents in Grey County and the wider region. Source: cknxnewstoday.ca Source: owensoundsuntimes.com Source: owensoundcurrent.com
