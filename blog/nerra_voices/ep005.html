Jason Shumard

Functional Medicine practitioner

A chiropractor who watched conventional care fail his mother now runs a virtual functional-medicine practice that treats Type 2 diabetes reversal as the measurable outcome and scales it with coaching teams.

What you need to know

Jason Shumard described testing 99 percent of diagnosed type 2 diabetics and finding toxin loads—mold, heavy metals, or environmental chemicals—while noting far lower rates in non-diabetics whose detoxification systems still function. His virtual functional-medicine practice therefore treats diabetes as cellular disease rather than a blood-sugar problem, scaling reversal through lab-guided protocols and coaching teams instead of direct physician visits. The measurable goal is healthier cells that naturally lower sugars, with patients required to remain under their own prescribing physicians.

What we talked about
  • Stepped back from seeing patients himself to build and oversee a full team handling visits, marketing, and acquisitions so the mission could reach a million lives through ripple effects rather than solo work.
  • Prioritizes toxin removal before liver or gut repair because toxins cause systemic damage; uses the triage analogy of stopping bleeding before treating a virus.
  • Screens every new patient with symptom-tailored panels—comprehensive blood work, hormones, urine, stool, and full toxin testing—and refuses cases without an existing medical doctor supervising medications.
  • States that 70 percent of healing comes from belief and motivation while only 30 percent comes from clinical steps, and therefore turns away prospects who want to be convinced rather than demonstrate strong internal drive.
  • Tracks progress with the triglyceride-to-HDL ratio (target below 2), serum insulin, inflammatory markers, and A1C, viewing sugars strictly as the scoreboard.
  • Requires patients to maintain medical supervision at intake and returns them to their physicians for any medication adjustments once sugars begin dropping naturally.
  • Learned from early hiring mistakes to screen first for mission belief, then skill, and now uses paid $97 consultations to filter workshop attendees who lack genuine commitment.
