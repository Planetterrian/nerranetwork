In their own words

I develop practical education and training approaches that help students, professionals and organizational leaders use AI while retaining independent judgment, verification skills and human responsibility. My work includes Human Judgment in the Age of AI and Learn AI Like a Pro.

What you need to know

Glenn Broder describes a test he ran on a chat model: a story so absurd that a child would have seen through it, and after about ten exchanges the AI was telling him to move away from the windows, lock the doors, call 911 and let nobody in. He now uses that exchange when he trains people, especially children, to show that ungoverned AI is dangerous and that governed AI can do remarkable work, as long as human judgment stays in place.

What we talked about
  • He calls it a cognitive jungle gym when people verify and confirm what the AI drafts, and a cognitive wheelchair when they just press send; timing the review step shows which is which.
  • His test of whether someone used AI well is whether they can explain their work. If they cannot, they used it as a wheelchair.
  • Students in their seventies catch the model's mistakes because they are expert in the work they are doing.
  • He teaches deterministic versus probabilistic software with a wrong button that returns an error, and a model learning to fill in "the dog is chasing a ___".
  • He applies the Talmudic logic he was taught as a child, questioning everything respectfully until the nuance is clear, to all of his systems, including HACS, the control layer that checks prompts going in and answers coming out against the company canon.
  • His newest formulation: build your business canon outside your network, so you are never locked into one AI provider.
