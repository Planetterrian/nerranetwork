Viktor Popovic

Founder, President, Avendo

An immigrant founder who rebuilt after losing Google traffic overnight now routes merchant payments in real time to expose the hidden fees his first company’s margins never revealed.

In their own words

I’m Viktor Popovic, Co-Founder and President of Avendo. Before Avendo, I spent 21 years building Ultimate Washer from a small startup into a national eCommerce and Amazon business, which I eventually sold. I came to the U.S. after leaving the former Yugoslavia during the civil war, so a lot of my life has been about starting over, building from limited resources, and figuring things out without a perfect playbook. Today I’m building Avendo, a fintech SaaS company focused on bringing more transparency and competition to payment processing for online merchants.

What you need to know

Viktor Popovic rebuilt after 21 years running Ultimate Washer by creating Avendo, a platform that routes each merchant transaction in real time across multiple processors so the lowest price wins without any lock-in. The most striking detail is that he now charges processors only ten cents per successful transaction and nothing to merchants, a direct response to the opaque statements and stagnant rates he accepted for over a decade.

What we talked about
  • After 14 years with one processor, Viktor saw volume grow while rates stayed fixed because statements were four to ten pages of line items that owners handed to accountants rather than challenge.
  • Avendo collects one application and set of documents, then forwards them to multiple processors; once several underwrite the merchant, the system lets those processors bid live on every card transaction.
  • The merchant dashboard shows only the final approved amount, statement, and chargeback tools; all routing logic, fallback decisions, and price responses stay hidden in the backend and complete in milliseconds.
  • A queue was added after live testing showed that transaction logging, not authorization, created stalls; the two backend teams met, mapped the bottleneck, and inserted the queue so results could be written without blocking the flow.
  • Weekly rotating tests now run across system segments to catch similar issues before they scale, because the architecture must duplicate cleanly for future markets such as Canada.
  • Viktor’s prior experience with seven separate product databases taught him that disconnected tools force constant manual updates; Avendo’s teams therefore meet at least weekly to keep every new component on a single source of truth.
