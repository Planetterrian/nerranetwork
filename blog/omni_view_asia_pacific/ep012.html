Twenty-five thousand Korean bank customers had data leaked, and the president ordered a probe.

What You Need to Know: Twenty-five thousand Korean bank customers had data leaked, and the president ordered a probe. President Lee Jae Myung told authorities to investigate hacking at major banks and other financial firms after a run of breaches. The Financial Services Commission says payment data does not appear to have leaked, but warns the stolen details could still be used in scams.

Lead

Lee calls for thorough probe into personal data leaks at financial institutions: Yonhap

President Lee Jae Myung on Sunday called for a thorough investigation into hacking attacks that leaked personal information at major banks and other financial institutions, presidential spokesperson Kang Yu-jung said. She said he was briefed on breaches at financial and public institutions and on the measures already taken, and that he told authorities to treat the matter seriously and spare no effort on a response. Yonhap reports that the firms include Hana Bank, KB Kookmin Bank and Shinhan Bank. Shinhan said on Thursday that names, phone numbers and annual income were among the details taken from around 25,000 customers, and sources told Yonhap the attackers used advanced artificial intelligence tools. Personal information of 89 Hana Bank customers was leaked, while Woori Bank and NH Nonghyup Bank were targeted, blocked unauthorized access, and reported no leak. Financial Services Commission Chairman Lee Eog-weon and Financial Supervisory Service Governor Lee Chan-jin held an emergency meeting with the heads of the affected firms; Lee Eog-weon said breaches in a short span had also hit savings banks and specialized credit finance firms, that information usable for unauthorized payments did not appear to have leaked, and that the sector should keep the highest vigilance because the data could still be used for voice phishing or other scams. He did not rule out the use of AI tools in the attacks. Source: en.yna.co.kr

Across the Region

India raises deepwater gas price to $9.89; APM capped at $7: Rediff MoneyWiz

India raised the deepwater gas price ceiling to 9.89 dollars per million British thermal units, up from 8.90 dollars. The price for APM gas from legacy fields remains capped at 7 dollars per million British thermal units. New wells from ONGC and OIL can fetch up to 7.70 dollars per million British thermal units, a step Rediff MoneyWiz says is meant to incentivize investment. The higher deepwater ceiling is aimed at encouraging production from challenging offshore fields, under separate pricing mechanisms for different gas sources. Source: money.rediff.com

Philippines arrests 244 foreign workers in online scam crackdown: CNA

Philippine authorities arrested 244 foreign workers in a crackdown on illegal online gaming. Authorities say this is the Philippines' largest crackdown on illegal online gaming operators this year. Source: channelnewsasia.com

Punjabi influencer, makeup artist shot dead in Amritsar: The Hindu

Two unidentified people on a motorbike shot makeup artist and influencer Mad Sandhu, whose real name is Madhusudan, on Loharka road in Amritsar at about 6 p.m. Saturday, and he died in hospital, Additional Deputy Commissioner of Police Sirivennela said. Source: thehindu.com

Indian and Pakistani diplomats summoned over BSF killing two ‘infiltrators’: The Hindu

The Hindu reports that Indian and Pakistani diplomats were summoned after the Border Security Force killed two people described as infiltrators, while Pakistan's Foreign Ministry accused Indian security forces of using unprovoked lethal force against unarmed civilians. Source: thehindu.com

Shark warning for NSW river after whale carcass removed: ABC News Australia

A marine biologist says sharks are likely to be attracted to a New South Wales sandbar where a dead whale was left for more than a day, after a warning for a New South Wales river once the carcass was removed. Source: abc.net.au

Beijing investigates imports of a European chemical over suspected dumping practices: Voice of Emirates

Beijing is investigating imports of a European chemical over suspected dumping practices. Source: voiceofemirates.com

The Region and the World

Japan's Takaichi presses US after marine arrested over murder-robbery of woman: South China Morning Post

Okinawa prefectural police said a 20-year-old U.S. marine, Davin Ballard, stationed at Futenma Airbase, was arrested on Sunday on suspicion of killing Anna Yagi, 39, and taking her wallet and backpack in the early hours of Saturday at a hotel in Naha. Police said he was seen entering the hotel with Yagi, who was found dead on Saturday morning, and that he has denied the charges. The New York Times reports that Japan protested after the marine was accused of killing a woman in Okinawa. South China Morning Post reports that Japan's Takaichi pressed the United States after the arrest. U.S. officials say a marine was detained in connection with a death in Japan. Source: scmp.com

Both Sides: Should India make confiscation of corrupt assets mandatory?

The Madurai Bench of the Madras High Court has not changed the Prevention of Corruption Act. Justice B. Pugalendhi said necessary amendments have to be carried out so that attachment and confiscation become mandatory for properties believed to have been obtained through corrupt means, or through wealth disproportionate to known sources of income.

The case for that change is the Court's. Justice Pugalendhi said the objective of the 1988 Act is not merely to punish persons found guilty of corrupt practices, but also to ensure that the fruits of such conduct do not remain available for enjoyment or disposal by those who acquired them through illegal means. Attachment, he said, is an important part of that mechanism. The Court said corruption has to be treated more seriously than any other offence, and that mandatory attachment and confiscation are essential because corruption has affected every part of society. Only if such deterrent measures are prescribed under the Act, the Court said, can the government effectively attach properties and instil fear in officials who enjoy properties obtained through corrupt means.

The case for leaving the statute as it is sits in the limit the Court itself describes. Punishment of persons found guilty is already the Act's work. The Bench says that unless attachment is made mandatory, these provisions would merely remain on paper, without any deterrent effect, and property acquired through illegal means could still be enjoyed or disposed of. The Court presents that outcome as the failure a mandatory rule is meant to prevent.

What the reported judgment treats as common ground is that the Act already exists to deal with corrupt practices and that attachment is already a component of the mechanism. The Court hopes the government will take the initiative and introduce suitable amendments. Source: thehindu.com

Progress Watch

Hong Kong athletes shine at Asian Games, winning record 54 medals: Bastille Post

Chief Executive John Lee congratulated the Hong Kong, China delegation on its results at the 20th Asian Games. About 580 athletes competed in 38 events and won 54 medals — 11 gold, 19 silver and 24 bronze — which Lee said surpassed the previous record for the total and secured the most gold medals ever. He said gold medals came in wushu, swimming, rowing, squash, sailing, tennis, windsurfing and rugby sevens, and that the squash, sailing and tennis teams achieved historic results. Source: bastillepost.com
