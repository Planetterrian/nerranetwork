About one acre of Chinatown grass is now a permanent park, keeping a fire-hall plan off the site.

By Mira November 18, 2026 Ep 57 15 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top Stories City & Province The Closer Look Also Today Getting Around What's On Both Sides: A universal tenant-rights bylaw

What You Need to Know: Vancouver council has given permanent park status to about one acre at Gore and Union, land once held for a freeway ramp and later eyed for a temporary fire hall. The same new council used its first motion day to start undoing the last term’s decisions and to back a look at saving 118 Downtown Eastside rooms. Downtown office vacancy also rose to 13.1 per cent after Amazon consolidated into The Post.

Top Stories

Chinatown green space at Gore and Union becomes a permanent park: Daily Hive

On Tuesday, November 17, 2026, Vancouver city council approved permanent park status for about 31,000 square feet, roughly one acre, of city-owned land at 722 Gore Avenue, on the northeast corner of Gore Avenue and Union Street. Until the vote, the triangle was still set aside for road use, kept as right of way for a highway on-ramp in the 1960s and early 1970s freeway plan between downtown and Highway 1. The Dunsmuir and Georgia viaducts were the only completed piece of that plan. The Vancouver Park Board now has authority over the space. Crews have maintained the informal green for decades, and the trees include a giant sequoia. In 2024 the city proposed a temporary fire hall there to help Firehall No. 2 with Downtown Eastside call volume. Residents fought that plan. Staff withdrew it and used 1756 East Hastings Street instead. Fire Hall No. 11 opened there in spring 2025, and there are directions to redevelop 496 Alexander Street as a permanent, expanded Fire Hall 2. Mayor Pete Fry, then a councillor, brought an October 2024 motion on permanent park status for 722 Gore and the strip north along Gore to Keefer Street. Park Board commissioner Tom Digby had a similar motion approved in September 2024. Tuesday’s designation covers only the triangle, not that strip to Keefer. Fry said people in Strathcona, Chinatown and the Downtown Eastside have used the green space for years, and that protecting it means it stays for people who use it. Digby, the board chair, said the designation was a coordinated effort between the Park Board and council. No formal name or park improvements are planned. Under existing policy, a permanent park designation can be removed only if council and the Park Board each pass a resolution with at least 66 per cent approval of all elected members. The site sits just north of the new St. Paul’s Hospital, set to open in February 2027. Source: dailyhive.com

New council starts reversing the last term and backs a look at 118 SRO homes: Vancouver Sun

Tuesday’s meeting was the first chance for the new council, a Green, COPE and OneCity coalition, to introduce motions, and many aimed at decisions of the ABC Vancouver council that ran city hall for the previous four years. Green Mayor Pete Fry wants to rescind ABC’s 2023 request that the B.C. government abolish Vancouver’s elected park board. Re-elected OneCity councillor Lucy Maloney wants to reverse the previous council’s 2026 rollback of low-carbon, sustainable building policies. Re-elected COPE councillor Sean Orr wants to revisit ABC’s 2025 freeze on adding new supportive housing. COPE councillor Chloe Leslie introduced an urgent motion on three single-room occupancy buildings in the Downtown Eastside that she said were at imminent risk of receivership, which could put more than 100 low-income tenants at risk of eviction. She told council the Downtown Eastside Community Land Trust had a purchase agreement for all three, set to expire November 18, and that the next foreclosure court date was Friday, covering 118 deeply affordable homes. Her motion directs staff to report by January 2027 on options to support the land trust, including partnerships with senior governments, to communicate urgently with the trust without binding council before that report, and directs the mayor to take the matter to the federal housing minister. Council approved it unanimously. ABC councillor Mike Klassen, the only member of the 11 not in the Green-COPE-OneCity coalition, thanked Leslie and called it a good catch. Speaking outside the meeting, Leslie said she was motivated by concern that when these buildings change hands, new private landlords might pressure tenants to leave. Source: vancouversun.com

Amazon’s move into The Post lifts downtown office vacancy to a 22-year high: Daily Hive

Amazon’s latest consolidation into another part of The Post left about 250,000 square feet empty in two other downtown buildings in the third quarter of 2026, Daily Hive reported, citing CBRE. About 160,000 square feet became vacant in July at the 24-storey Telus Garden tower at 510 West Georgia Street, about 29 per cent of that building’s office space, now leasable from the landlord. Amazon opened its first major Vancouver corporate office there in 2015. Another 90,000 square feet, about 25 per cent of The Exchange at 475 Howe Street, is available for sublease through 2035. CBRE said that move pushed downtown leased-office vacancy from 12.2 per cent in the second quarter to 13.1 per cent in the third, the highest in the city centre in more than 22 years. Across Metro Vancouver, vacancy rose from 11.7 per cent to 12.2 per cent. Outside downtown it rose from 11.2 per cent to 11.3 per cent. Downtown has now been higher than the suburban market for six straight quarters. The regional drop in occupied office space was about 259,000 square feet. CBRE estimates the regional market would have held steady without Amazon’s move, and vacancy stayed under the national average of 16.7 per cent. CBRE expects those large spaces to take time to fill, and sees a later risk of a shortage of premium downtown space because current rents are too low to cover a new downtown tower. It expects no new city-centre supply before 2032 at the earliest. All 492,000 square feet under construction in Metro Vancouver at the end of September was outside downtown. Those two Amazon spaces may be part of Vancouver’s private-sector bid to host the Defence, Security and Resilience Bank headquarters. Formal city bids are due in early December 2026. Amazon began using nearly 600,000 square feet in The Post’s North Tower in March 2026 and has leased 1.1 million square feet across the complex. Source: dailyhive.com

Surrey Police Service adds Nyx, its first electronic-storage detection dog: Vancouver Sun

The Surrey Police Service now has a two-year-old black Labrador retriever, Nyx, trained to find hidden electronic devices that might hold evidence. Her title is electronic storage detection dog, and police say she is the first at a municipal police force in British Columbia. She will work with the cybercrime team. Sgt. Ali Gailus said in a Tuesday news release that dogs like Nyx are trained to detect chemical compounds found in devices such as cellphones, USB sticks, GPS trackers and memory cards, and help locate concealed devices. Deputy Chief Const. Mike Procyk said electronic devices can hold important evidence but are hard to find when deliberately concealed, and that Nyx gives investigators another way to recover it. Indiana-based Jodan Detection K9 trained Nyx and Sgt. Emily Ovington. The money came from a gang violence action fund in coordination with the provincial government. Police say Nyx has already completed six successful searches, and they say digital crime is rising. Source: vancouversun.com Source: castanet.net Source: ca.news.yahoo.com

City & Province

Mayor Doug McCallum repeats a promise to bring racing back to Fraser Downs: Surrey Now-Leader

At the 11th annual Clovie awards on November 12 at Northview Golf and Country Club, hosted by the Cloverdale District Chamber of Commerce, Surrey Mayor Doug McCallum gave his first public speech since being elected and repeated his promise to rebuild the backstretch barns at Fraser Downs and bring horse racing back to the 5/8 oval in Cloverdale. He said the barns were razed at the end of 2025 under then-mayor Brenda Locke, and that racing was dropped two weeks before the fall meet was to start in September 2025. He said he and the new council directed staff to start on 16 priorities his Safe Surrey Coalition slate committed to, and that the first council meeting approved all 16. He said those include free business licences for the first two years for every new business, freezing property taxes for five years, and free lessons for children 16 and under at city pools. He also pointed to a new swimming pool, a SkyTrain extension to Langley that he said is just about built, and the new hospital in Cloverdale. He said he has had many calls from people who want to open businesses in East Surrey, and that he has heard residents want Cloverdale to keep its small-town character. Source: surreynowleader.com

Snuneymuxw First Nation and Nanaimo Forest Products sign a partnership: Global BC

Snuneymuxw First Nation and Nanaimo Forest Products Limited, which operates Harmac Pacific and has worked in the Nation’s territory for more than 75 years, signed an agreement to strengthen their relationship and create economic opportunities in the Nanaimo area. Mike Crucil of Nanaimo Forest Products said what they signed is much more than an agreement on paper. Nanaimo-Lantzville MLA George Anderson said it showed what partnerships based on respect, trust and a shared commitment to the future can achieve. Premier-designate Lorne Doerkson applauded the collaboration. The Union of B.C. Indian Chiefs, after the election, warned against ripping up the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act and urged using it as a framework. On the campaign trail the B.C. Conservatives said repealing that act was a top priority. Grand Chief Stewart Phillip, president of the Union of British Columbia Indian Chiefs, said First Nations will not stand idly by if the government-elect rips it up, and called that a declaration of war. Source: globalnews.ca

Filipino B.C. asks the auditor general to review the Lapu-Lapu Day response: CBC

Filipino B.C. is calling on the auditor general to examine the response to the Lapu-Lapu Day tragedy, the April 2025 car-ramming at a community festival in which a number of people were killed. The group said it had to shoulder much of the community response, which it said should have been handled by the province. Source: cbc.ca Source: cbc.ca

Labour board orders Highland Valley Copper to stop using replacement workers: CBC

B.C.’s Labour Relations Board has found Teck Resources violated the provincial labour code by using two promoted supervisors, a rehired retiree and an outside contractor to do the work of striking employees at Highland Valley Copper, and ordered the company to stop using replacement workers for that work during the dispute. Source: cbc.ca

The Closer Look

A 138-year-old Queen’s Park house faces a demolition question: Vancouver Sun

A New Westminster woman is asking city council to help keep a Victorian Gothic house at 239 Fourth Street, in the Queen’s Park neighbourhood, built in 1888. Marnie Ford says she fell in love with it on night walks. When a for-sale sign went up, she looked for a way to keep the house. She went to council in October and asked the city to buy and restore it, or to help a group of interested people buy and restore it. She said councillors were supportive, and that she is waiting to see whether the new council is as receptive. Mayor Daniel Fontaine and five councillors from the New Westminster Progressives were elected last month on a platform of financial restraint. Only one councillor not affiliated with the Progressives was elected.

The corner lot was listed in August for $1.425 million and can be bought now for $1.25 million. Samuel Boisvert, a director with heritage consultant Luxton and Associates, said the firm was hired to produce a heritage values assessment. The house is owned by the estate of William Shordee, who lived there from 1970 until he died in 2025. Boisvert said the city typically asks for these assessments when the owner of a property older than 100 years wants to develop the site. The house is on the city’s 1986 heritage inventory and is a protected property under the Queen’s Park heritage conservation area, adopted in 2017. It was built in 1888 and got a water hookup in 1893. Boisvert tied that era to an economic boom after the Canadian Pacific Railway arrived in 1886 and built a branch line to New Westminster from Westminster Junction, now Port Coquitlam. Between 1888 and 1892 the city’s population more than doubled, to 7,500. The original lot owner was Methodist minister Edward White, who came to New Westminster in 1859. The lot was once much larger and was subdivided over the years. Boisvert called the house a rare Victorian Gothic example drawing on English and medieval styles.

Rupinder Basi, the city’s deputy director of planning and development, said the owner has asked the city about options related to demolition, and that the city has not received further information about development of the property. Council’s community heritage commission can advise on identifying and protecting heritage buildings. Its role is advisory. Ford’s ask is still a request: a city purchase, or a private group purchase and restoration. The new council has not said which of those, if either, it will take up. Source: vancouversun.com

TransLink retires the last Orion V suburban express buses: Daily Hive

TransLink retired the last of its Orion V buses at the end of October, 25 years after the model entered service, and said double-decker buses have primarily replaced them on longer highway-style routes linking downtown, Richmond, Langley, Coquitlam, South Surrey and White Rock. Source: dailyhive.com

Researchers sequence humpback genomes from drone-collected blow off the B.C. coast: Global BC

Eadin O’Mahony’s team, in a study published this week in Molecular Ecology Resources, collected 58 blow samples from 26 humpbacks in 2022 and 2023 by flying a drone through the exhaled spray, and reported that about 84 per cent of the DNA was the whale’s, with little evidence of recent inbreeding and a population decline of about 20 per cent between 2012 and 2021. Source: globalnews.ca

A Duchenne muscular dystrophy drug will no longer be sold in Canada: Vancouver Sun

A drug used for Duchenne muscular dystrophy will no longer be sold in Canada, and the Vancouver Sun reports that an alternative is far more expensive and not yet funded by the province, leaving some B.C. families anxious about treatment when supplies run out. Source: vancouversun.com

Police name the man found in a burning vehicle in Langley: CBC

Police have identified the victim as 25-year-old Tushar Sharma of Surrey, who they say did not have a criminal record and had limited known contacts in Canada. Source: cbc.ca

Two CN workers hospitalized after a derailment near Terrace: CBC

Two CN workers were hospitalized after a train derailed into the Skeena River near Terrace; police say the injuries are not life-threatening and cleanup is underway. Source: cbc.ca Source: surreynowleader.com

Surrey police call a Lord Tweedsmuir threat unfounded and suspected swatting: Vancouver Sun

Lord Tweedsmuir Secondary, at 61A Avenue and 180th Street in Cloverdale, was evacuated Tuesday after a threat at about 12:45 p.m.; by 4 p.m. Surrey police, through Sgt. Ali Gailius, said the threat was unfounded and is being investigated as suspected swatting, with similar reports in the United States the same day. Source: vancouversun.com

Valemount mayor pleads not guilty to a sexual assault charge: CBC

Mayor Owen Torgerson, charged last month with one count of sexual assault in relation to an offence alleged at or near Valemount on April 2, pleaded not guilty on Tuesday. Source: cbc.ca

Three youths arrested after an alleged assault in Chilliwack: Global BC

Chilliwack RCMP say three youths were arrested after an alleged November 8 assault on a young woman with disabilities that was later filmed and shared online; the investigation is active, the youths cannot be identified under the Youth Criminal Justice Act, and Crown will decide on charges. Source: globalnews.ca

Both Sides: A universal tenant-rights bylaw

COPE councillor Sean Orr wants staff to look at a universal tenant-rights bylaw and at criminalizing predatory practices by landlords. The proposal is already drawing a public fight, and council has not voted on it.

COPE, which focused much of this year’s campaign on lower-income people, renters and working people, is using this week’s motions to pursue that platform. Orr’s motion is the piece aimed at landlord conduct: a bylaw covering tenant rights across the city, and a look at treating predatory practices as crimes. The party’s stated reason is the one it campaigned on, improving daily conditions for renters and working people rather than leaving those practices to existing rules alone.

The Goodman Report, a local commercial real estate publication, answered on Monday. It called Orr’s proposal a damaging, ideological policy assault on the city’s landlords and investors, and a governmental sledgehammer that could crush housing investment. That is the case against, as that publication framed it: the cost would fall on people who build and finance rental housing, and the city would scare off investment it needs.

The motions are being introduced this week. The article does not report a vote on Orr’s tenant-rights motion. Source: vancouversun.com
