Sixty-four votes flip Burnaby South-Metrotown to the Conservatives, lifting their majority to sixty-four seats.

By Mira November 10, 2026 Ep 49 10 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top Stories City & Province The Closer Look Also Today Getting Around Sports

What You Need to Know: Sixty-four votes have flipped Burnaby South-Metrotown from NDP incumbent Paul Choi to Conservative David Xie, taking the incoming Conservative majority to 64 of 93 seats. A 78-year-old woman has died after being struck at Dunbar Street and West 34th Avenue, Vancouver’s 13th fatal traffic collision this year, and police say no charges have been laid. In Chilliwack, a 55-year-old man has been charged with second-degree murder in the death of a 52-year-old woman.

Top Stories

Recount flips squeaker election night win for NDP in Burnaby South-Metrotown to Conservatives: Vancouver Sun

Elections B.C. completed the final count Sunday and declared Conservative David Xie the winner in Burnaby South-Metrotown, 64 votes ahead of NDP incumbent Paul Choi, who had won the seat in 2024. Choi’s election-night lead was 66 ballots, a 0.5 per cent margin; the final margin was 0.43 per cent, just enough to avoid an automatic judicial review, an Elections B.C. spokeswoman said. The Vancouver Sun reported the flip takes the incoming majority to 64 of 93 seats, and that under premier-designate Lorne Doerkson the Conservatives will control almost 70 per cent of the next legislature. Two other races kept their election-night winners but go to judicial review because each margin was under 0.2 per cent of the riding’s eligible votes. In Ladysmith-Oceanside, NDP Stephanie Higginson’s lead grew from 13 votes on election night to 50 in the final count. In Kelowna Centre, a partial recount of one polling station narrowed Conservative Kristina Loewen’s lead over NDP Loyal Wooldridge from 137 votes to 43. The final count added mail-in ballots, absentee ballots and telephone-assisted votes. Xie did not immediately return a request for comment. Source: vancouversun.com Source: vancouversun.com Source: cbc.ca

Orange warnings raised over next B.C. deluge, as second death linked to wild weather: North Shore News

Power is back for most B.C. Hydro customers ahead of the next round, the Vancouver Sun reported, with another strong series of storms expected from Tuesday through Thursday. North Shore News reported forecasters are escalating rainfall warnings in southwest British Columbia, and that some areas could receive more than 250 millimetres. That report also linked a second death to the wild weather. CBC reported the third storm in the series is heading for the coast after a weekend that knocked out power to thousands, triggered evacuations and left one person dead. In Prince George, CBC reported Friday night’s windstorm is still being cleaned up, and B.C. Hydro said power there may not be fully restored until Tuesday. Source: nsnews.com Source: vancouversun.com Source: cbc.ca Source: cbc.ca

78-year-old pedestrian dies after being struck during heavy rainstorm: Vancouver Police Department

A 78-year-old woman died after a Lexus sedan struck her while she crossed Dunbar Street in an unmarked crosswalk at West 34th Avenue. Vancouver police said the collision was about 11 a.m. on November 6, witnesses described heavy rain, the driver remained and cooperated, and the woman died the next day at Vancouver General Hospital. Sergeant Adam Donaldson said the investigation is ongoing and no charges have been laid. Police said it was Vancouver’s 13th fatal traffic collision of the year. The department has launched Project Vigilance for the longer hours of darkness, with patrols at high-risk intersections and school zones through the winter solstice on December 21. Donaldson said the clocks are not turning back, so people need to be more aware of the darkness, and that officers will target speeding, distracted driving, impaired driving, jaywalking and lack of helmets. He also said a reflector clipped to a bag or clothing will help drivers see people dressed in black. Source: vpd.ca Source: dailyhive.com Source: dailyhive.com

Chilliwack man, 55, charged with 2nd-degree murder of woman: Surrey Now-Leader

Kristopher John Hayward, 55, has been charged with second-degree murder after a 52-year-old woman was found dead Friday, November 6. Chilliwack RCMP said they were called at about 8:09 p.m. to a residence in the 46000 block of Mellard Avenue and found a woman who was pronounced dead at the scene. The Integrated Homicide Investigation Team took over, working with Chilliwack RCMP, the Integrated Forensic Identification Service and the BC Coroners Service. The BC Prosecution Service approved the charge the next day, and IHIT announced it Monday. Police did not identify the woman. Investigators believe Hayward and the woman knew each other and that the incident was not random. Sergeant Freda Fong of IHIT said charges met the approval standard within 24 hours of the team deploying, and that work continues with the family and the prosecution. Hayward’s next court appearance is Tuesday, November 10. Source: surreynowleader.com

City & Province

Vandalism prompts all-day closure of Langley elementary school: Vancouver Sun

Langley police are investigating vandalism discovered inside Noel Booth Elementary in the Brookswood community of Langley Township, and the school was closed to students for the full day Monday. The Langley school district first posted a 10 a.m. late start “due to vandalism and necessary cleanup,” then updated the post around 9 a.m. to say the school would be closed all day and asked families to pick up students. Spokesperson Joanne Abshire confirmed the closure, said the vandalism was found inside the school, and said Langley RCMP are investigating. She said the district would share an update with families later Monday. Source: vancouversun.com

Man charged after loaded handgun found during traffic stop: CityNews Vancouver

An 18-year-old Surrey resident, Paramveer Singh Deol, has been charged after Surrey police said they stopped a white Porsche whose driver was accelerating and went through a red light. With Project Assurance members, officers stopped the vehicle, identified and arrested the driver, and found a loaded firearm in a search after the arrest. CityNews reported the charges include failing to stop for police, occupying a vehicle with a firearm inside, possession of a loaded firearm without a licence, and careless transport of a firearm, a 9mm pistol. Deol remains in custody awaiting a bail hearing, the Surrey Police Service said. Source: vancouver.citynews.ca

Kelowna MP Stephen Fuhr speaks on federal budget to end homelessness: Surrey Now-Leader

The federal government tabled its 2027 budget on November 5, with almost $30 billion aimed at eliminating homelessness within 10 years and $15 billion to triple annual non-market housing starts to 24,000. Kelowna MP Stephen Fuhr, Secretary of State for Defence Procurement, spoke at Pleasantvale II, a mix of market and non-market units housing people evacuated from UBC Okanagan’s Hadgraft Wilson building. He said the building came together with federal, provincial and municipal support and the Society of Hope, and that communities can apply for the new funding. Fuhr said Kelowna “always meets the terms and conditions, gets projects out of the ground and they do it quickly,” and he called the $45 billion budgeted to address homelessness “aggressive, but it’s necessary.” He said he is looking forward to meeting premier-elect Lorne Doerkson. Black Press has asked Kelowna Mayor Tom Dyas whether the city plans to use the federal funding. Source: surreynowleader.com

Richmond autism network gets funding for independence programs: Richmond News

Richmond’s autism network has funding for independence programs, and Pacific Blue Cross members can vote daily until November 24 to direct an extra $50,000. Source: richmond-news.com

The Closer Look

Vancouver Whitecaps aren't the only Canadian MLS team in danger of relocating to United States: Daily Hive Vancouver

The Vancouver Whitecaps are for sale and at risk of a move to the United States, and Daily Hive Vancouver reports they are no longer the only Canadian Major League Soccer club in that position. On Monday, CF Montreal owner Joey Saputo announced that club is up for sale as well. He told reporters, “If the club doesn’t find a local buyer and I get an offer where the club needs to be relocated, then I have no choice. That is the decision I will take.” He also said, “I will not discount the club to the point of just giving it away,” that he loves the club and would love it to stay, and that selling to someone who cannot keep it there would not be doing anyone a favour.

The Whitecaps have been for sale for nearly two years and have yet to find a buyer who wants to keep the franchise in Vancouver. That uncertainty has run through the two most successful seasons the club has had in MLS. The Whitecaps reached the MLS Cup Final last year. This season they set a franchise record of 64 points, with a Western Conference-leading record of 19 wins, 8 losses and 7 draws.

Montreal’s season went the other way. The club finished with an MLS-worst record of 6 wins, 20 losses and 8 draws. Daily Hive described a stadium problem that does not match Vancouver’s: MLS is moving to a winter schedule, and Montreal’s outdoor stadium is not suitable for more matches on that calendar. The Whitecaps play at BC Place, which has a retractable roof, so winter weather is not the same obstacle. The problem in Vancouver, the outlet reported, appears to be the current business model.

Of Canada’s three MLS clubs, Toronto FC appears safe for now. The same report set the two sales against earlier departures from Canadian cities: the Quebec Nordiques moved to Colorado in 1995, the Winnipeg Jets left for Phoenix in 1996, the Vancouver Grizzlies left for Memphis in 2001, and the Montreal Expos relocated to Washington, D.C., in 2004. For people who follow the Whitecaps, the new dated fact is Saputo’s Monday statement that a Canadian owner will accept a move if that is what a sale requires, while Vancouver’s own sale, nearly two years on, still has no buyer committed to keeping the team here. Source: dailyhive.com
