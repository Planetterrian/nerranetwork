A one-thousand-dollar B.C. tax cut now waits for a spring twenty twenty-seven bill.

What You Need to Know: Premier Lorne Doerkson says the promised one-thousand-dollar B.C. income tax cut will reach the legislature in spring 2027, and he hopes, without promising the date, that it will be dated to January 1. Top Stories

Premier Lorne Doerkson signals $1,000 B.C. income tax cut in Spring 2027: Daily Hive Vancouver

Premier Lorne Doerkson says his newly sworn-in Conservative government will bring its promised $1,000 annual provincial income tax cut to the legislature in the spring 2027 session. He told reporters today, just after he and his cabinet were sworn in at Government House, "We're committed to doing that, and it will happen in certainly the spring session. My hope, don't hold me to this, my hope is that that will be dated back to Jan. 1." A fall sitting was ruled out this week. The Conservative platform would raise the basic personal amount from $13,216 to $32,000. The party estimated about $1,000 a year for someone who would otherwise pay at least that much in B.C. income tax, or about $2,000 for a couple if both qualify, and a smaller saving or none for someone who already pays little or no provincial income tax. The platform put the cost at $725 million before March 31, 2027, and $2.9 billion for each full year after. The former BC NDP September forecast put the 2026/2027 deficit at $13.8 billion; the Conservatives' own plan still projected $13.7 billion that year. Finance Minister Peter Milobar, sworn in today, accused former premier David Eby during the October 2026 campaign of breaking a grocery-rebate promise the NDP administration shelved in February 2025. Doerkson also named Carson Binda, formerly the B.C. director of the Canadian Taxpayers Federation, Minister of State for Red Tape Reduction, and first-time Coquitlam-Burke Mountain MLA Ryan Peterson as health minister, citing a ministry the previous government budgeted at $40.6 billion for 2026/2027. Source: dailyhive.com

Police identify victims of fatal double shooting at Surrey Walmart parking lot: Philippine Canadian Inquirer

The Integrated Homicide Investigation Team has identified the two people killed in a shooting outside a Walmart in Surrey as 23-year-old Gaganpreet "Garry" Rehal and 24-year-old Sukhjeet Kaur, both of Delta. Surrey Police Service officers and paramedics answered reports of gunfire in a parking lot near 88 Avenue and 124 Street at about 10:38 p.m. on November 15. Both were found with gunshot wounds and later pronounced dead in hospital. Police later found a burned four-door sedan in the 9700 block of Princess Drive that investigators believe is connected, and they are trying to identify occupants who fled. IHIT said Rehal had previous police interactions involving weapons and firearms-related offences, that Kaur had limited known contacts with authorities in Canada, and that investigators believe the shooting was targeted and showed indicators of organized crime. Spokesperson Sgt. Freda Fong said investigators are working with the families on the victims' movements before the shooting. Police want witnesses, surveillance and dashcam from the shooting scene and the vehicle fire between 10 p.m. and 11 p.m. on November 15, at 1-877-551-4448. Source: canadianinquirer.net Source: telegraphindia.com

Canada's first climate lawsuit to go to trial has wrapped up hearings: CBC British Columbia

Canada's first climate lawsuit to go to trial wrapped up witness testimony Friday in Federal Court in Vancouver. The case asks what level of responsibility the federal government bears for climate disasters Canadians are suffering. CBC says the outcome could have a large influence on a growing number of other climate lawsuits in Canada. Source: cbc.ca

City & Province

Premier Lorne Doerkson cites Newton SkyTrain extension 'commitment' in naming Surrey MLA to TransLink role: Daily Hive Vancouver

Premier Lorne Doerkson said today the parliamentary secretary for TransLink exists because the government "committed to a new line in Surrey-Newton," and that work is going on there now. He named Surrey-Newton MLA Harj Dhaliwal, who served about six years on TransLink's board, and said Transportation and Infrastructure Minister Trevor Halford will handle BC Ferries himself. The commitment he cited is an Expo Line extension along King George Boulevard from Surrey City Centre to Newton Town Centre. Source: dailyhive.com

Crucifix erected on White Rock breakwater ‘not appropriate’: Surrey Now-Leader

White Rock City Hall has been flooded with complaints after an unsanctioned crucifix was put on the breakwater by the pier. A visitor who asked not to be named told Surrey Now-Leader she and her husband saw it on November 19, estimated it at about four feet, and called it engineered rather than a couple of boards; it bears the inscription John 14:6. She said staff told her many complaints were already in and that a marine contractor would remove it sometime in December, and she is annoyed taxpayer money has to pay for that. The City of White Rock said the Parks Bylaw does not allow structures or other items on the breakwater, and that its marine contractor will remove the items in the next few weeks as scheduling permits. Source: surreynowleader.com

The Closer Look

Adopt-A-School: 'Halting Hunger' a priority for students facing challenges: Vancouver Sun

Learning Pathways, an alternative program for 18 students in Burnaby, is asking The Vancouver Sun's Adopt-A-School program for a $6,500 grant for breakfast, lunch and snacks. Youth engagement worker Adèle Cooshek added a line that matches the request's name, Halting Hunger: "Without food, our classes fall apart." The meals would be at the Canada Way Learning Centre, 5310 Woodsworth Street.

Cooshek said a number of the students have learning issues or mental health challenges, and some are dealing with trauma, family instability, financial hardship, and a lack of guaranteed access to food. She said Halting Hunger addresses food insecurity among vulnerable youth in the alternative secondary program. The students do not function well in a regular school, she said. Some will transition back. They can also complete a Dogwood diploma, the Grade 12 graduation certificate, through the program. That takes regular attendance, and she said being able to feed students is key to them showing up.

Adopt-A-School first helped the program last year. Cooshek called it magnificent and said attendance improved dramatically. She said a number of the kids come to school hungry, without breakfast, and then go through a great deal of food. She wants them to eat so they can self-regulate and focus on academics and the other things the program offers.

Other organizations are already involved. The Take A Hike Foundation provides a once-a-week trip outdoors. The Cinderella Project helps disadvantaged youth prepare for graduation, supplying attire, hair care, makeup, portraits and mentorship. Cooshek said one student graduating this year was enrolled in that project. That student's goal is to help people facing homelessness. She wants post-secondary courses after graduation so she can do that work, though she might not be able to afford them right away. Cooshek said the student has already taken in a classmate who does not have a home to go to.

Adopt-A-School has sent more than $17.5 million to hundreds of schools in B.C. since 2011 to feed and clothe children, setting up breakfast and lunch programs at schools' request. This year more than 300 schools are seeking $2.6 million, the largest number of requests ever, for thousands of children lacking food, clothing and other necessities. It is a registered charity administered by The Vancouver Sun Children's Fund board. No administration fees are taken from donations. The paper says 100 percent of the money is directed to schools. Donations can be made at vansunkidsfund.ca or by credit card at 236-998-7570. Source: vancouversun.com
