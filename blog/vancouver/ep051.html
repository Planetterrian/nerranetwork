What You Need to Know: Pemberton's evacuation order shut Airport Road, the airport and the treatment plant, as south-coast rain pushed more communities to move people or cut water use. Surrey's only opposition councillor used the first regular meeting to challenge parts of the mayor's priority list even as those plans advance. Homicide investigators are at a home on East 52nd Avenue after two people were found dead.

Top Stories

Impacts from B.C. drenching mount as water levels rise: Global BC

The Village of Pemberton has issued an evacuation order for properties on Airport Road, including the airport, the wastewater treatment facility, the wildland fire management base and a public golf course, because of flooding and high-risk river levels. The Lil'wat Nation has issued an evacuation order and an evacuation alert from Kwetsa7 along the Lillooet River corridor to Lillooet Lake. On northeast Vancouver Island, the Strathcona Regional District issued a water conservation notice for the Village of Sayward after high flows in Newcastle Creek put extra sediment in the water. An evacuation order for flooding and landslide risk remains in place for about 30 properties in Zeballos, on northwest Vancouver Island. Some areas had a lull on Wednesday, but orange rainfall warnings remain for west and inland Vancouver Island, Howe Sound and the Sea to Sky, with downpours expected today and a risk of significant damage and disruption. In Paradise Valley, a BC Hydro release at the Cheakamus Dam triggered a flood alert, and residents were told to shelter in place. Source: globalnews.ca Source: nsnews.com

Surrey's only opposition councillor kicks off term challenging mayor's 16 priority projects: Vancouver Sun

Coun. Harry Bains, the only councillor not on Mayor Doug McCallum's Safe Surrey Coalition, used Monday's first regular meeting to mark where he stands on 16 priorities the mayor wants implemented. Bains, elected to a second term last month after serving on former mayor Brenda Locke's slate, said he backed items he agreed with and offered amendments on others. Safe Surrey said on social media Tuesday that Bains is against a new hospital, that four years on council delivered no hospital, and that he is playing politics for Locke. Bains said he supports a hospital but wants a women's and children's hospital on a larger site than the three hectares the mayor has offered the province, and he called the slate's portrayal dishonest. He voted against five motions, including a helicopter and drone fleet for the Surrey Police Service unless the police board requests them, and he did not support a four-year property-tax freeze unless McCallum can guarantee it will not cut services, sell city land, cancel budgeted projects, or raise other fees and taxes. Every McCallum plan is still moving ahead, including a call for SkyTrain to Newton, withdrawal from Metro Vancouver, and free licences for two years for every new business. Bains said he worries the money has to come from somewhere, including the 10,000-seat sports and entertainment arena planned for City Centre; McCallum told Postmedia during the campaign he would cancel the arena and has not spoken about it since being elected, and he has not been made available for interviews since election night. Source: vancouversun.com

Homicide detectives called to Vancouver home after 2 people found dead: Global BC

Homicide detectives were called to a home in the 1000 block of East 52nd Avenue after officers were first called there around 12:45 a.m. The bodies of a man, 27, and a woman, 35, were found in the home. Source: globalnews.ca Source: vancouversun.com

City & Province

Risk of landslides in Cariboo following Pear Lake wildfire: report: CBC British Columbia

A preliminary report from B.C.'s Ministry of Forests says there is a high risk of landslides, floods and debris flow in parts of the Cariboo after a massive wildfire earlier this year. The assessment covers the area affected by the Pear Lake wildfire. The warning is aimed at people living in those parts of the region. Source: cbc.ca

New Coquitlam mayor cites affordability, safety as top issues at inauguration: Tri-Cities Dispatch

New Coquitlam mayor Hodge used the inauguration to name affordability and safety as the top issues. Tri-Cities Dispatch reported the remarks as he took office. Source: tricitiesdispatch.com

The Closer Look

VIDEO: Langley City service especially meaningful – emcee: Surrey Now-Leader

More than a thousand people filled Douglas Park in Langley City on Wednesday for the Remembrance Day ceremony at the cenotaph. Almost two hours earlier, Cliff Talerico had sat alone on a bench, watching the birds and wondering whether days of torrential rain would keep the crowd small. The rain had stopped. The sky stayed overcast and grey. At first only a few dozen people, mostly cadets and RCMP officers, stood near the bandshell. The park then filled.

Talerico, 84, a Langley City resident, told the Langley Advance Times he enlisted at 17 with his parents' support, mostly because he wanted to get out of Golden. After training he spent three years with the infantry in a small German village, two years in Victoria, and six months in Cypress and the Middle East, then joined the 3rd Field Engineer Squadron based in the eastern Fraser Valley. He served 12.5 years and left in 1972, when his brother Jack talked him into building houses in Chilliwack. He is not a Legion member and does not know many other local veterans. Each year he polishes his shoes, puts on dress pants, a blue blazer with service medals and a green beret with an engineers pin, and walks a few blocks to the City cenotaph. He said he was fortunate not to have seen battle or lost close friends. He was escorted to a seat in front of the cenotaph with a few dozen other veterans.

Sandy Dunkley, a long-time Langley resident, had worried attendance would be low. By the end of the service she said her heart was full, glad that so many still took the time. Emcee Chris Parsons led two minutes of silence. The service included The Last Post, Lament and Rouse, O Canada, God Save the King, In Flanders Fields, prayers and scripture. Parsons spoke about his family's recent trip to Normandy and about Jim McGregor, who led the Douglas Park ceremonies for years before his death earlier this year. Parsons said the day was especially meaningful because those were huge shoes to fill.

He asked the crowd to honour Langley's Pte. Hayden Lykkemark, who answered the call to serve and, with three other members of the Canadian Forces, died last month in an incident at CFB Wainwright. Among 41 wreaths laid at the City cenotaph, two were placed beside flowers and pictures of Lykkemark that have been there since his death. Parsons said Lykkemark will be laid to rest locally next week, and he asked that the service of those now serving, those who served, and those who died not be forgotten. In North Vancouver, the family of Pte. Aidan Goulding, killed in a fire at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright last month, laid a wreath in his honour. Source: surreynowleader.com Source: nsnews.com

Canucks Place Ilya Safonov on Waivers for Contract Termination: BVM Sports

Vancouver placed Ilya Safonov on unconditional waivers to terminate his contract after the forward left the Abbotsford AHL affiliate and returned to KHL club Ak Bars Kazan. Safonov said his inability to earn an NHL roster spot factored into the decision, and a larger role at centre awaits in Kazan. He had signed a one-year, $950,000 contract, recorded 4 points in 12 games with Abbotsford, and posted a career-high 37 points in 64 games for Kazan in 2022-23. The return ends a North American stint of five months after six years in Kazan's system. Source: bvmsports.com Source: prohockeyrumors.com

Rangers visit the Canucks after Zibanejad's 2-goal game: ABC News

Mika Zibanejad scored two goals in New York's 3-1 win over Calgary. The Rangers won the earlier meeting 6-2, with Will Cuylle scoring twice. Both Sides: Municipal density rules

Premier-designate Lorne Doerkson has to decide what the new government does with Bills 44 and 47, the two cornerstones of a cabinet-approved 28-point housing plan released in April 2023. Bill 44 gives owners in municipalities over 5,000 the right to build three to six units without a rezoning hearing if they comply with the official community plan. Bill 47 allows much higher density in 104 transit-oriented areas in 31 municipalities.

Gord Enemark, who led the cross-ministry team that built that plan while executive director of the housing policy branch, writes in the Vancouver Sun that the case for staying the course is supply. He writes that staff believed the province could not, alone, pay for tens of thousands of non-market homes and had to use the private sector. Economist Mike Moffatt of the University of Western Ontario, quoted in the December 5, 2023 Globe and Mail, said B.C. was "far ahead of anyone else when it comes to ambition and policy seriousness" and that the whole package, not one silver bullet, was what impressed him. UBC housing economist Thomas Davidoff co-authored a study that concluded the policy should generate over 200,000 new units over 10 years, after researchers looked at similar New Zealand measures from 2016. Enemark's own reason is price and form: a duplex, fourplex or townhouse will always cost less than a single-family house in the same neighbourhood, and most families need ground-oriented housing rather than a condo ten storeys up. He adds that federal housing-accelerator grants could be at risk, because communities over 10,000 must commit to at least four units per property to qualify, and over $500 million has been approved for B.C., most of it for 19 larger municipalities.

The case for slowing down is that the new government should not treat those bills as settled. The Victoria Residential Homebuilders Association told Doerkson he needs to pump the brakes, while saying Bill 44 has increased missing-middle homes 47 percent in Greater Victoria, homes it calls critical for young families. The B.C. Real Estate Association recommends the new government carefully consider its actions on Bills 44 and 47. Business in Vancouver reports that real estate industry groups want housing policies revisited, including tax changes and lower construction costs, to revive development. Enemark names the tradeoff himself: local input through long public rezoning hearings, generally dominated by people who already own housing, is given up so others have more options than they have now. No dated next step is set. Source: vancouversun.com Source: vancouversun.com Source: biv.com
