One point separates Fry and Sim for Vancouver mayor, inside the poll's margin.

Top Stories

One point splits Fry and Sim in a CityNews mayor poll: CityNews Vancouver:

With Vancouver's municipal election just over a week away, a Canada Pulse Insights poll done exclusively for CityNews and OMNI puts Green Party candidate Pete Fry at 28 per cent and incumbent Ken Sim at 27 per cent among decided voters, inside a margin of error of plus or minus 4.4 percentage points. Vancouver Liberal candidate Kareem Allam is at 19 per cent. Among decided voters who say they will definitely vote or have already cast a ballot, Fry is at 29 per cent and Sim at 28. Among decided voters, TEAM for a Livable Vancouver's Colleen Hardwick is at 13 per cent, Vote Vancouver's Rebecca Bligh at seven, and Bright Future Vancouver's Muhammad Ahmad at three. John Wright, CEO of Canada Pulse Insights, calls the field very fragmented, with two front-runners and two streams: he says Fry's supporters emphasize the environment, ethics, accountability, affordability and housing, while Sim's emphasize affordability first, then crime, municipal taxes and the bottom line. Ten per cent of all respondents were still undecided after people who first said they were unsure were asked if they were leaning. Wright says a typical campaign has about 18 to 20 per cent undecided, with 10 per cent who will not show up, and that the remaining share is often weighing personalities. Source: vancouver.citynews.ca

Dropped Conservative candidate Joachim Agou apologizes, will run as an independent: Global BC:

Victoria-Beacon Hill candidate Joachim Agou, removed from the B.C. Conservatives over online posts the NDP called racist, says he is truly sorry, takes responsibility for his words, and rejects any accusation of racism. In one post he said a car that drove through a pro-Palestine protest "should have reversed" over demonstrators; in another he shared a meme that used an expletive to describe Pakistan. Interim Conservative leader Lorne Doerkson said the posts are inexcusable and that, if Agou is elected October 24, he will not be part of the Conservative caucus. Agou says he should have expressed himself "more clearly and calmly," and that such demonstrations "should not bring a city to a standstill or put lives at risk." He says he understands why the party withdrew support and will run as an independent in a riding considered an NDP stronghold. CBC reports the Conservatives dropped him Thursday and that he still plans to run. Source: globalnews.ca · cbc.ca

Former attorney general Colin Gablemann dies at 82: CBC British Columbia:

Colin Gablemann, a former British Columbia attorney general who played a key role in the province's freedom-of-information laws and in recognizing Indigenous rights, has died at 82. CBC describes him as transformative. Source: cbc.ca

Squamish woman pleads guilty to terrorism charge: Vancouver Sun:

Kimberly Polman, a Squamish woman who wed an ISIS fighter, pleaded guilty to participating in ISIS about a month before her trial was expected to start in B.C. Supreme Court, after days of pretrial hearings that included Charter challenges. Source: vancouversun.com

City & Province

NDP promises a three-year freeze on ICBC rates if elected: Global BC:

The B.C. NDP is promising to freeze car insurance rates for three years if elected. NDP Leader David Eby and interim B.C. Conservative leader Lorne Doerkson traded barbs about the future of the no-fault insurance model, which the B.C. NDP introduced in 2021. The pledge is a campaign promise, not a rate decision in force. Source: globalnews.ca

Sooke infrastructure sits at the centre of its municipal election: CBC British Columbia:

In the District of Sooke on Vancouver Island, population has grown steadily since the early 2000s, and local infrastructure has struggled to keep pace because of environmental regulations and a growing strain on limited resources. CBC reports that infrastructure is set to play a key role in the municipal election there. Source: cbc.ca

Getting Around

DriveBC lists night paving on Highway 1A in Surrey from October 16 to 19, 9 p.m. on the 16th to 5 a.m. on the 19th: a northbound lane closed, the southbound exit to King George Boulevard closed, and a detour via the Highway 17 westbound off-ramp, Tannery Road, 120 Street and King George Boulevard. On Highway 99 in Vancouver, paving from October 14 to 16 runs 11 p.m. to 5 a.m. Wednesday and Thursday, with single-lane alternating traffic northbound and southbound open only to buses and emergency vehicles. In Delta, the Highway 99 Exit 23 ramp to Ladner Trunk Road stays closed through October 23, and night work from Ladner Trunk Road to Highway 17 runs to October 18, 9 p.m. to 5 a.m. Monday to Saturday, with one lane each way. Environment Canada has no watches or warnings for Vancouver: Friday showers end this morning, then sun and cloud, northwest wind 30 km/h gusting to 50, and to 70 near the Strait of Georgia, high 15, UV index 3. Friday night is partly cloudy with a 60 per cent chance of showers and a low of 9; Saturday is cloudy with a 60 per cent chance of early showers, clearing near noon, high 16; Sunday is sunny, high 16. Source: drivebc.ca · weather.gc.ca

Both Sides: Tax promises against a $13.8-billion deficit

British Columbians are being asked, in the first half of the provincial campaign, to choose among promises to hold, hike or cut taxes. Global News reports that whoever becomes premier will face a projected deficit of about $13.8 billion. These are campaign promises, not measures now in law. The vote is October 24.

NDP Leader David Eby's case for changing the tax mix is that services such as health care have to be paid for. He is proposing to suspend gasoline taxes, add a bracket for people making more than $1 million, and increase taxes on the two highest existing income brackets, which start at about $190,000. Eby has said those changes would generate $1 billion.

The Conservative case for cutting taxes is that the economy needs a jolt. Interim leader Lorne Doerkson is promising no new taxes, elimination of the PST on some items, and a $1,000 cut in provincial taxes for nearly every working British Columbian. He argues the cuts are needed to "supercharge" an economy that lost 26,000 jobs in August. The Conservatives say increasing taxes will drive away doctors.

Green Leader Emily Lowan's case is that extreme wealth should fund public services through a one-time "fair share tax": five per cent on net wealth above $50 million, 10 per cent above $100 million, and 20 per cent above $1 billion. The party estimates that would raise about $22.2 billion. Lowan has criticized Eby for co-opting "the language of the left" after, she said, he refused her extreme-wealth-tax proposal while he was in power.

The shared fiscal frame in the reporting is that projected deficit of about $13.8 billion. No party in the article has turned a promise into a passed budget. Source: globalnews.ca
