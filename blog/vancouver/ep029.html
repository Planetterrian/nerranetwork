Monthly B.C. food-bank visits topped 250,000, doubling since 2019 for families choosing rent or food.

By Mira October 21, 2026 Ep 29 9 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode

Top Stories City & Province The Closer Look Also Today Getting Around Sports Both Sides: Finishing Kelowna General's unused sixth floor

What You Need to Know: Monthly food-bank visits in B.C. topped 250,000 in 2026, about double the 2019 level, as Food Banks Canada says households are choosing between food and rent. Visits rose 14 per cent from 2025, and nearly 76,000 of this year's visits were from children. The same HungerCount says 18 per cent of clients are employed.

Top Stories

B.C. food bank use has increased by a shocking amount since 2019: Daily Hive Vancouver

Food Banks Canada's 2026 HungerCount puts monthly visits to B.C. food banks at just over 250,000 in 2026, up 100 per cent since 2019 and 14 per cent from 2025. Nearly 76,000 of those visits were from children. Canada-wide, use is up 117 per cent since 2019 and 8.7 per cent since 2025. Chief executive Kirstin Beardsley said a small setback used to be absorbable and "those days are over," with people choosing between food and rent. The organization says 18 per cent of clients are employed, that people with the lowest incomes spend about 60 per cent of disposable income on housing, and that since 2021 food prices are up 30 per cent, housing 28 per cent and transportation nearly 24 per cent. It wants governments to modernize employment insurance, double the Canada Workers Benefit and raise that benefit's income line to at least the poverty line, ease Disability Tax Credit barriers, fund deeply affordable housing, and speed homebuilding with federal land, financing and tax measures. Source: dailyhive.com

B.C. Election: Big jump in voters wanting change is the 'kiss of death' for NDP leader David Eby, says pollster: Vancouver Sun

Innovative Research Group's survey of 1,153 people, taken October 15 to 19, found 43 per cent of decided voters backing the B.C. Conservatives, 38 per cent the B.C. NDP and 12 per cent the B.C. Greens, ahead of Saturday's provincial election. President Greg Lyle said the share who think it is time for a change rose eight points, to 65 per cent, from when David Eby called the election just under a month ago, and he compared that 65 per cent with Christy Clark's and John Horgan's worst days in office. Lyle said Conservatives are most favoured among Punjabi and Chinese speakers, which he said could affect Surrey-Guildford, Surrey-Fleetwood, Surrey City Centre, Surrey Panorama and Richmond-Steveston. He said a quarter of respondents who voted NDP in 2024 are unsure they will do so again, 87 per cent of 2024 Conservative voters say they will vote Conservative on Saturday, and 14 per cent of previous NDP voters say they will back the Conservatives. On Tuesday, Vancouver mayor-elect Pete Fry travelled to Vancouver Island to endorse B.C. Green candidate and leader Emily Lowan. These are poll findings and a campaign endorsement, not a forecast. Source: vancouversun.com

B.C. pays $35 million to settle lawsuit by prisoners sexually assaulted by guard: Vancouver Sun

The B.C. government has agreed to pay $35 million to settle a class-action lawsuit on behalf of more than 100 prisoners who alleged they were sexually assaulted in their youth by a B.C. jail guard. The lawsuit alleged B.C. Corrections was negligent for ignoring reports that Roderic MacDougall was sexually assaulting prisoners. The settlement is an agreement to pay; it is not a criminal finding reported here. Source: vancouversun.com

City & Province

BC NDP, BC Conservatives support expanding SkyTrain to Newton: Global BC

Surrey's newly elected mayor, Doug McCallum, said in a statement that only the B.C. Conservatives have committed to expanding SkyTrain to Newton, while the B.C. NDP "offered a meeting." Railway Supply reports pledges from both B.C. rivals and no cost estimate yet. Those are campaign positions, not an approved project or a funded timeline. Source: globalnews.ca Source: railway.supply

Hudson's Hope voters approve borrowing for new community hall: CBC British Columbia

Residents of Hudson's Hope approved borrowing up to $10 million to help finance a new community hall and multi-use gathering space, in a referendum on Saturday. Construction is estimated to cost more than $34 million. Source: cbc.ca

Charges recommended in mallet attack in downtown Vancouver: Vancouver Sun

Charges have been recommended in a mallet attack in downtown Vancouver. Police said a mallet was found in the possession of one of the suspects. The people involved are accused, not convicted, in what has been published so far. Source: vancouversun.com

The Closer Look

Stalled Downtown Vancouver office tower project pivots to 295-room hotel: Daily Hive Vancouver

A narrow vacant lot at 619 West Hastings Street, approved years ago for offices, is now the subject of a hotel-heavy revision. Vancouver city council approved Uptown Property Group's rezoning in May 2020 for a 28-storey tower, 330 feet or 100.5 metres, with about 158,000 square feet of office space. A development permit for that all-office plan was issued in September 2022. It never advanced into construction. Daily Hive reports the stall followed weak office demand coming out of the pandemic.

The revised development permit was submitted this past summer and made public yesterday, ahead of formal public consultation. It calls for 32 storeys and 362 feet, or 110.5 metres, with almost 158,000 square feet of floor area. Levels two to 24 would switch from offices to a boutique hotel. Two hundred ninety-five guest rooms would occupy levels three through 24, about 126,000 square feet of hotel space. Levels 25 to 31 would remain offices, about 32,000 square feet, with a shared outdoor amenity deck on the roof. The mid-block site sits immediately east of the historic Royal Bank Building, near Granville Street and West Hastings Street. The hotel pitch leans on proximity to the Vancouver Convention Centre, frequent buses, and Waterfront Station for the Expo Line, the Canada Line and the SeaBus.

The revision keeps a distinctive piece of the earlier design: physically connecting the new tower to the 1931 bank so the new concrete walls and core can brace that building in an earthquake. Musson Cattell Mackey Partnership designed both concepts. It says work affecting the heritage building, including those structural connections and an enclosure over the existing light well, would be covered by a separate permit. Sumner Godfrey Davenport, Royal Bank's chief architect from 1920 to 1942, designed the bank with the company's then Montreal headquarters in mind. Regional offices remained until 1973, when Royal Centre opened at West Georgia and Burrard. A Royal Bank branch is still on the main and lower floors.

The lot is 52 feet wide at the street and 120 feet deep, too tight for a conventional ramp. A car elevator from the lane would serve two underground levels with 16 vehicle spaces, against five levels and 49 spaces in the office-only concept. The target is now LEED Gold, down from LEED Platinum. Daily Hive places the switch among other stalled office projects moving partly or fully to hotels while Metro Vancouver is short of hotel rooms. Just to the north, Cadillac Fairview's revised Waterfront Station office tower received preliminary development-permit-board approval earlier this year. The company has told Daily Hive it expects a shortage of new office space as early as late this decade, because no major new office projects are moving forward now. Source: dailyhive.com

Hurricanes score six, pull away from slumping Canucks: NHL.com

The Carolina Hurricanes beat the Vancouver Canucks 6-3 on Tuesday at Rogers Arena. Nicolas Deslauriers, Nikolaj Ehlers and Logan Stankoven each scored twice, and Jackson Blake had three assists. Linus Karlsson, Jonathan Lekkerimaki and Max Sasson scored for Vancouver, and Jake DeBrusk had two assists. The Canucks are 2-8-1 and have lost eight in a row; the Hurricanes are 8-2-1. Leevi Merilainen made 38 saves, and Pyotr Kochetkov made 14 and is 6-0-0. It was the fourth straight game Vancouver was tied or leading entering the third; they have lost all four and been outscored 9-0 in the third and overtime. Coach Manny Malhotra said 40, 45 or 50 minutes is not enough. The penalty kill went five-for-five, and Merilainen stopped a Sebastian Aho penalty shot. Vancouver was without Marco Rossi, listed as ill, and Thatcher Demko, listed with a hip injury. The club paid tribute during the game to former goalie and broadcaster John Garrett. Both Sides: Finishing Kelowna General's unused sixth floor

What is on the table is a campaign promise, not a funded decision: whether the province puts $30 million into finishing an unused inpatient floor at Kelowna General Hospital before Saturday's election. The sixth floor is one of two floors in Centennial Tower that have sat unfinished since the building opened in 2012, while patients are treated in hallway beds.

B.C. NDP Leader David Eby, announcing the commitment on a Kelowna campaign stop Tuesday, said the money would build out the sixth-floor inpatient unit and that "this isn't just a plan on paper" but "something that's actually going to be delivered." His best reason is capacity already waiting on staff: he said planning began before the election, hospital staff are there and need the space, and further recruitment will still be required as Kelowna grows and services are added. He said the B.C. Conservatives have no money set aside for the project and his party does, "so it's going to get done."

Gavin Dew, the Conservative candidate for Kelowna-Mission, said the only thing that has changed is that an election is happening, that local Conservatives have pushed for this for years, and that Eby "has not moved an inch." His best reason for waiting on a blank cheque is that a Conservative government would still move to open the unused space — he put it at 33,000 square feet — but would first review Interior Health's capital planning to see what is actually happening. Neither statement is a budget vote. Voters decide on Saturday; until then both remain promises. Source: globalnews.ca
