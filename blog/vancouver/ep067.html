Top Stories

Religious hospitals do not have to provide MAID, B.C. court rules: National Post

The B.C. Supreme Court has ruled that religious hospitals do not have to provide medical assistance in dying on site. Global News reports the case focused on 34-year-old Sam O'Neill, who had stage four cervical cancer and had chosen MAID. CBC reports the court upheld the exemption, so facilities such as Vancouver's St. Paul's Hospital can continue to require patients who want MAID to transfer to another facility. Source: nationalpost.com Source: globalnews.ca Source: cbc.ca

Update: Independent police watchdog investigating 'police-involved shooting' in Richmond: Richmond News

The Independent Investigations Office is investigating a police-involved shooting in the 3600 block of No. 3 Road in Richmond, across from Yaohan Centre, where a man died Friday afternoon. RCMP said officers went to a business there around 1:30 p.m., with the Lower Mainland Integrated Emergency Response Team, to arrest a man as part of an ongoing investigation, and officers discharged their firearms. The IIO said that despite life-saving efforts, the man was pronounced dead at the scene. A social-media video shows heavily armed officers shooting toward a pickup truck. The IIO will look at whether the death meets the Police Act threshold for serious harm or death and whether it was connected to an officer's action or inaction, then whether there are reasonable grounds to believe an offence may have occurred. Source: richmond-news.com

A woman was killed Friday morning in Vancouver's Mount Pleasant neighbourhood in a crash involving an off-duty Vancouver police officer. Source: cbc.ca

The Closer Look

New board chair of Metro Vancouver Regional District seeks to work with Conservative government on major reforms: Daily Hive Vancouver

North Vancouver Mayor Linda Buchanan was elected chair of the Metro Vancouver Regional District board on Friday, November 27, 2026, and Coquitlam Mayor Craig Hodge was chosen vice-chair. Both received one-year terms at the new board's inaugural meeting after last month's civic election. Buchanan succeeds Burnaby Mayor Mike Hurley, who had chaired the district since June 2024 and did not seek another term in the role. The change follows a wide turnover: 14 of the region's 21 municipalities are now led by a different mayor.

In a statement, Buchanan praised the district's employees and the services they provide, then said public confidence and trust have been eroded, the financial trajectory is not sustainable, and the organization as currently structured is no longer fit for purpose. She said the solution requires fundamental reform, not incremental change. Her first priority is a meeting with Hodge and Cathie Ounsted, B.C.'s minister of local government, housing, sport and culture, to establish what the Conservative-led provincial government expects and how the two governments can work together. Those talks are meant to shape a 100-day action plan covering responsibilities, finances, and public accountability.

The first part would examine which services the district should provide and how it should be governed, with renewed emphasis on utilities, less overlapping work, and the provincial legislation changes an overhaul would need. The second would address paying for increasingly expensive infrastructure without unaffordable demands on households. Buchanan's priorities there are fairer funding contributions across levels of government, stronger financial controls, independent examination of major capital projects, and a more stable approach to long-term financing. She has been a critic of higher North Shore utility bills tied to cost overruns at the North Shore Wastewater Treatment Plant, and she has called for an independent public inquiry and a fairer distribution of that project's financial burden. The third part would make decisions more transparent so board directors have the information they need to oversee the organization and answer to residents. Buchanan cautioned that fundamental reform will take longer than 100 days. She said the immediate responsibility is a clear direction, improvements where possible, and a credible roadmap for longer-term change.

That timeline roughly coincides with the new government's first legislative sitting, expected in spring 2027, the first chance for Premier Lorne Doerkson's administration to move new legislation. Buchanan's direction closely aligns with Doerkson's November 24, 2026 mandate letter to Ounsted, which directs the minister to narrow the district to a lean core of bulk drinking water, sewage treatment, and garbage disposal, simplify its structure, introduce a professional board, and transfer other responsibilities to municipal governments. Beyond utilities, the district is also responsible for regional parks, air quality, regional urban and growth planning, non-market rental housing, and Invest Vancouver. Buchanan chaired Invest Vancouver before the civic election.

The province has also directed Ounsted to commission an independent audit of the North Shore plant, covering costs, contracting, management, and construction delivery, alongside a review of the district's other major infrastructure commitments. The estimated cost has risen from about $700 million to about $3.9 billion, with a decade-long completion delay. Findings from the district's own separate review are expected in January 2027. Doerkson has ordered separate independent reviews of TransLink and BC Ferries spending and executive pay, and the province will not consider new or additional funding requests from TransLink until that review is complete. Last week, Port Coquitlam Mayor Brad West was re-elected chair of TransLink's Mayors' Council.

The district also parted ways in early September 2026 with Jerry Dobrovolny, chief administrative officer and commissioner since 2019. He had faced criticism over oversight of the North Shore plant, high compensation, and allegations of overstepping his authority internally. Source: dailyhive.com Source: globalnews.ca

Whitecaps sweep Salt Lake, advance to Western Conference semifinals: Sportsnet

The top-seeded Whitecaps, 19-8-7 in the regular season, were without Brian White, who left Sunday's game in the 63rd minute with tightness in his right quad, and without 19-year-old Rayan Elloumi, who is healing from a season-ending stress fracture in his left foot, so coach Jesper Sorensen moved Sabbi up from winger to striker.
