748 non-market homes go to a Surrey public hearing Monday night.

By Mira November 23, 2026 Ep 62 10 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode

What You Need to Know: Surrey council takes up a plan for 748 non-market homes in two City Centre towers after a public hearing Monday night. An 84-year-old woman was killed in a Burnaby collision Saturday, and a First Nations leader warned of dire consequences if the province repeals its Indigenous rights law. A coroner's jury has issued recommendations after police shot and killed an autistic Surrey teen in 2025.

Top Stories

Hundreds of non-market housing units in two towers proposed for Surrey City Centre: Vancouver Sun

Two proposed high-rises in Surrey's City Centre would replace the aging Jessica Place townhouse complex at 9883 140 Street, a short walk from the Green Timbers SkyTrain station under construction at 140 Street and Fraser Highway. Ron Gill, Surrey's general manager of planning and development, said the existing buildings hold 40 below-market rental units and that all of those residents will be offered the chance to move into the new buildings once they are built on the site. Gill said staff support the plan because it would add mixed-income affordable rental housing in City Centre and higher density near frequent transit. The first tower would be 35 storeys with 327 units and the second 39 storeys with 421 units, 748 in all, kept non-market for 60 years. Surrey council votes after a public hearing Monday night. Gill said a reputable non-profit would operate the homes at rent-geared-to-income or low-end-of-market rates. Entre Nous Femmes Housing Society says it first applied in June 2025, expects construction to start in summer 2027, expects that work to last three years, and projects an opening in summer 2030. The society says existing residents will have to relocate temporarily because every current building would be demolished. The plan includes a management office and two ground-oriented, daycare-ready townhouse units that could run child care as a home occupation. The 35-storey tower would have indoor and outdoor amenities, a community garden, meeting rooms, a gymnasium and a co-working area; the 39-storey tower would have a community garden, amenities, an outdoor playground, seating, a basketball court and a stage. Shared private amenities are proposed for both buildings. The school district estimates 62 school-age children. Source: vancouversun.com

Woman struck and killed by vehicle in Burnaby: Vancouver Sun

An 84-year-old woman was struck by a vehicle and killed in Burnaby on Saturday night. Burnaby RCMP Cpl. Mike Kalanj said it happened around 7:20 p.m. on Kingsway at Randolph Avenue. Kalanj said she was declared dead at the scene and the driver remained and is co-operating with the investigation. The Vancouver Sun notes two earlier deaths of elderly women on foot in Vancouver this month. On Nov. 6, a 78-year-old woman was struck while crossing Dunbar Street at West 34th Avenue; that driver stopped and co-operated, and she died the next day. On Nov. 11, an 87-year-old woman was crossing Dunbar Street at West 41st Avenue in a marked crosswalk when a white sedan turning off West 41st onto Dunbar struck her; she died on Nov. 17, and police have not located that driver. Source: vancouversun.com Source: cbc.ca

First Nations leader warns of consequences if B.C. repeals DRIPA: CBC British Columbia

A First Nations leader told CBC British Columbia that British Columbia's new Conservative government could face dire consequences if it repeals the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act as promised, and said he is willing to work with new Premier Lorne Doerkson. Source: cbc.ca

Pay halted for suspended Vancouver police officer charged with fraud and theft: CBC British Columbia

The Vancouver Police Board has discontinued pay for suspended high-profile officer Kal Dosanjh, who is accused of and charged with fraud and theft. Source: cbc.ca

City & Province

Tumbler Ridge begins consultation on design for new school building: Global BC

Residents of Tumbler Ridge are being asked for feedback on designs for a new secondary school after a mass shooting there in February. Global BC reports six people were killed when 18-year-old Jesse Van Rootselaar opened fire at the northeastern B.C. school on Feb. 10; before that, she killed her mother and half-brother at their home, and she died at the school from a self-inflicted gunshot. In July, School District 59 began demolition after then-Premier David Eby said students cannot return to the school where the shootings happened. Board chair Chad Anderson said the board has narrowed the choices to a pre-fabricated building, largely built off-site and assembled on site, and a site-built design, and hopes the result is a place where students and staff feel safe. A two-week survey of residents will feed a board decision expected by late this winter. Anderson said that if all goes well, construction could begin in summer 2027, depending on the method chosen. Source: globalnews.ca

B.C. trial in author Stephen Galloway’s defamation lawsuit enters final week: CityNews Vancouver

Final submissions in the defamation lawsuit of author and former UBC professor Stephen Galloway are set to begin today in B.C. Supreme Court in Vancouver. Source: vancouver.citynews.ca

Legal professionals await BC Conservative government direction on new regulations: Business in Vancouver

Legal professionals are waiting on the B.C. Conservative government for direction on new regulations under the Legal Professions Act, which Business in Vancouver says was under fire from the Law Society of B.C. before last month's provincial election. Source: biv.com

Lowan and Valeriote Sworn Into B.C. Legislature: Wisevoter

Lowan and Valeriote have been sworn into the B.C. Legislature. Source: wisevoter.com

The Closer Look

Coroner's jury makes recommendations in police shooting of autistic Surrey teen: Vancouver Sun

A B.C. coroner's jury has recommended that police and health agencies co-ordinate emergency responses more closely after 15-year-old Chase de Balinhard was shot and killed by police in Surrey. He died on Feb. 9, 2025, after a 911 call about a teen with a gun led to a standoff of nearly half an hour. The gun was fake. Officers did not know that. After he ignored repeated requests to drop it, he was fatally shot in the chest. An officer who testified during an emotional week in Burnaby coroners' court said police knew Chase was neurodivergent but had not received specific training on how to de-escalate a situation like that one.

The jury asked the Ministry of Public Safety, the Ministry of Health and the Fraser Health Authority to review how crisis and safety information can be easier to reach in an emergency. That would include voluntarily provided health records and personalized safety plans, such as safe words or calming techniques, for responders. Health officials are urged to make individualized safety plans easier to find and to review and update them regularly. Those plans could tell responders whether a youth's home contains low-velocity, imitation or conventional firearms.

Vancouver Coastal Health, Fraser Health, the RCMP and Surrey Police Service are asked to consider whether police and mental-health response teams have enough capacity and equipment, and whether mobile integrated crisis response teams — formerly known as Car 67 in Fraser Health — need more resources and a wider geographical range. The Ministry of Health is asked to review whether psychiatric nurses attached to police mental-health teams can get time-sensitive health information, including someone's individualized safety plan, in a crisis.

At the federal and provincial level, the jury asked whether realistic imitation firearms and pellet guns can be made easier to identify with a clear, conspicuous visual cue that they are fake or low-velocity, including product, manufacturing and import rules so those markers cannot be removed or altered, and whether the Firearm Violence Prevention Act should add provisions on imitation guns. The Ministry of Education and Child Care and the Ministry of Children and Family Development are asked to review how supports for children with autism or other neurodivergence are delivered and monitored, including how changes to those supports are communicated to families.

The Independent Investigations Office of B.C. is asked to review how family members get information during an investigation into a police-involved incident, including whether families get clear and timely information about what can be shared, what cannot yet be shared, and why, and whether that process can meet or exceed police-based victim services. Police in Surrey are urged to review how they contact family if a first attempt fails, including whether that should follow a set protocol or stay with the officers' discretion; to review whether Surrey Police Service has adequate access to helicopters and drones in an emergency; and to explore ways to improve trust between youth and police in Surrey schools. Health and school officials are asked to determine whether families of autistic and neurodivergent children and youth in Surrey have clear information about existing autism and mental-health supports and how to access them. A coroner's jury establishes the facts around a death and can make recommendations. It does not assign blame and cannot call for a criminal investigation. Source: vancouversun.com

Sports

Blackhawks rookie Roman Kantserov scores first NHL hat trick in win over Canucks: Toronto Star

The Chicago Blackhawks beat the Vancouver Canucks 6-3 at Rogers Arena. Rookie Roman Kantserov scored his first NHL hat trick, including the goal that ended Vancouver's comeback hopes. Chicago led 4-0 before Vancouver managed a shot; the first Canucks shot came 13 minutes in, and the crowd answered with a Bronx cheer. Frank Nazar, Tyler Bertuzzi, Kantserov and Dominic Toninato scored in that opening burst. Nikita Tolopilo was pulled after the fourth goal. Kevin Lankinen, in net for the first time since Nov. 3, stopped 17 of 19 shots. Head coach Manny Malhotra said Tolopilo was put in a bad situation, that the group in front of him was not ready, and that Lankinen did a good job after being thrown into a rough spot. Vancouver's goals came from a Zeev Buium point shot that Kevin Korchinski tipped into his own net, a Filip Chytil pass that banked in off Jordan Greenway, and a Jake DeBrusk rebound. Brock Boeser's assist on the DeBrusk goal extended his point streak to 11 games, tied for the 17th-longest in Canucks history; the club record is 15, shared by Petr Nedved and Todd Bertuzzi. Vancouver's power play was 0-for-5. Chicago's was 3-for-4 and moved from 27th in the league to 16th. Vancouver's penalty kill sits at 61.2 per cent, just ahead of Seattle's 60.7 per cent. The win moved Chicago out of second-last and into 29th, ahead of the Los Angeles Kings and the Pittsburgh Penguins. Chytil said the team did not help Tolopilo and that he was not happy with his own game. Boeser said an unlucky bounce after a good first shift cannot be allowed to deflate the team. Source: thestar.com Source: dailyhive.com Source: thehockeynews.com

Whitecaps 2, Real Salt Lake 0: Ascendant Bruno, Ocampo take the wheel to open MLS Cup playoffs: The Province

The Vancouver Whitecaps beat Real Salt Lake 2-0 to open their 2026 MLS Cup playoff series, a comfortable first win in which Bruno and Ocampo took the wheel, The Province and AFTN reported. Source: theprovince.com Source: aftn.ca
