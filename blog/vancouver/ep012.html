Under ninety minutes remain for Vancouver voters before polls close.

Vancouver Daily News

Under ninety minutes remain for Vancouver voters before polls close.

What You Need to Know: Vancouver voters are in the last stretch before polls close, with the city promising a count after 8 p.m. Police also have a fresh witness appeal in the CRAB Park homicide, an arrest after a fiery East Side crash, and a new warning about a high-risk offender now living in Vancouver. The Canucks took their first home win of the season.

Top Stories

Last chance to vote in Vancouver today: City of Vancouver

The City of Vancouver says voters still have just under an hour and a half before polls close for the day. The city says to stay tuned for today's count after 8 p.m. A where-to-vote page is posted on the city election site. Source: vancouver.ca

Pickup driver arrested after crash into nursing home: CityNews Vancouver

Vancouver police say a 34-year-old pickup driver is under arrest after a grey GMC Sierra crashed into a nursing home near Renfrew Street and East 22nd Avenue around 1:30 a.m. Friday, then split and burned. The truck had been travelling southbound and struck another vehicle before leaving the road, a witness told police. The driver and a passenger were taken to hospital. Police say no one in the other car or the building was injured. After a hospital assessment he was arrested and faces charges of dangerous operation of a motor vehicle and impaired operation of a motor vehicle. He was served a 90-day driving prohibition, is due in court later, and police have seized the burned truck. The investigation is ongoing. Anyone with information can call the VPD Collision Investigation Unit at 604-717-3012. Source: vancouver.citynews.ca

Police release video in CRAB Park homicide: CBC British Columbia

The Vancouver Police Department has released surveillance video of two potential witnesses it believes may have information on a homicide in CRAB Park last week. Source: cbc.ca

High-risk sex offender moving to Vancouver, police warn: CTV News

Police say a sex offender who was the subject of a Surrey warning last month is now moving to Vancouver. The Vancouver Sun reports that Skylar Wayne Pelletier will be monitored by corrections officials and must comply with specific conditions of release. Source: bc.ctvnews.ca Source: vancouversun.com

City & Province

Family offers reward in Highway of Tears case: CBC British Columbia

Tamara Lynn Chipman was 21 when she vanished along Highway 16 in northwestern B.C. in 2005. Her family is offering a $10,000 reward for information. Source: cbc.ca

B.C. judge names father guardian after child taken to Ukraine: Vancouver Sun

A B.C. judge has declared a father the guardian after the mother returned to Ukraine with their toddler. The couple now face a court battle on two continents over custody and the child's return to Canada. Source: vancouversun.com

Getting Around

DriveBC lists night paving on the Dollarton Highway on-ramps from Tuesday, October 6, to Wednesday, October 7, 8 p.m. to 6 a.m., with those ramps closed and detours via Riverside Drive, Mount Seymour Parkway and Keith Road eastbound, and via Main Street, Brooksbank Avenue and the Mountain Highway interchange westbound. On Highway 99 in Delta, night work runs from today through October 18, 9 p.m. to 5 a.m., Monday to Saturday, between Ladner Trunk Road and Highway 17, with one lane each way kept open. Highway 1 traffic remains shifted north of the median around 256th Street through October 31, with existing eastbound lanes closed. No weather watches or warnings are in effect. Today is mainly sunny after morning fog, high 18 except 22 inland, UV index 3. Tonight is clear, low 10, with fog after midnight. Monday clears late morning, high 18 except 21 inland, then a 30 percent chance of drizzle Monday night. Source: drivebc.ca Source: weather.gc.ca

Sports

Canucks 4, Flames 1: Sooke News Mirror

The Vancouver Canucks scored four third-period goals Saturday at Rogers Arena and beat the Calgary Flames 4-1, moving to 2-1-0. Liam Ohgren, defenceman Elias Pettersson, Drew O'Connor and Aatu Räty scored. Ohgren's marker, his first of the year, came on his 100th NHL game and was confirmed on replay after being called no goal on the ice. Matt Coronato had Calgary's only goal, on the power play. The Flames are 0-2-0. Leevi Merilainen, 24, from Oulu, Finland, claimed off waivers from the Ottawa Senators late last month, made 23 saves in his first start as a Canuck for his first win. He said it felt good to get the win and was weird playing for the first time in a long time. Head coach Manny Malhotra said Merilainen looked poised and calm, and that a late-second stop on Zayne Parekh in a 3-on-1 could have changed the game's momentum. Linus Karlsson and Tom Willander each had two assists. Source: sookenewsmirror.com Source: nanaimonewsnow.com Source: globalnews.ca

Clippers fall 3-2 in Coquitlam: Nanaimo News Now

The Nanaimo Clippers lost 3-2 to the Coquitlam Express. The Express led 2-0 by the two-minute mark of the second. Charles Beland set up Miller Kay, then scored himself, and the game stayed 2-2 until Justin Ivanusec put in a loose puck in the final minute. Source: nanaimonewsnow.com

Lions hold off Argonauts 24-17: Abbotsford News

Whyte kicked eight field goals as the B.C. Lions held on for a 24-17 win over the Toronto Argonauts. Source: abbynews.com

What's On

Seventeen screens at VIFF: CBC British Columbia

Métis filmmaker Justin Ducharme's first feature, Seventeen, set in Vancouver and shaped by his experience in sex work, screens at the Vancouver International Film Festival on October 4 and October 11. Source: cbc.ca

Both Sides: AI data centres in Vancouver

Hundreds marched Saturday from Mount Pleasant to downtown against AI data centres, Yahoo News Canada reports. No AI Vancouver called it part of an international day of protest, invited municipal candidates, and said the question is now a campaign issue. The file is a rezoning application for a proposed Mount Pleasant AI data centre. In July, Vancouver city council sent that application back to staff for further public consultation, which will not begin until after the October 17 municipal election.

The case for a pause is put by Torin Larocque, founder of No AI Vancouver, and by four council candidates from COPE, the Vancouver Greens and Bright Future. Larocque says the July referral happened partly because hundreds had signed up to speak, and he wants voters to know which candidates are against AI. COPE's Leslie said the proposal sits in a system that prioritizes the ultra-wealthy over everyone else. COPE's Yi-Kieran said residents do not want the centres, that consultation has been almost absent, and that AI comes up at the door as often as housing and affordability. Both support a moratorium and say the city controls this zoning. Greens candidate Burns wants a moratorium until there is more regulation, but said she does not hear the concern often while door-knocking. Bright Future's Lin said the issue shows a broader need for change in local government. Their stated reasons centre on energy, water and consultation.

Burns's doorstep account and Yi-Kieran's are the two strongest reads in the coverage of how widely people raise it. Council's July vote did not impose a moratorium and did not approve the project. It ordered more consultation after October 17. A 2024 International Energy Agency report cited in the story said data centres, largely driven by AI, used roughly 1.5 per cent of global energy, with that demand projected to more than double by 2030, and with data-centre electricity use growing about four times faster than all other sectors. The fact both the pause advocates and that July order share is that the extra consultation has not started. It is not set to begin before the October 17 vote. Source: ca.news.yahoo.com
