Sixteen years and eight months is the sentence for the Surrey bus attack.

Vancouver has named a new fire chief to start Monday. A jury has again found Alvaro Julio Roche-Garcia guilty of first-degree murder in a 2019 porch shooting.

Top Stories

Man sentenced to 16 years, 8 months for horrifying Surrey bus attack: Global BC

Abdul Aziz Kawam was convicted in 2025 in the 2023 Islamic State knife attack on a B.C. bus and sentenced to 15 years for attempted murder and 20 months for aggravated assault. He will serve 16 years and 8 months in prison, CBC reports. Global News reports that on April 1, 2023, security video showed Abdul Kawam walking onto the bus, followed a few seconds later by his victim. Source: cbc.ca Source: globalnews.ca

Vancouver names longtime deputy fire chief Robert Weeks to top job: Vancouver Sun

Robert Weeks, a longtime deputy fire chief, will take over Vancouver Fire Rescue Services on Nov. 2. The Vancouver Sun reports he won a countrywide search for the top job. Source: vancouversun.com

Vancouver man found guilty of first-degree murder for a 2nd time: Global BC

A jury has found Alvaro Julio Roche-Garcia guilty of first-degree murder for a second time in the January 2019 shooting of 69-year-old Zenen Cepeda Silva, who was gunned down on a porch on Fraser Street in Vancouver while sitting with his wife. Roche-Garcia was arrested shortly after. Silva’s wife suffered a stroke before the first trial and never took the stand; the jury in that trial heard an audio recording from a preliminary inquiry in which she said her husband’s dying words were “look how Alvaro has killed me.” B.C.’s highest court found the trial judge erred by allowing the jury to treat that alleged declaration as fact when the testimony was never cross-examined, struck the conviction in 2024, and ordered a new trial. After five weeks, the new jury returned a guilty verdict; Global News reports a motive was never made clear. Crown counsel Daniel Port said the couple knew the accused for about 15 years in Albuquerque, New Mexico, and Vancouver; Roche-Garcia told the court he had no reason to shoot Silva, and his lawyer argued there was a lack of hard evidence linking him to the crime. A sentencing hearing is set for next month. Source: globalnews.ca

City & Province

SURREY SCHOOLS Surrey Schools shares updated Accessibility Plan: Education News Canada

With the first three years of its Accessibility Plan complete, Surrey Schools has released an updated plan for 2026-2028. The first plan launched in September 2023 for 2023-2026, under the Accessible British Columbia Regulation, which required public organizations to establish accessibility committees, plans and public-feedback mechanisms as of September 2023. Assistant Superintendent Shauna Ross and District Principal Colin Reid presented the new plan at the October Surrey Board of Education meeting. Reid said a district-wide assessment of parking lots, entrances, doors, learning commons and emergency preparedness across all sites has informed recent renovations and new-building decisions, and that an animated accessibility video series has been viewed thousands of times and used by municipalities, non-profits, advocacy groups and other B.C. school districts. Looking ahead, the district’s priorities include annual site assessments, an accessibility space on each site’s website, National AccessAbility Week shaped by student input, and tactile wayfinding signage at three sites: DEC, REC and Sunnyside Elementary. Reid said the work has stayed with the statement “Nothing About Us, Without Us.” Source: educationnewscanada.com

SURREY SCHOOLS Surrey teacher wins national award for forest-based kindergarten program: Education News Canada

Ellen Petersson, a kindergarten teacher at Ray Sheperd Elementary in South Surrey, has received the Susan Langley Earth Steward Teaching Award, a national honour from Learning for a Sustainable Future. For six years her students have spent part of every school day in the forest beside the school, rain or shine. They practise math with pinecones, make art with leaves, and study science by watching insects, the weather and the seasons. Petersson said five-year-olds may not yet understand climate change, biodiversity loss or sustainability as adults do, but they understand wonder, curiosity and caring for a place they love. In 2020 a local senior the class calls Grandfriend Don began joining the outdoor lessons, and grandparents, retirees, parents and community elders followed. She brings in the principle of reciprocity — bird feeders in winter, bug hotels in the fall, keeping natural spaces clean — and said, “We need the children to love the earth before we ask them to save it.” Source: educationnewscanada.com

The Closer Look

City of Richmond moves to acquire Watermania aquatic centre through expropriation ahead of lease expiry: Daily Hive Vancouver

The City of Richmond is moving to take ownership of Watermania by expropriation as the long-term lease nears its expiry in March 2027. Daily Hive reports a formal expropriation notice dated July 3, 2026, was filed with the Land Title Office on July 6. It identifies 14300 Entertainment Blvd. Investments as the registered owner. Watermania sits at the Riverport Sports and Entertainment Complex in southeast Richmond, beside Silver City Riverport Cinemas, Splitsville Bowl Riverport and Richmond Ice Centre. The City operates the centre as a public recreation facility but does not own the building or the land; both have been leased from a private landowner since the centre was completed nearly 30 years ago. The notice says acquiring the property would serve the public interest by ensuring Watermania continues providing essential community services as a public indoor aquatic centre and fitness facility, and by supporting long-term financial and asset management through a fee-simple interest. Services could be provided by Richmond or by another public authority, person or organization acting on its behalf. The notice does not set out a purchase price or compensation offer. The private landowner has initiated a formal independent inquiry, scheduled to begin in late November 2026. Outgoing Mayor Malcom Brodie told Daily Hive that expropriation is used very infrequently and avoided whenever possible, that Watermania is an important recreation facility, and that the City’s goal is to secure its long-term future. He said that because the matter is before an independent inquiry, it would not be appropriate to comment further, and that the City respects the process and looks forward to a fair outcome.

The lease arrangement dates to the 1990s. Richmond voters approved borrowing about $20 million in a 1993 referendum to build a publicly owned aquatic centre on City-owned land. Council later cancelled that capital project because it wanted to avoid additional debt, and selected a proposal from the Riverport developer, which financed construction and leased the centre to the City. Watermania opened in 1997. BC Assessment’s latest roll values it at $49.4 million — $27.41 million for the land and $21.99 million for the building — as estimated market value as of July 1, 2025. In 2019 the City bought the adjacent Richmond Ice Centre, also privately developed and leased for public recreation, when that lease came up for renewal. The City said money previously spent on annual lease payments there would help fund renovations. Richmond Olympic Oval, by contrast, was built as a City-owned facility for the 2010 Winter Olympics. The City operates four public swimming facilities: year-round indoor swimming at Watermania and Minoru Centre for Active Living, and seasonal outdoor pools at South Arm and Steveston.

The people most directly affected are Richmond residents who use the indoor wave pool, two long waterslides and the 57-metre competition pool with a movable bulkhead, plus the fitness centre, diving boards and tower, whirlpools, steam room and sauna. Brodie’s stated case is that ownership secures that public use for the long term. The landowner’s response now on the record is the independent inquiry set for late November 2026. Daily Hive also notes a separate uncertainty over value: in August 2025, B.C. Supreme Court Justice Barbara Young granted the Cowichan Tribes Aboriginal title over about 740 acres of public and private land north and east of Riverport. The City of Richmond, the federal and provincial governments, Musqueam First Nation and Tsawwassen First Nation have appealed. The Cowichan Tribes have filed their own appeal seeking title over the full 1,846 acres of the original claim, and a City map shows that broader area includes the Riverport complex. Those appeals could run for years. The next dated step the source names for the pool itself is the inquiry in late November 2026. Source: dailyhive.com
