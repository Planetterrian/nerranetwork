116 Vancouver properties go to tax auction Wednesday, one reserved at $72,051.

By Mira November 04, 2026 Ep 43 10 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top Stories City & Province The Closer Look Also Today Getting Around Sports

What You Need to Know: One hundred sixteen Vancouver properties go to a city tax auction on Wednesday, and one East Vancouver house assessed at more than $1.7 million carries a reserve of $72,051. Surrey police say the city recorded no extortion-related shooting from August through October, the longest stretch without one since the violence ramped up in summer 2025. On the central coast, a second person has been found dead after a suspected grizzly attack near Bella Coola.

Top Stories

This $1.7M Vancouver home has an auction reserve of $72K. Yes, there’s a catch: Global BC

Global News reports that the City of Vancouver auctions 116 properties at city hall on Wednesday, starting at 10 a.m., in this year’s tax sale of homes whose owners have not paid taxes for three years. The reserve, or upset price, is the amount owed to the city. A Windsor Street house in East Vancouver, assessed by BC Assessment at more than $1.7 million, has a reserve of $72,051. Two side-by-side houses on Point Grey Road, valued around $5 million and $6 million, have upset prices of about $289,000 and $355,000. After auction day the current owners have a year to redeem by paying their taxes, which refunds the bidder plus interest. The city said there were only three instances in the last 10 years where a property changed hands from a tax sale, and that taxes on almost all properties have historically been paid within that year. The highest bidders must pay the upset price by noon Thursday. Retired notary Ron Usher said the law is structured to give the owner the maximum chance to sort the problem out, and that he suspects some sales are “whoops” rather than deliberate, citing inheritances. Realtor Sean Holden called bidding “an exercise in futility” and said he would not waste his time, citing how rarely sales are completed and the risk of not knowing a home’s condition. Source: globalnews.ca

Despite lull in Surrey extortion shootings, police not slowing down: Vancouver Sun

Surrey recorded no extortion-related shooting from August through October, the longest stretch without one since the issue ramped up in summer 2025, the Vancouver Sun reports. Ian MacDonald, media liaison officer with the Surrey police, said police are pleased but will not stand down, and that the drop in shootings is not a sign the work is done. Extortion-related violence peaked in January, when police recorded 44 extortion threats and 10 of those cases involved shootings. Since the beginning of the year, Surrey police have had 147 extortion threats reported, 21 of them involving shootings. In 2025, 49 of 133 extortion cases reported to police involved shootings. MacDonald said that as of Sept. 14 the Canada Border Services Agency had issued 188 removal orders and deported 111 individuals related to extortion cases. He said some victims are still targeted after suspects are arrested or removed, so investigations that look closed often are not. He also said the crime is not limited to Punjabi-Canadian business owners: employees, neighbours, suppliers and security guards are affected too. A $250,000 City of Surrey reward fund for information leading to arrests and conviction remains active. Source: vancouversun.com

Two dead in suspected bear attack near Bella Coola: Surrey Now-Leader

The BC Conservation Officer Service said the second victim of a suspected grizzly attack near Bella Coola was found dead Tuesday afternoon during a search by conservation officers and the RCMP. Family members have identified the two people as Jennifer Balcarras, 48, and her daughter Selah Reed, 20, CBC reports. The pair were reported missing by family on Nov. 1. One person was found dead Monday afternoon, Nov. 2, in the Snooka West Trail area after a search involving the RCMP and Bella Coola Valley Search and Rescue; officers were called in after evidence suggested an animal may have been involved. The service said it is not clear whether more than one bear was involved, and that officers are still working to locate and capture any bears involved. Any bear captured will be assessed; if it is determined to be responsible, it will be dispatched under provincial human-wildlife conflict guidelines. People are urged to stay away from the Snooka West Trail area. On Nov. 20, 2025, an adult and three children were attacked by a grizzly at the Old Trail, close to 4 Mile. Elected Chief of the Nuxalk Nation Samuel Schooner said the nation knows the trauma this will inflict on the families, last year’s victim and the community, and asked residents to stay away from the trail, stay close to young children and keep spaces free of attractants. Source: surreynowleader.com Source: cbc.ca

City & Province

Park Board's public meetings moved to Vancouver City Hall on a pilot project basis: Daily Hive Vancouver

Scheduled public meetings of Vancouver’s elected Park Board commissioners have moved, on a pilot, from the board’s headquarters at 2099 Beach Ave. to Vancouver City Council chambers at City Hall, Daily Hive reports. The change covers regular and committee meetings and is meant to make it easier for the public to attend and speak, and to encourage closer cooperation among the mayor, councillors and commissioners, a priority Mayor Pete Fry and the Green Party of Vancouver have identified. The first Park Board meeting at City Hall is Monday, Nov. 30, 2026, the first working meeting of this term. City Hall is near SkyTrain’s Broadway-City Hall station, served by the Canada Line and, starting in late 2027, the Millennium Line extension. The seven commissioners were sworn in Monday, Nov. 2, at VanDusen Botanical Garden. Green commissioner Tom Digby, the only incumbent, was elected the term’s first chair, and COPE’s Sacia Burton the first vice-chair. The other commissioners are COPE’s Jamie Han, Tamiko Suzuki and T’uy’t’tanat Cease Wyss, and the Green Party’s Spencer Van Vloten and Breanne Smart. Source: dailyhive.com

Major Sunshine Coast forestry operation bought for $80M by Tla'amin Nation: CBC British Columbia

The Tla’amin Nation’s forestry arm has acquired a 100 per cent ownership interest in the Stillwater Forest Operation near Powell River for about $80 million after adjustments, CBC reports. Source: cbc.ca

Vancouver Aquatic Centre demolition to proceed after interim injunction expires: CBC British Columbia

Demolition of the Vancouver Aquatic Centre will proceed later this month, after an interim injunction expired on Tuesday, as part of a controversial renovation that includes a 25-metre pool, CBC reports. Source: cbc.ca

The Closer Look

'Preserving the best of West Vancouver': Read Mayor Kirk LaPointe's full inaugural speech: Daily Hive Vancouver

Kirk LaPointe used his first speech as mayor of West Vancouver, after he and the new council were sworn in on the evening of Monday, November 2, to promise residents a greater voice in decisions, careful spending, and room for more people to build lives in the community. He defeated incumbent Mark Sager in last month’s civic election.

His career runs through newsrooms: managing editor of the Vancouver Sun, executive editor of the National Post, CBC ombudsman for English services, publisher and executive editor of Business in Vancouver, and an executive at Glacier Media, now Lodestar Media, until 2023. He has taught journalism at the University of British Columbia. He lives in Caulfeild. In 2014 he ran unsuccessfully for Vancouver mayor for the Non-Partisan Association, against Gregor Robertson.

On the campaign he promised to bring the projected 2027 municipal tax increase down from nearly seven per cent to three per cent, and after 2027 to cap annual municipal spending growth at half the inflation rate. He promised to eliminate municipal pay parking, to revitalize the commercial villages of Ambleside, Dundarave, Horseshoe Bay and Caulfeild, and to seek provincial permission for a tax on vacant storefronts. His housing pitch was smaller-scale multi-unit housing for seniors, young families and first responders, while preserving neighbourhood character. An urgent and primary care centre was his first priority for a public-private partnership. He also proposed arts, skating and seniors’ facilities, restoring advisory committees, twice-monthly public question-and-answer sessions, and fewer closed-door council meetings. At Metro Vancouver he said he would push for an independent review of the North Shore Wastewater Treatment Plant, changes to its funding formula and repayment period, and no additional regional compensation.

He told the inauguration that council cannot promise everyone the answer they want, but can promise respect, openness and an honest account of decisions. Preserving the best of West Vancouver and preparing for its future, he said, are one promise, like old trees rooted deep enough to withstand storms that still put out new growth each spring. He thanked Mark Sager and councillors Sharon Thompson, Christine Cassidy and Nora Gambioli. He serves with returning councillors Linda Watt, Peter Lambur and Scott Snider, and new councillors Lee Lau, Jaime Webbe and Tim Webb. Lau’s background is medicine and clinical leadership, Webbe’s is international diplomacy, executive leadership and financial management, and Webb’s is local business and commercial property. He noted that long before any of them arrived, the Squamish people knew these shores, rivers and mountains. Earlier Monday, he said, council met to share what the campaigns had taught them about what West Vancouver wants, so they can develop the plans to deliver it. Source: dailyhive.com

Sports

Gauthier has second straight hat trick, Ducks hold off Canucks' late rally: NHL.com

Cutter Gauthier scored his second straight hat trick and the Anaheim Ducks held off the Vancouver Canucks 5-4 at Rogers Arena on Tuesday, NHL.com reports. Gauthier is the first player in Ducks history with back-to-back hat tricks and has eight goals in the past three games after nine without a goal. Beckett Sennecke had a career-high four assists, all primary, including the passes on all three Gauthier goals. Mikael Granlund had a goal and two assists. The Ducks’ power play was 3-for-3. Jake DeBrusk scored twice in the third for Vancouver. Kevin Lankinen made 17 saves; Laurent Brossoit made 19. The Canucks are 3-13-1, and their only win in 14 games came in overtime on Oct. 27 against these Ducks. Coach Manny Malhotra said it was a little too little, too late, while citing compete and pushback. FOX Sports reports Vancouver visits Winnipeg after DeBrusk’s two-goal game. Source: nhl.com Source: foxsports.com

The Vancouver Canucks' AHL affiliate is a wagon right now: Daily Hive Vancouver

After dropping their first three games, the Abbotsford Canucks have won seven straight and sit at the top of the AHL standings, with the second-best points percentage, behind only the Grand Rapids Griffins, Daily Hive reports. They average 4.0 goals a game, the most in the league. Last season they won their first two, then lost 11 straight. Braeden Cootes had six points in his first six games before a lower-body injury kept him out the past four; Canucks co-president of hockey operations Daniel Sedin said they are hoping he is back this coming weekend, after a positive talk with Rick Seeley, and that it should not keep him out long. Ty Mueller leads Abbotsford with six goals and 10 points in 10 games. Kirill Kudryavtsev has six assists in five games. Goaltender Nikita Tolopilo’s .910 save percentage is a career high so far. Source: dailyhive.com
