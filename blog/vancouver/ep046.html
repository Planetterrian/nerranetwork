Top Stories

LifeLabs in B.C. ordered to pay $32K for misgendering transgender, non-binary patients: CBC British Columbia

B.C.'s Human Rights Tribunal has ordered LifeLabs and a subsidiary to pay three transgender and non-binary people $32,000 for injury to their dignity. The tribunal found staff misgendered and outed them at service centres in Vancouver and Burnaby in 2019. Source: cbc.ca

Two men charged in 2025 Surrey extortion shootings: Vancouver Sun

Pardeep Singh and Saveer Khan, both 22, are each charged with two counts of reckless discharge of a firearm in two October 2025 shootings. On Oct. 12, 2025, police responded to the 1300-block of 103A Avenue, where bullets were aimed at a home; a woman inside was shot in the torso, taken to hospital in critical condition, and later confirmed stable. On Oct. 16, Kap's Cafe, owned by Bollywood actor Kapil Sharma, was hit by gunfire — the third shooting at that restaurant in three months. The cafe was closed; two staff members were inside and neither was injured. The provincial extortion task force, the Combined Forces Special Enforcement Unit of B.C. and Surrey police arrested the two in Surrey on Nov. 1. Both remain in custody. Chief Supt. Duncan Pound of the B.C. RCMP said the charges followed a year of work, including judicial authorizations, search warrants and evidence analysis, and should be a message that police are working together on extortion-related violence. Since the beginning of the year, Surrey police have recorded 147 extortion threats, 21 of them involving shootings; in 2025, 49 of 133 extortion cases reported to police involved shootings. The City of Surrey has a $250,000 reward fund for tips that lead to charges. Source: vancouversun.com

Chilliwack poultry facility reports 1st case of avian flu in B.C. this season: CBC British Columbia

The Canadian Food Inspection Agency says the first avian flu detection in a commercial poultry flock in British Columbia this season was confirmed Friday in Chilliwack. Source: cbc.ca

Seaspan makes largest order for heavy metal with Canadian steel mill: North Shore News

North Vancouver shipbuilder Seaspan is paying $182 million for steel to build future icebreakers, its largest order of heavy metal with a Canadian steel mill. Source: nsnews.com

A lack of detectors in a North Vancouver apartment building let a gas leak go unnoticed earlier this year and sent six people to hospital, and the case has prompted a boiler safety order. Source: nsnews.com

The Closer Look

U.S. Treasury sanctions Canadian firms connected to Ryan Wedding and Iran: Global BC

The United States Treasury Department has sanctioned eight Canadian businesses for allegedly helping to evade sanctions on Iran's government. Bob Mackin reports that two of the money-services businesses are in North Vancouver. Global News says the Treasury acted with Fintrac, Canada's anti-money laundering watchdog, against money services businesses in British Columbia and Ontario.

Named companies based in Ontario and British Columbia include the Million Exchange, NIA Nations Currency, Farah Exchange, Darvish Exchange and several numbered companies. The department alleges several of the entities also worked with Ryan Wedding, the alleged drug kingpin and former Canadian Olympic snowboarder. It alleges he used several of these services to launder millions of dollars in drug money through digital assets before his arrest in January.

"Canada cannot be a safe haven for illicit financial facilitators that provide sanctions-evasion services to the Iranian regime and its proxies," U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said in a news release. "Treasury will pursue and disrupt the Iranian regime's financial channels anywhere in the world." Global News describes the sanctions as the latest U.S. move to clamp down on transactions evading sanctions as the United States continues its war with Iran.

Wedding was arrested in Mexico in January and taken to the United States to face drug trafficking, murder and attempted murder charges. He remains in federal custody awaiting two trials in March and October of next year. He represented Canada in a snowboarding slalom at the 2002 Winter Olympics in Salt Lake City, was later convicted in the United States of conspiracy to distribute cocaine, and was sentenced to prison in 2010. U.S. authorities allege that after his release he resumed drug trafficking. Eight people were also arrested in Canada a year ago as part of the U.S. investigation.

A Mexican judge was arrested this week on allegations that he accepted a $2-million bribe in exchange for granting bail to a man accused of acting as second-in-command to Wedding. The U.S. Department of Justice said Luis Benitez Alcantara is accused of unlawfully securing the release of Andrew Clark, a Canadian man alleged to have played a key role in a violent international drug trafficking ring. Wedding is alleged to have ordered the murders of several people, including a witness who could testify against him in a 2024 narcotics case. U.S. authorities have said he had been on the run for years.

For the North Vancouver businesses, the sanction is the change in force now: U.S. measures cover Canadian currency-exchange firms the Treasury accuses of serving the Iranian regime and, in several cases, Wedding. The next dates the coverage names are his trials in March and October of next year. Source: globalnews.ca Source: bobmackin.substack.com

Whitecaps' Halbouni says he has been diagnosed with Hodgkin lymphoma: TSN

Vancouver Whitecaps centre back Belal Halbouni said Friday he has been diagnosed with Hodgkin lymphoma. The 26-year-old from London, Ont., began treatment in his hometown last week. Sporting director Axel Schuster said Halbouni and his family have the full support of the club, that Halbouni will step away to focus on treatment and recovery, and that the club will be there and looks forward to welcoming him back. The club also confirmed a contract extension through the 2027-28 season. Halbouni said he is grateful the club stood by him beyond football, proud to represent the badge, grateful to call Vancouver home, and thankful for the light at the end of the tunnel. He joined in February 2024 and has made 26 appearances. Vancouver (18-8-7, 61 points) leads the Western Conference and closes the regular season Saturday at CF Montreal. Source: tsn.ca
