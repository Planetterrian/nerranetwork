Seventeen years in prison hang on a deadline this week for Congresswoman LaMonica McIver.

By Mira October 06, 2026 Ep 13 5 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents

What You Need to Know: Representative LaMonica McIver of New Jersey must decide by October ninth whether to press a further appeal of federal charges that carry a potential seventeen years in prison. The case follows a May 2025 confrontation with immigration agents outside the Delaney Hall detention center. She has pleaded not guilty.

Lead

McIver faces an October ninth choice on federal charges: The Guardian

A federal appeals court in late August declined to dismiss criminal charges against Representative LaMonica McIver, accused of interfering with an arrest in May 2025 outside Delaney Hall, an immigration detention facility in New Jersey. The Guardian reports she is the only member of Congress the Department of Justice has indicted, and that the charges carry a potential seventeen years in prison. McIver, 40, who represents New Jersey’s 10th district, has pleaded not guilty. She argues she is immune under the Constitution’s speech and debate clause because the visit was part of her job, and that the Trump administration has selectively prosecuted her. By October 9 she and her lawyers must choose a rehearing or an en banc hearing in the Third Circuit, a request that the Supreme Court consider the ruling, or a trial. She gave birth to her second child last Thursday; the Guardian says she is the 15th member of Congress to give birth while serving. Source: theguardian.com

Across the Region

Parti Québécois takes 59 seats, short of a majority: BBC

Canadian Press, as cited by iPolitics, says the Parti Québécois captured 59 seats after votes were counted late Monday, short of the 64 needed for a majority; the BBC reports the party secured a minority. Prime Minister Mark Carney congratulated premier-designate Paul St-Pierre Plamondon and wrote that building a stronger Quebec is core to building a stronger Canada. Carney also thanked Coalition Avenir Québec leader Christine Frechette, whose party, iPolitics reports, failed to win a single seat. The BBC says the Parti Québécois has vowed to hold an independence referendum in the years to come. Intergovernmental Affairs Minister Dominic LeBlanc said he looks forward to working with the new Quebec government. Source: bbc.co.uk https://www.ipolitics.ca/2026/10/06/conservatives-call-on-liberals-to-back-emergency-fuel-relief-plan/

Connecticut Sun sold to a Houston group after 24 seasons: The Guardian

The Connecticut Sun played their final WNBA game last month at Mohegan Sun Arena and, after 24 seasons, have been sold to a new ownership group in Houston. The Guardian reports the state of Connecticut is considering legal action, and that New England now has no WNBA team. The Mohegan Tribe bought the club in 2003, the first independently owned WNBA team and the first Native American-owned franchise in professional sports. Natalie Welch, a member of the Eastern Band of Cherokee and program director of an MBA in sport and entertainment management at Seattle University, told the Guardian the sale seems like another disregarding of the tribe’s sovereignty. The tribe, granted federal recognition in 1994, has more than 2,400 registered members. Source: theguardian.com

Also Today

Ohio early voting opens with Congress in the balance: Reuters

Early voting began in Ohio on Tuesday in an election Reuters says puts control of the US Congress on the line. Source: reuters.com

Federal judge in Miami strikes down a congressional ban on noncitizen voting: Miami Herald

A federal judge in Miami has struck down a congressional ban on noncitizen voting, the Miami Herald reports. Source: miamiherald.com

Tennessee’s lethal-injection attempt on Christa Pike fails: The New York Times

Tennessee’s attempt to end the life of Christa Pike by lethal injection was unsuccessful, and the New York Times reports the failure is spurring debate over the five methods used in executions and over whether the death penalty should exist at all. Source: nytimes.com

Trump warns of tariffs if a Canada–EU tie looks hostile: Financial Post

Financial Post columnist Gwyn Morgan writes that President Donald Trump said he would put very serious tariffs on, or stop trading with Europe on many things, if he judged a Canadian move toward European Union membership a hostile act, after what Morgan describes as a breakdown in United States–Canada trade talks and Prime Minister Carney turning toward Europe. Source: financialpost.com

Both Sides: A cap on what colleges can pay athletes

The Senate passed the Protect College Sports Act last week. The bill now awaits approval from the House. It would set a ceiling of around $49 million on what schools can direct to athletes, and, except in a few specified circumstances, limit players to one free transfer; a further move would mean sitting out a year.

Deion Sanders, in an advertisement for the bill, says he supports it because it gives student-athletes a chance to shine on and off the field, strengthens historically Black colleges and universities, and keeps men’s and women’s sports strong. He has said college athletes should be compensated for their name, image and likeness. In June he told reporter Baker Machado that the current system is not about name, image and likeness but about pay-per-view. Four years earlier, as coach at Jackson State, he said NIL deals turn NCAA athletes into professionals and that programs lack staff equipped to handle a young man with money. As the Guardian describes the bill, it would not end or cap NIL, but a player would have to show a deal was worth about what a comparable figure would earn and that it had a genuine commercial purpose, a test that could end deals that effectively pay athletes to play.

Journalist Rob Parker called Sanders a sellout for taking part in the advertisement, arguing the bill will hurt athletes. Former NFL player Domonique Foxworth said the core of the act is about reestablishing power the NCAA held in the past, power the court system determined was illegal.

Both the advertisement and the critics’ reading accept that the Senate has passed a bill that leaves some NIL compensation in place while adding a commercial-purpose test, a payment ceiling, and a tighter transfer rule. The next step the Guardian names is approval from the House. Source: theguardian.com

Progress Watch

Centre Block rebuild moves past demolition on Parliament Hill: National Post

The first phase of the Parliament Hill renovation — deconstruction, including demolition and excavation — is complete, and rebuilding is underway on a new parliamentary welcome centre and on upgrades to the historic Centre Block, the National Post reports. The project is estimated at $4.5 billion to $5 billion. As of March, up to 800 workers were on site daily, a figure expected to peak above 1,500. About 95 percent of the companies that have worked on the project are Canadian-owned, drawing on more than 400 companies across eight provinces. Main construction is due to finish between 2030 and 2031, with the Centre Block to reopen about a year later. Source: nationalpost.com
