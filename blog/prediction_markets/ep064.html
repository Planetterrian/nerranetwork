Fourteen days from now, Kalshi must block sports contracts on four New Mexico tribal lands.

By Patrick November 25, 2026 Ep 64 8 min read Listen to podcast

What You Need to Know: Fourteen days from now, Kalshi must block sports contracts on four New Mexico tribal lands. A federal judge issued that preliminary injunction while a lawsuit by three pueblos and the Mescalero Apache Tribe continues. Utah, separately, moved to drop criminal charges after Kalshi left the state.

Top Story

U.S. District Court Judge Margaret Strickland ruled Monday for the Sandia, Isleta and Pojoaque pueblos and the Mescalero Apache Tribe, ordering Kalshi to stop sports event contracts inside those four boundaries. The May lawsuit says New Mexico allows sports gambling only at tribal casinos, and that Kalshi’s offering on tribal land violates gaming compacts and federal law. Kalshi argued that the Commodities Exchange Act overrides the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act and those compacts, and that off-site servers mean it is not bound by laws of tribes it never joined. Strickland wrote that she is unpersuaded the internet insulates the company, and that the federal commodities statute was not meant to displace all state gaming laws or tribes’ power to enforce their compacts. Tribal IT staff reported about 30,000 visits to Kalshi’s site over three weeks on pueblo Wi-Fi, without counting wagers, and a Mescalero gaming agent said the contracts looked materially the same as bets at Inn of the Mountain Gods and Casino Apache. The order is a preliminary injunction; Kalshi must geofence or otherwise block sports access on those lands within 14 days, a tool the lawsuit says the app already uses in more than 50 other jurisdictions. Mescalero Apache President Duane Duffy said the tribes sued because prediction markets on tribal land without federal and tribal approval undermine sovereignty and revenue for government services. A Kalshi spokesperson declined to comment. Source: sourcenm.com

Source: bizjournals.com

The Ecosystem

Utah drops criminal charges after Kalshi exits the state: PYMNTS.com

Utah Attorney General Derek Brown’s office said Tuesday that it asked a court on Monday, November 23, to dismiss criminal charges against Kalshi and individual defendants without prejudice, after the company blocked Utah residents. The office opened a criminal investigation in September and filed charges on October 28, alleging prediction markets are gambling and that Utah allows no legal gambling of any kind. Brown said Kalshi has pulled out, the charges return if it comes back, and six other prediction-market companies have agreed to stop operating in the state. The charges named co-founders Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara. Kalshi general counsel Yaakov Roth said on X that the company is pleased Utah dismissed the criminal charges while civil litigation continues, and that good-faith jurisdictional disagreements should never be criminalized. He said Kalshi is ready to work with state attorneys general on interim measures until courts decide who regulates these markets. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission said on October 27 that it had sued Utah to block state enforcement against federally regulated markets, arguing those actions obstruct Congress’s preemption of state law for CFTC-registered exchanges. Prospect launches NBA event contracts on its U.S. platform: TradingView

Prospect Prediction Markets Inc. said NBA event contracts are now available to eligible customers on its U.S. platform, two weeks after that platform’s U.S. launch and one week after college football contracts. Each NBA game page puts live prices and order flow beside box scores, team statistics, play-by-play, injury reports and schedules. Co-founder and chief technology officer Trevor Broad said basketball was among the most requested additions and that the data and the trading screen are built together. The company also added Google sign-in; identity checks and eligibility still apply before any trade. Customers are introduced to Foris DAX FCM, LLC, doing business as Crypto.com FCM, by Prospect Brokerage USA LLC, a CFTC-registered introducing broker and NFA member. The contracts are listed, priced and cleared by North American Derivatives Exchange, Inc., doing business as Crypto.com | Derivatives North America, a CFTC-regulated exchange and clearinghouse. The company said no league has endorsed the contracts, live data may be delayed or wrong, users must be 18 or older and U.S. residents, and the product is not available in every state. The company is listed as MKT and is based in Vancouver. Source: tradingview.com

How It Works: The Winners You Hear About: Survivorship Bias

Survivorship bias is the error of scoring a strategy, a fund, or a forecaster only on the records that are still in the sample. The failures drop out — delisted, shut, demoted, or simply no longer posting — and the average of what remains looks better than the average of everyone who tried.

A 2026 study of India’s NIFTY Smallcap 250 makes the arithmetic concrete. The author rebuilt who was actually in the index, using market-capitalization ranking from price-and-volume data, including delisted names in the exchange’s bhavcopy files, across 1,437 stocks and nine years, 2016 through 2025. An equal-weight portfolio of only the stocks that are members now was then set beside an equal-weight portfolio of everyone who had been a member. Survivor-only backtesting overstated annual returns by 4.94 percentage points, which the paper calls a 23.3 percent overstatement, and overstated Sharpe ratios by 0.097, a 9.1 percent overstatement. Turnover over the window was 82.5 percent. Of that churn, 16.1 percent were delisted, 33.1 percent graduated out of the small-cap index, and 33.2 percent were demoted. All three groups contributed to the bias, so the distortion is not only bankruptcies. The reconstruction matched current members with 100 percent accuracy and was estimated at 85 to 90 percent accuracy for historical membership. That gap is the method’s own limit: a survivor-free test is only as complete as the list of who left.

A 2010 Review of Finance paper approached the same error from mutual funds, and it is described there as the first to calculate, test, and explain different definitions of survivorship bias in fund performance rather than a single headline gap. The survival-and-performance link was stronger for small funds. Non-survivors significantly underperformed. New funds did not significantly outperform, which cuts against the idea that the bias is just a parade of brilliant newcomers replacing the old book. The paper’s practical point is that the problem does not vanish because one famous database was cleaned: it remains where survivor-free data is unavailable, and some methods still require truncated samples. Which definition you use changes the measured bias, so two analysts can quote two different “survivorship penalties” from the same graveyard.

Read a public track record the way those studies read an index. A screenshot of forecasts that are still online, or a strategy page that shows only accounts still trading, is the current-constituent portfolio. The 4.94-point gap appeared because the leavers were put back in, not because the survivors’ returns were fake. Until the disappeared names are counted — shut funds, deleted accounts, markets a forecaster stopped publishing after a miss — the average is a description of who remained, not of the method. Nothing here is a verdict on any contract, venue, or trade. Source: arxiv.org
