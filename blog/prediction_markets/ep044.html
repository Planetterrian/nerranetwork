Arkansas sued Kalshi, seeking restitution, penalties and an injunction over sports bets.

By Patrick November 05, 2026 Ep 44 8 min read Listen to podcast

What You Need to Know: Arkansas sued Kalshi, seeking restitution, penalties and an injunction over sports bets. Attorney General Tim Griffin’s filing, reported November 4, treats sports event contracts as gambling under Arkansas law and asks a court to stop them. Elsewhere, Binance and Polymarket have asked Polygon governance to hard-fork hacked POL into recovery wallets, and the CFTC and USDA signed a data-sharing memorandum on agricultural markets.

Top Story

A day after other state cases against the exchanges were already on this show, Arkansas filed its own. Attorney General Tim Griffin’s lawsuit, reported November 4 by The Block, alleges Kalshi operated illegal gambling in Arkansas. Griffin said Kalshi had built a global business that lets users bet on the outcome of “almost anything,” and the state argues that putting money on sporting events is gambling in substance whatever the prediction-market label. Arkansas says Kalshi attracted users in the state without the licensing, oversight and restrictions imposed on gambling operators. The state is seeking restitution for losses, civil penalties and an injunction for alleged violations of Arkansas gambling law. Griffin added that Kalshi knows the platform can expose users to excessive betting, financial losses, depression and other mental-health harms. KARK reported that the same attorney general also sued gaming platform VGW. This is a filing, not a judgment. Source: en.bloomingbit.io

The Ecosystem

Binance, Polymarket seek Polygon hard fork to recover $6.8M in stolen crypto: TradingView

After Binance’s record $6.3 billion hack on October 29, and a separate incident in which Polymarket temporarily suspended trading after a third-party provider was compromised, the two firms have proposed a Polygon hard fork to recover stolen POL. A Wednesday governance proposal targets about 70.49 million POL, worth about $6.8 million, in five attacker-controlled addresses: 23.86 million POL, worth $2.31 million, across four addresses tied to the Polymarket hack, and 46.63 million POL, worth about $4.51 million, in one address tied to the Binance hack. The fork would remove those tokens at a predetermined block and transfer the same amounts to verified recovery wallets designated by the two companies, without changing POL’s total supply. Polygon block producers have already blocked transactions from the affected addresses; the proposal says that freeze is temporary and cannot return the funds. The plan needs governance approval and testing on the Amoy testnet. Discussion was scheduled for November 5, with no confirmed date for a mainnet hard fork. Source: tradingview.com

CFTC and USDA Sign MOU to Protect Integrity of American Agricultural Markets: CFTC

On November 4 the Commodity Futures Trading Commission and the U.S. Department of Agriculture announced a memorandum of understanding to safeguard the integrity of U.S. agricultural markets. The MOU is meant to let the agencies harmonize policies and practices, share information, increase access to risk-management tools, and monitor structural or potential systematic risks. Chairman Michael S. Selig said the partnership should strengthen protections for farmers, ranchers and producers through more efficient data sharing. Secretary Brooke Rollins said faster, more transparent sharing should help the agencies identify risks and improve responses to market events. Source: cftc.gov

The favorite on Kalshi won 479 of 487 midterm races so far.: Kalshi

Kalshi posted that the favorite on its markets won 479 of 487 midterm races so far, which it called 98 percent, forecast before a single vote was counted, and that the markets beat the polls and the models. A second post said candidates priced at 50 percent when polls opened won about 50 percent of the time, and that the same pattern held at the other prices on Kalshi. A third post said the markets forecast better than the models and that people know more than the experts. Those figures are the company’s claims about its own markets. Source: x.com

How It Works: Prediction Markets You Already Know: Futures Prices as Forecasts

A futures price is an agreement to exchange a commodity at a date that has not arrived. The research program treats that price as a forecast of the later spot: wait out the horizon, then ask whether today’s futures price was a systematically biased guess at the spot that printed. The object being tested is a later price, or the direction of that price, not a yes-or-no event that settles at a fixed payoff.

A 2009 neural-network study of crude oil walks through that test in days. The authors first built a benchmark from lagged, pre-processed spot prices and found a dynamic model of 13 lags the best short-term benchmark for direction. They then added pre-processed futures prices for one, two, three and four months to maturity, one maturity at a time and then all together. Directional accuracy was 78 percent one day ahead, 66 percent two days ahead and 53 percent three days ahead. In every experiment that included futures as an input, those prices held information on spot direction that the lagged spot alone did not.

An IMF working paper in 2011 scaled the same question to ten commodity futures and horizons out to two years. In-sample tests rejected efficient markets. Out of sample, the forecast from the futures market was hard to beat. Performance did not depend on the slope of the futures curve, against models that treat that slope as a signal, and it did not depend on whether prices were in an upswing or a downswing.

A 2013 paper in the Journal of Futures Markets split the record by group. Precious and base metals failed most tests of unbiasedness and were poor predictors of later price changes. Energy and agricultural futures fared much better. The authors found little evidence that liquidity explained the gap, and they documented a broad decline in the predictive content of commodity futures prices since the early 2000s.

A 2019 paper in Management Science separates fitting the futures curve from forecasting the spot. Pricing models can match the term structure of futures prices and its dynamics and still fail to generate accurate true distributions of spot prices. The authors conclude that reasonable expected spot curves need analysts’ forecasts, used alone or jointly with futures. Using both, rather than forecasts alone, does not change the short and medium expected spot curve by much, but long-term estimates differ significantly. Adding forecasts to futures, rather than using futures alone, likewise leaves the short and medium futures curve mostly intact and moves the long-term futures estimates.

Later work both extends the machinery and marks where an edge dies. A 2015 study explained the crude-oil futures curve with a dynamic Nelson-Siegel model and forecast it with a focused time-delay neural network on 24 years of prices, through recessions and crises. Forecasts one, three, six and twelve months ahead were significantly more accurate than the benchmark models, with the lowest errors across maturities. A 2024 paper trained a convolutional network on aerial images of winter hard red wheat and on cloud cover, as a proxy for yield, to forecast the wheat futures price 20 days ahead. It reports that positive alpha in a short window is possible when the algorithm and the data are distinctive, and that performance can deteriorate quickly once the imagery is easy to obtain or the strategy becomes crowded.

Read a futures price as a forecast of a later spot at a named horizon, and check whether that record has held out of sample. Do not read it as a probability on a contract that pays a fixed sum if a stated event occurs and nothing if it does not. Storage, interest and the shape of the curve can put the futures price far from today’s spot without making it a number between zero and one. Source: arxiv.org
