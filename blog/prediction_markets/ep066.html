Top Story

WellPlayed reported on November 27, 2026, that New York City Council data shows retail traders down 52.5 million U.S. dollars, in a piece that also notes Kalshi has exited Utah. The 52.5 million dollar figure is the council tally of retail results in that report. It is data the outlet attributes to the New York City Council, not a judgment from a court, and it arrives as Kalshi's time in Utah is already over. Source: well-played.com.au

On November 26, 2026, Wisevoter reported that the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission sent a prediction-market rule to the White House. As that report describes the step, it is a send to the White House in the federal process. Source: wisevoter.com

Better Markets Opposes CFTC Sports Event Contract Proposal: PlayPennsylvania

PlayPennsylvania reported on November 26, 2026, that Better Markets opposes the CFTC sports event contract proposal and is urging the agency to withdraw it. The objection is aimed at that proposal before the U.S. federal commodities regulator. Source: playpennsylvania.com

Kalshi, Polymarket asked to testify on deceptive marketing: Bushwick Daily

Speaker Menin has asked Kalshi and Polymarket to testify after a New York City Council inquiry found deceptive marketing aimed at young users, Bushwick Daily reported on November 27, 2026. The new step is the request that both venues appear, following that inquiry's finding on marketing. Source: bushwickdaily.com

The Kenya Capital Markets Sandbox is Considering a Prediction Markets Proposal: BitKE

BitKE reported on November 27, 2026, that Kenya's Capital Markets Sandbox is considering a prediction-markets proposal. The step, as reported, is consideration of a proposal, not a rule in force. Source: bitcoinke.io

Kalshi’s next big bet: ‘microinsurance’ for small businesses: Financial Times

The Financial Times reported on November 27, 2026, that Kalshi's next product push is microinsurance for small businesses. The report describes that direction; it does not describe the product as open for trading. Source: ft.com

How It Works: How Far Ahead Can Anyone See? Accuracy and the Forecast Horizon

A forecast horizon is the lead time at which a forecast stops beating a simple baseline. In weather, that baseline is a climatological distribution. In finance, it is often a null of no predictability. The horizon is not one number for every question. It moves with the variable, with whether the target is a single point or an average, and with how strictly the score treats uncertainty.

The Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society assessed ECMWF monthly ensemble forecasts and defined the forecast skill horizon as the lead time when the ensemble ceases to be more skilful than a climatological distribution, using the continuous ranked probability score. On 32-day forecasts, averaged over one year, that horizon for instantaneous grid-point fields sits between 16 and 23 days. Fields averaged in time or across space stay skilful considerably longer. The paper says horizons longer than the two weeks once treated as the limit are now achievable because forecasts are probabilistic, ensembles estimate a distribution, models include more components, including a dynamical ocean and ocean waves, and the runs start from more accurate initial conditions.

A Bayesian latent-factor study in the Review of Economics and Statistics looks at the far end of the clock. It models GDP per capita for 113 countries over the 118 years from 1900 to 2017. Convergence rates differ sharply. Some are so slow that a country might not converge, or diverge, inside a century-long sample, and the correlation pattern is sparse — what the paper calls convergence clubs. The joint model yields a predictive distribution for output paths over the next 100 years, the sort of distribution used in simulations of the costs of climate change. Pooling information across countries tightens prediction intervals relative to univariate information sets. Even then, the 100-year growth paths exhibit very wide uncertainty.

Long-horizon regressions in finance hit a related measurement problem. A 2011 paper in the Journal of Applied Econometrics revisits Hodrick's 1992 reverse regression, which runs short-horizon returns on the long-run mean of a predictor. That older device can test only a null of no predictability, and it assumes stationary regressors. The 2011 paper argues that reverse regressions still avoid serious size distortions when there is some predictability and when regressors are near unit roots, and that the method is practical when there are many predictors. Applied to excess bond returns and the term structure of forward rates, the authors find some return forecastability. Confidence intervals for the coefficients are about twice as wide as those from the conventional approach.

Water Resources Research separates two horizons for a single water-supply reservoir. The longest forecast horizon is how far a forecast must reach to set an optimal release for the current stage if forecast uncertainty is ignored. The effective forecast horizon is the reach that still supplies as much decision information as a prescribed reliability allows. That reliability is an error bound: the largest difference between optimal releases under any two inflow scenarios. The longest horizon is longer with a larger capacity, smaller inflow variability, and smaller forecast uncertainty. The effective horizon is longer with smaller forecast uncertainty, a larger allowable error bound, and a more accurate ending-storage estimate.

A 1993 paper in Science is the counterweight to a blanket claim that long leads are empty. Tropical Pacific sea-surface temperature was predicted first, at long lead times. Those ocean forecasts then forced an atmospheric general circulation model. Forecasts of seven large climatic events of the 1970s to the 1990s agreed well with observations over many regions. Skill at long range showed up when a slower piece was forecast on its own and the rest was conditional on it.

A listener reading a price on an event that resolves years from now can use that split. Clean skill against climatology, for raw grid-point weather fields, is mostly a matter of weeks in the ensemble evidence, while averages last longer. A century-ahead growth distribution can be tightened by pooling and still be very wide. A contract price is one point standing in for a distribution that this research says gets harder to pin down as the resolution date recedes. It is a price with a horizon attached, not a demonstration that the far question has been solved. Source: doi.org Source: doi.org Source: doi.org Source: doi.org Source: doi.org
