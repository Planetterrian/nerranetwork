TSMC expects A16 volume production in 2027, a year ahead of Intel's 14A.

By Patrick November 04, 2026 Ep 44 8 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents

What You Need to Know: TSMC expects A16 volume production in 2027, a year ahead of Intel's planned 14A high-volume ramp. The node is the company's first with Super Power Rail backside power delivery, and it is not using High-NA EUV to get there. AMD's data-center revenue nearly doubled in the same stretch, on Instinct MI450 and EPYC.

Top Story

Startup Fortune reports that TSMC expects its A16 process, a 1.6-nanometer-class node aimed at high-performance computing with complex signal routing and dense power delivery, to be ready in 2026, with customer product ramps pointing to volume production in 2027. Compared with TSMC's N2P, the company puts A16 at 8 to 10 percent faster performance at the same power, or 15 to 20 percent less power at the same speed, plus roughly 8 to 10 percent higher transistor density. A16 is TSMC's first process to use Super Power Rail, which moves the power-delivery network to the back of the wafer instead of routing it with the signal wires on the front. The report says TSMC is reaching that node without ASML's High-NA EUV tools, which run north of 350 million euros each; at an earlier briefing, Zhang's line was "I like the technology but I don't like the sticker price." On the roadmaps in that account, A16 volume would begin about a year before Intel's planned 14A ramp — Lip-Bu Tan has said the company is fully committed to high-volume 14A production in 2028, with risk production for internal chips in the second half of 2027 — and about two years before Samsung's SF1.4 mass-production target of 2029, two years later than the target Samsung announced in 2022. TSMC says it plans to more than double annual N2-family capacity in 2027 for smartphone and HPC demand; supply-chain reporting indicates much of the initial N2 capacity has been heavily booked. Source: startupfortune.com

Silicon

AMD Reports Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results: AMD

AMD's third-quarter release put revenue at a record $13.4 billion, up 45 percent year over year, with GAAP gross margin of 54 percent, operating income of $2.8 billion, net income of $2.4 billion and diluted earnings per share of $1.46; non-GAAP figures were a 56 percent gross margin, $3.9 billion of operating income, $3.5 billion of net income and $2.09 of diluted earnings per share, the last up 74 percent year over year. Data Center revenue was a record $8.5 billion, up 97 percent year over year, on EPYC processors and the launch of Instinct MI450 Series GPUs. Client and Gaming revenue was $3.7 billion, down 8 percent: client revenue was $2.9 billion, up 7 percent on Ryzen mobile, while gaming revenue was $773 million, down 40 percent on lower semi-custom and graphics sales; Embedded revenue was $1.1 billion, up 29 percent. Lisa Su said demand for Instinct and EPYC kept strengthening and that growth should accelerate in the fourth quarter as AMD scales Helios and Venice production. Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Oracle Cloud Infrastructure are running workloads on the first Helios rack-scale systems, with MI450 GPUs and 6th Gen EPYC CPUs; HPE said Vultr will deploy the first Helios by HPE systems, and OCI said its Compute E7 instances will use 6th Gen EPYC. AMD also announced an agreement to acquire Taalas for specialized inference silicon, and the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking selected Instinct MI430X GPUs and 6th Gen EPYC processors for the LUMI-AI supercomputer. Source: ir.amd.com

Intel Releases 0.9 PDK for 14A Node, Final Version in Q1 2027: TechPowerUp

In late October Intel released process design kit version 0.9 for the 14A node. Lip-Bu Tan called that kit the "holy grail" because customers can evaluate the node and start trial designs that can move into production with PDK 1.0, which Intel expects in the first quarter of 2027. The company has processed tens of thousands of 14A wafers in pilot runs and is working yield and minor node adjustments. Against 18A, 14A is designed for a 15 to 20 percent performance gain at the same power, or a 25 to 35 percent power cut at the same performance, with up to a 30 percent density improvement; TechPowerUp notes it is unclear whether that density figure is logic or SRAM. Source: techpowerup.com

ARIA’s Scaling Inference Lab Deploys Lumai’s Optical Compute Tech: HPCwire

HPCwire reports that the UK's Advanced Research and Invention Agency will deploy 3D optical inference technology from Oxford-based Lumai in ARIA's Scaling Inference Lab. Lumai says it uses light to perform AI inference to lower data-center power consumption by 90 percent and unlock 50 times higher processing speeds. Source: hpcwire.com

Data Centres & Power

TerraVolt acquires site in Idaho to develop 240MW gas powered behind-the-meter data center campus: Data Center Dynamics

TerraVolt has announced a 240 megawatt gas-powered behind-the-meter data-center campus after acquiring 1,077 acres in Cassia County, Idaho. The project is a development plan on a newly acquired site, not an energized hall. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Samsung SDS breaks ground on Gumi AI Data Center in South Korea: Data Center Dynamics

Samsung SDS has a Gumi AI data center under construction in South Korea. Data Center Dynamics says the site will eventually offer 120 megawatts. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

California Resources and Bloom Energy eye 1GW of fuel cells across California to power data centers and other offtakers: Data Center Dynamics

California Resources and Bloom Energy are eyeing 1 gigawatt of fuel cells across California to power data centers and other offtakers. They intend to couple the systems with carbon capture and storage. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Supply Chain & Policy

Bosch Establishes Dedicated Semiconductor Division to Strengthen Chip Business: Semiconductor Digest

Effective January 1, 2028, Bosch will transfer its semiconductor operations into a new division called Semiconductors, still assigned to the Mobility business sector. Those operations now sit inside the Mobility Electronics division. Source: semiconductor-digest.com

Is There Room for Small-Scale OSATs in India’s Chip Packaging Boom?: EE Times

Small outsourced assembly and test firms in India can start with 1.5 to 2 billion rupees, about $15.8 to $21 million, consultant Nitin Dodia said. He said anchor customers, skilled staff and qualification decide who survives. Source: eetimes.com

ACM Research Announces Shipment of Its First 310 × 310 mm Ultra ECP ap-p Panel-Level Horizontal Electroplating Production Tool: Semiconductor Digest

ACM Research has shipped its first 310 by 310 millimetre Ultra ECP ap-p panel-level horizontal electroplating production system to a customer in Asia outside mainland China. Source: semiconductor-digest.com

Also Today

Setara Launches Daily Index of On-Demand GPU List Prices: HPCwire

Setara Financial launched the Setara Index, a daily index of on-demand GPU list prices and the first of a planned family of compute benchmarks. Source: hpcwire.com

Tower Semiconductor and Innolight Advance High-Performance Near-Package Optics for AI Infrastructure: Semiconductor Digest

Tower and Innolight are using Tower's 200-millimetre and 300-millimetre silicon photonics platforms for multiple generations of near-packaged optics aimed at AI infrastructure. Source: semiconductor-digest.com

TSMC's acquisition of AUO plant reshapes panel supply chain: digitimes

Digitimes reports that TSMC's acquisition of an AUO plant is reshaping the panel supply chain. Source: digitimes.com

The Teardown: The Rack That Boils at the Chip

A two-phase direct-to-chip loop picks heat up at the processor by letting liquid boil against a cold plate, then moves that vapor to a condenser, where it gives the heat up and returns as liquid. The phase change is the point. Sensible cooling has to raise the temperature of a flowing liquid to carry energy away. Boiling spends the same energy on latent heat, so a smaller mass of fluid can move a larger load, and the cold plate can sit closer to the silicon than a room full of air ever will. The condenser is still mandatory. Orbit or a data hall, the heat has not left the building until something on the other side of the loop rejects it. In a rack, that something is a coolant distribution unit tied to the facility water or air the building already has.

HPCwire's account of Accelsius's NeuCool IR150 puts that loop in one frame: a fully integrated IT rack plus CDU, which Accelsius calls the industry's first of that kind, offered as shared two-phase infrastructure rather than a chip demo on a bench. The company is a member of the NVIDIA Inception program. It is giving the rack to Villanova University for the National Science Foundation Center for Energy-Smart Electronic Systems, an industry-university cooperative research center. Villanova is one of three university sites in ES2. Accelsius already supports a separate COOLERCHIPS project at the University of Texas at Arlington, led by Dereje Agonafer. Villanova's Laboratory for Advanced Thermal and Fluid Systems is also on the NVIDIA-led OMNICOOL project under ARPA-E's COOLERCHIPS program, and on work with ES2 industry members. Google, Microsoft and Meta are among the industry partners backing ES2.

The worked target is the center's, not a measured result from this donation. ES2's stated objective is to improve the energy efficiency of U.S. data centers by 20 to 35 percent. By the center's own estimate, that is the equivalent of removing one to two power plants every three years. Read that as a program goal attached to a percent band, not as watts this rack has already saved. The IR150 is the instrument for the percent, not the proof of it. Richard Bonner, Accelsius's chief technology officer, framed the gift as research published with the manufacturers building AI infrastructure, not as a finished efficiency claim. Alfonso Ortega, ES2 site director at Villanova, said the donation gives researchers a rack-level view of two-phase direct-to-chip against rising thermal demand. Visitors are invited to the onsite lab. The article's install expectation, early October 2026, sits before the November 3 announcement date, so the useful fact is the partnership and the hardware class, not a calendar you can schedule against.

The integrated two-phase rack stops being the right install when the hall can hold junction temperature with the air or single-phase loop it already runs. The IR150 exists to put a boiling cold plate and a CDU in the same frame so a lab can see the rack, not the chip, as the thermal object. If the cabinet never approaches the load that forced ES2's 20 to 35 percent problem, that frame is a research tool parked in a room that did not need a phase change. Source: hpcwire.com
