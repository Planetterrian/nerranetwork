SK Telecom's five-gigawatt aim now depends on a 345-kilovolt equipment plan.

By Patrick November 16, 2026 Ep 56 11 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top Story Silicon Data Centres & Power Supply Chain & Policy The Teardown: The Needle Count on the Probe Card On the Horizon

What You Need to Know: SK Telecom's five-gigawatt aim for 2029 now depends on a 345-kilovolt equipment plan with Hyosung Heavy Industries, set down as a memorandum in Seoul on November 16 rather than as a placed transformer order. The carrier's roadmap runs on to 15 gigawatts by 2035, and the partners will also trial 800-volt direct-current distribution closer to the servers. The same day, SK hynix's quarterly filing showed the United States taking 62.5 percent of its year-to-date sales, with Nvidia-tied revenue up 101.5 percent.

Top Story

SK Telecom and Hyosung Heavy Industries signed a strategic partnership memorandum on November 16 at SKT Tower in Jung-gu, Seoul, pairing the carrier's AI data-centre development and operations with Hyosung's power-equipment manufacturing. The first step is a stable supply of core parts for 345-kilovolt substations, including transformers whose long lead times can hold up an entire commissioning schedule, so the two companies will line up production and delivery plans in advance. They will also review and demonstrate 800-volt direct-current distribution, carrying direct current nearer the servers and cutting conversion steps. SK Telecom's stated capacity plan is 5 gigawatts by 2029 and 15 gigawatts by 2035; the document is a memorandum of understanding, not a construction start and not an energized site. Once halls are running, they intend to share operations and maintenance experience on ultra-high-voltage substations and to look jointly at new data-centre business models. Jung Jae-heon, SK Telecom's chief executive, described the tie-up as a way to secure the power gear that starts an AI data centre; Woo Tae-hee, Hyosung's chief executive, described it as joining that equipment with SK Telecom's operating record, including a hoped-for extension across SK Group. Source: finance.biggo.com

Silicon

SK hynix Bets on HBM4 Demand Beyond NVIDIA With New Equipment Orders: TechPowerUp

Korea's Electronic Times, as reported by TechPowerUp, says SK hynix is in talks with equipment suppliers on a fresh round of HBM4 orders covering at least 20,000 wafers a month, mostly back-end tools for bonding, dicing, and inspection. Suppliers are aiming to have the tools installed in the first half of 2027, with the likely site P&T6 in Cheongju, a former fab the company picked for HBM in 2025. Electronic Times put SK hynix's total HBM capacity at about 150,000 wafers a month at the end of last year, and had already floated a similar 20,000-wafer figure for P&T6 in February. The report treats demand from AMD and Broadcom as strong enough to carry orders even if Nvidia's AI business is hit, and says Nvidia's cut in the HBM4 speed for Vera Rubin, from 11.7 gigabits per second per pin to 9.6, eases the manufacturing burden and likely figured in the decision. Source: techpowerup.com

Intel Razor Lake Gets a 12 P-Core bLLC Chip With No E-Cores, Per Leak: TechPowerUp

Beyond model identifiers already on Intel's books, leaker Jaykihn describes an unlocked Razor Lake desktop part as 12+0+4: 12 performance cores, no regular efficiency cores, four low-power efficiency cores, and Intel's big last-level cache. He says those 12 performance cores sit on a performance-only compute tile built on Intel's own 18A-P node, and that this part replaces a locked 8+0+4 big-cache chip rumored earlier. He also says the 125-watt 6+12+4 big-cache chip, one of two 22-core parts that leaked in July as Nova Lake-S, has been pushed from Nova Lake to Razor Lake, which would leave Nova Lake-S with one fewer big-cache option. Razor Lake-S is described as a 2028 product on the same LGA-1954 socket as Nova Lake-S; none of these specifications are official, and Jaykihn frames the 12-core chip as a target. Source: techpowerup.com

Intel reclaims overall CPU market share for the first time since 2025: Tom's Hardware

Mercury Research, as reported by Tom's Hardware, says Intel reclaimed overall CPU market share for the first time since 2025, with desktop gains offsetting AMD's record server and mobile growth. AMD continued to gain server CPU share as data-centre processor shipments rose sharply, while sales of client CPUs dropped in the third quarter. Source: tomshardware.com

LineShine Supports Gordon Bell Finalist Work on Silicon and Battery Materials: HPCwire

HPCwire reports that Shenzhen's domestically developed LineShine supercomputers, which topped both the June 2026 TOP500 and HPCG lists, are supporting Gordon Bell finalist work on silicon and battery materials. The compute software, XLSDFT, was developed independently by a team including Associate Researcher Qimen Xu and Associate Researcher Yu Zhang. Source: hpcwire.com

Data Centres & Power

AWS breaks ground on $7bn data center in Indiana: Data Center Dynamics

Under construction in Indiana: AWS has broken ground on a 7-billion-dollar data centre. The same report says AWS also purchased land in Chantilly, Virginia, for a data centre, a land buy rather than a second groundbreaking. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Airsys invests in digital boiler startup ThermAI: Data Center Dynamics

Cooling company Airsys is investing in UK startup ThermAI and supplying it with spray-cooling technology for what Data Center Dynamics calls a digital boiler. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Why Greenpeace Interrupted Jensen Huang's Keynote: Data Center Knowledge

At Nvidia GTC Berlin, at least two Greenpeace protesters unfurled a banner and projected "Clean Up Your Dirty Business," and shouted about Nvidia technology reportedly found in Russian drones and about data-centre environmental effects. Then-CEO Jensen Huang, who resigned on November 12, asked security not to overreact and said, "Everybody has a chance to say something. I'll talk to you later. But let me get through my keynote." Data Center Knowledge's interview with Benjamin Stephan says the activists were not arrested, were banned from re-entering, and left; Stephan waited by Huang's car, and security had the car drive off empty. Greenpeace East Asia had earlier given Huang a five-layer cake in Taipei using his own metaphor, and he replied positively when asked about investing in renewable energy with Taiwan suppliers; Berlin was meant to hold him to that, because nothing had happened yet. Stephan's published statement said communities face damage to water supplies and soil and rising electricity bills from data-centre expansion, and he told the magazine Nvidia should audit distributors, subsidiaries, and third-party vendors so chips are not diverted into military equipment, while arguing the supply chain still runs largely on fossil fuels even as Nvidia helps customers finance more halls. In the 2026 Sustainability Report, Huang wrote that Nvidia matched 100 percent of global electricity consumption with clean energy for a second consecutive year. Source: datacenterknowledge.com

Supply Chain & Policy

SK hynix U.S. sales surge 102 trillion won on AI boom, Nvidia revenue doubles: Chosunbiz

In the quarterly report SK hynix filed on November 16, cumulative sales for the first three quarters were 236.5105 trillion won, of which the United States was 62.5 percent, or 147.7392 trillion won, against 45.1802 trillion won in the year-earlier period. Sales tied to Nvidia were estimated at 34.9778 trillion won, 14.8 percent of the total, up 101.5 percent from 17.3551 trillion won. A second customer at 10 percent or more was estimated at 23.8509 trillion won, 10.1 percent, and the article says the industry believes that buyer is likely Microsoft or Google. China sales were 56.1378 trillion won, 23.7 percent, about 4.8 times the year-earlier 11.7489 trillion won, with the company described as concentrating HBM and server memory on the United States and selling mobile LPDDR and NAND flash primarily in China. Citing last month's earnings release, the article says SK hynix had ramped mass production and shipments of HBM4, that server DDR5 sales volumes rose more than 40 percent from the previous quarter, and that production volumes of both DRAM and HBM through 2029 have been fully secured under sales contracts. The products named in that US flow include fifth-generation HBM3E and sixth-generation HBM4. Source: biz.chosun.com

Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron Fail to Get DRAM Antitrust Suit Dismissed: TechPowerUp

On November 14, U.S. District Judge Noël Wise of the Northern District of California denied a joint motion to dismiss by Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron, finding a complaint plausibly alleges an agreement in violation of Section 1 of the Sherman Act. The suit, filed in June by 17 consumers and small businesses, claims the three — described as holding over 91 percent of global DRAM revenue as of the third quarter of 2025 — coordinated production cuts starting in late 2022 and shifted capacity toward HBM while conventional DRAM prices rose 697 percent over 18 months. The judge pointed to newer alleged conduct, including long-term HBM contracts, Samsung and SK hynix allegedly refusing long-term deals with conventional DRAM buyers, Micron shutting down Crucial, and near-identical customer-vetting questions all three adopted in January 2026, and wrote that the alleged conduct is more like "hawks flying in formation." It is a pleading-stage ruling; nothing has been proven. The companies argued the case reran a suit over 2016-to-2018 supply cuts that the Ninth Circuit threw out in 2022. Source: techpowerup.com

NVIDIA Taiwan Executive Indicted Over Smuggling of 130 AI Servers; Reportedly Sought Help From Taiwanese Officials and TSMC Without Success: BigGo Finance

BigGo Finance reports that an Nvidia Taiwan executive has been indicted over the smuggling of 130 AI servers, and that the executive reportedly sought help from Taiwanese officials and from TSMC without success. Source: finance.biggo.com

Taiwan's Xintec Wins TSMC CoWoS Back-End Outsourcing Deal, Bets NT$15 Billion Convertible Bond on 2029 Mass Production: BigGo Finance

With advanced packaging already the tight step on accelerator output, TSMC has signed a seven-year advanced-packaging agreement with Xintec that runs through 2033. Xintec is backing the CoWoS back-end outsourcing win with a 15-billion-New-Taiwan-dollar convertible bond, and is aiming at mass production in 2029. Source: finance.biggo.com

The Teardown: The Needle Count on the Probe Card

A probe card is the last removable interconnect a wafer sees before dice are committed to a package. It holds a bed of probes that land on pads or microbumps, feed power and test patterns, and build the map of which dice are worth stacking. A larger logic die, a taller high-bandwidth memory stack, or a 2.5D or 3D assembly adds pads, contact force, and pattern length at that step, so the card gets more expensive and the touchdown takes longer even when the number of wafers does not. Yahoo Finance, tracing where investors might look once a single GPU vendor no longer anchors the hardware story, puts FormFactor on that test chokepoint and ACM Research on the plating tools that build the package the card has to qualify.

FormFactor's reported split is the worked example. Probe cards account for about 801.9 million dollars. Systems, the testers and handlers around those cards, account for about 178.2 million dollars. The interface that wears out against the wafer is the larger line. The analyst-consensus narrative in that piece says FormFactor's high-bandwidth-memory probe-card revenue guidance for the first half of 2026 sits more than 50 percent above the first half of 2025, and reads that gap as higher average selling prices and a larger test footprint, not merely as more units. On the tool side, ACM Research is credited with about 1.14 billion dollars of semiconductor equipment and services revenue, virtually all of it from mainland China, with production and evaluation orders already booked for panel-level electroplating and with advanced-packaging revenue growth described as triple-digit. The wafer engine those cards and tools serve is Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing's foundry segment, about 4.94 trillion New Taiwan dollars. An investor quoted in the piece, under the name steingar, frames the load as advanced logic demand that scales super-linearly with compute complexity rather than with unit volumes. Set against the FormFactor split, that is a test-time claim: if each package adds bumps and stacks, touchdowns and card prices rise faster than wafer starts, which is why a half-on-half jump of more than 50 percent in high-bandwidth-memory card guidance can outrun a matching jump in starts.

The same electrical idea explains why the card, not the handler frame, is where the money sits. Each high-bandwidth memory stack is a separate electrical citizen until a probe has touched it and the tester has decided it can be bonded. If probes cannot resolve contacts one from another, the card can still power the die and still lose the map of which contact is in trouble. Yahoo Finance does not publish needle counts or touchdown force. The dollars it does publish rank the steps. Probe cards at 801.9 million dollars against systems at 178.2 million dollars say the consumable interface is FormFactor's centre of gravity. A guidance line more than 50 percent higher for one half of high-bandwidth-memory cards says the mix inside that interface is shifting toward the harder wafers. Triple-digit advanced-packaging growth at ACM, on a 1.14 billion dollar base that is virtually all mainland China, says plating and panel work are scaling alongside the test step. The 4.94 trillion New Taiwan dollar foundry figure is the wafer volume both steps are trying to qualify and to build.

That choice — treating the probe card as the binding gate on an AI wafer — stops holding when the first-half 2026 card guidance, more than 50 percent above the year-earlier half, fails to print because packages stop adding bumps and stacks. Then the 801.9 million dollar probe-card line is no longer the scarce step, and the limit moves to the plating book, ACM's 1.14 billion dollars almost entirely in mainland China, or to the 4.94 trillion New Taiwan dollar foundry engine those cards were only qualifying. If card revenue goes flat while plating orders stay triple-digit, the qualification gate has loosened even as the package factory keeps adding tools. Source: finance.yahoo.com

On the Horizon

  • First half of 2027: equipment suppliers aim to have SK hynix's added HBM back-end tools installed at the Cheongju site now in procurement talks.
  • 2028: Razor Lake-S is described by the leaker as a 2028 product on LGA-1954; Intel has not made the 12-performance-core big-cache part official.
  • 2029: SK Telecom's roadmap year for 5 gigawatts, Xintec's mass-production target for the CoWoS back-end work, and the end of the window in which SK hynix says DRAM and HBM volumes are already under sales contracts.
  • 2033 and 2035: the Xintec advanced-packaging agreement runs through 2033; SK Telecom's capacity plan reaches 15 gigawatts in 2035.

Each high-bandwidth memory stack stays a separate electrical citizen until a probe has touched it. If the probes cannot resolve contacts one from another, the card can still power the die and still lose the map of which contact is in trouble. Yahoo Finance publishes no needle counts and no touchdown force.
