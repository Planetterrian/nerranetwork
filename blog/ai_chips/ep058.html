More than 3.5 times the substrate capacity for Nvidia starts in 2027, binding six manufacturers to the ramp.

By Patrick November 18, 2026 Ep 58 10 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Listen to this episode

What You Need to Know: Kinsus will expand advanced ABF package-substrate capacity dedicated to Nvidia by more than 3.5 times at its Youth campus in Taoyuan, with production expected to begin in the second half of 2027. The same set of commitments pulls in Amkor, Foxconn, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, SPIL and TSMC across substrates, packaging, wafers and system assembly. Vera Rubin NVL72 and Vera CPUs began volume shipments in the third quarter on TSMC’s 5nm, 3nm and CoWoS.

Top Story

Nvidia is locking manufacturing capacity to demand for its accelerated-computing platforms through expanded agreements with Amkor Technology, Foxconn, Kinsus Interconnect Technology, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Siliconware Precision Industries (SPIL) and TSMC, covering substrates, advanced packaging, wafer manufacturing, contract manufacturing and system assembly. Kinsus plans to expand advanced ABF package-substrate capacity dedicated to Nvidia by more than 3.5 times at its Youth campus in Taoyuan, Taiwan, with production expected to begin in the second half of 2027. Nvidia is backing Amkor’s advanced-packaging expansion in Arizona and South Korea through multiyear agreements announced in July and October 2026, totalling nearly $3 billion and including prepayments. In Taiwan, a multiyear SPIL agreement backed by multibillion-dollar funding covers bumping, interposer wafer fabrication, assembly and testing; Digitimes reports that deal is set to triple advanced packaging capacity by 2029. Samsung Electro-Mechanics amended an existing substrate agreement to improve factory operations in South Korea, building on work in Sejong and Busan for computing and high-speed networking, while Foxconn is extending collaboration on next-generation GPU and networking platforms across Asia, North America and Europe. Nvidia said Vera Rubin NVL72 and Vera CPUs began volume shipments in the third quarter, using TSMC’s 5nm, 3nm and CoWoS advanced packaging. Source: yourstory.com · digitimes.com

Silicon

Vera Rubin Rack Power: Penguin Solutions Launch Gives No kW Figure, Lenovo Lists 227 kW: Tech Times

Lenovo lists a Vera Rubin rack at 227 kW. Tech Times set that figure against Penguin Solutions’ Vera Rubin rack launch. Source: techtimes.com

Taiwan Mask expands 14-inch capacity to capture CoWoS and photonics growth: digitimes

Taiwan Mask, a subsidiary of Star Fusion Group, is expanding 14-inch capacity while overhauling its product mix and expanding overseas. Digitimes said the push is aimed at demand across advanced packaging, including CoWoS, silicon photonics and advanced-node photomasks. Source: digitimes.com

QUDORA Adds NVIDIA CUDA-Q Support to Its Qamelion Quantum Emulator: HPCwire

QUDORA Technologies, founded in 2021 and based in Braunschweig, has integrated NVIDIA CUDA-Q into its SDK so developers can run hybrid programs on Qamelion, an emulator with adaptive noise modeling of the company’s upcoming trapped-ion hardware. Its Near Field Quantum Control technology uses integrated microwave electronics to control trapped-ion qubits in an architecture the company says is compatible with standard semiconductor manufacturing. The CUDA-Q integration is available now; QUDORA offers systems for on-premises installation and cloud access, and is expanding in Asia-Pacific through QUDORA Japan K.K. Source: hpcwire.com

Data Centres & Power

Taiwan completes Asia's largest single offshore wind project off central coast, connecting 1 GW of capacity to supply TSMC in 2027: digitimes

Taiwan’s Hai Long Offshore Wind Project is energized off the central coast: construction is complete and all 73 turbines are connected to the grid, for 1 GW of capacity. Digitimes said that capacity is to supply TSMC in 2027. Source: digitimes.com

Public Pushback Complicates Data Center Buildouts: Data Center Knowledge

Community resistance is forcing developers to reassess permits, tax benefits and utility arrangements, including in states that had courted the projects. In Pennsylvania, gubernatorial candidate Stacy Garrity moved from praising Amazon’s $20 billion data-center investment to backing a pause and stronger requirements, and Gov. Josh Shapiro moved from promoting faster permitting to supporting stricter environmental standards and transparency, Spotlight PA reported. Wisconsin Gov.-elect David Crowley backed a pause and elimination of the industry’s state sales-tax exemption, and after the election said he was considering resource-use disclosures, community-support requirements and protections against shifting energy costs onto residents. GlobalData found that projects breaking ground between the third quarter of 2025 and the third quarter of 2026 waited an average of just over two years from announcement to construction, up from about 18 months for projects launched between early 2020 and mid-2025. IDC’s Sreenivas Duvvuri Venkata said community acceptance now sits with power, interconnection and environmental permits as a condition of timely execution, and that conditions can include full utility-cost responsibility, renewable requirements, closed-loop cooling and limits to designated industrial sites. Alston Mason of Oldcastle Infrastructure said a site can have its power plan set and still be waiting on environmental permits for on-site generation equipment. Source: datacenterknowledge.com

$3.26 million in SpaceX AI data center revenue will be pumped back into the City of Memphis: Tom's Hardware

Memphis is making its first major outlay under the Community Benefit Ordinance, allocating $3,259,282 from tax revenue on SpaceX AI’s Colossus 1, which has been operating since May 2025. Of that, $2 million goes to the Memphis HOPE home-repair program, offering low- and moderate-income homeowners and renters up to $25,000 as a deferred, interest-free, forgivable loan with zero monthly payments, forgiven after three years, and $125,000 is set for cooling and air-quality equipment for residents. Source: tomshardware.com

Supply Chain & Policy

NVIDIA's Supply Chain Chief Deb Shoquist: Allocating TSMC Chips Weekly, Delivery Cycles Can Be Compressed to Two Weeks: BigGo Finance

Deb Shoquist, Nvidia’s executive vice president of operations, described a supplier screen of three conditions — ramp cadence that matches Nvidia, technical precision for complex designs, and the balance sheet to invest ahead — and said Nvidia will inject capital if a partner has only two of the three. The network she named runs through TSMC (a partner since 1998), Foxconn, Wistron, SPIL, KYEC (packaging and test partners for more than two decades), Unimicron, Amphenol, Coherent and Lumentum; she meets major suppliers quarterly and spends six weeks a year on the road in Taiwan, mainland China, South Korea, Vietnam and the United States. Optics partners had technology but not the factory scale Nvidia needed, which is why Nvidia entered investment partnerships with Coherent and Lumentum, and why co-packaged optics is being built as a new supply chain. Amphenol’s cable assembly has 1,500 branches, each assembled to keep the defect rate below one per million, on more than a hundred automated lines in Vietnam, and is now in full production. Wafers are allocated weekly after packaging: a plant with low output or a short holding period does not get more chips that week, and every die is traced from its identification number to the customer invoice. When required, the cycle from chips leaving TSMC to systems shipping from Foxconn and Wistron can be cut from five weeks to two. On one compute-board ramp, automated test-handling throughput came in below plan; the line was paused, manual loading was restored with as many as several hundred operators, and an internal effort called Swarm redesigned tray, hardware, software and yield issues with Foxconn’s automation engineers before scaling resumed. Source: finance.biggo.com

S’pore can facilitate US checks on AI chip use, won’t allow circumvention of export controls: PM Wong: The Straits Times

A day after the November 16 licensing rule, Prime Minister Lawrence Wong said on November 17 that Singapore can facilitate US checks on how advanced chips are deployed in the city-state, will not enforce another country’s export controls, and will take clear and firm action against firms that use Singapore to circumvent them. He said only a small volume of chips recorded as Singapore sales actually enter the country; 2025 statistics showed Nvidia reporting Singapore as 22 percent of global chip-sales revenue while only 1 percent of the chips were used there. The same day, Minister Josephine Teo and US Under Secretary Jeffrey Kessler agreed to follow up on practical arrangements for continued trusted access, which Singapore’s digital-development ministry confirmed on November 18, and Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong said Singapore will act against those who flout its laws. China’s Ministry of Commerce said on November 17 that the tighter controls run counter to the leaders’ consensus and that China will take countermeasures if the US persists; Zaobao reported that overseas data centres providing substantial computing capacity to companies headquartered in China must obtain a US export licence within 90 days or face further restrictions, with applications reviewed case by case. The Commerce rule requires a licence to export, re-export or transfer advanced semiconductors to Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil. Source: straitstimes.com · zaobao.com.sg · businesstoday.com.my

Board chairman Harvey Jones said on the third-quarter fiscal 2027 earnings call that the board unanimously accepted Jensen Huang’s resignation and does not intend to reinstate him as CEO. Source: techpowerup.com

Nvidia Introduced Alibaba to Alice Huang for Workaround on U.S. Export Controls: The Information

TSMC reportedly demands cash up front from AI chip clients amid circular financing fears: investingLive

investingLive reported that TSMC is demanding cash up front from AI chip clients amid fears of circular financing. Source: investinglive.com

The Teardown: The Film Between the Resin and the Mold

Release film is the sheet that keeps epoxy off the tool. In compression molding for IC packaging, hot resin is pressed around the die and the substrate. If that resin wets the mold, the next shot is contaminated and the demold can tear the package. BigGo Finance, reporting chairman Yang Yun-pin’s pre-listing presentation, describes Suregiant’s film as the layer that isolates epoxy from the mold under high temperature and high pressure so the part releases clean.

The process choice is how the film is made. Yang said Japanese peers mostly extrude film. Suregiant coats it, drawing on more than 25 years of precision coating and formulation, from an optoelectronics start that entered semiconductors in 2014. Coating is what lets a supplier cut a custom sample for one customer’s automated line. The line cares about three outcomes: non-stick sheeting, no adhesive residue, and a release force that matches the tool. An extruded film can post the same force number and still fail if the surface chemistry is not the one that mold, resin and cycle were qualified against. The sample, not a catalog roll, is the product.

The package stack changed the mold face the film has to leave clean. Suregiant’s release film entered mass production for 2.5D advanced packaging in 2024, was applied to panel-level fan-out in 2025, and is advancing to 3D packaging in 2026. A 2.5D compression is wide and relatively flat. Fan-out on a panel moves more resin across a larger area. A 3D stack adds height and more interfaces that can trap residue. Residue that would pass a lab peel test can still stop an automated visual check or contaminate the next shot. Suregiant says it is a qualified supplier to the world’s top five semiconductor manufacturers, with film already in mass production at first-tier advanced packaging. Order visibility runs 2 to 3 years, and customer forecasts reach 5 to 6 years, so the coating line is booked against a package roadmap, not a spot order.

Plant 1 is at full load. Taoyuan Plant 2 completed trial production and is expected to begin official production in December, at 2.5 to 3 times the scale of Plant 1, with a full year of contribution in 2027. Against that, the first three quarters of 2026 produced NT$333 million of revenue, about $10.1 million, NT$229 million of gross profit, NT$175 million of operating profit, NT$140 million of after-tax profit and earnings per share of NT$6.35. Third-quarter revenue was NT$159 million, up 80.76 percent quarter on quarter, and quarterly after-tax profit was NT$66.51 million, up 92.72 percent. Multiplying Plant 1 by 2.5 to 3 does not by itself multiply profit; Yang is counting on a rising share of high-margin advanced-packaging film inside that volume.

That coating film ceases to be the consumable that gates output when the critical step is no longer demolding a compressed package. Grinding tape, supplied to the world’s top ten packaging plants for more than 12 years, is the second product, and a thick single-adhesive-layer version went to first-tier verification in the fourth quarter of 2026 for taller bumps and thinner wafers. The third is hydrolyzable tape, meant to replace temporary bonding adhesives that need laser or ultraviolet debond, using a plant’s existing pure-water clean and skipping the chemical clean. Official samples are expected in mid-2027, with a gradual ramp in 2028. When the line is releasing a thinned, bonded wafer in pure water rather than peeling resin off a compression mold, the extrusion-versus-coating contest on release film no longer decides who can ship. Source: finance.biggo.com

The books for the first three quarters of twenty twenty-six: revenue of three hundred thirty-three million New Taiwan dollars, about ten point one million US dollars; gross profit two hundred twenty-nine million New Taiwan dollars; operating profit one hundred seventy-five million; after-tax profit one hundred forty million; earnings per share six point three five New Taiwan dollars. Third-quarter revenue was one hundred fifty-nine million New Taiwan dollars, up eighty point seven six percent from the prior quarter, and quarterly after-tax profit was sixty-six point five one million New Taiwan dollars, up ninety-two point seven two percent. Multiplying the floor space by two and a half to three does not multiply profit by itself. Yang is counting on a larger share of high-margin advanced-packaging film inside that volume. That coating sheet loses its place as the gate on shipments once the critical step is no longer peeling resin off a compression mold. Grinding tape, already sold to the world’s top ten packaging plants for more than twelve years, is the second product, and a thick single-adhesive-layer version went to first-tier verification in the fourth quarter of twenty twenty-six for taller bumps and thinner wafers. The third product is hydrolyzable tape, meant to replace temporary bonds that today need a laser or ultraviolet debond, using the plant’s existing pure-water clean and skipping the chemical bath. Official samples are expected in mid-twenty twenty-seven, with a gradual ramp in twenty twenty-eight. When the line is releasing a thinned, bonded wafer in pure water, the argument between extrusion and coating on release film no longer picks the supplier who can ship. On the horizon, Suregiant’s move onto the OTC market is expected in mid-December, at a tentative price of four hundred thirty-eight New Taiwan dollars a share.
