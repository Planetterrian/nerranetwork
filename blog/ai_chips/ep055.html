Thirty-four billion dollars of HBM packaging starts in June 2028, so today's shortage stays.

By Patrick November 15, 2026 Ep 55 6 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents

What You Need to Know: Samsung has broken ground on a thirty-four-billion-dollar high-bandwidth memory packaging expansion at Onyang that does not begin operations until June 2028, leaving the current shortage untouched by the spend. ATTO is showing a pre-release Ethernet DRAM appliance at SC26, and TSMC is formalizing a five-hundred-million-dollar fund for startups and suppliers on top of equity already aimed at its largest AI customers.

Top Story

Samsung Electronics has broken ground on a high-bandwidth memory packaging expansion at its Onyang complex in Asan, South Korea, with a planned investment of ₩46 trillion, about $34 billion, covering five advanced packaging lines. Of that, ₩6 trillion is facility construction and ₩40 trillion, about 87 percent, is equipment. The new building is a back-end packaging operation, not a DRAM wafer fab: finished HBM still needs qualified memory dies, packaging tools, and yield. The facility holds about 31,000 square meters of cleanroom across three floors and grows the campus floor area from 270,000 to 420,000 square meters, about 55.6 percent. The new line is scheduled to begin operations in June 2028, a year earlier than first planned; that date is the start of operations, not full volume. In the same reporting, SK hynix’s August 2026 plan puts about ₩54.3 trillion into two fab projects, ₩35.2 trillion at Yongin Y2 and ₩19.1 trillion at Cheongju M17, with the M17 cleanroom expected in December 2028 and Y2 in June 2029. Source: 247wallst.com

Silicon

ATTO to Demonstrate Pre-Release SiliconDisk Ethernet-Connected, DRAM-Based Storage at SC26: HPCwire

ATTO Technology will demonstrate a pre-release SiliconDisk at SC26 in Chicago, an Ethernet-connected DRAM storage appliance that presents working data as NVMe over Fabrics block storage so several compute nodes can share it. The unit being shown has four 100-gigabit Ethernet ports, up to 35 gigabytes per second of sustained aggregate bandwidth, and up to 6.4 million 4K IOPS, with access latency designed below 600 nanoseconds. It is aimed at AI and machine learning, databases, imaging, and clusters, in direct-attached or fabric-attached layouts. ATTO Infinite Write Endurance is meant to remove flash wear and garbage collection. The platform can run with zero data persistence when data need not survive power loss, with optional persistence when it must. Source: hpcwire.com

Local AI hardware, memory compression and export controls define a volatile week in chips: news.lavx.hu

AMD described MEXT, a predictive memory engine that aims to deliver 99 percent of server performance while using half the DRAM. In the configuration AMD described, the system retains 95 percent of server performance. It combines a transformer model with flash storage to predict and manage memory use. AMD runs 145 servers with the technology in place, more than 100 of them already, and plans to expand that figure to 2,000 servers in 2027. Source: news.lavx.hu

Fractile raises $720 million for inference memory hardware: Wowtale

Fractile, which is building memory hardware to break the memory bottleneck in AI inference, raised $720 million. Source: en.wowtale.net

Data Centres & Power

AirTrunk signs MoU for 1.8GW of data center capacity in Andhra Pradesh, India: Data Center Dynamics

AirTrunk has announced, via a memorandum of understanding, 1.8 gigawatts of data-center capacity in Andhra Pradesh, India, aimed at two projects outside Visakhapatnam. A memorandum is not a construction start and not an energized site. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Developer Westend Real Assets buys two data centers in Hamburg: Data Center Dynamics

Westend Real Assets bought two data centers in Hamburg from private equity firm Deutsche Beteiligungs. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Supply Chain & Policy

TSMC wants to do more than make chips, now it plans to build the next Nvidia: India Today

Beyond equity already directed at model labs, TSMC is launching a $500 million fund for chip startups and for suppliers of equipment, materials, chemicals, and intellectual property, a step it has not taken with suppliers before. The fund is larger than a roughly $200 million emerging fund launched in 2021. Kevin Zhang, senior vice president and deputy co-chief operating officer, told Nikkei Asia the company wants to formalize the mechanism and extend it past immediate customers. Counting that earlier fund, the new arm, and planned stakes of up to $500 million each in OpenAI and Anthropic, total commitment could reach $1.7 billion. Source: indiatoday.in

Malaysia plans new law using data center power consumption to catch AI GPU smugglers: Tom's Hardware

Malaysia plans a law that would track AI GPU imports and data-center power consumption to catch chip smuggling, and that would also force operators to pay for unused electricity capacity. The same report notes that real-world thermal-design-power variance and a lack of physical audits could undermine the check. Source: tomshardware.com

The Teardown: The Thread Count Left in the Weights

A model’s first numbers are not a smooth random field. On NVIDIA GPUs, PyTorch’s CUDA path fills those numbers with Philox, a counter-based generator, and it does the fill in parallel. Each thread owns a stream of values and writes them into fixed slots in the weight matrix. XenoSpectrum, reporting Will DePue’s Unemployment Labs write-up of November 12, 2026, walks through why the slot pattern is a hardware fingerprint: even with the same seed, a different total thread count stores the same random stream in different places.

For the large tensors in this check, PyTorch caps the launch at 2,048 threads per streaming multiprocessor. A Hopper GPU with 132 streaming multiprocessors therefore launches 270,336 threads. A B200 or B300, at 148 streaming multiprocessors, launches 303,104. A GB200 or GB300, at 152, launches 311,296. The stride between elements one thread handles is that total. Change the streaming-multiprocessor count and the arrangement inside the matrix changes, even though the generator and the seed did not.

Training then overwrites most of those values. Rows that never see a gradient can keep the initial bits. Rare tokens in an embedding layer are the usual survivors. Once a seed and a thread layout are inferred, the initial matrix can be regenerated and compared with the published weights, bit for bit when the match is exact. That is a test of the initialization configuration, not of every GPU that later ran gradient steps, and not of where the machine sat.

The worked case in the XenoSpectrum account is DeepSeek-V4-Flash against a 148-streaming-multiprocessor Blackwell layout. DePue reproduced 461 of 469 weight rows at a statistical significance of 1,385 sigma. Bojie Li, reimplementing the method without DePue’s code, reproduced 461 of 468 rows at 1,384 sigma. On a B300, 461 of 469 rows correlated at 0.99 or higher, and 445 of them matched bit for bit. On an H100, the same style of check reproduced 57,344 unupdated values of DeepSeek-V3 exactly. On a rented GB200, all 13 unupdated rows of Kimi K3 reproduced from the seed 20256586; shifting that seed by one found no matching rows. Across a search of about 6 trillion seed-and-layout combinations, chance matches were estimated to top out near 8 sigma, against detections of 49 to 1,385 sigma on the six reported models. Li’s GPU-type call matched DePue’s on all 65 models both examined.

The spacing test stops being the right instrument when the published file has no unupdated rows left to read, when initialization is not this Philox layout at 2,048 threads per streaming multiprocessor, or when the weights are closed. A later checkpoint that shares Kimi K3’s seed, as Kimi K3.1-Pro does, can also look like a fresh run when it is only more post-training on the same start. Source: xenospectrum.com
