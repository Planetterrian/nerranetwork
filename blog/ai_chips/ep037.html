Seventy-two-GPU Vera Rubin racks are live on Oracle Cloud at multi-gigawatt scale.

By Patrick October 28, 2026 Ep 37 11 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top Story Silicon Data Centres & Power Supply Chain & Policy Also Today The Teardown: A Molecule That Emits the Link On the Horizon

What You Need to Know: Seventy-two-GPU Vera Rubin NVL72 racks are generally available on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, and Oracle and NVIDIA say OCI Superclusters on that platform will expand to multi-gigawatt scale. NVIDIA Vera CPUs are generally available on the same cloud, with an x86 Rubin server still only on the plan. Memory makers, meanwhile, are booking capacity years out while a Chinese HBM3E line is still fighting yield.

Top Story

NVIDIA and Oracle say Vera Rubin NVL72 and NVIDIA Vera CPUs are generally available on Oracle Cloud Infrastructure as of 27 October 2026, and they plan to expand OCI Superclusters built on NVL72 to multi-gigawatt scale. That expansion is a plan, not an energized multi-gigawatt site. Each rack links 72 GPUs through NVLink; OCI Superclusters combine those racks with Oracle Acceleron networking, NVIDIA Spectrum-X Ethernet, Spectrum-6 switches and BlueField-4 DPUs, and gigawatt-class power and cooling, with OCI’s multiplanar network described as extending across hundreds of thousands of GPUs. The estate already includes GB200 and GB300 NVL72 systems. OCI also plans an x86 HGX Rubin NVL8 with eight Rubin GPUs on NVLink, two 64-core Intel Xeon 6 CPUs, 4TB of DDR5 and 6.4 Tbps of RDMA networking. Tom's Hardware reports this as NVIDIA’s first standalone CPU push at a hyperscaler — OCI’s other Arm instance has been Ampere Altra — and says NVIDIA has put Vera memory bandwidth at 1.2 terabytes per second on LPDDR5X-9600. In Tom's Hardware’s own Stream triad test, not run on OCI, that system more than doubled a dual Intel Xeon 6980P server using MRDIMMs at 8,800 MT/s. In Abilene, Texas, OpenAI has already trained and served on an OCI Supercluster with more than 100,000 NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs. Google Cloud announced general availability for Vera Rubin last week. Source: datacentremagazine.com

Source: tomshardware.com

Silicon

SK Hynix reports record quarter and books memory through 2029: EE Times

SK Hynix posted third-quarter revenue of 104.62 trillion won, about $78 billion, operating profit of 83.33 trillion won, about $62 billion, and an operating margin of 80 percent. Net profit was 39.78 trillion won, about $30 billion, and net cash expanded to 98.7 trillion won, about $74 billion. Counterpoint’s MS Hwang said operating profit beat a 77 trillion won consensus, about $57 billion, and that standard DRAM pricing has moved from 20 to 30 cents per gigabit to $2 per gigabit, with the global DRAM market at $200 billion on Counterpoint’s count. He argued HBM4 unit price could reach $4 per gigabit, and said October has resolved worries that SK Hynix could not supply HBM4 to NVIDIA and that HBM volume per chip might fall. TechPowerUp reports average selling prices in the quarter rose more than 20 percent from the prior quarter. Digitimes reports the company says customers want more than twice the HBM it can supply. Korea JoongAng Daily reports DRAM and HBM booked through 2029. The company expects demand to outpace supply through 2030, citing 10-nanometre-class sixth-generation DRAM and 321-layer NAND, and says long-term agreements with 14 tech giants cover more than half of capacity for 2027 and 2028. Counterpoint expects Samsung to lead HBM share from the HBM4 generation in 2027; SK Hynix says proprietary packaging and customer partnerships will keep its lead. Source: eetimes.com

CXMT to start HBM3E mass production by the end of 2026: TechPowerUp

Reuters reports CXMT is expected to start mass production of its first HBM, in HBM3E, within two months, by the end of 2026. Risk production began in August, and yields are still a problem. TechPowerUp says it does not have CXMT performance figures, and cites JEDEC’s HBM3E envelope instead: 1,024-bit width, 9.2 to 12.4 gigabits per second per pin, with most standard configurations at 9.6, about 1.2 terabytes per second, and 24 GB in an 8-high stack or 36 GB in a 12-high stack of 24-gigabit layers. Front-end yield is described at around 30 percent, with back-end processing adding about 70 percent on top of that 30 percent. The report says CXMT is attempting HBM3E with about 3,000 through-silicon vias per layer, against about 5,000 per layer Samsung has used on HBM2 and more than 8,000 SK hynix has used on HBM3, in an 8-high stack, and is unlikely to move to 12-high soon. Source: techpowerup.com

Intel's Lip-Bu Tan discusses possible AMD cooperation and foundry timing: TechPowerUp

At an October 24 fireside chat with Jimmy Cheng of the Chinese American Semiconductor Professional Association, Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan said he has spoken with AMD CEO Lisa Su about possible cooperation. AMD was earlier reported to be evaluating Intel’s 14A node. TechPowerUp reports 18A is in volume production, while 14A has its 0.5 process design kit out, with 0.9 next and 1.0 aimed at volume production early next year. Intel had targeted October for handing the 0.9 kit to outside customers; Tan’s stay-tuned line is read as meaning that handoff has not happened. He said Intel has to reclaim CPU leadership and will tailor chips such as Xeon to customers. He named ASICs, co-packaged optics, physical AI and advanced packaging as growth areas, and wants two or three of them to reach about 10 percent of Intel revenue each. Source: techpowerup.com

Nebul says it will deploy more than 1,000 Blackwell Ultra GPUs in Belgium. That is a deployment plan, not a figure for site power. Source: techzine.eu

US Signal acquires a Hillsboro data center previously occupied by Oracle: Data Center Dynamics

US Signal has acquired a data center in Hillsboro, Oregon, previously occupied by Oracle. The report does not treat the deal as a new build. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Enphase and Infineon deploy 900V GaN switches in solid-state transformers: Data Center Dynamics

Enphase and Infineon have deployed 900-volt gallium nitride switches in solid-state transformers designed for AI data centers, and they call the deployment a world first. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

CoreWeave expands its global AI network to the Middle East and Asia Pacific: CoreWeave

CoreWeave says its Global AI Network backbone runs at petabit scale, with MACsec encryption in hardware at line rate across the backbone and inside its data centers. New points of presence in the UAE, India, Indonesia, Singapore, Australia, South Korea and Japan are coming online beginning in the fourth quarter, taking the network to four continents. The company compares that capacity to streaming a 4K movie to 66 million people at once. Source: x.com

Supply Chain & Policy

TSMC is reported preparing a CoWoS price rise of up to 20 percent: TIKR.com

A day after the CoWoS backlog was already in view, Taiwan’s Commercial Times reports TSMC is preparing to raise CoWoS advanced-packaging prices by up to 20 percent, and TSMC has not confirmed it. The report, citing supply-chain sources, says foundries and outside packagers are preparing increases of 10 to 20 percent as demand has spread from AI GPUs to server CPUs and custom AI chips, with the bill landing on customers including NVIDIA and AMD. Equipment lead times are reported at six to eight months, from three to six. TIKR notes TSMC’s third-quarter revenue of $46.7 billion, a gross margin of 66.4 percent versus 59.5 percent a year earlier, advanced packaging at about 8 percent of 2025 revenue and expected slightly over 10 percent this year, and capex of $60 billion to $64 billion this year aimed mostly at capacity arriving in 2028 and 2029. Source: tikr.com

SEMI scales workforce programs as the U.S. talent gap nears 200,000: Semiconductor Digest

Shari Liss, SEMI’s vice president of global workforce development, said the U.S. industry is going to be almost 200,000 people short in the next few years. Joe Stockunas, president of SEMI Americas, said talent is the top answer when members are asked what could hold back growth. The National Network for Microelectronics Education is a $200 million, five-year CHIPS-funded effort with the National Science Foundation and the Department of Commerce, operated by the SEMI Foundation. Its first four regional nodes bring together more than 325 partners. The foundation has an equipment-maintenance technician certification, and ChipPath lists about 199,000 job postings. SEMI is also standing up the India Semiconductor Academy, Taiwan training agreements for American students, a Global Schools Academy and a Chip the Future Challenge. Liss called federal funding a drop compared with what is needed. Source: semiconductor-digest.com

Solidigm drops a U.S. IPO review and is reported in $45 billion plant talks: EE Times

SK Hynix has discontinued its review of a U.S. IPO for flash and SSD subsidiary Solidigm. CEO Kwak Noh Jung said the IPO was meant to raise outside capital for Solidigm production expansion, and that market conditions made proceeding now the wrong way to get that outcome. Bloomberg, citing people familiar with the negotiations, reports Solidigm is in talks with the U.S. Commerce Department about investing $45 billion in a new U.S. chip plant, which would be the first new major flash-memory production facility on American soil. Before committing, Solidigm is seeking U.S. government permission to send equipment to upgrade an existing plant in Dalian, China. Those are talks, not a committed fab. Source: eetimes.com

The Teardown: A Molecule That Emits the Link

A spin that can be addressed one molecule at a time, and that emits a photon at a set optical frequency, is the part HPCwire’s account of NVision’s Nature paper treats as the missing connector. Until that interface existed, molecular spins could be interesting defects and still fail as qubits, because they could not be controlled individually and coherently. The paper, A Single-Molecule Spin-Photon Interface, is described as the first individually addressable organic molecular spin-photon interface. The molecule is a carbene. It is meant to be deposited as a thin film, the way OLED materials are deposited, straight onto a photonic integrated circuit, including HyperLight’s thin-film lithium niobate chiplets. Light, not a wire between neighboring dies, is the link that entangles a stationary spin with another spin on the same chip or on another chip.

That choice changes the floor plan. A monolithic quantum chip packs qubits until crosstalk, control wiring and heat set the spacing. NVision’s photonic integrated quantum circuit uses the optical link so the qubits do not have to sit in that tight neighborhood. The molecules run at 2 to 4 kelvin, not at the millikelvin stack many superconducting parts demand, and they emit at optical frequencies the photonic chip can take without a separate wavelength translation step. Chemistry is the design knob: memory time, gate speed and photon emission are tuned inside one molecule rather than by redrawing a silicon qubit cell. HPCwire reports the screen that produced the carbene: more than 150 candidate molecules evaluated, 40 of them synthesized in the lab.

The loss budget is the worked example, and it is why the foundry claim is not just a slogan. The platform is described as tolerating up to 70 percent photon loss and still operating on standard silicon photonics, instead of demanding an ultra-low-loss optical plant that most CMOS lines do not run. Take a link that throws away seven photons in every ten. If the interface still closes entanglement on the three that arrive, the interconnect can be a photonic chip a foundry already knows how to build, with HyperLight’s electro-optic lithium niobate sitting underneath a deposited molecular film. The alternative path in the same account is decades and multi-billion-dollar proprietary fabs for modalities that only scale inside a custom line. NVision’s commercial sketch puts the first integrated prototype chips in the fourth quarter of 2026, a live on-chip demonstration by the end of 2026, then a climb to 1,000 physical qubits and toward 1,000 logical, fault-tolerant qubits on chip by 2030, in a data-center form factor. Those dates are targets, not delivered systems.

The 70 percent figure is also the exit ramp. If a real photonic link loses more than that share of photons, standard silicon photonics stops being enough and the design is back in the specialized low-loss plant it was written to avoid. The same break appears if the thin-film deposit cannot keep the single-molecule addressability the Nature result showed in the lab, because a film that only works as an ensemble is not a qubit array a foundry can tile. And if the workload needs two-qubit gates that only exist when the spins share a neighborhood, the optical decoupling that removes crosstalk also removes the interaction, and proximity wins again. Source: hpcwire.com

A spin you can address one molecule at a time, and that emits a photon at a set optical frequency, is the connector HPCwire’s account of NVision’s Nature paper treats as newly real. Until that interface existed, a molecular spin could be studied and still fail as a qubit, because it could not be controlled alone and coherently. The paper is described as the first individually addressable organic molecular spin-photon interface. The molecule is a carbene. It is meant to go down as a thin film, the way OLED materials do, onto a photonic integrated circuit, including HyperLight’s thin-film lithium niobate chiplets. Light, not a wire between neighboring dies, is what entangles that stationary spin with another spin on the same chip or another chip. A monolithic quantum part packs qubits until crosstalk, control wiring and heat fix the spacing. This photonic circuit uses the optical link so the qubits do not have to live in that tight neighborhood. The molecules run at two to four kelvin, and they emit at optical frequencies the photonic chip can take without a separate wavelength translation step. Chemistry is the knob: memory time, gate speed and photon emission are tuned inside one molecule. The screen behind the carbene evaluated more than one hundred fifty candidate molecules, and forty of them were synthesized in the lab. The loss budget is why the foundry claim is concrete. The platform is described as tolerating up to seventy percent photon loss and still running on standard silicon photonics, rather than an ultra-low-loss plant most CMOS lines do not have. Throw away seven photons in every ten, and if entanglement still closes on the three that arrive, the interconnect can be a photonic chip a foundry already builds, with lithium niobate under a deposited molecular film. The path named beside that is decades and multi-billion-dollar proprietary fabs for parts that only scale on a custom line. NVision’s sketch puts first integrated prototype chips in the fourth quarter of twenty twenty-six, a live on-chip demo by the end of twenty twenty-six, then a climb to one thousand physical qubits and toward one thousand logical, fault-tolerant qubits on chip by twenty thirty, in a data-center form factor. Those are targets, not systems in a rack. If a real link loses more than that seventy percent, standard silicon photonics stops being enough, and the design is back in the specialized low-loss plant it was written to avoid. The same break hits if the thin film cannot keep the single-molecule addressability the lab result showed, because an ensemble film is not a qubit array a foundry can tile. And if the workload needs two-qubit gates that only exist when the spins share a neighborhood, the optical split that removes crosstalk also removes the interaction, and proximity wins again. On the horizon, CXMT’s reported HBM3E mass production is timed for the end of twenty twenty-six, inside about two months of this report. Intel’s fourteen-A one point zero process design kit is aimed at volume production early next year, while the zero point nine kit for outside customers was targeted for October and is not described as handed over. CoreWeave’s new Middle East and Asia Pacific points of presence are set to start coming online in the fourth quarter of twenty twenty-six. NVision is targeting those first prototype chips in the fourth quarter of twenty twenty-six and a live on-chip demonstration by the end of the year, with a twenty thirty goal of one thousand logical qubits on chip.
