Six hundred million dollars is what a two-week Fab 28 halt costs Intel in server-chip revenue.

By Patrick October 31, 2026 Ep 40 9 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top Story Silicon Data Centres & Power Supply Chain & Policy The Teardown: The Motor That Cannot Just Swap the Magnet On the Horizon

What You Need to Know: Six hundred million dollars is what Intel expects to lose across two quarters after a two-week shutdown at Fab 28 near Kiryat Gat, almost all of it in 10-nanometer-class server chips. The same reporting window has Sherman named as a leading candidate for a further TSMC fab search, and Google buying land in Hamina while Finnish projects of 50 megawatts and 40 megawatts move in opposite directions. A lab optical switch from PsiQuantum is already inside the loss budget data-center links allow.

Top Story

Intel Fab 28 Shutdown to Cost $600 Million in Lost Revenue, Production to Restart Soon: TechPowerUp

An explosion in mid-October at Intel’s Fab 28 near Kiryat Gat, Israel, killed two people and seriously injured one, and the fab has been shut for about two weeks. Chief financial officer David Zinsner said Intel expects to start production again by the end of this week while it sorts which wafers still in the line are usable. He put the revenue hit at about $300 million in the fourth quarter of 2026 and a similar $300 million in the first quarter of 2027 — about $600 million across the two quarters — and said almost all of the fourth-quarter figure is 10-nanometer server product. Tom’s Hardware reports the accident is also expected to cut fourth-quarter non-GAAP gross margin by about 2 percent, with most wafer write-offs landing in that quarter even though shipments stay disrupted into early 2027. Xeon 6 still uses Intel 7 input-output dies, and Intel still sells Xeon 5 Emerald Rapids on Intel 7; it stopped taking orders for Xeon 4 Sapphire Rapids in late September. Other 10-nanometer-capable fabs, including lines in Arizona, cannot fully cover the gap while server demand is already tight, and Intel has not said it will move Xeon 6 input-output production onto lines used for older CPUs. Source: techpowerup.com · tomshardware.com

Silicon

Intel Q3 2026 Earnings Data Center Soars While PC Faces Memory Pressure: ServeTheHome

Intel’s third-quarter 2026 revenue was $16.5 billion, up 21 percent year over year, with non-GAAP gross margin at 44.1 percent. Data Center and AI revenue was $7.3 billion, up 78 percent — David Zinsner called that the strongest data-center revenue growth in 15 years — and segment operating income was $3.0 billion at a 41.5 percent margin. “Purpose Built Silicon” revenue more than doubled, and CEO Lip-Bu Tan said the immediate priority is unlocking supply for Xeon; on the call he said substrate availability is limiting output and will take time to ease. Client Computing and Physical AI revenue fell 5 percent to $8.1 billion as higher DRAM and NAND prices lift system cost. TechPowerUp reports Intel expects the PC market to shrink about 12 percent in units this year and sees another 10 percent decline in 2027, with the PC slowdown continuing at least through the first half of next year and memory pricing pressure persisting into 2027. Intel Foundry revenue was $6.7 billion, up 58 percent including intersegment sales, with an operating loss of $1.9 billion versus $2.3 billion a year earlier; the Intel 14A 0.9 process design kit was released in October, Ohio expansion continues, and the call indicated 18A supply is being looked at for the data-center side pending substrate. Source: servethehome.com · techpowerup.com

GIGABYTE Advances Local AI Infrastructure with WRX90 AI TOP Motherboard: TechPowerUp

GIGABYTE announced the WRX90 AI TOP workstation motherboard for AMD Ryzen Threadripper PRO processors, aimed at on-premises local AI workloads. It has seven PCIe 5.0 x16 slots and two MCIO 8i connectors, which GIGABYTE says can support up to nine GPU devices through compatible expansion hardware. Eight-channel DDR5 supports up to 2 TB of ECC RDIMM, and the board includes enterprise-grade BMC remote management. Source: techpowerup.com

PsiQuantum Turns Its Photonics Know-How into an Optical Switch for AI Data Center GPU Networks: Tom's Hardware

Tom’s Hardware reports PsiQuantum has turned its photonics work into an optical switch aimed at AI data-center GPU networks. The eight-port device typically loses 1.2 dB — under a quarter of its light — against a data-center allowance of up to 3 dB. The 8x8 unit is a lab prototype, and the company plans larger devices. Source: tomshardware.com

Data Centres & Power

Google Buys 56 Hectares in Hamina, Finland, Amid Ongoing Issues over Muhos Tree-Felling: Data Center Dynamics

Google has bought 56 hectares in Hamina, Finland. Tom’s Hardware reports a criminal investigation into environmental damage tied to Google’s Finnish subsidiary, after local police acted on complaints about breaches of environmental law connected to tree-felling at Muhos. In the same Finland roundup, Tekova plans an announced 50-megawatt data center in Perho, and SRV has backed out of a 40-megawatt project in Eura. Source: datacenterdynamics.com · tomshardware.com

PowerBank Plans 2MW Modular Data Center in Ontario, Canada: Data Center Dynamics

PowerBank’s 2-megawatt modular data center in Ontario, Canada, is announced. The company has signed a first lease with an AI cloud partner. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

DayOne Signs MoU with Sembcorp and Inks MoU with Blue Whale Energy: Data Center Dynamics

DayOne has signed a memorandum of understanding with Sembcorp to integrate power and energy solutions across Asia-Pacific data centers, as part of DayOne’s Bring Your Own Power initiative. A separate memorandum with Blue Whale Energy will explore whether a distributed virtual power plant in Singapore is feasible, in support of DayOne’s SG1 data center. Both are memoranda, not energized supply. Source: datacenterdynamics.com · datacenterdynamics.com

Supply Chain & Policy

From Fire Watch to Fab Tools: The First Graduates of an ASU Program Built for TSMC Arizona: Digital Information World

TSMC Arizona employs more than 3,500 people in north Phoenix and has projected 6,000 employees to support its first three fabs. AZ Big Media, after the October 15 earnings call, reported that construction of the second fab is now complete within a site investment that now totals $265 billion, and that construction is well underway on the third fab. TSMC Arizona expects to fill more than 100 equipment technician positions by the end of 2026, with plans for hundreds more equipment, facilities, process, and manufacturing technician roles before the end of 2030. Arizona State University’s Foundations for Equipment Technician Program, built with TSMC Arizona, graduated its first two cohorts in late July; many participants already held full-time offers, the course is free to the student, and a successful graduate is guaranteed an interview. Each format is about 168 hours across nine topic areas and three professional certificates. Grand Canyon University’s 11-week manufacturing specialist program, launched with TSMC in 2025, has trained about 100 students, with an 83 percent completion rate, and roughly 70 percent of graduates have received offers; TSMC’s registered equipment-technician apprenticeship runs 12 to 18 months. Source: digitalinformationworld.com

Sherman Is Likely Contender for TSMC Semiconductor Expansion: Bisnow

Bisnow reports Sherman has been identified as the leading candidate for TSMC’s next fabrication plant, which the Dallas Business Journal reported could bring a $120 billion investment. State documents the Journal obtained describe an unnamed semiconductor manufacturer seeking Texas sites for a $45 billion investment in two plants, while Bloomberg has reported TSMC considering the Dallas-Fort Worth area for as many as six facilities at $20 billion each. On the October 15 earnings call, CEO C.C. Wei said U.S. customers have asked TSMC to expand beyond Phoenix and that nearly 20 fabs worldwide are in progress. Sherman officials said the city has not been contacted. Sherman has already lined up more than $40 billion from Texas Instruments, GlobalWafers, and Coherent, and land broker Rex Glendenning told the Journal the city’s water volume is the only supply he knows in the market that can feed that kind of plant. The Austin Business Journal reported Central Texas is also being considered. Source: bisnow.com

OpenAI CEO Says It's 'Quite Terrible' for Your PC to Have RAM: Tom's Hardware

In a podcast interview reported by Tom’s Hardware, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said it is “quite terrible” for people to have RAM in their own PCs when that memory could go into AI data centers, which he said offer better utilization. Source: tomshardware.com

The Teardown: The Motor That Cannot Just Swap the Magnet

A factory-automation motor stores part of its magnetic field in a permanent magnet, so the drive does not have to create that field from current on every cycle. The way to take it apart is to ask what the current, the steel, and the control loop have to replace if the magnet is no longer allowed to do that job. Asia Business Daily, translating Taiwan’s Economic Daily News, reports that TSMC has asked suppliers whether rare-earth-free motors could be used in factory automation equipment, and is putting components that use rare earths into its equipment supply-chain review.

Those magnets sit in the same class of material China restricted in April 2025: samarium, gadolinium, terbium, dysprosium, lutetium, scandium, and yttrium — seven elements. The report describes them as key inputs to high-performance permanent magnets used in electric vehicles, robots, and industrial automation, which is why a wafer fab’s handling and positioning motors are now on the same watch list as a robot joint. In October 2025 the controls were widened on paper toward more rare earths and toward permanent-magnet manufacturing technology. Some of those wider measures were postponed. The April 2025 list was not.

Swapping the magnet is not the design. The same report says efficiency, torque, control performance, and reliability all have to meet semiconductor-equipment requirements. A reluctance motor — the approach Shuo Yang is exploring for industrial equipment and for cooling systems in AI data centers — makes torque because a shaped rotor is pulled into the low-reluctance path of a rotating stator field. No rare-earth magnet, so none of those seven elements in the rotor. The stator still needs electrical steel, which is the materials path China Steel is pursuing, and a current waveform precise enough to keep torque smooth, which is why Fukuta’s motor-design work belongs in the same cluster as a drive specialist. In a tool bay, ripple and heat are process risks: a missed step or extra heat in the wrong enclosure is a defect, not a comfort complaint. That is the control-performance bar, and it is why the response is a materials company, a motor company, and a drive approach together, not a single magnet vendor.

The only capacity figures in the report are for a domestic materials line, not for a motor. The Industrial Technology Research Institute runs a rare-earth pilot line and, with government support, is moving a separate demonstration plant targeted for completion by the end of 2028, with annual production of 500 tons of rare earths by the end of 2030. The government plans to use that output to meet about 50 percent of the island’s domestic demand. At the stated 2030 rate, half of demand is still outside the new plant even if the tonnage lands on target. A design that still needs any of the seven April 2025 elements stays inside a licensing regime that is already live. The 500-ton plan does not retire that exposure; it only describes how much of a magnet-based chain Taiwan hopes to feed locally, and the stated share is about half.

The rare-earth-free motor stops being the right substitution when it cannot meet efficiency, torque, control performance, and reliability together on the tool. At that point the actuator stays on a permanent-magnet motor, and the supply plan is inventory plus a license, not a rotor redraw. The demonstration plant does not move that test. It only changes how much magnet material a design that failed the test could hope to source on the island. Source: asiae.co.kr

On the Horizon

  • Some expanded Chinese rare-earth control measures announced in October 2025 stay postponed until November 10, 2026; the April 2025 controls remain in place.
  • Arizona State University plans to run up to 12 additional equipment-technician cohorts a year through 2028.
