More than ten thousand jobs ride on TSMC's Arizona expansion groundbreaking December fourth.

By Patrick November 26, 2026 Ep 66 8 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Follow AI Chips & Data Centres Daily Apple Podcasts RSS
Listen to this episode

What You Need to Know: More than ten thousand direct jobs are attached to the expansion groundbreaking President Donald Trump is set to attend on December fourth at TSMC’s Phoenix site, where twelve thousand to fifteen thousand construction and support workers are already on the ground. Huawei and ChangXin Memory Technologies have papers on the February ISSCC draft program, including a high-bandwidth memory design US media describe as twice the data-pin density of Samsung and SK hynix parts now in mass production. Nvidia has told Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers into Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia under last week’s US licence rules.

Top Story

President Donald Trump will attend a groundbreaking on December fourth for an expansion of TSMC’s semiconductor plant in Phoenix, a company spokesperson confirmed, and Bloomberg reported a White House official expects TSMC executives plus supplier and customer representatives at the ceremony. Whether Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang attends has not been decided. TSMC already manufactures Nvidia’s Blackwell chips, and chips for AMD and Apple, at the first Arizona plant. The White House official said the expansion is expected to create more than ten thousand direct jobs once completed, and that twelve thousand to fifteen thousand construction and support workers are currently at the site. TSMC first announced the Arizona plant near the end of Trump’s first administration and, in March last year, unveiled a one hundred sixty-five billion dollar US investment plan to expand advanced semiconductor production in the country. The December fourth event is a groundbreaking for the expansion, not an energized new fab. Source: biz.chosun.com

Source: yahoo.com

Silicon

China's Chipmakers Step Up Efforts to Catch Samsung Electronics and SK hynix: Fnnews

IEEE’s draft program for the International Solid-State Circuits Conference, set for San Francisco next February, previews papers from Huawei and ChangXin Memory Technologies. Huawei plans to present next-generation high-bandwidth memory; US media reported the data-pin density is twice that of products Samsung Electronics and SK hynix currently have in mass production, a design that in theory would raise memory bandwidth. CXMT will show next-generation DDR5 and a paper on the LPDDR6 interface. Earlier this month CXMT said it had completed engineering-sample validation of a sixth-generation DRAM platform on a 4F2 cell and claimed it was first to finish that validation. Nikkei Asia reported Samsung and SK hynix agreed, through a patent cross-licence, to adopt some of Yangtze Memory Technologies’ Xtacking-related stacking technology for 3D NAND. A memory-industry official said the deals are mainly to avoid legal disputes after YMTC filed broadly, and that YMTC’s approach has also made it less exposed to US controls on advanced manufacturing equipment, with some leading firms now licensing the technology. Source: en.fnnews.com

Weimob Spends Over CNY 400 Million Buying 5,000 NVIDIA RTX PRO 5500 Blackwell GPUs: BigGo Finance

Weimob Group said on the twenty-sixth that wholly owned Weimob Zhisuan signed two independent GPU purchases, for five thousand Nvidia RTX PRO 5500 Blackwell processors, at about four hundred thirty million yuan, roughly sixty-four point six million dollars. Ingram Micro is supplying four thousand units for about three hundred forty-four million yuan, about fifty-one point seven million dollars, and Shanghai Fuxin one thousand units for eighty-six point five million yuan, about thirteen million dollars. Both sellers are independent third parties, and neither deal is conditional on the other. Weimob said the cards go first to multimodal, image and video generation for its own AI products, then some idle capacity may be leased to other enterprises. It said tight GPU supply led it to split the buy across suppliers. Source: finance.biggo.com

SK Hynix seeks TSMC 3nm expertise as Micron steps up hiring: digitimes

Digitimes reports SK hynix is seeking engineers with experience on TSMC’s 3-nanometer process to develop customized high-bandwidth memory, while Micron is increasing hiring in South Korea to support its Asian expansion. Source: digitimes.com

NVIDIA Cuts RTX 50-Series Supply as RTX 60-Series Faces Possible Delay: TechPowerUp

Board Channels, via TechPowerUp, reports Nvidia has told add-in-card partners they will get twenty to thirty percent fewer gaming GPUs over the next three months, November 2026 through January 2027. The post is about China. Taiwan’s Commercial Times, citing Taiwanese ODMs, reported new B300 orders take twenty-six to forty weeks and GB300 about fourteen to twenty-six weeks, with RTX Pro cards and DGX Station stretched past that. Industry sources expect wafers freed by the GeForce cut to move toward data-centre chips. These are trade-press reports, not a Nvidia shipment notice. Source: techpowerup.com

Data Centres & Power

MCRPS Power Supply for Enterprise Data Centers: EE Times

Infineon has a 5.6-kilowatt MCRPS reference design aimed at next-generation common redundant power supplies in enterprise servers. Source: eetimes.com

Supply Chain & Policy

Nvidia faces mounting scrutiny as export controls disrupt chip shipments: National Technology News

Nvidia has told manufacturing partners Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia, the Financial Times reported, citing three people familiar with the matter, after last week’s US rules on cloud access to restricted processors. It is unclear whether the stop is temporary. Providers have ninety days to obtain licences by showing restricted entities are not end users. Nvidia recorded one hundred four billion dollars of revenue through its Singapore subsidiary in 2025, up seventy-two percent on the prior year. Lenders have halted about five hundred million dollars of credit for Singapore cloud provider Aolani’s planned computing arrangement with ByteDance. S&P Global Ratings put Nvidia’s AA rating on downgrade watch, warning a one-notch cut could follow within ninety days; Moody’s kept Aa1 with a stable outlook. Source: nationaltechnology.co.uk

Source: ft.com

Source: straitstimes.com

Congress to Investigate Jensen Huang, Tighten Chip Controls: Domino Theory

Jensen Huang and Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter are due next week before three House committees and the Senate Banking Committee. Senator Chuck Schumer wrote that Huang shipped extremely advanced chips to China in violation of export-control law. Representative Hakeem Jeffries has called for export-control legislation in next year’s defense bill. Senator Elizabeth Warren said Huang’s return increases the urgency of passing bipartisan export-control bills. Three Republican members of the House Foreign Affairs Committee said the board’s brief investigation and reinstatement do not erase the national-security questions. President Trump said he personally convinced the board to rehire Huang. Bill Bishop wrote that Trump may ignore new statutes the way he set aside the TikTok law. Chris McGuire said legislation still on the table does not force licence denials in named circumstances. Derek Scissors said veto-proof legislation is easier in next year’s Democratic-controlled Congress, and that the open question is whether that Congress treats export controls as a priority. Source: dominotheory.com

SIA Applauds Introduction of Bipartisan Legislation to Extend Semiconductor Investment Credit: Semiconductor Digest

The Semiconductor Industry Association welcomed bipartisan legislation to extend the semiconductor investment tax credit, which is currently set to expire at the end of 2026. Source: semiconductor-digest.com

The Teardown: Years of Capacity Against Months of Permission

A supply commitment is a dated promise to take wafers, memory and buildings that do not exist yet. Longbridge, reading Nvidia’s CFO commentary, puts those supply and capacity commitments at three hundred ninety billion dollars. About two hundred three billion dollars falls in fiscal 2028, roughly February 2027 through January 2028, and about ninety-two billion dollars in fiscal 2029. Together that is about three quarters of the book. Inventory has risen to thirty-nine point seven billion dollars. The bottleneck Longbridge describes is no longer the processor alone. SK hynix said in October 2026 that, counting long-term agreements and demand still under negotiation, its DRAM and high-bandwidth memory output through 2029 is largely booked, and Nvidia’s commitments grew mainly through multi-year memory purchases. Power and credit sit on the same timeline. Nvidia has guarantees tied to customers’ and partners’ land, power, buildings and systems, with maximum gross exposure of one hundred eighteen billion dollars. Its neocloud agreements carry financing, deployment and acceptance conditions. The International Energy Agency’s Energy and AI outlook, as Longbridge cites it, projects data-centre electricity use of about nine hundred forty-five terawatt-hours by 2030, and says about twenty percent of planned projects could be delayed by grid constraints.

Walk the commitment book without treating it as cash already spent. Start at three hundred ninety billion dollars. Fiscal 2028’s two hundred three billion and fiscal 2029’s ninety-two billion add to two hundred ninety-five billion, the three-quarter slice that sits two fiscal years out. The nearer quarter is real, but the commentary Longbridge uses does not split it by quarter, so it stays an unnamed remainder. The one hundred eighteen billion dollars of guarantees is a different stack. It is not a purchase order for HBM. It is a backstop on land, power, buildings and systems, and it is gross exposure, a ceiling, not a forecast of what will be drawn. Acceptance conditions matter because a neocloud that cannot take delivery does not convert a guarantee into a running cluster. The twenty percent figure is a project-delay share in the IEA projection, not a slice of the one hundred eighteen billion. Multiplying them would invent a loss the source never calculated. What the two numbers do show, side by side, is that chip commitments can be fully placed while a fifth of the buildings those chips were meant to fill are still waiting on a grid connection. Memory makes the same split in time: a book through 2029 at SK hynix is a physical allocation, and it does not move if the building slips.

That guarantee-for-the-shell choice stops being the right tool when the thing that fails is not the customer’s ability to finance a building. Longbridge’s own observation is that commitments are counted in years while recent policy windows are counted in months: the latest US-China truce extension was only two months, and the export rules carry a sixty-day comment period. A prepayment to a memory supplier, which management told Tom’s Hardware it may offer, clears a supply queue. It does not clear a licence, and it does not energize a substation. When the binding limit is who may lawfully use the compute, or whether the interconnect arrives before the acceptance window, the one hundred eighteen billion dollars of building guarantees is aimed at the constraint that is no longer the one in front of the cluster. Source: longbridge.com

On the Horizon

  • The US-China truce Longbridge cites expires on 10 January 2027.
  • The Bureau of Industry and Security interim rule on remote access to advanced semiconductors, published in the Federal Register on 19 November 2026, stays open for comment until 19 January 2027.
  • Huawei and CXMT presentations on the ISSCC draft program are set for San Francisco next February.
  • The semiconductor investment tax credit SIA wants extended is currently set to expire at the end of 2026.
