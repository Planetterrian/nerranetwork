AI Chips & Data Centres Daily Blog

Twenty billion pounds is lined up for up to one gigawatt beside a Yorkshire power station.

By Patrick November 03, 2026 Ep 43 9 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top Story Silicon Data Centres & Power Supply Chain & Policy Also Today The Teardown: The Queue Outruns the Hall On the Horizon

What You Need to Know: Brookfield’s estimate for a campus of up to one gigawatt at Drax’s North Yorkshire station is more than twenty billion pounds, with a first phase of up to one hundred megawatts aimed at the first quarter of 2028. The same day’s silicon news is a modeled Arm rack comparison, a one-hundred-fifteen-billion-dollar Marvell–Lumentum agreement, and a DDR5-12800 module from Penguin Solutions. Reported HBM diversion into China, priced around four thousand dollars a stack, is the supply-chain item with a rule attached.

Top Story

Drax has announced, with Brookfield and Radiant, a plan to develop, deliver and operate data-centre capacity at the 2.6-gigawatt Drax Power Station in Selby, North Yorkshire. Data Center Dynamics reports a phased build: phase one targets up to 100 megawatts and is expected to be operational from the first quarter of 2028; phase two would take capacity to 500 megawatts between 2028 and 2031; phase three envisions further capacity after 2031. Brookfield estimates investment could exceed £20 billion — $26.8 billion in that report, $26.9 billion at BigGo Finance — including an initial £2.5 billion ($3.4 billion) for phase one, through a joint venture with Qatar’s Qai styled Brookfield-Qai AI. Phase one still requires planning, regulatory and network approvals and a tenant; BigGo Finance cites Citi reading the announcement as closer to a memorandum of understanding than a binding contract, with no final investment decision. The proposed site is 1,000 acres with 4 gigawatts of grid access, 2.6 gigawatts of active dispatchable generation, and cooling systems from the existing wood-pellet plant. The UK government had rejected an AI Growth Zone bid for up to 1.2 gigawatts at the same site, and Data Center Dynamics cites Ember putting Drax’s 2025 emissions at 14.1 million tonnes of CO2 equivalent. Source: datacenterdynamics.com · finance.biggo.com

Silicon

Arm AGI CPU Delivers Nearly 2x Performance per Rack vs x86: Arm

Arm is promoting an AGI CPU for data centres asked to deliver more compute inside fixed constraints, and says the part was built around performance, scale and efficiency. Independent analysis from Signal65, which Arm highlighted, shows nearly 2x modeled performance per rack for the Arm AGI CPU versus comparable x86 devices. Arm pointed listeners to a Six Five Media discussion of those results with Ryan Shrout, Russ Fellows, Jeff DeFilippi and Ravi Malhotra. Source: x.com · x.com

Marvell Snaps Photonics Chipmaker Lumentum to Bolster AI Connectivity: EE Times

Marvell has entered a definitive all-stock agreement to acquire Lumentum at an implied value of about $115 billion, pairing interconnect and custom silicon with lasers, optical circuit switching and compound-semiconductor manufacturing. ServeTheHome reports Marvell shareholders would own 67 percent and Lumentum shareholders 33 percent, with three Lumentum directors joining a 10-member board, and that the deal is expected to close in the first half of 2028. Lumentum chief executive Michael Hurlston has called the opening a $550 billion market; Marvell chief executive Matt Murphy said the combination doubles down on supporting Nvidia, which invested $2 billion in each company earlier this year. Lumentum’s indium phosphide process and MEMS optical circuit switches, including the R300, set optical paths without intermediate packet processing. Source: eetimes.com · servethehome.com

Penguin Solutions Introduces High-Bandwidth SMART Modular MRDIMMs: HPCwire

Penguin Solutions announced on November 2 a SMART Modular DDR5 multi-ranked DIMM running at DDR5-12800 for AI data centres. The module is offered with optional Zefr testing for reliability. Source: hpcwire.com

Data Centres & Power

Cloud Revenue Hits $168B as AI Capacity Expands: Data Center Knowledge

Worldwide spending on cloud infrastructure services reached $168 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 55 percent from a year earlier, Synergy Research Group reported. The market has doubled in eight quarters, and AI-specific services now account for roughly one-third of revenue. Chief analyst John Dinsdale told Data Center Knowledge the drivers are capacity coming online, higher pricing, and a shift toward more expensive AI services. JLL figures cited in the same report put hyperscalers at 59 percent of North American data-centre tenant demand, with 28 gigawatts of owner-occupied capacity under construction; neoclouds were 11 percent of demand and AI companies 7 percent. Dell’Oro projects global data-centre capital spending above $3 trillion by 2030. CBRE counted 7,481 megawatts under construction across eight primary North American markets in the first half of 2026, up 24.8 percent, with more than 80 percent preleased, up from 74.3 percent a year earlier. Source: datacenterknowledge.com

Putin demands Russian data centers supply capacity to the state: Tom's Hardware

President Putin has told non-state Russian data centres they must set aside 10 percent of total capacity for the country’s own IT infrastructure after Ukraine drone attacks. Tom’s Hardware reports that slice must be available on 24 hours’ notice. Source: tomshardware.com

Anthropic signs lease with Australian Data Centres in Western Downs, Queensland: Data Center Dynamics

Anthropic has signed a lease with Australian Data Centres in Western Downs, Queensland. Australian Data Centres is 85 percent owned by Dexus. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Supply Chain & Policy

Smugglers Bypass U.S. Controls to Ship HBM to China: Businesskorea

The Information, reported on November 2 and carried by Businesskorea, Seoul Economic Daily and Wccftech, describes Korean HBM reaching China inside packages that also carry a low-end server processor, so U.S. rules score the package on the processor rather than the memory; Wccftech traces that opening to December 2024 rules that exempted HBM already integrated into logic products. In China the memory is taken off the board, and failure rates are high because stacks warp or crack, a loss brokers accept because the margin is large. A broker told The Information that one HBM3E, a few hundred dollars a year ago, now trades at about $4,000, and that six to eight stacks on one AI package put memory alone at $24,000 to $32,000. People familiar with Huawei’s supply chain said Huawei told some customers the Ascend 960DT will carry substantial Samsung Electronics and SK hynix HBM, and that Huawei described the chip as twice the performance of existing products. Samsung and SK hynix hold more than 80 percent of the global HBM market, and Huawei has told some customers ordering its AI chips to secure HBM themselves. Source: businesskorea.co.kr · en.sedaily.com · wccftech.com · theinformation.com

Samsung And TSMC Foundry Cuts Push 8-Inch Chip Capacity Utilisation Towards 90%: Electronics For You BUSINESS

Samsung and TSMC are cutting 8-inch foundry output. Electronics For You Business reports those cuts are pushing 8-inch chip capacity utilisation towards 90 percent. Source: electronicsforyou.biz

Also Today

TSMC Has Raised T$14.8 Billion Via Unsecured Bonds: TradingView

TSMC has raised T$14.8 billion through unsecured bonds. Source: tradingview.com

As Samsung and SK hynix rally, three global semiconductor equipment makers add 2,500 workers in South Korea: Fnnews

Three global semiconductor equipment makers have added 2,500 workers in South Korea as Samsung and SK hynix rally. Source: en.fnnews.com

TSMC Eyes Texas Site Near Samsung’s Taylor Chip Plant: Businesskorea

TSMC is reported to be eyeing a Texas site near Samsung Electronics’ Taylor chip plant, sharpening U.S. foundry competition between the two. Source: businesskorea.co.kr · mt.co.kr

Hyperscale market shifts: Data Center Dynamics

An AFL whitepaper says AI workloads are pushing rack power requirements well beyond 100 kilowatts and that cluster fabrics at 800G are part of the physical redesign. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

The Teardown: The Queue Outruns the Hall

New grid infrastructure takes five to fifteen years to build, while a data centre takes one to three, and that mismatch is now visible in how long a project sits between announcement and groundbreaking. Data Center Knowledge, drawing on GlobalData, reports that projects which broke ground between the third quarter of 2025 and the third quarter of 2026 waited an average of just over two years after they were announced. Projects that started construction between early 2020 and mid-2025 waited about eighteen months. The building did not suddenly take six extra months to pour. The clock that moved is the one that starts when a developer has a site and a customer story and ends when someone will actually energize a connection.

An interconnection is a reserved slice of a substation, a line, and a protection scheme, not a cable the tenant can order with the switchgear. Martina Raveni, senior analyst on GlobalData’s Strategic Intelligence team, told Data Center Knowledge the grid is the hardest constraint because it is structural: the line is a five-to-fifteen-year asset and the hall is a one-to-three-year asset. Luis Fernandes, IDC senior research manager for cloud and data centres, went further from his conversations with developers, designers and large operators. He said grid access is the dominant obstacle by a long shot, to the point that his team had not seen projects delayed for any other reason, because the absence of a connection halts the exercise. Other problems — labour, water, local votes — still exist. They only become the binding delay once a connection is real enough that the rest of the schedule can run.

Walk the published intervals against each other. Eighteen months was the older announcement-to-groundbreaking average. Just over two years is the average for the cohort that broke ground across the latest four reported quarters. That is a lengthening of roughly half a year on a clock that is still shorter than either build time in Raveni’s pair. A one-to-three-year hall that is waiting on a five-to-fifteen-year line is not late concrete. It is a project whose useful start date is the energization date of someone else’s asset. In Europe’s main data-centre hubs, Raveni said developers can wait up to a decade for a connection. Set that decade against a one-to-three-year building and the queue is several construction cycles long before the first rack lands. Skilled-labour shortages sit on top of that: insufficient electricians and other trades are pushing some delivery schedules back by around a year, a delay that only starts once the connection exists.

GlobalData’s demand figure shows why the queue does not clear by itself. Annual data-centre electricity consumption is forecast to rise from 590 terawatt-hours in 2025 to 1,520 terawatt-hours in 2030. That is the load the five-to-fifteen-year system is being asked to absorb while halls are still being promised on a one-to-three-year brochure. Investment does not compress a transmission build into a data-centre build. It bids for the connections that already have a date.

On-site gas is the design that looks like a way around the queue. Raveni said it can bridge a power gap, but redundancy requirements may force generating capacity substantially greater than the data centre’s normal needs. The bridge is then larger than the load it serves. Air pollution and water use from that extra plant feed the same local resistance — bills, water, noise, land — that Ya-Han Brownlee-Chen at DP World described as a growing barrier, and Joe Johnston at Xylem tied to power, water and cooling as one infrastructure problem. GlobalData lists closed-loop cooling, water stewardship and diversified power among operator responses, and calls progress uneven.

The gas bridge stops being the right plant when the temporary machines are still burning after the surrounding grid has moved the other way. Raveni’s line is that a temporary fix becomes permanent and pushes emissions up just as grids are decarbonizing. At that point the developer has not escaped the five-to-fifteen-year system. The developer has built a second power station whose size is set by redundancy, not by the IT load, and has inherited the permitting fight the interconnection queue was supposed to avoid. Fernandes, for his part, said there is still no hard evidence that compute capacity itself is limiting AI development. The dated constraint in this account is the connection, not the accelerator. Source: datacenterknowledge.com

On the Horizon

  • 2028 through 2031: the same plan schedules a further build toward 500 megawatts, with additional capacity only envisioned after 2031.
  • First half of 2028: Marvell’s all-stock acquisition of Lumentum is expected to close.
