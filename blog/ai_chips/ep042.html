A 72-GPU Rubin rack now carries 20.7 terabytes of HBM4 for inference.

By Patrick November 02, 2026 Ep 42 8 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top Story Silicon Data Centres & Power Supply Chain & Policy Also Today The Teardown: When the Old Well Still Pumps On the Horizon

What You Need to Know: ASRock Rack showed a Vera Rubin NVL72 at OCP with 20.7 terabytes of HBM4 across 72 GPUs. OpenAI left its Seoul meetings without a new memory contract, while TrendForce sees HBM's blended price more than doubling in 2027. DigiTimes reports Elon Musk is now discussing operational control and joint investment with TSMC on Terafab.

Top Story

ServeTheHome reports that ASRock Rack showed the XGMRACK-VR/NVL72 at the OCP Global Summit in San Jose: a 48RU NVIDIA MGX rack with 72 Rubin GPUs, 20.7TB of HBM4, a 5000A busbar and a liquid manifold. Inside are 18 Vera Rubin compute trays and nine NVLink 6 switch trays, for 36 NVIDIA Vera CPUs with those GPUs. NVIDIA lists the rack at 3,600 PFLOPS of NVFP4 inference (sparse) and 2,520 PFLOPS of NVFP4 training (dense), and each Rubin GPU gets 288GB of HBM4. Power comes from four 3RU shelves rated at 110kW each, using six 18.3kW supplies plus bulk capacitors; cooling-distribution options are in-rack, in-row, rear-door heat exchanger, or a liquid-to-air sidecar. Each compute tray is liquid-cooled, with eight 800Gbps OSFP ports from ConnectX-9 SuperNICs and two 400Gbps QSFP112 ports from a BlueField-4 DPU. Source: servethehome.com

Silicon

Xeon 6 leads a recorded agent-workload bake-off: Intel

Intel says a Xeon 6 processor-based instance delivered 1.66 times the normalized agent-task throughput of a 5th Gen AMD EPYC instance and 4.5 times an Arm v9.2A instance on a healthcare Terminal-Bench trace. The comparison ran at 24-way concurrency for 60 minutes with identical code, inputs and agent trajectories. A record-and-replay method held the workflow fixed so the gap is attributed to the platform rather than to changing tool choices. The healthcare set included clinical documentation, evidence retrieval, fraud detection and laboratory diagnostics, among other tasks. The write-up is by Nithya Rao and Saumya Buliya of Intel. Source: t.co

AI Reshapes Memory Architecture as Macronix Expands Flash Portfolio: EE Times

Martin Lin, Macronix marketing director, told EE Times Taiwan that AI is changing memory architecture, not only memory demand. Macronix raised 2026 capital expenditure to NT$37.8 billion to expand 12-inch wafer capacity for 3D NOR, NAND, eMMC and related products. Lin said a previous-generation rack used about 600 to 800 NOR flash devices and the latest generation has more than 1,000, for boot, firmware, control and security as controller and switch counts rise. Market data in the piece put the memory market above $20 billion in 2025 and near $70 billion in 2026, with NOR flash, SLC NAND and low-capacity eMMC together around $24 billion in 2026. The article describes high-bandwidth flash, built on 3D NAND, as a higher-capacity tier for data that does not have to stay in HBM, and says equipment install, qualification and yield ramp can take two to three years. Source: eetimes.com

Data Centres & Power

Musk outlines Colossus 2 halls at roughly 220,000 Blackwell GPUs each: Wccftech

Wccftech reports that Elon Musk shared plans, so this is announced rather than energized, for Colossus 2 halls named Macroharder, Minihard and Gun Range, each with roughly 220,000 NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs. Source: wccftech.com

The Datacenter Backlash Goes Bipartisan: SemiAnalysis

SemiAnalysis says the 2027 US data-centre buildout is largely locked in, while a prolonged Texas permit freeze is now the biggest single risk to 2028, and that both parties are running on tighter rules. Source: newsletter.semianalysis.com

Supply Chain & Policy

OpenAI seeks memory and cash in Seoul, and leaves without a new contract: ET CIO

ET CIO, citing The Korea Herald, reports that OpenAI chief Sam Altman met about 50 Samsung executives in Yongin on Saturday and then SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won with SK hynix and SK Telecom executives, and that no new supply or investment agreements were announced. Altman, as carried by that report, said simply using AI will no longer be enough to secure a sustainable competitive advantage. TrendForce forecasts that HBM's blended average selling price could rise 121 percent in 2027 from 2026. Samsung said its HBM supply for 2027 is sold out; SK hynix said output through 2029 is effectively accounted for through firm contracts and volumes under negotiation, and HBM sales head Kim Ki-tae said customers are requesting more than twice the volume the company can supply. The Herald says OpenAI has reportedly secured about $11 billion in commitments against an initial $30 billion target — figures not independently confirmed — and that the October 2025 Stargate outline of demand equivalent to 900,000 DRAM wafer starts a month was a production target, not a binding order. International Business Times, citing The Wall Street Journal, adds that a hoped-for valuation fell to about $1.25 trillion from $1.4 trillion and that the raise was later cut to a target of as much as $20 billion. Source: cio.economictimes.indiatimes.com

Musk may cede Terafab control to TSMC, Digitimes reports: Wccftech

After earlier casting TSMC as a subcontractor at best, Musk is reported by DigiTimes, via Wccftech, to be renegotiating, with operational control and joint investment on the table, including advanced packaging. TSMC chief C.C. Wei said on the latest earnings call that the firm was in discussions with Musk and would have to run the plants for any deal to work. The Terafab concept, as described in the report, envisions more than a terawatt of computing capacity. The account is a report of talks, not a signed contract. Source: wccftech.com

TSMC's Arizona Plant Creates Magnet Effect: BigGo Finance

BigGo Finance reports TSMC's cumulative Arizona investment has reached $265 billion, including an additional $100 billion announced this year, which the piece calls the largest foreign investment on record in the United States. The first fab is operational. TSMC employs about 3,500 people in Arizona and plans to hire 2,500 more. Colliers figures in the article show industrial vacancy in the northwestern Phoenix cluster fell from 16 percent a year earlier to 8 percent in June. Zillow data cited there put six-year home-price gains in North Gateway and Desert View at about 53 percent, against 42 percent for Phoenix overall, and after TSMC took about 900 acres in NorthPark the planned residential scale there was cut by about 20 percent. Source: finance.biggo.com

Also Today

Ace nanochem Develops Three New Semiconductor Materials for Power Devices and Advanced Packaging: Yahoo Finance

Yahoo Finance reports that Ace nanochem has developed three new semiconductor materials aimed at power devices and advanced packaging. Source: finance.yahoo.com

The Teardown: When the Old Well Still Pumps

A token is the barrel. Each GPU generation is a well. EE Times, walking through a Gartner forecast and an a16z rental chart, treats the cost of that barrel as mostly energy plus whether the chip can do the job at all. Easy wells — newer silicon that finishes more work per watt — stay open when the token price falls. Hard wells shut first. Rent sitting above a depreciation line does not make the well profitable: power, cooling, networking, the building and the cost of borrowing still come out.

Gartner's arithmetic, as EE Times reports it, starts with providers spending close to $1 trillion on AI infrastructure in 2026 and another $6 trillion from 2027 through 2030. To pay for that spend, cover borrowing, and still earn a 12 percent return, the owners of those data centers would need about $8.5 trillion in revenue through 2030. Gartner expects the AI revenue that actually reaches those data centers to be $3.6 trillion at most. The gap is what Gartner calls more than $3.5 trillion of spending that could be exposed to write-downs by 2030. EE Times is careful: that is a risk estimate under Gartner's assumptions, not a prediction that the full amount will be written off. On the same arithmetic, more than 80 percent of neoclouds would no longer exist as independent companies.

Set that against the price of the work. Epoch AI, as cited in the piece, estimates that the price of reaching a given benchmark score has fallen by roughly 13 times a year since 2023, on the benchmarks it tracks. Cheaper tokens can pull in new jobs — the Jevons pattern, where a lower cost per use raises total use — but nothing in the chart guarantees that demand grows faster than the price falls. If use only doubles while the price of a scored result drops thirteenfold, revenue still shrinks.

The rental chart is the field log. a16z, using Ornn data as of October 19, drew a straight line from each Nvidia generation's first observed rent down to zero over six years, about the period hyperscalers use to write the servers off. Every generation was renting above that line. The H200 sat 143 percent above it. EE Times notes that most of the gap opened after April, when token demand took off, and that for most of their lives these chips had rented below the line. A busy well can still lose money once the power bill is paid.

The oil picture breaks where tokens are not the same crude. Interactive and agent work wants low latency and the newest memory. Overnight document work, synthetic data and evaluations mostly want a low cost per token and barely notice delay. A chip whose capital is already written off, with power, cooling and upkeep left as the main costs, can be the cheapest barrel for that second market. There is not one token price. There are at least two.

Keeping every generation online stops being the right call when a newer generation is arriving in volume and the token price has fallen far enough that an unpaid-off older chip's energy cost exceeds what even the latency-blind jobs will pay. Until that crossover, the expensive well still pumps, because the alternative is turning away work the new wells cannot yet absorb. Source: eetimes.com

On the Horizon

  • Macronix expects NOR flash, SLC NAND and eMMC prices to rise further in the third and fourth quarters of 2026, then stabilize at elevated levels.
  • Two further TSMC Arizona fabs are expected to be completed by 2030.
  • Microsoft plans to scale its own Maia accelerators to multi-gigawatt deployments from 2027, claiming 40 percent more tokens per megawatt, a figure EE Times says is not independently verified.
