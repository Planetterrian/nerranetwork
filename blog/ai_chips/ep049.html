Two billion dollars in restricted AI chips are tied to the probe now sitting with NVIDIA's board.

By Patrick November 09, 2026 Ep 49 10 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode

What You Need to Know: Two billion dollars in restricted AI chips are now tied to an independent probe NVIDIA's board has opened around Jensen Huang. Korea's memory makers are spending well above 100 trillion won this year while Yangtze Memory raises the share of domestic tools in new NAND fabs. NVIDIA, Rebellions and SK Telecom announced a link that puts Korean-designed processors on NVLink Fusion.

Top Story

NVIDIA's board is initiating an independent investigation into reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, while Tom's Hardware reports Megaspeed has been accused of smuggling two billion dollars worth of AI chips into China through its subsidiary Speedmatrix. A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside independent counsel, will oversee the inquiry; the board says it has no reason to believe national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised, says it has confidence in Huang and his leadership team, and says he supports the review and will cooperate, with further updates as appropriate. TechPowerUp, citing The New York Times, describes a close relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, which is believed to be helping Chinese companies obtain NVIDIA chips barred from shipment to Chinese customers, and says a network of sister companies is building data centers in Malaysia for those customers. Tom's Hardware adds that Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, was allegedly recruited by China's Ministry of State Security, that the suspected operation has not been independently confirmed, and that the relationship is alleged to follow a honeypot approach. Huang wrote that he was unfaithful to his wife, Lori, to whom Tom's Hardware says he has been married for 41 years, that he supports the investigation, and that he is committed to protecting NVIDIA and America's national security; NVIDIA has rejected suggestions of improper dealings, and Chinese officials have dismissed the espionage allegations as unfounded. Tom's Hardware notes Huang has criticized limits on AI GPU supply to China, including calling the "China hawk" label a badge of shame and saying it "doesn't have to be all us or them," drawing fire that included the CEO of Palantir, and that it remains unclear whether he will leave the CEO role as Intel's Brian Krzanich did in June 2018, when Robert Swan became interim chief executive. Source: techpowerup.com Source: tomshardware.com Source: nvidianews.nvidia.com

Silicon

DeepX Tapes Out Second-Gen Chip, Projects 6.7× Performance Gain With LPDDR5x-PIM: EE Times

DeepX has taped out a second-generation AI accelerator aimed at edge markets including robotics. EE Times reports the part can run large language models on device in under 5 W, and the company projects a 6.7× performance gain with LPDDR5x-PIM. Source: eetimes.com

NVIDIA, Rebellions and SK Telecom Announce Collaboration to Advance Sovereign AI Infrastructure With NVIDIA NVLink Fusion: NVIDIA Newsroom

NVIDIA, Rebellions and SK Telecom announced a collaboration to advance Korea's sovereign AI infrastructure, supported by the Ministry of Science and ICT. NVLink Fusion is the link that brings together NVIDIA's open infrastructure platform, Rebellions' Korean-designed AI processors, and SK Telecom's AI data center, model and service capabilities. The same collaboration lays the groundwork for Korean NPU and XPU companies to explore participation in the NVLink Fusion ecosystem. Source: nvidianews.nvidia.com Source: blogs.nvidia.com

NVIDIA Announces New Planned Investments in Korean Startups at the MSIT-NVIDIA AI Startup Partnership Forum: NVIDIA Blog

At the MSIT-NVIDIA AI Startup Partnership Forum at COEX, vice minister Ryu Je-myung and NVIDIA leaders met more than 20 Korean startups, and NVIDIA announced an investment in Point2 Technology. Point2 builds radio-frequency interconnects that move data between chips and racks in AI factories, offered as an alternative to copper and optical links for cluster scale-up. Additional investments are planned in Motif Technologies, RLWRLD and Upstage, and NVIDIA cited more than 1,000 Korea-based members of NVIDIA Inception. Source: blogs.nvidia.com

ASRock Rack Shows Early Xeon 7 Diamond Rapids Motherboard for Up to 4 TB of DDR5: TechPowerUp

ASRock Rack's DMRD16-2L+, shown at the OCP Global Summit 2026 and detailed by ServeTheHome, reaches a theoretical bandwidth of roughly 1.6 TB/s and 4 TB of capacity when all 16 slots hold 256 GB MRDIMMs at up to 12,800 MT/s. Regular RDIMMs on the one-DIMM-per-channel, 16-channel layout top out at 128 GB and 8,000 MT/s. The 12-by-13-inch EEB board has no standard PCIe slots and no OCP NIC 3.0 connector; all I/O goes through up to 16 MCIO x8 connectors, each rated for PCIe 6.0 or CXL 3.0, for 128 lanes, plus one PCIe 5.0 x4 M.2 slot, two Intel I350 Gigabit Ethernet ports, an ASPEED AST2700 BMC with a dedicated management port, and VGA. Intel has the Xeon 7 family slated for a 2027 debut on the LGA9324 socket; the board is aimed at servers, and TechPowerUp says anyone planning a high-memory workstation for local AI will probably keep an eye on it. Source: techpowerup.com

Dell PowerEdge R770AP Review One Fast Intel Xeon 6 Server: ServeTheHome

ServeTheHome reviewed Dell's PowerEdge R770AP, a 2U server built around a dual-socket Xeon 6 setup and 24 channels of DDR5. Source: servethehome.com

Data Centres & Power

Every virtual megawatt needs a meter: Utility Dive

Grid-enhancing technologies, demand response and virtual power plants all rely on the meter, a device utilities are about to replace by the millions. Source: utilitydive.com

Building AI Kill Switches in Data Centers: Data Center Knowledge

A data-center-level kill switch would shut down or isolate AI workloads facility by facility, cutting power, connectivity, or both, without fine-grained control of individual agents or models, and without necessarily stopping every process at once. Cutting the utility feed is unlikely to halt GPU servers, because UPS units and backup generators are built to ride through a grid outage and do not distinguish that outage from an intentional kill; abrupt power loss also risks the hardware, which is why operators resist it. Severing the network can stop external communication while workloads keep running inside the hall, but most modern facilities have multiple upstream carriers, redundant paths and diverse interconnection points, so every external path has to go dark. A single-site cut does not stop a model spread across facilities, and it does nothing once copies have already replicated outside; early detection is what makes the switch useful. Operators are unlikely to volunteer the capability, because the business is uptime, and a regulatory requirement aimed at network disconnection rather than power interruption only works if jurisdictions align — otherwise high-risk workloads move to facilities elsewhere. Source: datacenterknowledge.com

Hundreds arrested after Microsoft data center protest turns violent: Tom's Hardware

A Microsoft data-center site is under construction, and Dutch police arrested 150 to 200 protesters after the action at that construction site turned violent. It is unclear which group entered the site first. The Stop Dirty Data Centers coalition demands a halt to AI data-center expansion, and police seized break-in tools and suspected stolen items from the construction site. Source: tomshardware.com

Supply Chain & Policy

BofA: Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix Near a 'TSMC Moment' — Earnings Durability Is the Key to Re-rating: BigGo Finance

Simon Woo of Bank of America, speaking on November 9, put South Korean memory makers' capital expenditure this year well above 100 trillion won, about $74.1 billion, against combined Chinese memory investment estimated at 20 trillion to 30 trillion won, about $14.8 billion to $22.2 billion, and he pointed to equipment import restrictions that apply only to Chinese companies. At Yangtze Memory, Chinese-made tools are about 20% of equipment at Wuhan fabs 1 and 2, a share expected to rise above 50% at new fab 3, with a plan under discussion to take fab 4 to at least 60% and as much as 70%. Fab 4 is expected to produce NAND with more than 300 stacked memory cells for AI data-center storage. Combined capacity at fabs 1 and 2 is around 200,000 wafers per month, and bringing fab 3 online would lift total capacity by about 25%. Counterpoint Research says YMTC held 14% of global NAND shipments in the second quarter of 2026, overtaking Kioxia for third place, eight percentage points behind SK Hynix at 22%, while a 33 billion yuan, about $4.9 billion, IPO by parent CCSH is temporarily suspended. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, through Solidigm, each run major NAND bases in Xi'an and Dalian, exposing them to U.S. equipment restrictions on China; the prevailing industry view in the piece is that more Chinese tools will not immediately close the technology gap, because high-layer NAND needs precision and stable yields, and enterprise SSD sales turn on reliability and customer relationships. Source: finance.biggo.com

Two Chinese companies cleared to buy banned Nvidia AI chips have empty offices and unknown addresses: Tom's Hardware

New Matrix and Maginfra, both approved to receive H200 chips, appear to have abandoned offices and unknown addresses, and people in the buildings say they are unfamiliar with the companies or have never seen anyone working there. The investigation says the businesses have ties to sanctioned Chinese military suppliers. Source: tomshardware.com

The Teardown: The Capacitor in the Wiring

A one-transistor, one-capacitor cell can sit in the metal stack above a processor instead of taking a die of its own. BigGo Finance describes that cell as back-end-of-line memory: the capacitor and its access transistor are built in the interconnect layers, not in the silicon where the logic transistors are drawn. The channel is an amorphous oxide semiconductor, deposited cold enough that the copper wiring underneath is not put through a process above 400 degrees Celsius. Conventional silicon transistors leak when they are off. The oxide channel's off-current is low enough, in the account BigGo Finance carries from the foundry's own presentation, to wipe out gate-induced drain leakage, which is what lets a capacitor hold charge without a six-transistor latch watching it.

That six-transistor latch is the SRAM bit cell. Six devices per bit is why cache eats area that could have been arithmetic. A capacitor cell uses one transistor and one capacitor. Spread across the wiring above a large processor, the same footprint holds more bits because the memory is no longer competing for the transistor layer. It is also not monolithic 3D DRAM in the usual sense, where memory cells are stacked vertically on a memory die. The array uses the fact that a processor die is typically much larger than a memory die, so the bits can spread out instead of climbing.

The test chip BigGo Finance cites, presented at the 2025 Symposium on VLSI Technology and Circuits, combines memory and logic on a single die and ran at 0.75 volts. It retained data beyond 128 milliseconds, finished random read and write cycles in under 8 nanoseconds, and withstood more than 10^14 program-and-erase cycles. Walk those in order. At 0.75 volts the array sits on a low logic-like rail, so the reported test did not need a separate high-voltage domain just to store a bit. Retention beyond 128 milliseconds means the cell held data for longer than a tenth of a second in that test; a designer still refreshes, but the leakage that forced SRAM's six transistors is the thing the oxide channel is there to cut. Under 8 nanoseconds is the random-cycle time the test chip showed, an access time, not a bandwidth for a stack of DRAM dies. More than 10^14 program-and-erase cycles is the endurance the presentation reported for that test chip.

Frederick Chen, quoted by BigGo Finance, puts the area argument in one comparison. Because the array can use the processor's larger footprint instead of a DRAM die's usually much smaller one, "It could be >10x," so the embedded array would not need as many layers as standalone monolithic 3D DRAM.

The same article records the foundry's public line. A spokesperson said TSMC focuses on its core business, dedicated IC foundry, and that its mission is "to be the trusted technology and capacity provider of the global logic IC industry for years to come." The statement never used the word DRAM. Chao Yu-chun, chair of Winbond Electronics, said a direct entry into conventional DRAM is unlikely, pointing to proprietary process technology and a tangled patent landscape, and noted that TSMC already works with memory makers including Samsung and Winbond on advanced packaging. An embedded layer, in the article's distinction, extends the foundry business. A full DRAM push would put the foundry in the merchant memory market beside Samsung Electronics and SK hynix.

This wiring-layer cell stops being the right substitute when the customer needs a merchant memory die with its own stack, sold beside the logic chip, rather than bits spread through the interconnect of a processor that is already large. If the logic die is not the bigger footprint Chen describes, the greater-than-ten-times area does not show up, and the layer count of a standalone 3D DRAM is back in the problem. An embedded capacitor also does not, on the figures reported, replace a high-bandwidth stack: the test chip's numbers are 0.75 volts, retention beyond 128 milliseconds, access under 8 nanoseconds, and endurance past 10^14 cycles, not a per-stack bandwidth. When the workload is that stack, the foundry's logic-capacity business and the memory makers' dies stay on opposite sides of the package. Source: finance.biggo.com
