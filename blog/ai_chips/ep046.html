Reported China orders of six million RTX PRO 5500s exceed a reported three-million 2027 build.

By Patrick November 06, 2026 Ep 46 10 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Listen to this episode

Top Story Silicon Data Centres & Power Supply Chain & Policy Also Today The Teardown: The Decoder Beside the Qubit On the Horizon

What You Need to Know: Reported orders from China for Nvidia’s RTX PRO 5500 top six million cards, against a source’s ceiling of three million units that can be built in 2027, while a US listing prices the cut-down card above the flagship RTX PRO 6000. Reuters, via Semiconductor Engineering, puts about 600,000 H200 chips in Hong Kong awaiting approval for mainland shipment as Beijing limits imports. A European Commission analysis puts EU fab production costs 40% to 50% above Taiwan, South Korea, and China, and US voters moved to kill or slow data-center projects near them.

Top Story

Chinese buyers have ordered Nvidia’s RTX PRO 5500 past a reported 2027 build ceiling, and a US government reseller lists the cut-down card above the flagship. TechPowerUp reports that SHI Government Solutions listed a PNY-built RTX PRO 5500 at an MSRP of $17,999 and a discounted $17,408.63, while Nvidia’s own US marketplace lists the RTX PRO 6000 at $16,000 even though that card has 24,064 CUDA cores and 96 GB of memory against the 5500’s 21,760 cores and 84 GB. At the discounted listing the lesser card is still around $1,400 more, and Nvidia has not posted an official price since quietly adding the 5500 in September, so TechPowerUp says it is unclear whether $17,999 is Nvidia’s figure or PNY’s. Videocardz, citing Reuters, reports that ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent have ordered more than 3 million cards at roughly $11,400 each, with total orders from China reportedly topping 6 million, while one source said Nvidia can make no more than 3 million of them in 2027. With the B300 barred from China and the H200 reviewed case by case, TechPowerUp calls the 5500 one of the strongest Nvidia GPUs Chinese firms can still buy; the card carries 28 GDDR7 modules, and the outlet says orders ahead of supply are a reason to expect the price to stay high. Source: techpowerup.com

Silicon

Skymizer and MiTAC to Showcase Single-Server Kimi K3 Platform at SC26: HPCwire

Skymizer and MiTAC have announced a joint SC26 showcase aimed at running Moonshot AI’s Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model, on one MiTAC server. The box they describe carries eight Skymizer HTX301 LPU cards and 2 TB of card-attached memory. This is a showcase target, not a stated deployment: the companies say they aim to run that model on the single server. Source: hpcwire.com

Supermicro Launches KV Cache Solution with IBM and NVIDIA: HPCwire

Supermicro on November 5 announced a KV-cache storage solution with IBM and Nvidia for inference. It pairs Supermicro Petascale all-flash storage servers using AMD EPYC 9005 Series processors, Turin, with IBM Storage Scale, the Nvidia Dynamo inference framework, and Spectrum-X Ethernet. The announcement is of the joint stack, not of a named site turning it on. Source: hpcwire.com

Intel Core Ultra 4000 Series "Nova Lake-H" SoC Surfaces: TechPowerUp

A reported die photo adds a measured package map for the Core Ultra 4000 Nova Lake-H part already tied to Intel’s 18A-P process. TechPowerUp describes a 4+8+4 configuration — 4 P-cores, 8 E-cores, and 4 LPE-cores — on a compute die made on Intel 18A-P, in a package of about 12 by 23 mm and 276 mm². The graphics tile, on Intel 3-E, holds 4 Xe GPU cores in roughly 26 mm², beside a 21 mm² dummy die; a 72 mm² 18A-P die hosts the 4 LPE-cores as a central hub, a peripheral control tile measures 58 mm², and the 4P+8E compute tile measures 55 mm², both on 18A-P. The outlet presents this as a raw picture that surfaced, not as an Intel launch. Source: techpowerup.com

AMD "Medusa Point" With 10-Core, 20-Thread CPU Configuration Surfaces: TechPowerUp

An AMD Medusa Point sample has surfaced on Geekbench as a 10-core, 20-thread part split into 4 Zen 6 cores and 6 Zen 6c cores, not as a formal launch. Each core shows 32 KB of L1 instruction cache, 48 KB of L1 data cache, and 1 MB of L2, with a shared 16 MB L3; the listing gives a 2.0 GHz base and a maximum of 1996 MHz, which TechPowerUp treats as a misread of early silicon. The instruction set on the sample includes AVX-512, AVX512-FP16, and AVX512-VNNI, and the test system had 64 GB of DDR5, 55.58 GB usable, at 5,188 MT/s. Talk of an RDNA 4m mobile graphics block remains rumor in the same report; what the listing measured is the CPU configuration and those instruction extensions. Source: techpowerup.com

Data Centres & Power

Google to serve as credit backstop for TeraWulf data center in Kentucky: Data Center Dynamics

Google is to serve as credit backstop for a TeraWulf data center in Kentucky that is contracted to Anthropic under a $19 billion deal. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Bank of England queries speculative data center loans tied to projects without tenants: Data Center Dynamics

The Bank of England is querying speculative data-center loans tied to projects without tenants, trying to understand the scale of risk in the sector. The report is of the inquiry, not of a published exposure total. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Eaton CEO: AC to DC migration for data centers is real: Utility Dive

Eaton chief executive Paulo Ruiz says the migration from AC to DC for data centers “is real,” and that Eaton is investing in solid-state transformers to meet it. He also said the “timing of that transition is not under our control.” Source: utilitydive.com

Anti-Data Center Sentiment Rides Blue Wave, Threatening AI Boom: HPCwire

HPCwire reports that this week’s US election, a major Democratic victory, came with voters moving to kill or slow data-center projects near them. The outlet says that souring local sentiment called the longevity of the current AI buildout into question and punished tech stocks. Source: hpcwire.com

Supply Chain & Policy

Nvidia H200s held in Hong Kong: Semiconductor Engineering

Reuters, via Semiconductor Engineering, reports that approximately 600,000 Nvidia H200 AI chips are being held in Hong Kong awaiting approval for shipment to mainland China. Beijing has limited imports in an effort to reduce reliance on US technology and encourage adoption of domestically developed processors. Source: semiengineering.com

Europe’s Chip Industry: Perfect Storm or Perfect Timing?: EE Times

SEMICON Europa 2026, co-located with electronica in Munich from November 10 to 13, is framed as “Europe’s Next Era: Navigating the Perfect Storm.” A European Commission analysis published in June 2026 finds total fab production costs in EU fabs 40% to 50% higher than in Taiwan, South Korea, and China; in 2024 the EU consumed €55 billion of semiconductors and imported €43.6 billion from outside the bloc, with the sharpest exposure in leading-edge chips for AI and high-performance computing. The European Court of Auditors concluded in 2025 that the first Chips Act strategy is very unlikely to deliver a 20% EU share of global market value by 2030, and the share is now projected at 11.7% by 2030. Chips Act 2.0, published in June 2026 and targeted for adoption in the second quarter of 2027, keeps advanced manufacturing but widens the frame to design, equipment, materials, and advanced packaging, pilot lines, and customers in automotive, industrial systems, and data centers. SEMI Europe president Laith Altimime said, “Europe’s semiconductor competitiveness depends on the strength of the entire ecosystem, from design and materials to equipment, manufacturing, and advanced packaging.” Source: eetimes.com

Micron pauses Taiwan fab work: Semiconductor Engineering

Bloomberg, via Semiconductor Engineering, reports that Micron paused construction of a $4.5 billion memory-chip plant in Taiwan because of a prolonged labor dispute. Micron has not confirmed the suspension. Source: semiengineering.com

MeitY secretary S. Krishnan says India plans a homegrown GPU under Semiconductor Mission 2.0 in about two years and intends to commercialize its SHAKTI and VEGA RISC-V processors while expanding domestic server production. Source: tomshardware.com

Marvell and Lumentum Announce Combination: Semiconductor Digest

Marvell and Lumentum have entered a definitive all-stock agreement for Marvell to acquire Lumentum. Source: semiconductor-digest.com

Entegris Expands Partnership with Kioxia: Semiconductor Digest

Chemours releases two fluids for two-phase liquid cooling: Data Center Dynamics

Chemours released two fluids for two-phase liquid cooling, aimed at direct-to-chip and immersion. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Moonshot AI Looks to Europe to Lease Nvidia Blackwell Clusters, Report Says: BigGo Finance

Moonshot AI is reported to be looking to Europe to lease Nvidia Blackwell clusters. Source: finance.biggo.com

MediaTek Recruits Intel Packaging Veteran Navid Shahriari as AI Chip Push Accelerates: BigGo Finance

MediaTek has recruited Intel packaging veteran Navid Shahriari as its AI chip push accelerates. Source: finance.biggo.com

Taiwan's reported 1nm chip site plan in Tainan: NeoTeo

Arista Lines Up Networking For 10 Gigawatts Of AI Systems: The Next Platform

The Teardown: The Decoder Beside the Qubit

Error correction is a split job. The quantum chip holds a fragile state; a classical machine has to decide, continuously, which error just happened and which fix to apply before that state drifts. HPCwire’s account of the November 5, 2026 announcement from IBM, the Quantum Science Center at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, and AMD describes a bench built for that split. IBM’s piece is the Nighthawk processor: 120 qubits, square-lattice connectivity, a high rate of circuits per second, and pulse control that can act while a circuit is still running. Oak Ridge’s piece is Umbra, powered by AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs and 5th Gen AMD EPYC CPUs. They are co-located so a team can score each error-correction method by qubits, time, samples, and classical cost.

On that quantum hardware a researcher can measure qubits mid-calculation and branch on the result immediately, set the timing of the pulses that guard the qubits, see what the machine actually ran, and work from the raw measurement signal. A code uses many noisy physical qubits to stand in for fewer logical qubits a scientist can trust. The classical decoder reads the syndrome — the pattern of those stabilizer checks — and names the correction. If the name arrives late, it refers to a state that has already moved. Umbra is there so that decoder can be tried on GPU nodes large enough to resemble the classical side a later machine would need, and so competing codes can be ranked by the classical burden they impose. The announcement is explicit that the open question is no longer whether error correction can work on quantum hardware, but how to accelerate and optimize it.

The figures in the HPCwire account separate a modest qubit step from a large operations step. IBM’s recorded experiments have reached up to 70 logical qubits and 438 hard operations. The Department of Energy’s Quantum Genesis mission, through its Q Competition, targets 100 logical qubits running 100,000 hard operations by late 2028. From 70 logical qubits to 100 is 30 more logical qubits, under half again the demonstrated count. From 438 hard operations to 100,000 is the gap the announcement calls roughly 230-fold. Closing that gap, the write-up says, is what the field still has to do. A decoder that keeps up for a few hundred operations has not shown it can keep up when each added operation is another round of measurement and another classical decision. Stretch the circuit by that roughly 230-fold factor and a decoder that was merely fast enough beside Nighthawk becomes the limit, even though the logical-qubit count only had to rise by 30.

This first pairing stops being the right instrument the day the code needs a 121st physical qubit, because 120 is all Nighthawk brings, or the day the question is 100,000 hard operations rather than 438. The announcement says next approaches need those pulse-level controls together on systems large enough to test real codes, and it lists the stack that has to arrive as one piece: concatenated codes, space-time codes, algorithm-aware decoders, hardware-aware compilation, and software orchestration. Until a larger quantum processor shares the bench, Umbra’s job is to price methods in qubits, time, samples, and classical resources, not to deliver the late-2028 operation count. Source: hpcwire.com

Quantum error correction is the mechanism to take it apart, and the split is physical. The chip holds a fragile state. A classical machine has to name the error and the fix before that state drifts. HPCwire's account of the November fifth announcement puts IBM, Oak Ridge's Quantum Science Center, and AMD on one bench for that split. IBM's Nighthawk brings one hundred twenty qubits, square-lattice links, a high circuit rate, and pulse control that can act while a circuit is still running. Oak Ridge's Umbra, with AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs and fifth-generation EPYC CPUs, is the classical side. Together they let a team score each code by qubits, time, samples, and classical cost. On the quantum box a researcher can measure mid-calculation and branch at once, time the guard pulses, see what actually ran, and work from the raw signal. Many noisy physical qubits stand in for fewer logical ones. The decoder reads the syndrome and names the correction. Late means the name no longer matches the state. Umbra is where that decoder gets tried on GPU nodes big enough to resemble a later machine, and where codes get ranked by the classical load they impose. The announcement says the open question is no longer whether correction works on the hardware, but how to speed it. IBM's recorded experiments sit at up to seventy logical qubits and four hundred thirty-eight hard operations. The Department of Energy's Quantum Genesis mission, through the Q Competition, wants one hundred logical qubits and one hundred thousand hard operations by late twenty twenty-eight. Seventy to one hundred is thirty more logical qubits, under half again. Four hundred thirty-eight to one hundred thousand is the gap they call roughly two hundred thirty-fold. A decoder that keeps up for a few hundred operations has not shown it can keep up when every added operation is another measurement round and another classical decision. Nighthawk drops out of the experiment the day the code needs a hundred twenty-first physical qubit, or the day the question is one hundred thousand operations rather than four hundred thirty-eight. Until a larger quantum processor shares that bench, Umbra prices methods. It does not deliver the late-twenty-twenty-eight count. November tenth is on the horizon for TSMC's October revenue figures.
