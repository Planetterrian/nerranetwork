A reported six-point-nine-billion-dollar Huawei licence is not Qualcomm's deal, killing the LogicFolding claim.

What You Need to Know: Qualcomm says a reported six-point-nine-billion-dollar Huawei licence is not its deal, and that LogicFolding is not part of the agreement. Uptime Institute's 2026 outage analysis puts UPS failures at 39 percent of power-related incidents. TrendForce expects TSMC mature-process prices to rise about 3 to 10 percent from January next year.

Top Story

Qualcomm Licenses Huawei's LogicFolding, 5G, and Other Technologies: TechPowerUp

TechPowerUp reported a Qualcomm–Huawei licence estimated at $6.9 billion and, citing Bloomberg, tied it to Huawei's LogicFolding design. That initial account described two-sheet vertical stacking, a 55 percent transistor-density increase, and a 41 percent power-efficiency boost, framed as a way around sanctions and manufacturing limits. At 06:30 UTC on October 6, Qualcomm said the companies entered a multi-year, broad patent cross-licence across a range of technology fields. Terms are confidential. Qualcomm said reports that it is a net payor are incorrect, and that assertions the agreement is related to LogicFold are not accurate. Separately, Qualcomm has agreed to purchase certain Huawei non-cellular U.S. patents across multiple technology areas. Source: techpowerup.com

Silicon

SK Hynix Slips as Foreign Funds Rotate Out of Korean Memory Stocks: AD HOC NEWS

SK Hynix completed joint validation of HBM5 memory with TSMC's CoWoS technology, AD HOC NEWS reports. The step pairs the next HBM generation with TSMC's advanced-packaging flow rather than with a standalone memory qualification. Source: ad-hoc-news.de

Chip Industry Technical Paper Roundup: Oct. 6: Semiconductor Engineering

Semiconductor Engineering's October 6 library add lists a HUST, PolyU, UC Santa Barbara, and National University of Singapore paper on low-contact-resistance ballistic p-type transistors in oxygen-doped WSe₂ monolayers. UC Santa Cruz contributed a design-space study of backside clock meshes for 2 nm GAAFET backside power delivery. Syracuse University, FAU Erlangen-Nürnberg, and TU Dresden added RAPID, on row-parallel arithmetic processing in DRAM. UC Berkeley and FuriosaAI added a characterization of high-bandwidth flash for LLM serving. Source: semiengineering.com

Data Centres & Power

The UPS Is Uninterruptible by Design. Its Future Is Not.: Data Center Knowledge

Data Center Knowledge reports that rack-level batteries, battery energy storage systems, and solid-state transformers are reopening where a UPS sits in the data-centre power train. Uptime Institute's Annual Outage Analysis 2026 found power-related issues behind more than one in five IT service outages over the past three years. Inside those power incidents, UPS failures led at 39 percent, ahead of transfer-switch failures at 34 percent and generator failures at 28 percent. The same research calls lithium-ion UPS batteries a clear contributing factor in a gradual rise in major data-centre fires, and notes the rise may partly track rapid facility build-out rather than battery chemistry alone. Hyperscale operators had already moved to Open Compute Project direct-current designs with distributed or rack-level UPS, including battery backup units, beside or instead of a centralized UPS. Higher-voltage direct current in AI factories, including 800 V DC, plus sidecar power cabinets, extends that distributed pattern toward extreme rack density. Source: datacenterknowledge.com

AI Buildout Meets the Power Grid: Morgan Stanley Sees a 32 GW Shortfall by 2028: igor'sLAB

Morgan Stanley sees a 32 GW shortfall by 2028 where the AI buildout meets the power grid, igor'sLAB reports. The figure is a grid-scale gap, not a single campus rating. Source: igorslab.de

Supply Chain & Policy

TSMC Foundry Prices Expected to Rise Up to 10% Next Year, Signaling Domino Effect Across Fabless Industry: BigGo

TrendForce says TSMC is planning to raise mature-process prices by about 3 to 10 percent starting January next year, BigGo reports. TSMC has reportedly decided on an additional 6 to 8 percent increase on 2 nm wafer prices in the first quarter of next year, citing manufacturing costs and electricity expenses. Individual customers have already seen increases of 10 to 20 percent in their pricing. Suppliers of power-management ICs, display-driver ICs, and microcontrollers are considering product-price rises of 5 percent to double-digit levels between late this year and early next year. SMIC has already raised prices on some demand-concentrated capacity for wafers produced from the third quarter; its second-quarter average wafer selling price rose 5.7 percent quarter over quarter, and utilization reached 93.7 percent. Some customers are pulling orders forward, citing capacity risk from Middle East conflict concerns and growing AI demand. Source: finance.biggo.com

TSMC expansion pushes Da Yi Cheng Technology's orders over NT$10 billion, visibility through 2028: DigiTimes

TSMC's expansion has pushed Da Yi Cheng Technology's orders over NT$10 billion, DigiTimes reports. Order visibility runs through 2028. Source: digitimes.com

The Teardown: Why the Cell Loses a Track When p Sits on n

A standard-cell track is a wiring lane that sets how tall the cell is. Gate-all-around nanosheet transistors, in imec's roadmap as published by Semiconductor Engineering, scale through the A10 logic generation and there enable 5.5-track cells. From the A7 node, imec says industry may move to a complementary FET: pMOS stacked on nMOS, so the two polarities stop sitting side by side and the cell can drop to 4.5 tracks and below. Push that stack further and the same roadmap puts cells at 3 tracks, which imec says can carry classical CMOS logic out to at least the A3 node. The height cut is not free silicon. Contacts, gates, and power still have to reach the buried device, which is why imec's design-technology work treats a backside contact and a gate stack that can set more than one threshold voltage as CFET-specific modules. Part 2 of that work, the piece dated for this window, adds a sequential-CFET cell that can be built as split-gate or common-gate. Split-gate keeps the two devices independently controllable, which logic and SRAM both need; common-gate is the tighter option when the pair should switch together. The SRAM result in that study is a 5-track-plus-one CFET bit cell. A second study in the same article works on back-end-of-line routability, because a shorter cell only helps if the wires above it can still escape. At A7, imec also leaves another path open: CMOS 2.0, partitioning a system-on-chip into heterogeneous tiers and stitching them with 3D interconnects, with power from the front or the back if nanosheets are pushed instead. The stacked cell stops being the right next step when the buried device cannot get an independent gate and a backside contact without giving the track height back to extra routing.
