Forty-four billion dollars in notes will fund SpaceX's Vera Rubin purchase.

By Patrick November 21, 2026 Ep 61 7 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode

What You Need to Know: Apollo and Goldman Sachs have priced about forty-four billion dollars in notes to finance SpaceX's purchase of Nvidia Vera Rubin chips and related equipment. High-bandwidth memory is projected to take 23 percent of the three big suppliers' DRAM wafer starts in 2026 and nearly 30 percent in 2027. In Osaka, a 48-megawatt hall under a CyrusOne and KEP joint venture has topped out.

Top Story

Apollo Global Management and Goldman Sachs priced about $44 billion in senior and subordinated notes to buy GPUs and related equipment for SpaceX, citybiz reported, after the book was oversubscribed and the financing was increased. The companies call it the largest GPU financing executed to date. The notes back SpaceX's terrestrial compute build with Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips. Funds and accounts managed by BlackRock and Goldman Sachs Asset Management, plus capital accounts advised by KKR, joined as anchor commitments. A BASENOR account of the same announcement says the hardware sits in Saturn Funding Trust, which leases it back to SpaceX so the debt stays off the corporate balance sheet, and that lenders required the GPUs — the collateral — to remain on Earth in U.S. data centers majority-owned, controlled, and operated by SpaceX. Lease schedules start when the equipment is delivered in the first half of 2027. Apollo partner Jamshid Ehsani said SpaceX is "one of the most ambitious builders of compute in the world." Source: citybiz.co Source: basenor.com

Silicon

HBM Memory Shortage: 23% of DRAM Wafers, 30% by 2027: shattered.io

TrendForce projects that high-bandwidth memory will take 23 percent of DRAM wafer input at Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron in 2026 and nearly 30 percent in 2027, shattered.io reported, because HBM needs three to four times as much wafer capacity as general-purpose DRAM. A shortage tracker summarized by Futu News puts HBM supply at roughly 58 percent of estimated first-quarter demand. TrendForce expects conventional DRAM contract prices to rise 10 to 15 percent quarter over quarter in the fourth quarter, while NAND is falling. SK hynix says its HBM and related DRAM are sold out through 2029, and Micron reportedly covers only half to two-thirds of customer demand. SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won said the current shortage could continue until 2030, and SK hynix chief executive Kwak Noh-jung said customer demand will remain higher than supply capacity even beyond 2030. New capacity from China's CXMT clouds the 2028 outlook, and major new memory fabs are not expected to add meaningful output before around 2028. Source: shattered.io

NVIDIA Vera CPU coming to CoreWeave with 3x faster agent sandboxes: CoreWeave

Nvidia's Vera CPU is coming soon to CoreWeave, the company posted, and it is described there as the first CPU purpose-built for AI agents. Diana Aung, senior product manager at Nvidia, shared a measurement of more than 3x faster agent-sandbox startup than on an alternative x86 CPU. The figure is a startup-time comparison, not a claim that the CPU has shipped into CoreWeave's cloud. Source: x.com

World’s First Mass Production of Ultrahigh-Mobility Oxide Technology Paves the Way for a New Generation of Displays: Semiconductor Digest

TCL CSOT has mass-produced ultrahigh-mobility oxide technology on an 8.6-generation line, Semiconductor Digest reported, and calls the run a world first. The stated electron mobility is about 50 square centimetres per volt per second. Source: semiconductor-digest.com

Data Centres & Power

CyrusOne and KEP top out 48MW data center in Osaka, Japan: Data Center Dynamics

Under construction in Osaka, a 48-megawatt data center from CyrusOne and KEP has topped out, Data Center Dynamics reported. It is the first facility under the pair's Japanese joint venture. Topped out is not the same as energized. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Microsoft files for 316.7-acre data center campus in Mecklenburg County, Virginia: Data Center Dynamics

Announced by filing, Microsoft is seeking a 316.7-acre data center campus in Mecklenburg County, Virginia, Data Center Dynamics reported. The plan is five data center buildings on the site. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

US Senate passes bill requiring data centers to pay more for grid infrastructure: Data Center Dynamics

The U.S. Senate has passed a bill requiring data centers to pay more for grid infrastructure, Data Center Dynamics reported. The bill now heads to the House. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Supply Chain & Policy

AI Server Boards Are Now Draining the PCB Supply Chain: TechPowerUp

The Global Electronics Association, formerly IPC, warned that AI servers are taking board capacity and materials, TechPowerUp reported. North American PCB bookings are up 56.1 percent this year through September, while shipments have grown 13.3 percent. Lead times for high-layer boards are 16 to 20 weeks or more; the outlet said some PCB orders were taking up to 30 months, and that motherboard prices rose roughly 15 to 50 percent. A typical board has about 12 layers, while AI server boards are moving to 40 to 60 layers or more, so each board consumes more glass fabric, resin, copper foil, and laminate. At least $10 billion has gone into PCB plants in Southeast Asia and China over the past three years, and the association expects the shortage to last through at least the first half of 2027, with meaningful relief unlikely before mid-2028. Source: techpowerup.com

US chip export curbs impact Alibaba Cloud, Tencent data center growth: ET Datacenters

Alibaba Cloud expects reduced long-term data center expansion and capital-expenditure cuts as a new U.S. policy bans remote access to advanced AI chips, ET Datacenters reported. The same note says the Bureau of Industry and Security allowed Nvidia and AMD to sell 879,000 AI chips to military-linked Chinese entities, and that Alibaba and Tencent, listed as Chinese military companies, received approval for chip sales. Source: datacenters.economictimes.indiatimes.com

YTL Power denies role in alleged Nvidia chip diversion to China, says US curbs will not affect data centres: The Malaysian Reserve

YTL Power denies any role in an alleged diversion of Nvidia chips to China, The Malaysian Reserve reported. The company says the U.S. curbs will not affect its data centres. Source: themalaysianreserve.com

Also Today

Gordon Bell Prize Goes to Chinese Team for Exascale FeMoco Solution: HPCwire

A 10-member Chinese team won the ACM Gordon Bell Prize at SC26 for Closing the FeMo-Cofactor “Quantum-Advantage” Gap with Exascale Supercomputers, using conventional supercomputers on a quantum-chemistry problem. Source: hpcwire.com

RIKEN Confirms Theft of 430 Private Keys in Fugaku and RIKYU Intrusion: HPCwire

RIKEN now confirms theft of 430 private keys in the intrusion on the Fugaku and RIKYU supercomputers, after the November 18 warning of possible personal-data exposure. Source: hpcwire.com

The Teardown: Reading another PC's memory over USB4

Remote Direct Memory Access lets a workload on one machine touch memory on another without the usual copy through the operating system. Microsoft has turned that path on for Windows 11 over USB4, so several PCs can be daisy-chained and share a job. TechPowerUp's account of the work is a latency measurement, not a bandwidth bake-off, and the number that matters is how long a message takes to go and come back.

USB4 is the pipe, rated at up to 80 gigabits per second. Ethernet can already run over that same cable, and a UDP packet on that Ethernet path is the comparison Microsoft rejected. UDP still climbs the network stack: the message is wrapped, queued, copied, and unwrapped on the far side before the other PC's memory is involved. RDMA is built so the transfer lands in the other machine's memory with the CPU on that machine mostly out of the way. The engineering bet is that the stack, not the copper, was the wait.

The worked example is two PCs and a sweep of message size. TechPowerUp reports round-trip latency of 28 to 50 microseconds for RDMA over USB4. The same sweep on Ethernet over USB4 using UDP landed between 116 and 194 microseconds. At the fast end, 28 against 116; at the slow end, 50 against 194. The outlet calls the RDMA path roughly four times faster. Those four bounds are the whole published result: two machines, one cable family, two protocols, latency versus message size. The illustration of DGX Spark boxes linked by Ethernet is explicitly not this USB4 setup, so it does not add a third data point.

Eighty gigabits per second is the other limit, and it does not show up in a microsecond measurement of a short message. A short message finishes before the pipe is full, so the 28-to-50-microsecond access is what the user feels, and the 116-to-194-microsecond UDP path is the tax of the stack. Stretch the message and the bytes have to occupy that 80-gigabit USB4 pipe for longer, while the protocol gap stays in the same band the measurement already drew. Daisy-chained Windows 11 PCs stop being the right fabric when the job is limited by how many bytes that 80-gigabit pipe can move, not by the 28-to-50-microsecond memory access — a wider network then finishes the step, and the fourfold latency win no longer decides it. Source: techpowerup.com
