Samsung plans fivefold Xi'an V9 NAND growth, to fifty thousand wafers, for AI SSDs.

By Patrick November 25, 2026 Ep 65 9 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Follow AI Chips & Data Centres Daily Apple Podcasts RSS
Listen to this episode

What You Need to Know: Samsung is planning to lift 286-layer V9 NAND at its Xi'an X2 fab from about 10,000 wafers a month to about 50,000 by the end of 2027, aimed at enterprise SSDs for AI data centers. Chinese cloud buyers are cutting 2027 memory orders, and TrendForce now puts that year's NAND revenue near $419.3 billion and DRAM revenue at $1.15 trillion. In Phoenix, a utility contract would take TSMC toward 1.88 gigawatts.

Top Story

Samsung is planning a large internal conversion of V9 NAND at its second Xi'an fab, not a new factory, TechPowerUp reports from Seoul Economic Daily and industry sources. The X2 line now turns out about 10,000 300 mm wafers a month of 286-layer V9; the plan is about 50,000 by the end of 2027, aimed at enterprise SSDs for AI data centers. An electroplating line would go into empty cleanroom space, with equipment moving in around February 2027 and the conversion finished by November 2027, while 20% to 30% of the fab's existing V6 line would also switch to V9. Samsung has previously denied claims of expanding production capacity, so the report reads as a reshuffle inside an existing fab even as the wafer count rises sharply. NAND spot prices have already started falling. Source: techpowerup.com

Silicon

Cuts to Chinese CSP Demand Expecting to Slow Memory Industry Revenue Growth in 2027: TechPowerUp

TrendForce says Chinese cloud service providers have reduced their 2027 memory orders, putting downward pressure on the industry price outlook. NAND Flash is expected to enter a downward pricing cycle as supply keeps expanding, and TrendForce has lowered its 2027 NAND Flash revenue forecast to about US$419.3 billion. DRAM is still expected to stay undersupplied, but quarterly price increases are projected to moderate. That revision takes the 2027 DRAM revenue forecast down to $1.15 trillion. Source: techpowerup.com

Intel Wins Microsoft Packaging Order but Hits '35% Yield' Wall in Bid to Enter Google TPU Supply Chain: BigGo Finance

BigGo Finance reports that Intel has won a Microsoft packaging order. The same report says a 35% yield wall is in the way of a bid to enter the Google TPU supply chain. Source: finance.biggo.com

LOTA caches data on GPU nodes at up to 7 GB/s per GPU: CoreWeave

CoreWeave says robotics data is 3D scenes and sensor streams, and a single physical AI program can generate petabytes of it, reread throughout training. On CoreWeave, LOTA, the Local Object Transport Accelerator, caches that data on GPU nodes at up to 7 GB/s per GPU so the GPUs train instead of waiting on reads. The post says the pieces then have to run as one loop. Source: x.com

Data Centres & Power

TSMC Arizona offers a look at what could come to Brazos County: KBTX

TSMC's north Phoenix campus is under construction toward a contracted load of about 1.88 gigawatts, KBTX reports, with the first fab already in production since late 2024. Arizona Public Service signed a direct pricing contract on October 16 to ramp service to about 1.88 gigawatts for fabs and advanced packaging over an initial 25-year term. Engineers at the site's water resource center say the campus uses 4.75 million gallons of water a day, mostly reclaimed wastewater plus a small amount of groundwater, all through the City of Phoenix, and TSMC says it recycles 65% of intake, about 3.5 million gallons a day. Arizona's Family, cited by KBTX, put the first fab at about 5,300 acre-feet a year, roughly 1.7 billion gallons, enough for about 15,000 Phoenix households; Sarah Porter of Arizona State University's Kyl Center estimated about 15% or slightly more evaporates. The campus also makes nearly 15 megawatts of solar on site, with panels doubling as covered parking for about 4,000 vehicles. The first three fabs are estimated to employ 6,000 people, up from about 3,500; the county lists the project as an active Title V air-permit source; the 2025 sustainability report says most pollutants were reduced 95% or more and volatile organic compounds by 98.9%; and TSMC says it recycles 96% of waste, with less than 1% reaching a landfill. Source: kbtx.com

Google plans 1.2GW data center near Châteauroux, France: Data Center Dynamics

Google has announced a data-center plan near Châteauroux, France, and Data Center Dynamics reports a connection request for a second data center in the area. The headline states 1.2GW while the story URL reads 12GW, so the capacity unit in the visible text is ambiguous. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Supply Chain & Policy

US lawmakers from both parties pressure Nvidia, call for tighter chip export controls on China: Zaobao

Zaobao, citing Reuters, reports that Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer wrote on X on Tuesday, November 24, that Jensen Huang shipping highly advanced chips to China was akin to selling rocket engines to the Soviet Union during the space race, and alleged a violation of US export-control laws. Republican Senator Dave McCormick said Congress must get to the bottom of what happened and then strengthen export controls so China cannot reach the most advanced chips through a back door. Democratic Representatives Frank Pallone, Gregory Meeks, Jamie Raskin and Robert Garcia wrote to President Donald J. Trump asking how much he knew about ties between Huang and suspected Chinese government agents, requesting administration correspondence with Nvidia's board, and asking whether a personal relationship had influenced the handling. Senate Banking Committee chairman Tim Scott said Huang would attend a public hearing on December 3; a closed-door briefing by three House committees is expected on December 1. Republican Representative Pat Harrigan said reinstating Huang as chief executive at this time was inappropriate and sent a dangerous signal. Zaobao notes its English text is an AI translation and that the original Chinese remains authoritative. Source: zaobao.com.sg

TSMC tax break application heads to governor and school district: KBTX

One day after the filing, Deputy Comptroller Noah Betz signed a recommendation on November 24 that TSMC meets Jobs, Energy, Technology and Innovation eligibility for a Brazos County logic plant at 2-nanometer or better. The comptroller's report estimates a $354 million school property-tax break over 20 years, while the project would still generate about $405 million in school operating taxes in that time; those figures are projections, and the state would replace the revenue College Station ISD loses. The break covers maintenance-and-operations taxes for 10 years, not the debt-service portion. The report says the project would need to meet a minimum of 35 jobs at a required wage of at least $73,603. One no from the governor or the district stops the deal, and the project remains subject to a final investment decision. Source: kbtx.com

Also Today

Taipower to pour NT$109.6 billion into grid for TSMC-led chip boom in southern Taiwan: digitimes

Digitimes reports Taipower will put NT$109.6 billion into the grid for a TSMC-led chip expansion in southern Taiwan. Source: digitimes.com

HBM Prices to More Than Double with Big Tech Entry: Chosun

Chosun reports HBM prices are set to more than double as Big Tech enters the market. Source: chosun.com

Winbond Widens Edge AI Reach, Certifies NOR Flash: EE Times

EE Times reports Winbond has certified NOR flash while widening its edge AI memory reach. Source: eetimes.com

Singapore, US work on mechanism for chip export control: Singapore Business Review

Singapore Business Review reports Singapore and the United States are working on a mechanism for chip export control. Source: sbr.com.sg

US Congress advances three AI chip export bills as CEO returns to Nvidia: digitimes

Digitimes reports the US Congress is advancing three AI chip export bills as Nvidia's chief executive returns. Source: digitimes.com

Chip Industry Technical Paper Roundup: Nov. 25: Semiconductor Engineering

Semiconductor Engineering's November 25 roundup points to work on hybrid-bonded 3D NAND analog in-memory compute, defect-aware chiplet matching, HBM latency reduction for GPU systems, backside clock meshes, post-quantum hardware verification, CMP virtual metrology, and e-beam stitching losses in silicon-on-insulator waveguides. Source: semiengineering.com

The Teardown: The Core Count Left in the First Weights

A published weight file still carries a trace of the machine that first filled it. Training does not start from a blank tensor. A library writes an initial pattern, and that pattern is a function of how the initialization kernel was launched: how many streaming multiprocessors took part, and how the random streams were mapped onto them. Israel Defense, reporting a forensic study by Unemploymentlabs, says researchers reconstructed those initial values from publicly released model weights and then searched for the hardware and initialization configurations that reproduce the numbers exactly. The evidence is in the weights themselves, not in a leak or an insider account.

The discriminator they publish is the core count. A Blackwell B200 or B300 signature in this method is 148 streaming multiprocessors. A GB200 or GB300 signature is 152. Six models released since April 2026 by DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group contain patterns consistent with Blackwell, while earlier models from the same firms line up with Hopper. DeepSeek-V4-Flash and MiniMax-M3 match the 148-core B200 or B300 pattern. DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3, Kimi K3.1-Pro and Ling-3.1-flash match the 152-core GB200 or GB300 pattern. For DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Kimi K3, the match was checked on physical Blackwell chips, not only in a search over configurations. DeepSeek-V3, Kimi K2.5 and Ling-3.0-flash stay on the Hopper side of the same test. Kimi K2.7-Code sits in between: Hopper-era weights, plus a minor portion consistent with B200 or B300. That is a generation change with a date window, not a single ambiguous file.

The control set is the part that makes the count usable. The method was run on 24 models whose developers had already disclosed the hardware. Every one matched the disclosure, including Nvidia's Nemotron-3 Ultra, which showed the same 152-core signature. Israel Defense says those correlations are far stronger than a statistical-chance explanation. The only other configuration the researchers identified that could mimic the pattern is custom-configured AMD hardware, which is itself restricted for export to China. That alternative was ruled out for DeepSeek-V4-Flash and called highly improbable for the other five of the six.

The fingerprint names how the weights were generated. It supports the hypothesis that Blackwell also ran the training, and it does not prove that hypothesis. It also does not say where the chips sat, how access was obtained, or whether an export rule was broken. The design choice — treat a reproduced initialization, verified on silicon for two models and against 24 disclosed cases, as evidence of Blackwell use — stops being the right instrument when the question is a place, a shipment, or a violation, or when a later training stack could have left the initialization's machine behind. At that point the 148-versus-152 split still describes the first fill, and it no longer identifies the training run. Source: israeldefense.co.il
