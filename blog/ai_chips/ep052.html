Seventeen degrees in fifteen seconds: Waterloo's droplets cut simulated hotspots for data-centre cooling.

By Patrick November 12, 2026 Ep 52 10 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Listen to this episode

What You Need to Know: University of Waterloo researchers reported a hybrid cooler that uses tiny drops of coolant to cut simulated chip hotspots by up to 17°C in 15 seconds, aimed at cooling AI data centres more effectively and economically. Top Story

University of Waterloo researchers are developing a hybrid cooling method that puts tiny drops of coolant on chip hotspots, HPCwire reported on November 12. The method cut the temperature of simulated chip hotspots by up to 17°C in 15 seconds. The group says data centres at the centre of the AI buildout could be cooled more effectively and economically with the system, which is still under development. Source: hpcwire.com

Silicon

AMD Announces 6th Gen EPYC Embedded 9006 Series Processors: AMD

AMD announced the 6th Gen EPYC Embedded 9006 Series on the SP7 socket, built on Zen 6. The line goes up to 256 cores and adds PCIe Gen 6 and CXL 3.1 connectivity. AMD is offering seven-year product longevity for the embedded parts. Source: x.com

Compal Unites NVIDIA Compute and Networking at SC26: HPCwire

Compal will show NVIDIA-based AI servers at Supercomputing 2026, November 17–19, at Booth 5008 in McCormick Place, Chicago. The lineup includes the air-cooled SGX30-2 and the direct liquid-cooled OG430-2-L1 on NVIDIA HGX B300, plus the OG231-2-L1 accelerated server with NVIDIA HGX Rubin NVL8, with NVIDIA MGX and RTX PRO called out for enterprise AI and visual computing. Compal will also show the NVIDIA Vera CPU Compute Tray, and for clusters the NVIDIA BlueField-4 DPU, ConnectX-9 SuperNIC and Spectrum-6 Ethernet switches. Its Daxi AI Server Manufacturing Center in Taiwan opened in August 2026, alongside manufacturing investments in Texas and Vietnam. Alan Chang, vice president of Compal's Infrastructure Solutions Business Group, said high-speed interconnects and integrated system architectures are essential to scaling AI infrastructure efficiently. Source: hpcwire.com

This is the Meta MetaRoCE Network Adapter: ServeTheHome

ServeTheHome reported that Meta showed its MetaRoCE network adapter at OCP Summit 2026. The adapter is designed to scale to million-GPU training clusters. Source: servethehome.com

Data Centres & Power

Struggling marijuana farmers pivot to becoming AI data center sites: Tom's Hardware

In Arizona, former dispensary owner Moe Asnani told Arizona Public Media that cultivation is being abandoned and that existing grow houses could be reused as mini data-centre nodes, after state cannabis sales peaked in 2024 and oversupply pushed prices down. Forbes described a 32,850-square-foot cannabis facility in Kalamazoo, Michigan, wired for 1.5 megawatts, expandable to 2.5 megawatts in under a year and to 15 megawatts longer term, with fibre and HVAC already in place. Tucson City Council member Paul Cunningham said reusing buildings that already drew that power and water is more acceptable than a new data centre on the edge of town, and the piece said even OpenAI and Anthropic are interested in smaller sites as large campuses draw opposition. Niki Krear of New Lake Capital Partners told MJBizDaily that operators want affordable power, dense fibre and favourable tax policy together. The same account said data centres often need two to three times the power a grow already has, that upgrades can take up to five years, and that smaller sites face zoning, moratoriums, neighbour opposition and higher per-unit upgrade prices because suppliers discount large orders. Source: tomshardware.com

Energy storage firm Torus launches prefabricated modular data center product, raises $500m: Data Center Dynamics

Energy-storage firm Torus launched a prefabricated modular data-centre product, Torus Foundry. It raised $500 million to scale that product. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Chatsworth Products Showcases Rack, Power, and Cooling Solutions for AI and HPC at SC26: HPCwire

Chatsworth Products, based in Simi Valley, California, said it will show infrastructure for AI and high-performance computing at SC26. The showcase includes its OCP Open Rack V3, previews of upcoming power and cooling products, and integrated liquid-cooling gear for high-density rooms. Source: hpcwire.com

Supply Chain & Policy

Malaysia moves to tighten data centre controls amid scrutiny over Nvidia chip exports: The Vibes

Investment, Trade and Industry Minister Datuk Seri Johari Abdul Ghani said the government is considering a law that would make companies planning data centres declare their operators, facility locations and the number of GPUs they intend to import, so chips under US export controls stay in domestic use and are not diverted. He said the country must not be used for what is defined as an illegal transaction. Malaysia introduced export controls last year on the movement of high-performance AI chips of US origin. Johari said there are no plans to investigate Speedmatrix, the Malaysian subsidiary of Singapore-based Megaspeed, because he does not see a law that applies if GPUs are bought for the buyer's own data centres; Megaspeed chairman and chief executive James Tan Yong Pong said, in a statement Reuters cited, that the firm takes US export-control duties seriously and will cooperate with Washington. Johari said trade and Taiwanese investment continue, Malaysia has followed the One-China policy since 1974, and both sides remain willing to negotiate under a 1993 agreement. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim told the Dewan Rakyat the postponed sessions were the Economic Cooperation Committee, the Trade Working Committee and the Investment Working Committee, not all trade and investment talks, and that Kuala Lumpur has already asked for those meetings to resume. Source: thevibes.com

TSMC confirms employee data leak as hackers seek 3 BTC for the records: Crypto Briefing

TSMC said on November 12 that personal data on some current and former employees was exposed through a third-party cloud system, and that its internal systems, wafer production and customer data were not breached. Hackers listed about 46,000 records totalling 6.9 GB on DarkForums and BreachForums for 3 bitcoin, about $220,000 or NT$7.4 million, and threatened to publish the set if it was unsold within a week. A sample file named tsmc.txt held 499 records with 19 fields, including names, national IDs, home addresses, phone numbers, emails, bank information, job titles and emergency contacts. TSMC said the leaked content matches a partner cloud system, and that it is notifying affected people and working with the vendor, law enforcement and outside cybersecurity firms. The Taiwan Information Security Association told Current Weekly the records could support phishing and longer-term poaching of advanced-process staff. In June 2023 LockBit demanded $70 million after hitting hardware supplier Kinmax rather than TSMC's own network; KuCoin noted TSMC's third-quarter 2026 net profit of NT$763.064 billion, with advanced-process revenue at 79 percent. Source: cryptobriefing.com · kucoin.com

Jensen Huang's affair with alleged spy revives push to tighten China chip curbs ahead of Trump-Xi summit: CNBC

Days before President Donald Trump is due to meet Xi Jinping at the APEC summit in Shenzhen on November 18–19, CNBC reported that the Jensen Huang scandal is strengthening lawmakers who want the AI Overwatch Act, the Chip Security Act and the MATCH Act written into this year's National Defense Authorization Act. Semafor reported that China this week extended the current truce until January, and Bloomberg analyst Michael Deng, a former Commerce Department official, said Beijing will know Trump may not be able to hold commitments he makes. The Information, cited by CNBC and by Yahoo Finance, says the Commerce Department is finalizing a rule to stop Chinese AI firms from remotely using advanced chips in data centres outside China. CNBC, citing The Information, said Alice Huang helped facilitate at least $7.5 billion in Nvidia chip shipments to China, and it cited a May Culper Research estimate that more than 20 percent of Nvidia revenue for the year ended July 2025, about $43.2 billion, came from China through illegal GPU diversion and Southeast Asian intermediaries. Paul Triolo of DGA-Albright Stonebridge Group said Chinese officials have been clear they will answer passage of the MATCH Act, including by tightening controls on rare earths and magnets. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said in September that negotiators had taken export controls off the table as a national security issue; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Tuesday he wants a constructive relationship based on balance and reciprocity; Trump said Sunday he did not know whether Huang would join the trip but would certainly take him. Source: cnbc.com · semafor.com · finance.yahoo.com

Also Today

Chinese universities obtain Nvidia chips despite US export controls: Digitimes

Reuters, as carried by Digitimes, reported that several Chinese universities, including Northwestern Polytechnical University, one of China's Seven Sons of National Defense, have obtained Nvidia chips despite US export controls. Source: digitimes.com

Hardware-Based Location Checks Target AI Chip Smuggling (Texas A&M): Semiconductor Engineering

Texas A&M researchers published a paper, “5G CATS: 5G-Based Cryptographic Authorization Against Trafficking of Semiconductor Devices,” arguing that round-trip time to a trusted landmark can bound where an AI accelerator is operating after it ships, though jitter in honest replies forces the verifier to loosen that bound. Source: semiengineering.com

The Teardown: The Voltage Where Delay Bends

Near threshold, a transistor sits so close to the voltage where it just begins to conduct that a small sag in the supply stretches delay much more than the same sag would on a normal rail. Semiconductor Engineering's November 12 account of edge-AI system-on-chip design places that regime at about 0.4 to 0.5 volts in advanced FinFET processes. Energy per operation falls as the rail falls. Dynamic energy tracks the square of voltage, so the same switching activity on a lower rail spends sharply less energy. What arrives with that saving is delay that grows rapidly, device-to-device variation that widens, and noise margins that shrink. Timing paths become sensitive to small voltage changes. IR drop and local voltage droop become first-order effects. A guard band drawn from nominal-voltage behaviour stops predicting the silicon, and models taken at the nominal voltage no longer describe the part.

Memory is often the floor under that rail. A single-rail memory with silicon-validated assist features can support operation down to about 600 millivolts, which lets the designer take the V-squared energy cut without splitting the power grid. A dual-rail memory separates the logic supply from the memory supply so compute can drop further while the array stays where it still holds state. Semiconductor Engineering points to an ISSCC 2025 result: a dual-rail SRAM at 38 megabits per square millimetre, logic at 380 millivolts and the memory rail at 540 millivolts, built in a 3-nanometre FinFET. Ultra-low-power register files hold local data next to compute. The split of jobs follows the block. Multiply-accumulate tiles want small, distributed SRAM. Activation units want those register files with wide data paths. Shared system buffers want a high-bandwidth AXI interface.

Logic libraries have to be built for the bend in the curve, not borrowed from a nominal-voltage deck. The account calls for moment-based LVF timing and noise models across a wide process-voltage-temperature range, delay and slew models that capture the nonlinear sensitivity near threshold, and extra checks for MIS effects, pulse-width integrity, skew interactions and mixed-device behaviour, because noise margins are thinner. Silicon validation, including extended timing and functional sweeps, is what keeps those models honest. I/O is the quiet veto on the whole plan. Control interfaces such as GPIO, I3C, I2C and SWI3S, high-speed links such as LVDS and RGMII, clock and reference inputs, SD and eMMC, test I/O, and 2.5D or 3D integration I/O for chiplets each have to run consistently across the supplies the core wants. If the periphery cannot, it sets the system minimum voltage, and the core's near-threshold work never appears in the product.

A closed loop among foundation IP, EDA tools and silicon feedback is what the account says makes operation around 0.4 to 0.5 volts predictable. Memories, cell libraries and I/Os have to be chosen and validated early. The near-threshold rail stops being the right choice when the memory or the I/O cannot follow the compute supply down, or when the extra delay and variation force guard bands that hand back the energy the lower voltage was meant to buy. In that case a dual-rail split, or a rail held above the sharp part of the delay curve, is what keeps a real-time edge workload inside its timing budget. Source: semiengineering.com
