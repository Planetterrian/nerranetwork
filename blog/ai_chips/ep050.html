TSMC's 17.61 billion dollar October shows AI chip demand still filling the foundry.

By Patrick November 10, 2026 Ep 50 10 min read Listen to podcast Watch on YouTube
Listen to this episode
Contents
Top Story Silicon Data Centres & Power Supply Chain & Policy Also Today The Teardown: The Insertion That Cannot See the Next Model On the Horizon

What You Need to Know: TSMC's October consolidated revenue reached 17.61 billion dollars, the foundry's clearest monthly print yet that AI chip orders are still filling leading-edge capacity. The month beat August's record, and the company's own fourth-quarter range still implies two more heavy months. Separately, filings described to Bloomberg put Nvidia B300 servers inside China, and that report is now sitting beside a live congressional push on export bills.

Top Story

TSMC reported October consolidated revenue of NT$586.89 billion, which ANI put at 17.61 billion dollars, up 59.7 percent from October 2025 and 14.7 percent from September's NT$511.86 billion (15.36 billion dollars). Asia Business Daily notes that print cleared the prior monthly record, August's NT$514.81 billion, and puts October near 23.8 trillion won; October 2025 had been NT$367.47 billion, or 11.02 billion dollars. January through October reached NT$4,485.62 billion, about 134.57 billion dollars, up 43.3 percent from 93.91 billion dollars a year earlier. The company's own fourth-quarter forecast remains 52.3 billion to 54.3 billion dollars; the midpoint, 53.3 billion, is 58 percent above the year-ago quarter and 14 percent above the prior quarter, and readers of that range expect November and December each at NT$538.1 billion to NT$569.9 billion. A second Asia Business Daily report, citing United Daily News and CNBC, says the 2-nanometer process has entered mass-production ramp, that 3-nanometer and 5-nanometer demand from NVIDIA, AMD and cloud providers remains strong, and that TSMC is still adding CoWoS capacity. That report also says capital spending for 2026 through 2028 is planned substantially above the prior three years; third-quarter operating margin was 58.7 percent, above the top of the company's 58 percent forecast band, on revenue of NT$1.49 trillion and net income of NT$763.1 billion. Source: aninews.in Source: asiae.co.kr Source: asiae.co.kr

Silicon

AMD's RDNA 5 Inches Closer as GFX13 Support Lands in Linux Driver Patches: TechPowerUp

AMDGPU maintainer Alex Deucher submitted eight patches on November 9 that add GFX13 support to the open-source AMDGPU driver, more than 12,600 lines excluding the usual large headers. The series brings version 13 of the graphics block, the MicroEngine Scheduler, and the IMU, and it names two targets, GFX 13.0.1 and GFX 13.0.4, which are IP configurations rather than product names. On the compute side, AMDKFD gains initial GC 13 support, a CWSR trap handler that saves and restores compute wave state, and a temporary disable of queue reset in the topology. The last patch turns on MGCG, CGCG, and CGLS clock gating so idle blocks draw less power. AMD has not confirmed that GFX13 is RDNA 5; TechPowerUp notes the assumption rests on GFX12 having been tied to RDNA 4. Source: techpowerup.com

Google TPU 8tx Pairs AMD EPYC Turin Hosts with Confidential Computing while TPU 8i Max Doubles Memory Bandwidth: ServeTheHome

AMD said it has expanded its Google Cloud collaboration so that 5th Gen EPYC processors host Google Cloud TPU 8tx and help protect sensitive AI models. ServeTheHome describes the new TPU 8tx as pairing those EPYC Turin hosts with confidential computing, and says TPU 8i Max doubles memory bandwidth for inference. The posts do not add a wattage, a process node, or a ship count. Source: servethehome.com Source: x.com

Infineon Processes Functional Power Devices on 300mm SiC Wafers: Semiconductor Digest

Infineon has processed 300-millimetre silicon carbide wafers into functional power devices at its fab in Villach, Austria. The result is a process demonstration on the larger wafer, not a stated high-volume shipment. Source: semiconductor-digest.com

Fraunhofer IPMS Highlights Semiconductor Technology Developments at SEMICON Europa: Semiconductor Digest

Inside the European APECS pilot line, Fraunhofer IPMS has for the first time integrated chiplets into pockets in a silicon wafer, planarized the surface, and prepared it for wafer-level interconnect — the first major milestone on its quasi-monolithic integration roadmap, on work it runs at 200-millimetre and 300-millimetre. Director Wenke Weinreich framed the problem as integrating chiplets that carry memory or photonic functions while validating the processes under industry-relevant conditions, including back-end steps moving into front-end flows. The institute's silicon-nitride waveguide platform, on both wafer sizes, is described as extremely low loss from the visible spectrum into the mid-infrared. Its hafnium-oxide ferroelectric FRAM and FeFET pilot-line devices switch below one volt and in the nanosecond range. The same portfolio is what IPMS will show at SEMICON Europa, alongside FAMES pilot-line work, under the Chips for Europe Initiative. Source: semiconductor-digest.com

Oklahoma State Expands Regional Research Computing with Cimarron Supercomputer: HPCwire

Oklahoma State University says Cimarron, operated by its High Performance Computing Center, is now available to researchers across Oklahoma, Arkansas, and Kansas. The machine provides 37,760 processor cores, 392 GPUs, and more than 10 petabytes of storage. Source: hpcwire.com

Data Centres & Power

AI’s Efficiency Challenge For The Semiconductor Industry: Semiconductor Engineering

John Kibarian's October 15 keynote at PDF Solutions CONNECT, written up today, treats a McKinsey base case as a capacity problem rather than a capital problem: device revenue from 975 billion dollars in 2025 to about 2.3 trillion in 2030, an 83.5 percent rise, with 58 percent of that growth from data centers and servers compounding at 29 percent a year. Installed AI data-center capacity of 45 to 60 gigawatts in 2026 is projected at 100 to 160 gigawatts by 2030. At 150,000 to 200,000 wafers per gigawatt, half of them HBM, that implies 9.6 million to 17.5 million additional wafers and 16 to 29 new fabs sized at 50,000 wafer starts a month; Kibarian notes a modern leading-edge logic fab runs closer to 20,000 starts, which could double the fab count. The same talk puts 2030 AI revenue at 3.9 trillion dollars and margins at roughly 30 million dollars per megawatt, and says a GPU on inference can spend over 90 percent of its power on memory updates and idle time. A manufacturing executive, he said, had already been told he could not get more tools than had been promised, so the only path left was more output from the tools on the floor. Source: semiengineering.com

Greenpeace projects three chipmakers could use 15 percent of Taiwan's power by 2030: TNGlobal

Greenpeace projects that three chipmakers could use 15 percent of Taiwan's power by 2030. The projection is a demand forecast for the island's grid, not an energized campus and not a megawatt figure for any one site. Source: technode.global

Supply Chain & Policy

Nvidia’s China Spy Fallout Deepens: Report Says Moonshot AI Accessed NVDA’s Advanced Chips Despite Export Controls: Stocktwits

Bloomberg, as reported by Stocktwits, says Moonshot AI signed an August contract to rent 32 Super Micro servers equipped with Nvidia B300 GPUs, and that Beijing filings valued those servers at 432 million yuan, 64.8 million dollars, and placed them in China — a possible breach of rules that bar such sales without a license. Moonshot disputed the documents; after Bloomberg asked for comment, they no longer named Moonshot. House Republicans Brian Mast, John Moolenaar, and Rick Crawford have asked Jensen Huang to a closed briefing and for a reply; Senators Elizabeth Warren and Jim Banks, in a November 9 joint statement, want the AI Overwatch Act in the fiscal 2027 National Defense Authorization Act, and House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries has called for a full briefing and tighter controls. Chosun Biz, citing Reuters, says three bills already in the Senate's fiscal 2027 defense package — AI Overwatch, the Chip Security Act, and the MATCH Act, each cleared earlier by the House Foreign Affairs Committee — now have a better chance, that Nvidia is viewed as the strongest opponent of the first two, and that the White House has not taken a position; President Trump said Huang told him he had not known the woman was suspected of being a Chinese spy. In the quarter ended July 26, revenue from customers headquartered in China, including Hong Kong, was 7.9 billion dollars, about 8 percent of total revenue, and Hopper shipments bound for China were less than 1 percent of data-center revenue; Nvidia does not include China data-center compute revenue in its forward outlook. Source: stocktwits.com Source: en.fnnews.com Source: biz.chosun.com

SCHMID to Supply Equipment for Advanced Packaging Facility in South Asia: Semiconductor Digest

SCHMID has an equipment order for an advanced-packaging facility in South Asia, framed as a step from pilot lines toward volume manufacturing of glass-core substrates for AI and high-performance computing. Source: semiconductor-digest.com

Micron Supports Workforce Training at SUNY Jefferson with $750K Grant: Micron

Micron said a 750,000 dollar Community Investment Fund grant is backing SUNY Jefferson's workforce program in the renovated Dulles Building, focused on mechatronics and advanced manufacturing. The first bootcamp class is underway, aimed at students, service members, veterans, military spouses, and military-connected families, toward certifications and college credit for semiconductor manufacturing in New York. Source: x.com

Also Today

Deutsche Telekom Expands AI Factory with NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra GPUs: The Fast Mode

The Fast Mode reports Deutsche Telekom is expanding its AI factory with Nvidia Blackwell Ultra GPUs. Source: thefastmode.com

Streaming Scan Network Test Over PCIe: Semiconductor Engineering

Teradyne has integrated Siemens Tessent Silicon Executable into the UltraPort server and IG-XL on UltraFLEXplus so Streaming Scan Network patterns reach the chip over its own PCIe link, through an on-chip controller on AXI, rather than only through a dedicated test port. Source: semiengineering.com

Microsoft Patent Describes Paying PC Owners to Train AI on Idle GPUs: TechPowerUp

A Microsoft Technology Licensing application, submitted in April 2025 and made public on November 5, describes chopping training jobs into micro-tasks for opted-in phones, laptops, and gaming PCs with spare GPU capacity, with credits redeemable for software, model access, or money — an application, not an announced Windows or Xbox service, and with no payout rate. Source: techpowerup.com

The Teardown: The Insertion That Cannot See the Next Model

A test insertion used to be a cut the process and the physics already knew how to make. Semiconductor Engineering's account of where AI silicon is now starts from the opposite problem: the workload can change in the time it takes to get the chip out of the building, so the question is no longer only whether a contact is good. It is which slice of the build is still worth a tester hour once the algorithm that justified the die has already moved on.

The path is a chain of partial views. Wafer sort and singulated-die probe can sit at the fab, with that site's test program and its own record of failure mechanisms. The next assembly — a compute die, a stack of high-bandwidth memory, other chiplets, an interposer — is likely an OSAT job, on a different program, and the two data sets do not have to meet. Advantest's Scott Segura describes the physical reason the chain is getting longer: more active parts in the substrate, parts moved closer together, functional test creeping into what used to be a contact check, and performance probe at die level that now has to hold thermals. Known-good die, in that account, has to handle 3 kilowatts while it is still a singulated die. Silicon photonics, already, is five insertions. The substrate those dies land on is leaving the round wafer for a 310 by 310 millimetre panel, chosen so the tools can hold more dies, and that panel test is the step Teradyne's Jeorge Hutarte puts at the OSAT.

Walk the clock against that chain. ChatGPT arrived in November 2022. Widespread commercial use of agentic systems took slightly more than two years — about the time to design, verify, validate, ride a shuttle, package the part, and drop it into a working system. The design cycle those customers are trying to shrink is still 18 to 24 months, and some large chipmakers have told their EDA suppliers they want six. Even if the drawing board hit six months, the fab, the package, and the board would still be staring at a model generation that turned over inside the same window. Segura's customers already tape out, run the model or look at the end application, and then do not ship that chip. They ship a derivative, or a different version. Every insertion written for the first tapeout — the 3-kilowatt singulated probe, the five photonics stops, the panel-level functional test — was aimed at a part the lab may already have walked away from.

The cut stops being the right cut when the failure you still need to catch only exists after the compute die, the memory stack, the interconnect, the firmware, and the software are in one box, and the company holding that box cannot see the wafer-sort mechanisms from the company that probed the die. More insertions then buy contact confidence on a workload that has already left, while the combined behavior the field will actually run sits in a data set the previous insertion never receives. Source: semiengineering.com
