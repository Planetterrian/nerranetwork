TSMC booked $5.10 billion in foreign subsidies, funding fabs from Arizona to Nanjing.

What You Need to Know: TSMC booked $5.10 billion in foreign subsidies, funding fabs from Arizona to Nanjing. The nine-month haul, NT$167.586 billion from the United States, Japan, Germany and China, landed almost entirely in the third quarter and is tied to property, construction and tools at the overseas subsidiaries. Arizona’s second fab is scheduled for 3-nanometer mass production in the third quarter of 2027.

Top Story

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing’s Friday financial report puts combined subsidies from the United States, Japan, Germany and China at NT$167.586 billion (US$5.10 billion) for the first nine months of this year, a record for that stretch and cover for property purchases, plant construction and production equipment. The third quarter alone was NT$166.996 billion, after NT$590 million in the first half. The recipients are TSMC Arizona, European Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. in Dresden, Japan Advanced Semiconductor Manufacturing in Kumamoto, and TSMC Nanjing, each under agreements with local governments. Nanjing runs a 12-inch wafer fab. Arizona’s first fab began commercial production in the fourth quarter of 2024 on 4-nanometer; construction of the second fab is complete, equipment installation is underway, and mass production is scheduled for the third quarter of 2027 on 3-nanometer. Kumamoto’s first fab began commercial production at the end of 2024, with a second fab under construction for mass production in 2028; Dresden is scheduled to begin mass production next year. Over the nine months, TSMC Arizona posted a profit of NT$53.986 billion and JASM a profit of NT$2.396 billion, while ESMC reported a loss of NT$1.079 billion. Source: focustaiwan.tw

Source: taiwannews.com.tw

Silicon

South Korea Expects Nvidia GPU Deliveries Under Contracts to Continue: TokenPost

South Korea’s government expects Nvidia GPU deliveries covered by signed contracts to continue through the chipmaker’s leadership change, and it put a deployed count on the buildout: about 13,100 Nvidia B200 GPUs are already in the country. Plans to introduce next-generation GPUs, including Vera Rubin, are expected to proceed as planned. Nvidia’s work in South Korea spans AI data centers, autonomous driving and robotics, with NAVER, Hyundai Motor Group, SK Telecom and Kakao Mobility. The industry minister had given a similar assurance on November 9 that contracted deliveries would not be disrupted; this statement added the B200 count and the next-generation plans. Source: tokenpost.com

Penguin Solutions Introduces CXL-Enabled DDR4 Module for AI Data Centers: HPCwire

Penguin Solutions introduced the SMART CXL-8F2W-D4, an eight-DIMM DDR4 Compute Express Link memory add-in card that lets organizations adapt prior-generation DDR4 for use in AI systems. The Fremont company describes it as validated reuse that offers performance, cost savings, and another path to procuring memory for AI infrastructure buildouts. Source: hpcwire.com

Staera Semiconductor Emerges from Stealth with $4.6M to Break AI’s Data Bottleneck: HPCwire

Staera Semiconductor, formerly New Silicon Corporation, emerged from stealth with $4.6 million and a method that builds LED optical data links directly into silicon chips. The San Francisco company is aiming the links at AI systems that need to move data faster and scale further without adding power or heat. Source: hpcwire.com

Intel’s ISA Reference Confirms Razor Lake Desktop and Laptop Model IDs: TechPowerUp

Intel’s November Architecture Instruction Set Extensions Programming Reference, revision 063, adds Razor Lake and Coral Rapids. Razor Lake gets four model values under family 18 (12H), the same family as Nova Lake: 12_06H and 12_0AH for desktop, 12_07H and 12_09H for laptops. Coral Rapids Xeon is 13_08H, after Diamond Rapids at 13_01H. InstLatX64 spotted the update. Razor Lake picks up SSBD_PSFD_U_S, described as separate user-mode and supervisor-mode control over certain speculative memory behavior; both families gain APIC-Timer Virtualization; Coral Rapids adds VPMLDSABFLYDQ, aimed at ML-DSA. GCC patches posted this week give Razor Lake the same ISA feature set as Nova Lake, which should mean AVX10.2 and APX carry over, while Coral Rapids stays on AVX10.2 rather than AVX10.3 for now. Source: techpowerup.com

Data Centres & Power

European Union offers to build data center for Moldova: Data Center Dynamics

The European Union has offered to build a data center for Moldova, proposed by enlargement commissioner Marta Kos on a visit last week, to help protect citizens’ personal data from cyberattacks. If accepted, the facility would be built with assistance from Sweden, Europa Libera reported. Prime minister Vasile Tofan welcomed the offer and said preparatory work is advancing for a secure data center and a stronger link to the European artificial intelligence ecosystem. The offer came at the first meeting of the High-Level Digital Connectivity Platform in Chișinău. The European Commission said the summit’s purpose was to invest up to €623 million ($689 million), pledged by nations and private-sector partners, in digital connectivity between Europe and Central Asia, including 5G, space-based connectivity and data center infrastructure in the Black Sea region, Turkey and the southern Caucasus. A separate Logos Press estimate put the sum required to build a government facility consolidating public digital services at €50 million ($55 million). The offer follows an October 2026 cyberattack that exposed personal data of an estimated 450,000 Moldovan citizens. DataCenterMap lists six facilities in the country, all in Chișinău. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

NTT Docomo to pilot data center heat-reuse for on-shore shrimp farm in Japan: Data Center Dynamics

NTT is set to pilot waste heat from a data center to warm an on-land shrimp farm, under a Tokyo Metropolitan Government program for unused data-center heat. IMT Engineering and NTT Docomo announced “Construction of a Waste Heat Utilization Type Land-Based Aquaculture System,” starting next year with heat from an NTT Docomo Business data center in or near Tokyo and running until March 2028. Recovered heat will warm rearing water for Pacific white shrimp, a use NTT framed for waste heat that has been hard to apply because it is relatively low temperature. Hibiya General Equipment will design and build the heat-recovery and supply gear; NTT Green & Food, formed in 2023 with Regional Fish, will run the aquaculture and judge whether production costs fall. The four firms want a model that can move to other data centers. Elsewhere in the same city program, KDDI and ElleThermo are deploying a self-powered temperature sensor that can generate electricity from heat around 20–40°C, and Kratwerk and Tokyu Construction are taking waste heat from a small modular data center under a railway overpass for server cooling. NTT Docomo’s Nexcenter sites are seven around Tokyo, one in Yokohama, two in Osaka, one in Kagawa, one in Shanghai and two in Hong Kong. Source: datacenterdynamics.com

Supply Chain & Policy

GlobalFoundries Breaks Ground on First New Semiconductor Fab Under Landmark U.S.-Japan Initiative: TechPowerUp

GlobalFoundries broke ground on the first new semiconductor manufacturing facility planned for its Malta, New York, site under a U.S.-Japan initiative: a 300-millimeter fab pairing Japanese investment with GF’s technology and manufacturing, aimed at automotive, industrial and connected-infrastructure demand. On October 14, GF said it was selected as technology provider and operator for two new fabs at Malta, under an initiative providing up to $21 billion. Japan provides the investment; GF contributes differentiated technologies and operations. An initial investment of about $10 billion, with $570 million in New York State incentives, is accelerating the first fab, expected online in 2029. About $10 billion more is set for a second fab as the project advances. Source: techpowerup.com

Chinese AI labs are training models on Nvidia’s Blackwell despite export controls, says ex-OpenAI engineer: The Register

A November 12 forensic analysis from Will DePue, a former OpenAI engineer now at Unemployment Labs, reports that six Chinese models from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group were initialized on Nvidia Blackwell systems, four of them matching Grace Blackwell GB200 and GB300 configurations in Nvidia’s liquid-cooled rack-scale lineup. The names every account agrees on are DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3 and MiniMax-M3; shattered.io says the models date from April 2026 onward, unlike earlier Chinese releases that matched Hopper. DePue said he searched more than six trillion combinations of chip layout and seed number against open weights, and that initialization traces survive into published models. The Register says this is the first public link of MiniMax and Ant Group to Blackwell, and that the report includes seeds tying DeepSeek-V3 to Hopper and DeepSeek-V4-Flash to Blackwell. The method identifies the hardware used to set initial weights, not where those chips sat, who owned them, or whether the rest of training stayed on Blackwell; a lab could have initialized through a U.S. provider. DePue said senior researchers inside and outside frontier labs, including OpenAI and Anthropic, confirmed the result. Separately, Israel-based Lava reported 32 Nvidia B300 GPUs, Blackwell Ultra parts under U.S. export controls, through publicly exposed monitoring endpoints with IP addresses geolocated to China. DePue also said Unemployment Labs is raising $5 million for six more months of research. Source: theregister.com

Source: shattered.io

Instead Of Lowering GPU Prices, Former NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang Reportedly Financed AI Startups To Absorb Excess Silicon: Wccftech

The Wall Street Journal, as reported by Wccftech and TechPowerUp, describes an August 2026 meeting in which Colette Kress, then chief financial officer and now interim chief executive, pushed to rein in circular financing to smaller cloud providers and to OpenAI and Anthropic. Initial discussions had floated underwriting roughly 8 gigawatts of OpenAI compute valued at up to $250 billion; Kress scaled the signed guarantee to roughly 4.25 gigawatts, or up to $125 billion. Huang reportedly called her “a deer in the headlights.” To justify underwriting buyers, he privately admitted major cloud companies were pivoting to in-house chips and that prepaid TSMC wafers and memory were “coming at him like an avalanche.” Funding data-center buildouts for AI startups, the report says, created demand to clear surplus silicon rather than let it force GPU prices down. Analyst Ed Zitron has estimated that at least $200 billion of Nvidia AI GPUs sit idle in warehouses after cloud purchases that lacked grid capacity or immediate demand. Nvidia reportedly then contacted TSMC to wind down upcoming chip orders. Source: wccftech.com

Source: techpowerup.com

US lawmakers push for tighter chip export controls on China as Jensen Huang resignation controversy grows: Zaobao

After Jensen Huang’s resignation, House Speaker Mike Johnson said on November 12 that he favors limiting China’s access to advanced chips and sees a developing consensus in Congress. Republican chairs of three House committees urged that four chip-control bills go into the annual National Defense Authorization Act. Three are already in the Senate version: more congressional oversight of advanced-chip export policy, a requirement that manufacturers track where advanced chips go, and pressure on allies to restrict sales of advanced chipmaking equipment and related maintenance. The fourth, the Remote Access Security Act, would let Commerce restrict Chinese companies from renting controlled Nvidia chips at overseas data centers; the House passed it in January and it awaits the Senate. The same chairs asked Huang to appear for closed-door questioning on December 1 and to confirm in writing by November 13 or face a subpoena. Attorney General Todd Blanche said the FBI is investigating allegations that Alice Huang worked for the Chinese government, and that the Justice Department has not determined she was a spy. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said on Friday that China opposes abuse of export controls that disrupts supply chains. Bloomberg, citing people familiar, said Manhattan federal prosecutors are investigating whether companies linked to Huang Le smuggled Super Micro Computer products into China, and that Justice is also examining Megaspeed International. Malaysia’s investment, trade and industry ministry said it is working with customs on whether AI chips imported by Speedmatrix, Megaspeed’s Malaysian subsidiary, were re-exported. Since July last year Malaysia has required prior approval to export, re-export or transit high-performance U.S. AI chips. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said there is currently no indication that AI chips linked to Megaspeed had been moved out of Malaysia. Source: zaobao.com.sg

Also Today

Nvidia board reportedly approached Arm CEO Rene Haas during replacement search: Tom’s Hardware

Nvidia’s board approached Arm chief executive Rene Haas, a long-time Nvidia veteran, during the search for a replacement chief executive. Source: tomshardware.com

Shifting HBM4 Controller Functions Closer To Memory (UC Riverside): Semiconductor Engineering

Tharunya Yogananda and Wantong Li of the University of California, Riverside, published a paper on partitioning the HBM controller across the host and the HBM base die, noting that recent generations such as HBM4 let that base die be manufactured on a logic process. Source: semiengineering.com

The Teardown: The Copy After Every Layer

A training step is arithmetic plus a copy, and the copy is what infra.bea.ai times when it asks whether a consumer GPU can stand in for a data-center accelerator. The guide puts an RTX 4090 at 82.6 teraflops of BF16, then draws three gaps compute does not close: 24 gigabytes of memory against 80, an interconnect of 32 gigabytes per second against 900, and no error-correcting memory where training expects it. Parameters, optimizer state and activations all have to live in that 24 gigabytes. The link has to move a slice of those numbers after every layer if the model is split across cards. PCI Express is the only path on a consumer board. There is no NVLink, so tensor-parallel all-reduce cannot hide inside a dedicated GPU fabric.

Take the guide’s 7-billion-parameter case, sharded across eight 4090s. With a micro-batch of one, a sequence length of 2048 and full checkpointing, activations are about 2 gigabytes and the running total is 18 gigabytes. Headroom is 6 gigabytes. That remainder is the budget for a longer sequence. At 4096 tokens the same card needs gradient checkpointing and a micro-batch of one. Extra sequence length is not a software preference. It is the memory that is left after weights and optimizer state have taken their share.

The interconnect numbers show why adding cards does not refill that budget. Tensor-parallel all-reduce has to finish after every layer. infra.bea.ai times it at 640 milliseconds on PCI Express and 22 milliseconds on NVLink. If the forward and backward pass takes 500 milliseconds, the PCI Express copy more than doubles the step. Fully sharded data parallel does not dodge the wire: an all-gather at the start of each layer and a reduce-scatter at the end add about 900 milliseconds on PCI Express to a 7-billion-parameter step that would spend about 32 milliseconds on the fast link. Eight cards therefore buy eight small memories and a bus that spends more time synchronizing than the arithmetic spends computing.

That design stops being the right one when either budget is gone. When sequence length consumes the 6 gigabytes of headroom, another 4090 does not create one larger address space. When the 640-millisecond all-reduce exceeds the 500-millisecond compute step, another card adds a participant to the copy, not a shorter step. The 82.6 teraflops still sit on the board. They wait on 32 gigabytes per second, and on 24 gigabytes that cannot see the other 24. Source: infra.bea.ai
