120-mile-an-hour Isaias threatens a deadly United States Gulf landfall tonight.

By Mira October 09, 2026 Ep 17 7 min read Listen to podcast

What You Need to Know: Hurricane Isaias is a category 3 storm heading for the United States Gulf coast, where Alabama, Florida and Mississippi have declared states of emergency ahead of a forecast landfall tonight. Elsewhere, Brussels and Beijing reached preliminary trade understandings, and Russia's draft budget would cut federal cancer-programme funding as defence takes a larger share of spending. This edition draws on 3 of the 5 Omni View regional desks live by press time.

The Ten

Hurricane Isaias barrels toward Gulf coast as category 3 storm: The Guardian

The United States, in North America, is in the path of Hurricane Isaias, which strengthened into a category 3 storm on Friday morning as it moved toward the Gulf coast. The National Hurricane Center said maximum sustained winds rose from 105 miles an hour at daybreak to 120 miles an hour by mid-morning, with the center about 145 miles south-east of the mouth of the Mississippi River and moving north-north-east at 15 miles an hour. Officials forecast landfall Friday night along the Alabama-Florida line, with surging seas up to 7 feet and waves as high as 30 feet; Alabama, Florida and Mississippi have declared states of emergency. The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration said damaging hurricane-force winds are expected over the Florida panhandle, southern Alabama and southern Mississippi, and that residents should prepare for long-duration power outages. A storm surge is expected from south-east Louisiana to Suwannee in north-east Florida, with up to 15 inches of rain and possible tornadoes overnight Friday into Saturday from Alabama to the Florida panhandle and south-western Georgia; the center said Dauphin Island could see a 7-foot surge and Indian Pass a 6-foot surge, and that preparations should be rushed to completion.

Ranks: Coastal residents in three Gulf states · life-threatening surge, wind and outages · tonight Source: theguardian.com

EU and China strike preliminary trade deal ahead of crucial leaders’ summit: EU Reporter

China and the European Union, in East Asia and Europe, reached preliminary agreements on hybrid-car exports, market access and rare-earth supplies after two days of talks in Beijing, ahead of next week's European Council summit. The sides also recorded a shared understanding to facilitate exports of rare earths and permanent magnets through an existing fast-track licensing system, and agreed to lower most-favoured-nation tariffs on EU products from car parts to olive oil and footwear, covering almost 4 billion euros in current export value and an estimated at least 225 million euros in duty savings. Šefčovič called the trade deficit "unsustainable" and the step important but limited; Sigrid de Vries, director-general of ACEA, welcomed the interim deal as a way to manage Chinese presence in the European market in an orderly way.

Ranks: European and Chinese manufacturers and buyers · export volumes and tariffs on billions of euros of goods · endorsement sought next Thursday Source: eureporter.co

Russia slashes cancer care as defence spending hits post-Soviet record: Euronews

Russia, in Europe, would cut its federal cancer programme from 44.9 billion roubles to 3.87 billion roubles in 2027 under the draft federal budget, Euronews reports, a drop of nearly twelvefold from that 44.9 billion figure. Compared with 148.5 billion roubles in 2025, federal cancer-control spending would fall roughly 38-fold in two years if the budget passes as proposed, while defence spending reaches 35 percent of all federal expenditure, the highest share since the Soviet Union collapsed. Funding is projected to rise only to 5.76 billion roubles in 2028 and 12.78 billion in 2029, still far below current levels, with the remaining money expected to go mainly to diagnostic and radiology equipment while specialised units in smaller towns and rural areas face cuts. Officials said some costs will be transferred to the compulsory health insurance system and other budget lines rather than eliminated; patient organisations and some experts questioned whether those mechanisms could fully offset the federal reduction. The NGO Movement Against Cancer found that between 2019 and 2025 patients bought roughly one in every six or seven packs of cancer medicines out of pocket, and that in 2025 household spending on anti-tumour drugs rose from 8.8 billion to 11.4 billion roubles, with about 94 percent of those drugs on the essential-medicines list that should be provided free.

Ranks: Cancer patients across Russia · a proposed multi-year cut in federal cancer funding · if the 2027 budget passes Source: euronews.com

U.S. imposes sanctions on International Criminal Court, hours after former judge wins Nobel: The Globe and Mail

The United States, in North America, imposed sanctions on the International Criminal Court hours after former judge Navi Pillay won the Nobel Peace Prize. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in a statement, "We will ban transactions with this rogue court, cutting off their resources and crippling its ability to operate." The New York Times reported that Pillay served as a judge at the court in The Hague and that the Trump administration has repeatedly attacked the international legal order and the court.

Ranks: The court's staff, states and victims' cases · US transactions with the court banned · immediate Source: theglobeandmail.com

Canada lost 68,300 jobs as trade war clouds outlook: The Real Economy Blog

Canada, in North America, lost 68,300 jobs in September, a second straight monthly decline, for 110,000 jobs lost in August and September combined, as the trade war with the United States and high oil prices tied to the Iran war cloud the outlook. The unemployment rate edged up 0.1 percentage points to 6.5 percent; education lost 35,300 jobs, healthcare and social assistance 23,100, and manufacturing 12,700, while professional, scientific and technical services added 10,600. Quebec lost 49,000 jobs, Ontario and British Columbia 20,000 each, and Alberta added 23,000; the economy lost 35,000 full-time jobs and 33,000 part-time jobs, and youth employment fell by 48,000. The Real Economy Blog says the Bank of Canada is likely to hold its policy rate at 2.25 percent in October, and that a 25 basis-point hike could come as early as December if oil prices stay elevated; new US tariffs took effect in September at rates as high as 25 percent or 50 percent, with trade talks frozen.

Ranks: Tens of thousands of Canadian workers · a second month of job losses, unemployment at 6.5 percent · already, with more layoffs described as likely Source: realeconomy.rsmus.com

Both Sides: Should the United States seek a quick end to the Iran war?

The decision in front of Washington is whether to press for a quick end to the Iran war. Michigan Republican Senate candidate Mike Rogers and Republican gubernatorial candidate John James have recently called for that quick end, citing high prices and, in James's case, the "terrible" nature of warfare, Bridge Michigan reported. Their case rests on the war's cost at home: Iran shut the Strait of Hormuz at the start of the war, a vital shipping lane for oil and products such as fertilizer, and that shutdown led to cost spikes for diesel and fertilizer. Amid high prices, both have also advocated a temporary suspension of federal and state gas taxes. The war has already run far longer than President Donald Trump said it would; on March 1, a day after US forces began bombing Iran, he said the conflict would last four to five weeks, and multiple ceasefire agreements have since fallen apart.

The case for having fought the war is the one Trump has given: he has said it was necessary to prevent Iran from developing a nuclear weapon. Public comments and congressional records reviewed by Bridge Michigan show Rogers and James previously backed the war, and James voted against war-powers resolutions to end the conflict. Democratic rivals accuse both of flip-flopping to try to win votes in the November 3 election, saying both have long supported the war.

Both sides are arguing over a war that is still under way and over prices that have already risen. The next dated political test in this reporting is the November 3 election; the article does not describe a new ceasefire taking effect. Source: theoaklandpress.com
