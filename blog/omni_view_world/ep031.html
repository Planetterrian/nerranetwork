Two soldiers dead near Hebron, Israel orders a West Bank-wide closure before Tuesday's election.

By Mira October 23, 2026 Ep 31 11 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
The Ten Progress Watch

The White House says a local pause in occupied Oleshky will open a humanitarian corridor on October 29, while gunmen killed mourners at a church funeral in Jordan and a Paris-suburb attack is under a terrorism inquiry. This edition draws on 1 of the 5 Omni View regional desks live by press time.

The Ten

Netanyahu orders ‘very extensive’ operation in West Bank after two Israeli soldiers killed: The Guardian

In the occupied West Bank, the Israel Defense Forces said two Palestinian gunmen opened fire on Friday at a temporary checkpoint outside Hebron after failing to reach a nearby highway, where the military believes they had meant to attack civilians. The Globe and Mail named the dead soldiers as Staff Sgt. Ron Yonatan Roytman, 23, and Sgt. Yuval Shai Svilem, 20, and said three other Israelis were lightly wounded; The Guardian reported that a preliminary military investigation found the gunfight lasted more than 20 minutes and left five Israelis wounded, two of whom later died. The army said both Palestinians, from Hebron and armed with an M16 and a handgun, were killed near the Yehuda Junction, and an AFP photographer who witnessed the battle saw two men with a handgun and an automatic rifle exchanging fire. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s office said it had ordered Hebron cordoned off at once and a full closure of the West Bank from Friday, the fourth such territory-wide restriction in a month, shutting gates that link Palestinian villages to major roads while Israeli drivers, including settlers, can still pass. Netanyahu said forces would evacuate residents of the attackers’ neighbourhood, go house to house, seize weapons and destroy what he called terrorist infrastructure, on a scale he described as unseen in the West Bank for decades, using a model the army applied in the “Iron Wall” operation that the UN rights office says forcibly displaced tens of thousands from northern refugee camps from January 2025. He blamed Hamas for “planning a major attack aimed at murdering innocent civilians”; army chief Eyal Zamir said a large quantity of ammunition found in the gunmen’s vehicle was meant for a larger attack, and the army said dozens of operations afterward led to dozens of arrests, including people affiliated with Hamas and Islamic Jihad, and the seizure of M16 rifles and submachine guns. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich called for a West Bank campaign “exactly like in Gaza,” including dismantling the Palestinian Authority, collecting weapons and eliminating those he called terrorists, and said that would be among the first demands of the next government. National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir said Palestinian freedom of movement should be restricted because the lives of settlers, soldiers and police “are a million times more important.” Former Peace Now director Yariv Oppenheimer told Al Jazeera that demolitions, curfews, arrests and settler revenge attacks were likely, and that daily life for Palestinians was already impossible. Israeli voters decide next Tuesday whether Netanyahu gets another term, three years after the Hamas-led attacks that triggered the Gaza war.

Ranks: Palestinians across the West Bank and Israeli voters · a territory-wide closure and a threatened house-to-house campaign · starting Friday, with an election next Tuesday Source: theguardian.com · theglobeandmail.com · nytimes.com

Melania Trump brokered ceasefire at besieged Ukrainian town, White House says: The Washington Post

In Russian-occupied Oleshky, in Ukraine’s southern Kherson region, the White House said on October 23 that First Lady Melania Trump had helped broker a local ceasefire so aid can enter and civilians can leave. The White House said Ukraine and Russia agreed to pause military action, with the ceasefire set for October 29, and that she and a representative negotiated timing, technical procedures and implementation directly with both leaderships as what it called an “Exclusive Intermediary.” Melania Trump said the town is “a front-line war city on the brink of disaster,” that those left are “primarily elderly” and urgently need medical attention, medicine, food and clean water, and that without her intervention aid and evacuation of the elderly “would be impossible.” UNITED24 Media reported the White House estimate of 1,700 to 2,000 residents still there, mostly elderly, plus about 50 children, against about 24,000 before the full-scale invasion, and said no comprehensive census has been taken since 2022. It also said about 90 percent of the city flooded when the Kakhovka Dam collapsed in June 2023. Under the announced plan, civilian representatives from both sides would visit homes to assess needs, people who choose to leave would get transport either to Ukrainian-controlled territory or to Russia, an independent American media organization would document the process so residents’ preferences are respected, and the sides would coordinate demining because roads remain contaminated with explosives. The White House did not say how long the pause would last or how compliance would be monitored. The Guardian said there was no comment yet from Moscow or from Ukraine, and that its correspondents were still trying to corroborate the statement. Ukrainian Human Rights Commissioner Dmytro Lubinets said on October 23 that talks on evacuations were still under way, though nearing completion, and UNITED24 Media said Ukraine had not officially confirmed the deal. Russia and Ukraine have each accused the other of blocking supplies since May, a stretch linked by the White House and The Guardian to deaths from starvation and disease, a destroyed morgue, problems burying bodies, and no running water. Pope Leo XIV urged Vladimir Putin on October 16 to accept a ceasefire so food and medicine could reach civilians. By October 19, Kyiv and Moscow were moving to finish an evacuation from Oleshky and six nearby settlements, where around 6,000 civilians, including up to 200 children, were believed to remain. The White House tied the channel to earlier contacts that, it said, returned seven Ukrainian children on April 2, 2026, and five more on July 30 in a fifth such reunion.

Ranks: roughly 1,700 to 2,000 people still in Oleshky, mostly elderly · a dated pause for aid and a choice of evacuation route, if both armies honour it · ceasefire set for October 29 Source: washingtonpost.com · theguardian.com · united24media.com

Six shot dead by gunmen at church funeral in Jordan, officials say: BBC World

In Jordan, government spokesman Mohammad al-Momani said gunmen killed six people and wounded 28 others on Friday at a church funeral in the village of Hmoud, in the Karak governorate. France 24’s account of the Public Security Directorate placed the shooting at a Greek Orthodox church in Karak, about 180 kilometres south of Amman. Al Jazeera reported at least seven killed, and said survivors described two masked men opening fire as dozens attended the funeral procession. Mourner Khaled Burqan told al-Mamlaka television that two attackers, with a rifle and a pistol, fired for several minutes inside a church where dozens had gathered; BBC-verified CCTV appears to show three armed men moving from a road toward the church, two entering what looks like a vehicle entrance, retreating, then returning with a third, all apparently carrying handguns. Separate footage the BBC said appeared to be from the aftermath showed dead and wounded on the ground. Al-Momani called the attack heinous, said security forces were deployed and were investigating, and said the prime minister would chair a Security Council meeting attended by all ministers, with an interior-ministry briefing expected shortly. No one had claimed responsibility. The unidentified gunmen were reported to have arrived by car, opened fire, and fled, and a manhunt was under way. Karak has a sizeable Christian community in majority-Muslim Jordan. The BBC noted that such violence is rare, though deadly attacks have occurred before; in 2016, gunmen ambushed police in Karak, including at a Crusader castle popular with tourists, killing 10 people, seven of them officers, before security forces killed the gunmen after a long standoff.

Ranks: the funeral congregation and Karak’s Christian minority · six or at least seven dead and 28 wounded, with attackers still sought · Friday, with a security meeting the same day Source: bbc.co.uk · aljazeera.com

Deadly shooting near Paris investigated as 'crime of terrorism', minister says: BBC World

In France, Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said a series of drive-by shootings on Friday in Trappes, southwest of Paris, and then in nearby La Verrière killed one person and wounded five others, three of them seriously, in a rampage of almost 15 minutes. France 24 reported one killed and four wounded, including a 12-year-old girl, and said authorities suspect the attack may have been racially motivated. The New York Times said the shooter fired from a car in several locations southwest of the capital and that antiterrorism prosecutors were exploring a racial or religious motive. The National Anti-Terrorist Prosecutor’s Office said the case appeared to be murder in connection with a terrorist enterprise, including the aggravated circumstance of acts committed because of real or perceived membership of a particular race, ethnicity, nation or religion, and Nuñez told reporters in Trappes that it was clearly a terrorist crime. Trappes Mayor Ali Rabeh said an individual fired shots from his vehicle at several people he encountered, at a petrol station, a medical centre, a playground and several vehicles, wanting “as many casualties as possible” and to “cause carnage”; witnesses said the car was a grey Renault Clio. Rabeh said the person killed was of African origin and one person in critical condition was of North African descent, and called the attack racist and probably Islamophobic, while the anti-terror prosecutor urged caution on the victims’ identities. A local police chief told BFMTV the suspect used an older-style semi-automatic weapon in bursts; Le Parisien reported the gun dated to the Second World War. The prosecutor’s inquiry also covers possession of a Category A weapon, which includes military-grade weapons or weapons that can fire in bursts, and conspiracy with a terrorist group to prepare crimes against people. The suspected attacker, a 39-year-old known to police but not to France’s intelligence services, local media reported, was arrested; Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu’s statement placed that arrest in nearby Coignières, and a police union official said the suspect was detained on the N10 in La Verrière. The motive, Nuñez said, remained unclear.

Ranks: residents of Trappes and La Verrière, including a wounded child · one dead and four or five wounded, now a terrorism case · Friday morning, suspect already held Source: bbc.co.uk · france24.com · nytimes.com

French authorities tighten restrictions and ban specific protests: France 24

In France, President Emmanuel Macron is set to meet student-union representatives on Friday, the first such meeting since the mobilisation began, as the government prepares for Saturday’s protests by high-school pupils whose complaints over school conditions grew into a wider movement. France 24 reported that Paris is extending a partial curfew in parts of the city until Monday, that protests are banned altogether in Marseille and Lyon, that 70,000 security personnel will be deployed on Saturday, and that only union demonstrations will be authorised. The Globe and Mail reported that those 70,000 police officers and gendarmes would be deployed with helicopters, armoured vehicles and water cannons, and that the Louvre and major Paris museums will close on Saturday over concerns about violence. The measures follow weekend looting and clashes that injured police officers and damaged stores, which authorities are trying not to repeat; authorities have also banned some student protests and called for videos of looting to identify rioters. Paris Saint-Germain’s game was postponed after a police request amid the protests, Al Jazeera reported, and Ousmane Dembélé is to miss the Ballon d’Or event because of that move. Shop owners have been preparing for the weekend in fear of riots. Aaron Rodgers of the Pittsburgh Steelers said the NFL would cancel Sunday’s Paris game against the New Orleans Saints in Saint-Denis, about 10 kilometres from where Saturday’s protests are expected, if the city were unsafe. The meeting with students is a scheduled talk, not an agreement yet announced. France’s 10-year government bond yield has pushed above 5 percent for the first time since 2002 as debt fears grow, France 24 reported, with a Moody’s verdict awaited and a downgrade widely expected.

Ranks: people in Paris, Marseille, Lyon and other protest cities · curfews, city bans, closed museums and 70,000 officers on the streets · Saturday’s mobilisation, curfew through Monday Source: france24.com · theglobeandmail.com · aljazeera.com

Russia and Ukraine swap 270 prisoners each: Reuters

Russia and Ukraine each swapped 270 prisoners of war, Russian state media told Reuters.

Ranks: 540 freed prisoners and their families · release from captivity · announced Friday Source: reuters.com

Saskatchewan, Newfoundland push Ottawa for greater control over immigration: The Globe and Mail

Saskatchewan and Newfoundland asked Ottawa for more say over permanent-resident nominations after Alberta voters backed that control.

Ranks: provincial nominees and employers in three provinces · who may be put forward for permanent residence · federal targets due within weeks Source: theglobeandmail.com

Ambush of Salvadoran Soldiers Escalates Haiti’s Anti-Gang Mission: The New York Times

Four Salvadoran soldiers are missing after an ambush, the first crisis for Haiti’s new Gang Suppression Force.

Ranks: the missing soldiers and Haiti’s anti-gang mission · a force already off to a rocky start now in crisis · immediate Source: nytimes.com

As Trump Threatens New Strikes, U.S. Ambassador Says Iran War Could Last Another Year: Democracy Now!

A U.S. ambassador said the Iran war could last another year as Donald Trump threatened new strikes.

Ranks: people living with the Iran war and oil-importing economies · a possible further year of fighting if that warning holds · stated Friday, no new strike date given Source: democracynow.org

Russia Holds Key Rate as Ukrainian Strikes Spur Inflation: Bloomberg.com

Russia’s central bank held its key rate, with Ukrainian strikes cited as a spur to inflation.

Ranks: Russian borrowers and households facing higher prices · no rate cut while war-linked inflation persists · the decision announced Friday Source: bloomberg.com
