The Ten Both Sides: Should Ukraine cede the Donbas for a NATO seat? Progress Watch

Washington has floated a NATO path for Ukraine if Kyiv left the Donbas, and Australia welcomed a Trump-Xi-Putin summit that Europe wants held without surprises on the war. This edition also draws on the Omni View Omni View North America desk.

The Ten

Iran targets Bahrain airbase as strikes resume in Gulf: The Guardian

In Bahrain, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said six drones belonging to its Aerospace Force targeted a U.S. air-defence radar at the Al-Riffa helicopter base on Sunday. Bahrain's military said it intercepted several Iranian attacks and kept them from their intended targets, while explosions were heard around Manama and authorities posted shelter alerts, their first warnings since September. France 24 reported blasts around 4 a.m. local time and a second series around 6:30 a.m., and said this was the second strike on the island in three days after a lull. The island sits across the Persian Gulf from Iran and hosts the U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet; the Guardian says Tehran has hit it repeatedly in a war of flare-ups and long pauses, and France 24 says the energy-rich Gulf has taken the brunt of Iranian strikes. The Guard said the Strait of Hormuz is closed and that any aggression by the United States would meet a harsher, more forceful response. Bahrain's foreign ministry said the kingdom rejects being turned into an arena for settling scores and holds Iran fully responsible. The Guardian reports talks remain at a stalemate, with Washington keeping a naval blockade and Iran its hold on the strait, while President Donald Trump has threatened major combat and has not returned to it. Yemeni government forces backed by Saudi Arabia have retreated from the Bab el-Mandeb area, the same coverage says, giving Iran-aligned Houthi fighters greater control of a lane Gulf oil producers had used to move supplies. Hamas said an Israeli strike on Saturday night killed Samir Mahmoud Hassan Abu Younis, commander of the Rafah Brigade, whom the group said helped plan the 7 October attacks; the BBC, as cited by the Guardian, reported eyewitnesses in Gaza saw four missiles hit a tent in the Al-Waha camp at Al-Mawasi, which shelters hundreds of displaced people. On Friday the Guard said it had targeted a U.S. early-warning radar in Bahrain, the first Gulf strike since early September, and U.S. Central Command said it shot down two Iranian drones with no casualties or damage; the war began on 28 February with U.S.-Israeli strikes that killed Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, and Central Command said last month it had helped Gulf states export 1.5 billion barrels of oil since the summer via Hormuz.

Give Donbas, take NATO seat: Could new US pitch work for Ukraine, Russia?: Al Jazeera

In Ukraine, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Saturday that U.S. negotiators Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner asked, "What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?" Al Jazeera reports the question came at the latest U.S.-mediated talks between Ukraine and Russia in Abu Dhabi last week, almost five years into the war. After that round, Witkoff and Kushner met President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Thursday, then briefed Zelenskyy in Dublin before a meeting of European leaders in the Irish capital on Friday. Zelenskyy said NATO is back on the agenda because it sits inside an extremely difficult conversation that would decide the future of European security. He repeated that Kyiv will not give up any of its territories, and he said alliance members had not raised this land-for-membership prospect before. He called the idea dangerous, saying things like it lead to wars or escalations, and that real security in Europe is the European Union and NATO and nothing else. The Kremlin has repeatedly said Ukrainian membership would demand a harsh response, and Putin has demanded that the alliance pull troops back from countries bordering Russia. President Donald Trump had publicly told Ukraine to give up hopes of joining, even as Lithuania and Poland have been among the members most enthusiastic about speeding accession. Ukraine applied in September 2022, months after Russia's full-scale invasion; members remain divided over offering membership during the war, with some seeking an end to the fighting, and raising corruption concerns, as conditions.

Ranks: People in Ukraine and NATO members · a proposal, not an agreed deal · talks already under way Source: aljazeera.com

Anthony Albanese welcomes Trump-Xi-Putin summit: The Guardian

In the Philippines, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said Sunday in Manila that Australia welcomes this week's planned summit of Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin. The Guardian reports the three-way meeting is set for Shenzhen, where the leaders will gather for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation meeting, and that European leaders are warning Washington not to make major concessions on Ukraine. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has said the talks must not lead to a redistribution of influence among world powers or the abandonment of his country, more than four years after Putin's full-scale invasion, and he has warned the meeting must not become "Yalta 2, 3, 4." Chancellor Friedrich Merz, at a Friday meeting of European Union leaders, said, "please, no surprises," and asked the United States to stay with Europe and Ukraine and work together for a genuine peace. President Emmanuel Macron warned against forcing an unjust peace on Ukraine and, by extension, all of Europe, calling that the spirit of defeat and a recipe for another war. Albanese said dialogue is a good thing, that the clear message to Putin is the war should end, and that he wants something constructive, including de-escalation in conflicts around the world. He left open joining an Apec group photo with Putin if China, as host, wanted it, and said that if Putin is in a room with him, people will hear his contribution. He said Ukraine's stand is also for the international rule of law, that there are two superpowers, and that the summit would not undercut a push by countries including Australia and Canada for middle powers to carry more weight. On Sunday he signed a new defence agreement with the Philippines while visiting the supply ship HMAS Stalwart in the Port of Manila; the Guardian says Australia is helping strengthen Manila's defensive capability against Chinese aggression.

Ranks: Ukraine and the leaders at the table · no bargain yet, Europe asking for no surprises · this week Source: theguardian.com

Global diesel prices ease as winter approaches: France 24

Global diesel prices have fallen sharply in recent weeks, France 24 reports, after staying elevated since the Iran war began and the Strait of Hormuz was blockaded. The network says Gulf exports are recovering and world oil supply is back on track for the first time since February. In the United States, the national average fell below six dollars a gallon on Saturday. In Europe, prices remain roughly seventy-seven percent above pre-war levels even after the drop. The easing is being felt on both sides of the Atlantic as winter approaches. Buyers in Europe are still paying far more than before the war. France 24 ties the change to recovering Gulf exports and supplies getting back on track, not to an end of the fighting. The same war that lifted those prices is the one in which Iran has kept its hold on Hormuz.

Ranks: Drivers in the United States and Europe · prices down, Europe still far above pre-war · this weekend Source: france24.com

Three men sentenced to death in Jordan over fatal church mass shooting: BBC

In Jordan, a court on Sunday sentenced three men to death by hanging for the 23 October shooting at a funeral at St George's Greek Orthodox church in the village of Hmoud, in Karak governorate. The BBC reports the attack killed nine people and injured thirty-four others; France 24 reported nine killed and twenty-five wounded. Jordan's Armed Forces said the men were convicted of terrorist acts, possessing weapons, promoting the ideas of a terrorist organisation, and conspiracy to commit terrorist acts. The three — two Jordanian brothers and their brother-in-law — have not been publicly identified; they fled, were found in an abandoned house in Karak, and were wounded after they refused to surrender and fired on security forces, who reported no personnel hurt. CCTV showed three men with guns firing on the church, and a second video showed dead and wounded people on the ground; why that church was chosen is still unclear. King Abdullah II wrote after the attack that it would meet the harshest retaliation and that those who thought bullets could divide Jordanians had no chance against unity. The week after the shooting, Jordan said its jets struck what it called terrorist organisations in Syria in retaliation; Syrian health officials told state media five people, including women, children and an elderly man, were killed in a village in southern Daraa province. Jordan said it killed and wounded several terrorists and had coordinated with the Syrian government; Syria said it had not been notified in advance. Christians make up around three percent of the population, Karak has a sizeable Christian community, and executions are by hanging after pauses from 2006 to 2014 and from 2017 until June this year.

Ranks: The Karak community and the three condemned men · death sentences imposed · Sunday's ruling Source: bbc.co.uk

Both Sides: Should Ukraine cede the Donbas for a NATO seat?

The United States has put a trade in front of Ukraine: membership in NATO if Kyiv gives up the whole Donbas. Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner raised it with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy at talks in Abu Dhabi, he told reporters on Saturday, in the question, "What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?" They then met President Vladimir Putin in Moscow on Thursday and briefed Zelenskyy in Dublin before European leaders gathered there on Friday.

Zelenskyy's case against giving up land is that Kyiv will not hand over any territory. His best reason, as he stated it, is security: real security in Europe is the European Union and NATO, and a bargain that swaps land for membership can itself cause war. He said alliance members had not put that question before, and he called the idea dangerous because such proposals lead to wars or escalations. Ukraine applied to join in September 2022 to fortify its border after Russia's full-scale invasion.

The case for even asking, as Washington framed it, is that NATO membership is the guarantee Kyiv has sought for almost five years, and that Putin has made all of the Donbas a condition for ending the war. The Kremlin's reason for rejecting the NATO half is that Ukrainian membership would demand a harsh response, and that the alliance should pull forces back from states on Russia's border.
