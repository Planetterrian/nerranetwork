Half a day without Outlook has left businesses and governments unable to work.

What You Need to Know: Half a day without Outlook has left businesses and governments unable to work, as a Microsoft email failure entered a second day. Oil supply is set to exceed demand for the first time since the Iran war, and President Donald Trump said Washington does not need a deal with Tehran. The Ten

Microsoft outages extend into 2nd day, company not aware of malicious activity: CBC

A failure in Microsoft services tied to Exchange Online and email is in a second day. CBC reported Friday that an hourslong outage was affecting a wide range of Microsoft 365 offerings that interact with those email services. Users reported Outlook, Teams and the Copilot assistant down. France 24 reported that the break began on Thursday and was still hitting people around the world on Friday, November 13. Microsoft blamed server problems and said it was struggling to fix them. The company said malicious activity was not to blame, and that it was not aware of malicious activity. It also reported making headway on the repair. France 24 said businesses, governments and likely millions of people have been without services like Outlook email for over half a day, triggering wide-ranging consequences. France 24’s tech correspondent Peter O’Brien said the scale and duration make it one of the biggest outages ever. Offices and public agencies that run mail and meetings on those tools are the ones living through the second day.

Oil supply to exceed global demand for first time since Iran war: Belga

The International Energy Agency said on Friday that more oil will be available worldwide than is needed to meet demand in the fourth quarter. It is the first time supply has exceeded demand since the war in Iran began in February. Belga reported the agency’s line that the market is showing tentative signs of rebalancing. Gulf exports are now at 74 percent of pre-war levels. Belga reported that Saudi Arabia and Iran have increased production, and that more tankers are passing the Strait of Hormuz even though attacks are still taking place there. The agency said Gulf exports have recovered much faster than global oil demand. Brent crude is trading at 96 dollars a barrel, down from more than 117 dollars at the end of October. Belga tied the drop to higher Middle East exports and hopes of a diplomatic solution. Rigzone, on the same monthly report, said the agency raised its estimate of global oil supplies in 2026 by 920,000 barrels a day. This year’s average deficit is now 685,000 barrels a day, against 1.7 million a day in last month’s projection, and the annual shortfall is 60 percent lower than that earlier report. This quarter shows an overhang of roughly 2 million barrels a day where a deficit of similar size had been projected a month ago. Saudi production reached 10 million barrels a day in October. Iraq’s output hit a seven-year high of 4.6 million a day. Gulf shipments excluding Oman reached 18.4 million barrels a day last month. Observed inventories rose in October for the first time since June, mostly as barrels at sea, while onshore stocks remained at their lowest in a decade. Global demand is forecast to fall by 2.68 million barrels a day this year, to an average of 102.3 million. IEA members will speed the release of remaining emergency stockpiles, discharging a further 71 million barrels through the end of February. Benchmark crude is down about 18 percent this month. The agency said the outlook for gasoil and diesel has marginally improved as high prices reined in consumption, and it expects the surplus emerging this quarter to widen next year.

Ranks: oil buyers and producing states worldwide · first surplus since the Iran war, with Brent down from over 117 dollars to 96 · this quarter Source: belganewsagency.eu

Source: rigzone.com

Why is Trump saying ‘no deal necessary’ with Iran as he sidelines JD Vance?: Al Jazeera

Hours before Tehran said it had hit a United States early-warning radar in Bahrain on Friday, President Donald Trump said he had told Vice President JD Vance, the country’s lead negotiator, “We don’t need a deal with Iran.” Al Jazeera reported that Trump wrote on Truth Social that he had asked Vance to focus his complete and total attention on what the president called massive corruption and crime in Democratic-run cities and states, after Democrats took control of Congress. Trump added, “Those days have long since passed. We’ve got their money, Hundreds of Billions of Dollars, and we’ve got their Strait!” Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it destroyed a US early-warning radar in Bahrain in retaliation for an attack on a telecommunications centre in Iran, and threatened an unlimited response over the Strait of Hormuz after a US attack. Tehran said the radar strike answered an air attack on a telecommunications tower in the southern port city of Sirik. Trump continues to claim Washington controls the strait. Al Jazeera reported that several commercial vessels trying to transit it have hit naval mines, or were struck by the Iranian military, over the past week. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Washington’s economic pressure campaign against Iran is proving effective, so a resumption of large-scale military operations is unnecessary, and that the economic pressure is going to get even worse. Trump threatened last week that he might “destroy” Iran; this week he said further strikes against Iran are “quite unlikely.” Vance, a known critic of the US-Israeli war on Iran, favours a deal. He wrote on November 4 that the reason Republicans lost is the war, that the president himself said so, and asked whether losing the Senate for the next six years was worth it. Vance led the April talks in Islamabad that produced a June memorandum of understanding; that deal collapsed over gaps on control of the strait. He has also led talks in Switzerland and has engaged Qatari and Pakistani mediators. At the G7 in Evian-les-Bains on June 17, Trump told reporters that if the effort worked he would take the credit and if it did not he would blame JD; Vance said the next day he believed the president was joking.

Ranks: people living with the US-Iran war, and ships in the Strait of Hormuz · talks set aside by the president, and a claimed strike on a US radar · immediate Source: aljazeera.com

Source: aljazeera.com

Why Aid Isn’t Reaching Ebola’s Epicenter: The New York Times

In the eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, the Ebola outbreak has a new epicenter largely isolated from aid. The New York Times reported that an armed group has killed and kidnapped aid workers, cutting that center off from help. Declan Walsh, the paper’s chief Africa correspondent, reported from a town where health workers are struggling to contain the outbreak. A second Times report calls the outbreak fast-spreading. It places the new epicenter in a region scarred by rebel violence. That account frames the danger as Ebola on one side and Islamic State on the other. Responders are struggling to help the people there. For patients and health workers in that town, isolation means aid is not reaching the place the outbreak has moved to.

Ranks: patients and health workers at Congo’s new Ebola epicenter · aid largely cut off by an armed group · now Source: nytimes.com

Source: nytimes.com

The U.S. and Iran are at war. But ICE is still working with Tehran on deportations: Los Angeles Times

In the United States, newly unsealed documents in a lawsuit show the administration has been communicating with Iran about deporting people back while the two countries are at war. The Los Angeles Times reported that at an October 15 hearing, Judge Ana C. Reyes voiced strong opposition to the Trump administration’s coordination with the Iranian government. She did not grant an injunction to halt information sharing. She will decide in the coming months whether the case may proceed. The government told the court it is “executing lawful orders of removal.” The Department of Homeland Security said ICE “actively coordinates” with countries to secure travel documents and is committed to ensuring that people are informed of their right to communicate with their consular representatives. The suit, filed in July by the Iranian American Legal Defense Fund against Secretary of State Marco Rubio, accuses immigration officials of sharing confidential information about Iranian asylum seekers with their home country and of a coordinated effort to identify Iranians and pressure them to return. Evidence includes a June incident at a California detention facility in which Iranian officials from Pakistan’s embassy in Washington interrogated an Iranian man days after he was detained. Mohammadjamal Azizi, an Iranian refugee in Tujunga who with his wife claimed asylum as Christian converts, said his wife received a message from ICE last month telling her to contact Iranian government officials in Washington. She had been held close to two years and feared deportation. Yasmine Chubin, executive director of an Iranian advocacy group, said attorneys knew of at least a dozen detainees approached about removal in recent weeks. All were asylum seekers, a majority Christian converts who entered through the southern border and had been held 18 months or longer. Chubin said one attempted suicide after hearing he might be returned. Pastor Kevin Kang of Tujunga United Methodist Church, who has housed Azizi and other formerly detained refugees, said applicants are pressed to put detailed information in asylum papers and fear that information reaching the government they fled. The Times noted that the Los Angeles area is home to the largest population of people of Iranian descent outside Iran.

Ranks: Iranian asylum seekers in US detention, at least a dozen approached recently · removal talks with Tehran while the countries are at war · court decision in the coming months Source: latimes.com

Leaders meet in Dublin amid search for lead EU negotiator for peace talks with Russia: The Guardian

In Dublin, Ukraine and Russia agreed to widen the next talks to European countries, after Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff spent over four hours with Vladimir Putin in the Kremlin on Thursday and then briefed Volodymyr Zelensky; Putin indicated Moscow will not compromise on hardline demands to end almost five years of war, and Luxembourg’s Luc Frieden stood in for the absent Belgian and Dutch prime ministers, saying a Benelux meeting in Maastricht two days earlier left them aligned on Ukraine.

Ranks: Ukrainians and European governments · a seat in the next round, with Moscow still refusing compromise · next round, date unstated Source: theguardian.com

Rescue Ends in Brooklyn After 8 Die in Building Collapse: The New York Times

In New York, officials ended the Flatbush search on Thursday after eight people died and eighteen were injured, including four firefighters, when 1321 Flatbush Avenue collapsed around 4 p.m. Wednesday into an excavation at 2625 Bedford Avenue; an internal police report said two of the dead were children who were cousins, three were construction workers, and about 250 personnel had searched.

Ranks: eight dead, eighteen injured, and a Flatbush neighborhood · rescue over after Wednesday’s collapse · already in effect Source: nytimes.com

Humanitarian situation in Gaza worsens amid political deadlock: DW

In Gaza, heavy rain flooded and damaged thousands of tents and several people died, while Al Jazeera reported the same rains flooding shelters, damaging solar power systems and threatening essential healthcare as peace talks remain stalled.

Ranks: displaced people in flooded tents · several deaths, damaged shelter and power · with the current rains Source: dw.com

Nvidia scandal: Howard Lutnick under pressure over alleged lack of action: The Guardian

In the United States, Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia’s chief executive on Thursday after reports of affairs, including one with Alice Huang, who is suspected of being a Chinese spy; Colette Kress is interim chief, the shares fell 4.3 percent by Thursday’s close, and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick faces calls that he could be fired as Congress summoned Huang for December.

Flooding worsens along B.C.’s South Coast as heavy rains continue: The Globe and Mail

In British Columbia, a week of storms left Port Mellon with 307 millimetres of rain from Tuesday morning to Thursday noon, the provincial high, and Kennedy Lake with 261; Squamish, Pemberton and the Lil’wat Nation faced evacuation orders or alerts after the Cheakamus Dam release and high flows on the Green River cut roads, including a flooded stretch of Highway 99.

Ranks: South Coast residents under orders and alerts · roads cut and homes at flood risk · through Friday’s rain Source: theglobeandmail.com

Both Sides: A former defence site for detained asylum seekers

The question is whether a former Ministry of Defence site near the Oxfordshire village of Piddington should house more than 1,200 detained asylum seekers. Housing and communities secretary Angela Rayner approved full planning permission, a decision that emerged on Thursday. The Home Office welcomed that step because it wants to end the use of asylum hotels. A spokesperson said the government is working on a fairer distribution of asylum accommodation across the country, with over one billion pounds saved in costs since the election. The same statement said former military sites are designed to be self-contained, with security and cameras around the clock, to limit the effect on the local community. Defence minister Luke Pollard told LBC there is a strong sense of feeling around Bicester and especially in Piddington, that the Home Office continues to engage, and that a few more stages remain so the plan is not yet fully locked in and concerns can be mitigated. Parish council chair Tim McNally told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme that villagers will take many actions and that ministers should absolutely expect a legal challenge, which would have to be lodged within the next six weeks. The village, about 350 residents, has already made a symbolic attempt to declare independence from the rest of the country after a local referendum. McNally said today is a day of reflection and that campaigners will apply the Hillsborough law, still being considered by parliament; once passed, he said, it would create a legally enforceable duty of candour compelling public officials to act transparently in investigations and inquiries. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch called the plans a betrayal of the women of Piddington and emblematic of what she called Labour and Angela Rayner’s disregard for the law and for women and girls’ safety. She said the more than 1,000 male migrants Labour is about to place in Oxfordshire are in Britain illegally and should be deported as soon as possible. The Home Office said a final decision to proceed has not yet been taken.
