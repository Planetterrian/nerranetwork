Pedro Sánchez dissolved Spain's parliament, forcing an election on 29 November over blocked housing decrees.

What You Need to Know: Pedro Sánchez dissolved Spain's parliament, forcing an election on 29 November after parliament blocked his housing decrees. The euro hit a 17-month low on French debt fears, and India's cabinet approved a vast corridor to move renewable power. This edition draws on all 5 Omni View regional desks.

The Ten

Housing, Corruption and a Migrant Surge: Why Spain Is Holding an Early Election: The New York Times

In Spain, in Europe, Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez dissolved Parliament on Monday and called an election for 29 November. The New York Times reports he acted after months of spiraling crises. The Globe and Mail reports a fragmented parliament last week rejected the government's key housing decrees amid widespread protests, and that Sánchez called the vote to strengthen his mandate after that defeat on emergency housing measures.

Ranks: Spain's voters, number unstated · parliament dissolved after housing decrees failed · 29 November Source: nytimes.com

Euro falls to 17-month low against dollar amid French debt fears: The Guardian

In France, in Europe, the euro fell as much as 0.8% against the dollar on Monday to below $1.12, its lowest since May 2025, before recovering slightly, The Guardian reports. It is down about 1.2% this month and about eight cents from a January peak of $1.20. Investors tied the sell-off to France's rising debt costs before next year's presidential election; the CAC 40 fell 1%, the FTSE 100 rose 0.2%, and Germany's Dax was little changed. French 10-year yields hit their highest since 2002 last week before dipping on Friday, and the gap with German borrowing costs widened to its widest since 2012, amid a sovereign-debt sell-off as the Iran war rattles markets. Kathleen Brooks of XTB called France the epicentre. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu's minority government has announced a €54bn savings drive, with investors worried a hung parliament and gains for Marine Le Pen's National Rally could block deficit cuts.

Ranks: eurozone borrowers and French taxpayers · a 17-month currency low and 2002-high yields · this week Source: theguardian.com

Union Cabinet approves ₹1.86 lakh crore Green Energy Corridor Phase-III: The Hindu

In India, in Asia, the Union Cabinet approved the ₹1.86 lakh crore Green Energy Corridor Phase-III to move renewable power from generation-rich states to consuming centres, The Hindu reports. ₹1.36 lakh crore is for intra-state transmission and ₹50,000 crore for 50 gigawatt-hours of battery storage, with ₹54,082 crore in central support and viability-gap funding. The corridor is designed to evacuate up to 135 gigawatts and is targeted for completion by fiscal year 2032-33. India had commissioned 263 gigawatts of renewable capacity by January 2026, against a policy target of 500 gigawatts by 2030. The International Energy Agency expects Indian electricity demand to grow 6.4% a year through 2030.

Ranks: India's electricity users · up to 135 gigawatts of renewable power to be moved · by fiscal 2032-33 Source: thehindu.com

Strike on WFP Aid Trucks in Sudan Kills One: ASHARQ AL-AWSAT English

In Sudan, in Africa, a World Food Program driver was killed on Saturday when an airstrike hit two contracted trucks carrying food between Dilling and Kadugli in South Kordofan. The agency said the trucks were clearly marked as humanitarian vehicles and did not say who carried out the strike. Famine was confirmed last year in Kadugli and in El-Fasher in Darfur. On Friday a WFP truck struck an explosive device in Dilling after a delivery. Aid workers estimate more than 200,000 people killed since the war began in April 2023, and more than 11 million displaced; UN figures say drones killed more than 1,100 people in the first half of this year.

Ranks: people needing food aid in South Kordofan · one driver killed and a marked convoy hit · immediate Source: english.aawsat.com

Nobel Prize awarded for showing how nerve cells shape feelings: BBC

In Sweden, the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine went to US psychiatrist Karl Deisseroth and German colleagues Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel for optogenetics, the BBC reports. In the early 2000s Hegemann and Nagel discovered channelrhodopsin, an algal protein that lets researchers switch individual neural circuits on or off with light. The Nobel Assembly said the work reveals circuits behind memories, feelings and behaviours linked to neurological and psychiatric disorders, and that researchers are using the method to try to restore sight. The three share 12 million Swedish kronor, about £900,000. Committee chair Per Svenningsson said optogenetics maps the brain in a way scientists could once only dream of.

Ranks: brain researchers and patients with visual impairment · a prize and a light-based way to switch circuits · now, with treatment still early Source: bbc.co.uk

Brazil, in South America, heads to a 25 October runoff after Flávio Bolsonaro beat President Lula without an outright majority.

Quebec, in North America, votes today, its campaign turned more on President Trump's tariffs than on independence.

U.S. Supreme Court to weigh bid by Suncor, ExxonMobil to avoid climate lawsuit: The Globe and Mail

The United States, in North America, will see its Supreme Court weigh Suncor and ExxonMobil's climate-suit bid, backed by the Trump administration.

Ranks: the companies and the suing state, number unstated · whether a climate suit can proceed · court timetable unstated Source: theglobeandmail.com

Both Sides: Should Switzerland ratify the Malaysia trade deal?

Switzerland must decide whether voters get a say on a free-trade agreement with Malaysia, and then whether that agreement stands. Campaigners handed 85,000 signatures to parliament in Bern on 5 October, The Straits Times reports. A referendum needs 50,000 valid signatures within 100 days of a law's publication. Parliament finished its approval in June. The paper reports that if the signatures are approved, the vote would likely come in the first half of 2027. The deal, between Malaysia and the European Free Trade Association, takes effect only once domestic ratification in the contracting states is complete.

The Alliance Against Tropical Deforestation, which led the signature collection, says the agreement plans to lower tariffs on 12.5 million kilograms of palm oil without effective measures against rainforest deforestation and forced labour. The alliance says Switzerland would help accelerate the destruction of a Malaysian rainforest it calls already critically threatened, while jeopardising plantation workers and indigenous communities.

Swiss authorities, when the deal was signed, said Switzerland had agreed a market-compatible, quota-based reduction in palm-oil tariffs so as not to hurt domestic vegetable-oil production. They said the agreement includes a comprehensive section on environmental protection and labour rights, with specific provisions to ensure palm oil traded under it is produced sustainably.

Both sides accept that parliament has approved the text, that palm oil tariffs are part of it, and that officials must still verify the signatures. Verification is the next step; a valid count would put the question to voters in the first half of 2027.

Renewables' share of India's electricity generation rose: The Hindu

Central Electricity Authority data cited by The Hindu show renewables contributed 17% of India's electricity generation in fiscal year 2025-26, compared with 14% the previous fiscal. Source: thehindu.com
