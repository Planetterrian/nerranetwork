Ukraine says seven drones sank a Russian warship off Libya, 1,500 miles from the front.

What You Need to Know: Ukraine says seven naval drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury off Libya and hit a cargo ship, a claim Moscow has not confirmed and that eastern Libyan authorities treat as an attack on their port. Oxfam says contamination now covers more than 99 percent of Gaza, and the U.S. Justice Department admitted in court that it deported a citizen. The Ten

Ukraine sinks Russian warship off Libya in latest expansion of the war: NBC News

Ukraine's president said Monday that Ukrainian forces sank a Russian warship in the Libyan port of Benghazi on Sunday and struck a cargo ship, in an operation Ukraine's Security Service put about 1,500 miles from the established front. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said seven Ukrainian-made naval drones sank the corvette Merkury, which he described as one of Russia's newest ships, commissioned in 2023, and hit the transport ship Angara, calling it an exemplary Ukrainian operation. NBC News reported that he posted footage of drones approaching vessels and of fireballs. The Security Service said the corvette regularly escorted naval convoys and guarded the Angara, which it said carried weapons, that both were legitimate military targets, and that the drones were launched from a mother ship after delivery to a set point. It said a Ka-27 helicopter on the corvette was also sunk, despite attempts to destroy the drones from the air. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Moscow had noted Zelenskyy's statement and left comment to the defence ministry, which had not yet spoken; Al Jazeera reported no official Russian comment and that it was unclear whether anyone was killed. Libya's eastern administration said an explosion struck a Russian vessel docked at Benghazi Naval Base on its way back to Russia after a stop to refuel, and called a strike inside a Libyan port a serious assault on sovereignty, without naming Ukraine. Saddam Haftar, deputy commander of the eastern forces, said Libya rejects the use of its territory to settle scores, while the United Nations Support Mission in Libya demanded an immediate, independent and transparent investigation. The eastern health ministry said every crew member from the cargo ship had been rescued and was being treated; Libya has been split since 2011 between a U.N.-backed government in Tripoli and a rival eastern administration tied to Russia.

Ranks: crews and port workers in Benghazi, plus Ukraine and Russia at war · a claimed sinking far outside the Black Sea, disputed by silence from Moscow · already done Sunday, with an investigation called now Source: nbcnews.com

Source: aljazeera.com

‘Environmental crimes’: Israel blamed for toxic pollution in 99% of Gaza: Al Jazeera

In the Gaza Strip, Oxfam and the Conflict and Environment Observatory say Israel's explosives and incendiary weapons have contaminated more than 99 percent of the territory, which they call a toxic wasteland for about 2.3 million people. Amitabh Behar, Oxfam's international executive director, said assaults on Gaza's environment are environmental war crimes that threaten life now and for generations, and that Palestinians are exposed through air, food, water, soil and rubble. The groups used satellite imagery, remote sensing, scientific analysis and open-source data, and they point to the highest concentrations in Gaza City, Jabalia and North Gaza. Al Jazeera, citing the report, said the war has killed more than 74,000 Palestinians, and that more than 1,500 have been killed since a ceasefire signed in October 2025. A U.N. commission of inquiry said in September 2025 that Israel had committed genocide in Gaza; the International Court of Justice is hearing a genocide case and said in January that it was plausible Israel had committed genocide. The U.N. Environment Programme estimates 61 million tonnes of debris, about 29 tonnes for each person living in Gaza now, and Oxfam says clearing it would take 2.1 million dump-truck loads. By 11 October 2025, Oxfam estimates, Israeli ground operations had directly affected about 283 square kilometres of Gaza's 365, or 78 percent, and more than 90 percent of Palestinians had been forcibly displaced. A study cited in the report put direct conflict emissions above 1.3 million tonnes of carbon-dioxide equivalent by January 2025. Oxfam said a full independent investigation is urgently needed to identify and reduce the risks.

Ranks: about 2.3 million people in Gaza · contamination reported across 99 percent of the land they live on · already in soil, water and air, with effects the authors say will last generations Source: aljazeera.com

Source: aljazeera.com

Trump administration deported US citizen to Mexico, DoJ admits in court: The Guardian

In the United States, the Justice Department admitted in federal court on Friday that it had deported a U.S. citizen to Mexico after authorities wrongly concluded he was undocumented. Brian José Morales García, 25, of Austin, Texas, was born in Denver and raised in Mexico; he was a passenger in a work vehicle stopped near Fredericksburg on 3 April, without identification, and he told officers his birth certificate and Social Security card were at home. Customs and Border Protection agent Robert Gil Jr consulted Mexican records that listed a Mexican birthplace, accused him of saying he was born in Mexico, and noted that he spoke only Spanish, according to court records cited by The Guardian. He was deported on 7 April, and the administration later said in a media statement that he had been in the country illegally. His lawyers sued on 1 May, saying he had falsely admitted an illegal entry and signed rapid-deportation papers because agents threatened imprisonment, and that family and friends were turned away when they tried to deliver proof of citizenship. On Friday the department denied that account, said he went to Mexico voluntarily and therefore had no basis to sue, and still conceded he was allowed back on 30 August after a review of his place of birth. U.S. Attorney Justin Simmons wrote that Morales García is a dual citizen of the United States and Mexico. Judge Robert Lee Pitman did not immediately rule on the request to dismiss; an October 2025 ProPublica report found agents had detained more than 170 U.S. citizens in the first nine months of President Donald Trump's second term, and Texas's public safety department works with federal immigration authorities under the 287(g) program.

Ranks: one citizen already deported, and others in similar stops · loss of home and work for months, then a court fight still open · back in the U.S. since 30 August; the dismissal request is undecided Source: theguardian.com

The worst COP ever? Climate talks collapse in chaos: France 24

In Turkey, COP31's final agreement was rejected on Sunday, ending the talks in failure. France 24 said a contentious deal collapsed over fossil fuels. The summit ran two days past its schedule. The network called that stretch record overtime and the ending chaotic. It said the collapse underscored strains on diplomacy in a fractured world. On Monday, several Pacific island nations expressed despair and disappointment that the U.N. talks had produced no final agreement on the climate crisis. France 24 said those small states are among the places most exposed to seas rising because of human-caused warming.

Ranks: governments at the Turkey summit, and Pacific island populations · no agreed climate text after a scheduled close · already failed Sunday; island governments answered Monday Source: france24.com

Source: france24.com

Rule changes leave international students rethinking plans to study in the U.S.: The Globe and Mail

In the United States, first-time international students at colleges and universities fell 15 percent this fall after a 17 percent drop the year before, according to the Institute of International Education. Applications by Canadians to first-year U.S. undergraduate programs for fall 2027 fell 13 percent from the previous year, according to Common App. In March 2025, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the United States had revoked more than 300 student visas and was looking to revoke more every day; by January 2026 the State Department said it had revoked some 8,000, including for protests and social-media activity. Some students were told their records on the Student and Exchange Visitor Information System had been terminated, which blocks class registration and practical training; others were arrested and detained. In April 2025, government lawyers told courts that Immigration and Customs Enforcement would restore those records but keep the power to terminate them again. The State Department told The Globe and Mail the United States remains the world's leading destination for international students. Zuzana Cepla Wootson of the Presidents' Alliance on Higher Education and Immigration, a plaintiff in one lawsuit that helped reverse some terminations and a group working with almost 600 campuses, said an atmosphere of fear remains because the rules keep changing. An unnamed Ontario lawyer who studied at Georgetown said he erased his social-media accounts, and a British Columbia science student said classmates at Northwestern University use a private app called Fizz to warn one another about immigration raids nearby.

Ranks: international students on U.S. campuses, and Canadians applying for 2027 · a 15 percent drop in new enrolments and thousands of visa revocations · already in this year's intake, with applications still falling Source: theglobeandmail.com

German police respond to multiple school bomb threats: DW

German police are checking school bomb threats they do not treat as credible, and Denmark is reporting a similar series of apparent hoaxes.

Ranks: pupils and parents in several German cities, and in Denmark · schools disrupted while police treat the warnings as not credible · happening Monday Source: dw.com

Chamonix mayor plans tourist limits for Alpine beauty spot: The Guardian

Chamonix's mayor plans tourist limits after as many as 4,000 people a day visit Lac Blanc, more than double the 2020 numbers.

Ranks: summer visitors to Lac Blanc and wildlife on those trails · crowding, litter and damage the mayor says already spoil the lakes · a limit is planned, not yet in force Source: theguardian.com

Daily cannabis use more common among Canadian adults than daily alcohol consumption, study finds: The Globe and Mail

In Canada, a medical-journal study of nearly 100,000 adults finds daily cannabis use is now more common than daily drinking.

Ranks: Canadian adults in a national health survey · daily cannabis ahead of daily alcohol, including among people under 35 · measured in 2023–2024 and published Monday Source: theglobeandmail.com

'Out of control': ICE arrests at Seattle airport spark outcry: France 24

Washington's governor called federal agents out of control after officers detained a father and daughter on a Seattle airport jet bridge.

Ranks: the father and daughter detained Saturday, and passengers who filmed it · an arrest in a boarding bridge by masked officers in unmarked jackets · already done; the governor answered in public Source: france24.com

Dozens Unaccounted for After Fire Rips Through Candy Factory in Indonesia: The New York Times

Dozens remain unaccounted for after a fire tore through an Indonesian candy factory where hundreds were present on Saturday.

Ranks: hundreds who were in the factory compound · dozens still unaccounted for after a blaze that lasted hours · the fire began Saturday; the search is the current fact Source: nytimes.com

Both Sides: Should Natural England be abolished?

Britain's first secretary of state, Louise Haigh, has put every arm's-length body in view for devolution, dissolution, or a return into a department, and she has not ruled out abolishing Natural England. The case reported for scrapping the nature watchdog is that faster planning could boost housebuilding and growth. Last month the Conservatives said that if they won power they would abolish Natural England and the Environment Agency to help create more homes. Haigh's own reason is institutional. She told Sky News that successive governments created more quangos, pushed decisions out of departments, and then objected when those bodies decided differently, and that her government is committed to devolving responsibilities where they can be devolved. On BBC Radio 4 she said all such bodies are in her sight to be devolved to mayors and strategic authorities, brought back under a secretary of state, or dissolved entirely. She did not say Natural England will be abolished. Tony Juniper, the chair of Natural England, answered the reports by saying he is proud of work to unite national ambitions for growth with nature's recovery, and that healthy nature is not opposed to economic development but enables it. The Guardian said removing or curbing the watchdog would be likely to prompt concern from environmental groups. Haigh spoke Monday before a CBI speech in which she argued for greater public control of water and energy, sectors where she said privatisation had not worked, a separate argument from the quango review. Source: theguardian.com
