At least thirty-four people died off Senegal as a migrant boat headed for the Canary Islands sank.

By Mira November 09, 2026 Ep 48 10 min read Listen to podcast
Listen to this episode
Contents
The Ten Both Sides: Is French police violence systemic or isolated?

What You Need to Know: At least 34 people died when a migrant boat sank off Senegal on the way to Spain’s Canary Islands, while arrivals have overwhelmed El Hierro. In Washington, President Trump said deportations might have been too tough and that an arrangement for some undocumented immigrants is doable, with Qatar’s prime minister due at the White House today. This edition also draws on the Omni View Omni View North America desk.

The Ten

At least 34 dead after Europe-bound migrant boat sinks off Senegal: BBC World

Senegal’s interior ministry said at least 34 people died after a small wooden boat capsized between Dakar and Saint-Louis while heading toward Spain’s Canary Islands, the Senegalese Press Agency reported, citing an official source. The ministry said one person was rescued. Saliou Diouf, founder of the Senegalese migrant aid group Boza Fii, told AFP that all the bodies recovered so far were Senegalese, with search operations continuing. President Bassirou Diomaye Faye expressed condolences, urged nationals not to risk the crossings, and pledged on X to keep fighting the networks that organize the journeys and to give young people reasons to hope at home. Spanish group Walking Borders recorded 635 deaths on the Atlantic route to the Canary Islands between January and May 2026, the highest toll among the routes it monitors, and estimated about 21 deaths for every 100 people who reached the islands in that period. Senegalese authorities said they intercepted another boat on Friday carrying 142 migrants, including 23 women, two babies and two other children, nationals of Senegal, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea and Nigeria. On El Hierro, local authorities said more than 3,300 people had reached the island in about 30 boats since the beginning of November, almost a third of the population, and a state of emergency was declared on Saturday. Fernando Clavijo, president of the Canary Islands regional government, called the situation absolutely unsustainable, Reuters reported. Nearly one in four young people in Senegal was unemployed in the second quarter of 2026, the country’s statistics agency said, and the interior ministry said on Sunday it would keep combating smuggling networks, including with neighboring countries.

Ranks: people on the Atlantic route and residents of El Hierro · at least 34 dead and arrivals near a third of one island’s population · already, with the search continuing Source: bbc.co.uk

Deportation policy ‘might have been too tough’, says Trump, as Miller starts chief-of-staff role: The Guardian

In the United States, President Donald Trump said he thinks his administration was too aggressive on deportations and that he was open to an arrangement to let some undocumented immigrants remain. He said, “I think it might be too tough, yeah,” adding that a lot of people would say it is great and a lot of people think it is too tough. He said, “We would work something out,” and called such an arrangement “actually doable.” On whether he would endorse a Republican successor for 2028, he said “probably, not necessarily,” and said, “We have a tremendous bench.” He said on Sunday that he “was only kidding” when he announced a day earlier that he had offered the White House press secretary job to Dana White, chief executive of the Ultimate Fighting Championship. The White House schedule shows he meets Qatar’s prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al-Thani, also foreign minister, in a closed-door meeting today, as the conflict with Iran continues and diplomacy faces obstacles; a U.S. official said Vice President JD Vance is also scheduled to meet the Qatari leader. Attorney Ali Rahnama of the Iranian American Legal Defense Fund said a deportation flight of Iranian nationals bound for Iran by way of Pakistan was canceled Sunday morning without explanation after at least eight Iranians were loaded onto a plane in Mesa, Arizona, headed for Georgia and then Pakistan. The comments come as Stephen Miller starts as White House chief of staff.

Ranks: undocumented immigrants in the United States, and the governments meeting today · a possible change he called doable, plus a canceled flight · the Qatar meeting is today; any new arrangement is not yet a rule Source: theguardian.com

French police officers face trial over beating of black man they accused of not wearing Covid face mask: The Guardian

In France, three police officers go on trial Monday over the beating of music producer Michel Zecler, now 47, outside his Paris studio on 21 November 2020, when they accused him of failing to wear a mandatory face mask and suspected him of cannabis use. Officers Aurelien B., now 29, Pierre P., 37, and Philippe T. face charges of intentional violence committed in a group and of forgery in an official report, and they face up to 15 years in jail. Their lawyers declined to comment before the trial. The investigation found he suffered about 30 punches and truncheon blows over about five minutes; the medical report noted craniofacial trauma, a wound to the lip and scalp, and a complete rupture of a biceps tendon, leaving him unable to do his job for 45 days. Zecler told AFP he expects the trial to hold the officers accountable, that he was insulted and will say so at the hearing, and that without the studio cameras their report could have sent him to prison. Campaigners and researchers describe police violence as systemic; authorities and law enforcement unions say it is limited to isolated cases. A report last year by the rights ombudswoman, after a survey of more than 5,000 people, said young men perceived as Arab, black or from North Africa are four times more likely to be stopped than the rest of the population and 12 times more likely to face more severe measures, including being frisked. In recent student-led protests a 14-year-old lost most of his teeth when an officer appeared to fire a grenade at close range; in 2024 a judge gave suspended jail sentences over irreversible rectal injuries to Théo Luhaka in a 2017 stop-and-search, and another officer is to be tried over the June 2023 point-blank shooting of 17-year-old Nahel Merzouk.

Ranks: Michel Zecler, three accused officers, and people stopped by French police · a rare criminal trial, not a verdict · the trial starts today Source: theguardian.com · france24.com

They signed up for a ‘forever’ cruise. Their dream ran aground after 750 days: The Globe and Mail

Florida-based Villa Vie Residences said it could no longer operate the Odyssey, and about 200 passengers left the ship at Cambodia’s port of Sihanoukville on October 28, about 750 days into a voyage billed as lasting 1,301 days. Last Wednesday the ship sailed for Singapore without them and is expected to be auctioned to pay the company’s debts as Villa Vie faces potential bankruptcy. The company said it continues to evaluate paths forward, but no financing or transaction has been finalized. Founder Mikael Petterson told Business Insider the project would have been fine, and would have been seen through, if there was no Iran war. Passengers who spoke with The Globe and Mail pointed to company decisions they said left the business vulnerable before the closing of the Strait of Hormuz and the later spike in oil prices. Mary Ann Demsar, of Stony Creek, Ontario, said money was not spent properly and that the strain became clear when residents, not only founders, were asked for money; she said a middle-of-the-night message from owner Kathy Villalba told everybody to be out by 2 p.m. Alberta entrepreneur Gian Perroni said he had sold his house to join, having never taken a cruise, and that cash calls grew more frequent and more outrageous. Demsar credited the crew and Cambodia’s government, which helped with accommodations and food.

Ranks: about 200 passengers who had made the ship home · the voyage has stopped and the ship is headed toward auction · already; bankruptcy is not finalized Source: theglobeandmail.com

The American and Ukrainian Chaplains Traveling to the War’s Frontlines: Christianity Today

Ukrainian American Mikhail Pavenko, 34, of Seattle, a power-line repairman, left his wife and two sons on October 29 for his 10th two-week rotation as a military chaplain since Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine. He said he had never felt that kind of fear, and that on arrival he goes to the front, ministering to soldiers, helping with evacuations, tending the wounded and delivering supplies. He said first-person-view attack drones generate the most fear; some can reach roughly 90 miles per hour and carry warheads powerful enough to disable a tank. In April 2025 in the Zaporizhzhia region, he said, a drone circled the pickup he shared with two other chaplains; he believes a jammer suppressed the operator’s signal, and the drone hit the ground about 15 feet in front of a military truck. Christianity Today reported that Ukraine has more than 400 military chaplains for an estimated 800,000 troops, plus about 700 volunteer chaplains and foreign volunteers, and is about 40 percent short of the number needed, while some soldiers’ morale is low nearly five years into the full-scale war. Pavenko said many fear allies may abandon Ukraine as Russia increases attacks on energy and water systems ahead of winter. The magazine said President Volodymyr Zelensky expressed dismay over a Trump administration deal with President Vladimir Putin to export diesel fuel to the United States, which some critics described as an attempt to sway voters before the November midterms. It cited the Council on Foreign Relations listing a call for Zelensky’s removal, claims that Putin has done more to pursue peace than Zelensky, and disruptions to Starlink service used to intercept drones, and reported that Russia lost more territory than it gained in September and October while attacks on civilians continued.

Ranks: Ukrainian troops and the chaplains who serve them · a reported 40 percent shortfall while drone and winter strikes continue · ongoing Source: christianitytoday.com

Ukraine Peace Talks Resume This Week in Abu Dhabi: The New York Times

U.S.-led talks to end the war between Russia and Ukraine resume this week in Abu Dhabi, and a cease-fire will not be on the table.

Ranks: people living through the Russia-Ukraine war · talks return without a cease-fire on the agenda · this week Source: nytimes.com

Gaza authorities slam global ‘inaction’ after mudslide kills five people: Al Jazeera

In Gaza, four children and a woman were killed when heavy rain collapsed a sandbank onto their tent, and authorities condemned global inaction.

Ranks: the family in that tent, and people still in Gaza’s camps · five killed, four of them children · already Source: aljazeera.com

Nvidia probe 'to impact the whole of the world': France 24

Nvidia opened a probe into Jensen Huang after he acknowledged an affair with a woman U.S. officials say worked for Chinese intelligence.

Ranks: Nvidia, its customers, and U.S.-China technology ties · a board probe of the chief executive is underway · now Source: france24.com

How a Bank Implosion in Paraguay Ensnared a President: The New York Times

Prosecutors accused Paraguay’s President Santiago Peña of funneling nearly a billion dollars of government funds to the Ueno bank.

Ranks: Paraguay’s public funds and its presidency · a prosecutorial accusation, not a conviction · the charge is public; no trial date is in the report Source: nytimes.com

Both Sides: Is French police violence systemic or isolated?

What is in dispute is how France should account for police violence, as three officers face a criminal trial over the 21 November 2020 beating of music producer Michel Zecler. No new statute is being voted today. The trial itself is the next formal step, and it opens Monday. The officers are accused. They have not been convicted.

Campaigners and rights groups say the violence is systemic, and that ethnic minorities receive harsher treatment than white people. Their strongest reason is the pattern they cite around this case and beyond it. France’s human rights ombudswoman said police used racist insults, according to four witnesses. Zecler says he was insulted with a racist slur and will repeat that at the hearing. A report last year by the ombudswoman, after a survey of more than 5,000 people, said young men perceived as Arab, black or from North Africa are four times more likely to be stopped than the rest of the population and 12 times more likely to face more severe measures, including frisks. Campaigners also point to other filmed cases, including the June 2023 shooting of 17-year-old Nahel Merzouk and the 2017 injuries to Théo Luhaka, for which a judge in 2024 gave three officers suspended jail sentences.

Authorities and law enforcement unions say brutality is limited to isolated cases. Their strongest reason is that a criminal trial is the exception: most alleged brutality is handled internally, and guilty verdicts are rare. Their lawyers declined to comment before the trial. If a court convicts them, the charges carry up to 15 years in jail. No article gives a verdict date.
