The Ten

Putin hosts Trump envoy Witkoff for Ukraine talks, frees U.S. citizen held in Russia: The Globe and Mail

Russia hosted U.S. Special Envoy Steve Witkoff in St. Petersburg on Wednesday for a ninth in-person meeting with President Vladimir Putin on ending the war in Ukraine, and released retired American teacher Stephen Hubbard, 74, from custody. Hubbard, from Michigan, was sentenced in 2024 to nearly seven years after prosecutors accused him of serving in a defence unit in Izyum; he denies the mercenary charge, the U.S. State Department had classified him as wrongfully detained, and Russian state media said Putin told Witkoff that Hubbard had been taken to Pulkovo Airport. The Globe and Mail reported the talks opened with that release inside the Constantine Palace, with investment envoy Kirill Dmitriev present, as Washington presses a new diplomatic push more than two years after President Donald Trump promised to end the war within 24 hours. Witkoff told Putin he had brought ideas for a peace deal; after the meeting he said the Oslo talks were constructive, that a foundation exists on which a Ukraine ceasefire can be built, and that he hopes Putin will consider trilateral talks with Kyiv on technical issues. A day earlier Witkoff met President Volodymyr Zelensky in Oslo in talks a U.S. official called constructive; Secretary of State Marco Rubio called them positive and said Witkoff would carry those ideas to Russia, while Zelensky said the meeting agreed to resolve disruptions to intelligence sharing and the Starshield satellite system. Trump said it was time for Ukraine to end the war or face "a very bad thing," and that Zelensky will have to want a deal because "I don't think his people are going to stand for it any longer." Putin, who has sought full control of Donetsk and no NATO membership for Ukraine, told Witkoff Russia is prepared to help resolve the Iran crisis and wants economic cooperation restored; he said the first shipment under this month's diesel export deal with the United States leaves in two to three days, and Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov said Moscow would listen but expected no miracle. Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store said Witkoff planned to return to Oslo and that Russia may be looking for a diplomatic opening; separately, Russia said its position on an energy ceasefire is unchanged and that it seeks peace rather than a truce, while the Wall Street Journal reported Elon Musk ordered a Starshield slowdown that Reuters could not verify, Trump denied ordering restrictions, and Rubio said Washington had not pulled back Kyiv's Starlink access.

Is Ebola under control in Kenya after nine cases confirmed?: BBC

Kenya is facing its first Ebola outbreak, with nine confirmed cases all linked to a man who travelled from the Democratic Republic of Congo and died two weeks ago, the BBC reported. Four relatives of that first patient, three health workers and a hospital cleaner have tested positive. The United States has advised its citizens to reconsider travel to Kenya, and Uganda, Ethiopia and Somalia are on alert. The International Rescue Committee says more than 9,500 cases in Congo since May, and more than 4,000 deaths, make this the second most deadly outbreak of the disease and leave Kenya exposed to further imported infections. The outbreak is the Bundibugyo species, which has no approved vaccine or treatment, though trials are under way. Health Director-General Patrick Amoth said Tuesday evening that the eight surviving patients were improving and responding to treatment. Kenya has ordered enhanced screening for travellers from ten countries it calls high risk, including digital forms and QR codes, and has designated isolation facilities and a dedicated test laboratory at Kenyatta National Hospital. Of 135 listed contacts of the first patient, 134 remain in quarantine; the International Rescue Committee said that pattern shows the early response is working, while doctors' union representative Dennis Miskellah told the BBC that border and port facilities are grossly understaffed.

Ranks: Kenyans, travellers, and neighbours of a regional hub · nine linked cases in a first outbreak, against a much larger Congo epidemic · now, while contacts stay in quarantine Source: bbc.co.uk

Wall Street Journal: Regional Oil Exports Exceed Pre-War Levels: IranWire

Middle Eastern oil exports, including shipments through the Strait of Hormuz and alternative routes, have risen above pre-war levels even as attacks on shipping continue, IranWire reported from a Wall Street Journal account of Kpler tracking data. Regional exports reached over 24 million barrels a day in the week ending October 18, compared with 23.3 million before the war. Oil moving through Hormuz itself is about 14 million barrels a day, 82 percent of the previous volume. The Journal described that gap as a sign of Iran's diminished ability to stop regional oil reaching world markets, citing U.S. naval protection, ship-to-ship transfers off Oman, and pipelines in Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates. In September, about 40 percent of crude exported by Persian Gulf countries other than Iran bypassed Hormuz, up from 17 percent before the war. About 30 attacks on vessels were recorded in October, maritime security reports cited by the paper said, while the cost of moving a barrel from the Gulf to China on a very large crude carrier reached roughly $37.37 and war-risk insurance rose to as much as 10 percent of a vessel's value in some cases. High profit margins are still drawing tanker owners into the region despite drone and missile strikes. The same account said Iran's own oil exports have virtually stopped under a U.S. maritime blockade.

Ranks: oil buyers and Gulf exporters worldwide · volumes above the pre-war rate, at much higher shipping and insurance cost · already, in the latest tracking week Source: iranwire.com

UK approves Jackdaw gasfield in North Sea: The Guardian

The United Kingdom has approved production at the Jackdaw gasfield in the North Sea, and the government said gas will reach homes and businesses from the end of the month. Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh said she agreed the regulator can grant consent, that the field will create 27 jobs and secure 273, and that the decision supports winter energy security and is compatible with legally binding climate commitments and a 2050 net-zero target. Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the step will protect hundreds of jobs and help keep millions of homes warm, arguing Britain will still need oil and gas for decades even on an ambitious climate path. Government data cited in a Guardian analysis show oil and gas supplied 75 percent of UK energy in 2025 and about 43 percent of overall energy was imported. Jackdaw, about 150 miles east of Aberdeen, is owned and operated by Adura, a venture half owned by Equinor and half by Shell; a Guardian column said it will be 6 percent of UK North Sea gas output at peak over an 11-year life. Greenpeace UK's Ami McCarthy said the approval will not lower bills, will not meaningfully add to supply, and will create just 27 full-time jobs, putting Jackdaw at around 2 percent of gas demand for roughly ten years. Uplift's Tessa Khan said the field has been oversold and that no new oil and gas fields fit safe climate limits; Green leader Zack Polanski accused Labour of climate vandalism. Fahnbulleh said the government will launch an energy wealth fund with a £2 billion down payment, and Rosebank, mostly oil, is being decided separately.

Ranks: UK households that use gas · a new domestic supply from month's end, not a promised cut in bills · this winter Source: theguardian.com

'Hunted like animals': inside Trump's war on the people who build America: The Guardian

The United States is running its largest immigration-arrest effort in Texas construction, where the Guardian reported more than 12,000 ICE arrests in July, almost double Florida or California. The paper said four in ten Texas construction workers are foreign born, most of them Hispanic and undocumented, and that the administration's ICE 2.0 drive is chasing a target of 3,000 immigration arrests a day. Houston immigration lawyer Jacob Monty said agents are hunting construction workers in the early morning the way hunters go after deer. The Guardian reported that house framer Lorenzo Salgado Araujo was killed by ICE agents, and that street arrests are leaving sites unfinished and pushing up house prices and grocery costs. Sergio Benavides, a naturalised citizen and home-building company owner in the Rio Grande valley who voted for Donald Trump in 2024, said a subcontractor and the man's wife were picked up by ICE and left three U.S.-citizen children. Benavides said home, food and gas prices have risen and that remaining workers must be paid more because so many have been deported. The account said Latino Texans who can vote, including some who backed Trump for the economy and the border, are angry as midterm elections approach within days. The Guardian described the shift as a move from last year's militarised operations in Los Angeles and Minneapolis to less visible stop-and-search tactics with, it said, rich pickings in Texas.

Ranks: Texas construction workers and the families around them · thousands of arrests and emptied job sites · now, with midterms days away Source: theguardian.com

Both Sides: Should Britain produce gas from Jackdaw on the way to net zero?

The United Kingdom has decided to let the Jackdaw gasfield produce, with supply due from the end of the month. The question is whether that gas should be brought ashore while the government still says it will reach net zero by 2050.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham's case is that the real question is where oil and gas come from, not whether Britain will still burn them. He says the approval will protect hundreds of jobs and help keep millions of homes warm this winter, and that even on an ambitious climate path the country will need oil and gas for decades. Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh says homegrown gas this winter supports energy security, reduces use of higher-emission imports, fits legally binding climate commitments, will create 27 jobs and secure 273, and will sit beside a £2 billion down payment on an energy wealth fund for the climate transition. A Guardian analysis adds that oil and gas supplied 75 percent of UK energy in 2025, that about 43 percent of energy was imported, and that Jackdaw — 6 percent of UK North Sea gas at peak, over an 11-year life — pipes fuel into the national network.

Greenpeace UK's Ami McCarthy says there is nothing pragmatic about rubber-stamping a project that will not lower bills, will not boost energy security, and will create just 27 full-time jobs, while leaving households tied to expensive oil and gas and to petrostates. She puts Jackdaw at around 2 percent of gas demand for roughly ten years. Uplift's Tessa Khan says the field will be largely unmanned and operated remotely, will not stop the decline in North Sea jobs, and that no new oil and gas fields are compatible with safe climate limits. Green leader Zack Polanski has accused Labour of climate vandalism.

Gas is scheduled to flow by the end of the month. Rosebank, mostly oil, is still a separate decision, and the government has asked the regulator to consider other urgent tie-back fields.
