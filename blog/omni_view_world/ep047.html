Seventy thousand tents flooded in Gaza, leaving displaced families without shelter tonight.

The Ten Both Sides: Who is responsible for the latest civilian deaths in Yemen? Progress Watch

What You Need to Know: Heavy rain has flooded seventy thousand tents across the Gaza Strip, and displaced families are without adequate shelter. Missiles aimed at Riyadh damaged a Saudi Aramco building, climate talks open in Antalya, and a gunman killed six people in Kraków. This edition also draws on the Omni View Omni View North America desk.

The Ten

Storm floods tens of thousands of tents across war-ravaged Gaza: The Arab Weekly

In the Gaza Strip, heavy rain and strong winds since late Friday flooded shelters used by displaced Palestinians, Al Jazeera reported, and France 24 said the water reached camps in Deir al-Balah as well as elsewhere in the territory. The Government Media Office said the storm flooded 70,000 tents and brought down dozens of walls, and it estimated the damage at $3 million. Al Jazeera’s Ibrahim al-Khalili, reporting from Gaza City, said the makeshift tents do not keep out harsh weather, that wind and rain tore through shelters families had spent days trying to reinforce, and that he saw soaked mattresses, pillows and clothes. He said nearly 400,000 displaced Palestinians are sheltering in locations considered highly vulnerable to more flooding. From Deir el-Balah, Al Jazeera’s Ashraf Abu Amra said families whose shelters flooded had not received food aid since early Saturday. France 24 reported that residents said overflowing sewage had mixed with the floodwater, and that families were building earthen barriers and draining water by hand after essential belongings were soaked. The Gaza Civil Defence said it received at least 1,500 emergency calls, that more than 2,000 buildings are at risk of collapse, and that at least 300,000 tents are needed for adequate shelter. Jonathan Veitch, UNICEF’s special representative in Palestine, said most people are living in terrible conditions and called for more funding, while relief groups have struggled for years to bring supplies in. Al Jazeera reported that Israel continues to impose severe restrictions on aid, shelter supplies and reconstruction materials. Al Jazeera also reported that more than 74,640 people have been killed since 7 October 2023, and that Israel has killed at least 1,610 Palestinians since a ceasefire took effect in October 2025.

Saudi Aramco building in Riyadh damaged in ‘attempted’ attack: Al Jazeera

In Saudi Arabia, an administrative building belonging to Saudi Aramco in Riyadh was damaged by falling shrapnel, with no injuries or fatalities, the energy ministry said through state media on Sunday, after an attempted attack. Several news agencies reported damage to a tower in the King Abdullah Financial District. The Saudi-led coalition said its air defences destroyed several ballistic missiles launched by the Houthis. Spokesman Major-General Turki al-Maliki said two missiles were launched toward Riyadh, another targeted the Eastern Province, and that defences also destroyed two missiles in the south and a drone. France 24 reported that the Aramco Tower was damaged by falling debris after two ballistic missiles headed for the capital’s financial district were intercepted. A day earlier, Yemen’s Houthi-run health ministry said Saudi-led air strikes killed at least 33 people, including eight women and 13 children, in the village of al-Shariyah in the Bani Hushaish district near Sanaa, and wounded at least 39, hitting homes, roads, paramedics and civil defence teams. France 24’s live account stated the same death toll from Saturday’s strikes on Bani Hushaysh and said most of those killed were women and children. Hospital director Abdul-Aziz al-Dailami said many victims remained under rubble, including first responders. The coalition had no immediate comment, and Al Jazeera said it could not independently verify that toll. On Friday an attack on a market in Taiz province killed 30 people: the Houthis blamed Saudi air strikes, while Yemeni government forces accused the Houthis of firing a ballistic missile. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree claimed the coalition carried out 105 air strikes and missile attacks in 24 hours on Sanaa and seven other governorates, taking the total since the escalation began to 4,369. The United Nations says more than 1,600 people have been killed and almost 300,000 displaced since a 2022 ceasefire collapsed in July, after the Houthis announced a blockade of Saudi ports. Al Jazeera reported that the Houthis have hit Saudi airports, the Ras Tanura and Rabigh refineries and other Aramco sites since that collapse, and that at least 13 people were killed and more than 300 wounded in a Houthi strike on Riyadh airport on 10 October.

Ranks: People in Yemen and in Riyadh, hundreds of thousands already displaced · a damaged oil building and disputed civilian deaths · immediate Source: aljazeera.com

Source: france24.com

US launches task force to disrupt land transit routes into Iran: France 24

The United States military has announced a 20-member task force to find ways to disrupt land routes into Iran that it says are used for shipments of weapons and illicit materials, France 24 reported. US Central Command chief Brad Cooper said in a statement posted on Saturday that the work will further constrain Iran’s ability to finance terrorism, rebuild weapons factories and replenish its military. The team of civilian and military personnel, based in Florida, would coordinate with US government agencies on preventing the movement of advanced weapons systems, dual-use technologies, sanctioned materials and illegal revenue streams across Iran’s land borders and the Caspian Sea. The restrictions would expand a US naval blockade of Iranian ports during the two countries’ eight-month war. Tehran has also restricted passage through the Strait of Hormuz, choking global energy supplies. Revolutionary Guards spokesman Hossein Mohebbi, quoted by the semi-official Fars news agency, said the Iron Gate plan is merely wordplay and that the Americans cannot achieve economic collapse in Iran this way, because Tehran has made preparations and communicates closely with neighbours. Israel’s defence minister, Israel Katz, said on Sunday at a ceremony that the mission regarding Iran is still not complete. He charged that Iran is trying to renew efforts to obtain nuclear weapons, which the Islamic republic has long denied, alongside other efforts he said place Israel in real danger.

Ranks: Oil buyers and people living with the Iran war, a global set · a wider blockade, if it works · starting now, over months Source: france24.com

Who’s who at Cop31: key players at the climate talks: The Guardian

In Turkey, the COP31 climate summit opens on Monday in Antalya, with Australia in charge of the negotiations after a bitter hosting row with Turkey, the Guardian reported, and Anadolu Agency said the talks kick off that day. Hosting the summit was a pledge from Prime Minister Anthony Albanese in 2021, while he was still in opposition. Arcane rules made Australia wait its turn to bid, and the compromise put the talks in the Turkish city while leaving Australia to run them. Albanese will speak at the opening of the leaders’ segment and is unlikely to stay long, and Australian media has been quieter than in previous host countries. Climate and energy minister Chris Bowen will be in daily charge of the negotiations, while parts of Australia’s energy supply also sit in his portfolio, so he has had to boost fossil fuels while insisting on a clean-energy transition. The Guardian said Australia remains a major fossil-fuel exporter and has also invested heavily in renewables, to the point that consumers are sometimes offered free electricity. President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan showed little interest in climate before bidding to host. In the last five years the summit has also been held in Egypt, the United Arab Emirates and Azerbaijan, which the Guardian said has given the Arab group an outsize role and a strong interest in fossil-fuel representation, including lobbyists. Environment minister Murat Kurum controls the action agenda of voluntary initiatives, which Erdoğan has used to advance his wife Emine Erdoğan’s push to cut waste and promote a circular economy. She has called for an end to throwaway culture and for recycling instead of landfill, though Turkey still has sites that leak vast quantities of methane. Fatih Birol, head of the International Energy Agency, is understood to be among the advisers behind this year’s emphasis on electrification for heating, transport and industry, which could halve the energy needed after renewable generation is expanded. Brazil’s re-elected president, Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, will come for his first trip abroad after winning a fourth term and will bring two non-binding roadmaps, one on a transition away from fossil fuels and one on halting and reversing deforestation, with no deadlines or firm requirements on governments. President Xi Jinping will not attend. China is the world’s second-biggest economy and the biggest emitter, making up nearly a third of annual global carbon output, mainly from fossil fuels used in 25 years of rapid growth, while its renewable investment is also reshaping the energy business. The officials he sends will know that Russia, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates are important allies.

Ranks: Governments at the talks, and people exposed to a hotter climate · the agenda for this round of talks, not a new treaty yet · from Monday Source: theguardian.com

Briton among six dead after gunman opens fire in Kraków market square: The Guardian

In Poland, a gunman opened fire with an automatic weapon in Kraków’s main market square at about 3 p.m. on Saturday, the Guardian reported. Authorities said six people were killed and 16 wounded in what was a busy square. A Briton was among the six dead. Three more people were found dead at the home of the suspect. The suspect, a 25-year-old Polish citizen, was apprehended by police. Poland’s defence minister called the shooting an act of terror on the people of the city.
