Top 12 Space & Astronomy Stories

  1. Fermi Closes the MeV Gap with Fourteen-Year Map — Phys.org

    The Fermi Large Area Telescope Collaboration has produced the clearest view yet of low-energy gamma rays by identifying four hundred fifty cosmic sources between twenty and two hundred megaelectronvolts. This range had remained a persistent blind spot because earlier instruments lacked the sensitivity to measure it reliably. The new catalog supplies the first systematic bridge across what astronomers call the MeV gap. The work appears in The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series. Source: phys.org

  2. James Webb Telescope Captures Stellar Merger Aftermath — ScienceDaily

    Astronomers observed a small star spiral into a giant star eighteen thousand times brighter than the Sun inside the Andromeda galaxy. The collision produced a violent eruption whose aftermath James Webb Space Telescope imaged just forty-seven days later. The data provide fresh detail on the physical processes that occur when two stars merge into one. Source: sciencedaily.com

  3. NASA Tests Moon Rocket Engine for Two Hundred Seconds — ScienceDaily

    A powerful engine intended for NASA’s Moon rocket completed a two-hundred-second hot-fire test. The firing demonstrated sustained performance under conditions that simulate an actual lunar mission burn. Engineers will now analyze the data to confirm the engine meets requirements for crewed flights. Source: sciencedaily.com

  4. First Caught Starship Upper Stage Will Become Museum Piece — Space.com

    SpaceX founder Elon Musk stated that the first Starship upper stage recovered by the launch tower’s mechanical arms will not fly again. The vehicle will instead be preserved for public display. The decision marks the end of the booster’s flight career after its successful catch. Source: space.com

  5. Isar Aerospace Begins Construction at Spaceport Nova Scotia — Maritime Launch Services

    Isar Aerospace has taken operational control of a dedicated launch complex at Spaceport Nova Scotia and started building its own pad and assembly facilities. The site will support Spectrum launches beginning in twenty twenty-eight and gives Canada a second orbital launch location on its Atlantic coast. The Canadian government has backed the project as part of its sovereign space capability goals. First orbital launches from the new pad are targeted for twenty twenty-eight. Source: maritimelaunch.com

  6. Cyprus Opens New Space Research Centre and Unveils Satellites — Cyprus Mail

    The Cyprus Space Research and Innovation Centre inaugurated its completed facilities this week, including a mission control room and a space weather operations centre that uses artificial intelligence to forecast solar activity. Three locally built Kyrenia3 satellites were unveiled at the same time. Hungary’s backup astronaut Gyula Cserenyi attended the events and discussed plans for a Space for Schools programme that would let students receive signals from the satellites. The centre was co-funded by Cyprus and the European Union. Source: cyprus-mail.com

  7. SpaceX Prepares NROL-136 Launch for National Reconnaissance Office — Spaceflight Now

    The NROL-136 mission will add satellites to the National Reconnaissance Office’s proliferated architecture constellation in low Earth orbit. It marks the fourth such launch this year and the fifteenth overall. Liftoff from Space Launch Complex 4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base is scheduled for four twenty-three a.m. PST. Falcon 9 booster B1097 will fly its fourteenth mission and target a landing at Landing Zone 4, which would be the thirty-eighth landing at that site and the six hundred eightieth Falcon booster landing overall. Source: spaceflightnow.com

  8. Commercial Astronauts Share Overview Effect at Boston Museum Panel — Cambridge | Somerville Independent

    Amanda Nguyen, the first Vietnamese woman in space, Sirisha Bandla, the third Indian woman in space, and Sian Leo Proctor, the first Black female spacecraft pilot, spoke at the Museum of Science. They described the overview effect and credited commercial spaceflight with increasing diversity in gender, age and race. The panel was moderated by astronomer Athena Brensberger and included discussion of privately funded missions from Blue Origin, Virgin Galactic and SpaceX. Source: csindie.com

Cosmic Spotlight

A team at the Trottier Institute for Research on Exoplanets discovered an unusual triple-star system just twenty-eight light-years away while they were actually searching for planets. The system, named Welopōʻai, contains two white dwarfs and one red dwarf—an arrangement researchers say should not exist because the white dwarfs would have disrupted the red dwarf during their earlier evolution. The find came from precise radial-velocity measurements that revealed velocity changes far larger than those expected from planets alone. Professor Patrick Dufour noted the configuration challenges current models of how stars interact in close multiples. The discovery offers a nearby laboratory for testing binary and triple-star formation theories. Source: inshorts.com

Cosmic Deep Dive: Closing the MeV Gap

Gamma rays carry the highest energies in the electromagnetic spectrum, yet a wide slice of that range has stayed stubbornly difficult to map. Between roughly twenty and two hundred megaelectronvolts lies the so-called MeV gap, a band where photons are too energetic for conventional X-ray telescopes and too soft for the large-area detectors built for higher-energy gamma rays. Instruments simply could not collect enough clean signals to build reliable sky maps, leaving a blind spot right where many astrophysical processes are expected to shine brightest.

The Fermi Large Area Telescope has now changed that picture. Over fourteen years the instrument recorded enough photons to identify four hundred fifty distinct sources across the twenty-to-two-hundred megaelectronvolt window. The long integration time allowed the team to separate faint celestial signals from instrumental and cosmic-ray background noise that had previously swamped the data. Because the telescope orbits above the atmosphere, it avoids the absorption that blocks these photons from reaching ground-based detectors, giving it a unique vantage on the gap.

Even with this advance, important questions remain open. The catalog shows where the sources are, but astronomers still need to determine what physical mechanisms dominate at these energies and how the sources evolve over time. Follow-up observations with future instruments that can measure polarization or rapid variability in the same band would help pin down whether the emission comes from pulsar magnetospheres, active galactic nuclei, or something entirely unexpected. Until those measurements arrive, the MeV sky stays only partially charted.

New measurements keep rewriting what we thought we knew about the universe.

James Webb a photographié une fusion stellaire dans Andromède, un moteur de fusée lunaire a été mis à feu lors d’un essai, et un Starship capturé prend la direction d’un musée.

La Fermi Large Area Telescope Collaboration, selon Phys point org, a produit la vue la plus nette à ce jour des rayons gamma de basse énergie, entre vingt et deux cents mégaélectronvolts.

Cette tranche du spectre restait un angle mort persistant, parce que les instruments précédents n’étaient pas assez sensibles pour la mesurer de façon fiable. Le catalogue est le premier pont systématique à travers ce que les astronomes appellent le fossé des mégaélectronvolts, et les travaux paraissent dans The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series.

Des astronomes, rapporte ScienceDaily, ont vu une petite étoile s’enrouler en spirale dans une étoile géante dix-huit mille fois plus lumineuse que le Soleil, au sein de la galaxie d’Andromède. La collision a produit une éruption violente, et le James Webb Space Telescope a photographié ces lendemains seulement quarante-sept jours plus tard. Les données apportent un détail inédit sur les processus physiques qui se mettent en marche lorsque deux étoiles fusionnent en une seule.

Un moteur puissant, destiné à la fusée lunaire de l’Administration nationale de l’aéronautique et de l’espace, ou NASA, a achevé un essai à feu de deux cents secondes. La mise à feu a démontré des performances soutenues dans des conditions qui simulent une véritable combustion de mission lunaire. Les ingénieurs vont maintenant analyser les données pour confirmer que le moteur satisfait aux exigences des vols habités.

Le fondateur de Space X, Elon Musk, rapporte Space point com, a déclaré que le premier étage supérieur de Starship saisi par les bras mécaniques de la tour de lancement ne volera plus. Le véhicule sera plutôt conservé pour une exposition publique. Cette décision referme le chapitre des vols pour cet engin, après le succès de la capture.

Isar Aerospace, rapporte Maritime Launch Services, a pris le contrôle opérationnel d’un complexe de lancement dédié à Spaceport Nova Scotia et a commencé à construire son propre pas de tir et ses installations d’assemblage. Le site soutiendra les lancements de Spectrum à partir de deux mille vingt-huit et offre au Canada un deuxième lieu de lancement orbital sur sa côte atlantique.

Le gouvernement canadien a appuyé le projet dans le cadre de ses objectifs de capacité spatiale souveraine.

Chypre a inauguré cette semaine les installations achevées du Cyprus Space Research and Innovation Centre, rapporte Cyprus Mail, y compris une salle de contrôle de mission et un centre d’opérations de météo spatiale qui s’appuie sur l’intelligence artificielle pour prévoir l’activité solaire. Trois satellites Kyrenia trois construits sur place ont été dévoilés au même moment.

L’astronaute de réserve de la Hongrie, Gyula Cserenyi, a assisté aux événements et a évoqué les plans d’un programme Space for Schools qui permettrait aux élèves de recevoir des signaux provenant des satellites. Le centre a été cofinancé par Chypre et l’Union européenne.

Vandenberg Space Force Base s’apprête au décollage de la mission NROL-cent trente-six, rapporte Spaceflight Now, un vol qui ajoutera des satellites à la constellation d’architecture proliférée du National Reconnaissance Office en orbite terrestre basse. Ce vol marque le quatrième lancement de ce type cette année et le quinzième au total. Le décollage depuis Space Launch Complex quatre E est prévu à quatre heures vingt-trois du matin, heure normale du Pacifique.

Le propulseur Falcon neuf B mille quatre-vingt-dix-sept effectuera sa quatorzième mission et visera un atterrissage à Landing Zone quatre, ce qui serait le trente-huitième atterrissage sur ce site et le six cent quatre-vingtième atterrissage d’un propulseur Falcon au total.

Amanda Nguyen, la première femme vietnamienne dans l’espace, Sirisha Bandla, la troisième femme indienne dans l’espace, et Sian Leo Proctor, la première femme noire pilote d’engin spatial, selon le Cambridge Somerville Independent, ont pris la parole au Museum of Science. Elles ont décrit l’effet de vue d’ensemble et ont crédité le vol spatial commercial d’avoir accru la diversité en matière de genre, d’âge et de race.

La table ronde était animée par l’astronome Athena Brensberger et comprenait une discussion sur les missions financées par le privé de Blue Origin, Virgin Galactic et Space X.

En bref, le sénateur Ted Cruz, rapporte The Hill, dit qu’il travaille déjà avec le représentant Joaquin Castro pour amener un programme Space Academy au Texas. Le Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex, rapporte Jalopnik, construit des montagnes russes intérieures baptisées Aero Test Lab, qui ouvriront en deux mille vingt-huit et simuleront des scénarios d’essais en vol de la NASA.

Une équipe de l’Institut Trottier de recherche sur les exoplanètes, rapporte Inshorts, a trouvé un système triple d’étoiles inhabituel à seulement vingt-huit années-lumière, alors qu’elle cherchait des planètes. Le système, nommé Welopōʻai, abrite deux naines blanches et une naine rouge. Les chercheurs affirment que cette configuration ne devrait pas exister, puisque les naines blanches auraient perturbé la naine rouge au fil de leur évolution antérieure.

Des données précises de vitesse radiale ont montré des changements de vitesse bien plus importants que ce que des planètes à elles seules produiraient. Le professeur Patrick Dufour a déclaré que cette disposition met au défi les modèles actuels sur la façon dont les étoiles interagissent dans les systèmes multiples serrés. Le système offre un laboratoire tout proche pour tester comment se forment les étoiles binaires et triples.

Les rayons gamma sont la lumière la plus énergétique du spectre électromagnétique, mais une large tranche de cette gamme s’est révélée obstinément difficile à cartographier. Les photons de cette bande sont trop énergétiques pour les télescopes à rayons X classiques, et trop mous pour les détecteurs de grande surface construits pour les rayons gamma de plus haute énergie.

Les détecteurs ne pouvaient pas rassembler assez de photons propres pour des cartes du ciel fiables, laissant un trou pile à l’endroit où de nombreux processus astrophysiques devraient briller le plus fort.

Une longue intégration a permis à l’équipe de tirer de faibles signaux célestes hors du fond instrumental et des rayons cosmiques qui avaient noyé les données antérieures. Parce que le télescope orbite au-dessus de l’atmosphère, il évite l’absorption qui empêche ces photons d’atteindre le sol.

Le catalogue montre où se trouvent les sources, pas quelle physique domine à ces énergies, ni comment ces sources évoluent avec le temps. De futurs instruments capables de mesurer la polarisation ou la variabilité rapide dans la même bande aideraient à déterminer si l’émission vient des magnétosphères de pulsars, de noyaux actifs de galaxies, ou de quelque chose d’entièrement inattendu.

En attendant ces mesures, ce ciel reste seulement en partie cartographié.

Pour revenir sur les sept derniers jours, la NASA a annoncé que les premières images du Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope arriveront le sept décembre. Des chercheurs de l’université de Sydney ont découvert que la barre centrale de la Voie lactée déclenche de nouvelles étoiles le long de ses fronts de choc. Sept engins spatiaux s’apprêtent à suivre comment l’énergie du Soleil se déplace à travers le bouclier magnétique de la Terre.

Туристический комплекс Космического центра Кеннеди, сообщает Джалопник, строит крытые американские горки под названием Аэро Тест Лаб, которые откроются в две тысячи двадцать восьмом году и будут имитировать сценарии лётных испытаний НАСА.

Команда Института Тротье по исследованию экзопланет, сообщает Иншортс, обнаружила необычную тройную звёздную систему всего в двадцати восьми световых годах, пока искала планеты. Исследователи говорят, что такая конфигурация не должна существовать, поскольку белые карлики должны были возмутить красного карлика, когда эволюционировали раньше.

Точные данные по лучевым скоростям показали изменения скорости куда больше тех, что могли бы дать одни лишь планеты. Профессор Патрик Дюфур сказал, что такая компоновка бросает вызов нынешним моделям того, как звёзды взаимодействуют в тесных кратных системах. Система даёт близкую лабораторию для проверки того, как формируются двойные и тройные звёзды.

Гамма-лучи — это свет с самой высокой энергией в электромагнитном спектре, но широкий участок этого диапазона упрямо трудно картографировать. Фотоны в этой полосе слишком энергичны для обычных рентгеновских телескопов и слишком мягки для детекторов большой площади, построенных для гамма-лучей более высокой энергии.

Детекторы не могли собрать достаточно чистых фотонов для надёжных карт неба, оставляя дыру как раз там, где многие астрофизические процессы должны светить ярче всего.

Длительное накопление позволило команде вытянуть слабые небесные сигналы из инструментального фона и фона космических лучей, которые затапливали более ранние данные. Поскольку телескоп обращается выше атмосферы, он избегает поглощения, которое не даёт этим фотонам достичь земли.

Будущие приборы, которые смогут измерять поляризацию или быструю переменность в том же диапазоне, помогут установить, идёт ли излучение от магнитосфер пульсаров, активных ядер галактик или от чего-то совершенно неожиданного.

Fermi’s fourteen-year sky map has now catalogued four hundred fifty sources in the hard-to-reach MeV gamma-ray band. This is Fascinating Frontiers, episode 261. James Webb imaged a stellar merger in Andromeda, a Moon-rocket engine was test-fired, and a caught Starship is headed for a museum. The Fermi Large Area Telescope Collaboration, according to Phys.org, has produced the clearest view yet of low-energy gamma rays between twenty and two hundred megaelectronvolts. That slice of the spectrum had stayed a persistent blind spot, because earlier instruments were not sensitive enough to measure it reliably. The catalog is the first systematic bridge across what astronomers call the MeV gap, and the work appears in The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series. Astronomers, ScienceDaily reports, watched a small star spiral into a giant star eighteen thousand times brighter than the Sun inside the Andromeda galaxy. The collision produced a violent eruption, and the James Webb Space Telescope imaged that aftermath just forty-seven days later. The data give fresh detail on the physical processes that run when two stars merge into one. A powerful engine intended for the Moon rocket of the National Aeronautics and Space Administration, or NASA, completed a two-hundred-second hot-fire test. The firing demonstrated sustained performance under conditions that simulate an actual lunar mission burn. Engineers will now analyze the data to confirm the engine meets requirements for crewed flights. SpaceX founder Elon Musk, Space.com reports, said the first Starship upper stage grabbed by the launch tower’s mechanical arms will not fly again. The vehicle will instead be preserved for public display. That decision closes the flight chapter for that craft after the catch succeeded. Isar Aerospace, Maritime Launch Services reports, has taken operational control of a dedicated launch complex at Spaceport Nova Scotia and started building its own pad and assembly facilities. The site will support Spectrum launches beginning in twenty twenty-eight and gives Canada a second orbital launch location on its Atlantic coast. The Canadian government has backed the project as part of its sovereign space capability goals. Cyprus opened the completed facilities of the Cyprus Space Research and Innovation Centre this week, Cyprus Mail reports, including a mission control room and a space weather operations centre that uses artificial intelligence to forecast solar activity. Three locally built Kyrenia3 satellites were unveiled at the same time. Hungary’s backup astronaut Gyula Cserenyi attended the events and discussed plans for a Space for Schools programme that would let students receive signals from the satellites. The centre was co-funded by Cyprus and the European Union. Vandenberg Space Force Base is set for liftoff of the NROL-136 mission, Spaceflight Now reports, a flight that will add satellites to the National Reconnaissance Office’s proliferated architecture constellation in low Earth orbit. The flight marks the fourth such launch this year and the fifteenth overall. Liftoff from Space Launch Complex four E is scheduled for four twenty-three a.m. PST. Falcon nine booster B1097 will fly its fourteenth mission and target a landing at Landing Zone four, which would be the thirty-eighth landing at that site and the six hundred eightieth Falcon booster landing overall. Amanda Nguyen, the first Vietnamese woman in space, Sirisha Bandla, the third Indian woman in space, and Sian Leo Proctor, the first Black female spacecraft pilot, according to the Cambridge Somerville Independent, spoke at the Museum of Science. They described the overview effect and credited commercial spaceflight with increasing diversity in gender, age and race. The panel was moderated by astronomer Athena Brensberger and included discussion of privately funded missions from Blue Origin, Virgin Galactic and SpaceX. In brief, Senator Ted Cruz, The Hill reports, says he is already working with Representative Joaquin Castro to bring a Space Academy programme to Texas. Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex, Jalopnik reports, is building an indoor roller coaster called Aero Test Lab that will open in twenty twenty-eight and simulate NASA flight-test scenarios. A team at the Trottier Institute for Research on Exoplanets, Inshorts reports, found an unusual triple-star system just twenty-eight light-years away while searching for planets. The system, named Welopōʻai, holds two white dwarfs and one red dwarf. Researchers say that arrangement should not exist, since the white dwarfs would have disturbed the red dwarf as they evolved earlier. Precise radial-velocity data showed speed changes far larger than planets alone would produce. Professor Patrick Dufour said the layout challenges current models of how stars interact in close multiples. The system gives a nearby laboratory for testing how binary and triple stars form. Gamma rays are the highest-energy light in the electromagnetic spectrum, but a wide slice of that range has been stubbornly hard to chart. Photons in that band are too energetic for conventional X-ray telescopes and too soft for the large-area detectors built for higher-energy gamma rays. Detectors could not gather enough clean photons for reliable sky maps, leaving a hole right where many astrophysical processes should shine brightest. A long integration let the team pull faint celestial signals out of instrumental and cosmic-ray background that had swamped earlier data. Because the telescope orbits above the atmosphere, it avoids the absorption that blocks these photons from reaching the ground. The catalog shows where the sources sit, not which physics dominates at these energies or how those sources change with time. Future instruments that can measure polarization or rapid variability in the same band would help pin down whether the emission comes from pulsar magnetospheres, active galactic nuclei, or something entirely unexpected. Until those measurements arrive, that sky stays only partly charted. University of Sydney researchers found that the Milky Way’s central bar is sparking new stars along its shock fronts.
