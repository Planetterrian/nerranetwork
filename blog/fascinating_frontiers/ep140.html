Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories

  1. ESA Invites Public to View Total Solar Eclipse in León — European Space Agency

    2. The European Space Agency, the City of León and the University of León are organizing public events around the total solar eclipse. Attendees will hear talks on the physics of the Sun’s corona and the alignment geometry that produces totality. The program pairs ground observations with data from ESA’s solar missions to compare real-time images with spacecraft views. Participants can also join workshops on safe eclipse photography and the historical role of eclipses in testing general relativity. The event runs on the day of the eclipse with viewing sites chosen for clear horizons. Source: esa.int

  2. NASA Astronaut Chris Williams Set to Return After 241 Days — NASA

    3. NASA astronaut Chris Williams is scheduled to land on July 26 after completing 241 days aboard the International Space Station. Coverage will include live views from the Soyuz spacecraft during re-entry and post-landing medical checks. This marks the end of Williams’ first long-duration mission. Ground teams in Kazakhstan and Houston will coordinate the handover of station operations to the remaining crew. The return brings the total number of days Williams has spent in space to 241. Source: nasa.gov

  3. Moon-Orbiting Tree Seeds Now Face Endangered Status on Earth — AJC.com

    4. Seeds that circled the Moon aboard a spacecraft were later planted in Georgia and have grown into trees now listed as endangered. The project tracked how microgravity and radiation affected germination rates compared with control seeds kept on the ground. Researchers are studying whether the space-exposed trees show genetic changes that could help conservation efforts. The trees belong to a species already under pressure from habitat loss and disease. Follow-up studies will monitor growth patterns over the next decade. Source: Google News

  4. Mercury Ends Retrograde Motion on July 23 — Astronomy Magazine

    5. Mercury reaches its stationary point at 1 P.M. EDT while in Gemini and begins direct motion. The planet remains visible low in the eastern sky before sunrise for the next several weeks. Observers can use the date to note the shift from its earlier westward drift against background stars. The event coincides with Dione passing north of Saturn as seen from Earth. Daily sky charts show Mercury’s changing position relative to Castor and Pollux. Source: astronomy.com

  5. Cyprus Study Identifies Genes Altered by Spaceflight — Cyprus Mail

    6. Researchers in Cyprus have pinpointed specific genes whose expression changes during extended time in orbit. The work analyzed blood and tissue samples from astronauts before, during and after missions. Results point to pathways involved in immune response and bone metabolism that activate differently in microgravity. The team plans to compare these markers with data from future commercial crew flights. The findings may help design targeted countermeasures for longer missions. Source: Google News

  6. NASA to Test SpaceX and Blue Origin Lunar Landers — ScienceDaily

    7. NASA will conduct integrated flight tests of the SpaceX Human Landing System and Blue Origin’s Mk1 lander before the next crewed lunar landing. The tests will evaluate propulsion, navigation and docking systems in Earth orbit. Data from these flights will inform certification for Artemis missions. Engineers will compare performance against requirements for crew safety and payload delivery. The schedule calls for the tests to occur ahead of the next planned lunar landing attempt. Source: Google News

  7. Webb Telescope Finds Hidden Giant Planet in Beta Pictoris — ScienceDaily

    8. NASA’s James Webb Space Telescope has detected a giant planet orbiting within the debris disk of Beta Pictoris. The planet was identified through direct imaging after earlier data sets were re-examined with improved calibration. Its presence helps explain gaps and structures previously seen in the disk by other telescopes. The discovery adds a third giant planet to the system, which is only about 20 million years old. Follow-up observations will measure the planet’s orbit and atmospheric composition. Source: Google News

  8. Rocky Exoplanet Found with Detectable Atmosphere — Caliber.Az

    9. Astronomers have confirmed an atmosphere on a rocky exoplanet located in the habitable zone of its star. Transmission spectroscopy during transits revealed absorption features consistent with a substantial gaseous envelope. The planet’s size and density suggest a composition similar to Earth or Venus. The detection relied on multiple transit observations to separate the atmospheric signal from stellar variability. Additional spectra are planned to identify specific molecules. Source: Google News

  9. NASA Seeks Volunteers for Yearlong Virtual Moon and Mars Mission — bgr.com

    10. NASA is recruiting volunteers to participate in a simulated year-long mission to the Moon and Mars conducted entirely on the ground. Participants will live in isolation and follow schedules that mimic communication delays and daily operations. The study will collect data on team dynamics, psychological resilience and performance under constrained conditions. Results will inform crew selection and habitat design for future deep-space missions. Applications are open through the agency’s human research portal. Source: Google News RHNIbHhjZEg5QUdlSmdvZ0tf?oc=5

  10. Possible First Exosatellite Detected Beyond Solar System — Evrim Ağacı

    11. Astronomers report evidence for a satellite orbiting an exoplanet outside our solar system. The candidate was identified through timing variations in the planet’s transit and subtle changes in the host star’s light. If confirmed, the object would be the first known exosatellite and would expand the range of objects that can form and remain stable in other systems. Further observations with larger telescopes are required to verify the signal. The detection method combines transit photometry with dynamical modeling. Source: Google News

  11. Planet with Earth-like Atmosphere Reported — Mashable

    12. A newly characterized exoplanet shows atmospheric properties resembling those of Earth in transmission spectra. The data indicate a clear atmosphere with detectable molecular features rather than a thick haze layer. The planet orbits a Sun-like star at a distance that places it near the inner edge of the habitable zone. Confirmation came from repeated observations that ruled out stellar activity as the source of the signal. Researchers will next attempt to measure the planet’s mass and density more precisely. Source: Google News

  12. SpaceX Launch Carries Northrop Grumman Rescue Robot — WyomingNews.com

    13. A recent SpaceX launch delivered Northrop Grumman’s two-handed robotic system designed for satellite servicing and rescue operations. The robot will demonstrate capture and repair techniques on defunct spacecraft. Mission planners expect the demonstration to validate robotic arms and sensors for future on-orbit assembly tasks. The payload shares the ride with other commercial satellites. Data from the mission will feed into designs for larger servicing vehicles. Source: Google News

  13. Raspberry Sugar Molecule Detected in Deep Space for First Time — AOL.com

    14. Astronomers have identified a sugar molecule previously found in raspberries within a star-forming region. The detection used radio telescopes tuned to the molecule’s characteristic emission lines. Its presence suggests that complex organic chemistry can occur even in cold, dense clouds before planets form. The finding adds to the inventory of prebiotic molecules available during planet formation. Further surveys will search for related compounds in the same cloud. Source: Google News

  14. New Horizons Awakens for Science Beyond Pluto — Live Science

    15. NASA’s New Horizons spacecraft has ended a year-long hibernation to begin new observations in the outer solar system. The craft will study distant Kuiper Belt objects and measure the heliosphere’s edge. Instruments will collect data on cosmic rays and dust particles at distances never before sampled. Mission controllers have confirmed all systems are operating normally after the long sleep. The new phase extends the spacecraft’s exploration well past its original Pluto encounter. Source: Google News

  15. China’s Shijian-31 Satellite Uses Novel Orbit to Monitor GEO Belt — SpaceNews

    16. China’s Shijian-31 satellite, launched in June, operates in a specially designed orbit that allows repeated passes over the geostationary belt. The unique trajectory provides extended viewing time of GEO satellites from varying angles. Analysts note the orbit enables continuous monitoring without remaining fixed in one location. The spacecraft carries sensors suited for tracking objects in the crowded GEO region. The mission supports China’s space situational awareness efforts. Source: spacenews.com

Cosmic Spotlight

A candidate exomoon roughly the size of Jupiter has been inferred from transit timing variations and subtle brightness changes around a distant planet. The detection pushes the boundary of what astronomers traditionally classify as a moon versus a free-floating planet. Confirmation will require additional observations to distinguish the signal from other dynamical effects. If verified, the object would be the first known exosatellite outside our solar system. How many more such borderline worlds might current surveys be missing?

Cosmic Deep Dive: The Universe’s Extreme Range of Scales

If you shrank the observable universe down to the size of Earth, an atom would be smaller than a single proton in that model—yet both extremes are described by the same laws of physics. Traveling inward from a galaxy cluster, you cross 100 million light-years, then 100,000 light-years inside a typical galaxy, then a few light-years between stars, then the 1.4-million-kilometer width of the Sun, then the 1.4-million-kilometer journey shrinks to the 1.4-meter height of a human, then to the 0.1-nanometer width of an atom, and finally to the 10^-15-meter scale of its nucleus. At each step the dominant forces switch: gravity yields to electromagnetism, then to the strong force, and the mathematics that works at one level fails at the next. A single number captures the gulf: the ratio between the Planck length and the diameter of the observable universe is roughly 10^61. We can measure both ends with instruments, yet no single theory yet bridges them without contradictions or infinities. Why does nature allow such an unbroken hierarchy of scales while hiding a consistent description that covers all of them at once?

New data on exomoons, atmospheres, and outer-solar-system missions keep adding concrete pieces to long-running questions.
