    7. Ukrainian polar researchers examined chemical pollutants in Antarctic waters. The study focused on environmental contaminants in remote marine environments. Findings contribute to understanding pollution pathways in polar regions. Water samples were collected from multiple sites around research stations during the austral summer. Analysis targeted persistent organic pollutants and heavy metals. Source: universemagazine.com

  8. NASA administrator addresses proposed budget cuts in Tucson — tucson.com

  9. Low cloud layer blankets Northwest coast — NASA Science

    10. A low-lying cloud layer created autumn conditions over western Washington and Oregon. Mountainous coastal areas remained above the cloud deck. The image highlights regional atmospheric patterns. Satellite observations captured the sharp boundary between the marine layer and higher terrain. The scene illustrates typical fall weather dynamics along the Pacific Northwest. Source: science.nasa.gov

  10. New lunar crater reveals shallow subsurface details — Universe Today

    11. Asteroid impacts on the Moon create craters that expose the shallow subsurface. Scientists use these fresh craters to study lunar material properties. The observations provide direct access to layers normally hidden from view. Recent imaging shows ejecta patterns that trace subsurface layering. The data help refine models of regolith depth and composition. Source: universetoday.com

  11. NASA works to modernize commercial airline systems — NASA

    12. NASA researchers are updating systems used by commercial airlines. The work targets smoother takeoffs, descents, and reduced delays. Projects focus on air traffic management improvements. New algorithms aim to optimize routing and spacing between aircraft. Test flights have demonstrated reduced fuel burn during approach phases. Source: nasa.gov

  12. ISS crew conducts health technology studies — NASA Science

    13. Expedition 75 crew performed ultrasound scans with augmented reality and artificial intelligence. Blood and saliva samples were collected to study immune system changes in weightlessness. The crew is also preparing for an upcoming crew swap. Augmented reality overlays guided precise probe placement during the scans. Sample processing occurred in the station's glovebox facility. Source: nasa.gov

  13. NASA aircraft to fly low over Colorado farmland — NASA

    14. Aircraft will conduct low-altitude research flights over Colorado agricultural areas. The FarmFlux mission deploys sensors to measure ozone, methane, ammonia, and particulates. Data will examine interactions between agricultural emissions and the atmosphere. Multiple sensor suites will operate simultaneously during each sortie. Flights are scheduled to capture seasonal variations in emission rates. Source: nasa.gov

  14. Habitable Worlds Observatory precursor studies seek reviewers — NASA Science

    15. NASA is recruiting virtual panelists and external reviewers for Habitable Worlds Observatory precursor science investigations. Participation does not commit reviewers to serve on proposals. The call supports early planning for the future observatory. Reviewers will evaluate proposals focused on exoplanet characterization techniques. The process runs through established NASA research announcement channels. Source: science.nasa.gov

  15. Kelsey Young receives Eugene Shoemaker Lecture award — NASA Science

    16. Kelsey Young, Artemis II lunar science lead at NASA Goddard, receives the 2026 Eugene Shoemaker Lecture award. The award recognizes excellence in planetary exploration. Young focuses on integrating science into human spaceflight. The lecture will highlight lessons from previous lunar missions applied to Artemis planning. Selection criteria emphasize both research impact and mission support contributions. Source: science.nasa.gov

Engineers will now verify subsystems before moving to environmental testing and launch preparations. The mission timeline remains tight because Apophis arrives in 2029 regardless of delays. This first activation confirms the spacecraft bus responds to commands as expected. The next major milestone will be full functional testing of the science instruments. Cosmic Deep Dive: How Black Hole Jets Heat Galaxy Gas

A single supermassive black hole can drive jets that extend tens of thousands of light-years, far beyond the scale of an entire galaxy disk. If you could ride one of those jets outward from the galactic center, you would first encounter gas heated to millions of degrees by shock waves, then see that material cool and form new structures along the jet path. In Messier 106 the jets have created two extra arms of glowing gas that sit at right angles to the normal spiral arms. Chandra X-ray observations show these arms glow in royal blue because the gas reaches temperatures hot enough to emit X-rays. The jets themselves move at a substantial fraction of the speed of light, carrying energy equivalent to the output of millions of ordinary stars. Yet astronomers still cannot explain exactly why some black holes launch steady, powerful jets while others remain quiet despite similar masses and accretion rates. We can map the jets and measure the heated gas, but the trigger that decides whether a jet forms remains unknown.
