Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories

  1. Total Solar Eclipse Due Across Northern Spain in August — Phys.org

    2. The Moon will completely cover the Sun on August 12, briefly turning day into night along a path through northern Spain. Observers in the zone will see the solar corona and planets that are normally hidden by daylight. The event is the first total solar eclipse visible from mainland Spain in decades. Local interest has surged, with rural residents who once watched the night sky alone now part of widespread public excitement. Follow-up coverage will track viewing conditions and any last-minute weather forecasts closer to the date. Source: phys.org

  2. Solar Eclipse Fever Sweeps Rural Spain — Phys.org

    3. Enrique Bordallo, who grew up stargazing in the Spanish countryside, now sees his childhood passion shared by thousands preparing for next month’s eclipse. Towns along the path of totality report packed accommodations and organized viewing events. The eclipse has turned a once-quiet astronomical rarity into a national talking point. Organizers expect clear skies to draw both locals and international visitors. Updates on crowd logistics and safety measures will follow in coming weeks. Source: phys.org

  3. Asteroid 18 Melpomene Reaches Opposition — Astronomy Magazine

    4. The 9th-magnitude main-belt asteroid will be visible all night on July 8 in southeastern Aquila near the border with Scutum. Opposition places it opposite the Sun in Earth’s sky, maximizing its brightness for the year. Observers can track its motion against background stars throughout the night. The asteroid is roughly 150 kilometres across and has been studied for decades. Amateur astronomers are encouraged to attempt imaging or visual observations while it remains well placed. Source: astronomy.com

  4. Space Force Adds Relativity and Impulse Space to Launch Program — SpaceNews

    5. The U.S. Space Force has included Relativity Space and Impulse Space in the National Security Space Launch Phase 3 Lane 1 program. The move opens military launch opportunities to newer companies beyond traditional providers. Both firms will now compete for future national security payloads. The decision reflects an effort to broaden the industrial base for assured access to space. Next steps include contract negotiations and mission assignments. Source: spacenews.com

  5. Extragalactic Supernova Remnant Imaged in Large Magellanic Cloud — Astronomy Magazine

    6. LHA 120-N 40 appears as a glowing shell of debris from a star that exploded in the Large Magellanic Cloud, 163,000 light-years away in Dorado. The blue outer shell marks material expelled by the supernova, while reddish patches show ongoing emission from smaller regions. The image was captured from Rio Hurtado, Chile. Such remnants help astronomers study how heavy elements are distributed into surrounding galaxies. Additional multi-wavelength observations could reveal more about the progenitor star. Source: astronomy.com

  6. New Horizons Emerges from Record Hibernation — Phys.org

    7. NASA’s New Horizons spacecraft has completed its longest hibernation period of nearly a year and is now transmitting data collected in the distant Kuiper Belt. The spacecraft remains in good health after waking and is ready to resume science operations far beyond Pluto. Mission teams will begin processing the new observations in the coming days. This marks another step in the extended mission studying the outer solar system’s small bodies. Future data releases will focus on objects encountered during the hibernation window. Source: phys.org

  7. Skyroot Prepares for First Orbital Launch Attempt — SpaceNews

    8. Indian startup Skyroot Aerospace aims to conduct its first orbital launch attempt as soon as July 12. The company plans to scale quickly toward monthly launches once initial success is achieved. The vehicle will carry a small satellite payload on this debut flight. Ground teams are completing final checks ahead of the window. Success would mark India’s first private orbital launch. Source: spacenews.com

  8. D-Orbit to Launch Satellites for ArkEdge Space — SpaceNews

    9. Italian firm D-Orbit has signed a contract to provide multiple launches for Japanese startup ArkEdge Space using its ION Satellite Carrier. The agreement covers a series of missions beginning later this year. ArkEdge plans to deploy small satellites for communications and technology demonstrations. D-Orbit’s carrier will handle deployment and initial orbit adjustments. The deal expands European launch services for Asian customers. Source: spacenews.com

  9. Centaurus A Shows Subtle Signs of Past Merger — Phys.org

    10. The bright galaxy Centaurus A, 11 million light-years away, continues forming stars rapidly despite an ancient collision that is not immediately obvious in visible light. The galaxy ranks as the fifth-brightest in the sky and has been observed extensively by both professionals and amateurs. Its warped disk and active nucleus hint at the merger that occurred billions of years ago. Radio and infrared data reveal structures left by the event. Further studies will examine how the collision continues to influence star formation. Source: phys.org

  10. Milky Way May Be Larger Than Current Estimates — Space.com

    11. Astronomers used observations of cosmic explosions to suggest the Milky Way’s true size exceeds previous measurements. The technique relies on light from distant events passing through galactic material. Revised dimensions would affect models of the galaxy’s mass and rotation. The finding challenges assumptions built from earlier surveys. Additional data from upcoming telescopes will test the new estimate. Source: space.com

  11. Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope Expected to Find 100,000 Exoplanets — Space Daily

    12. NASA’s Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, scheduled for launch in August 2026, is projected to discover around 100,000 exoplanets during its mission. That total would surpass the combined yield of every previous telescope. The observatory will use wide-field imaging and microlensing to detect planets across a broad range of distances. Mission planners anticipate the largest statistical sample of exoplanets yet assembled. Early data releases after launch will begin validating the prediction. Source: Google News

  12. Apolink Establishes Contact with First Relay Satellite — SpaceNews

    13. Apolink has successfully contacted its initial satellite after a July 7 launch on a SpaceX rideshare mission. The contact clears the path for a data-relay demonstration under an experimental Federal Communications Commission license. Engineers will now test the satellite’s relay capabilities in orbit. The mission aims to prove low-latency data forwarding between spacecraft. Follow-on tests are planned once the demonstration phase begins. Source: spacenews.com

  13. Ferris State Graduate Joins Blue Origin — Ferris State University

    14. Recent Ferris State University graduate Francisco Vasquez has started a role at Blue Origin focused on space exploration hardware. The position places him in the company’s engineering teams working on crewed vehicles. Vasquez’s academic background prepared him for systems integration and testing tasks. His hiring reflects growing pathways from university programs into commercial spaceflight. Future updates may highlight project assignments tied to upcoming launches. Source: Google News

  14. NASA Tracks Cottonwood Fire in Utah from Orbit — NASA

    15. The Cottonwood Fire burned more than 150 square miles and affected parts of a Utah ski resort. Satellite imagery captured the fire’s extent and burn scar for damage assessment. Firefighters used the data alongside ground reports to monitor spread. The event highlights how Earth-observing satellites support wildfire response. Additional images will track vegetation recovery in the affected area. Source: science.nasa.gov

  15. PACE Mission Validates Ocean Data Products — NASA

    16. NASA’s Plankton, Aerosol, Cloud, ocean Ecosystem satellite is undergoing validation of its science data products through coordinated field campaigns. Teams compare satellite measurements with ship and buoy observations to refine ocean color and atmospheric retrievals. The effort supports studies of marine ecosystems and climate interactions. Early results show good agreement between space and surface data. Further campaigns will expand geographic coverage. Source: science.nasa.gov

Cosmic Deep Dive: How Galaxy Clusters Assemble Through Repeated Collisions

If the Milky Way sat at the center of one of these forming clusters, the nearest neighboring sub-cluster would lie more than a million light-years away—roughly ten times the distance to Andromeda. Two massive elliptical galaxies anchor each sub-cluster like gravitational anchors, pulling smaller galaxies into orbit while the whole system hurtles through space. When the sub-clusters slam together, their galaxies pass through one another with relatively few direct collisions because the spaces between stars are so vast; instead, the dark-matter halos and hot gas interact, slowing the motion and allowing eventual merger. The entire process can repeat several times before the final cluster settles. We still do not know exactly how much of the final cluster’s mass ends up locked in the central giant ellipticals versus remaining in the diffuse intracluster light.

New discoveries keep rewriting what we thought we knew about how the universe builds its largest structures.
