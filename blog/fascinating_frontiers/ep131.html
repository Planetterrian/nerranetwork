Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories

  1. NASA Picks Four Missions for Permanent Lunar Base — ScienceDaily

    2. The selections focus on technologies and infrastructure needed to support sustained human presence on the Moon. These missions build directly on Artemis program goals for crewed landings and Gateway readiness by developing habitats, power systems, and resource utilization hardware. NASA will assign the projects to teams that must demonstrate key capabilities before full-scale deployment. The effort addresses open questions around landing cadence and long-term surface operations that have persisted since earlier Artemis planning updates. This selection advances the Artemis architecture by moving specific hardware development into funded phases that target reliable multi-month operations. Source: Google News

  2. Perseverance Rover Completes Marathon Distance on Mars — ScienceDaily

    3. The rover has now driven the equivalent of a full 42-kilometre marathon across Jezero Crater and surrounding terrain. Its instruments continue to collect rock and soil samples for eventual return to Earth. Mission teams use the distance milestone to refine navigation and energy management strategies for future traverses. This progress keeps the Mars Sample Return pathway on track by expanding the set of cached materials. Robotic Exploration teams note that the accumulated traverse distance directly supports sample diversity goals established in prior mission reviews. Source: Google News

  3. Arctic River Fans Show Sediment Patterns from Ice Melt — NASA Science

    4. Eroded material from ice-capped mountains on Russia’s Severny Island spreads across a broad valley in striking fan shapes. The image captures how seasonal meltwater redistributes sediment in real time. NASA’s Earth Observatory uses these observations to track changes in polar hydrology. Follow-up imagery will monitor how the fans evolve through the remaining summer season. The patterns provide a visible record of how warming temperatures alter sediment transport in high-latitude river systems. Source: science.nasa.gov

  4. SunRISE Mission Switches to Falcon Heavy Launcher — NASA Science

    5. The heliophysics mission will now ride a SpaceX Falcon Heavy instead of the originally planned Vulcan Centaur. SunRISE consists of six CubeSats that will form a radio interferometer to study solar radio bursts. The change keeps the project on schedule for its science objectives while NASA updates the exact launch timing. The spacecraft will operate in a near-Earth orbit to triangulate emissions from the Sun. This launcher adjustment maintains the mission timeline for mapping solar radio phenomena at multiple frequencies. Source: science.nasa.gov

  5. Curiosity Examines Transition to Rougher Bedrock — NASA Science

    6. The rover spent sols 4941-4947 approaching a geologic boundary between smooth sandy terrain and rougher bedrock on Mount Sharp. Its cameras captured fine stripes and polygonal patterns in the transition zone. Science teams are using the data to understand how ancient water flows shaped these layers. The next planning cycle will target specific bedrock targets for closer inspection. The observations add detail to the stratigraphic record Curiosity has built while ascending the slopes of Mount Sharp. Source: science.nasa.gov

  6. Lab Device Mimics Rotating Black Hole Wave Effects — Universe Today

    7. Researchers at the CUNY Advanced Science Research Center built a radio-frequency device whose properties vary in space and time to simulate a spinning body. The setup demonstrated wave amplification without any physical rotation of matter. This approach tests predictions about energy extraction near rotating black holes in a controlled environment. Further experiments will explore different modulation patterns to map the full range of amplification conditions. The laboratory result provides a new route for studying ergosphere physics without requiring astrophysical-scale masses. Source: universetoday.com

  7. Four Nearby White Dwarf Binaries Found Within 65 Light-Years — Phys.org Space

    8. Astronomers at the University of Warwick and University of Colorado Boulder directly imaged the systems for the first time. One pair contains the ninth-closest white dwarf to the Sun. The discoveries relied on high-resolution imaging and radial velocity measurements to confirm the companions. Continued monitoring will reveal orbital periods and masses that refine models of binary evolution in the solar neighborhood. These nearby systems offer the first direct views of white-dwarf binaries at distances small enough for detailed follow-up. Source: phys.org

  8. DART Spacecraft Software Updated 140 Million Kilometres Away — Universe Today

    9. Engineers completed two full reboots of the spacecraft with an eight-minute light-time delay on commands. The upgrade prepares the probe for a close flyby of the asteroid it previously impacted. Data from the encounter will answer lingering questions about the exact outcome of the 2022 collision. The next observation window opens once the spacecraft reaches its new target geometry. Robotic Exploration continues to extract new measurements from the spacecraft that altered Dimorphos’s orbit. Source: universetoday.com

  9. Sub-Neptunes May Host Vaporized-Rock Clouds — Space.com

    10. Models show these planets could maintain atmospheres dense enough to turn surface rock into vapor that condenses into high-altitude clouds. The process would trap heat and sustain global magma oceans beneath. JWST transmission spectra of several targets already hint at the unusual chemistry required. Future observations will test whether specific absorption features match the predicted rock-vapor signatures. This class of worlds expands the range of atmospheric compositions JWST can characterize in the sub-Neptune regime. Source: space.com

  10. Soyuz to Carry NASA Astronaut and Two Cosmonauts to ISS — Space.com

    11. NASA astronaut Anil Menon and two Russian cosmonauts are scheduled for liftoff on July 14 aboard Soyuz MS-29. The flight will deliver the crew to the International Space Station for a standard six-month expedition. Coverage begins at 9:45 a.m. ET with live views from the launch site. This mission continues the long-standing crew rotation partnership between the agencies. Commercial Spaceflight operations rely on these Soyuz flights to maintain continuous station occupancy while new commercial vehicles mature. Source: space.com

  11. Interstellar Comet Carries Chemical Fingerprints from Another Star — Universe Today

    12. The object passed near Earth on October 30, 2025, after ejection from its home system long ago. Spectroscopic analysis revealed abundance patterns that differ from Solar System comets. Researchers traced the chemistry back to an aging star that lost the comet through gravitational scattering. Additional telescope time will search for more fragments or related objects on similar trajectories. The comet’s composition offers a direct sample of material formed around a distant stellar system. Source: universetoday.com

  12. Space Cargo Costs Decline Faster Than 1800s Steamship Rates — r/space

    13. Historical comparisons show launch prices per kilogram have dropped more steeply than equivalent freight costs during the steamship era. The next data points will come from operational flights of next-generation vehicles later this year. Commercial Spaceflight cost reductions directly affect the economics of constellation deployment and station resupply. Source: reddit.com

  13. NASA Tests Air Taxi Comfort Factors for Passenger Acceptance — NASA Science

    14. Researchers flew instrumented vertical-takeoff aircraft to measure motion and vibration levels that affect rider comfort. The study links specific ride qualities to willingness to use air taxi services for short urban trips. Results will guide vehicle design and flight-path planning for the emerging industry. Follow-on work will expand the dataset with passenger surveys during actual operations. The measurements provide quantitative guidance for operators seeking to increase public adoption of urban air mobility. Source: nasa.gov

  14. Roman Workshop Explores Shifting Astrophysics Frontiers — NASA Science

    15. The July 13-16 event focuses on new science opportunities opened by the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope. Sessions cover wide-field surveys, exoplanet microlensing, and supernova cosmology. Participants include mission scientists and guest investigators preparing early data analysis plans. Outcomes will shape the first observing programs once the telescope reaches its operational orbit. Cosmology & Astrophysics teams are using the workshop to align early Roman data with ongoing Hubble-tension investigations. Source: science.nasa.gov

Cosmic Spotlight

NASA’s selection of four new lunar missions adds concrete hardware development steps to the Artemis architecture. The projects target power, resource extraction, and habitat systems that must work reliably for months at a time. Success here would directly reduce risk for the first crewed landing and subsequent surface stays. The open question is which of the four will deliver flight hardware first and how that timeline aligns with Gateway module deliveries already in production. Artemis teams now have defined technology paths that address long-standing surface operations challenges.

Cosmic Deep Dive: How Laboratory Devices Simulate Black Hole Rotation

If the Sun were compressed into a sphere the size of a city, its surface gravity would still be modest compared with a stellar-mass black hole whose event horizon spans only tens of kilometres yet contains several times the Sun’s mass. In the new experiment, researchers created a radio-frequency structure whose electromagnetic properties change continuously in both space and time, reproducing the frame-dragging effect that occurs outside a rotating black hole without any moving parts. Photons passing through the device experience an energy boost whose magnitude depends on the exact pattern of modulation, reaching amplification factors that match analytic predictions for orbits skimming the ergosphere. The setup operates at centimetre wavelengths and completes a full spatial-temporal cycle in microseconds, allowing thousands of trials per second. The remaining puzzle is whether every detail of the energy extraction process scales exactly the same way when the “rotation” is produced by material motion instead of engineered modulation.

New lunar hardware and a distant spacecraft reboot both keep long-running programs moving forward.
