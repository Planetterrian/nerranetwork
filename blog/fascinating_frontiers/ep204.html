Côte d'Ivoire becomes the seventy fifth nation to sign the Artemis Accords, expanding international commitments for lunar operations.

Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories

  1. Côte d'Ivoire Joins Artemis Accords as 75th Signatory — NASA

    2. Côte d'Ivoire signed the Artemis Accords. The country became the seventy fifth nation to join the agreement. Officials committed to principles for safe, peaceful, and prosperous space activities. NASA issued the formal announcement of the signing. Signatories agree to common standards for exploration on the Moon and beyond. The step adds another partner to coordinated lunar efforts. Source: nasa.gov

  2. Dragon Arrives at Pad 40 Ahead of Crew-13 Launch — SpaceX

    3. A Dragon spacecraft reached pad 40 at Cape Canaveral. The arrival prepares the vehicle for the Crew-13 mission. Four astronauts will travel to the International Space Station on a long-duration expedition. SpaceX released imagery showing the spacecraft at the pad. The company confirmed the upcoming launch target remains on schedule. Source: spacex.com

  3. Launch Rehearsal Complete Ahead of Starship Flight 14 — SpaceX

    4. SpaceX completed a full launch rehearsal for Starship Flight 14 at Starbase. The test sequence verified vehicle systems and ground operations. Engineers checked every step required for the next integrated flight. The company shared video of the rehearsal activities. The milestone moves the vehicle closer to its planned launch window. Source: x.com

  4. NASA Astronaut Victor Glover Signs With CAA — The Hollywood Reporter

    5. NASA astronaut Victor Glover signed with the Creative Artists Agency. The deal covers representation in television, books, speaking, and endorsements. Glover retired from active flight status after completing his NASA assignments. He piloted the first operational Crew Dragon mission to the space station. Glover also served as pilot on the Artemis II flight around the Moon. He joined California Polytechnic State University as an executive advisor. Source: hollywoodreporter.com

  5. Is China Catching Up on Reusable Rockets — New Scientist

    6. Engineers in China are testing reusable rocket stages. The stages return to landing sites after completing orbital missions. Recent flights demonstrated controlled descent sequences. The vehicles also performed engine relight sequences after separation. The developments narrow the gap with established reusable systems operated by other nations. Source: newscientist.com

  6. The Artemis Accords Are Rules for a New Moon Era — EarthSky

    7. The Artemis Accords establish shared guidelines for resource use. The rules also cover safety zones and debris mitigation on the lunar surface. Multiple nations have adopted the framework to coordinate future landings. The agreement adds practical operational rules to the Outer Space Treaty. Infrastructure planning now follows these coordinated standards. Source: earthsky.org

  7. SpaceX Launching Prototype Google AI Satellite Next Week — Space

    8. SpaceX will launch a prototype satellite carrying Google tensor processing units. The mission evaluates how the chips handle machine-learning tasks in the space environment. Engineers will test performance under radiation and thermal stress. Launch is scheduled aboard a Falcon 9 from Florida. Data will inform future on-orbit artificial intelligence applications. Source: space.com

  8. ISRO Chief Outlines Roadmap to 2040 at IIT Delhi — ET Education

    9. ISRO chairman S. Somanath presented a timeline to 2040. The plan includes reusable launch vehicles and a crewed lunar landing. A space station is also targeted within that period. The roadmap emphasizes incremental technology demonstrations. The Gaganyaan program serves as the first major step. Students at the Indian Institute of Technology Delhi heard the details during a public lecture. Source: education.economictimes.indiatimes.com

  9. ESA and EU Partner with Museo Galileo on Exhibition — ESA Activities

    10. The European Space Agency partnered with the European Commission and Museo Galileo. The exhibition links Galileo Galilei’s observations to the modern Galileo satellite navigation system. Artifacts from the seventeenth century sit alongside models of current European satellites. The display runs in Florence. It highlights continuity in European space efforts across centuries. Source: esa.int

  10. Earth from Space Venetian Lagoon — ESA Activities

    11. IRIDE satellites captured an infrared image of the Venetian Lagoon. The data were collected on May seventeenth, twenty twenty-six. The images reveal temperature variations across channels and islands in northern Italy. ESA released the image as part of its regular Earth observation series. The observations support ongoing monitoring of coastal environments. Source: esa.int

  11. NASA Shares SpaceX Crew-14 Assignments — NASA Breaking

    12. NASA named Kayla Barron as commander for the Crew-14 mission. Chris Birch will serve as pilot. JAXA astronaut Makoto Suwa and Roscosmos cosmonaut Ivan Vagner complete the four-person crew. The mission will travel to the International Space Station. Launch is targeted for no earlier than spring twenty twenty-seven. Source: nasa.gov

  12. NASA Unveils Winning Designs for Mars Space Food Systems Challenge — NASA Breaking

    13. Chinyere Ukeje of Philadelphia received the top three hundred thousand dollar prize. The award recognized the Adaptive Nourishment Infrastructure concept. The challenge sought integrated systems that grow, process, and prepare food for long-duration Mars missions. Eight additional teams earned smaller awards for complementary technologies. The winning designs address nutrition and sustainability needs for future crews. Source: nasa.gov

  13. NASA’s Machines for Mars Make Beer Bubbly — NASA Breaking

    14. Carbon-capture hardware originally developed for Mars habitats is now purifying carbon dioxide in craft breweries. The technology removes unwanted gases during fermentation. It improves consistency of the final product. Several breweries have adopted the systems through NASA technology transfer agreements. The adaptation demonstrates practical Earth applications of space hardware. Source: nasa.gov

  14. Practicing for Safe Landings on the Moon and Beyond — NASA Breaking

    15. An Alta-X drone tested the Safe and Precise Landing Integrated Capabilities Evolution system. The test occurred near NASA Armstrong Flight Research Center. The experiment simulated lunar descent profiles using sensors and algorithms. The algorithms were developed at Johnson Space Center. Results will refine guidance software for future landers on the Moon and other bodies. Source: nasa.gov

Cosmic Spotlight

NASA astronaut Victor Glover has signed with CAA after completing his NASA career and joining the emeritus program. The agency representation covers unscripted television, book deals, speaking engagements, and brand partnerships. Glover’s social media reach exceeds seven hundred eighty thousand followers across platforms. His Artemis II flight made him the first Black astronaut to travel around the Moon. The move opens new avenues for communicating spaceflight experiences to wider audiences while he advises his alma mater on engineering programs. Glover continues to support future missions through the emeritus track.

Cosmic Deep Dive: How Close Asteroid Flybys Reveal Internal Structure

Apophis will pass Earth at a distance roughly one tenth the average Earth-Moon separation, close enough for tidal forces to flex the asteroid’s crust by several millimeters. Picture a city block of solid rock bending like a steel beam under its own weight; that is the scale of deformation expected during the April twenty twenty-nine encounter. Radio tracking and laser ranging from the Ramses spacecraft will measure those tiny shifts in real time, turning the flyby into a natural seismic experiment. The data should reveal whether Apophis is a single monolithic body or a loosely bound rubble pile, something current ground observations cannot resolve. Yet scientists still cannot predict exactly how much energy will couple into surface motion or whether dust will lift off and form a temporary coma. The unanswered question is whether the same tidal stresses that expose internal layers also trigger unpredictable surface changes that could affect future deflection planning.

That covers today’s space and science developments from multiple agencies and missions.

The winning designs address nutrition and sustainability needs for future crews. Carbon-capture hardware originally developed for Mars habitats is now purifying carbon dioxide in craft breweries. The technology removes unwanted gases during fermentation. It improves consistency of the final product. Several breweries have adopted the systems through NASA technology transfer agreements. The adaptation demonstrates practical Earth applications of space hardware. An Alta-X drone tested the Safe and Precise Landing Integrated Capabilities Evolution system. The test occurred near NASA Armstrong Flight Research Center. The experiment simulated lunar descent profiles using sensors and algorithms. The algorithms were developed at Johnson Space Center. Results will refine guidance software for future landers on the Moon and other bodies. NASA astronaut Victor Glover has signed with CAA after completing his NASA career and joining the emeritus program. The agency representation covers unscripted television, book deals, speaking engagements, and brand partnerships. Glover’s social media reach exceeds seven hundred eighty thousand followers across platforms. His Artemis II flight made him the first Black astronaut to travel around the Moon. The move opens new avenues for communicating spaceflight experiences to wider audiences while he advises his alma mater on engineering programs. Glover continues to support future missions through the emeritus track. Apophis will pass Earth at a distance roughly one tenth the average Earth-Moon separation. That closeness allows tidal forces to flex the asteroid’s crust by several millimeters. Picture a city block of solid rock bending like a steel beam under its own weight. That scale of deformation is expected during the April twenty twenty-nine encounter. Radio tracking and laser ranging from the Ramses spacecraft will measure those tiny shifts in real time. The measurements turn the flyby into a natural seismic experiment. The data should reveal whether Apophis is a single monolithic body or a loosely bound rubble pile. Current ground observations cannot resolve that question. Scientists still cannot predict exactly how much energy will couple into surface motion. The unanswered question is whether the same tidal stresses that expose internal layers also trigger unpredictable surface changes that could affect future deflection planning.
