Top 12 Space & Astronomy Stories

  1. FCC Approves One Million Starmind Satellites — Phys.org Space

    The U.S. Federal Communications Commission authorized SpaceX to deploy up to one million orbital compute modules forming the Starmind constellation. The order requires at least one authorized space station by 2033 and full deployment by 2040. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr called the move a historic first in the artificial intelligence race. Scientists including astrophysicist Adam Becker called the plan a fantasy citing basic physical limits on radiating heat in vacuum. The American Astronomical Society said the larger and more numerous objects would undermine decades of investment in astronomical facilities and the ability to study the night sky. The regulator dismissed environmental concerns outside its authority while maintaining the constellation serves the public interest. Source: phys.org

  2. Europa Clipper Swings Past Earth Toward Jupiter — Phys.org Space

    NASA’s Europa Clipper will fly within 1,950 to 2,133 miles of Earth on December 3 to gain a gravity-assist boost toward Jupiter. The 12,800-pound spacecraft will turn on all nine instruments during the flyby to calibrate them against known Earth data. The magnetometer will use Earth observations to pin its measurements to an absolute reference before reaching Europa. The E-THEMIS thermal imager will scan Earth every eight hours while the ultraviolet spectrograph will study the geocorona alongside Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter observations. Source: phys.org

  3. Lunar Craters Could Shield Moon-Base Reactors — Phys.org Space

    Researchers simulated placing a 40-kilowatt nuclear reactor 1.5 meters above the lunar surface on a rover. A three-meter-deep crater or three-meter-high dirt hill reduced radiation to roughly one-tenth of a rem per year. At Connecting Ridge near Shackleton Crater a 120-meter crater wall dropped habitat radiation to four-hundredths of a rem per year. The simulations used Monte Carlo N-Particle software to track millions of particles from the core. Source: phys.org

  4. Hubble Maps Galactic Bulge for Roman Telescope — Phys.org Space

    Hubble observed the Milky Way’s galactic bulge in red and near-infrared wavelengths that overlap with the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope. The data will help characterize stars before they produce microlensing events that Roman will use to find exoplanets. Hubble’s high resolution records brightness, ages, composition and motion of individual stars in the crowded region. The observations add to legacy surveys including the WFC3 Galactic Bulge Treasury Program and the Sagittarius Window Eclipsing Extrasolar Planet Search. Source: phys.org

  5. NASA Tests Strawberries, Cucumbers and Lemon Basil for Space — NASA Breaking

    Researchers grew strawberries in flight-like chambers and found the Delizz variety outperformed a wild type that produced too many runners. Cucumbers showed steady edible fruit yield under simulated spaceflight conditions despite some leaf enlargement. Lemon basil passed seed sanitization and preliminary growth tests and is now moving to plant-pillow evaluation. Space crops absorb carbon dioxide, produce oxygen and recycle water on long-duration missions. Source: science.nasa.gov

  6. Blue Origin Offers Limited-Edition New Glenn LEGO Set — autoevolution

    The 550-brick set depicts the New Glenn rocket and Jacklyn landing barge and stands 10.5 inches tall when complete. It is sold exclusively through the Blue Origin website for $294.95 with deliveries expected the week of November 30. The real New Glenn has flown three times in its 7x2 configuration and will launch the Blue Moon lander and VIPER rover next year. Source: autoevolution.com

  7. Landsat Captures Resolution Island Sanctuary — NASA Breaking

    An Operational Land Imager image from Landsat 9 on December 12, 2025 shows Resolution Island rising to Mount Clerke at 1,069 meters amid Fiordland’s glacier-carved valleys. Thomas Henry Potts proposed the island as a refuge for kiwi and kākāpō in 1872. Caretaker Richard Henry transported more than 700 birds by rowboat after the 1891 reserve designation. A Department of Conservation program begun in 2008 continues stoat control on the island that lies within swimming distance of the mainland. Source: science.nasa.gov

  8. NASA Signs Mentor-Protégé Agreement with Boeing and GRACE — NASA Breaking

    The agreement signed October 29, 2026 at the U.S. Space and Rocket Center in Huntsville values nearly $500,000 and pairs Boeing with GRACE Aerospace. NASA is streamlining the Small Business Supplier Development Program application process and reducing administrative requirements. The partnership aims to expand GRACE’s capabilities in mission-critical aerospace hardware while strengthening the domestic industrial base. Source: nasa.gov

Cosmic Spotlight

OpenAI Codex and Astra models scanned public telescope archives and flagged dozens of transit-like signals around nearby stars. A SETI astronomer cross-checked two of the signals against existing radio data but found no confirmed extraterrestrial origin. The effort demonstrates how machine-learning tools can accelerate candidate identification even when follow-up observations are still required. Source: techtimes.com

Cosmic Deep Dive: Density Patterns in Cosmic Web Filaments

Cosmic web filaments are the largest structures in the universe, vast bridges of dark matter and gas that connect galaxy clusters across hundreds of millions of light-years. New research published by the American Astronomical Society refines how dark matter is distributed inside these filaments and points to a previously hidden universal pattern in their density. Astronomers have long mapped the web through galaxy surveys and weak-lensing measurements, yet the precise way mass concentrates along the filaments has remained difficult to pin down because the structures are so diffuse and distant. The latest analysis untangles conflicting signals from earlier studies by comparing density profiles across multiple filament samples and simulation runs. It shows that the central density of filaments follows a consistent scaling relation once projection effects and galaxy bias are carefully removed. The work draws on both observational catalogs and large-scale cosmological simulations to isolate the underlying dark-matter distribution. What remains unknown is whether this scaling holds at the highest redshifts or in the most massive filaments, and whether a single physical mechanism sets the pattern across all environments. A larger sample of filaments mapped at higher resolution with upcoming surveys would settle whether the relation is truly universal or varies with cosmic time and local density. Source: aasnova.org

The universe keeps delivering surprises when we look closely enough.

Europa Clipper, a National Aeronautics and Space Administration, or NASA, spacecraft, will take a gravity-assist from Earth on the way to Jupiter. A gravity-assist is a close pass that uses a planet’s pull to pick up speed. On December third the twelve thousand eight hundred-pound spacecraft will fly within one thousand nine hundred fifty to two thousand one hundred thirty-three miles of Earth and switch on all nine instruments so they can be checked against known Earth data. The magnetometer will use those Earth looks to lock its readings to an absolute reference before it arrives at Europa. The E-THEMIS thermal imager will scan Earth every eight hours, and the ultraviolet spectrograph will study the geocorona together with Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter observations. Cosmic web filaments are the largest structures in the universe, vast bridges of dark matter and gas linking galaxy clusters across hundreds of millions of light-years. Research newly out from the American Astronomical Society sharpens how dark matter sits inside those filaments and finds a universal density pattern that had been hidden. Galaxy surveys and weak-lensing measurements have long traced the web, but pinning down how mass piles up along the filaments has been hard because they are so diffuse and far away. The new work sorts out clashing signals from earlier studies by stacking density profiles from several filament samples and simulation runs, and it finds that central density follows a consistent scaling relation after projection effects and galaxy bias are stripped out. Observational catalogs and large-scale cosmological simulations were both used to isolate the dark-matter distribution underneath. It is still unknown whether that relation still applies at the highest redshifts or inside the heaviest filaments, and whether one physical mechanism sets the pattern in every environment; mapping a larger sample at higher resolution with coming surveys would show if the relation is truly universal or shifts with cosmic time and with how dense the surroundings are. Connecting Ridge near Shackleton Crater is one site where a crater wall was tested as reactor shielding for a lunar habitat. Microlensing is a brief brightening caused when one star’s gravity magnifies another, and Roman will use those events to find exoplanets. Those measurements will characterize stars before they cause those microlensing events. At high resolution, Hubble records how bright those individual stars are, plus their ages, composition, and motion, in a crowded field.
