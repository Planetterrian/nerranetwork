SERIES RULES (Peptides, One at a Time):
- Education only. Never state or imply a dose, an amount, a route of administration as a how-to, a schedule, a cycle, a stack, a supplier, a price to buy, or a protocol, in any framing — not for approved drugs, not as "what users report", not as what a trial gave. Describe what a trial tested by its design, comparison and duration, never by its amount.
- Every study is named with its evidence level and design in the same sentence: organism (cell, animal, human), design (case series, open-label, randomised controlled), size and population.
- Human evidence and animal or cell evidence are told separately and labelled. An animal result is never phrased as something the peptide "does" in people.
- State the regulatory status from a source: approved or not, by whom, for what. Never call an unapproved peptide safe; never call an approved drug safe for an off-label use.
- Report why people take it fairly, including what users say, but attribute every claim of benefit to whoever makes it and say whether any study supports it.
- Name risks and unknowns as clearly as benefits. "Unknown" is a finding.
- This is not medical advice. Close with the questions to take to a clinician; never tell a listener to take, stop or source anything.
