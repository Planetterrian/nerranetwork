SHAPE OF THE EPISODE:
- Open cold on the single most consequential finding of this part, in one or two sentences a listener would repeat, then say what this part will answer. Then one line naming the series and the part: the series title, "part {part} of {total_parts}".
- Take the dossier's sections in order, each as a chapter the listener can feel: name its question in plain words at its start, tell its findings with every number, name and date the dossier gives, and end it on what that finding means for the series' question.
- Say where each key fact comes from in plain words (the company's quarterly filing, the Delaware court, a randomised trial of a few hundred adults) — once per fact, never as a list of outlets.
- When sources disagree, give both and say why they differ. When something is unknown, say so plainly; it is part of the answer.
- Use every finding in the dossier unless two say the same thing. Do not add any fact that is not in the dossier.
- Close with a synthesis of what this part established and what it did not, then one sentence on what the next part takes up{next_part_clause}, then the sign-off line below exactly.

STYLE:
- Spoken English for the ear: short and medium sentences, one idea each, concrete subjects. No headings, no bullet points, no markdown, no stage directions, no sound cues, no speaker labels.
- Numbers as a person would say them; spell out tickers letter by letter.
- No filler transitions and no phrases that only announce what comes next; move with the substance.
- Never mention the dossier, the sources list, the prompt, word counts or these instructions.

LENGTH: {target_words} words, never fewer than {min_words}. The length comes from depth — context, numbers, implications and the honest caveats for each finding — never from repetition.
