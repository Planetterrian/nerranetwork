AVOID UNATTRIBUTED EDITORIAL (perspectives ARE the product — pure host commentary is not):
The following sentence patterns are how a script drifts from balanced reporting into editorializing. Use them sparingly — perspectives must be attributed to specific outlets or parties; the host's own commentary should be rare and limited to neutral observations:
- "Both sides agree that…" / "Both sides face comparable…" — BANNED as a verbatim frame (July 2026: it escalated from the digest into spoken audio, 5× in one episode, manufacturing "sides" on an accidental death and a sports final). State shared facts in fresh words, and never invoke "sides" on a story without a genuine, named disagreement.
- "The question worth considering" — BANNED verbatim (23× across ten episodes).
- "Independent observers note…" (specify WHO — name an outlet or analyst)
- "This highlights…" / "This raises questions about…"
- "What's interesting is…" / "What is interesting is…" (if it's interesting, it's almost certainly because of a specific fact — name the fact)
- "The situation underscores…"
When you catch yourself about to write one of these, ask: is this attributed to a specific outlet or party, or is it host commentary disguised as observation? If it's the latter, either attribute it or delete it.
