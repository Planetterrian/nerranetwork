TRACK A — ``debut`` (the series premiere; the longest, richest episode)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
This is the first episode. It must convince a new listener to subscribe and tell a friend. Produce these segments:

### Segment 1 — The Cold Open
3-5 sentences. Open on the single most striking number in the episode (e.g. a finished part costing many times its raw materials). Land the promise of the show in one breath.

### Segment 2 — Why This Show Exists
16-20 sentences. Most people reason by analogy — they copy what already exists and tweak it. First-principles thinking instead asks: what are the raw facts, and what does physics actually require? Make clear this is not new and not the property of any one person — gesture at the long lineage (for example Henry Ford rethinking the cost of a car, Henry Bessemer collapsing the price of steel, or the steady march of solar and semiconductors down their cost curves) and note that Elon Musk popularized the two tools' language and is the most famous modern practitioner, but the method is much older and much bigger than any one figure. Introduce the two recurring tools by name and explain them carefully: the "magic wand number" (the raw-material floor) and the "Idiot Index" (finished cost divided by material cost). Make the Idiot Index click by walking ONE small worked example out loud — name a rough finished price, a rough material value, and divide them so the listener sees where a ratio of, say, fifty comes from and what it implies (the cost is in the design and process, not the material). Explain WHY a high Idiot Index is an opportunity rather than a fact of nature. State the show's promise: every day, either one concrete example of this thinking in action — historical or modern, from any field — or one industry that is ripe for it.

### Segment 3 — Worked Example One (Concrete): The Raptor Engine
24-30 sentences. Trace the development path of SpaceX's Raptor engine as first-principles iteration. Cover: why a full-flow staged-combustion cycle was chosen (more energy extracted from the same propellant mass, higher chamber pressure, better specific impulse) and what that buys; why methane and liquid oxygen were chosen (Mars producibility, no coking versus kerosene, easier handling than hydrogen); and then the relentless drive across versions to cut part count, mass, and cost per engine. Walk SEVERAL specific consolidation moves step by step — combining propellant manifolds, eliminating valve families by changing sequencing logic, moving from machined forgings to cast or additively manufactured parts — and for EACH one explain why it removed mass, leak paths, or assembly hours and thereby pushed the Idiot Index toward the physical floor. Give the rough cost-per-engine trajectory across versions as clearly-hedged approximations, and say where the Idiot Index sits now versus comparable legacy booster engines. Make the cost-reduction LOGIC the star, not any single dollar number.

### Segment 4 — Worked Example Two (Opportunity): An Industry Ripe For This
24-30 sentences. Pick the opportunity domain named in the topic brief. Establish what the finished thing actually costs today and why. Then estimate its rough magic-wand floor, reasoning the arithmetic out loud (name the major raw materials and their rough commodity values, add them up, and flag every figure as an estimate). Compute the rough Idiot Index from that floor and the finished price. Reason carefully about WHERE the gap lives — which specific steps carry the waste (regulation and permitting timelines, fragmented supply chains, on-site hand assembly, custom one-offs, carrying costs that compound through delay). Then lay out, concretely, what a from-scratch redesign would attack FIRST and what would have to be true — materials, manufacturing, scale, policy — for the cost to fall toward the floor. Name the genuinely hard parts honestly rather than hand-waving a utopia. This is an exposé of the OPPORTUNITY, framed as "here's the prize if someone reasons this through."

### Segment 5 — The Lesson & The Promise
6-9 sentences. Name two portable principles and explain each in a sentence (e.g. "a part whose price greatly exceeds its materials is announcing a design problem, not a material shortage"; "question the requirement before optimizing the part that satisfies it"). Restate the cadence: tomorrow and every day after, one example or one opportunity. End on a specific forward-looking question.

TRACK B — ``concrete_example`` (a real first-principles win — historical or modern, from ANY person, company, or lab; Musk's teams are eligible but are NOT the default)

### Segment 1 — The Cold Open
3-4 sentences. Open on the surprising before/after — the old way versus the from-scratch way — in vivid, specific terms.

### Segment 2 — The Old Way (Reasoning By Analogy)
10-13 sentences. How did the field "normally" build this, and why was that the default? Who set the convention and what assumptions were baked into it? What was the finished cost, mass, or part count under the conventional approach? Establish the baseline the innovator refused to accept, and why everyone else accepted it.

### Segment 3 — The First-Principles Move
20-26 sentences. The heart of the episode — give it the most room. What did they break down to raw materials and physics? Name the magic-wand floor (reasoning the arithmetic out loud and hedging every figure) and the Idiot Index of the old design. Then walk the causal chain of the redesign step by step across SEVERAL distinct moves — which parts were eliminated, combined, re-specified, or re-manufactured — and for EACH one explain WHY that specific change moved the cost toward the floor. Surface the key engineering or process trade-off they had to win for the move to work.

### Segment 4 — The Result & The Limits
9-12 sentences. What did it achieve — in cost, mass, part count, or speed — stated as well-established approximations? Where is the new Idiot Index now, roughly, and how far is it still from the floor? Be honest about the trade-offs accepted and what remains genuinely hard or unsolved.

### Segment 5 — The Lesson
6-8 sentences. Extract two portable principles and explain each ("if a part costs far more than its materials, the design is the problem," "question the requirement before optimizing the part"). Close with a forward-looking question or a hint at where this same move could go next.

CRITICAL CONSTRAINTS (all tracks):
- Do NOT fabricate statistics, dates, quotes, or part counts. Every number is well-established or clearly hedged ("roughly," "on the order of," "a rough magic-wand estimate"). Prefer ratios and ranges over false precision. Hedging lets you reason about real numbers OUT LOUD — it is the opposite of writing less; use it to go deeper, not to retreat into vagueness.
- Make the Idiot-Index reasoning visible so the listener can follow and judge it — never present a back-of-the-envelope figure as a measured fact.
