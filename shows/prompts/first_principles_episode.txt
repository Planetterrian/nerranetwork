This is the first episode. It must convince a new listener to subscribe and tell a friend. Produce these segments:

### Segment 1 — The Cold Open
2-4 sentences. Open on the single most striking number in the episode (e.g. a finished part costing many times its raw materials). Land the promise of the show in one breath.

### Segment 2 — Why This Show Exists
5-8 sentences. Most people reason by analogy — they copy what already exists and tweak it. First-principles thinking instead asks: what are the raw facts, and what does physics actually require? Introduce the two recurring tools by name and explain them simply: the "magic wand number" (the raw-material floor) and the "Idiot Index" (finished cost divided by material cost). Use the well-known illustration of a small rocket part that cost far more than the metal inside it to make the Idiot Index click. State the show's promise: every day, either one concrete example of this thinking in action, or one industry that is ripe for it.

### Segment 3 — Worked Example One (Concrete): The Raptor Engine
8-12 sentences. Trace the development path of SpaceX's Raptor engine as first-principles iteration: the choice of a full-flow staged-combustion cycle burning methane and liquid oxygen, and the relentless drive across versions to cut part count, mass, and cost per engine. Show the REASONING at each step — why simplifying a component or removing a part lowered the Idiot Index toward the physical floor. Keep all figures clearly approximate and well-established; make the cost-reduction logic the star, not any single dollar number.

### Segment 4 — Worked Example Two (Opportunity): An Industry Ripe For This
8-12 sentences. Pick the opportunity domain named in the topic brief. Estimate its rough magic-wand floor versus what the finished thing actually costs today, and reason about why the Idiot Index is so high (regulation, fragmented supply chains, hand assembly, custom one-offs). Lay out, concretely, where a from-scratch redesign could compress the cost — without overpromising. This is an exposé of the OPPORTUNITY, framed as "here's the prize if someone reasons this through."

### Segment 5 — The Lesson & The Promise
3-5 sentences. Name one or two portable principles. Restate the cadence: tomorrow and every day after, one example or one opportunity. End on a forward-looking question.

TRACK B — ``concrete_example`` (a real first-principles win inside one of Musk's companies)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

### Segment 1 — The Cold Open
2-3 sentences. Open on the surprising before/after — the old way versus the from-scratch way — in vivid, specific terms.

### Segment 2 — The Old Way (Reasoning By Analogy)
4-6 sentences. How did the industry "normally" build this, and why was that the default? What was the finished cost, mass, or part count under the conventional approach? Establish the baseline the team refused to accept.

### Segment 3 — The First-Principles Move
6-9 sentences. The heart of the episode. What did they break down to raw materials and physics? What was the magic-wand floor, and what was the Idiot Index of the old design? Walk the causal chain of the redesign step by step — which parts were eliminated, combined, or re-manufactured, and WHY each change moved the cost toward the floor.

### Segment 4 — The Result & The Limits
4-6 sentences. What did it achieve — in cost, mass, part count, or speed — stated as well-established approximations? Be honest about trade-offs and what is still hard. Where is the new Idiot Index now, roughly?

### Segment 5 — The Lesson
3-5 sentences. Extract one or two portable principles ("if a part costs far more than its materials, the design is the problem," "question the requirement before optimizing the part"). Close with a forward-looking question or a hint at where this same move could go next.

TRACK C — ``opportunity_area`` (an industry ripe for first-principles thinking)

### Segment 1 — The Cold Open
2-3 sentences. Open on the gap: what this thing costs today versus what the raw materials suggest it could cost. Make the prize vivid.

### Segment 2 — Why It Costs What It Costs Today
5-8 sentences. How is this done now, and why? What are the entrenched reasons for the price — regulation, custom fabrication, fragmented supply chains, legacy assumptions? Establish the baseline cost with well-established approximate figures.

### Segment 3 — The Magic Wand Number & The Idiot Index
6-9 sentences. Estimate the raw-material floor for this thing, reasoning out loud and clearly flagging every estimate as an estimate. Compute the rough Idiot Index. Show where the gap between floor and finished cost actually lives — which steps are carrying the waste.

### Segment 4 — The First-Principles Opportunity
6-9 sentences. Concretely, what would a from-scratch redesign attack first? What would have to be true — materials, manufacturing, scale, policy — for the cost to fall toward the floor? Reason carefully; name the hard parts honestly rather than hand-waving a utopia.

### Segment 5 — The Lesson
3-5 sentences. One or two portable principles, plus a forward-looking question: who could do this, and what would be the first signal that someone is actually trying?
