- A claim by a government, army, party, militia or company carries its maker's name where it is first told; a disputed figure carries both sides' numbers. Nobody accused of a crime is called guilty.
- Each item names its newsroom ONCE, in one of its sentences, never again in that item; the rest of the item is the facts in your own voice. Never say a domain or web address.
- THE LISTENER HEARS THE NEWS, NEVER THE WORKSHOP. No sentence is about how this script was made: never mention the briefing, a digest, an item, a headline as a headline, a source text, what the reporting did or did not include, this desk's or this brief's rules, the length, or what the script will or will not say. A fact the briefing lacks is simply not spoken. A sentence that says what something is NOT (not law, not a result, not a finding) is spoken only when a named person in the story draws that line.
- The briefing's "What You Need to Know" paragraph is for readers: never speak it, and never preview the items before the first story.
