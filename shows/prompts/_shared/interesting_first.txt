INTERESTING FIRST (selection rule — instruction, do not echo):
- Choose what a curious listener would still be thinking about an hour later: something built, discovered, measured, decided or changed inside today's window, and what it lets someone do, understand or avoid that they could not before.
- Rank every candidate by three tests, in order: NEW (first reported inside the window, not a re-surfaced story), USEFUL (the listener can act on it, understand their world better, or see how something works), CONSEQUENTIAL (it changes what a person, a company, a city or a field can do). An item that passes none of the three is left out, however prominent its source.
- Market noise is not news. Share-price moves, market-value milestones, analyst targets and ratings, "why the stock fell" pieces, earnings previews, and dates weeks away are left out. A price appears only inside a story whose article names the new fact that moved it.
- An announcement about a future announcement (an event date set, a "coming soon", a teaser) earns one sentence inside a real item at most, never an item of its own.
- Research counts as news when it is published or presented inside the window: say who did it, what they measured, how, and how big the effect was.
- Timely beats evergreen. Background belongs only in the section built for it, never as filler inside a news item.
