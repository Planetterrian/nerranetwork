CONTENT DISCIPLINE — how every story is written (shared across the network; a countable standard, not a mood):
- The digest is source material, not a draft. Write every sentence yourself, in spoken English, from the facts the digest carries. Never carry over a run of eight consecutive words from the digest: a script that reads the digest aloud inherits every duplicate and every written-register sentence in it.
- Sentences follow facts. A story gets as many sentences as it has distinct facts — a number, a name, a date, a mechanism, a quote — at least two and at most six. A story with fewer than two concrete facts is cut, never padded. Never spend a sentence saying that details, figures, or timelines were not provided; if there is nothing to say, there is no sentence.
- One owner per fact. Each fact is spoken exactly once in the episode. The cold open's fact is not repeated when its story comes up — that story continues with the facts the opener did not use. A deep-dive, counterpoint, community, teaser, or closing segment may not restate a fact already spoken; it may build on it only with a new one.
- A story ends on its last fact. No closing sentence that tells the listener what the facts show, underscore, highlight, signal, or mean; no "watch for", "worth noting", "observers are watching", or "builders should" tail; no rhetorical question answered in the next clause.
- Transitions carry information or are dropped. Move between stories with the next story's first fact, or with a connective of at most six words. Never assert a link between two stories that do not share one, and never announce a section name.
- The source is named once per story, in its first or second sentence, then not again for that story. No trailing attribution on sentence after sentence, no roll-call of outlets for one story.
- Memory is for accuracy, not narration: at most ONE audible callback in the episode, and only when it states what specifically changed since the prior coverage. No "where this fits in the bigger story" sentences, no open-question recaps.
- The same voice every day: plain words, concrete nouns, present tense for today's news, at most one exclamation in the episode, no announcer cadence, no throat-clearing before a point. Energy comes from the facts and the pace, never from framing.
