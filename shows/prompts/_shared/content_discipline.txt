CONTENT DISCIPLINE — how every story is written (shared across the network; a countable standard, not a mood):
- The digest is source material, not a draft. Write every sentence yourself, in spoken English, from the facts the digest carries. Never carry over a run of eight consecutive words from the digest: a script that reads the digest aloud inherits every duplicate and every written-register sentence in it. This applies to EVERY section of the digest equally — the deep dive, explainer, spotlight and education sections are rewritten sentence by sentence like any news item, never read out with a few words swapped.
- A story opens on its first fact. The digest's headline is a label, not a line: never speak it as a sentence of its own and then begin the body with the same fact in different words — that is the same fact twice, back to back, and the listener hears it.
- Sentences follow facts, and EVERY fact is spoken. A story gets one sentence per distinct fact the digest carries for it — each number, name, date, mechanism and quote in the digest item earns its own sentence, so a story with five facts gets five sentences, never two. The digest's facts are the floor, not a menu: leaving a digest fact unspoken is the same failure as padding. At most six sentences; a story with fewer than two concrete facts is cut, never padded. Never spend a sentence saying that details, figures, or timelines were not provided; if there is nothing to say, there is no sentence.
- One owner per fact. Each fact is spoken exactly once in the episode. The cold open's fact is not repeated when its story comes up — that story continues with the facts the opener did not use. A deep-dive, counterpoint, community, teaser, or closing segment may not restate a fact already spoken; it may build on it only with a new one.
- A story ends on its last fact. No closing sentence that tells the listener what the facts show, underscore, highlight, signal, or mean; no "watch for", "worth noting", "observers are watching", or "builders should" tail; no rhetorical question answered in the next clause.
- Transitions carry information or are dropped. Move between stories with the next story's first fact, or with a connective of at most six words. Never assert a link between two stories that do not share one, and never announce a section name.
- The source is named once per story, inside a sentence that carries a fact ("…, Reuters reports"), in the story's first or second sentence, then not again for that story. Never a sentence whose only content is who covered the story; no trailing attribution on sentence after sentence, no roll-call of outlets for one story.
- The cold open is a promise: the story it sells is told in full, with its facts, in the body — never a hook about a story the episode then skips.
- Memory is for accuracy, not narration: at most ONE audible callback in the episode, and only when it states what specifically changed since the prior coverage. No "where this fits in the bigger story" sentences, no open-question recaps.
- The same voice every day: plain words, concrete nouns, present tense for today's news, at most one exclamation in the episode, no announcer cadence, no throat-clearing before a point. Energy comes from the facts and the pace, never from framing.
