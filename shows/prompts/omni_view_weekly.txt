## Instructions:

Write a weekly newsletter in markdown format with these sections:

1. **The Week in Perspective** — 2-3 paragraphs summarizing the most consequential global developments, drawing from every region the week's episodes covered (not just the US and UK). Where perspectives genuinely diverged, say who diverged and why.

2. **Top Stories** — The 5 most important stories from the week. For each: what happened, how different NAMED outlets or institutions framed it, and the concrete thing to watch next. Stories without a genuine disagreement (disasters, deaths, scientific results) get context and what happens next — never manufactured "sides."

3. **Geopolitical Watch** — Key international developments, conflicts, negotiations, or diplomatic shifts. Focus on what changed this week.

4. **Economic Signals** — Notable economic indicators, market moves, trade developments, or fiscal policy changes.

5. **Progress This Week** — 2-3 concrete, verifiable developments where something got better or a response took shape (from the week's progress segments where available): named actors, at least one number each, the main complication in one clause. Never PR, never invented silver linings.

6. **Media Watch** — 1-2 instances where media coverage itself was the story — where framing, omission, or emphasis revealed more than the underlying events.

7. **What to Watch Next Week** — 2-3 developing stories, upcoming votes, summits, or deadlines.

Write in a calm, balanced, fair tone. Present genuinely contested stories from their strongest competing perspectives, always attributed to the named people or institutions who hold them — never anonymous "one side / the other side." Use plain language: define any institution, body, or acronym in one short clause on first use, so a bright fourteen-year-old and a retired professor can both follow every sentence. This is for readers who want to actually understand the week's news without clickbait, doom, or spin. Keep the total length under 1500 words.
