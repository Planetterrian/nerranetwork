Omni View Central & South America

Anses raises November pensions 1.93 percent, so the minimum reaches 514,158 pesos.

La Nación ties the adjustment to September inflation of 1.9 percent as Indec published it, applied to two decimal places, while a 70,000-peso bonus frozen since March 2024 still tops up the minimum and several non-contributory benefits. Elsewhere on October 15, El País reports the Colombian left’s first marches against De la Espriella and the release of people held after Guadalajara’s clean-water march, and O Globo reports federal police action in São Paulo against a suspected appliance-shipping drug network.

Lead

Jubilaciones de Anses: cuánto cobro en noviembre 2026, con el bono oficial y el aumento confirmado: La Nación

La Nación reports that older adults paid through Anses, the National Social Security Administration, receive a 1.93 percent rise on the pensions and benefits the agency pays in November, the eleventh month of the year. The paper says the monthly formula follows the price increase to two decimal places, which is why a September inflation reading of 1.9 percent, as Indec published it, becomes a 1.93 percent adjustment of benefits. After that rise, the minimum pension stands at 444,158.45 pesos. The extraordinary bonus of 70,000 pesos is still paid with it, so the minimum total is 514,158.45 pesos. The Universal Pension for Older Adults is 355,326.76 pesos plus the same 70,000-peso bonus, for 425,326.76 pesos. The non-contributory pension for disability or old age is 310,910.91 pesos plus 70,000 pesos, for 380,910.91 pesos. La Nación says the bonus figure has not changed and has stayed frozen since March 2024. It is credited with the benefit and is aimed mainly at people on the minimum, on non-contributory pensions, and on the universal pension. For benefits above the minimum, the bonus is paid on a sliding scale until the payment reaches a cap of 514,158.45 pesos. The update changes the minimum pension, contributory benefits paid by Anses, the universal pension, non-contributory pensions for old age, disability, mothers of seven or more children, and other non-contributory and grace pensions. For someone starting a retirement claim, La Nación lists the agency’s own steps: enter Mi Anses with a CUIL and a social-security key, check that contributions are recorded under Work and then Consult Labor History, and, if contributions are missing, submit service certificates, pay slips, health-plan affiliation proof, or a sworn statement, complete form 6.18 for pension benefits, book an office appointment, and bring a national identity document that day. Source: lanacion.com.ar

The Closer Look

Muebles de autor, recuerdos del 83 y vanguardia: comenzaron las obras de rescate patrimonial en “el gigante de la calle Sarmiento”: La Nación

Work to recover the Centro Cultural General San Martín is under way in Buenos Aires after several years of deterioration. La Nación reports that the city has opened a full intervention at Sarmiento 1551, in the City of Buenos Aires, with the stated aim of returning one of the country’s emblematic cultural sites to its former condition. The plan covers putting the structure back in order, modernizing the installations, and preserving the original design. The intervention is described as an attempt to hold heritage conservation together with current technical standards.

The complex is a work of architect Mario Roberto Álvarez. It opened on May 8, 1970. It occupies more than 42,000 square meters, split among an administrative tower, a main building of theater halls, and six basements with room for 900 people. Jorge Macri, head of the city government, said the administration is recovering a symbol of Buenos Aires. He said the San Martín is part of the city’s cultural identity and that the work is meant to put the building back at the level of its history, with infrastructure that is modern, safe, and accessible to everyone.

The first stage gives priority to renewing installations and to structural improvements. Crews are demolishing old masonry, partitions, and ceilings, and they are raising new internal structures. At the same time, specialists are restoring the heritage facades of Halls A and B. The project includes a complete replacement of the electrical, sanitary, thermomechanical, and fire-protection systems, presented as the means of making the building run efficiently. Universal accessibility and updated evacuation protocols are a central part of the plan, with improvements to the elevator passageways. Historic furniture is to be preserved, including the Churba chairs and the armchairs in the foyers, and new equipment is to be added for workshops and offices. In later stages, the city government would install advanced professional lighting and more efficient climate control in order to reduce energy use. Those measures are described as a way to remove risks and to use resources through sustainable technology.

The building’s public record, as La Nación sets it out, is not only theatrical. It was the main computing center during the 1983 presidential election. It hosted the 76th World Inter-Parliamentary Congress. In 1990 it brought together figures of world literature at the Latin American Writers’ Meeting, with talks by Mario Benedetti, Mario Vargas Llosa, and José Donoso. The material published with the project says that, when every stage is finished, a place full of those memories is meant to recover its standing. The stated central aim is to turn the building into a renewed base of national and international culture, adapted to the technical demands of this century while remaining a reference for the national scene. Mercado de escritórios de alto padrão no RJ registra menor vacância desde 2014: O Globo

O Globo reports an unpublished Newmark survey finding that vacancy in Rio de Janeiro’s high-standard corporate offices, classes AAA, AA, and A, fell to 23.5 percent in the third quarter of 2026, the lowest level since 2014. In that period, net absorption reached 17,000 square meters, above the 15,000 square meters of the previous quarter, while the average asking rent rose 4.2 percent to 83.43 reais per square meter a month. Over 12 months, the accumulated rise in those values is 7.6 percent. The movement has been led mainly by the city center, which accounted for 62 percent of absorption accumulated in 2026. Among the largest moves, Coren-RJ took about 5,200 square meters in the Bolsa do Rio building, and Cepel arrived at AQWA Corporate in Porto Maravilha. Despite the improvement, vacancy remains high in the center, Porto Maravilha, and Cidade Nova, at 26.9 percent, 27.4 percent, and 39.1 percent. Newmark’s expectation for the coming months, as O Globo reports it, is that the recovery continues, although new developments coming onto the market and caution in the face of the macroeconomic and electoral outlook may leave companies more selective about the space they occupy. For firms looking for high-end offices in Rio, the survey is a measured tightening of empty space, not a claim that the emptiest districts have filled. Source: oglobo.globo.com

Omni View Central & South America

Nine hundred fired Oi workers lose their severance schedule after Rio's bankruptcy ruling.

What You Need to Know: About nine hundred people dismissed by Oi in August must wait for a new severance deal after the company's bankruptcy changed the rules on an installment agreement already approved in court. In Bahia, a jury cleared the man charged with the 2024 killing of former child actor João Rebello. In Buenos Aires province, former world champion Jorge "Locomotora" Castro died overnight at 59.

Lead

Falência da Oi muda acordo de rescisões e trabalhadores terão de negociar novo pagamento: O Globo

Judicial administrator Bruno Rezende says Oi's bankruptcy decree changed the company's regime, so an installment agreement on severance has to be remade under the new rules. About 900 people dismissed in August, who had expected payment in six to ten installments, now wait for that new deal, and unions say they were not told beforehand. The court approved 98.5 million reais for Rezende while he made that argument. O Globo asked the Rio de Janeiro Court of Justice about breach of the agreement homologated by the 7th Business Court, the same court that authorized that pay, and had no reply by publication. The column also asked Rezende, who sent clarifications. He says the deal was signed in a judicial-recovery setting, and that after the bankruptcy decree payments must follow that regime's own rules, including how creditors are treated and how much cash the bankrupt estate has. Federations Livre, Fenattel and FITRATELP say the argument does not hold. They say the dismissals happened before the bankruptcy, so the earlier installment schedule should be kept. For the unions, a new agreement cannot simply repeat the old installments and will have to pay the amounts owed in full. At a meeting last Wednesday with representatives of those three federations, Judge Simone Gastesi Chevrand said the 18 August days worked that sat outside the agreement would be paid this week, between Wednesday and Thursday. Union representatives call that a partial victory only if the money actually arrives. The main demand remains a decision on the severance sums themselves. Workers describe financial strain and collections among colleagues to cover basic costs, including rent and food, for former employees in vulnerable situations. Another point put on the table is subsidiary Serede: the federations say those employees have gone about ten months without amounts tied to severance. The next stage is a meeting between the entities and Rezende to discuss how payment can proceed. Even after the judge pressed him to set that meeting, the administrator had not done so. Source: oglobo.globo.com

The Closer Look

Ministério do Esporte pode voltar a ser Secretaria? Com receio, COB procura candidaturas para defender a área: O Globo

The Brazilian Olympic Committee will deliver a suggested sports-government plan this week to the campaign committees of both candidates in the presidential runoff. Marco La Porta, the committee's president, told O Globo the aim is to give the sports portfolio real weight, because sport is not a priority area in Brazil and keeping a ministry without protagonism, while constantly dealing with held-back funds, does not change that. He said the most important point is to value the Ministry of Sport and give it real relevance inside government, whether the next government is new or the current one. The fear he described is that the ministry returns to a special secretariat, the form it took under Jair Bolsonaro, father of Liberal Party candidate Flávio Bolsonaro, and that successful programs and public policies are discontinued. La Porta spoke to O Globo before the first-round result. Flávio Bolsonaro took 47.03 percent and Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva of the Workers' Party 45.16 percent. The two will face voters on October 25. Asked about the Olympic community's fear that a Flávio Bolsonaro win would turn the ministry back into a secretariat, La Porta said the fear is always present, because the tendency is to repeat what was done in the previous government, his father's. He noted that Flávio Bolsonaro had said, before the first round, that he intends to cut the number of ministries and commissioned posts to reduce public spending if elected, without naming which ones. In 2019 Jair Bolsonaro folded the Ministry of Sport into the Ministry of Citizenship. La Porta said that if Bolsonaro wins, a change of government still brings a ministerial reform, and the sector does not know what can happen. His message, he said, is clear: they do not want the end of the Ministry of Sport. He had already raised the point in his opening speech at COB Expo, the committee's sports fair, at the start of this month. That stance led allies of Flávio Bolsonaro, including Marcelo Reis Magalhães, special sports secretary at the Ministry of Citizenship under Jair Bolsonaro, to take the question to the candidate. On a live broadcast the same day, Flávio Bolsonaro said there is no such plan and that the Ministry of Sport will be one of the main ministries of his government, and that people can stay calm. On that same live, Magalhães said restoring the ministry had been Jair Bolsonaro's dream and that the secretariat had deserved to be a ministry. The campaign press office answered O Globo in a note that incentive to sport and a reduction in the number of ministries are both commitments in Flávio Bolsonaro's government plan, framed as the need to advance administrative reform against what the note called the disorder of spending under Lula's government. The note says, without naming the Ministry of Sport, that no essential ministry will be extinguished, and that the whole government structure will be analyzed during the transition and through the government, once technical teams are structured.

The paper itself, La Porta said, was written by entities, committees and other players in sport. It says Brazil still faces significant challenges in widening access to sport and physical activity. It uses Vigitel 2024, which found that only 42.3 percent of adults in Brazil's state capitals reach recommended levels of leisure-time physical activity. It suggests keeping the Ministry of Sport, implementing the National Sport Plan, strengthening the Bolsa Atleta program, and improving tax incentives for sports development, among other themes. La Porta said sport is not cited in the electoral campaigns, so the sector thought of presenting proposals to the candidates, because afterward it cannot complain that nothing was done. He spoke of turning Brazil into a sporting nation, and said that the day the Ministry of Sport is relevant — because it never has been — that nation can start to be built, and that without support it will not happen. The ministry is, in his word, new. It was created officially in 1995, under Fernando Henrique Cardoso, with Pelé as the first minister. It has passed through mergers, extinctions and recreations. In 1998 it merged with tourism as the Ministry of Sport and Tourism. In Lula's first government, in 2003, sport was separated from tourism and the Ministry of Sport was born again. The current government once more separated sport from citizenship, the configuration held from 2019 to 2022, and recreated the portfolio. La Porta said there should be a state plan, not a government plan, so that sport is included as a factor in health, education and security, and that this does not happen: with each government the ideas change. He added that a budget is not enough if projects are not put into practice, and that relevance, money, planning, and ministers who do not rotate constantly are required. Many resources are held back and never arrive, he said, which hits confederation projects that depend on them. He cited Revelar Talentos, launched in April 2024 to identify, select and support young athletes across Brazil: a project he called wonderful, covering five confederations, none of which has yet received this year's 2026 amount. Projects over time rather than one-off efforts, he said, lose their whole sequence. Among actions of the current government, the article notes approval of the New Sports Incentive Law and the General Sports Law, recent sanction of the Women's World Cup Law, which sets rules on commerce and security and also protects players and the development of the sport, and a rise in the value of Bolsa Atleta grants. The portfolio's budget for 2026 is 2.5 billion reais. The suggested plan is due with both campaign committees this week. Source: oglobo.globo.com

Lead

Brazil’s Senate may free party lines on a vote to end the 6x1 workweek: O Globo

The Liberal Party is expected to release its senators on the constitutional amendment that would end the six-days-on, one-day-off schedule, Senator Wellington Fagundes, leader of the Senate opposition bloc, said. Campaign coordination for Flávio Bolsonaro prefers abstention, he said, but most of the bench believes senators should take a position; Senator Tereza Cristina said other opposition parties will also free their members, with the line to be set at a Wednesday lunch. The text would swap that week for five days of work and two of rest, cutting weekly hours from 44 to 42 sixty days after enactment and to 40 a year later. Each round needs 49 of the Senate’s 81 votes. Government leader Teresa Leitão plans to file by Wednesday a special-calendar request so both rounds can be held before the campaign ends on October 25; Senate president Davi Alcolumbre has scheduled debate on Tuesday and Wednesday and said a first-round vote can follow. Flávio Bolsonaro opposes the amendment and, through campaign coordinator Senator Rogério Marinho, wants hours negotiated between workers and employers; party Senate leader Carlos Portinho called the measure an electoral rush. Amendments still in discussion would stretch the transition to six months and open exceptions for shift workers such as oil and airline crews. Source: oglobo.globo.com

Both Sides: When a school should expel a student

Ten days ago at unit two of Colégio Ícone, in Taquara, Rio de Janeiro, a physical-education teacher refereeing a football match marked a foul. Some students disagreed and physically attacked him. He says the school suspended them at once, supported the teacher, reviewed the images, called a council, and summoned the families because they have a right to speak. Mônica Donetto Guedes, a psychoanalyst and parent guide, argues that violence is a limit that cannot be crossed or treated as normal, because settling conflict by force breaks the use of words. She distinguishes a fight between students, which she says the school should mediate, from an attack on a teacher, which she calls an attack on authority and on the rule. She argues that expulsion also shows the other students that aggression is not an accepted way to end a conflict, and that families have to help build limits.

Priscilla Montes, a specialist in neuroeducation and child development, argues that the productive question is which intervention protects the school community and still teaches, in proportion, not which punishment the student deserves. She says a bad act is not an identity, and that a purely punitive answer can deepen a student’s distance from school. She argues that a transfer does not end the school’s and the family’s responsibility, that schooling should continue, and that moving a student without a plan for the behavior can carry the problem somewhere else.

She says the gravity has to be proved, and she lists serious physical assault, persistent threats, repeated bullying or cyberbullying, harassment, discrimination, carrying weapons, drug trafficking, or intentional destruction that puts people at risk. She says everyday indiscipline, low achievement, one-off conflicts, undone assignments, or family disagreements with the school do not justify expulsion, and that a student should not be punished for acts done only by the adults responsible for them.

Gabriel de Britto Silva, a lawyer, argues that expulsion must stay reasonable relative to the act, and that repeated disciplinary offenses, insubordination, disrespect of staff, and a serious insult can allow enrollment to be closed or not renewed only if the constitutional rights to a full defense and to answer the case are respected, under an administrative procedure based on an ethics code or the school’s internal rules.

The speakers accept that students physically attacked the teacher and that the school has to protect its community.
