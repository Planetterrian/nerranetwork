Omni View Central & South America

Brazil may keep a two-real-twelve diesel subsidy thirty more days, shielding bus and truck costs.

What You Need to Know: Brazil may keep a two-real-twelve diesel subsidy for thirty more days, a step meant to shield bus and truck costs. Planning Minister Bruno Moretti says the benefit that lapses on Saturday will be extended, while extra fuel relief is costing the federal treasury seven billion reais a month. In Argentina, the administrator of the armed forces’ health fund has resigned with provider debt above two hundred billion pesos; in Honduras, civil-society monitors counted seven hundred eighty-six bills still pending.

Lead

Brazil set to keep a R$2.12-a-litre diesel subsidy for 30 more days: O Globo

Brazil’s federal government is expected to announce, still this week and on the eve of the presidential election, that it will keep a diesel subsidy of 2.12 reais a litre for another thirty days. Planning Minister Bruno Moretti said the subsidy will be extended. The extra real a litre published this month has been running on top of an existing 1.12-real subsidy, and that combined measure loses effect on Saturday. The provisional measure that authorized the newer subsidy lets the amount be changed at any time; the government says the war in the Middle East may require a benefit larger than one real after September 26, because higher oil prices and refinery problems threaten local prices and domestic supply, above all for trucks and buses. Early this month the government also announced Pis/Cofins relief of 0.63 reais a litre on gasoline and 0.19 reais a litre on fuel ethanol. Together with the newer diesel subsidy, those extra fuel-price benefits cost the Union seven billion reais a month. Source: [oglobo.globo.com](https://oglobo.globo.com/economia/noticia/2026/09/24/as-vesperas-das-eleicoes-governo-deve-manter-subvencao-de-r-212-por-litro-do-diesel-por-mais-30-dias.ghtml)

Across the Region

Military health-fund administrator resigns as the debt crisis deepens: La Nación

Retired colonel Ariel Guzmán has resigned as administrator of the armed forces’ health fund, a day after meeting Defense Minister Carlos Presti. La Nación reports the debt now reaches 200 billion pesos, while the old Instituto Obra Social de las Fuerzas Armadas still owes providers 213 billion pesos. A new Obra Social de las Fuerzas Armadas receives members’ contributions; a source close to the agency said Presti’s design left a residual Iosfa structure to pay the old debt, with a commitment that the Economy Ministry would supply the money. Presti called Guzmán in because he was not satisfied with the pace of the liquidation, which the minister considers a fundamental step toward the past debt and a new management model. The State Workers’ Association, led by Rodolfo Aguiar, says an emptying-out puts about 600 jobs at risk and that benefit cuts now reach the interior; at the Aeronautical Military Lyceum in Funes, Santa Fe, civilian staff are protesting pay deductions while care is cut. The union protested at Paso 551, whose doors had been shut over fear of an occupation, and then took the building after forcing the shutter. Source: [lanacion.com.ar](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/politica/renuncio-el-administrador-de-la-obra-social-de-los-militares-y-la-crisis-se-profundiza-nid24092026/)

Honduras Congress left 786 bills pending after a busier first half: Infobae

The Association for a More Just Society counted 926 bills filed in Honduras’s National Congress between January 25 and June 30, of which 140 were approved, about 15 percent, leaving 786 pending. The Honduran civil-society group logged 62 sessions averaging 3.7 hours, with average attendance of 98.9 percent, helped by fines for absences without a justification it accepted. About 713,000 lempiras, roughly 26,589 dollars, were deducted from the pay of 48 deputies. Of the bills that passed, infrastructure and transport led with 28, health with 18, and economy and finance with 12. Democracy specialist Emily Flores asked, at the report’s presentation, whether the country wants quantity or quality. Source: [infobae.com](https://www.infobae.com/honduras/2026/09/24/congreso-de-honduras-deja-786-proyectos-pendientes-pese-a-mayor-actividad-legislativa-en-el-primer-semestre-de-2026/)

Also Today

Forecasters say this El Niño may rank among the strongest on record: La Nación

The International Research Institute for Climate and Society reported on September 21 that all 22 models keep the 2026 El Niño in the “very strong” category through the southern summer of 2026–27, after the weekly Niño 3.4 reading centered on September 16 reached plus 3 degrees Celsius, up from a monthly August average of plus 2.52. Source: [lanacion.com.ar](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/el-mundo/anticipan-que-el-super-nino-alcanzara-su-pico-en-las-proximas-semanas-podria-ser-uno-de-los-eventos-nid24092026/)

School minibus crash injures at least seven in San Salvador: Infobae

At least seven people, including students, were hurt Thursday morning when a school minibus struck the rear of a trailer truck on the Oro highway at the Ticsa bridge in San Salvador; the Medical Emergency System treated the injured and firefighters freed one person from the wreckage. Source: [infobae.com](https://www.infobae.com/el-salvador/2026/09/24/microbus-escolar-choco-contra-una-rastra-dejando-al-menos-siete-lesionados-en-san-salvador/)

Sheinbaum says prosecutors are still checking seized-fuel permits: Reforma

President Claudia Sheinbaum said Mexico’s Attorney General’s Office is still reviewing permits tied to 59 million litres of fuel seized in Guanajuato in a case of alleged fuel theft. Source: [reforma.com](https://www.reforma.com/fgr-sigue-revisando-permisos-dice-csp-de-presunto-huachicol/ar3281770)

Former Miss Universe Jamaica says her 2025 fall was avoidable: Jamaica Observer

Former Miss Universe Jamaica Dr Gabrielle Henry told Good Morning America she had not tripped on November 19, 2025, that she was following organizers’ instructions, and that she was later told she had an intracranial hemorrhage plus facial and back injuries. Source: [jamaicaobserver.com](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/09/24/not-trip-dr-gabrielle-henry-speak-terrifying-fall-new-interview/)

The Region and the World

Guatemalan business group backs deeper U.S. cooperation against cross-border crime: Infobae

Guatemala’s main business chamber, CACIF, on September 23 backed tighter cooperation with the United States against transnational crime after Guatemalan authorities met a U.S. Justice Department delegation in Guatemala. President Bernardo Arévalo, Interior Minister Marco Antonio Villeda, and Attorney General Gabriel García Luna met five federal prosecutors named by President Donald Trump and representatives of nine federal districts, a first for that grouping in Guatemala, in a visit coordinated by Erik Peterson’s overseas prosecutorial training office. The government said acting Deputy Attorney General Brian Skaret took part, along with the DEA, the FBI, Homeland Security Investigations, and Joint Task Forces Alpha and Vulcano. The agenda covered prosecutions, border security, extraditions, and building cases for U.S. courts, including drug trafficking, money laundering, and human trafficking and smuggling. CACIF said joint investigations can hit networks that operate across countries and asked for a professional, objective, and independent prosecution based on evidence; the executive said Guatemala takes the cooperation as a strategic partner. Source: [infobae.com](https://www.infobae.com/guatemala/2026/09/24/el-sector-empresarial-organizado-respalda-la-cooperacion-de-guatemala-y-estados-unidos-contra-las-redes-criminales-transnacionales/)

Both Sides: Anonymous accusations and platform orders

Argentina’s Supreme Court has ordered Facebook Argentina, the local unit of Meta, to delete anonymous Facebook and Instagram posts that called F.C., a regular student at the National University of La Plata’s Faculty of Humanities and Education, an “abuser,” and to turn over available data identifying the account holders. The order sets aside a ruling of the Federal Chamber of La Plata that had rejected his habeas data claim.

F.C.’s case, which the Court accepted, is that the campaign left him unable to defend himself. Posters with his face and the word “Abuser” went up across the faculty. Anonymous profiles, presented as linked to the faculty gender commission, accused him of imposing sex on his partner even when she objected, while he publicly backed feminism and abortion rights. He argued that a private matter that did not rise to public interest had been turned into a public shaming. The Court called it a kind of virtual escrache, said it put his honor and privacy at risk, and held that freedom of expression, including on the internet, is especially broadly protected but is not absolute and must be reconciled with honor, privacy, and the right to a defense. It told Facebook to remove the challenged URLs if they are still up.

The strongest case for leaving the posts in place is the one the La Plata chamber acted on when it rejected the claim, together with the weight the Supreme Court itself gave speech. The posts’ authors are not quoted in the reporting; what they published is the accusation, framed as a warning about someone they called a hypocrite. The chamber declined to order removal or identification. The Court did not treat the abuse allegation as a finding.

Both the student and the publishers of the posts are dealing with the same material: anonymous posts on Facebook and Instagram that used the word “abuser.” The Court and the chamber split on whether that required takedown and naming. Source: [lanacion.com.ar](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/seguridad/la-corte-suprema-ordeno-a-facebook-eliminar-las-publicaciones-anonimas-que-acusaban-de-abusador-a-un-nid24092026/)

Ep 1: Category 5 Hurricane Polo menaces Mexico's Pacific coast, with no landfall expected.
Download Read transcript

Omni View Central & South America

Category 5 Hurricane Polo menaces Mexico's Pacific coast, with no landfall expected.

What You Need to Know: Hurricane Polo is a Category 5 off Mexico's Pacific coast. Mexican authorities have warned of heavy rain, strong winds and dangerous surf, and the storm is not expected to make landfall. UN experts say Nicaragua's crackdown on opponents amounts to crimes against humanity, and Brazil is moving to bar candidates with suspected criminal ties from October's election.

What This Show Covers

Listeners who want one region, every day, told straight, start here: Central and South America and the Caribbean, with what was decided, what happened, and what it changes and for whom. Each edition also holds one contested question, with the strongest case on each side, and one measurable sign of progress when the day's reporting has one.

The promise on geography is simple. This desk covers Mexico, Central America, the Caribbean islands and every country of South America. Mexico's domestic politics, economy and security belong here. A story from outside the region appears only for a concrete consequence inside it, and that consequence comes first.

The brief is read by Mira, the network's AI host. Stories are chosen and written by software from named newsrooms, and every item names its source. It will not take a side: every claim is attributed, and no party, government or people is praised or mocked.

Other Omni View desks and Omni View Top World News sit alongside this one.

Omni View Central and South America is part of the Nerra Network, an independent, ad-free network of shows at nerranetwork.com. If this is the region you want each morning, it is here.

Lead

Hurricane Polo strengthens to category 5 off Mexico's Pacific coast: Reuters

Hurricane Polo has strengthened into a Category 5 storm off Mexico's Pacific coast and, in the Associated Press account, one of the strongest storms on record there. Mexican authorities have issued warnings as the storm threatens coastal areas with heavy rains, strong winds and dangerous surf. It is not expected to make landfall. Forecasters warn of rapid intensification, potential flooding and strong winds for coastal regions and resorts. Reuters said the storm is driven by warm waters linked to El Niño. Source: [reuters.com](https://www.reuters.com/business/environment/hurricane-polo-nears-category-5-strength-off-mexicos-pacific-coast-2026-09-22/)

Across the Region

Nicaragua's crackdown on opponents amounts to crimes against humanity, UN experts say: Reuters

UN experts say Nicaragua's state institutions have carried out enforced disappearances, torture and arbitrary detentions of political opponents since 2018. They describe those actions as amounting to crimes against humanity and point to ongoing repression by the government. Source: [reuters.com](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/nicaraguas-crackdown-opponents-amounts-crimes-against-humanity-un-experts-say-2026-09-22/)

As gangs gain influence, Brazil seeks to shield election from candidates with criminal ties: Reuters

Brazil is taking unprecedented steps to bar candidates with suspected criminal links from October's general election amid rising gang influence. Reuters said experts view the effort as a significant move against organized crime in Latin American politics, aimed at protecting the electoral process. Source: [reuters.com](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/gangs-gain-influence-brazil-seeks-shield-election-candidates-with-criminal-ties-2026-09-22/)

Mexico classrooms go phone-free under new school rules: AP News

Mexico has implemented new national rules restricting cellphone and screen use in schools, effective November, aimed at reducing distractions and promoting learning. The policy aligns with similar measures in other Latin American countries. Source: [apnews.com](https://apnews.com/hub/latin-america)

18 suspects in 2021 Haiti president's killing sent to US for trial: South China Morning Post

Eighteen suspects in the 2021 killing of Haiti's president, Jovenel Moise, have been sent to the United States for trial. They were flown to Florida as US prosecutors expand the case over a mercenary plot against Moise. Source: [scmp.com](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/americas)

Both Sides: Foreign action against organized crime

At the UN General Assembly, the question is how far governments outside a country should go in fighting organized crime in the region, and who decides. Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva told the assembly that Brazil needs cooperation, not intervention. He rejected any foreign role in combating organized crime. Reuters said the remarks were seen as directed at Washington and at domestic rivals. He also cautioned against outside interference in Brazil's October elections. The South China Morning Post reported that he told the United States Brazil is no one's backyard. The strongest case for that limit, as Lula stated it, is cooperation with Brazilian authorities rather than a foreign role he rejects, and an election free of outside interference.

The strongest case for going further, as officials have stated it, is the threat they name. The South China Morning Post reported that Secretary of State Marco Rubio defended US strikes on boats, warned of a drug-cartel "cancer," and pledged continued military operations against suspected drug traffickers in Latin American waters. The New York Times reported that 14 Latin American countries are expected, on the sidelines of the General Assembly, to designate 25 criminal groups as terrorist organizations, following the US lead, to enhance regional security cooperation.

What both accounts treat as given is that organized crime is a problem governments say they are responding to. The next dated marker Lula named is Brazil's October elections. The Times reported the designations as expected during this General Assembly.
