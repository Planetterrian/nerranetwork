CSIRO queries genomic variants on AWS without a central copy: InfoQ

CSIRO’s Serverless Beacon, sBeacon, queries genomic variant data on Amazon Web Services without copying large datasets into a central database. Variant Call Format files stay in Amazon S3; metadata such as disease status, age, and location is indexed separately, with Athena tables built at onboarding and ontology terms handled through CSIRO’s Ontoserver. API Gateway sends requests to Lambda; the Variant Querying Module uses bcftools and htslib to pull file ranges over HTTP byte-range requests, then returns results through the GA4GH Beacon API. On chromosome 1 from the 1000 Genomes Project — 2,504 samples in a 1.1 GB compressed VCF — onboarding took 18 seconds. A query covering 10,000 base pairs averaged 1.52 seconds at an estimated compute cost of 0.00013 dollars, and latency stayed broadly consistent as matching variants rose from four to 400. Anuradha Wickramarachchi, solutions architect for bioinformatics products at CSIRO, said querying data while it stays compressed is greatly beneficial. Earlier sBeacon research in Nature Biotechnology reported support for up to 40 million individuals and 3 billion genomic locations. Source: infoq.com

The Thread: Four signatures, one lawful-purpose clause

Three of the seven signed the same kind of door this year. Wocult, citing the BBC, reports that Google, Microsoft and Amazon — and OpenAI, which is not one of the seven — signed agreements with the Pentagon in 2026 to deploy AI in classified military environments. The Google deal, signed earlier this year, allows its models to be used for classified work including what the contract describes as any lawful governmental purpose. US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth has made AI integration in the armed forces a stated priority. The shared fact is permission: a public-company model, once inside that environment, is available for lawful governmental use, and the companies named are the ones that already sell cloud and model access at scale.

What is new against yesterday’s resignations is who left and what they said the contract does not let them stop. Research engineer Sofia Liguori, based in London, left this week after three years. She told the BBC, in a personal capacity, that she had lost faith in Google’s stated ethical values over growing military work, and that her employment contract restricts her from criticising the company in public effectively for life. AI researcher Andreas Kirsch resigned earlier in October. He told the BBC that Google’s governance and oversight were not sufficient or meaningful enough to deploy AI safely in military and classified settings, and that the company appeared to have no right to control or veto how the Pentagon uses its technology. In June, René Mayrhofer, then a director for Android platform security, resigned and published a farewell letter saying leaving had become unavoidable and that Google management had lost its moral compass. Current employees told the BBC they fear retaliation and feel unable to speak openly about AI safety or the military direction. Google told the BBC it follows a consistent process for reviewing public-sector contracts in line with its acceptable-use policies and AI principles, and that it has dedicated channels for staff to raise concerns with protections against retaliation.

The split inside the seven is not a split in the signature list. Alphabet is the company whose staff are leaving in public. Microsoft and Amazon are named in the same BBC account as signatories of Pentagon agreements this year, without the named resignations attached to them in this report. OpenAI is the fourth name on that list. For Alphabet, the operational change is a classified-use right the company told the BBC it reviews under existing principles, set against three dated exits — June, earlier in October, and this week — and a claim, from Kirsch, that Google cannot veto Pentagon use. For Microsoft and Amazon, the report’s change is the same class of agreement: models in classified military environments, tied to Hegseth’s priority, with no separate remedy or opt-out described in the piece. The next dated mark the sources actually give is not a hearing. It is the pattern of 2026 signatures already on the page, and the October exits that are still being named. Source: wocult.com

Top News

Apple Ordered to Open iPhone to Rival App Stores in Australia: MacRumors

Australia's Federal Court today ordered Apple to allow competing app stores, direct downloads, and alternative payment services for users whose Apple Account region is Australia, from noon Melbourne time on March 1, 2027. The orders follow the court's August 2025 finding that Apple misused its market power by restricting distribution and payments, and Apple Developer Program members worldwide may take part. Apple must not charge for access to those paths, must give developers tools on substantially the same terms as App Store distribution, and cannot require its own purchasing system, transaction reports, or an audit of alternative payments. For App Store apps, fees on alternative payments are capped at the charge for physical goods or services plus genuine, reasonable extra costs of digital purchases; apps outside the store still need an automated security or privacy scan and notarization by Apple or a qualified third party. Apple told Reuters it plans to appeal and to ask the court to pause enforcement, citing intellectual property and App Store security; it may apply between February 1 and February 15, with evidence, for an extension to May 3. Epic Games told Reuters that Fortnite returns to Australia's App Store today under an order that does not wait for the March 2027 changes; Australia was the only country left out when Fortnite returned worldwide in May. Source: macrumors.com

The Thread: One assistant, two dashboards

2019 is when Google Maps traffic reports lived only on phones. Early in 2024 those hazard pins reached Apple CarPlay; later in 2024 they reached Android Auto. On October 22, 2026, Google put Gemini's voice inside Google Maps on CarPlay. The Mac Observer calls it the first time that navigation app has had built-in voice on this dashboard. Drivers in the United States have it now. A wider global release is in progress. During a route, a new icon on the right edge of the screen starts Gemini. The questions can run past yes or no: a park known for views over the city, a time when it is not busy and the weather stays fair, then navigation that avoids toll roads. The same voice can file a traffic disruption. The Mac Observer says the car screen now lines up with Android Auto and with the voice tools Google added to the iOS Maps app last year. Before this, those tools stayed on the handset.

Autoevolution's Bogdan Popa writes that the CarPlay build is already on an iPhone 15 Pro in Europe, and that a follow-up keeps its subject: a coffee place with the best cappuccino in the neighborhood, then whether that cup is expensive, then set the shop as the destination. He found it responsive, and more refined than Gemini on Android Auto, which he says has been buggy since the late-2025 rollout. Last month Google retired Assistant on Android Auto, so the old assistant is not a switch a driver can flip. People who had that fallback had called Gemini too chatty for the wheel, and some said it could not call 911 or place phone calls. On CarPlay, Popa says, ordinary speech is enough for a traffic report: say the small street has become a nightmare, and Gemini can file heavy traffic without a memorized phrase.

The shared piece is the assistant, not the car. Apple owns the CarPlay glass. Google owns Maps, Gemini, and Waze. Maps and Waze stayed separate because Waze still takes a wider set of reports — objects on the road, broken lights, floods, potholes — while Maps stays with accidents, roadworks, and speed cameras. Google now also pulls police reports from Waze into Maps. For Apple, a Google app on Apple's dashboard gains a conversational layer the day before the iPhone Duo goes on sale, and Apple is not the one shipping the model. For Google, CarPlay stops being the screen where Gemini voice was missing, and the Android Auto path no longer offers Assistant as the quieter alternative. For a driver on either platform, the voice that answers a multi-step route question is Gemini, tapped from the map rather than from the phone in a lap. Source: macobserver.com

Fourteen days, not six months, gets an NVIDIA AI factory into production.

What You Need to Know: NVIDIA’s factory-built DSX POD containers are meant to put a Vera Rubin AI factory into production in as little as fourteen days instead of six months. The same Berlin keynote added an agent-safety stack that can stop a runaway agent in milliseconds, while Microsoft’s NHS Copilot trial is now a staffing number, not a pilot anecdote. Britain’s competition regulator, meanwhile, wants a cheaper way onto the iPhone’s contactless chip.

Top News

Factory-built containers cut an AI factory install from six months to fourteen days: NVIDIA Newsroom

At GTC Berlin, NVIDIA introduced DSX POD, a reference design for complete Vera Rubin AI factories that partners build inside modular containers, integrate, and validate before delivery. The units are meant to connect to a prepared site’s power, cooling, and networking, cutting onsite deployment from six months to as little as fourteen days. Jensen Huang said an AI factory creates value when it starts producing tokens, not when construction is finished. The wider DSX platform pairs liquid-cooled computing with dry coolers so cooling liquid stays in a closed loop. Vera Rubin supports 100 percent liquid cooling with coolant temperatures up to 45 degrees Celsius. NVIDIA says that at 2-gigawatt scale the design can save 54 million liters of water a day versus cooling towers, equivalent to the daily water use of 180,000 households, and that local climate decides whether extra mechanical cooling is needed in the hottest periods. DSX MaxLPS is described as helping operators generate the same number of tokens with up to 30 percent less power. CoreWeave, Firmus, Google Cloud, IREN, Lambda, Nebius, Nscale, and Sharon AI are exploring it. In a proof of concept on HGX B200 systems, Lambda reported 24 percent more cluster token throughput and 19 percent more GPU capacity inside a fixed power budget. Partners working to deliver DSX POD factories worldwide include Dell Technologies, HPE, Foxconn, Lenovo, Schneider Electric, and Vertiv. Source: nvidianews.nvidia.com Source: nvidianews.nvidia.com

OpenShell puts agent control on the CPU, the rack, and the DPU: NVIDIA Newsroom

NVIDIA also announced the OpenShell Reference Architecture for building, containing, observing, and enforcing agents on enterprise infrastructure and at the edge. It stacks Vera Rubin systems for inference, Vera CPUs for agent harnesses and tool use, open-source OpenShell runtime software, and BlueField-4 DPUs running NVIDIA Sentry. Sentry, built on DOCA, watches agent behavior out of band. If an agent moves outside its software boundary, Sentry quarantines and stops it in milliseconds. NVIDIA says Sentry can boost agent capacity by up to 20 percent. The design extends to RTX Spark, DGX Spark, and DGX Station for retail, healthcare, manufacturing, robotics, software development, and telecommunications. It runs on Linux and Kubernetes from Canonical, SUSE, and Red Hat, and on Microsoft Windows using Microsoft Execution Containers. Huang’s line for the split was that agents can think on Rubin, act on Vera, and be governed on BlueField. Source: nvidianews.nvidia.com

Weights, training data and scripts are out, so a developer can run and change the model locally through the strands-decider package, on the command line or inside Strands agents.

The clock AWS cites is short. Median decision latency was about 115 milliseconds on an NVIDIA RTX 3090. Small tasks took 153 milliseconds on an Apple M3 MacBook. On JevBench, the v19 checkpoint scored 72.3 percent accuracy across 231 public tasks. Those two machines are not AWS products. They are the hardware a developer already has on a desk: an NVIDIA consumer GPU in one test, an Apple silicon laptop in the other. The model’s job is to sit in front of a larger system and choose a tool or a route. If that choice takes a tenth of a second on a 3090 and about a sixth of a second on an M3, the wait is in the same band as a sluggish click, not a cloud round trip the article never claims to have measured.

InfoQ places Decider against TypeSafe AI’s Jev, another model aimed at structured decisions, classification, routing and tool selection, also returning confidence scores.
