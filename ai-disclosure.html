AI Disclosure

Nerra Network · Effective March 31, 2026 · Updated September 3, 2026

Nerra Network is an AI-produced podcast network with human editorial ownership. This page says exactly what that means: what the machines do, what people do, and what safeguards sit between the two.

Every Nerra Network episode is written and voiced by AI from sources a human curated, under rules a human wrote, and ships through an automated pipeline. A person sets the brief, chooses the sources, owns the editorial standards, reviews the output, and answers for it. No episode hides that it was made this way: each one says so out loud, and every page on this site carries the disclosure.

What AI does

Writing

Each episode's digest and script are generated by a large language model (xAI's Grok) from that day's curated articles, feeds, and web searches, using a show-specific prompt.

Narration

English shows are voiced by a custom voice trained on founder Patrick Novak's own speech. Russian shows use a custom Russian voice. Two shows, The Age of AI and Nerra Daily, are hosted by Mira, an AI persona that introduces itself as one.

Imagery and video

Episode artwork, video scenes, and the image gallery are generated with Grok Imagine. Show cover art and logos are designed by people.

Translation

French, Russian, and Chinese audio tracks are machine-translated from the English script and re-voiced. English is always the canonical version.

Books

Our anthology books compile published episodes; the audiobook editions are digitally narrated and declared as such on every store that sells them.

Quality review

An automated reviewer reads every episode after publication and flags factual, structural, or tone problems to the editor. It is a second pair of eyes, not the last word.

What people do

Nerra Network is built and run by one person, Patrick Novak, in Vancouver. Production is automated so that seventeen shows can publish daily; editorial ownership is not. Concretely, a human:

  • Decides what each show is. The brief, the tone, the audience, the segments, and the rules each show's prompt enforces are written and revised by hand.
  • Curates the sources. Every show pulls from a hand-picked list of outlets, feeds, and search queries. Sources that prove unreliable are removed.
  • Sets the editorial standards. No undisclosed AI, no paid placement, no filler, no invented facts. Those rules are enforced in code, and the code is public.
  • Listens and reviews. Patrick spot-listens to episodes, reads the automated quality reports, and A/B-listens before any change to how a voice sounds ships.
  • Owns corrections. Reported errors are investigated by a person, and substantive corrections are noted in the next episode's show notes.

What a human does not do is read and approve every episode before it publishes. We say this plainly because the alternative claim would be false, and because the safeguards below exist precisely to make an automated pipeline trustworthy.

Safeguards against AI mistakes

Language models can state things confidently and wrongly. We build for that assumption rather than against it:

  • Source-integrity gate. Each digest carries a claims ledger that ties every asserted fact to a source URL and a supporting quote. Before an episode is saved, the pipeline re-fetches each source and checks the quote is really there, and it flags citation-shaped sentences that have no ledger entry, the signature of a fabricated reference. On our narrative shows a failed claim blocks the episode.
  • Grounding rules. Prompts forbid filling gaps from the model's training data; if the day's sources are thin, the run skips rather than pads.
  • Repetition and recurrence checks. Degenerate output triggers a retry; stories the show has already covered are flagged so they are told as updates, not re-told as news.
  • Spoken disclosure. Every episode ends with a spoken line stating that it was produced with AI.
  • Real guests stay verbatim. On The Age of AI, guests are real people interviewed by Mira. Their words are never rewritten, and each guest approves their transcript before the episode publishes.

The full process, step by step, is on our editorial process page.

Why we work this way

  • Honesty builds trust. We would rather tell you how the shows are made than have you guess.
  • Breadth. Automation is what lets a one-person network cover Tesla, SpaceX, AI, investing, space science, and environmental policy every day in four languages, ad-free.
  • Accountability. Because the pipeline is code, every rule it follows can be read, audited, and improved, and every mistake can be traced to its cause.

Regulatory notes

United States (FTC)

The Federal Trade Commission expects clear disclosure when AI-generated content could mislead. We disclose AI use on every page, in every feed description, and in every episode's audio.

California AI Transparency Act (AB 942)

California requires disclosure of AI-generated content in certain contexts. Nerra Network is not a political entity and runs no advertising, but discloses all AI use regardless.

Copyright

Show formats, prompts, source curation, music briefs, and editorial rules are human-authored work. Where the law limits protection for machine-generated material, we do not claim more than it grants. You may share individual episodes with attribution; please ask before reproducing or commercialising our content.

Report a problem

Found an error?

If something in an episode or article is wrong, tell us and we will check it against the sources and correct the record.
