About 100,000 households around Kyiv lost internet after Russian strikes that killed four.

Russia targets Ukraine's digital nerve centres as war reaches data infrastructure: The Times of India

Russian strikes have widened onto data centres and telecommunications sites, disrupting internet service for around 100,000 households in Kyiv and the surrounding region. The Times of India, citing the BBC and Ukrainian officials, reported that the strikes damaged data centres run by internet providers in the capital. Datagroup said its services continued from backup sites. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia had widened attacks onto data centres and internet providers to cause maximum disruption, and that the strikes affect people’s ability to stay connected, study and work. Russia said it struck the data centre because Ukrainian military intelligence was using it. The Russian defence ministry confirmed the Datagroup strike and said its forces had also targeted New-Telco, United DC, Kyivstar and Parkovyi, alongside continued strikes on energy and railway infrastructure. Source: [timesofindia.indiatimes.com](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/defence/international/russia-targets-ukraines-digital-nerve-centres-as-war-reaches-data-infrastructure/articleshow/134498977.cms)

European Commission proposes efficiency labels for data centres: Cyprus Mail

The European Commission has proposed labels showing how efficiently data centres use energy and water, for operators of sites of 500 kilowatts or more. Those operators would disclose energy and water efficiency, the link between water use and local water stress, and whether they can serve the local energy system, including by reusing waste heat. The scheme sets no cap on electricity or water use and does not require disclosure of total electricity consumed. European Union data put data centres at about 2.5 percent of EU electricity, with combined capacity expected to rise from 12 gigawatts last year to 28 gigawatts by 2030, while the EU aims to triple capacity over seven years. Countries and lawmakers have two months to object; otherwise the rules enter into force, and the labels could later become a condition for mandatory minimum standards. Source: [ua.news](https://ua.news/en/world/evrokomisiia-zaproponuvala-markuvannia-efektivnosti-data-tsentriv-cyprus-mail)

Drones could speed up getting defibrillators to people having cardiac arrests, study suggests: The Guardian

Matthieu Heidet and colleagues mapped 28,349 out-of-hospital cardiac arrests from 2011 to 2024 in the Greater Paris area, excluding central Paris, against 1,893 fixed automated external defibrillators. Hillary Minka, first author, said only 30 percent of those cases fell within 500 metres of the nearest recorded device. The team found 910 extra fixed devices would be needed for 84.5 percent of the recorded cases to sit within 500 metres, and 1,712 for full coverage, while drones flying within 3,900 metres could raise coverage with fewer machines. In the United Kingdom, the British Heart Foundation counts more than 30,000 out-of-hospital cardiac arrests a year in which emergency services try resuscitation, and fewer than one in ten people survive; each minute without CPR and defibrillation cuts the chance of survival by 10 percent. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2026/sep/26/drones-could-speed-up-getting-defibrillators-to-people-having-cardiac-arrests-study-suggests)

Both Sides: Faster returns from Spain to Morocco

Spain’s government is drafting a real decreto ley that could let some people who reached Ceuta be returned to Morocco before every administrative and judicial appeal has ended. The text is not in force. It would cover citizens of countries the European Union classifies as safe, including Morocco, nearly two months after a mass arrival and as authorities handle thousands of arrivals from Ceuta.

The government’s case is speed. Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska has said people with no right to remain will be returned, and that applicants for international protection will be returned only after their claims have been examined and rejected. The stated aim is to stop an asylum request from freezing a removal for months. Territorial Policy Minister Ángel Víctor Torres has described roughly 150 to 200 voluntary returns to Morocco a day, and 160 returns in one recent 24-hour period. Officials are already processing returns and are not waiting for the decree.

The case against that timetable, as the draft is set out, is that the shorter path would fall on people whose cases are not finished. The Comisión Interministerial de Asilo could lose its preliminary review. Rejected applicants might have five days to appeal, with their cases put first. Very few asylum applications from Morocco succeed, according to the same account, yet those applicants would still face a different process before their cases were fully settled. A separate disputed provision could archive the applications of people who leave reception centres and expose them to expulsion, while investigations into suspected ill-treatment at some facilities remain open.

The government and the account of the draft both treat the decree as not yet law, and both record that returns to Morocco are already under way. The next step is whether the government adopts the real decreto ley. Source: [russpain.com](https://russpain.com/news/government-targets-faster-migrant-returns-to-morocco-99251/)

Both Sides: The Garvaghy Road parade

Northern Ireland’s Parades Commission is due on Friday to rule on an Orange Order application for 60 members to march down the Garvaghy Road in Portadown on Sunday, a route the Order has not taken in almost 30 years.

The Portadown District, backed by the three unionist parties, says the procession would involve a much reduced number, would be silent, and would spend only a short time on the road. The strongest point they can cite in this week’s reporting is procedural: a High Court ruling last month quashed the Commission’s most recent determination, after unionist politicians had heavily criticised that decision. The Commission has blocked the Order from marching along the mainly nationalist road on the way to Drumcree Church every July since 1998.

Garvaghy Road residents say they will mobilise and come together if the parade is allowed through their community. Linda O’Neill of the Garvaghy Road Residents Coalition said their plan A is that the parade will not go down the road. She said the community is also concerned about a large gathering of bands in the town on Saturday night, and that Monday morning will bring consequences whether or not the parade passes. If the decision goes against them, she said, the community will mobilise and come up with a plan B, and she added that residents have been let down before. In the 1990s the Drumcree parade brought violent clashes and a major security operation after residents rejected the march. People living on the road met politicians in Leinster House in Dublin on Thursday.

Both sides accept that the Commission, not the Order or the residents, decides on Friday, that the application is for 60 people on Sunday, and that the July march has been blocked on this road since 1998. The next dated step is that Friday ruling; the Sunday procession follows only if the Commission allows it. Source: [irishtimes.com](https://www.irishtimes.com/ireland/2026/09/25/parades-commission-set-to-rule-on-orange-orders-application-for-drumcree-march/)

Poland says fire at Starlink station is sabotage as Denmark warns of rising Russian threat: BBC Europe

Denmark’s Defence Intelligence Service said in a new assessment that it expects Russia to escalate hybrid warfare in the coming months, that an attack remains highly unlikely, and that there is a low but growing risk of a limited military attack on Nato countries. Both Sides: The Parthenon sculptures

What is being decided is whether the Parthenon sculptures in the British Museum return permanently to Athens, and whether that step is for the museum or for Parliament.

Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis, writing in The Guardian, says the strongest reason to act is that the pieces are one monument and should be seen together in the Acropolis Museum, opened in 2009 with a gallery built for them. He says what is needed is the political will and courage to reunite the sculptures and turn a historic dispute into cultural friendship. He rejects any deal in which Greece would recognise the British Museum’s ownership, or borrow what he calls parts of its own monument. A Bayeux-style loan, he writes, would repeat the split: nobody would cut what survives of that tapestry in two and show half in London and half in Bayeux. He notes that Andy Burnham had previously called for the sculptures’ return with no strings attached.

The case against a permanent return, as put by the museum and by critics in the reporting, starts from the law., so a permanent transfer would need MPs. Director Nicholas Cullinan told the Financial Times last month that the Bayeux Tapestry loan had given hope that some tangible progress can happen. A spokesperson told AFP that the museum’s openness to a deal is unchanged, and did not confirm a Daily Telegraph report that the Greek side had walked away from years of talks over a lack of progress. The museum’s website says no loan request has been received. Burnham, who replaced Keir Starmer in July, told the Financial Times in August that the question is for the British Museum, the government’s longstanding position. Critics warn that moving the marbles could set a precedent for other objects held by UK museums.

Both accounts accept that the museum has held the friezes for more than two centuries and that the Bayeux loan has put cross-border cultural deals back in view. Greece says it. The museum says it remains open to a deal. Source: [france24.com](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20260924-greek-pm-urges-courage-in-talks-with-uk-over-parthenon-marbles)

What This Show Covers

One region, every day, told straight: what was decided, what happened, what it changes and for whom. One contested question is presented with the strongest case on each side, and one measurable sign of progress is noted when the day supplies one.

This desk covers the European Union and the UK, Ireland, Norway, Switzerland and Iceland, the Balkans, Ukraine, Russia and Belarus, Turkey and the Caucasus. A story whose primary actor or place lies outside the region appears only when it produces a concrete consequence inside it, and that consequence is stated first.

The brief is read by Mira, the network’s AI host. Stories are chosen and written by software from named newsrooms, and every item names its source. The one thing it will not do is take a side: every claim is attributed, and no party, government or people is praised or mocked.

Other Omni View desks cover their own regions and Top World News covers the rest of the globe. Omni View Europe is part of the Nerra Network, an independent, ad-free network of shows at nerranetwork.com. Listeners are invited to return each day for the same format.

Lead

Italian parliament backs Meloni’s plan to restart nuclear power: The Mighty 790 KFGO

Italy’s Senate gave final approval on Wednesday to legislation that lets the government restart nuclear power generation almost 40 years after a 1987 referendum closed the country’s reactors. The law requires the government to adopt implementing decrees within 12 months and creates an independent regulator while setting rules for decommissioning old facilities. Energy Minister Gilberto Pichetto Fratin said sustainable nuclear power together with renewables will make Italy a safe, independent and competitive country. The government estimates nuclear could reach at least 11 percent of the energy mix and save €17 billion in decarbonisation costs by 2050, with the national plan projecting a possible rise to 22 percent. Centre-left opposition parties opposed the measure and called for any new plants to be sited only on already developed or degraded land. A June poll by Demopolis found 51 percent support for restarting nuclear generation overall, though a majority opposed construction of a plant near their homes. The government argues that technological and safety advances have rendered the 1987 vote obsolete and that the move will shield the economy from energy-price surges linked to conflicts in the Middle East. Anti-nuclear campaigners maintain that safety concerns and lengthy construction times outweigh the benefits, and Italy currently relies heavily on energy imports including electricity from nuclear plants in neighbouring countries such as France. Five Star Movement senator Elena Sironi said any new plants should be located primarily in areas already developed, degraded or affected by decommissioned industrial activities without consuming additional land. Source: [kfgo.com](https://kfgo.com/2026/09/23/italian-parliament-backs-melonis-plan-to-restart-nuclear-power/)

Across the Region

£84 million to boost Plymouth's defence growth plans: GOV.UK

The UK government confirmed release of £50 million through the Defence Growth Deal for Plymouth, unlocking up to £34 million more from industry and local partners to create a National Centre for Marine Autonomy. The package is expected to create nearly 1,000 jobs, support 500 people into work and benefit more than 400 UK businesses in robotics, artificial intelligence and autonomous systems. Defence Minister Lord Vernon Coaker approved Team Plymouth’s strategic business case and announced the investment at Market Hall in Plymouth. The National Centre for Marine Autonomy is expected to create 120 jobs and attract more than 30 businesses including start-ups and scale-ups. A separate £1.5 million Get Plymouth Working scheme will help 500 people into jobs in defence, advanced manufacturing, engineering and construction with a focus on veterans and young people aged 18 to 25. Around 750 people will gain new technical skills and qualifications while more than 7,000 young people will benefit from STEM training. Lord Coaker said the investment strengthens national security while backing British jobs, businesses and communities. Source: [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/84-million-to-boost-plymouths-defence-growth-plans)

Germany commits for first time to phasing out fossil fuels by 2045: The Guardian Europe

Germany published a roadmap that for the first time sets an explicit target of transitioning away from fossil fuels by 2045, alongside raising renewable electricity from 55 percent to 80 percent by 2030 and cutting methane emissions 30 percent by 2030. The plan reaffirms the coal exit by 2038 with a possible earlier date of 2035 and adds 12 GW of onshore wind plus a 215 GW solar target by 2030. Andreas Sieber of 350.org called the explicit phase-out commitment a notable shift but warned that the economic ministry continues to push for greater use of gas. Former climate envoy Jennifer Morgan said the strategy must be paired with faster rollout of green electrification in transport and heating. The push for heat pumps to replace gas boilers faced attacks from the far-right after a 2023 heating law that would have banned most oil and gas installations. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/sep/23/germany-commits-for-first-time-to-phasing-out-fossil-fuels-by-2045)

Finland and Ukraine sign Drone Deal to boost their defence and security cooperation: patriagroup.com

Finland and Ukraine signed an agreement to expand defence and security cooperation through joint drone development and production. The deal aims to strengthen both countries’ capabilities in unmanned aerial systems amid ongoing regional security challenges. Kosovo Court Orders MPs to Elect President by October 6: Balkan Insight

Kosovo’s Constitutional Court set an October 6 deadline for parliament to elect a new president or face fresh snap parliamentary elections. The ruling requires MPs to complete the election process within the specified timeframe to avoid dissolution of the assembly. Both Sides: Restarting nuclear power in Italy

Italy’s Senate approved legislation that authorises the government to restart nuclear power generation. The government argues that advanced modular reactors will help decarbonise industry and strengthen energy security while shielding the economy from price surges caused by conflicts in the Middle East. Energy Minister Gilberto Pichetto Fratin stated that sustainable nuclear power together with renewables will make Italy a safe, independent and competitive country, and the government estimates the move could save €17 billion in decarbonisation costs by 2050 with the share potentially rising to 22 percent under the national energy and climate plan. The administration maintains that technological and safety advances have made the 1987 referendum obsolete and that reduced dependence on foreign energy supplies will protect households and businesses. Anti-nuclear campaigners counter that safety concerns and the long construction times outweigh any benefits, noting that a 1987 referendum led to the closure of existing reactors and that many Italians still oppose building plants near their homes. A June Demopolis poll found 51 percent support for restarting nuclear generation overall but a majority opposed to local construction. Centre-left opposition parties, including the Five Star Movement, urged that any new plants be limited to already developed or degraded land without consuming additional territory. Senator Elena Sironi said new facilities should be located primarily in areas already developed, degraded or affected by decommissioned industrial activities. The law gives the government 12 months to adopt the necessary implementing decrees and to establish an independent regulator. Both sides accept that Italy currently relies heavily on energy imports, including electricity from nuclear plants in neighbouring countries such as France.
