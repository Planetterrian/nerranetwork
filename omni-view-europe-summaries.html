Both Sides: Lethal conditions on the Polish-Belarusian border

The contested question is whether deaths in the Polish-Belarusian borderlands since 2021 are the result of policies that knowingly exposed migrants to lethal conditions, as a report published on September 8 argues, or whether that account distorts the picture, as Poland’s Interior Ministry says.

The case for the report is led by Katarzyna Czarnota, who researched that border for the Helsinki Foundation for Human Rights from 2021 to 2024 and is a lead researcher on The Death Zone, published by Border Forensics with two research projects. She says hypothermia describes how a person died, not why they were exposed to lethal conditions, and recalls a dispatcher refusing an ambulance for three boys, aged 16 or 17, in the Bialowieza forest after days of eating leaves and drinking from a puddle, because they were “illegal migrants.” Charles Heller, the report’s director of research, says people crossing remain trapped for weeks or months in cycles of push-forwards and pushbacks. Drawing on testimonies, medical data, field research, maps and geostatistics, the researchers examined 102 documented deaths and 214 fragmentary reports of potential disappearances, and allege that policies, infrastructure and enforcement on both sides “knowingly exposed” migrants to conditions already shown to be lethal.

The Interior Ministry’s stated case is that the report distorts the picture. Official records, as Czarnota describes them, stop at physiological causes — hypothermia, drowning, cardiac arrest — which in ordinary language make the cold or the river the culprit rather than the person who left someone there.

Both sides are speaking about deaths that have occurred on that border; the report’s documented count is 102. The article names no dated official review. Source: [balkaninsight.com](https://balkaninsight.com/2026/09/30/poland-belarus-borderlands-how-a-forest-is-made-lethal/rd/)
