# When has the guest finished? (Oct 28 2026)

Patrick, after the Oct 28 test room: Mira waits about four seconds after
every answer (4.4 s median from the guest's last word to her first, on that
room and on Glenn Broder's). He asked whether the guest's own cues, on camera
and in the voice, can tell the room an answer is over sooner without ever
cutting in. This is what 22 recorded rooms say, measured with
`pipelines/voices/turn_cues.py`. Nothing here changed the room.

## Method

Every pause of 0.25 s or more in the guest's speech (per-speaker tracks,
speech found against each track's own noise) is labelled by what happened
next: the guest went on (a **hold**), the guest stopped for at least one
second and the host spoke without the guest coming back over them (an
**end**), or the room decided for them (set aside: 134). 10,433 holds and
643 ends from 22 rooms (Sept 10 to Oct 26). At each pause the tool measures
what the room could have known at that moment: the voice in the half second
before it (pitch slope, how low the last pitch sits in the speaker's range,
voiced share, energy fade, how long the last voiced stretch ran), how long
the answer had run, and, where per-word audio was transcribed, the last words.
Cues are scored room by room by a model trained on the OTHER rooms.

## What it found

1. **Most mid-answer pauses are short, but a few guests pause long, often.**
   Holds: median 0.48 s, 95% under 1.56 s, 99% under 3.98 s. Over 2 s:
   294 of 10,433. They are not spread evenly: most rooms have 0-11 pauses
   over 2 s in the hour, and run c868dac8 (the Oct 22 room) has 80,
   checked by hand against Mira's track (none is her speech; they are the
   guest thinking). One timer cannot serve both
   kinds of guest, which is why every timer change helped one room and hurt
   the next.
2. **The voice does not tell "done" from "thinking".** At a pause still
   silent one second in, no voice cue separates ends from holds (AUC 0.48 to
   0.55 alone; a model of all of them scores 0.55 to 0.61 on rooms it never
   saw). A falling pitch is as common before a thinking pause as before the
   end of an answer. **No prosody rule should go into the room.**
3. **Neither do the words, mostly.** On the two rooms transcribed so far
   (Glenn Broder's, d0cdb835, and b579faba; 237 pauses over 1 s), the room's own
   unfinished-clause rule reads every real end as finished (it never delays
   an end) and catches 19 of 167 long thinking pauses (11%). The other 89%
   come after a complete sentence. Guests think between sentences, which is
   exactly where a transcript says "finished". The rule is worth keeping; it
   cannot be the answer.
4. **A wait learned from the guest's own pauses helps a little.** Waiting a
   fixed 3.0 s would cut into 22 holds per 100 answers. Learning each
   guest's habit as the hour goes (the 98th percentile of their own pauses
   so far plus 0.8 s, between 1.5 and 4 s, 3 s until twenty pauses are
   heard) cuts into 21.5 per 100 at a mean wait of 2.84 s: the same safety,
   0.2 s sooner on average, and much sooner for a guest who never pauses
   (their wait drops to 1.5-2 s) while a guest who thinks in long pauses
   gets the full 4 s. That is the change the data supports, and it is a
   room change, so it goes through a candidate rehearsal and a listen first.
5. **The camera is the open question, and no room has a usable picture
   yet.** The Voximplant recordings carry no readable video (Glenn's: 16
   kbps, 502 gaps; the Oct 28 test room: no packets). The studio now records
   the guest's camera itself, and every test room records it. The cues to
   test are the ones the voice and the words cannot give: the guest looking
   back at the screen, settling, the face going still. Run
   `python pipelines/voices/turn_cues.py --run <test room id> --video
   --words` after the first test rooms with the camera on; a test room's
   takes are placed on the fly from its own recording. Five rooms of a
   person on camera is the first useful read.

## What the room does today, for reference

Server VAD silence 1.8 s, then the room's decision (0.5 s), one extra wait
of 1.5 s when the clause reads unfinished, and the voice API's time to its
first audio. The guest hears her after about 3.7 s (median over these
rooms' ends; 4.4 s on the Oct 28 test room).
