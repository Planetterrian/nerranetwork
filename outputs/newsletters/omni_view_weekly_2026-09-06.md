## The Week in Perspective

Direct fighting between the United States and Iran resumed after weeks of relative quiet, then moved onto the Strait of Hormuz — the narrow waterway through which a large share of the world's seaborne oil passes. On 31 August Iran reported that a U.S. strike on Larak Island killed two people and injured two others, the first known U.S. action against Iran since late July. By 2 September the two governments were exchanging attacks on bases. Iranian statements said a U.S. strike killed five people at a wedding party, and the Iranian Red Crescent said a child was among those killed when shrapnel hit a home; the U.S. military said it never targets civilians. By 6 September the fighting had reached three Iranian oil tankers and a U.S. naval drone near Hormuz. The BBC centered casualty figures and civilian claims. The Hindu placed the same events in a longer pattern of regional incidents. Reuters described a response cycle between the two governments. The South China Morning Post framed Iran's effort to hold the strait against a U.S. counter-blockade of Iranian ports.

Diplomacy ran on a separate clock. President Trump dispatched Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner first to Moscow and then to Kyiv with a proposal aimed at ending more than four years of war in Ukraine, which began with Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022. Prime Minister Narendra Modi attended the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO, a Eurasian security and economic grouping) summit in Kyrgyzstan on 31 August and 1 September. In India, large protests targeted the exam system that controls access to stable government jobs after years of preparation that often lead nowhere. Argentina's President Javier Milei announced sanctions on companies drilling near the Falkland Islands without Argentine approval and said Argentina would build a naval base in Tierra del Fuego, testing Britain's administration of the islands and reports that Washington has cast doubt on its traditional neutrality in the dispute.

In Washington, President Trump signed a last-minute funding bill that keeps federal agencies operating through the midterm elections — the U.S. congressional elections held in the middle of a president's term — avoiding a shutdown for air-traffic control, border security, and benefit processing. Russia ordered the closure of Germany's Goethe-Institut centers (the country's main cultural institutes abroad) in retaliation for German measures. Vietnam's exports and imports both reached new highs, according to the week's reporting, amid supply-chain shifts tied to artificial intelligence.

## Geopolitical Watch

Three tracks shifted this week. First, the U.S.–Iran lull ended and the fighting moved onto energy infrastructure: an island, bases, tankers, and a drone on the Hormuz approaches, while Washington continued a counter-blockade of Iranian ports. Second, the White House reopened a Ukraine diplomatic channel after years of stalemate, with envoys scheduled for both capitals; previous tracks have stalled on territory and alliance membership. Third, two older disputes were poked: Argentina's Falklands sanctions and naval-base pledge, and Russia's order to shut Germany's Goethe-Institut centers. The SCO summit in Kyrgyzstan put India, among other members, in the same room as a Eurasian grouping that presents itself as a counterweight to Western-led forums. None of these tracks produced a ceasefire, a signed deal, or a settled legal claim — only a change in the mix of force, envoys, and sanctions.

## Economic Signals

Oil-route risk rose as strikes reached three Iranian tankers and a drone near Hormuz and Kharg Island. The U.S. funding bill removed an immediate shutdown risk for federal pay, services, and economic-data releases ahead of the midterms. Vietnam's exports and imports both hit record levels amid AI-related supply-chain shifts, a reminder that trade rerouting continues even as security crises dominate headlines. Argentina's sanctions aim to deter unauthorized hydrocarbon work near the Falklands, adding legal risk to a long-running resource dispute. Washington, for its part, is still trying to squeeze Iran's economy through the port counter-blockade and related measures.

## Progress This Week

- President Trump signed a funding bill before the 1 October fiscal-year deadline that kept federal agencies open through the midterms and avoided furloughs of non-essential staff, covering pay for millions of employees and contractors — though the bill only moves the next spending confrontation past Election Day.
- Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner were dispatched with a peace proposal after more than four years of full-scale war, with a Kyiv stop confirmed by a senior Ukrainian official — though neither Kyiv nor Moscow has shown public movement on territory or security guarantees.

## Media Watch

On the first Larak Island strike, the BBC led with casualty counts and the end of a quiet spell; The Hindu led with the Strait of Hormuz and a longer chain of regional incidents. The facts overlapped; the emphasis did not. Later in the week, the BBC gave prominent space to Iranian statements about a wedding party and a child killed by shrapnel, while U.S. military sources were quoted saying they never target civilians and Reuters organized the story as a tit-for-tat response cycle. Independent verification of the civilian claims was not in the reporting that reached this briefing — a gap that matters more than the adjectives.
