**They solved the bottleneck they could see**

Seven cases this week show how solving the visible bottleneck left the deeper system almost untouched.

**By the numbers**

- **97%** — Gyps vulture collapse after diclofenac
- **100,000+** — extra human deaths a year in India
- **90%** — new LPG households still burning wood
- **7% → 43%** — Black–White callback gap after ban-the-box
- **24%** — drop in deer-vehicle collisions with wolves

There is a particular kind of competence that gets careful people into trouble: seeing the obvious missing piece, naming it, and supplying it. This week's seven episodes are stories of that skill meeting a system that was never waiting on a single piece. Each plan pulled the lever that sat in the light. The outcome was set by what the plan treated as background.

In 1927 Capt. E.B. Purefoy released thirty-eight Dutch Large Coppers onto Woodwalton Fen, a small National Trust reserve in Huntingdonshire. The English race, *Lycaena dispar dispar*, had vanished by 1851 after centuries of fen drainage. Purefoy planted great water dock in advance. The next summer more than a thousand adults crossed the sedge and pools. Habitat loss had caused the extinction, so habitat plus fresh stock should reverse it. What the surrounding farmland kept doing was draining the fen drier. The colony survived only because wardens bred and released fresh adults every season for more than sixty years. A year after the 1986 People Power revolt, Philippine lawmakers tried again to break up concentrated estates. President Corazon Aquino signed Republic Act 6657 on 10 June 1988. On her family's Hacienda Luisita in Tarlac, the aim was to move 4,900 hectares to the families who tilled them. Section 31 offered a stock-distribution option because officials believed corporate farms could remain efficient while still giving workers an ownership stake. Officials feared that carving up integrated plantations would wreck production. The Cojuangco family retained majority ownership. Farmworkers received shares instead of title. Portions of the estate later converted to industrial and commercial use. Land-to-the-tiller had become paper that left control where it had always sat. [▶ Episode 148 · 2026-10-20](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/unintended_consequences/ep148.html)

Not every second-order effect this week ran the wrong way. Gray wolves recolonized Wisconsin from Minnesota and Michigan in the mid-1970s under the Endangered Species Act. Managers were restoring an apex predator whose removal had let white-tailed deer climb. Then Jennifer Raynor, at the time a graduate student, followed the trail into human systems. In the winter of 2009 a radio-collared wolf scent-marked a county road that also served as a deer corridor. Within two years local deer shifted off that stretch. Across 29 Wisconsin counties, deer-vehicle collisions fell by about 24 percent. The recovery program had never put road safety in the ledger. The wolves did the accounting anyway. [▶ Episode 149 · 2026-10-21](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/unintended_consequences/ep149.html)

On 1 May 2016 Prime Minister Narendra Modi stood in Ballia, Uttar Pradesh, and launched the Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana: free LPG connections so women would no longer cook over wood and dung. Indoor smoke from solid fuels was killing hundreds of thousands a year, women and children first. The scheme treated the up-front cost—connection, regulator, first cylinder, up to Rs 1,600, registered in a woman's name—as the decisive barrier. By September 2019 more than eighty million connections had gone out. A 2018 survey of 4,994 forest-fringe households found that 90 percent of those who received LPG still collected and burned firewood every day. The cylinder in the courtyard was real. Refills were not free. Wood still was. Ownership of the hardware had been mistaken for a change in the daily fuel market. [▶ Episode 150 · 2026-10-22](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/unintended_consequences/ep150.html)

After diclofenac's patent expired in the early 1990s, Indian manufacturers sold cheap versions for cattle. Farmers finally had a way to ease pain that cut milk yields and draft power. Because the drug was already approved for people, extending it to livestock looked like a minor administrative step. No one tested it on the scavengers that cleaned the countryside. Within a decade the three Gyps vulture species that had numbered in the tens of millions fell by roughly 97 percent or more. Carcasses that once vanished in hours now rotted. Districts that had held the largest vulture populations recorded human death rates more than 4 percent higher than comparable districts without them—over 100,000 additional deaths a year. A veterinary kindness had removed the sanitation service no one had thought to price. [▶ Episode 151 · 2026-10-23](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/unintended_consequences/ep151.html)

Ban-the-box laws were written to give people with convictions a fairer first look. By the mid-2010s more than thirty states had versions of a rule Hawaii had begun in 1998. The logic was decent. Many employers used the checkbox as a blunt screen, and unemployment after prison raised the odds of reoffending. In 2018 the *Quarterly Journal of Economics* reported a field study of roughly 15,000 fictitious applications in New Jersey and New York City, before and after local rules. Where employers still asked, a record cut callbacks sharply. Once the box disappeared, the callback gap between otherwise identical white and Black applicants at the affected firms rose from 7 percent to 43 percent. Hiding the information did not stop employers from guessing. It changed what they guessed with. [▶ Episode 152 · 2026-10-24](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/unintended_consequences/ep152.html)

Israeli water planners, facing chronic shortage after statehood, treated every drop of municipal effluent as a resource. By the early 2000s more than 80 percent of wastewater went to farms. Then the Volcani Institute compared orchards after ten years of drip irrigation with treated wastewater. Avocado and citrus yields ran 20 to 30 percent lower than trees watered with freshwater in the same fields. Salts, sodium, and boron that secondary treatment left behind had accumulated in clay soils, cutting hydraulic conductivity and oxygen in the root zone. When researchers switched test plots back to freshwater, infiltration began to recover within months. Stretching supply had slowly salted the trees. Two principles keep surfacing. The first is that the lever you can count is rarely the system you need to change. Connections, share certificates, butterflies, checkboxes, cubic meters, treated cattle: the measurable input moved. The outcome that actually mattered—self-sustaining fen hydrology, cooking fuel used the next morning, control of land, callbacks that did not sort by race, soil that still took water, a scavenger guild that still ate the dead—lived one layer out, in a market, a landscape, or a set of beliefs the plan had treated as scenery.

The second is that time and the unprotected outside will finish the story. The surrounding pumps, the refill price, the employer's prior, the residual sodium, the vulture kidney: none of them signed the plan. The Wisconsin wolves are the mirror image, an effect that lived outside the official ledger, this time as a gift. We still design as if the boundary of the program were the boundary of the world. It is not. The world keeps a second set of books.

Modern versions of the same move are not hard to find. We still celebrate accounts opened rather than payments made, and dashboards that turn green when the form is submitted. The people who designed this week's cases were not careless. They were looking at a real wound and applying the tool that fit in their hand. The work is to ask what else has to be true for that tool to matter—and to budget for the thing that will still be true after the announcement.
