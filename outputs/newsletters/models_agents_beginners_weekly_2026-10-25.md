## This Week's Big Picture

The chat window is no longer the whole story. This week AI showed up as a four-player Minecraft party, a background assistant that keeps working after you close the app, a lab partner inventing proteins, and a design tool that sketches a layout before it draws. The common thread is collaboration: models talking to other models, tools that stay on the job without you watching, and everyday people describing what they want in plain English instead of waiting for a company to ship it.

Start with the spectacle. Four Gemini 4 agents — called Argon agents in the report — shared messages, flagged mistakes, and beat Minecraft's Ender Dragon with zero human instructions after the run began. That is the same week Anthropic put Claude Duet on a waitlist: an Opus 5.5 agent that lives on a cloud machine, reads Gmail and Slack, and pings you only when something needs a yes. One story is a game. The other is your morning. Both say the same thing: a single chatbot answering one prompt at a time is starting to look old.

Then the week split in two directions at once. Claude designed hundreds of thousands of new proteins aimed at scorpion neurotoxins, and 17 of 211 lab-tested designs actually bound toxins from Mexico, North Africa, and the Middle East. Canva released Craft, which plans a design before it generates pixels. A Gears of War fan used ChatGPT to build an offline toybox of mods. People pointed agents at their own smart TVs to cut ads and bloat. And an eighth-grader in Salt Lake City put ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini through 84 real teen emotional situations and found the replies often missed the feeling. Capability is racing ahead. Care is not.

## Top Stories

### Four Gemini agents beat the Ender Dragon

Four separate Gemini 4 agents played Minecraft as a team — exploring, gathering, fighting, and keeping the plan — until they defeated the Ender Dragon without a person steering after launch. They messaged one another with tips, warnings, and admissions of mistakes, learning from each other's successes and failures during the run. If helpers can share context and correct each other in real time, longer jobs stop being a single-chat problem and start looking like a study group you can actually assign work to.

### Claude Duet keeps working after you close the app

Anthropic's research preview of Claude Duet runs on cloud computers even when you are offline, connecting to Gmail, Slack, Stripe, Docusign, and Shopify through more than 6,000 marketplace connectors. Every morning it sends a short summary, then reaches out only when something needs attention; it asks for approval before sending messages or making payments, and bank transfers are blocked entirely. Built on Claude Opus 5.5 as a standalone app for iOS, Android, and the web — Pro and Max subscribers in the US can join a waitlist — it is the clearest sign yet that AI is moving from something you open on purpose to something that can quietly handle parts of your day.

### Claude designed proteins that bind scorpion venom

Anthropic's Claude proposed hundreds of thousands of brand-new protein shapes meant to stick to deadly scorpion neurotoxins instead of copying ones that already exist in nature. Researchers tested 211 designs in real labs, and 17 bound tightly to toxins including Cn2, Ts1, AaH II, AaH I, and LqhIII, with binding measured by Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience using surface plasmon resonance. These are early-stage research results, not medicine — but they show AI compressing the idea stage of antidote science, which usually takes years of trial and error, into a partner that invents molecular keys from scratch.

### Canva Craft plans the picture before it draws

Canva's first in-house image model, Craft, builds a layout first — where objects go, how big they are, where text belongs — then generates the image, at up to 2K and more than 30 aspect ratios. Training on Canva's own template library of posters, social graphics, presentations, and infographics, rather than mostly photographs, makes text more readable and elements easier to edit later because they stay separate. For students and creators who already live in Canva, that two-step approach is the difference between a blob you cannot fix and a design you can actually rearrange.

### An eighth-grader's empathy test found the gap

Salt Lake City student Ellianna Gu ran ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini through 84 teen situations — friendship drama, school pressure, family arguments, self-doubt — and found replies that were often generic, overly formal, or too quick to jump from feeling to advice. Even when the facts were right, the tone often felt off, like texting a friend who always sends the safest possible answer. As more students use these tools for homework and late-night questions, her project is a reminder that helpful is not the same as heard.

## Trend Watch

**Agents that team up — and stay on.** Minecraft's four-agent party and Claude Duet's always-on cloud worker are the same idea at different scales: split the job, share state, and only interrupt the human when it matters. ChatGPT's teammate-splitting feature and its move into the browser point the same way. The products to watch next are the ones that assume you have a crew of models, not one oracle in a tab.

**Plain English as a build tool.** A longtime Gears of War fan used GPT-6 Astra to ship Gears Toybox — 365 playable appearances in Gears 5, custom loadouts, Horde tweaks, and offline bots — without a coding class, all strictly offline. Canva Craft is the design-world version of that loop: plan, generate, edit. The pattern is not learn-to-program-first. It is describe what you wished existed, test it, report what broke, and ask for the next fix.

**Science is speeding up; social intelligence is not.** The same week Claude invented proteins that bound real toxins, three flagship chatbots failed a teenager's empathy exam. That split will define the next product cycle: more autonomy in email, labs, games, and shopping, and more pressure to sound like a friend instead of a FAQ.
