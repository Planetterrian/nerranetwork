## This Week's Big Picture

This was a week of spectacle and subtraction. Tesla finally put the next-generation Roadster through a ramp run, a mid-air flip, and a kickflip—ten cold-gas nozzles, 250 kilograms of compressed gas, three trims that bottom out at a claimed sub-0.59-second 0–60—while confirming Solar Roof is over. The tiles stopped selling in August; the product page now redirects to ordinary panels. After seven years and roughly 3,000 U.S. installs against a 1,000-per-week target, the miss is north of 95 percent. One program went Hollywood. The other went to a redirect.

The week's first print was $382.70 on Monday. By Friday's episode it was $362.44, and the weekend recaps sat at $354.72—about 7.3 percent below Monday, and the low of the week—with almost no bounce on the demo. The tape had plenty else to price: NHTSA reviewing video of robotaxis allegedly speeding in school zones, a separate look at Cybercab self-certification, Korean insurers at a 107.6 percent EV loss ratio after Tesla sales tripled, and a China print that was both trophy and warning. Model Y was China's best-selling car in September. Tesla's China sales still fell 9.35 percent year over year. In Texas, 319 Cybercabs are registered, Model Y robotaxis are frozen at 420, and 739 robotaxis sit on the app—while the agencies circle the same fleet.

The through-line is concentration. Tesla is pouring attention into robotaxis, silicon, and a halo sports car, pruning an energy product that never hit volume, and absorbing the regulatory cost of looking unsupervised while the software is still, legally, supervised. Energy did not vanish—a South African estate went off-grid on five Powerwalls and 86 panels, and Superchargers are landing at 21 Korean highway rest areas—but the center of gravity was vehicles, thrusters, and whoever is watching them.

## Top Stories

**Solar Roof is finished.** Tesla stopped selling the tiles in August 2026 with no announcement. Reuters put U.S. volume at about 3,000 installations over seven years versus a goal of 1,000 per week. Integrated tiles have to be a roof and a power plant at once—codes, fire, waterproofing, custom sizes. The cheaper-than-a-roof-plus-panels claim never showed up in the install count. **The Roadster performed, with caveats.** On October 15 Tesla ran the postponed demo: upside-down on a curved ramp, a mid-air flip, a kickflip over a lineup that included the Semi. Ten nozzles and about 250 kilograms of compressed gas supply the stunts; the tank can replenish after a recharge. Three configurations followed—Performance under 1.6 seconds 0–60 with active aero and suction, Thrust under 0.9 with rear thrusters, Rocket under 0.59 and over 230 mph with perimeter thrusters. Hidden headlights, yoke steer-by-wire, magnesium/titanium/carbon, 300-plus miles of range, production now 2028. Musk warned that success was not guaranteed. ## Elon Watch

The Roadster stream was the Elon event: experimental cold-gas propulsion, a public warning that the stunts might not work, and a 2028 date that puts the car outside this decade's delivery math. The demo revived a serious-sounding joke—whether perimeter thrusters pull the car toward aircraft rules. Treat that as color, not a filing. The industrial headline was Tesla pressing Samsung on price and capacity for AI5 and AI6 inference silicon. Same company, same parts bin, four programs.

## Competitor Corner

BYD still owned China's September list even after Model Y took first—seven of the other top-ten spots, with Geely's EX2 in second. Hyundai and Kia remain the repair-cost benchmark Tesla is losing in Korea. Rivian and Lucid did not move the week. Samsung showed up as a supplier under pressure, not a rival on the road.

## Trend Watch

**The parts bin is the strategy.** Cybercab structure in the Semi, Optimus actuators in the Roadster—four programs, shared hardware, different regulators. That is how a halo car with 250 kilograms of gas becomes fundable.

**Scale and scrutiny are arriving together.** Texas fleet counts are up; Model Y robotaxis are being capped for purpose-built Cybercabs. In the same seven days: NHTSA on school zones and Cybercab certification, a sleeping driver on supervised FSD injuring two CHP officers ([▶ Episode 637 · 2026-10-17](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/tesla/ep637.html)), the Ninth Circuit tossing three supervised-FSD class actions, FSD Supervised v14 Lite reaching Intel Model S and X, and Slovakia's first supervised drive a day after approval. Legal wins and safety headlines are stacking, not sequencing.

**Energy is splitting in two.** Solar Roof is closed. Powerwall plus commodity panels still does real off-grid work, and Superchargers keep filling highway rest stops. Storage and charging survived. Custom photovoltaic roofing did not.
