## Strategy of the Week

**Covered calls on a Canadian equity ETF you already own.**

This was the most usable idea in a week full of macro noise ([▶ Episode 206 · 2026-10-20](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/modern_investing/ep206.html)). If you do nothing else, do this.

1. **What it is.** You hold a broad Canadian equity ETF inside a TFSA and sell monthly out-of-the-money calls against it, targeting about 1–2% premium per month — cash flow without selling the shares.
2. **When to use it.** Sideways or mildly bullish markets with elevated vol. That’s this tape: BoC uncertainty, a chopping NASDAQ, and you still want income from banks and utilities you refuse to dump.
3. **How to set it up.**
   - **Wealthsimple:** Buy XIU or VCN in the TFSA. If that account can’t sell covered calls, keep the stock leg there and write the option at Questrade or IBKR.
   - **Questrade:** Enable options, buy 100-share lots, pull the ~30-day chain, sell calls ~3–5% out of the money (one contract per 100 shares).
   - **IBKR:** Same structure; target delta around 0.25–0.30 and never overwrite more than you’re willing to have called away.

Main risk: the market rips and shares get called. That’s opportunity cost, not a blow-up. Pair it with monthly-pay iShares distributions if you want a second income layer. BlackRock Canada’s October iShares payouts are the reminder that the overlay works best as a complement, not a substitute for owning the thing.

## Canadian Corner

The 3.1% print is a portfolio problem. Canadian dividend investors stuffed banks, REITs, and utilities into TFSAs because the distributions are tax-free there. Still true — TFSA income isn’t taxed — but the *prices* of those names are rate-sensitive, and a hold-or-hike BoC reprices them whether the dividend is tax-free or not. CIBC ~7% below $170.50 after a $1 billion long-dated note deal ([▶ Episode 207 · 2026-10-21](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/modern_investing/ep207.html)) is the market charging a funding-cost discount. Watchlist it; don’t blind-dip it.

Two account-placement rules for this week:
- **US dividends belong in the RRSP** when you can. The treaty generally waives withholding there; in a TFSA the 15% US withholding is gone for good.
- **US growth and AI (Meta, Microsoft, the capex screen) are fine in the TFSA.** You’re not clipping a fat dividend, so withholding is small and the room is better used on compounding.

CAD/USD is the stealth risk: a hawkish BoC can firm the loonie and make unhedged US names look worse in CAD even if NASDAQ is flat. FHSA holders targeting a 2027 purchase should treat CMHC’s “variable-rate hikes starting early 2027” as a calendar, not a headline. If you want income without piling on more bank duration, the covered-call overlay — and vehicles like Middlefield’s infrastructure dividend ETF (MINF) — is how you stay paid while Wednesday does its thing ([▶ Episode 209 · 2026-10-23](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/modern_investing/ep209.html)).
