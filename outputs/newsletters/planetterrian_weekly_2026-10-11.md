## This Week in Science & Health

The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine went to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann, and Georg Nagel for optogenetics, the method that uses a light-gated algal protein, channelrhodopsin, to turn defined neurons on or off in milliseconds. It is a methods prize: it does not approve a therapy, but it is why many circuit-level claims about thirst, fear, and parental behavior can now be causal rather than correlative. [▶ Episode 205 · 2026-10-06](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep205.html)

Closer to the clinic, two results deserve a careful reading. Researchers at the University of British Columbia and the University of Toronto traced a previously unnamed route—the posterior ocular lymphatic outflow, or POLO pathway—that carries fluid and metabolic waste from the mouse retina into the lymphatic system. The retina is among the most metabolically active tissues in the body; how it clears byproducts has been an incomplete chapter in glaucoma and macular degeneration research. The finding is anatomical and preclinical. [▶ Episode 210 · 2026-10-11](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep210.html) Separately, a head-to-head look at supplement forms found that vitamin D2 lowers circulating vitamin D3, the sunlight-derived species prior work links more strongly to antimicrobial defense. People who treat vitamin D as a single interchangeable nutrient should not. [▶ Episode 207 · 2026-10-08](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep207.html)

The rest of the week filled in smaller, still useful pieces: a comfortable twenty-minute walk briefly increases intestinal motility; naturally occurring dairy trans fats showed no association with heart disease or type 2 diabetes in a 22-study analysis; large-dog genomes implicated jumping-gene changes in shorter lifespans; and E-cadherin, the epithelial adhesion protein, also helps cell sheets engulf dead neighbors without opening the barrier. ## Top Stories

**Optogenetics takes the medicine Nobel.** Hegemann and Nagel identified channelrhodopsin; Deisseroth expressed it in rat and mouse neurons and drove spikes with light. The method maps circuits with cell-type and millisecond precision. Human use is still limited by gene delivery and getting light into tissue. Deisseroth, 54, a Stanford professor and HHMI investigator, made school lunches after the call—an incidental human detail that does not change the science. [▶ Episode 205 · 2026-10-06](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep205.html)

**A lymphatic drain at the back of the eye.** Direct tracing in mice defined the POLO pathway as a posterior route for retinal fluid and waste. Dr. Neeru Gupta framed it as an explanation for how the eye flushes metabolic load. It does not alter current glaucoma drops or surgery; it adds a structure that disease models have not been testing. [▶ Episode 210 · 2026-10-11](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep210.html)

**Vitamin D2 can pull down D3.** In participants, D2 intake reduced the body’s D3. If you supplement because of low 25-hydroxyvitamin D, the form on the label (ergocalciferol vs cholecalciferol) is part of the decision, not a footnote. This is not a reason to megadose either form. [▶ Episode 207 · 2026-10-08](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep207.html)

**Dairy trans fats: no signal for CVD or diabetes.** Across 22 studies, trans fats native to milk, butter, yogurt, and cheese were not linked to heart disease, stroke, cardiovascular death, or type 2 diabetes. Industrial trans fats from partial hydrogenation are a different molecule class and a different risk story. Do not confuse the two. [▶ Episode 207 · 2026-10-08](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep207.html)

**Jumping genes and large-dog aging.** Genome comparisons across breeds found alterations around transposable elements that correlate with faster aging in bigger dogs—a molecular correlate of a familiar veterinary fact. Causal tests and any human parallel remain open. [▶ Episode 209 · 2026-10-10](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep209.html)

## Research Spotlight

**POLO: mapping waste clearance from the retina**

**Investigators.** University of British Columbia (Dr. Neeru Gupta, head of ophthalmology and visual sciences) and the University of Toronto. [▶ Episode 210 · 2026-10-11](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep210.html)

**Approach.** The group traced outflow in mice and showed fluid and waste leaving the posterior eye into lymphatics, naming the route posterior ocular lymphatic outflow (POLO). Standard glaucoma teaching emphasizes anterior drainage through the trabecular meshwork and uveoscleral flow. A posterior lymphatic limb was not part of that schematic.

**Findings.** The retina’s high metabolic rate implies a clearance problem. POLO offers a physical answer: at least some load can exit posteriorly into the lymphatic network. Coverage this week presented the result as a challenge to long-standing assumptions about ocular fluid handling, with possible future relevance to glaucoma and macular degeneration.

**Limits.** Mice are not people. Tracing demonstrates a path, not its quantitative share of total clearance, its behavior under high intraocular pressure, or its failure in disease. No human histology, imaging, or outcome data were in this week’s reports. Therapeutic language would be premature.

**For patients and the field.** No change in screening or treatment. The useful next steps are independent replication, quantification, and tests in human tissue or in vivo imaging. If POLO is real and sizable in people, it becomes a variable that existing drugs and surgeries have never been scored against.

## Longevity Corner

**Comparative aging.** The large-versus-small dog lifespan gap is one of the cleaner natural experiments in mammals. Linking it to transposable-element landscapes is a hypothesis generator, not a supplement target. [▶ Episode 209 · 2026-10-10](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep209.html)

**Vitamin D, form first.** Practical step: read whether your product is D3 or D2. If you are plant-based and using D2, ask a clinician about algae- or lichen-derived D3 and about which 25-OH-D assay your lab uses. Do not stack high-dose D2 on the assumption it is the same vitamin. [▶ Episode 207 · 2026-10-08](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep207.html)

**Motility, not a protocol.** A 20-minute comfortable walk increased bowel-sound frequency, strength, and duration within 1–2 minutes; the effect faded within minutes. That supports walking as a short-acting motility nudge for constipation-prone days. It does not treat obstruction, IBD flares, or unexplained weight loss. [▶ Episode 204 · 2026-10-05](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep204.html)

Epithelial housekeeping also belongs on the aging-biology shelf: live imaging in zebrafish and mouse embryos showed E-cadherin lets sheets reshape their basal surface to engulf corpses while the apical barrier stays sealed—local debris clearance without calling in immune cells. ## Clinical Pipeline

No FDA actions or registrational trial results were in this week’s roundups. Treat the following as research, not pipeline catalysts:

- Optogenetics: still a laboratory method; gene delivery and light hardware remain the bottlenecks for any human circuit therapy. [▶ Episode 205 · 2026-10-06](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep205.html)
- POLO: preclinical anatomy until human confirmation. [▶ Episode 210 · 2026-10-11](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep210.html)
- PFAS-degrading enzymes: Murdoch University’s Bioplastics Innovation Hub used machine learning to mine natural enzyme space for forever-chemical and microplastic breakdown. That is environmental biotechnology. PhD candidate Joseph Boctor noted agricultural soils can hold roughly 23 times more microplastics than oceans; hormone-mimic concerns motivate cleanup, not a drug candidate. [▶ Episode 205 · 2026-10-06](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep205.html)

Nanoparticle-formulated gibberellic acid raised grape cluster weight 104% and length 67% versus free hormone in field tests—efficient agrochemical delivery, not a human IND. [▶ Episode 206 · 2026-10-07](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/planetterrian/ep206.html)
