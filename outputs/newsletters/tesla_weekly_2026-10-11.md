## This Week's Big Picture

Tesla spent the week proving two things at once: the car business still has volume, and the compute story is getting funded like it is the main event. Third-quarter deliveries landed at 486,532 against 464,391 units produced, with Model 3 and Model Y accounting for 478,237 of those cars. That print sat next to a 2026 capital plan of more than $25 billion aimed at AI compute, robotaxis, Optimus, chips, and factories. The stock noticed. TSLA was quoted at $370.59 on Monday after a 4.7% jump on AI-chip patents and a thirty-billion-dollar credit line, and it finished the week at $382.70.

The arc underneath was vertical integration with a regulatory haircut. Four patent applications on base-2 softmax math showed how Tesla wants inference to draw less power on car chips. Elon Musk then shut down the Terafab rumor mill: Tesla and SpaceX will build and operate the plant, TSMC can sublease, and Intel stays on design, build, and packaging. In Europe, years of criticism produced a label change. Full Self-Driving (Supervised) is now Tesla Assisted Driving, and original Summon is Dumb Summon.

Product and energy kept moving around those headlines. Model Y is crowding the Kia Sorento for South Korea's overall sales crown even as FSD stays tightly limited there. China-spec Model 3s got a 16-inch screen and 2.2 kW of vehicle-to-load that North America did not. Pilot opened the first of more than a dozen Tesla Megacharger sites for the Semi, and a Dutch storage developer taking gigawatt-hour projects into Germany quietly widened the Megapack path in Europe.

## Elon Watch

Musk was blunt on two fronts. On robotaxis, he said gray kittens are grounding the Austin service at night; hours moved from 10 p.m. to 11 p.m., and the problem he named was making sure the cars do not run over pets. On Terafab, he left no room for TSMC running the plant. Ownership is not in doubt: Tesla and SpaceX will build and operate it, TSMC may sublease part of the complex, and Intel remains involved in design, build, and packaging. That closed speculation after reports that TSMC was exploring a larger operating role. ## Competitor Corner

Hyundai Motor is counting on new SUVs and a small EV to reverse its weakest sales since the pandemic, with Tesla's Model Y doing much of the damage in South Korea and Kia's Sorento the name on the leaderboard. Rivian sales climbed 46 percent — a strong rate from a much smaller base, and a reminder that the rest of the U.S. EV pack is not frozen in place.

## Trend Watch

**Compute is becoming the product.** Base-2 softmax patents, a $30 billion credit line, a $25 billion-plus 2026 capex plan, Terafab ownership, and a forecast that humanoid-robot DRAM demand rises twenty-fold by 2030 all point the same way. Cars still pay the bills. Chips and memory are where the story is compounding. [▶ Episode 631 · 2026-10-11](https://nerranetwork.com/blog/tesla/ep631.html)

**Software names are being sanded down in Europe.** Autopilot already got renamed once. FSD (Supervised) is now Tesla Assisted Driving. Summon is Dumb Summon. The stack did not change. The labels did, under pressure Tesla is not getting in the United States — at least not yet.

**Energy and truck networks are catching up.** Pilot Megachargers, Giga Storage's German GWh push, and a sixteen-stall A303 Supercharger plan are unglamorous, and they are how you industrialize the rest of the lineup.

## What to Watch Next Week

Tesla's Q3 2026 earnings report is scheduled for October 21. That is the print that will test whether this week's delivery beat and the $25 billion capex figure hold up under a full P&L. An EU FSD vote remains on the calendar after the Netherlands approval, which will show whether Assisted Driving is a naming exercise or a path to broader access.
