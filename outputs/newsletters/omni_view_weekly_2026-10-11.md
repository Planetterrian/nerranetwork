## The Week in Perspective

Governments from Sanaa to Taipei spent the week raising the price of waiting. Yemen's internationally recognised government opened a campaign against territory held by the Houthis — the Iran-aligned movement that controls much of northern Yemen — after rebels cut the main road between Taiz and Aden. In Taipei, President Lai Ching-te announced a record 2027 defence budget, more than 3 percent of GDP, as China kept up military and grey-zone pressure across the Taiwan Strait. North Korea's state media condemned U.S. fighter-jet deliveries to Taiwan and restated Kim Jong Un's support for Russia. In Canada, the Parti Québécois, Quebec's main separatist party, won the most seats in a provincial election and pledged another independence referendum, even without a majority.

Where outlets disagreed, they disagreed on motive, not on the core facts. Deutsche Welle treated Yemen's offensive as a reply to a severed supply line; Al Jazeera treated the same announcement as an escalation that could pull in regional powers already in the war. On Taiwan's budget, The Hindu framed the spending as a signal of seriousness to the United States, Taiwan's main security backer; Al Jazeera emphasised Lai's insistence that any U.S.–China talks respect Taiwan's right to exist. Quebec's result revived a debate last settled, narrowly, in 1995 — and immediately ran into minority arithmetic and Canadian rules that require federal involvement for any binding vote.

Nature and markets set their own terms: two major earthquakes in Panama, school-closing haze over Malaysia, a weakening hurricane on the U.S. Gulf coast, a sharp TSMC sales jump, and a possible U.S. skip of an Asia-Pacific finance meeting in favour of China trade talks.

## Top Stories

### 1) Yemen's government opens a "battle for liberation"

Yemen's government announced a campaign to retake Houthi-held areas after the Taiz–Aden road was cut, calling it a "battle for liberation." Later in the week it urged tribes in Houthi-controlled regions to join, citing limited advances along the Red Sea coast, in Marib and in al-Jawf.

Deutsche Welle presented the move as a response to disrupted internal supply lines. Al Jazeera framed it as an escalation that could draw in additional regional actors. Both agreed the targets have been Houthi-held for years; they differed on whether the timing is mainly Yemeni or mainly external.

### 2) Quebec separatists win — without a majority

The Parti Québécois took the most seats on Monday but fell short of a majority in the 127-seat National Assembly, winning nearly 60. Leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon said voters want the party to work with others while still advancing sovereignty, and he ruled out a referendum while Donald Trump remains U.S. president.

Deutsche Welle and AP News framed the win as a return of long-dormant separatist momentum, while noting the limits of minority status. Al Jazeera covered the renewed push for a third referendum. Federalist parties and polls showing roughly 30 percent support for independence already signal resistance. Any binding vote would still require federal approval or negotiated terms.

### 3) Taiwan sets a record defence budget

Lai announced a record NT$1.1 trillion ($34.5 billion) defence allocation for 2027, saying the aim is to deter war rather than provoke it. China continues to claim the island and has threatened force to annex it.

The Hindu reported the budget as part of successive efforts to improve fighting ability and demonstrate seriousness to Washington. Al Jazeera noted that Lai welcomed U.S.–China talks while insisting on respect for Taiwan's right to exist. The accounts share the fact of rising Chinese military activity; they differ on whether the spending mainly reassures the United States or simply raises the cost of a future confrontation.

### 4) Washington prepares China talks, possibly skipping APEC

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent may skip an Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC, a 21-member regional forum) finance ministers' meeting in Hong Kong later this month, the South China Morning Post reported, to prepare for President Trump's planned November visit to Shenzhen and a meeting with Chinese Vice-Premier He Lifeng. The immediate goal is extending the current bilateral trade truce ahead of expected talks between Trump and President Xi Jinping.

The South China Morning Post framed the choice as a practical use of limited preparation time. Skipping the broader gathering would show where the administration places its highest near-term priority.

### 5) Panama declares an emergency after two major earthquakes

Two earthquakes, 7.7 and 6.6 on the U.S. Geological Survey scale, struck on Friday. Panama declared a state of emergency on Saturday. Officials reported 27 injuries and no deaths, more than 100 aftershocks, and damage in all 10 provinces and two Indigenous territories. The National Civil Protection System counted 420 homes and 420 public schools damaged, plus seven hotels, at least 13 churches and nine bridges. Damage was also reported in neighbouring Colombia.

The Guardian described harm concentrated in Veraguas, Herrera, Coclé and Los Santos, with landslides cutting off some communities. This is a physical event, not a dispute.

## Geopolitical Watch

Yemen's campaign is the week's most immediate conflict shift. The government is courting tribes that have often stayed neutral. Outside powers will be judged by whether they contain any spillover into Red Sea shipping lanes.

In East Asia, three moves landed together: U.S. delivery of F-16V Block 70 fighters to Taiwan; a warning from KCNA (Korean Central News Agency, North Korea's state media) plus Kim's pledge of support for Russia; and Taipei's record 2027 budget. The Japan Times treated Pyongyang's statement as part of an effort to tighten ties with Russia and China and to influence missile-defence calculations in Seoul and Tokyo. ## Economic Signals

TSMC recorded September net revenue of 511.86 billion New Taiwan dollars, up 54.6 percent from a year earlier; third-quarter revenue reached about 1.49 trillion. The company supplies advanced chips to Nvidia and Apple and said it will adopt ASML's High-NA extreme-ultraviolet lithography machines, which enable smaller, more powerful chips. CNBC framed the results as evidence that demand for AI hardware remains strong even as foundation models improve and pricing pressure grows, and noted that September did not set a new monthly sales record.
