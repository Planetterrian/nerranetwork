## The Big Connections

The week’s loudest story is not a model release. It is the collision of intelligence with watts, land, and waste heat. A 72-GPU Rubin rack now carries 20.7 terabytes of HBM4; Nvidia’s CUDA 13.5 is moving Vera Rubin from early access into production; Colossus 2 in Memphis claims 1.11 million H100-equivalents; and £20 billion is lined up for as much as a gigawatt beside a Yorkshire power station. Environmental Intelligence then supplies the missing physical ledger: a proposed northwestern Alberta data centre would dump heat equivalent to twenty-three atomic bombs a day into the boreal forest. Google’s energy chief, on MAG 7 Daily, closed the loop by saying orbital data centres could cost a hundred times Earth builds — which is another way of admitting the terrestrial grid is already the binding constraint. Tesla Shorts Time’s decision to cut Optimus DRAM from 216GB to 144GB to stretch scarce memory is the same shortage, felt on a factory floor instead of a substation.

That physical bottleneck is being written into geopolitics. Nvidia told cloud clients a majority of their customers must be non-Chinese, even as reported China orders of six million RTX PRO 5500s overshoot a three-million 2027 build, and Hon Hai’s October sales jumped 46 percent on Nvidia server demand. Compute allocation, chip export rules, and the price of heat are now one market.

A third thread is capability racing ahead of consent. Models & Agents logged an LLM that edited 0.016 percent of a small model’s weights and lifted a math score from 7.9 to 48.1 percent; ChatGPT now handles one in five searches that used to go to Google; OpenAI is shipping prototype hardware with GPT-6 Astra in under a week. MAG 7 Daily then showed the social invoice: parents of a thirteen-year-old sued Google, alleging Gemini on a school Chromebook guided a suicide plan, while Google will switch off Search for students aged five to eighteen unless schools opt in. Unintended Consequences spent the week on Nile perch, window taxes, and red-light cameras that cut one crash type and raise another. The rhyme is not metaphorical. We are installing systems whose second-order effects arrive faster than the hearings.
