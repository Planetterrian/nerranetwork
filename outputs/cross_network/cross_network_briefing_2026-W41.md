## The Big Connections

The week’s loudest signal is not a model release. It is the collision of compute, electrons, and balance sheets. AI Chips tracked a four-billion-dollar, 20,000-unit Rubin order that would add seventy megawatts of Indian capacity, while twenty-four percent annual data-centre power growth still lags demand. MAG 7 and Modern Investing both carried SpaceX lining up forty billion dollars for Nvidia silicon; Tesla Shorts added a thirty-billion-dollar credit line plus patents that cut on-car AI-chip power. Google now traces more than 1.5 gigawatts of U.S. nuclear to uprates and restarts, even as Omni View Europe reported Finland freezing two Google sites pending environmental review. Samsung’s estimated eighty-billion-dollar quarter topping Nvidia’s last operating profit, a two-billion-dollar U.S. interposer pact into TSMC’s CoWoS flow, and Taiwan’s leading foundry posting a 55 percent sales jump complete the picture: the constraint has moved from transistors to power, permitting, and who can finance the next rack. First Principles’ Idiot Index—Parsons collapsing electricity cost by expanding steam through blade rows; air tickets at ten times fuel—is the right lens. Compute is being sold like a finished good while the underlying energy system still looks hand-forged.

A second thread is provenance versus premature use. OpenAI is watermarking ChatGPT text and dumping Lean-formalized math from an internal frontier model onto public GitHub; Google put 180 billion SynthID images in front of anyone who will check. That gap rhymes with Longevity Weekly’s eleven-gene reprogramming already given to people before any published human data, and Peptides Weekly’s warning that unapproved compounds could reach temporary sale while treatment claims remain under-tested. Prediction Markets Daily adds the market-structure version: Kalshi listing crypto perps and an S&P 500 contract the CFTC just cleared, Ohio ordering Polymarket and Coinbase out, the NFL staring at $1.8 billion in Sunday football flow and asking states to police it.

Space is the industrial hinge. Dragon now owns every U.S. ISS speed record after a 7-hour-55-minute Crew-13 docking; Crew-12 came home after nearly eight months; Starship pads at Kennedy are nearly complete; the FCC cleared 15,000 more Starlinks; spectrum buys open indoor Starlink Mobile. Astronomy did not sit still: Sombrero’s tidal streams record repeated mergers; a radio galaxy from ~12.5 billion years ago ranks among the most powerful ever seen; a high-school student flagged three dwarf galaxies that may explain isolated star formation. Planetterrian’s optogenetics Nobel—light as a control loop for neurons—sat beside those flight records on Nerra Daily. Different labs, same pattern: cheaper access, tighter loops, and consequences that arrive before the paperwork.
