# Nerra Network Weekly Intelligence — Week of 2026-10-25

## The Big Connections

AI’s constraint this week was not model quality. It was steel, substrate, land, and electrons. Fifty gigawatts of data-centre load by 2027 would match every facility built through 2024; NVIDIA is planning a 2.5-times substrate expansion so “AI factories” can actually ship, and it now claims a factory can go into production in fourteen days rather than six months. Meta’s capex hit $33.9 billion this quarter. Against that surge, Amazon cancelled four Loudoun County sites and flipped 122 acres toward housing, even as it earmarked €16 billion in Germany for 2027 data centres, satellites, and video. Four deaths at TSMC’s Kaohsiung fab halted steel work across Taiwan. Canada’s clean-power additions remain stuck behind interprovincial transmission even with federal tax credits and a coal phase-out. The same week, Tesla-backed storage broke ground in Bulgaria and Fort Bend County offered up to 35 years of tax breaks for a proposed $10 billion Tesla solar factory. Compute demand, grid bottlenecks, and competing uses of land are now one story.

Agents, meanwhile, left the demo loop. Four Gemini agents wrote 2,783 programs and cleared Minecraft without a human; the beginners show covered the same boss fight as a consumer fact. Devin now commits 89% of Cognition’s own code as the firm hits a $1 billion annualised run-rate on a $48 billion valuation. RL-trained models are dropping English from chain-of-thought, reasoning in a private substrate. A persistent agent keeps working after the app closes; Canva’s image model plans before it draws. *The Age of AI* asked the uncomfortable counterpart: whether embedding agentic systems and fractional CAIO roles can scale judgment without eroding the critical thinking those systems are supposed to serve. Efficiency cut the other way too — frozen 16 MB embedding tables let ternary 46 MB models hit 615–740 tokens/sec on laptop CPUs, matching float baselines on LAMBADA and PIQA.

Space is industrialising on a similar clock. ESA’s lunar “service station” stores solar power through the two-week night so experiments survive; Argonaut is slated for independent European cargo from 2030. SpaceX is preparing to catch both Super Heavy and Starship on Flight 15, Falcon 9 booster B1063 flew its 36th mission, Turkey’s first homegrown satellite rode a Falcon, and the next LC-36A tower module pulls Florida Starship operations closer to flight. Amazon’s German envelope explicitly includes satellites. The sky is becoming plant, power, and logistics — while diplomacy on the ground failed a simple test: Russia freed 74-year-old Stephen Hubbard as Witkoff met Putin, then missiles hit Kyiv hours later.

## Cross-Domain Watch

1. **Housing vs. compute.** Amazon’s Loudoun conversion is the rare case of AI infrastructure losing a land fight — while Meta, NVIDIA, and Amazon-Germany still spend as if gigawatts were abundant. Watch Canadian transmission and Texas solar subsidies as the other side of the same ledger.
2. **Agents that do not narrate.** Non-English chain-of-thought plus Devin writing the company’s own codebase plus a Minecraft clear with zero humans is a single shift: oversight that assumes readable reasoning is already lagging the systems.
3. **Side-effect literacy.** GLP-1 nail loss, diclofenac-vulture-human mortality, LPG that did not replace firewood, and electricity that cut sleep 35 minutes in rural Argentina are the same discipline: measure the second-order outcome, not the press release.
4. **Markets vs. states.** Ohio’s October 30 cutoff, Utah’s damages claim, and Missouri’s CFTC fight will decide whether prediction markets are federally supervised contracts or state-level betting — a template for every other novel venue.
5. **Talks, then missiles.** Hubbard’s release and the promised Oleshky corridor sat beside a Kyiv strike and a South Korean embassy drawdown. Treat diplomatic “process” as a headline until logistics on the ground move.
