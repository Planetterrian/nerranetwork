What the guest does

  • Books a time with their own email address, so the prep brief and studio link reach them directly.
  • Joins from a computer browser with headphones. There is nothing to install.
  • Sends up to five points they want covered; Mira reaches every one.
  • Gets a short prep brief the day before, with what Mira is curious about.

What the guest keeps

  • Their own transcript before anything is assembled, with a week to approve it, cut from it or refuse it. What they cut is removed from the audio, not bleeped.
  • A takedown, available after publication.
  • A human editor reviews every episode before release.
  • On publish day: the link, a short moment from the conversation and a share image.

How an interview runs

  1. You apply

    A short form: who you are and what you would talk about. No media training, no pitch deck, no AI angle required.

  2. A human reads it

    Patrick triages every application himself. This is the step that decides whether an interview happens.

  3. You talk to Mira

    Open the studio link, pick a microphone, join — or take a call on your phone if you would rather. Mira hears you and answers in real time. It is a conversation, not a questionnaire, and it runs under an hour.

  4. A human edits it

    Patrick reviews the episode before anything is assembled. This gate has no timer: nothing publishes because a review was slow.

  5. You approve your transcript

    You read what you said, and you have a week to approve it, cut anything from it, or refuse it. What you cut is removed from the audio before the episode is built — not bleeped, cut. After seven days without a reply the episode goes ahead as sent, and you can ask for a takedown at any time.

Mira, the AI host

Mira is the Nerra Network's AI host: she anchors the daily combined edition, conducts the network's interviews herself with real people on the record, and reads the personalised editions subscribers build.

The Age of AI and Nerra Voices are the first interview shows of their kind: an AI asks the questions, and the human guest decides whether the conversation is published at all.

What is unusual is not that the host is an AI — it is the terms. Mira holds a live, unscripted conversation rather than reading a script at a recording. She discloses on air that the host is a machine. A human editor reviews every episode before release, and that review has no timer that expires into publication. And the guest gets their own transcript before anything is assembled, with a week to approve it, cut anything from it, or refuse it outright: what they cut is removed from the audio, not bleeped, and a takedown stays available after publication. Plenty of shows use AI to write, narrate or edit, and some now let it interview. We have not found another that hands the guest the final say.

If you know of an interview show that was already doing this, we would genuinely like to hear about it — write to hello@nerranetwork.com and this page will say so.

One host across three jobs: a daily edition that carries every English show the network published that day, two interview shows, and a personalised feed that greets each subscriber by name.

Every factual claim an episode makes is recorded with its source and checked against that source's text. A claim that cannot be verified is published with a public flag rather than hidden, and the whole record is open on the claims and corrections ledger. Corrections are dated on the episode's page and named in the next episode's show notes; audio is never edited silently.

Founders

PN

Patrick Novak

Co-founder and producer

Patrick Novak is a Vancouver-based entrepreneur, a physical chemist by training and a published science fiction novelist. The network began when a daily Tesla podcast he relied on stopped publishing and he rebuilt it as an automated show; he established the Nerra Network name in February and March 2026. He designs the production pipeline, writes the editorial rules the AI works under, and built Mira, the network's AI host. He co-hosts The DP Pod.

Available to talk about

Questions we are asked

Is the host really an AI?
Yes. Mira is an AI. Every episode says so, and she introduces herself as the network's AI host when she first writes to a guest. Patrick Novak built her, and he joins an interview as co-host when a guest asks for him.
Who decides what gets published?
A human editor reviews every interview before release, and the guest has a week with their own transcript to approve it, cut from it or refuse it. A takedown stays available afterwards.
Does anyone pay to appear?
No. There is no fee in either direction, and sponsors have no say over who appears or what is covered.
How are the news shows made?
The pipeline fetches the day's reporting, AI writes and voices each episode under editorial rules people wrote, and every claim is checked against its source before publication; a claim that could not be verified is checked again each night for a week. The AI disclosure sets out exactly what a person does and does not do.
Can we quote an episode or use a clip?
Yes, with credit to the show and a link to the episode. Every episode has a written article and transcript to quote from.
