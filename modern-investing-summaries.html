Imagine you hold Enbridge in your TFSA and want to verify whether the recent bought-deal issuance and acquisition spending have shifted your overall allocation targets. Next compare those weights against your stated targets, noting any drift above or below your tolerance bands, then calculate time-weighted returns by isolating each cash flow date and applying the appropriate growth factor. Investor Education: Canadian Discount Brokerages Compared

Imagine you want to buy 100 shares of a TSX-listed energy name at the open; Wealthsimple Trade executes commission-free but routes through a single market maker that can widen the spread by 3-5 cents on less-liquid names during the first 15 minutes. Questrade charges $4.95-$9.95 per trade yet offers free ETF purchases and direct access to U.S. exchanges with real-time Level 2 data on its higher tier. Interactive Brokers posts the lowest margin rates at roughly 6.5% for balances under $100k and supports 150+ global markets, though its platform requires learning a steeper interface. The pro habit is to run a side-by-side cost calculator for your typical trade size and frequency before committing, then route larger U.S. orders through the lowest-fee desk. The misconception that “free is always cheapest” ignores hidden spreads and limited order types that can cost active investors more than a transparent commission. Canadian investors also need to verify which platforms support automatic dividend reinvestment inside registered accounts, because missing that feature can force manual purchases that incur extra commissions or FX fees. Finally, margin availability and interest rates matter for those who occasionally use leverage; Interactive Brokers' lower rates can save hundreds of dollars annually on a $50,000 borrowed balance compared with bank platforms, but only if the investor is comfortable navigating its order types and reporting tools.

Investor Education: The Canadian Home Bias Problem

An investor who keeps 40% of a TFSA in TSX-listed financials and energy names because they feel familiar with the companies is effectively doubling down on the same economic exposures already embedded in their job and housing. The mechanism that creates the gap is simple: Canada represents roughly 3% of global equity market capitalization, yet many portfolios hold 30-50% in Canadian stocks, producing heavy concentration in two sectors that together make up about half the TSX. A pro habit that narrows the gap is to run a quick geographic allocation report in the brokerage once a quarter and compare the Canadian weight against the MSCI World ex-Canada benchmark. The common misconception is that familiarity with domestic names automatically justifies the overweight; the fix is to treat home bias as an explicit, sized decision rather than the default. Investor Education: Passive Indexing Versus Active Management

Imagine you bought an S&P 500 ETF in your TFSA last year and it returned roughly the index gain after fees. The mechanism at work is that most active managers must overcome higher expense ratios and trading costs before they can beat the benchmark, and SPIVA data shows the majority fail to do so over five- and ten-year periods. What most retail investors do not realize is that the tax efficiency of low-turnover index ETFs compounds the advantage inside registered accounts where capital-gains distributions are not an issue. The pro tip is to reserve active management for pockets where the benchmark itself is inefficient, such as small-cap or emerging-market segments where information asymmetry can still be exploited. The concrete misconception to avoid is treating every active fund as a potential outperform; the fix is to compare any active choice against the relevant low-cost index ETF on a net-of-fees, after-tax basis before committing capital. Active strategies can still add value through factor tilts that emphasize value or momentum characteristics not fully captured by broad indexes. Risk management techniques such as tax-loss harvesting become more practical when an active manager can adjust holdings without triggering unwanted distributions. Small-cap segments often show larger gaps between active and passive results because information about those companies travels more slowly. The decision between approaches ultimately depends on the specific market segment and the investor's time horizon rather than a blanket preference for one style.
