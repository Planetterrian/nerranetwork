What every booking includes

Written with you

We write each message from your facts, in the show's own voice, and you approve the facts before it runs. You approve your message; you never see or approve the show around it.

Voiced by the show's host

The hosts are AI voices, and every episode says so. Your message is read in the same voice, labelled as sponsored, in its own slot.

Measured the same way

At the end of each month you get the downloads and views of the episodes that carried your message, from the same sources as the numbers on this page.

An episode for an episode

If a booked episode does not publish, your message runs on the next one. You never pay for an episode that did not go out.

How a booking works

  1. Write to usTell us the shows or package, the month and your category.
  2. We confirmWe confirm the dates are open and the category is yours.
  3. We write the messageA draft from your facts, for you to approve.
  4. It runsLabelled, on every episode of the term.
  5. You get the numbersDownloads and views of every episode that carried it.

What a sponsor can and cannot do

Labelled, every time

A sponsored message says it is sponsored, in the audio and in the show notes. It is never written to sound like the news around it.

Kept apart from the reporting

A sponsor read sits in its own slot, never inside a story, and a sponsor's own news is reported the same way as anyone else's or not at all.

No say over coverage

Sponsors do not choose, review, preview or change what a show covers. A sponsor that is in the news is covered like anyone else.

The same sourcing rules

Claims in a sponsored message must be the sponsor's own and must not be presented as the show's reporting or its fact-checked claims.

Every episode stays free

Sponsorship pays for the network; it never puts an episode behind a paywall.

Never on an interview

The Age of AI and Nerra Voices carry no sponsor. A guest gives an hour of their own words, and those words are never sold.

These rules are part of our editorial standards. A sponsor that wants something they rule out is not a fit.
