Requirements:
- The set must fit {{minutes}} minutes of live conversation with follow-ups. Fewer, better questions beat a checklist: Mira follows what the guest actually says, so every prepared question she does not need is one she can drop.
- If the guest named THE SUBJECT THEY CAME FOR, or sent notes before the call, that subject is the spine of the set: at least two "core" questions come from it, in the guest's own terms, and every point they asked to cover has a question that reaches it.
- Honour the depth and personal-question answers above. If the guest asked to keep it to the work, no question in this set touches their private life. If they asked for depth, no question in this set is definitional.
- Exactly one "opening" question: concrete, easy to answer well, grounded in something specific from the brief.
- At least two "core" questions that only THIS guest could answer — anchored in their actual work, not generic takes on the show's theme.
- At least one "tension" question that respectfully probes an open question or live disagreement IN THE SUBJECT: two ways their field could go, a trade-off they have had to make, a claim in their work that others contest. It is phrased from the subject, never from the guest's biography or self-description.
- At least one "forward" question about where their field goes next.
- Questions are for a spoken conversation: short, single-barreled, no stacked sub-questions.
- Never leading, never fawning, never gotcha.
- At least one question should offer something before it asks for something:
  a precedent the guest will recognise, or a comparison to how the thing
  looks from where the show sits, with the question attached to it. The
  co-host's best moments on this show all have that shape, and a guest
  answers an offer more openly than an interrogation.
- If they have been on before, at least two questions must come from what
  they said last time — a prediction with a date on it above all. "You told
  us in March that X would happen by now. Did it?" is the reason to have
  someone back, and it is the only question nobody else can ask them.
- If anything under WHAT THIS SHOW HAS LEARNED genuinely touches this
  guest's subject, write one question that puts that previous guest's
  answer to them by name, as an offer: who they are in a few words, their
  point, and whether it holds from where this guest sits. Only if it
  genuinely touches it; a forced callback is worse than none.
- At least one question should invite the uncomfortable version of the
  answer explicitly — make it clear that the bleak or unpopular reading is
  welcome, because guests hedge by default.
  That is about the SUBJECT, not the guest's private pain. Unless they asked
  for real depth on the personal side, a loss, a failure, an illness or a
  regret is asked about for what came out of it (the rule, the change, what
  they would tell someone else), never for what hurt, and never as a
  question they would need to brace for.
