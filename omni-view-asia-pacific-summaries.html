Both Sides: Should South Australia ban good-character references at sentencing?

South Australia’s government is proposing to abolish good-character references at sentencing for every criminal offence in the state’s courts. Attorney-General Kyam Maher says consultation begins in the coming days and a bill should be introduced in the first half of next year. He says a review was a recommendation of the Royal Commission into Domestic, Family and Sexual Violence, and that the change would bring South Australia into line with New South Wales and Western Australia. He described child sex offences as having a profound and lasting impact on victim-survivors.

Survivor advocate Harrison James, who co-founded the Your Reference Ain’t Relevant campaign four years ago to ban these references in child sex cases, says the proposal would make a tangible difference. His reason is the courtroom itself. He says there is nothing more dehumanising than sitting there while the person who harmed you is described as kind, generous and a good person, after you have survived the worst thing that person did to you.

The case for leaving a path open is the law the state government describes as still in force. An offender may tender evidence of good character, or call friends, family or employers, to argue for leniency. Judges already may not consider that evidence when sentencing a child sex offender if the suggested good character assisted the offence. The same law still allows it if the offending happened without the aid of that reputation, or if the person had no prior convictions. That is the allowance the proposal would close for all offences, not only child sex cases.

Both accounts start from that present rule: outside the assisted-offending limit, good-character evidence can still reach a sentencing court. Next is the consultation Maher has set for the coming days, then the bill he expects in the first half of next year.
