> **Economic pressures forced Thomas Ruyant to withdraw from the Route du Rhum this week as IMOCA project costs hit ten million euros.**

## The Canadian Boat
As of 2 November 2026 the team's YB tracker shows EMIRA IV alongside in Gosport, UK (Portsmouth Harbour). Source: [my.yb.tl](https://my.yb.tl/emira4)

Canada Ocean Racing, 31 October: the team posted a YouTube video in which Scott Shawyer breaks down the Route du Rhum 2026 start, covering weather routing, wind patterns and the tactical choices facing the fleet in the first days. Shawyer notes the race runs from Saint-Malo to Guadeloupe and explains how pressure systems and routing decisions shape the early stages even though he is not competing. The video is the campaign's most recent public output and focuses on the conditions the 115-boat fleet faced after the 1 November start. Shawyer describes the light north-easterly winds at the gun and the mandatory mark at Cap Fréhel sixteen nautical miles from the line. He walks through how the fleet would position for the passage toward the Isles of Scilly and Fastnet Rock in the opening hours. The analysis draws on his own offshore experience to highlight the trade-offs skippers face when the first systems move through the English Channel.

That analysis matters because Shawyer's next confirmed race is the double-handed RORC Transatlantic in January 2027 on the same boat. Ocean time and routing practice now directly support the longer-term goal of a solo Vendée Globe finish in 2028, the first by any Canadian. The video gives listeners a direct window into the decision-making that will matter when Shawyer returns to solo racing on EMIRA IV. ### The Fleet
The Route du Rhum started on 1 November with 115 solo boats leaving Saint-Malo in light north-easterly winds. Violette Dorange on Initiatives-Cœur collided with Romain Pilliard's trimaran Use It Again near Île de Bréhat shortly after the start, damaging her starboard foil; both boats diverted to Roscoff for repairs while the skippers remained unhurt. The incident shows how quickly a single collision can remove a top-ten contender from contention in a solo race where every hour of lost time compounds. Dorange had been running inside the top ten in the IMOCA class in the early miles. Technical stops are permitted without penalty in the first 150 miles, yet the foil damage still threatens her overall result. The episode underscores the narrow margins that separate a strong opening from an early setback when boats are packed together in the first night. Source: [ouest-france.fr](https://www.ouest-france.fr/route-du-rhum/route-du-rhum-violette-dorange-file-vers-roscoff-pour-reparer-son-imoca-7b12e3da-d62b-11f1-84f3-5611f158a658)

Thomas Ruyant withdrew from the Route du Rhum before the start because he could not close his budget. The article reports IMOCA project costs have risen to ten million euros, driven by design, materials and insurance, with sponsorship now the sole revenue source and no television or ticket income to offset shortfalls. Ruyant said he is unwilling to race at any cost and expects difficult months ahead; the withdrawal leaves the defending champion's boat on the dock and illustrates how rising entry costs can sideline experienced campaigns even when interest exists. Guillaume Gouze of the Centre de droit et d’économie du sport noted that some sponsors are renewing partnerships while others face broader economic uncertainty. He compared the ten-million-euro figure to the cost of shirt sponsorship for a top Ligue 1 football club. The piece frames the Route du Rhum as a test case for whether the class can sustain its current scale without diversified revenue. Anthony Marchand stopped his Actual Ultim 4 after a rudder failure early in the race. The incident adds to a pattern of mechanical issues appearing in the first days and forces another skipper out of a race already under way. Marchand had been running near the front of the Ultim class alongside Tom Laperche on SVR Lazartigue and Armel Le Cléac'h on Banque Populaire XI after the Cap Fréhel mark. The failure occurred in the same window when the fleet was still compact and manoeuvring frequently in building winds. ## Plain Sailing
An IMOCA campaign's annual operating budget typically runs between four and five million euros once a new boat is in the water. That figure covers the permanent shore team, spare parts, insurance, travel to events and the constant cycle of repairs that follows every offshore race. The number comes from repeated reporting across the class and explains why title sponsorship is the only realistic funding model; there are no television rights or gate receipts to spread the load. When a sponsor pulls out or a budget gap appears, the entire season can collapse because no secondary revenue streams exist to bridge it. The ten-million-euro project cost cited this week is the capital outlay for the boat itself; the four-to-five-million figure is what it then costs to keep that boat racing year after year. Insurance alone can represent nine to ten percent of the build price, and every major component from foils to mast requires dedicated spares and specialist labour. The model leaves teams exposed when economic conditions tighten, as seen in the Route du Rhum withdrawals this season.

## The Countdown
Seven hundred forty-one days remain until the Vendée Globe start on 12 November 2028. The campaign's next concrete milestone is the RORC Transatlantic Race start on 10 January 2027 from Marina Lanzarote.
