An Imoka 60s foils do not simply reduce drag, they lift the entire hull clear of the water
once boat speed is high enough.
When that happens, the wedded surface drops dramatically, and the boat can sustain speeds
above 30 knots for long periods. The trade-off is that the boat must be kept relatively flat
so the foils stay at the correct angle. Too much heel and the leeward foil loses lift
while the windward one comes out of the water. Modern designs therefore adjust foil angle and hull
shape together, which is exactly what the shirol refit is testing. The result is a boat that spends
more time flying and less time pushing a heavy hull through waves. The new hull on shirol 2
is intended to improve glide while accepting a slight mechanical loss of power from reduced
heel, with the net gain coming from lower hydrodynamic drag once the boat is airborne.
This kind of optimization is constant in the class because small changes in how long a boat stays
foiling can add up over thousands of miles. And the countdown. 769 days remain until the
