That brings us to Plain Sailing.

The Golden Globe Race and the Vendée Globe both send one person alone around the world without stopping, yet they test entirely different skills.

The Golden Globe uses only boats designed before nineteen eighty-eight and forbids GPS, satellite phones or weather-routing computers, so skippers navigate with a sextant and paper charts the way the original nineteen sixty-eight competitors did.

The Vendée Globe, by contrast, is sailed in current-generation foiling IMOCAs that can exceed forty knots and carry the full suite of modern satellite systems.

Because the boats are slower and the information poorer, a Golden Globe skipper’s daily decisions rest on what can be seen from the deck and what the barometer and paper weather maps suggest.

That constraint turns every cloud build-up and wind shift into a high-stakes calculation made without the safety net of real-time forecasts or outside assistance.

A skipper may spend weeks without speaking to another human, rationing food, water and energy while the boat moves at perhaps one-third the speed of an IMOCA.

The absence of electronic autopilots and routing software also means the sailor must steer and trim continuously during daylight hours and accept whatever weather arrives rather than threading between systems.

The result is a race whose difficulty lies less in raw speed than in sustained self-reliance over roughly eight months at sea.

A listener can picture it as the difference between driving a modern rally car with live telemetry and driving a nineteen-sixties vehicle across the same continents using only a road atlas and a compass.

The Golden Globe therefore measures endurance of isolation and judgment under deliberately limited tools, while the Vendée Globe measures the ability to extract maximum performance from the most advanced offshore machines ever built.

Both formats reward the same core trait—making correct decisions when no one else can help—but they remove different variables to isolate that trait.

One removes technology; the other removes company.

A listener who finishes this explanation can state that the Golden Globe’s eight-month duration and pre-nineteen-eighty-eight boats create a test of pure self-reliance that modern IMOCA racing deliberately replaces with speed and connectivity.
