Time for Plain Sailing.

Double-handed offshore racing requires two people to share every task that a solo skipper must perform alone.

In the RORC Transatlantic the pair must keep the boat moving at all times, manage sail changes, repairs and routing decisions, and still find time for sleep in short watches.

The boat itself does not change.

The same foiling IMOCA that will race solo in the Vendée Globe must be handled by two instead of one.

The key difference is that one person can always be on deck while the other rests, which reduces the extreme sleep fragmentation a solo skipper accepts.

This format therefore serves as useful preparation for the longer solo races that count toward Vendée Globe qualification.

The three thousand-nautical-mile distance from Lanzarote to Antigua forces the pair to manage the same weather systems and boat systems that appear in solo transatlantic races, yet the workload is divided.

A solo skipper on the same boat would face twenty-minute sleep cycles and full responsibility for every decision at three in the morning.

Double-handed racing lets the team test those systems and that boat in real ocean conditions without the full solo burden.
