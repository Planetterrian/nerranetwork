Now, the fleet.

Charal 2 has been cut in half at the waterline so the scow-shaped hull bottom can be replaced with a new shape designed to let the boat fly flatter and longer on its foils.

The work, the first of its kind on an IMOCA, began after the boat's return from the Transat Café L'Or victory and is scheduled to finish in June, leaving six months before the Route du Rhum.

The change trades a small loss of power for reduced hydrodynamic drag once the boat is fully airborne.

Jérémie Beyou explained that when the boat flew flat the original scow hull was not optimal.

Nicolas Andrieu added that the heel was lower than expected, prompting the new hull shape.

The two new hull halves were fabricated over the past year by Gepeto Composite a few hundred metres from Charal's hangar.

The structural work is being done with Gurit, the same company that built the original structure.

VPLP collaborated on the design since winter 2024.

The goal is to improve foiling phases, enhance performance on certain points of sail, and make the boat easier to handle solo.

A permanent 1,674-square-metre race-control building is under construction at Port Olona in Les Sables-d'Olonne at a cost of 8.8 million euros.

The facility, due for delivery in June 2028, will replace temporary structures used only during the Vendée Globe and will serve year-round for other offshore events including the Vendée Arctique and New York Vendée.

The building permit was signed on 16 September 2026.

Demolition of existing buildings is scheduled to begin in January 2027.

The project is carried by Vendée Expansion and the building will also host seminars and professional events outside race periods.

The question of utilisation rate between editions remains open, and the programming of events and activities is intended to keep the site active year-round.

Baptiste Hulin, 29, will line up on 1 November for his first Route du Rhum in the Ocean Fifty class aboard Viabilis Océans.

He won the 2025 Transat Café L'Or and says the result gave the team legitimacy in a class where the top four boats finished within 45 minutes of one another last year.

Hulin is aiming to win more major races in the tightly matched 50-foot trimaran fleet.

He has made Saint-Malo his home and is one of eleven sailors entered in the Ocean Fifty for the solo transatlantic.

Time for Plain Sailing.

An IMOCA 60's foils do not simply reduce drag; they lift the entire hull clear of the water once boat speed is high enough.

When that happens the wetted surface drops dramatically and the boat can sustain speeds above 30 knots for long periods.

The trade-off is that the boat must be kept relatively flat so the foils stay at the correct angle; too much heel and the leeward foil loses lift while the windward one comes out of the water.

Modern designs therefore adjust foil angle and hull shape together, which is exactly what the Charal refit is testing.

The result is a boat that spends more time flying and less time pushing a heavy hull through waves.

The new hull on Charal 2 is intended to improve glide while accepting a slight mechanical loss of power from reduced heel, with the net gain coming from lower hydrodynamic drag once the boat is airborne.

This kind of optimisation is constant in the class because small changes in how long a boat stays foiling can add up over thousands of miles.
