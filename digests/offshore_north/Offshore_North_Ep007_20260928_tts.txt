Time for Plain Sailing.

The Route du Rhum starts all classes on one line, from the 32-metre Ultims down to the Vintage monos.

That single start line creates the festival atmosphere the race is known for, but it also means the smaller boats must navigate the same weather systems and traffic as the giants for the first forty-eight hours.

The 118 entries this year will share the same departure from Saint-Malo, with the fastest boats pulling away quickly while the Class40s and Vintage entries settle into their own rhythms.

This format rewards skippers who can manage their own race without being distracted by the leaders disappearing over the horizon.

The six official classes are ULTIM, Ocean Fifty, IMOCA, Class40, Vintage Multi and Vintage Mono.

Each class has its own target time, yet every boat crosses the same starting line at the same moment.

For a solo skipper this means the first day and night are spent threading through a crowded fleet while the weather routing decisions that will shape the rest of the crossing are already being made.

The format turns the departure into a single shared moment rather than staggered starts, which is why the race draws such large crowds in Saint-Malo and why the early hours often decide who stays in contention.
