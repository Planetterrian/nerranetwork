## What This Show Is
Every four years a small number of people sail alone, without stopping and without assistance, around the world. The race is called the Vendée Globe. It is the hardest thing in sailing, the kind of contest that gets called the Everest of the seas, and no Canadian has ever finished it. Scott Shawyer and Canada Ocean Racing are trying to change that at the edition that starts on 12 November 2028. This show follows that attempt week by week and uses it as a door into the whole sport.

Canadians have history here. A Canadian won the very first Route du Rhum in 1978, beating a much larger boat by 98 seconds after 23 days at sea. This series is the next chapter of that story.

The sport is reported almost entirely in French. Stories reach English speakers late and stripped of meaning. This show closes that gap weekly.

Each Monday the programme covers The Canadian Boat with consequence rather than recap, The Fleet with the week in offshore racing and the layer that explains why any of it matters, Plain Sailing with one concept explained properly, and The Countdown with the days left to the start and where qualification stands.

Sources are named and linked every week. There is no hype, no play-by-play that will be stale by Monday, and nothing stated that the sources do not support. Uncertainty is flagged as uncertainty.

The audience is three rings at once: lifelong offshore fans who want the depth the English press does not give them, weekend sailors who know a boom from a bow but not the points table, and people who have never touched a rope and never will. No sailing knowledge is required and none is assumed. Whatever needs explaining gets one clean sentence.

Offshore North is part of the Nerra Network, an independent, ad-free network of daily shows built by two friends covering technology, science, markets, world news and language learning in several languages, free with no ads and no paywall, at nerranetwork.com.

## The Canadian Boat
As of the team’s 17 August 2026 update, EMIRA IV had departed Collingwood the weekend of 8–9 August for the return transatlantic delivery to France.

Canada Ocean Racing’s own site posted a Toronto Star interview with Scott Shawyer on 17 August. The piece places Shawyer’s 2028 Vendée Globe goal against the 1997 disappearance of Gerry Roufs and the later death of Derek Hatfield. Roufs was lying second in the 1996–97 Vendée Globe when his last message described 15-metre seas and winds above 80 knots; he was never heard from again and wreckage reached the Chilean coast months later. Hatfield campaigned Spirit of Canada through the round-the-world races of the 2000s and started a Vendée Globe attempt that fell short. Shawyer, who came to the sport from 26 years as president and CEO of JMP Solutions, completed his first solo IMOCA race in the 2024 New York Vendée aboard the 2011 Owen Clarke design now named Be Water Positive before acquiring the current-generation foiling IMOCA EMIRA IV in 2025. The Toronto Star framing treats these earlier Canadian losses as the direct backdrop for Shawyer’s stated intention to become the first to finish.

## The Fleet
Philippe Hartz, after 17 years in the French navy, is preparing an IMOCA campaign for the 2028 Vendée Globe. The move adds another experienced solo sailor to the growing list of entrants already working on qualification. Hartz left the navy to pursue the race full time and has spoken publicly about the transition from military service to the demands of solo ocean racing on a 60-foot foiling monohull. His entry increases the number of skippers with documented offshore and navigation experience targeting the same 2028 start line that Shawyer must reach through the published qualification path of at least one Grade 2 solo race finished within twice the winner’s time.

The 1997 Vendée Globe winner IMOCA Géodis will start the 2026 Route du Rhum. The boat’s return to the start line keeps a proven hull in the fleet while newer designs continue to launch. Géodis, sailed by Christophe Auguin to victory in the 1996–97 edition, will race again under its original name in the solo transatlantic event that begins 1 November from Saint-Malo. Its participation demonstrates that older, race-proven IMOCAs remain competitive options even as teams such as Sébastien Simon’s launch new builds like Groupe Dubreuil II.

A Scuttlebutt column examined whether modern IMOCA cockpits, which allow the boat to be sailed largely from below, conflict with the COLREG requirement for a proper lookout by sight. The question directly affects how the class will be raced and regulated in future solo events. The column notes that IMOCA 60s are now designed to be fully sailed indoors and that COLREG Rule 5 requires every vessel to maintain a proper lookout by sight at all times. Faster boats mean less time on deck, raising the practical tension between design choices that reduce physical exposure and the international collision regulations that predate foiling monohulls.

## Plain Sailing
The Vendée Globe is the only round-the-world race that is solo, non-stop and unassisted. A skipper leaves Les Sables d’Olonne, sails south through the Atlantic, rounds the three great capes of the Southern Ocean, then returns the same way without any outside help and without ever stopping the boat. The 2024–25 edition was won by Charlie Dalin in 64 days 19 hours, nine days inside the previous record.

Because the race is unassisted, every repair, every routing decision and every meal must be handled by one person on a 60-foot foiling monohull that can exceed 40 knots. The boat carries roughly 5,000 calories a day in freeze-dried food, makes its own water from the sea, and generates electricity from hydrogenerators and solar panels. Sleep comes in fragments of 20 minutes while the autopilot steers.

No Canadian has ever finished the race. That single fact is why the campaign exists and why every qualifying race Shawyer sails on EMIRA IV now carries an extra layer: each completed Grade 2 solo event both banks points toward the 2028 entry list and proves the boat and skipper can survive the conditions that have already claimed lives in the fleet’s history. The published qualification outline requires at least one such Grade 2 solo finish on the boat that will be raced in the Vendée Globe, completed within twice the winner’s time, plus a top-37 ranking in the IMOCA Globe Series championship ahead of the 40-boat cap. Shawyer’s first opportunity at that requirement arrives with the Route du Rhum on 1 November 2026.
