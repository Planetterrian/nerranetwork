Time for Plain Sailing.

Qualification for the Vendée Globe is a points-based system run by the IMOCA class.

A skipper must accumulate a minimum number of points by finishing designated races in an IMOCA within a set period before the start.

The exact points table and race list are published by the class and updated each cycle.

The system exists because the race is solo, non-stop and around the world.

Organisers need evidence that every entrant has already managed an IMOCA alone for long distances and in varied conditions.

A single training passage cannot supply that evidence, so results in named events are used instead.

The chosen races typically include transatlantic and round-the-world courses that replicate the Southern Ocean demands the skipper will face, and only finishes within the qualification window count toward the total.

In practice this means a skipper without recent IMOCA miles must choose which qualifying races to enter and when, because the calendar is fixed and the points window closes months before the Vendée start.

The choice directly determines whether a campaign can reach the start line at all.

Missing a key points race because of a late boat delivery or an injury can leave a skipper short even if the boat itself is ready, forcing a last-minute scramble for an alternative event that may not award enough points to close the gap.

The arithmetic is unforgiving.

Most editions require at least two strong results in high-value races, and the points scale rewards both distance and difficulty.

A podium in a shorter transatlantic event may deliver fewer points than a mid-fleet finish in a longer Southern Ocean leg, so skippers weigh the risk of damage against the reward of a higher-scoring result.

Campaigns that already hold points from the previous cycle can sometimes coast.

New entrants must build their entire tally inside the current window.

The rule also protects the race from untested boats and skippers.

An IMOCA is a high-performance machine whose foils, rigs and electronics can fail dramatically when pushed for weeks on end.

By requiring documented finishes, the class reduces the chance that an unprepared entry will need rescue in the Southern Ocean or create a hazard for the rest of the fleet.

The same rule set that keeps the start list small also keeps the safety record strong.

For a new national campaign the practical effect is a compressed timeline.

The skipper must locate a suitable IMOCA, complete any required refit, and then immediately begin accumulating results before the points deadline passes.

Any gap between the public announcement and the first entered race shortens the available opportunities, because the class does not add extra events to accommodate late arrivals.
