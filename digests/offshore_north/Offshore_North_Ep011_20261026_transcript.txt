Time for plain sailing.
Double-handed offshore racing requires two people to share every task that a solo skipper
must perform alone.
In the RORC transatlantic, the pair must keep the boat moving at all times, manage sail
changes, repairs and routing decisions, and still find time for sleep in short watches.
The boat itself does not change.
The same foiling IMOXA that will race solo in the Vendée Globe must be handled by two instead
of one.
The key difference is that one person can always be on deck while the other rests, which reduces
the extreme sleep fragmentation of solo skipper accepts.
This format therefore serves as useful preparation for the longer solo races that count
toward Vendée Globe qualification.
The 3,000 nautical mile distance from Lanzarote to Antiga forces the pair to manage the
same weather systems and boat systems that appear in solo transatlantic races, yet the workload
is divided.
A solo skipper on the same boat would face 20-minute sleep cycles and full responsibility
for every decision at 3 in the morning.
Double-handed racing lets the team test those systems and that boat in real ocean conditions
without the full solo burden.
