# Offshore North
**Week ending:** August 15, 2026
> **A Vendée Globe podium boat is now in testing after its launch this week, while another two-time winner returns to the Route du Rhum after years abandoned.**

## The Canadian Boat
Canada Ocean Racing made no public announcements this week.

**Standing item:** EMIRA IV is scheduled to return across the Atlantic to France in autumn 2026 for Route du Rhum preparation; this will be Scott Shawyer’s first solo transatlantic race on a foiling IMOCA. The boat arrived in Collingwood, Ontario, at the start of June 2026 after an Atlantic crossing and transit through the St. Lawrence Seaway and Welland Canal. It completed its first Canadian summer training programme on Georgian Bay with support from the Town of Collingwood and Collingwood Yacht Club. Free public tours took place on 13 and 20 June 2026. The vessel is a current-generation foiling IMOCA 60 previously raced as 11th Hour Racing Team — Mālama, where it won The Ocean Race 2023 under Charlie Enright, then as Groupe Dubreuil where Sébastien Simon took third in the 2024–25 Vendée Globe despite a broken foil. Shawyer acquired the boat in early 2025 and renamed it EMIRA IV. The campaign’s stated goal remains Shawyer becoming the first Canadian to finish the Vendée Globe, with the next edition starting 12 November 2028. ### The Fleet
Sébastien Simon launched his new IMOCA Groupe Dubreuil II on August 13 in Southampton. The boat was designed by Guillaume Verdier and features foils along with a new cockpit layout. It now enters a testing phase before heading to Les Sables-d’Olonne ahead of the Route du Rhum. Simon finished third in the previous Vendée Globe on the boat’s predecessor hull and set a solo 24-hour speed record aboard that earlier version. Construction details include the specific foil configuration and deck arrangements now undergoing sea trials. Simon has expressed excitement about the new platform while stressing the need to remain focused on preparation.

Why it matters: A new-generation IMOCA entering the water this late in the season shortens the available testing window before the November Route du Rhum, directly affecting how much data the team can gather on the new foils and systems.

The restored IMOCA Aïto, formerly PRB 3, is being prepared for the Route du Rhum by Kieran Le Borgne’s team. The boat won the Vendée Globe twice under Michel Desjoyeaux and Vincent Riou before years of abandonment. The refit brings a two-time winner back into competition after structural work and restoration. Video coverage shows the interior and deck layout now being readied for offshore racing. Le Borgne’s crew is focusing on returning the proven hull to competitive condition for the November start.

Why it matters: Returning a proven hull to the start line increases the number of experienced boats in the Route du Rhum fleet and changes the competitive depth for other entrants.

Preparations in Saint-Malo for the 13th Route du Rhum-Destination Guadeloupe include new “Off” festival events beyond the main village. Local coverage notes the city’s transformation and the need for skippers to stay focused amid the buildup. ## Plain Sailing
An IMOCA 60 launch marks the shift from construction to the critical testing phase that determines whether a boat can be raced safely offshore. Sébastien Simon’s new Groupe Dubreuil II, designed by Guillaume Verdier, left the shed on August 13 and immediately began sea trials. The boat carries the same hull pedigree that carried Simon to third place in the 2024–25 Vendée Globe, but every new generation of foils, deck layout, and systems must be validated before it faces the Southern Ocean or a solo transatlantic. Construction details released with the launch include the specific foil shape and a revised cockpit arrangement intended to improve long-watch ergonomics. Simon has already set a solo 24-hour speed record on the prior version of this hull, yet that performance came only after extensive refinement of the earlier boat’s systems.

Testing is not optional because the loads on a foiling IMOCA are extreme. A new hull requires data on foil behaviour in waves, cockpit ergonomics for long watches, and the integration of every sail and control line. The Route du Rhum start in November leaves roughly three months for this work; any delay in solving a single system can force a team to race with reduced confidence or miss the event entirely. Simon’s previous third-place result came despite a broken foil reported in early December of that Vendée Globe, showing how even small unresolved issues compound over thousands of miles. The new boat’s testing must therefore cover not only straight-line speed but also the ability to maintain control when one foil is compromised or when sailing upwind in steep seas.

The same logic applies to refitted boats such as Aïto. Even a proven winner must re-prove itself after structural changes and years out of the water. The difference between a boat that finishes and one that retires often comes down to how thoroughly those unknowns were reduced during the short window between launch and the first race. Aïto’s history includes two Vendée Globe victories under Michel Desjoyeaux and Vincent Riou, yet the current refit by Kieran Le Borgne’s team must still validate every updated component before the Route du Rhum. Data gathered in the coming weeks will decide whether the boat can be pushed to its historical performance levels or must be sailed more conservatively.
