The requirement is written into the notice of race and applies to every team that wants to start. The rule changes how campaigns select sailors and how they train.
A skipper can no longer fill a five-person watch system with the four most experienced offshore sailors available. At least two of those sailors must be women who meet the same physical and skill thresholds as their male teammates. That constraint affects hiring timelines, training walks, and even boat layout because watch systems and birthing must now accommodate the mandated balance rather than the fastest available combination.
The practical effect is that teams must develop a wider bench of qualified offshore sailors earlier in the campaign cycle. For the two new IMOCA's already launched, the rule also means their first serious ocean miles will be sailed by crews that were assembled under the parity constraint rather than under an open selection process.
