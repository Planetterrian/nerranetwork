Every four years a handful of sailors set out alone around the world without stopping or outside help, and no Canadian has ever finished that race.

On the Canadian boat first, then out in the fleet with the Route du Rhum results, then Plain Sailing on how qualification actually works.

As of 9 November 2026 the team's YB tracker shows EMIRA IV alongside in Gosport in Portsmouth Harbour, and the boat has logged no movement in the thirty-day window since 10 October.

Canada Ocean Racing's post dated 23 October announced that Scott Shawyer and Alan Roberts will sail the RORC Transatlantic Race double-handed on EMIRA IV, starting 10 January 2027 from Marina Lanzarote to Antigua.

Roberts is a British offshore sailor who finished fourth in The Ocean Race 2022–23 with Biotherm, spent nine seasons on the French Figaro solo circuit, and served as co-skipper and performance coach to Vendée Globe sailor Clarisse Crémer.

The pair's 2023 RORC Transatlantic result was a class win and double-handed course record on the team's earlier IMOCA, not on EMIRA IV.

The announcement positions the January race as the next concrete step after the decision not to start the Route du Rhum, giving Shawyer additional offshore miles on the foiling boat.

The Vendée Globe 2028 Notice of Race, published in November 2025, requires each skipper-boat pair to finish at least one solo race from a designated list on the exact boat they will sail in the event, within twice the winner's time.

The list includes Vendée Arctique 2026, Route du Rhum 2026, Retour à la Base 2027, The Transat CIC 2028 and New York Vendée – Les Sables d'Olonne 2028.

Skippers must also finish in the top 37 of the IMOCA Globe Series championship based on their nine best results from Grade 1 to 3 events, and the fleet itself is capped at 40 boats with up to three wild cards.

The RORC Transatlantic is neither solo nor a Globe Series race, so it earns no qualification points.

Shawyer still needs a solo finish from the listed races on EMIRA IV plus the ranking.

The rules therefore place a premium on consistent participation across multiple seasons rather than a single high-profile result, and the January double-handed crossing supplies ocean time without advancing the qualification tally.

Time for Plain Sailing.

The Vendée Globe qualification system rewards sustained participation across multiple seasons rather than a single standout result. A skipper must complete at least one solo race from the official list on the exact boat they will sail in the event, finishing within twice the winner's time. The fleet itself is limited to 40 boats.

This structure means a campaign cannot rely on one strong performance. It must demonstrate consistent offshore miles and ranking points over time. The rules therefore favour teams that keep their boat racing year after year instead of those that appear only for a single qualifier.

Because the RORC Transatlantic is double-handed and outside the Globe Series, it supplies valuable experience without satisfying either requirement, leaving the Canadian campaign to target one of the remaining solo races on the list plus the necessary points total before the 2028 start.
