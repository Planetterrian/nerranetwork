on the machine in the upcoming transatlantic event. Time for plain sailing. An IMOCA 60 carries two
very different foil systems. Dagger boards are vertical blades that slide up and down to reduce leeway
and add lift when the boat heals. They work in a wide range of conditions and are simpler to manage.
True foils are L-shaped or T-shaped appendages that generate enough upward force to lift the entire
hull clear of the water once the boat reaches a certain speed.
On a foiling IMOCA, the transition happens around 12-15 knots.
Above that, the drag drops sharply and the boat can sustain 30-plus knots for hours.
The trade-off is that a foil boat is more sensitive to setup and more vulnerable if one foil
is damaged, which is why some teams still choose dagger boards for their first seasons on the
class.
