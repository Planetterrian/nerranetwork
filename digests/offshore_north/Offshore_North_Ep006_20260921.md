# Offshore North
**Week ending:** September 21, 2026
> **Thomas Ruyant, the defending IMOCA champion for the Route du Rhum, has withdrawn from the November start because he could not secure a major sponsor for his new foiling boat.**

## The Canadian Boat
As of the team's 2 September post, EMIRA IV was leaving Canada and heading back to Europe. The post is Scott Shawyer's own account of the summer training on Georgian Bay. He described the biggest learning as understanding how the boat works system by system, including sails, rigging, foils, electrical systems, hydrogenerators, solar panels and autopilot overlays. Scott noted that after more than forty years sailing on Lake Huron, the water conditions held few surprises, but the boat itself did. He mentioned that on two heavy days his triceps seized on the grinder, which he traces to a shoulder injury and not drinking enough water with electrolytes. He added that he has still not sailed EMIRA IV in open-ocean waves and swell and needs to remain humble. The support team named includes Martin Amescua, who has been with the boat for a long time, Gaby Bucau, Clovis Gautier, Giulina Bayat for rigging and systems, Alberto Zaccaria for electrical, computer and sensor systems, Alan Roberts for coaching on the Exocet routing system and autopilot overlays, and Nick Moloney as sailing coach. The boat was not among the 14 IMOCAs that started the 48H Azimut in Lorient on 17 September. The next confirmed milestone for the campaign remains the Route du Rhum start on 1 November from Saint-Malo, Shawyer's first solo transatlantic on a foiling IMOCA. ### The Fleet
Thomas Ruyant announced on 21 September that he will not start the Route du Rhum on 1 November. The defending IMOCA champion cited the failure to secure a major sponsor for his new foiling IMOCA and the roughly 2.5 million euros required for the race and ongoing operations. The boat, launched on 19 June, remains in storage in Lorient. Ruyant called the decision immensely disappointing, noting that the race has a particular place in his history, yet stated that his ambitions for the IMOCA circuit and the 2028 Vendée Globe remain unchanged, with the energy even stronger. He had sought major backing for about 2.5 million euros to participate competitively, and the team needs 4 to 5 million euros per year until the Vendée Globe. Other skippers, including Armel Tripon and Maxime Sorel, are also still seeking funding for the same start in a tense sponsorship market. The withdrawal leaves one fewer established name on the 26-boat IMOCA entry list for a race that already sits in a tight sponsorship market. Source: [lequipe.fr](https://www.lequipe.fr/Voile/Actualites/Tenant-du-titre-en-imoca-thomas-ruyant-renonce-a-la-route-du-rhum-faute-de-sponsor/1720261)

Anthony Marchand completed winter modifications on Actual Ultim 4 that included a taller mast at the class maximum height and a revised sail plan. The changes allow earlier take-off, more mast bend, and the ability to flatten the mainsail further without reefing early. Marchand described the work as preparation for his primary 2026 objective, the Route du Rhum on the 32-metre trimaran. He noted that the new mast allows proactive adjustments that were not possible with the previous wing mast, and the sail inventory was adjusted accordingly, with mainsail, J3 and J2 all at the same sail area. The modifications illustrate how even established Ultim teams continue to chase marginal gains in a class where small differences in rig behaviour can decide a solo Atlantic crossing. ## Plain Sailing
An IMOCA campaign's annual operating budget typically runs between four and five million euros once a new boat is in the water. That figure covers the boat itself, shore team, spares, travel, insurance and the skipper's salary. Most of it must be raised from title sponsors who treat the campaign as media exposure rather than philanthropy. When a sponsor relationship ends or a new one cannot be closed in time, the boat stays ashore even if it is ready to race. The Route du Rhum entry list shows how quickly that arithmetic can remove a defending champion from a start line. Ruyant had launched his new boat in June but could not raise the necessary funds, highlighting the business reality behind even the most competitive campaigns. A skipper must also complete at least one Grade 2 solo race on the boat they will race in the Vendée Globe, finishing within twice the winner's time, and finish among the top 37 in the IMOCA Globe Series championship for a place in the 40-boat fleet.

## The Countdown
Seven hundred eighty-three days remain until the Vendée Globe start on 12 November 2028. The campaign's next concrete milestone is the Route du Rhum start on 1 November 2026 from Saint-Malo.
