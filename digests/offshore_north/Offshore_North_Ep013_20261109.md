## The Canadian Boat
As of 9 November 2026, the team's YB tracker has EMIRA IV alongside in Gosport, UK (Portsmouth Harbour). The tracker window covers the preceding thirty days, and the most recent fix places the boat at the berth with no movement logged after the single training run recorded earlier in the autumn.

Canada Ocean Racing's most recent post, dated 23 October, announced that Scott Shawyer and Alan Roberts will sail the RORC Transatlantic Race double-handed on EMIRA IV starting 10 January 2027 from Marina Lanzarote to Antigua. Roberts is a British offshore sailor who finished fourth in The Ocean Race 2022–23 with Biotherm, spent nine seasons on the French Figaro solo circuit, and served as co-skipper and performance coach to Vendée Globe sailor Clarisse Crémer. The 2023 RORC Transatlantic record the pair set was a class win and double-handed course record on the team's earlier IMOCA, not on EMIRA IV. The announcement frames the January race as the next concrete step after the decision not to start the Route du Rhum, giving Shawyer additional offshore miles on the foiling boat ahead of the longer qualification path.

The Vendée Globe 2028 Notice of Race, published in November 2025, requires each skipper-boat pair to finish at least one solo race from a designated list on the boat they will race in the event, within twice the winner's time. The list includes Vendée Arctique 2026, Route du Rhum 2026, Retour à la Base 2027, The Transat CIC 2028 and New York Vendée – Les Sables d'Olonne 2028. Skippers must also finish in the top 37 of the IMOCA Globe Series championship based on their nine best results from Grade 1 to 3 events. The fleet is capped at 40 boats with up to three wild cards. The RORC Transatlantic is neither solo nor a Globe Series race, so it earns no qualification points; Shawyer still requires a solo finish from the listed races on EMIRA IV plus the ranking. The rules therefore place a premium on consistent participation across multiple seasons rather than a single high-profile result, and the January double-handed crossing supplies ocean time without advancing the qualification tally. ### The Fleet
Tom Laperche crossed the Route du Rhum finish line off Pointe-à-Pitre on 9 November in record time aboard the Ultim SVR-Lazartigue. The win came after a fast final stretch in which the 320-mile lead reported on 9 November held. A new course record changes the benchmark every future solo transatlantic campaign will measure itself against. The result also produced an emotional moment on the dock when Basile Bourgnon, a friend of the winner, was visibly moved by the achievement. The record stands as the fastest solo crossing of the Saint-Malo to Guadeloupe course in the race's history.

Louis Burton finished third in the Ultim class on Armand Thiery, completing the podium behind Laperche and Thomas Coville. The result shows a newcomer to the class can reach the front in his first Route du Rhum. Burton crossed the line on 8 November at 1430 local time after a race that tested the entire Ultim fleet with shifting conditions and equipment demands. The podium placement confirms that experience in the class is not the only route to a top finish.

Alexia Barrier suffered a torn mainsail during the night and chose to repair it at sea rather than divert. The decision kept her in the race but required working alone on a damaged sail in the middle of the Atlantic; the same failure on a solo IMOCA would leave one person to manage both the repair and the boat's course through the night. Barrier stated she knew the boat could finish without the full sail, and the repair allowed her to continue under reduced canvas while the rest of the fleet contended with the same weather systems. ## Plain Sailing
The Vendée Globe qualification system rewards sustained participation across multiple seasons rather than a single standout result. A skipper must complete at least one solo race from the official list on the exact boat they will sail in the event, finishing within twice the winner's time. The fleet itself is limited to 40 boats. This structure means a campaign cannot rely on one strong performance; it must demonstrate consistent offshore miles and ranking points over time. The rules therefore favour teams that keep their boat racing year after year instead of those that appear only for a single qualifier. Because the RORC Transatlantic is double-handed and outside the Globe Series, it supplies valuable experience without satisfying either requirement, leaving the Canadian campaign to target one of the remaining solo races on the list plus the necessary points total before the 2028 start.
