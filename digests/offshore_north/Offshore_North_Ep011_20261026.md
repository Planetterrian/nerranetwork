# Offshore North
**Week ending:** October 26, 2026
> **Scott Shawyer will return to competition in January 2027, teaming up with Alan Roberts for the RORC Transatlantic Race on the foiling IMOCA EMIRA IV.**

## The Canadian Boat
As of 26 October 2026, the team's YB tracker has EMIRA IV alongside in Gosport, UK (Portsmouth Harbour). Source: [my.yb.tl](https://my.yb.tl/emira4)

Canada Ocean Racing, 23 October: Scott Shawyer will team up with British offshore sailor Alan Roberts for the RORC Transatlantic Race, which starts on 10 January 2027 from Marina Lanzarote. Roberts finished fourth in The Ocean Race 2022–23 with Biotherm and raced nine seasons on the French Figaro solo circuit. In 2023 the pair won their class and set the race's double-handed course record, 9 days, 12 hours and 26 minutes, on the team's earlier IMOCA, not EMIRA IV. This time they will sail the 3,000-nautical-mile crossing from Lanzarote to Antigua on the foiling EMIRA IV. The post says Shawyer is looking ahead after deciding not to compete in the Route du Rhum, and that the crossing will get him back on the water and build valuable experience aboard EMIRA IV.

The RORC Transatlantic isn't a Vendée qualifier, but it's valuable ocean time on EMIRA IV. Scott still needs a solo Grade 2 finish, such as Retour à la Base 2027 or The Transat CIC 2028, plus a top-37 Globe Series ranking. The campaign's public goal remains the first Canadian finish in the 2028 edition, which starts on 12 November 2028.

### The Fleet
The expert panel assembled by Sailorz for the 2026 Route du Rhum IMOCA class named Jérémie Beyou the clear favourite, followed by Sam Goodchild and Ambrogio Beccaria. Four of the six skippers polled picked Beyou on Charal. The panel included the last two Route du Rhum IMOCA winners, Paul Meilhat from 2018 and Thomas Ruyant from 2022, plus Yoann Richomme, Nicolas Lunven, Romain Attanasio and Justine Mettraux. The panel noted a drop to 22 IMOCA entries from the record 38 in 2022, which Thomas Ruyant attributed to companies hesitating on long-term sponsorship commitments. Ruyant said the projects are built around long-term commitments, so as soon as confidence in the future starts to waver the impact is felt immediately. He added that companies are finding it difficult to commit for the long term, yet paradoxically enthusiasm for what the sailors do has not diminished. The panel agreed that around ten skippers have a realistic chance of a podium finish or a top-five result. Nicolas Lunven said he was blown away by Charal's performance in the Transat Café L'OR, where the boat posted significantly higher average speeds than rivals downwind in the trade winds. Lunven highlighted the team's work on the rudder system that helps lift the stern, calling it a clever solution that delivers a significant performance gain. Thomas Ruyant added that Jérémie has come through a season in which he has continued to learn a lot about Charal, whose hull underwent a major refit last winter.

This matters because the reduced field still contains roughly ten realistic podium contenders, and the economic pressure described by Ruyant affects every campaign's ability to keep boats competitive through the 2028 Vendée Globe cycle. A smaller entry list does not reduce the technical demands on the boats or the skippers who must manage them alone across the Atlantic. ## Plain Sailing
Double-handed offshore racing requires two people to share every task that a solo skipper must perform alone. In the RORC Transatlantic the pair must keep the boat moving at all times, manage sail changes, repairs and routing decisions, and still find time for sleep in short watches. The boat itself does not change; the same foiling IMOCA that will race solo in the Vendée Globe must be handled by two instead of one. The key difference is that one person can always be on deck while the other rests. This format therefore serves as useful preparation for the longer solo races that count toward Vendée Globe qualification. The 3,000-nautical-mile distance from Lanzarote to Antigua forces the pair to manage the same weather systems and boat systems that appear in solo transatlantic races, yet the workload is divided. Double-handed racing lets the team test those systems and that boat in real ocean conditions without the full solo burden.
