### Lead
**More than 5,000 people killed or injured in Haiti due to gang violence this year, UN says:** Yahoo News Canada
United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Türk said more than 5,700 people were killed or injured by gangs or during operations against them in Haiti between January 1 and September 11, 2026. He said more than 230 people were kidnapped, and that an estimated 1,000 women and girls were raped, a figure he called likely much higher. More than half of the casualties came in security operations, he said, with hundreds struck by stray bullets and explosive drones. Recruitment of children into gangs has nearly tripled, Türk said, because for many minors joining a gang can be the only means of survival. Executions by specialised police units dropped in recent months, he added, but people alleged to be associated with gangs still face a high risk of summary execution. Gangs continue to adapt and expand, he said, and UN evidence points to a strategy of seizing new territory and key transport routes. ### Both Sides: Destroying suspected trafficking boats off Ecuador
United States forces and Ecuadorian courts are now the actors in a fight over what may be done to crews stopped at sea. Between August 28 and September 16, 2026, Human Rights Watch said, US forces intercepted and destroyed eight Ecuadorian fishing vessels and detained more than 140 of 160 crew members. The case for the operations, as US forces stated it, is that the boats were floating refueling stations for drug trafficking linked to Los Choneros, an Ecuadorian criminal group. Juanita Goebertus, Americas director at Human Rights Watch, said intercepting fuel trafficking at sea may be an important step to stop criminal groups. The case against, which she put first as a limit on that purpose, is that the aim does not allow security forces to arrest people without evidence, destroy their vessels, or abuse them. Crew members she interviewed denied any illicit refueling role. Some reported beatings, threats meant to coerce confessions that they were trafficking fuel, and pressure to sign documents they could not understand. Of the 160 crew members, 90 face criminal investigations in Ecuador, judges ruled that the arrest of 56 was unlawful, and 14 were not brought before a judge. Neither government has publicly disclosed evidence linking individual crew members to a crime, Human Rights Watch said, and neither answered its request for comment. What both the US allegation and the crews’ denials sit on is that the vessels were intercepted and destroyed and that most of the crews were detained. Human Rights Watch said the sinking may have eliminated evidence of what the boats were doing, and it asked both governments to disclose the legal basis, the criteria for interception and destruction, and to investigate the reported abuses. The reporting names no further hearing date. Source: [miragenews.com](https://www.miragenews.com/rights-concerns-over-us-maritime-operations-1754343/)

**De escolas a presídios: alunos da rede particular criam projeto para fazer livros circularem entre instituições públicas:** O Globo
Students at the American School of Rio, Eduardo Campos, 16, and Felipe Alhante, 17, built Virando Páginas to send finished books to public schools, libraries, and prisons. The first donation was in November last year, and the project has since reached about ten institutions, including Casa Maria de Nazaré in Rocinha. Partnerships with the municipal education department and the state prison administration moved copies into public schools and into dozens of prison units. Brazil’s National Justice Council recognizes reading as one route to remission of a sentence; the founders said they see a purpose in those prisons beyond that benefit.
