### Across the Region
**Deputados destinam R$ 6 de cada R$ 10 em emendas para municípios onde tiveram melhor desempenho nas urnas: O Globo**
Of every ten reais federal deputies sent through individual amendments over the last three years, six went to cities where those deputies took the largest share of the local vote, an O Globo review finds. The 15 percent of municipalities with each lawmaker’s highest vote shares account for half of 43 billion reais in individual amendments directed since 2024. Adolfo Viana sent 4.9 million reais to Pojuca, Bahia, where he took 19.6 percent of valid votes for federal deputy in 2022, and nothing to neighboring Mata de São João, where his share was 0.32 percent. Elmar Nascimento, a candidate for re-election, told O Globo he helps the places where he is voted, because he does not control the whole federal budget. Danilo Forte said the budget-guidelines law sets where amendments may go, and that more requests reach a deputy from towns where he is voted; Jaguaribe, Ceará, where his share was 14.9 percent, received 10.8 million reais, while neighboring Icó, at 0.32 percent, received none. ### Both Sides: Alcohol sales on Brazil’s election day
Brazilians vote on Sunday, 4 October, and whether alcohol may be sold that day is decided state by state. The Superior Electoral Court sets no national ban. Regional electoral courts, working with public-security bodies, write the local rule, and state security secretariats may add limits of their own. A ban can cover a whole state or only some municipalities and electoral zones, and it can start the night before the vote.

The case for a restriction is the order those courts and security bodies have already signed where they chose one. They stop sale, supply, distribution and consumption, including delivery, for a fixed window around polling. Acre’s regional court set that ban from midnight to 16:00 on 4 October, and the same hours on 25 October if a runoff is held. Amapá runs from 22:30 on 3 October until 18:00 on 4 October. Alagoas suspends sales from 03:00 to 18:00. Amazonas runs from 02:00 to 17:00. Maranhão runs from midnight to 22:00. Pará and Piauí run from midnight to 18:00. In Bahia, 18 municipalities have adopted local hours that differ by electoral zone.

The case against a statewide ban is the choice of the three largest electorates, and a request from the trade. São Paulo, Minas Gerais and Rio de Janeiro will apply no alcohol restriction. São Paulo has had no dry law since 2006. Rio has not applied the measure since 1996. The Minas Gerais chapter of the Brazilian Bars and Restaurants Association asked the state government not to decree one. The Federal District, Espírito Santo, Paraná, Rio Grande do Sul, Mato Grosso do Sul, Paraíba, Pernambuco, Rio Grande do Norte, Rondônia and Santa Catarina also will not. Ceará and Roraima had not yet decided.

What both patterns accept is that there is no single national hour, and that Sunday’s vote goes ahead either way. Where a ban exists, it begins tonight or in the early hours of Sunday. Several of those orders repeat on 25 October if a second round is required.
