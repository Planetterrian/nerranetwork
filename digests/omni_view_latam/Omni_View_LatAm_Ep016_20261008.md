### Lead
**Heavy rain for Argentina’s northeast, lighter totals for the maize belt: La Nación**
The Bolsa de Cereales de Buenos Aires, in an agroclimatic outlook by Eduardo Sierra, forecasts rain over most of the country’s productive area from October 8 to October 14. Sierra says a storm front finishing its crossing will leave an uneven pattern: strong storms and torrential downpours on the northern Fluvial Litoral, moderate totals over the rest of the farm area, and some isolated heavier pockets. For most of that area the outlook is 10 to 25 millimeters, with the larger accumulations in the east of the NOA, the north of the Chaco region and the north of Mesopotamia; on Misiones and nearby areas the figure is 100 to more than 200 millimeters. The main maize zones of the Pampas would therefore get a more moderate share than the northeast while planting of the cereal continues. The following week is expected to bring much scarcer rain over most of the agricultural region. After the front, cold air is forecast to reach almost to the north of the farm area, with below-normal temperatures and little frost risk: minimums of 10 to 15 degrees Celsius in the north and center of the Pampean region, and 5 to 10 degrees in the south, with a slight risk of localized frost. ### Both Sides: Whether Brazil’s betting ban leaves a hole in federal revenue
O Globo reports that the government of Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has prohibited bets. The question that report puts to the treasury is whether the prohibition cuts federal revenue or only moves the spending that used to go into bets.

The case that the ban need not open a hole in the tax take is made by Robinson Barreirinhas, secretary of the Receita Federal. He told O Globo he is not worried about the tax loss from the prohibited bets. His reason is a shift in household spending, not a claim that bets never raised money. He said collection should be offset by higher revenue from other sectors. Those sectors, in his account, benefit because people who are no longer spending on bets spend that money on something else, and that other spending can be taxed.

The case for the prohibition itself, as published today by SBC News, is institutional: Brazil’s constitutional backbone backs Lula’s gambling ban. The two reports treat the prohibition as the government’s act. Barreirinhas supplies the fiscal reason for not treating the lost betting tax as decisive. SBC News supplies the report that constitutional authority is lined up behind the ban. ### Progress Watch
**Eighty-eight birds taken from illegal trade released in Zárate: La Nación**
The National Environment Subsecretariat says 88 birds rescued from wildlife trafficking were released in a nature reserve in Lima, in the Buenos Aires district of Zárate. The birds, from nine species, had been recovered in four operations between May and June in Florencio Varela, La Matanza and Merlo, with the Argentine Federal Police and the National Gendarmerie. Before the release they went through rehabilitation and quarantine at the Temaikèn foundation’s rescue center. The Environmental Control Brigade and the foundation carried out the release. The brigade’s inspections were conducted under wildlife law 22.421, its regulatory decree, and resolution 306/2020.
