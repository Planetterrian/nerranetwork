> **Argentina gives Britain two weeks to halt Falklands drilling or face a maritime case.**

**What You Need to Know:** Argentina has given Britain two weeks to halt planned oil drilling off the Falkland Islands or face a maritime case. Drilling is still described as due early next year, and only if the UK foreign secretary approves it.

### Lead
**Argentina’s Milei declares war on Falklands oil with bizarre two-week ultimatum: London Business News**
President Javier Milei has instructed Argentine government lawyers to begin proceedings under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea over the planned Sea Lion project in the North Falkland Basin. He has called the project illegitimate and accused Britain of plundering resources, while referring to the islands as the Malvinas. Israeli energy company Navitas Petroleum is leading the development, with UK-listed Rockhopper Exploration as a minority partner. Drilling is expected to begin early next year, subject to approval from the UK foreign secretary. Foreign Minister Pablo Quirno said Britain had been formally notified that an arbitration process was being initiated, that anyone exploring or exploiting resources there without Argentine authorization must face the consequences, and that Argentina is acting under international law and by peaceful means. Source: [londonlovesbusiness.com](https://londonlovesbusiness.com/argentinas-milei-declares-war-on-falklands-oil-with-bizarre-two-week-ultimatum/)

### Across the Region
**Brazil’s Electoral Justice Receives 51,395 Complaints of Irregular Propaganda: teleSUR English**
Brazil’s Electoral Justice has received 51,395 complaints of irregular propaganda through Pardal, an electronic application the court created for such reports. São Paulo accounts for 10,388, followed by Bahia with 5,969, Minas Gerais with 4,635 and Pernambuco with 3,505. Irregular propaganda, or propaganda outside the rules, on public roads is the main category, at 43.78 percent of complaints received so far. So far, 27 percent of the complaints have been converted into cases that advanced to formal processing. The first round is scheduled for October 4, when voters choose the president, vice president, governors, 54 of 81 senators, and federal, state and district deputies, with a second round three weeks later if needed. Source: [telesurenglish.net](https://www.telesurenglish.net/brazils-electoral-justice-receives-51395-complaints-of-irregular-propaganda/)

**Hurricane Polo makes landfall on Mexico's Pacific coast: BBC Latin America**
Hurricane Polo has made landfall just south of Las Barrancas, in Baja California Sur, after moving toward Mexico’s Pacific coast for days, and Mexican officials said its maximum sustained winds were 110 mph (175 km/h). It is moving northeast, and on the forecast track its core is predicted to keep crossing Baja California Sur before moving inland over southern Sonora later on Tuesday. The US National Hurricane Center said Polo was expected to weaken over the peninsula but still to be a hurricane at a second landfall on mainland Mexico. The center said the storm could drop 6 to 12 inches of rain, with a risk of life-threatening flooding and mudslides, and that storm surge would bring large, destructive waves; officials have urged residents in threatened areas to seek shelter, and shopkeeper María Palomino, 57, told AFP she spent the night in a high school turned into a shelter. Source: [bbc.co.uk](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cw8d3z5y7958o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)

### Both Sides: Opening Venezuela’s oil, gold and minerals to foreign companies
Daniel DePetris writes in the Chicago Tribune that the question is whether Venezuela’s turn toward Washington is good for Venezuelans. He reports that interim president Delcy Rodríguez addressed the United Nations General Assembly and met President Donald Trump for fifteen minutes, less than a year after both had treated her as something of a pariah. Nicolás Maduro, whom he calls her old boss, is in a jail in Brooklyn awaiting trial on drug-trafficking and narcoterrorism charges that DePetris says could keep him imprisoned for decades.

The case he attributes to the Trump administration is success on Washington’s terms. He writes that Trump has complimented Rodríguez and is pleased by concessions he can boast about, and that the United States, using the prospect of investment and the pressure of coercion, has pushed Venezuela to reopen its oil, gold and mineral industries to foreign companies. DePetris says relations are the best they have been in a quarter-century, and that there is no disputing that the United States has achieved success on those terms.

The case against, which DePetris himself makes in the same column, is that the bargain is modern-day colonialism, with a distant power setting the terms. He writes that it would be too unfair to call Rodríguez a puppet, but that she is demonstrating usefulness because she has no other choice but to comply, and that the reopenings are policies that would have Hugo Chávez turning in his grave.

The column treats as established that Maduro is awaiting trial in the United States and that Rodríguez has moved to reopen those industries while seeking Washington’s favor. It names no further date for a next decision. Source: [chicagotribune.com](https://www.chicagotribune.com/2026/09/29/column-delcy-rodriguez-venezuela-depetris/)
