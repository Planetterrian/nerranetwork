**Meta faces election interference accusations from Brazil's Lula da Silva; court gives company 48 hours to clarify: Hindustan Times**
On Saturday, Brazil’s Superior Electoral Court gave Meta forty-eight hours to clarify whether it reduced the reach of posts by the campaign of President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. The campaign accused Meta, parent of WhatsApp, Instagram, and Facebook, of election interference after content was allegedly blocked, according to AFP as cited by Hindustan Times. The campaign said Meta prevented Lula’s profile from promoting Instagram feed posts, that a post from his official account had an interruption in view counts, and that before last Sunday’s first round the profiles of Lula and other leftist leaders suddenly disappeared from Instagram’s search function. The campaign’s statement said the technical issues raise strong suspicions that Meta’s actions were an attempt to interfere in the Brazilian electoral process to the benefit of Flávio Bolsonaro’s candidacy. The court ordered Meta to report, profile by profile, all measures applied to Lula’s official accounts and to explain the anomalies in the metrics of the videos. ### Both Sides: Paid citizenship by decree
Argentina’s government, under President Javier Milei, has approved by decree a regime that offers foreigners naturalization rather than stopping at residence. El País reports the price as three hundred fifty thousand dollars a person, with discounts for families. The contested question is whether that paid path to citizenship should stand, and the body now in a position to challenge it is Congress, where the opposition is trying to stop the regime.

The case for the measure, as the decree is drawn in that report, is that the government has already set a priced route to nationality. Milei’s administration put naturalization, not only residence, into a decree, so the offer does not wait on a new statute before it exists. The stated terms are the per-person price and the family discount.

The case against is the opposition’s attempt to halt the regime in Congress. El País reports that effort. Today’s text does not carry a named lawmaker’s speech or a drafted bill, so the opposition’s position on the record is the act of trying to stop a decree that creates citizenship for a fee.

Both sides are acting on one reported fact: a decree is already approved, it prices naturalization at three hundred fifty thousand dollars a person, and it goes beyond residence. What happens next is the opposition’s attempt to stop it in Congress.

### Progress Watch
**Barra da Tijuca é o bairro do Rio onde comércio e serviços mais cresceram no pós-pandemia: O Globo**
Data.Rio, the Rio city government’s open-data portal, recorded fifteen thousand nine hundred seventy-eight new establishment openings in Barra da Tijuca between twenty twenty-two and twenty twenty-five, more than any other neighborhood, rising from three thousand eight hundred twenty-three openings in twenty twenty-two to four thousand three hundred three in twenty twenty-five. The municipal Secretariat of Economic Development, using the Annual Social Information Report of the Ministry of Labor and Employment, counted formal job ties in the area rising from one hundred eighty-four point four thousand to two hundred forty-six point two thousand, an increase of sixty-one point nine thousand, above the city’s twelve percent job growth in the period. Micro-enterprises, those with revenue up to three hundred sixty thousand reais under the BNDES definition, rose from ten point eight thousand to twelve point nine thousand and accounted for about eighty percent of the increase in establishments. Large firms, with revenue above three hundred million reais, grew thirty-seven point nine percent in number and fifty-seven point three percent in job ties, generating forty-four point five thousand jobs, about seventy-two percent of the new posts. Health and social services added ten point one thousand job ties.
