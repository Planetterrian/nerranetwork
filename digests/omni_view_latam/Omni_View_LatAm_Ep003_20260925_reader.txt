MercoPress says the National Institute of Statistics and Census put that figure out on Thursday, for the first half of this year.

It sits 4.1 points above the second half of 2025. Yesterday the government said this rise would be published today. The slide since mid-2024 has stopped.

Extreme poverty moved from 6.3 percent to 7.5 percent, a gain of 1.2 points.

Inside the 31 urban areas of the Permanent Household Survey, 9,729,000 people fell under the poverty line and 2,249,000 under the extreme line.

Argentine media, projecting those urban readings onto the whole country, put poverty at 15.4 to 15.5 million people and extreme poverty near 3.6 million.

By household, 24.4 percent were under the poverty line and 5.6 percent under the extreme line.

Set against the first half of 2025, the moves are smaller: poverty was 31.6 percent then, extreme poverty 6.9 percent, so the gaps are 0.7 and 0.6 points.

The second half of 2025, at 28.2 percent, had been the lowest mark since 2018, when the year ended at 27.3 percent.

Argentina counts poverty by income, against the cost of a basic basket, so inflation moves the line.

In June a family of four needed 1,531,473 pesos to stay above it, and 689,853 pesos to stay out of extreme poverty.

Children carry more of it. 44.5 percent of those under 14 live in poor households, against 36.9 percent of people 15 to 29 and 28.8 percent of people 30 to 64.

Concordia is highest among the places named, at 56.2 percent, then Gran Resistencia at 48.6 and Posadas at 42.2.

Around Buenos Aires, the outer districts reached 36.9 percent. The City of Buenos Aires stood at 11.1 percent.

Places under 500,000 people rose 4.9 points. Larger centres rose 3.9.

Across the region, Hurricane Polo is bearing down on Baja California with sustained winds near 150 miles an hour, The Guardian reports, and gusts stronger than that in the core.

On the 22nd of September the pressure was 892 millibars. That reading makes it the second-most intense hurricane on record in the east Pacific.

Exceptionally warm Pacific water is feeding it, in a season the paper calls notably active.

The track is expected to bend west-north-west, then more to the north-west on Saturday.

Some wobble in strength is possible. It is still expected to stay powerful as it runs north-west.

Hurricane-force wind reaches about 40 miles from the centre. Tropical-storm-force wind reaches about 125. Torrential rain and dangerous seas could hit hard along with that wind.

In Nicaragua, a United Nations group of human rights experts has published a 47-page dossier, dated the 21st of September, under the title The anatomy of State cruelty. Latin America Reports has the account.

The group denounces eight years of government repression, including disappearances and killings.

It details 161 enforced disappearances since April 2018, when protests over social security changes became a national challenge to Daniel Ortega and a violent crackdown left more than 300 people dead.

The experts say the methods have widened the range of people targeted, and they point to what they call coordinated action by several state bodies, on a chain of command that reaches senior government.

Eight people held on political grounds died what the report calls preventable deaths in custody.

One of them is Brooklyn Rivera Bryan, a Miskito Indigenous leader, 73. Authorities announced his death on the 31st of May.

Ortega and co-president Rosario Murillo recently suspended elections indefinitely, a move described in the account as criticized for locking in an effective dictatorship.

The dossier splits the repression into four phases, from Operation Clean-Up in 2018 to measures after 2023 aimed at absolute control of the state and the population.
