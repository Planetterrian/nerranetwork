> **Fifteen hundred Devoto inmates are slated for Marcos Paz as Argentina launches the move.**

**What You Need to Know:** Argentina has started the operation to empty the Devoto federal prison in Buenos Aires and shift an estimated 1,500 inmates to a new complex in Marcos Paz. The move is a security plan, not a completed transfer: a federal command will run it, and the government says the Villa Devoto site is to be urbanized once it is clear. Elsewhere, a blast has stopped Venezuela’s Cardón refinery, and Tulum is down a third in traveler arrivals.

### Lead
**1. El Gobierno puso en marcha el operativo para desalojar el penal de Devoto y trasladar a los presos a Marcos Paz: La Nación**

Argentina’s national government has launched the operation to move every inmate out of the Complejo Penitenciario Federal in the city of Buenos Aires, known as Devoto prison, to the new federal complex in Marcos Paz, in Buenos Aires province. Resolution 1018/2026, published Tuesday in the Boletín Oficial, has the Ministry of National Security convene the Comando Unificado Federal and create a specific command inside it to plan, coordinate and carry out the transfer. That device draws representatives from the ministry, the Servicio Penitenciario Federal, the Policía Federal Argentina, the Gendarmería Nacional, the Prefectura Naval, the Policía de Seguridad Aeroportuaria and the Dirección Nacional de Inteligencia Criminal, among others. The resolution’s stated aims are security for inmates and equipment on the move, protection of civilians and staff, and custody of the prisons, road corridors and other strategic points between the city and Marcos Paz; the plan must weigh the criminological profile of those being moved and risks on the route. The City and the province of Buenos Aires were invited to name representatives. The step sits inside a 2018 agreement to build the Marcos Paz prison and later vacate the complex in Villa Devoto; the government says that once the transfer is done it must move to close the federal prison in the city. The new prison is to hold 2,200 people; an estimated 1,500 places are Devoto inmates, and the other 700 are for people now held in Buenos Aires police stations. After Villa Devoto is emptied, the site is to be urbanized. **1. Venezuela's 300kbpd Cardon refinery hit by blast, ops suspended: Quantum Commodity Intelligence**

Venezuela’s state oil company, PDVSA, has suspended operations at the Cardón refinery after a major blast on Tuesday, Quantum Commodity Intelligence reports. The plant’s reported capacity is 300,000 barrels a day. The suspension takes that volume offline until operations resume. Tulum, in Quintana Roo, the tourism project treated as an emblem of Andrés Manuel López Obrador’s government, is in a slump that El País ties to sargassum, few tourists and weak connectivity. ### Both Sides: Whether the EU should cap Ukrainian farm access after accession
The decision on the table is not a Latin American law. On October 6 the European Commission proposed special terms for Ukraine’s entry into the European Union that would limit market access for some sensitive farm products and reduce access to the Common Agricultural Policy, the bloc’s farm subsidy system. The Rio Times says that design could open space for Brazilian and Argentine suppliers.

The Commission’s case is scale. It said Ukraine’s agricultural sector has a unique scale, structure and productivity, and therefore proposed special arrangements that would significantly limit market access for some sensitive products and cut subsidy access. The Rio Times reports that those limits would protect Polish and French farmers from Ukrainian grain and redirect Ukrainian exports toward North Africa, the Middle East and Asia, with possible openings for Brazil and Argentina in European and third-country markets.

Ukraine’s case is against a lasting cap. Agrarian Policy Minister Taras Vysotskyi said Kyiv cannot accept permanent restrictions.

The fact both sides share is that the paper is not yet a shipment ban. It does not impose immediate restrictions on Ukrainian grain. As of October 7, no product list, quota volume, subsidy ceiling or expiry date had been published. Euronews reported that wheat and other grains were being considered as principal targets, while the Commission’s published language stayed broader than grain alone. Until named products and quotas exist, any gain for Brazilian or Argentine exporters remains a possible shift in flows, not a booked sale.
