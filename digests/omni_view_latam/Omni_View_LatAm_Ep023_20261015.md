### Lead
**Jubilaciones de Anses: cuánto cobro en noviembre 2026, con el bono oficial y el aumento confirmado: La Nación**
La Nación reports that older adults paid through Anses, the National Social Security Administration, receive a 1.93 percent rise on the pensions and benefits the agency pays in November, the eleventh month of the year. The paper says the monthly formula follows the price increase to two decimal places, which is why a September inflation reading of 1.9 percent, as Indec published it, becomes a 1.93 percent adjustment of benefits. After that rise, the minimum pension stands at 444,158.45 pesos. The extraordinary bonus of 70,000 pesos is still paid with it, so the minimum total is 514,158.45 pesos. The Universal Pension for Older Adults is 355,326.76 pesos plus the same 70,000-peso bonus, for 425,326.76 pesos. The non-contributory pension for disability or old age is 310,910.91 pesos plus 70,000 pesos, for 380,910.91 pesos. La Nación says the bonus figure has not changed and has stayed frozen since March 2024. It is credited with the benefit and is aimed mainly at people on the minimum, on non-contributory pensions, and on the universal pension. For benefits above the minimum, the bonus is paid on a sliding scale until the payment reaches a cap of 514,158.45 pesos. The update changes the minimum pension, contributory benefits paid by Anses, the universal pension, non-contributory pensions for old age, disability, mothers of seven or more children, and other non-contributory and grace pensions. For someone starting a retirement claim, La Nación lists the agency’s own steps: enter Mi Anses with a CUIL and a social-security key, check that contributions are recorded under Work and then Consult Labor History, and, if contributions are missing, submit service certificates, pay slips, health-plan affiliation proof, or a sworn statement, complete form 6.18 for pension benefits, book an office appointment, and bring a national identity document that day. The paper’s reconstruction concerns detentions after the march of October 11 in Guadalajara, which it describes as a demand for clean water. Its account says those detained were kettled and beaten. **Operação da PF em SP prende suspeitos de enviar drogas escondidas em eletrodomésticos: O Globo**
O Globo reports that, on Thursday, the São Paulo Public Prosecutor’s Office and the Federal Police moved against a group suspected of using internet purchases to send drugs hidden inside household appliances. The investigation identified about 120 suspicious parcels addressed to three groups of people in Greater São Paulo. Authorities are executing 14 temporary arrest warrants and 15 search-and-seizure warrants in the state capital, in São Caetano do Sul, and in Praia Grande, and also in Palhoça, in Santa Catarina, and in Foz do Iguaçu and Pinhais, in Paraná. Three people were arrested. O Globo says the inquiry opened in September 2025 after authorities identified online orders with drugs concealed in appliances. Since 2024, residents of Greater São Paulo had been receiving shipments of that kind, posted mainly in Foz do Iguaçu. According to the investigation, as O Globo reports it, the group also hid trafficking proceeds through split transfers, cash withdrawals, and pass-throughs to third parties and to newly opened companies with no real economic activity. Financial reports, the paper says, showed movements incompatible with the declared income of those under investigation. The suspects may face charges of drug trafficking, criminal organization, and money laundering. Seized material is to be examined, and the authorities are seeking other people involved and tracing the group’s assets. O Globo describes them as suspects, not as people already convicted. ### The Closer Look
**Muebles de autor, recuerdos del 83 y vanguardia: comenzaron las obras de rescate patrimonial en “el gigante de la calle Sarmiento”: La Nación**
Work to recover the Centro Cultural General San Martín is under way in Buenos Aires after several years of deterioration. La Nación reports that the city has opened a full intervention at Sarmiento 1551, in the City of Buenos Aires, with the stated aim of returning one of the country’s emblematic cultural sites to its former condition. The plan covers putting the structure back in order, modernizing the installations, and preserving the original design. The intervention is described as an attempt to hold heritage conservation together with current technical standards.

The complex is a work of architect Mario Roberto Álvarez. It opened on May 8, 1970. It occupies more than 42,000 square meters, split among an administrative tower, a main building of theater halls, and six basements with room for 900 people. Jorge Macri, head of the city government, said the administration is recovering a symbol of Buenos Aires. He said the San Martín is part of the city’s cultural identity and that the work is meant to put the building back at the level of its history, with infrastructure that is modern, safe, and accessible to everyone.

The first stage gives priority to renewing installations and to structural improvements. Crews are demolishing old masonry, partitions, and ceilings, and they are raising new internal structures. At the same time, specialists are restoring the heritage facades of Halls A and B. The project includes a complete replacement of the electrical, sanitary, thermomechanical, and fire-protection systems, presented as the means of making the building run efficiently. Universal accessibility and updated evacuation protocols are a central part of the plan, with improvements to the elevator passageways. Historic furniture is to be preserved, including the Churba chairs and the armchairs in the foyers, and new equipment is to be added for workshops and offices. In later stages, the city government would install advanced professional lighting and more efficient climate control in order to reduce energy use. Those measures are described as a way to remove risks and to use resources through sustainable technology.

The building’s public record, as La Nación sets it out, is not only theatrical. It was the main computing center during the 1983 presidential election. It hosted the 76th World Inter-Parliamentary Congress. In 1990 it brought together figures of world literature at the Latin American Writers’ Meeting, with talks by Mario Benedetti, Mario Vargas Llosa, and José Donoso. The material published with the project says that, when every stage is finished, a place full of those memories is meant to recover its standing. The stated central aim is to turn the building into a renewed base of national and international culture, adapted to the technical demands of this century while remaining a reference for the national scene. ### Progress Watch
**Mercado de escritórios de alto padrão no RJ registra menor vacância desde 2014: O Globo**
O Globo reports an unpublished Newmark survey finding that vacancy in Rio de Janeiro’s high-standard corporate offices, classes AAA, AA, and A, fell to 23.5 percent in the third quarter of 2026, the lowest level since 2014. In that period, net absorption reached 17,000 square meters, above the 15,000 square meters of the previous quarter, while the average asking rent rose 4.2 percent to 83.43 reais per square meter a month. Over 12 months, the accumulated rise in those values is 7.6 percent. The movement has been led mainly by the city center, which accounted for 62 percent of absorption accumulated in 2026. Among the largest moves, Coren-RJ took about 5,200 square meters in the Bolsa do Rio building, and Cepel arrived at AQWA Corporate in Porto Maravilha. Despite the improvement, vacancy remains high in the center, Porto Maravilha, and Cidade Nova, at 26.9 percent, 27.4 percent, and 39.1 percent. Newmark’s expectation for the coming months, as O Globo reports it, is that the recovery continues, although new developments coming onto the market and caution in the face of the macroeconomic and electoral outlook may leave companies more selective about the space they occupy. For firms looking for high-end offices in Rio, the survey is a measured tightening of empty space, not a claim that the emptiest districts have filled.
