### Lead
**Hurricane Polo strengthens to category 5 off Mexico's Pacific coast: Reuters**
Hurricane Polo has strengthened into a Category 5 storm off Mexico's Pacific coast and, in the Associated Press account, one of the strongest storms on record there. Mexican authorities have issued warnings as the storm threatens coastal areas with heavy rains, strong winds and dangerous surf. It is not expected to make landfall. Forecasters warn of rapid intensification, potential flooding and strong winds for coastal regions and resorts. Reuters said the storm is driven by warm waters linked to El Niño. Source: [reuters.com](https://www.reuters.com/business/environment/hurricane-polo-nears-category-5-strength-off-mexicos-pacific-coast-2026-09-22/)

### Across the Region
**Nicaragua's crackdown on opponents amounts to crimes against humanity, UN experts say: Reuters**
UN experts say Nicaragua's state institutions have carried out enforced disappearances, torture and arbitrary detentions of political opponents since 2018. They describe those actions as amounting to crimes against humanity and point to ongoing repression by the government. Source: [reuters.com](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/nicaraguas-crackdown-opponents-amounts-crimes-against-humanity-un-experts-say-2026-09-22/)

**As gangs gain influence, Brazil seeks to shield election from candidates with criminal ties: Reuters**
Brazil is taking unprecedented steps to bar candidates with suspected criminal links from October's general election amid rising gang influence. Reuters said experts view the effort as a significant move against organized crime in Latin American politics, aimed at protecting the electoral process. Source: [reuters.com](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/gangs-gain-influence-brazil-seeks-shield-election-candidates-with-criminal-ties-2026-09-22/)

**Mexico classrooms go phone-free under new school rules: AP News**
Mexico has implemented new national rules restricting cellphone and screen use in schools, effective November, aimed at reducing distractions and promoting learning. The policy aligns with similar measures in other Latin American countries. Source: [apnews.com](https://apnews.com/hub/latin-america)

**18 suspects in 2021 Haiti president's killing sent to US for trial: South China Morning Post**
Eighteen suspects in the 2021 killing of Haiti's president, Jovenel Moise, have been sent to the United States for trial. They were flown to Florida as US prosecutors expand the case over a mercenary plot against Moise. Source: [scmp.com](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/americas)

### Both Sides: Foreign action against organized crime
At the UN General Assembly, the question is how far governments outside a country should go in fighting organized crime in the region, and who decides. Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva told the assembly that Brazil needs cooperation, not intervention. He rejected any foreign role in combating organized crime. Reuters said the remarks were seen as directed at Washington and at domestic rivals. He also cautioned against outside interference in Brazil's October elections. The South China Morning Post reported that he told the United States Brazil is no one's backyard. The strongest case for that limit, as Lula stated it, is cooperation with Brazilian authorities rather than a foreign role he rejects, and an election free of outside interference.

The strongest case for going further, as officials have stated it, is the threat they name. The South China Morning Post reported that Secretary of State Marco Rubio defended US strikes on boats, warned of a drug-cartel "cancer," and pledged continued military operations against suspected drug traffickers in Latin American waters. The New York Times reported that 14 Latin American countries are expected, on the sidelines of the General Assembly, to designate 25 criminal groups as terrorist organizations, following the US lead, to enhance regional security cooperation.

What both accounts treat as given is that organized crime is a problem governments say they are responding to. The next dated marker Lula named is Brazil's October elections. The Times reported the designations as expected during this General Assembly.
