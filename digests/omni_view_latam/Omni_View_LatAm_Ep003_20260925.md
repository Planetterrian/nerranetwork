### Lead
**Poverty in Argentina rises to 32.3%, ending two years of decline: MercoPress**
Argentina’s National Institute of Statistics and Census reported on Thursday that poverty reached 32.3 percent of people in the first half of 2026, 4.1 points above the second half of 2025. That is the rise the government said yesterday would be published today, and it interrupts a downward path running since mid-2024. Extreme poverty rose 1.2 points, from 6.3 percent to 7.5 percent. In the 31 urban agglomerations of the Permanent Household Survey, 9,729,000 people were below the poverty line and 2,249,000 below the extreme poverty line; estimates published by Argentine media put the national figures at 15.4 to 15.5 million people in poverty and about 3.6 million in extreme poverty. In household terms, 24.4 percent fell below the poverty line and 5.6 percent below the extreme poverty line. Against the first half of 2025, when the rates were 31.6 percent and 6.9 percent, the increases are 0.7 and 0.6 points; the second-half 2025 reading of 28.2 percent had been the lowest since 2018, when the year closed at 27.3 percent. Poverty is measured by income against a basic basket: in June a family of four needed 1,531,473 pesos to stay above the poverty line and 689,853 pesos to avoid extreme poverty. Among children under 14, 44.5 percent live in households below the line, against 36.9 percent of those aged 15 to 29 and 28.8 percent of those aged 30 to 64. Concordia recorded 56.2 percent, Gran Resistencia 48.6 percent and Posadas 42.2 percent; surrounding districts of the Buenos Aires metropolitan area reached 36.9 percent against 11.1 percent in the City of Buenos Aires, and agglomerations of fewer than 500,000 people rose 4.9 points, against 3.9 points in larger centres. **Hurricane Polo barrels towards Baja California region of Mexico: The Guardian**
Hurricane Polo is moving toward the Baja California region of Mexico with maximum sustained winds of around 150 miles per hour and stronger gusts in its most intense parts. On 22 September a pressure of 892 millibars was registered, which The Guardian says makes Polo the second-most intense hurricane on record in the east Pacific, fuelled by exceptionally warm Pacific waters in a season the paper calls notably active. The storm is expected to turn west-north-west, then take a more north-westerly track on Saturday, and to remain powerful even if intensity fluctuates. Hurricane-force winds extend about 40 miles from the centre and tropical-storm-force winds about 125 miles; the paper says those winds, with torrential rain and dangerous seas, could have significant impacts. **‘Anatomy of State Cruelty’: UN report alleges deliberate and escalating rights abuses in Nicaragua: Latin America Reports**
The United Nations Group of Human Rights Experts on Nicaragua published a 47-page report on 21 September, titled “The anatomy of State cruelty,” denouncing a pattern of government repression, including disappearances and killings, over eight years. The dossier details 161 cases of enforced disappearance since April 2018, when protests over social security reforms widened into a national challenge to Daniel Ortega’s government and a violent crackdown left more than 300 people dead. The experts say the tactics have shifted toward a wider set of victims and cite evidence they describe as coordinated action by multiple state institutions under a chain of command reaching senior levels of government. They profile eight people detained on political grounds who died what the report calls preventable deaths in state custody, including Miskito Indigenous leader Brooklyn Rivera Bryan, 73, whose death authorities announced on 31 May. Ortega and co-president Rosario Murillo recently suspended elections indefinitely, a step the account says has been criticized as cementing an effective dictatorship; the dossier splits the repression into four phases, from the 2018 Operation Clean-Up to post-2023 measures aimed at absolute control over state institutions and the population. ### Both Sides: A larger fall in the peso
What is in front of Argentina is not a statute signed today. It is advice to the government on the peso, set beside what a large foreign fund says it will do with its money.

Gita Gopinath, former number two at the International Monetary Fund, makes the case for a bigger drop. Her reason, given to Tyler Cowen of George Mason University in the interview series Conversations with Tyler, is that a larger adjustment, plus a bigger build-up of reserves, would leave a better setting for economic activity. She said the peso has to adjust more and should be allowed to depreciate more than it is doing now. La Nación carried the remarks on Friday as fresh comment on the government’s exchange-rate stance.

The case for standing aside comes from the head of a major investment fund that deals in Argentina from Manhattan. His reason is the election, not a claim that a weaker currency would be an error. He said that for most investors the year is already closed on Argentina, that the reasonable course is to wait until electoral uncertainty clears, and that nobody will sensibly enlarge a local portfolio — exposure may even be cut a little.

Both treat investor caution as the present fact, and both tie the next move to a calendar that is still open. The report does not record a government answer to Gopinath, and it does not date a decision on the rate.
