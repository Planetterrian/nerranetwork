### Lead
**Terremoto no Panamá deixa cidades isoladas e moradores com medo de voltar para casa: O Globo**
On Friday, October 9, at 12:56 Panama time, a quake struck about 10 kilometers from Pitaloza Arriba in Herrera province and about 196 kilometers from Panama City; the US Geological Survey revised the magnitude to 7.7 after an initial estimate of 8 and a second assessment of 7.6, O Globo reports. The National Civil Protection System said 27 people were injured nationwide, with damage to homes, schools, hospitals, churches, government buildings, ports and roads, while René García, head of the Emergency Operations Center, told AFP that at that point there were no reports of deaths. President José Raúl Mulino, visiting an affected area, said the disaster is large and that roads are totally destroyed; Health Minister Fernando Boyd said Tonosí, in Los Santos, was cut off by land. The heaviest damage was in Los Santos, Herrera, Veraguas and Coclé, and authorities recorded more than 100 aftershocks, some as strong as magnitude 6. Panama Canal authorities said the waterway, which carries about 5 percent of international maritime trade, was operating normally, while Tocumen airport closed temporarily and flights were diverted. Tsunami alerts covered coastal areas of Costa Rica, Ecuador, Peru, Nicaragua, Guatemala, El Salvador, Chile and Mexico, with Hawaii also flagged; US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said his department was mobilizing resources and personnel, and the Development Bank of Latin America, CAF, donated 250,000 US dollars and made 50 million available on an emergency line. ### Across the Region
**Fux quer analisar inquéritos de Moraes: O Globo**
Justice Luiz Fux ordered the Supreme Federal Court’s technical staff to release to his office access to confidential inquiries reported by Justice Alexandre de Moraes, O Globo reports. The inquiries involve former president Jair Bolsonaro and allies and include the investigation into an attempted coup and the fake-news inquiry. Columnist Malu Gaspar reports that a meeting this week between Moraes and directors of the Federal Police was the trigger. The newsletter places the order amid a right-wing wave that sent to Congress lawmakers whose main banner is the impeachment of court ministers; ministers assess that the court has two fronts of action after that wave. Source: [oglobo.globo.com](https://oglobo.globo.com/blogs/fumus-boni-iuris/post/2026/10/10/fux-quer-analisar-inqueritos-de-moraes.ghtml)

**A hit to the wallet: Fuel prices to rise by up to ¢89 in November: Q Costa Rica**
Recope, the state oil company, filed preliminary figures with the public-services regulator ARESEP on Friday, October 9, pointing to higher November fuel prices, Q Costa Rica reports. Regular gasoline would rise by 89 colones, the largest move, to 808 a liter; super by 42 colones to 806; diesel by 31 colones to 816. A 25-pound cylinder of LPG, the size most used in home kitchens, would rise by 547 colones to 7,719. Recope attributes the changes to the crisis in the Middle East, risks along oil transport routes, and a decline in global reserves. ARESEP still has to review the filing and set the final adjustments; the higher prices take effect in the first days of November only if that review approves them. ### The Region and the World
**Argentina gets taste of global data-centre boom: Buenos Aires Times**
Argentina has 67 registered data centres, 52 of them operational and 13 announced projects, the Buenos Aires Times reports, as a global build-out of digital infrastructure reaches the country. The government is looking at billions of dollars of investment in that infrastructure. The Times reports mounting concern over energy use, water consumption and job creation. ### Both Sides: Whether Bolsonaro’s criminal review should wait out the second round
Brazil’s Supreme Court is weighing, on more than one track, when legal steps around former president Jair Bolsonaro should move. O Globo’s Fumus Boni Iuris newsletter is the source for both clocks below.

Justice Nunes Marques evaluates that a criminal review of the case should wait until after the second round. The reason the newsletter attributes to him is that the measure has the potential to interfere in the elections. He also evaluates that the request should be submitted to the full court, so the timing is not left as a step outside the plenary. The newsletter presents that as his evaluation, not as a vote the plenary has already taken.

Senator Flávio Bolsonaro, the PL presidential candidate, carries the other position. O Globo reports that a pardon for his father is one of the promises he has repeated most often in the campaign. The newsletter describes the former president as convicted of attempting a coup. Jurists, in the same newsletter, ask whether he could grant that pardon in time to take the inaugural ramp with his father. The case for acting on the promise, as the newsletter frames it, is that repetition and that calendar.
