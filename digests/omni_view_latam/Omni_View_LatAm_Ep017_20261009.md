> **Brazil's gasoline tax exemption of eighty-nine centavos zeros PIS and Cofins before the runoff.**

**What You Need to Know:** Brazil's government has announced a gasoline tax exemption of eighty-nine centavos that it says wipes out PIS and Cofins on the fuel, days before the presidential runoff. In Argentina, federal justice charged a former prisons chief over phones that let jailed bosses keep giving orders. Brazil's health regulator ordered every lot of the supplement Peptistrong off the market.

### Lead
**Governo amplia subsídio para gasolina às vésperas do segundo turno das eleições: O Globo**
The government of Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva announced on Friday an expansion of the gasoline subsidy, on the eve of the second round. The exemption will be 0.89 reais. O Globo reports that this zeros all PIS/Cofins on the fuel. The change is aimed at the tax taken on gasoline as voters head toward the runoff. The announcement is a government decision on the levy, not a published pump-price survey. ### Across the Region
**Procesaron a la exjueza María Laura Garrigós de Rébori por la falta de controles a los jefes narco en las cárceles: La Nación**
Federal justice charged former judge María Laura Garrigós de Rébori, who had been intervenor of the Federal Penitentiary Service. The court found she did not install signal inhibitors and did not control the entry of telephones. With those phones, bosses such as Guille Cantero kept giving orders from prison, La Nación reports. The case is a charge over prison controls, and it reaches people outside the walls who were still taking instructions from inside. **Anvisa determina recolhimento de suplemento Peptistrong: O Globo**
Brazil's National Health Surveillance Agency, Anvisa, ordered on Friday the recall of every lot of Peptistrong, made by Manipulação Stevia Ltda. (Biostevi Pharma). Resolution 3,967/2026, published in the Official Gazette, says the product is hydrolyzed fava-bean protein and that this ingredient has not passed a safety evaluation for use in food. The order covers all lots. Anvisa also suspended manufacture, distribution, sale, advertising, and use. Anyone holding the supplement is inside the use ban. Source: [oglobo.globo.com](https://oglobo.globo.com/saude/noticia/2026/10/09/anvisa-determina-recolhimento-de-suplemento-peptistrong.ghtml)

### Both Sides: Whether banker money for a campaign film is private
Voters and investigators are being asked to treat money Senator Flávio Bolsonaro sought from former banker Daniel Vorcaro either as private funds or as a Federal Police matter. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva made the case against that money at his first second-round event, in Ceilândia, the largest administrative region of the Federal District, beside Workers' Party gubernatorial candidate Leandro Grass. Lula said this country "will not again be governed by fascism" or by "those assassins of thousands of people in Covid-19," and "will not be governed by the partner of Banco Master, by Vorcaro's relatives." He said it cannot be governed by those who "stole 12 billion reais from the BRB," and he asked for votes for Grass. Audios, the paper reports, show the senator asking the former banker for money to produce a film about Jair Bolsonaro. Flávio says the money is private. The Federal Police is investigating whether the resources financed Eduardo Bolsonaro's activity in the United States against Brazilian authorities. That is an investigation Folha describes, not a finding stated today. The shared electoral facts are the first-round valid-vote shares Folha records — Lula 45.2 percent, Flávio 47 percent, on Sunday, October 4 — and a runoff on October 25. Grass took 34.47 percent of valid votes against Governor Celina Leão. Datafolha, released Thursday and cited by Folha for the district race, has Celina Leão at 55 percent and Grass at 37 percent. Folha reports the president's campaign will now stress social networks, a harder line on public security, and the Banco Master crisis involving the Supreme Court. The next dated step is the vote on October 25. ### Progress Watch
**Retail in Latin America: Optimism grows, prices climb: Abasto**
Grupo Ohla's seventh Latin American Survey of Consumer and Retail Executives, released October 8, found that 41 percent of senior consumer and retail leaders in 21 countries expect their national economy to improve, against 40 percent who foresee stability and 19 percent who expect a decline. Peru's share of optimists rose from 30 percent to 79 percent; Chile from 18 percent to 53 percent; Colombia from 17 percent to 45 percent. Argentina sits at 47 percent expecting a rebound. Mexico is more cautious: 64 percent of its executives project a stable economy. Price expectations still run the other way for many: 57 percent regionally expect higher prices over the next six months, including 68 percent in Chile, 67 percent in Mexico, and 60 percent in Colombia, while 71 percent in Peru anticipate stable prices. On margins, 38 percent project stability this year, 35 percent an improvement, and 27 percent a decline; Peru has 67 percent positive, Mexico 44 percent, and Argentina 63 percent foreseeing a decrease against 5 percent expecting an increase. Antonio Mönckeberg, chief executive of Grupo Ohla, said confidence in the outlook is returning while firms still face concrete challenges on prices, profitability, and consumption. The U.S. Department of Agriculture's Economic Research Service, cited in the same report, put imports at 60 percent of fresh-fruit availability in 2021 and 38 percent of fresh-vegetable availability. Source: [abasto.com](https://abasto.com/en/news/retail-in-latin-america-optimism-grows-prices-climb/)
