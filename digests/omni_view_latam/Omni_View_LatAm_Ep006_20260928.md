### Lead
**Accused drug lord known as "The Spider" extradited to California from Colombia: CBS News**
Colombian authorities flew Geovany Andres Rojas, known as Araña or The Spider, to California and placed him in the custody of federal agents, CBS News reports. He faces drug-trafficking and terrorism charges in the United States and had acted as a spokesman and leader of Comandos de la Frontera, a rebel group known for trafficking cocaine in Putumayo, on the borders with Ecuador and Peru. Rojas was arrested in Bogotá in February 2025; Colombia’s Supreme Court approved extradition in October, and former President Gustavo Petro then suspended it, arguing he needed Rojas for peace talks with rebel groups, including Comandos de la Frontera. Conservative President Abelardo de la Espriella, elected in June, lifted that ban and on Friday personally supervised the transfer, saying, “You don’t build peace by protecting narco-terrorists like this bandit,” and asking U.S. authorities that Rojas not negotiate deals that leave his crimes unpunished. U.S. Attorney Adam Gordon said Colombia and the Department of Justice were “making a clean sweep of narcoterrorists, starting with Araña.” De la Espriella has suspended talks with rebel groups; the iron-fist approach recently included militarizing a city in northern Colombia after a drug gang tried to impose a three-day curfew, and he has said he will work closely with the United States against drug trafficking. Colombia has struggled to contain rural violence as drug gangs and rebel groups fight over territory left by the FARC after the 2016 peace deal. Source: [cbsnews.com](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/drug-lord-the-spider-extradited-to-us-from-colombia/)

### Across the Region
**Operação na Maré tem seis feridos, oito presos e policial baleado: O Globo**
Military police are running an operation on Monday in the Complexo da Maré, in Rio de Janeiro’s North Zone, O Globo reports. Eight suspects were arrested, six people were wounded, and one officer was shot. About 200 officers are deployed to combat criminal activity and to suppress drug trafficking and the circulation of weapons. Officers seized 12 rifles and five pistols, and also found grenades and drugs that had not yet been tallied. The force includes the special-operations battalion Bope, the motorcycle tactical unit, the shock battalion, and the canine unit, plus police aircraft and seven armored vehicles. **Terrorist Designations Increase Pressure on Criminal Networks in Ecuador: Diálogo Americas**
Pressure on Ecuadorian criminal networks tightened after U.S. terrorist designations, and the day after Los Tiguerones was designated on September 9, Colombian police working with Ecuador’s National Intelligence Center captured Guido Fernando Rodríguez Vargas, alias Comandante 7, in Soacha, Colombia, Diálogo Americas reports. Colombian authorities called him a high-value target for Ecuador and one of the group’s main links between the two countries; they said he helped administer illicit proceeds used for logistics and weapons in Esmeraldas, Ecuador, and Nariño, Colombia, and linked him to money laundering from drug trafficking and extortion. He was wanted in Ecuador and was the subject of an Interpol Red Notice for homicide and organized crime. The U.S. State Department designated the Chone Killers a Foreign Terrorist Organization in July and Los Tiguerones in September; the State Department says the Chone Killers, a faction that broke from Los Choneros in 2020, has carried out high-profile attacks and killings of civilians, officers, and public officials. After a leader’s death in Colombia in late 2024, five former factions competed for territory, Reuters reported, and by February 2026 Ecuadorian newspaper Primicias said authorities had identified new subdivisions, some under different names. Source: [dialogo-americas.com](https://dialogo-americas.com/articles/terrorist-designations-increase-pressure-on-criminal-networks-in-ecuador/)

**‘Hoje, 100% do petróleo da Galp vem do Brasil’, dizem executivos da portuguesa que investe na Margem Equatorial: O Globo**
Executives of Portuguese oil company Galp say that today 100 percent of Galp’s oil comes from Brazil, O Globo reports. Country manager Paula Pereira da Silva called the company’s Brazil business, concentrated in the pre-salt, the “filé-mignon” of its sector projects. The same report places that Brazilian output beside the company’s investment interest in the equatorial margin. ### Both Sides: Must Niterói feed weekend vaccination crews?
Niterói’s health-workers association and the city government disagree on whether the municipality must feed staff so a weekend animal-vaccination campaign can run its scheduled hours. On September 19, a rabies vaccination drive in the North Zone was planned from 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. The Association of Health Workers of Niterói says the crews had no food during the shift and had to stop around 12:30 p.m.

The association’s strongest case is that a full-day outdoor campaign fails residents if the city calls workers in and does not feed them. Director Jorge Gomes called the situation absurd and said people who came to the posts in the afternoon left without service, and that the association had no official explanation. In a letter to Health Secretary Ilza Fellows, who presides over the Health Foundation, the group said this was the second time workers were summoned for a Saturday without adequate meal plans, and it asked for changes in how later vaccination campaigns and other departmental activities are organized.

The city’s strongest case is that the law does not require those meals. In a note, the city government said no legislation establishes an obligation to provide food. The secretary said a contracted supplier failed on this one occasion and that the problem was resolved.

Both sides accept that the September 19 North Zone drive did not run until 5 p.m. and that the assigned workers were not fed. The association wants the secretariat to change how future campaigns are set up. The city says the supplier failure is already fixed.
