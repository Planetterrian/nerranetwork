### Lead
**Cidadania caminha para desaparecer do mapa político nacional: O Globo**
After a run of court fights over who commands the party, Cidadania reached the end of the first round of Brazil’s 2026 elections in steep decline, O Globo reports. Federated with the PSDB, the party began as the PCB, became the PPS, and later took the name Cidadania as it moved away from its communist origins. It received 60 million reais from the Electoral Fund and gathered only about 250,000 votes nationwide. It elected one candidate to a state assembly and none to the Chamber of Deputies. In 2002, still as the PPS, it elected 15 federal deputies; in 2006 it peaked at 21. National president Alex Manente lost his bid for re-election to the Chamber from São Paulo, and former president Roberto Freire also failed in a race for a federal deputy seat. The only winner was GCM Romário Policarpo, elected state deputy in Goiás with 55,337 votes. ### Across the Region
**‘They take our water, our soil’: protesters fight ‘foreignisation’ as Javier Milei encourages land sales in Argentina: The Guardian**
The Guardian reports that President Javier Milei is courting billionaire investors by removing a block on foreign ownership of land and natural resources. On September 19, Marta Sahores, an 84-year-old chemistry professor, marched in Esquel, in Chubut province, during the marcha de la bronca. She held a sign defending the country’s water against what she called the government’s handover to investors; other signs read that the land is not for sale. Workers, retirees, students, Indigenous groups and human rights organisations marched with her. Sahores has mobilised against mega-mining projects for more than two decades. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2026/oct/06/argentina-land-reform-protests-javier-milei)

**A bola vai rolar no Brasileiro de futebol adaptado, em Niterói: O Globo**
Niterói hosts the 12th Brazilian power-soccer championship this Tuesday and Wednesday at the Associação Niteroiense dos Deficientes Físicos, in Rio do Ouro, with free entry, O Globo reports. Six teams take part: Clube Novo Ser, Rocinha Power Soccer, Rio de Janeiro Power Soccer, Minas Power Soccer, Fortaleza Power Soccer and Blue Angels. O Globo names as favorites Rio de Janeiro Power Soccer, Brazilian champion in 2025, and Clube Novo Ser, a four-time national champion and the first powerchair football team in Latin America. Four of eight players are on court at a time, the ball is 33 centimetres across, and the motorized chairs are limited to 10 kilometres an hour. Ricardo Gonzalez, director of Instituto Novo Ser, said the championship makes these athletes protagonists of a competition, a team and a sport; the discipline is seeking a place at the Los Angeles Paralympics in 2028. ### The Region and the World
**Revolução dos vinhos brancos; queda mundial do consumo; a safra espetacular de 2026: leia entrevista exclusiva com enólogo Sebastián Zuccardi: O Globo**
Sebastián Zuccardi, of the family winery in Mendoza’s Uco Valley, told O Globo in Rio de Janeiro — his first visit to the city — that he expects Argentina’s 2026 harvest to be memorable, a cooler year without excessive heat or rain, and that the first Zuccardi wine of that vintage should appear late this year or next year. He said Argentina is 4.8 percent of the world wine market, that about 80 percent of Argentine wine is drunk at home and 20 percent is exported, and that Brazil is one of the few countries where consumption is still growing, though tax makes wine expensive there. On a Mercosur–European Union agreement he said he does not know the detailed clauses, but he believes it would ease European wine into Latin America more than it would boost Argentine sales in Europe, and that market access is not equal if other producers receive subsidies. The family is building two wineries, one in the Uco Valley for Alambrado and one in Maipú for Santa Julia with a wine-tourism center, and he said they reinvest rather than take money out for personal use. ### Both Sides: Whether Uruguay should keep a president’s affinity with Lula apart from relations with Brazil
What is being decided is how Uruguay deals with Brazil after the October 25 presidential runoff, and the person drawing the line is President Yamandú Orsi.

Orsi’s case is that personal and party affinity must not write state policy. At a press conference on Monday he said that affinity, or what one knows of a person, cannot interfere in relations between countries. His best reason is continuity: he said his relationship with neighboring countries has been the same under one government or another, and that elections elsewhere have not, so far, pushed Uruguay to mix the two. He calls himself a personal friend of Lula and met him on September 21 in New York, during the UN General Assembly, where Orsi said they discussed trade, shared infrastructure, productive development, integration and regulation of sports betting. He said that friendship will not condition Uruguay’s stance if Flávio Bolsonaro is elected. His phrase was that one should not mix the matters up.

The other position is already public inside his own coalition. Infobae reports that the Frente Amplio, which Orsi belongs to, has explicitly backed Lula’s candidacy. So has the Movimiento de Participación Popular, the sector historically led by José Mujica, described there as an intimate friend of Lula. Several leaders of that sector were in Brazil, and Lucía Topolansky, Mujica’s widow, sent a message of support. Their stated act is an open political endorsement of Lula, not a neutral wait for the neighbor’s result.

Both are working from the same calendar: a second round on October 25, and a presidency in Montevideo that has already dealt with Lula as Brazil’s current president. Lula said he thanked Orsi for work so Brazil could take Uruguay’s beef-export quota to China, a step China did not accept. What happens next is that round. Orsi said Uruguay will see how to move according to the result that comes.
