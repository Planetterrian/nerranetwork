### Lead
**Brazil’s Senate may free party lines on a vote to end the 6x1 workweek: O Globo**
The Liberal Party is expected to release its senators on the constitutional amendment that would end the six-days-on, one-day-off schedule, Senator Wellington Fagundes, leader of the Senate opposition bloc, said. Campaign coordination for Flávio Bolsonaro prefers abstention, he said, but most of the bench believes senators should take a position; Senator Tereza Cristina said other opposition parties will also free their members, with the line to be set at a Wednesday lunch. The text would swap that week for five days of work and two of rest, cutting weekly hours from 44 to 42 sixty days after enactment and to 40 a year later. Each round needs 49 of the Senate’s 81 votes. Government leader Teresa Leitão plans to file by Wednesday a special-calendar request so both rounds can be held before the campaign ends on October 25; Senate president Davi Alcolumbre has scheduled debate on Tuesday and Wednesday and said a first-round vote can follow. Flávio Bolsonaro opposes the amendment and, through campaign coordinator Senator Rogério Marinho, wants hours negotiated between workers and employers; party Senate leader Carlos Portinho called the measure an electoral rush. Amendments still in discussion would stretch the transition to six months and open exceptions for shift workers such as oil and airline crews. ### Across the Region
**Uruguay’s government proposes retirement at 60 with 30 years of work: Infobae**
President Yamandú Orsi’s government will send Parliament a bill opening early retirement at 60 with 30 years of work, Presidency secretary Alejandro Sánchez said Monday after a cabinet meeting. The path is aimed at people who struggle to keep working until 65, the minimum set by the 2023 reform under Luis Lacalle Pou. The economic team argues the change will not throw off fiscal sustainability. A joint note from the Economy and Labor ministries and the Budget and Planning Office says low- and lower-middle-income workers could retire on amounts similar to or higher than those in force before that reform; Infobae reports the design is meant to let the 30 percent on the lowest wages do so. People who retire at 60 would not be allowed to return to formal work, a limit the previous reform did not impose. **Ezeiza cuts a runway to 1,850 meters and long-haul flights pull back: FTN news**
Works at Ministro Pistarini International Airport will restrict operations from October 25 through November 11 while runway 17-35 and its crossing with runway 11-29 are rebuilt. Argentina’s airport regulator, ORSNA, says the airport stays open on runway 11-29, with usable length cut from about 3,300 meters to 1,850 meters, too short for many fueled wide-body departures. An October 6 Aviación News review, cited by FTN news, lists carriers planning to suspend direct Ezeiza flying in that window, among them American Airlines, Lufthansa and Emirates. Some airlines are routing passengers through partner hubs elsewhere in South America; American revised an earlier plan to keep some services with extra fuel stops. Travelers in those dates are told to check with their airline even if a booking still shows as confirmed. ### Both Sides: Ending Brazil’s 6x1 workweek before the runoff
Brazil’s Senate is deciding whether to replace six days of work and one day off with five days on and two off, and whether to finish both required votes before the presidential campaign ends on October 25. The amendment already passed the Chamber of Deputies at the end of May and the Senate’s Constitution and Justice Committee in early September. It is the only item on the Senate floor this week.

President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s government treats the change as an electoral banner and wants both rounds done before October 25. Senate government leader Teresa Leitão of the Workers’ Party plans to file, by Wednesday, a request for a special calendar that would break the usual gap between rounds. Each round needs 49 of 81 votes. Under the text, the week would fall from 44 hours to 42 sixty days after enactment, then to 40 hours a year later.

Flávio Bolsonaro, the Liberal Party’s presidential candidate, opposes the amendment. His campaign coordinator, Senator Rogério Marinho, argues for a more flexible regime in which workers and employers negotiate hours, and prefers that the party abstain. Senator Carlos Portinho, the party’s Senate leader, called the proposal an electoral rush. Senator Wellington Fagundes says most of the opposition bench nonetheless wants senators to take a position, and Senator Tereza Cristina says other opposition parties will also free their members. Options still in discussion include emptying the quorum and floor amendments for a six-month transition and exceptions for shift workers such as oil and airline crews. Opposition leaders expect to settle their line at a lunch on Wednesday. Debate is set for Tuesday and Wednesday, after which Senate president Davi Alcolumbre has said a first-round vote can follow.
