Johns Hopkins researchers, writing through Mirage News, followed Medicare beneficiaries who were at least sixty-five and free of dementia when the National Health and Aging Trends Study clock started in twenty thirteen.

The Journal of Gerontology: Medical Sciences posted the observational paper online on October fifth, and follow-up ran as long as ten years.

Frequent meant most days or every day, set against people who rarely or never napped, after demographics, health conditions, night sleep length, daytime sleepiness, and an apnea-risk measure.

Falling asleep without meaning to carried a thirty-nine percent higher risk, and naps of thirty minutes or less carried a forty percent higher risk that survived those same adjustments.

Naps longer than thirty minutes looked weaker once nighttime sleep was in the model, and Adam Spira said the pattern may mark brain change rather than cause it.

Wakefulness circuits can fail early in dementia and push napping up, and dropping diagnoses from the first five years left the frequent and unintentional links looking similar.

Sanford Burnham Prebys put a different assumption under a microscope in Aging Cell on September twenty-seventh, twenty twenty-six, and ScienceDaily carried the report.

Caroline Kumsta said people often claim autophagy fades with age, yet the human evidence for that fade is very thin.

Volunteers from age twenty-three to seventy-two, reporting no chronic disease, gave blood and skin, and some skin cells were turned into induced neurons so three cell types could be compared.

Cleanup activity rose, fell, or held steady depending on the cell and on sex, and the write-up says a small study suggests gentle exercise may nudge older adults back toward a more youthful pattern.

Tatiana Moreno noted growing interest in targeting that recycling system, and nothing in the report is a regulator approval.

ScienceDaily also carried a nail comparison from the European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology Congress twenty twenty-six, the first controlled look at GLP-1 medicines and nails.

Five hundred seventy-five users were set against one thousand three hundred twenty-nine people with type two diabetes or obesity who had never taken the drugs, drawn from France, the United States, Brazil, and Mexico.

Detachment showed up in thirty-nine percent of users and nine percent of never-users, more than fourfold, and discoloration in forty-six percent versus seventeen.

Any nail disorder was sixty-six percent against fifty-three, and the report says weight loss alone did not fully explain the gap.

The summary also ties the same medicines to hair loss and scalp inflammation. Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound, Victoza, and Trulicity are described as weight-loss and diabetes drugs, not as approved aging drugs.

Nature Aging, on October twelfth, twenty twenty-six, reported a large postmortem association study, not a clinic test.

One hundred seven brain D N A methylation sites tracked Alzheimer pathology, thirty-eight of them replicated in an independent set, and methylation partly mediated the link from APOE genotype to diagnosis.

Fight Aging! summarized a preprint in which an antisense oligonucleotide against miR-128-3p restored muscle mass and function in aged mice.

The same inhibition improved heart function and limited bad remodeling after a heart attack in mice, and it eased skeletal and heart muscle disease in mouse and pig models of Duchenne muscular dystrophy.

Mitochondrial programs rose and inflammatory and fibrotic signals fell, a pattern the authors compare with known longevity interventions.

The miR-128-1 locus sits in a human stretch of chromosome 2q21.3 tied, in genetic association data, to grip strength, lung function, and cardiometabolic traits.

Elenae Therapeutics is aiming first at severe uses such as heart muscle after a heart attack, and this report has no human result and no approval.

This week’s mechanism is sleep, and the largest measurement set starts with five thousand four hundred seven adults in the Sleep Heart Health Study.

Mean age was sixty-three, the range ran from forty-five to ninety-nine, and fifty-two percent were women. Home polysomnography plus questionnaires, published in the Journal of the American Geriatrics Society, still only give a cross-section.

The night breaks into N1, light sleep; N2, where spindles fire; N3, slow-wave sleep; and rapid eye movement sleep, when the cortex is busy and most dreaming happens. A circadian clock sets the likely hours.

A twenty fifteen review in Current Topics in Behavioral Neurosciences describes healthy aging as less slow-wave sleep and less of its power, fewer and smaller spindles, lower rapid-eye-movement density, a smaller circadian swing, and a clock that runs earlier.

Mild cognitive impairment cuts sleep quality, slow-wave sleep, spindles, and the share of rapid eye movement sleep further, and the same review describes a profound circadian break as Alzheimer’s disease develops.

In younger adults, sleep consolidates both facts and skills. In healthy aging, that review says fact memory still consolidates in sleep, while some skill tasks, including motor sequences, do not.

Fragmented sleep can blunt consolidation in the young, and more fragmentation with age may explain part of the change. Spindles and slow-wave sleep are the features most tightly tied to consolidation, and both shrink again from mild cognitive impairment into Alzheimer’s disease.

Back in the Sleep Heart Health Study, older age meant shorter sleep, worse efficiency, and more arousals in men and women.

Men specifically showed more stage one and stage two and less slow-wave and rapid eye movement sleep. Women’s stage pattern was a weaker straight line.

Complaints ran the other way: older age did not track poor subjective sleep in men, while older women had more trouble falling asleep, with a trend toward waking at night and waking too early.

Self-report and the machine agreed only modestly on sleep time and how long it took to fall asleep, correlations from zero point zero six to zero point three two.

Extra chronic disease, including sleep apnea, did not account for the age pattern or for those sex differences.

A twenty twenty-two systematic review in the International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health gathered seventy-one studies, with sample mean ages from fifty-eight point three to ninety-three point seven.

Thirty-three were subjective, thirty-one objective, and ten used both. Pathological aging looked worse than healthy older age on both kinds of measure.

In Alzheimer’s disease and mild cognitive impairment, rapid eye movement sleep, efficiency, latency, and duration were the pieces most often off. The reviewers read severity as following the cognitive decline, and objective measures as more consistent than questionnaires.

Men born in nineteen fifty-three in the Danish Metropolit cohort, written up in Sleep Medicine, had thinking tests about a decade apart, two hundred six at the first wave and one hundred six at the second.

Baseline polysomnography covered one hundred seventy-six men, and spindle, slow-wave, and coherence analyses only eighty-two with enough non-rapid-eye-movement sleep.

Trail Making Test B slowed by six point one seconds, and the Symbol Digit Modalities Test dropped two point three points.

Better sleep efficiency went with better maintenance of cognition. More wake after sleep onset and a higher arousal index went with poorer memory and learning.

More N1 went with poorer executive function. N2 and N3 went the other way. Fast spindle size and frequency, slow-wave features, and coherence in N2 and rapid eye movement sleep tracked processing speed, attention, and global scores.

Those were Spearman correlations with a multiple-testing correction, and the authors call them exploratory. They do not claim a forecast of neurodegenerative disease.

Molecular Psychiatry reported twenty-four older adults without dementia, mean age seventy-one point two nine, fifty-eight point three three percent women, a quarter with amnestic mild cognitive impairment.

They had lab polysomnography, cognitive testing, MRI, and a FEOBV PET scan of cholinergic nerve endings. Those endings keep the cortex active in rapid eye movement sleep, when other transmitter systems are nearly quiet.

More EEG slowing in that stage, over frontal, central, parietal, occipital, and temporal leads, tracked cholinergic loss, especially in fronto-parietal cortex and the medial temporal lobe, after sex was controlled.

Split by sex and adjusted for age, the link sat mainly in medial temporal regions in women and in neocortex in men. Women also took up more tracer than men.

The authors float that slowing as a possible marker of cholinergic loss before dementia. Twenty-four people, one night, no diagnostic standard.

The twenty fifteen review adds that insomnia, obstructive sleep apnea, and narcolepsy can hurt sleep-dependent memory even without neurodegeneration, and that sleep disorders get more common with age and more common again with neurodegenerative disease.

Rapid-eye-movement behavior disorder can come before Parkinson’s disease. A twenty eighteen review in Sleep Medicine Reviews only proposes a model: low-grade inflammation might sit between bad sleep, dysfunctional body fat, and cognitive decline, tangled with the circadian system and hormone resistance.

None of these papers show that a nap, a short night, or a missing stage causes dementia, and none test whether changing sleep changes the trajectory.

The decade-long microstructure result is men only, eighty-two of them for the fine EEG measures. Questionnaires and machines disagree, and they disagree differently by sex.

No drug, device, or regulator decision about sleep as an aging treatment is evaluated here. The large study can say age tracks worse measured sleep in thousands of adults after the illnesses they could count, and it still cannot say what would happen if sleep were changed.

One thing worth knowing is that frequent and unplanned naps showed up before many dementia diagnoses, which fits a marker of failing wakefulness at least as well as a cause.

Another is that recycling inside healthy volunteers’ cells had no single direction with age, so a simple fade story is ahead of that evidence.

A third is that a congress comparison, not a randomized trial, found more nail trouble in GLP-1 users than weight loss alone explained in that report.

And postmortem methylation tracked Alzheimer pathology and partly sat between APOE and diagnosis, a biological association rather than a clock a clinic has validated.
