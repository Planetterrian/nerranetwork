After that mouse result, the week moves through antiprotozoal treatment records, a microbe-made NAD precursor, a weighted vest in a diet study, and early Alzheimer imaging, then inflammaging.

University of Sydney researchers, in work ScienceDaily describes from Advanced Science, packed messenger RNA into lipid nanoparticles and put it in the noses of mice so local cells would manufacture brain-derived neurotrophic factor. That protein helps neurons survive, form connections, and change as memories are stored.

In the aged animals, learning and memory rose until performance looked like the younger group’s. Mice bred to develop Alzheimer-like disease were kept from the severe memory drop the model usually produces. Professor Greg Neely, at the Charles Perkins Centre, has said a human trial could test whether the nose offers a non-invasive path around the blood-brain barrier.

No such trial is reported here. The team also lists stroke, traumatic brain injury, and rare genetic conditions as later possibilities, which is a hope, not a finding in people.

Fight Aging! describes an observational comparison, not a randomized aging trial, in people treated for protozoal infection versus people who were not treated. The drugs are those used against Toxoplasma gondii, which causes toxoplasmosis, and against other protozoa. Treated people showed surprisingly large reductions in disease risk and in mortality.

The commentary says the gap is big enough to take seriously, and also says healthier, more active, or more medically savvy people may simply be the ones who got the drugs. One reading is that toxoplasmosis harms people whose immune systems are otherwise normal. Another is that the drugs themselves do something useful.

Because treatment in those people is usually short, a lasting effect on the pace of aging is treated as the weaker reading. These sources do not establish approval of antiprotozoal drugs for any aging-related use.

A laboratory study summarized by Fight Aging! from Science Advances found that some gut microbes, including particular probiotic species, make amide-N-methylnicotinamide, or N-MNA. Human intestinal cells, and cells from fruit flies and mice, took that molecule up and folded it into NAD redox reactions. NAD is the coenzyme mitochondria need.

People with gut dysbiosis and inflammation had less of it in stool, which is an association, not a demonstration that the missing molecule caused the illness. In mice, giving N-MNA throughout the body promoted intestinal stem-cell regeneration and repair of the gut lining. Work in Drosophila implicated SIRT1, an enzyme that consumes NAD.

The paper says conversion to NAD+ runs through the Preiss-Handler pathway, not the salvage pathway, and that the molecule brings a methyl group into the host cell. The same commentary says clinical evidence for material benefits from vitamin B3-derived NAD precursors, in specific conditions, is poor, and that those precursors do not raise NAD as well as exercise does.

Approval of those precursors for an aging-related use is not established here.

EcoPortal reports a human diet comparison, not a lifespan trial, in older adults. People who wore a two-pound vest, with plates added each week, barely changed their resting energy burn. People who dieted without the vest saw that burn fall hard. The result is about resting metabolism during weight loss, not about disease or years of life.

A vest in a study is not a regulator-reviewed treatment for aging.

Fight Aging! describes imaging in people during the long early phase of Alzheimer disease, when symptoms are still mild and easy to confuse with ordinary cognitive aging. The hippocampus, the memory center that draws most of the attention, is not where tissue loss first becomes visible on scans.

The amygdala and the entorhinal cortex thin earlier, while biochemical and structural change is already building. Whether acting in that window, with tools available now, can delay or prevent later disease is not settled by this report.

Inflammaging, this week’s mechanism, is chronic sterile low-grade inflammation that rises with age when there is no fresh infection and no fresh injury, and a 2026 human cohort shows how slippery the age signal is.

Among 153 treatment-naive patients with six kinds of cancer, split at age 60, tumor mutational burden sat higher in the older group: a median of 4.55 mutations per megabase against 2.84. Whole-genome sequencing supplied that burden and the fraction of the genome that was altered. RNA sequencing and proteomics scored inflammatory pathways in paired tumor and normal tissue.

A 2007 perspective in Mechanisms of Ageing and Development treated much of the aging phenotype, immunosenescence included, as an imbalance between inflammatory networks and anti-inflammatory ones. Lifelong antigens and damaging agents keep the pro-inflammatory side busy.

On that view, a long healthy life would need both a quieter inflammatory response and anti-inflammatory networks strong enough to cancel the load. When the balance fails, frailty and ordinary age-related disease follow. The authors said the evidence they were drawing on came largely from people, and they framed the problem as a whole system rather than one switch.

A 2014 report from a National Institute on Aging session, in The Journals of Gerontology, Series A, called this pattern a highly significant risk factor for illness and death in older adults, because most age-related diseases share an inflammatory pathogenesis. It also said the exact cause, and any proof that the inflammation itself produces the bad outcomes, was still largely unknown.

The session’s job was to name the pathways that set inflammation across organs, not to close the case.

A 2018 review in Nature Reviews Endocrinology described the inputs. A small set of innate-immune receptors reads many signals: pathogens, labeled non-self; cell debris and molecules sitting in the wrong place, labeled self; nutrients and gut microbes, labeled quasi-self.

Metaflammation, the inflammation that comes with metabolic disease and too much nutrient intake, was said to use the same machinery. Microbes matter to both because they release inflammatory products, join circadian rhythms, and talk to other organs. The review argued that chronic disease is not only a result of aging. It can speed aging and look like aging that arrived early.

DNA methylation, glycomics, metabolomics, and lipidomics were offered as ways to tell biological age from the birthday in metabolic disease. That is a measurement proposal, not a test already tied to lifespan.

The 2026 study, in Immuno-Oncology Technology, asked which of those age patterns survive once cancer type is accounted for. The age groups did not carry the same cancers, so the main links were rerun in multivariable models. Most of the age-associated molecular differences were substantially explained by cancer type.

What held was an age-related increase in normal-tissue inflammation at the protein level, and a larger tumor-versus-normal inflammation jump in MSI-high tumors: plus 0.065, P equal to 0.015, and plus 0.087 after adjustment. Normal-tissue inflammation tracked with the paired tumor’s mutational burden at the protein level, Spearman rs equal to 0.387.

The authors say that correlation is not evidence that body-wide inflammaging drove the genomic instability. Inflammation scored inside the tumor did not track mutational burden, rs equal to 0.07, P equal to 0.54.

Berberine, an alkaloid from Berberis species and from Coptis chinensis, is the intervention a 2026 Fitoterapia review puts against several of these processes. The review is mechanism writing from cells and animals, not a randomized human trial of aging.

It describes activation of AMP-activated protein kinase through mild inhibition of mitochondrial complex I, which can suppress mTOR-linked building pathways, increase autophagy and mitophagy, and help clear damaged mitochondria and clumped proteins. Respiratory efficiency is said to improve. Pathological reactive oxygen species are limited while ordinary redox signaling stays.

Antioxidant defenses rise through Nrf2. NF-κB is kept from moving into the nucleus, and transcription of TNF-alpha, IL-6, and IL-1 beta falls. The review calls the compound senomorphic: it may quiet the senescence-associated secretory phenotype without killing cells broadly, partly by changing p16 and cyclin expression.

These sources do not establish approval of berberine for any aging-related use, and the review flags pharmacokinetic problems and safety questions.

What remains open is the jump from a shared inflammatory signature to a cause of aging that a treatment can safely cut. The 2014 session left cause and causality unresolved. The cancer cohort shows an age gap that is mostly a mix-of-diseases gap. The pattern sits on the hallmark of chronic inflammation.

The limit the cancer authors state is the one to keep: inflamed normal tissue tracking with tumor mutational burden does not mean inflammaging is what destabilized the genome.

It is worth knowing that a large observational link, in people, between antiprotozoal treatment and later disease or death can still be a record of who received care. Holding resting burn steady with a light vest, in a human diet comparison, speaks to metabolism during weight loss and not to extra years of life.

Imaging in early Alzheimer disease puts tissue loss in the amygdala and entorhinal cortex before the hippocampus, so the memory center is a later chapter. And a higher mutational burden in older cancer patients can still be mostly which cancers were in the room.
