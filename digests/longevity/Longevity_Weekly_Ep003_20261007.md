# Longevity Weekly
> **Eleven-gene reprogramming was given to people before any published human data.**

**What You Need to Know:** Eleven-gene reprogramming was given to people before any published human data, in an uncontrolled use that is not a clinical trial. Large observational cohorts, not experiments, again link cardiorespiratory fitness with later cardiovascular disease and death. A Greek criminal inquiry into a death at an anti-aging clinic has not established that plasmapheresis caused it.

### The Week in Longevity
**KHL Foundation Reports the Likely First Human Partial Epigenetic Reprogramming Treatments:** Fight Aging!
This is an uncontrolled, unpublished use, not a randomized human trial and not a peer-reviewed outcome study. Fight Aging! reports that partial epigenetic reprogramming with adeno-associated viral vectors was given to a small number of people through medical-tourism manufacture, including a Triple Helix Science initiative, and that this was not the first patient in Life Biosciences’ recently launched optic-neuropathy trial. The KHL Foundation, linked to Kenneth Scott (born 1942) and Helga Sands (born 1938), is described as adapting rejuvenation technologies for people who want them now. One recipient, Christine Scott, received an experimental therapy described as an 11-gene construct containing OCT4, SOX2, and KLF4 on March 11, 2026, given submucosally with cognition as the stated target, and with expression described as switched by a doxycycline activation approach. The same report says this corner of practice usually does not produce formally published data. No regulator approval for aging is described, and composition, safety, and effect are not established by a published human study. Source: [fightaging.org](https://www.fightaging.org/archives/2026/10/khl-foundation-reports-the-likely-first-human-partial-epigenetic-reprogramming-treatments/)

**Direct Pharmacological Activation of AMPK Modestly Extends Life in Short-Lived Species:** Fight Aging!
This is a preclinical study in short-lived species, not a human trial. Fight Aging! reports a paper in which the direct AMPK activator compound 991 extended lifespan in Drosophila melanogaster, Caenorhabditis elegans, and Schizosaccharomyces pombe, while in mice it induced what the authors call a pro-longevity proteomic profile rather than a reported lifespan gain. The commentary calls the lifespan effect modest and argues these species respond more readily to metabolic alteration than humans do. The same piece says metformin activates AMPK indirectly by shifting the cellular AMP/ADP:ATP ratio and that animal data on that indirect route are mixed. Nothing in the report is a human result, and no regulator approval of compound 991 for aging is described. Source: [fightaging.org](https://www.fightaging.org/archives/2026/10/direct-pharmacological-activation-of-ampk-modestly-extends-life-in-short-lived-species/)

**After Giorgos Mazonakis’s death, Greece weighs tighter rules for plasma exchange clinics and anti-aging claims:** Ecostylia
This is investigative reporting of a death and a criminal inquiry, not evidence that plasmapheresis slows aging or that it caused the death. Giorgos Mazonakis, 54, died on September 9, 2026, after a cardiac arrest at a Kolonaki clinic where he had an appointment for plasmapheresis offered as an anti-aging service; Elpis Hospital said he arrived at 4:45 p.m. with no spontaneous breathing or pulse and was pronounced dead at 5:40 p.m. The two doctors under investigation deny wrongdoing and say he lost consciousness before treatment; the outlet reports that investigators dispute that account. As of September 24 both were in pretrial detention on homicide charges, which does not establish guilt. On October 6 the family’s lawyer, Charalambos Lykoudis, said toxicology and histology results were still awaited. Health Minister Adonis Georgiadis said on September 9 that the facility’s internal-medicine license did not allow plasmapheresis, and the case prompted the government to announce a new regulatory framework for such centers. The outlet states that the sequence of an appointment and a cardiac arrest does not itself prove a causal link. Source: [ecostylia.com](https://www.ecostylia.com/en/mazonakis-death-clinic-antiaging/)

**Chronic Inflammation in the Aging of Bone and Brain Tissue:** Fight Aging!
This is a review framework, not a new human trial and not a measured treatment effect. Fight Aging! describes a paper that treats the correlation between osteoporosis and neurodegenerative disease as more likely to reflect shared upstream processes than one disease causing the other. The process it centers is inflammaging: a chronic, low-grade, sterile systemic inflammatory state driven by senescent cells and the senescence-associated secretory phenotype. The review is presented as consolidating existing evidence into an immunopathological frame for bone and brain aging together. Source: [fightaging.org](https://www.fightaging.org/archives/2026/10/chronic-inflammation-in-the-aging-of-bone-and-brain-tissue/)

### Mechanism of the Week: Cardiorespiratory Fitness and Mortality
Directly measured maximal oxygen uptake, VO2max, during cardiopulmonary exercise testing is the gold-standard reading of cardiorespiratory fitness in this literature. A 2009 meta-analysis in JAMA expressed that capacity as maximal aerobic capacity in metabolic equivalents and treated one metabolic equivalent as corresponding to about one kilometre per hour higher running or jogging speed. Newer cohorts often estimate the same idea instead of measuring gases. One 2026 UK Biobank analysis used submaximal cycle ergometry. A companion 2026 paper estimated fitness from the heart-rate response to incremental workload on a risk-stratified submaximal test. A reported fitness number may therefore be a laboratory maximum or a calculation.

These papers do not establish one molecular switch. A 2010 review says several biological mechanisms suggest that fitness improves insulin sensitivity, blood lipids, body composition, inflammation, and blood pressure. That is a review of proposed links, not an experiment that isolated a path. A 2026 observational molecular study found higher fitness characterized by lower pathway signals for inflammation, triglyceride metabolism, glycolysis, and vascular dysfunction, and higher signals for cholesterol transport, apolipoprotein particle size, and cytoskeletal remodeling. Those are circulating associations. They are not proof that changing one protein changes lifespan.

The strongest human evidence is prospective and observational. The 2009 JAMA meta-analysis pooled 33 cohorts: 102,980 participants and 6,910 deaths for all-cause mortality, and 84,323 participants and 4,485 cases for coronary or cardiovascular events. Each 1-MET higher maximal aerobic capacity was associated with a relative risk of 0.87 for all-cause death (95% confidence interval 0.84 to 0.90) and 0.85 for those events (0.82 to 0.88). Low versus high fitness carried a relative risk of 1.70 for all-cause death (1.51 to 1.92). Participants at 7.9 METs or more had substantially lower rates of all-cause death and of those events than people below 7.9 METs. A 2015 meta-analysis in Annals of Oncology, six prospective studies, 71,654 people and 2,002 cancer deaths, median follow-up 16.4 years, found a graded inverse association with total cancer mortality. Versus low fitness, intermediate fitness had a summary relative risk of 0.80 (0.67 to 0.97) and high fitness 0.55 (0.47 to 0.65). Studies that adjusted for adiposity looked similar to those that did not. A 2017 review says that inverse pattern persisted across a decade even when methods ranged from directly measured VO2max to exercise-test estimates and non-exercise equations, and it notes that the American Heart Association had advocated routine fitness assessment as a clinical vital sign. That is a professional-society position in a review, not a drug approval.

A 1996 JAMA clinic cohort used a maximal exercise test in 25,341 men and 7,080 women: 601 deaths in 211,996 man-years and 89 deaths in 52,982 woman-years. Low fitness remained an independent predictor, adjusted relative risk 1.52 in men (1.28 to 1.82) and 2.10 in women (1.36 to 3.21). In women the other statistically significant independent predictor reported was smoking, relative risk 1.99 (1.25 to 3.17). Inverse gradients held inside strata of smoking, cholesterol, blood pressure, and health status. Fit people with any combination of smoking, elevated blood pressure, or elevated cholesterol had lower adjusted death rates than low-fit people with none of those traits. A 2010 review says prior studies find that fitness appears to attenuate the higher death risk associated with obesity. None of these cohorts randomized people to a fitness level.

Two 2026 UK Biobank analyses ask whether change, and whether a blood signature of fitness, still track later disease. In 11,530 participants aged 40 to 73 with two submaximal assessments, prior cardiovascular disease excluded, and events within two years of follow-up excluded, Cox models adjusted for baseline fitness, sociodemographic and lifestyle factors, genetic predisposition to cardiovascular disease, body-mass-index change, and physical activity. Median follow-up was 14.0 years (interquartile range 13.0 to 14.6), with 921 incident cardiovascular events and 119 cardiovascular deaths. Each additional 1 mL·kg−1·min−1·year−1 of estimated VO2max was associated with a hazard ratio of 0.85 for incident disease (0.75 to 0.96) and 0.66 for cardiovascular death (0.51 to 0.87). The authors describe an approximately linear pattern without a threshold; declines were associated with higher risk. Metabolomic and proteomic signatures built from the submaximal test reflected fitness with an R-squared of about 0.50 to 0.60 and were checked in 354,222 people with metabolomics and 29,961 with proteomics. Over an average of nine years, 27,659 all-cause deaths occurred. The metabolomic signature was associated with a 39 to 54 percent lower risk of all-cause death, a 90 percent lower risk of type 2 diabetes in both discovery and validation, a 42 to 47 percent lower cardiovascular risk, and a 33 to 39 percent lower colorectal-cancer risk. The proteomic signature was associated with a 17 percent lower risk of all-cause death and a 22 to 39 percent lower risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease, after adjustment for age, sex, ethnicity, socioeconomic status, smoking, alcohol, diet, body mass index, and relevant medical history.

Cause is not established. Illness can lower a fitness score and raise risk together. An estimate is not a gas-measured maximum. A signature is not itself an outcome, and the outcome links remain associations. Intervals on rarer deaths are wide. These papers do not report a regulator approving fitness testing, or exercise, as a treatment for aging. The limit on the newest result is the limit on the older ones: after baseline fitness is already in the model, a yearly rise of 1 mL/kg/min in estimated VO2max still lines up with lower cardiovascular risk, and that lineup does not show that producing the rise will produce the drop.

### Worth Knowing
- A fitness change that still tracks with later cardiovascular disease in an observational cohort, after starting fitness is already accounted for, is a sharper description than a one-time label, and it is still not proof of cause.
- Lower cancer mortality in higher fitness categories, across prospective observational studies, does not separate fitness itself from the rest of the life that produces the score.
- An anti-aging clinic appointment and a cardiac arrest the same afternoon, in investigative reporting, do not establish what the procedure did.

### Evidence Ledger
- Directly measured VO2max is described as the gold-standard fitness method, and the American Heart Association had advocated routine assessment as a clinical vital sign; evidence level: review. https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28286137
- Each 1-MET higher maximal aerobic capacity was associated with lower all-cause and coronary or cardiovascular risk in pooled observational cohorts; evidence level: meta-analysis of cohorts. https://europepmc.org/article/MED/19454641
- Higher cardiorespiratory fitness was associated with lower total cancer mortality in six prospective studies; evidence level: meta-analysis of observational studies. https://europepmc.org/article/MED/25009011
- Low fitness independently predicted death in a preventive-clinic cohort of men and women who completed a maximal exercise test; evidence level: observational cohort. https://europepmc.org/article/MED/8667564
- Reviews summarize fitness as a strong mortality predictor and list proposed links through insulin sensitivity, lipids, body composition, inflammation, and blood pressure; evidence level: review. https://europepmc.org/article/MED/20923918
- Annual change in estimated VO2max predicted incident cardiovascular disease and cardiovascular death beyond baseline fitness in UK Biobank; evidence level: observational cohort. https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42605962
- Metabolomic and proteomic signatures of fitness were associated with later death and noncommunicable disease in UK Biobank; evidence level: observational cohort. https://europepmc.org/article/MED/42394615
