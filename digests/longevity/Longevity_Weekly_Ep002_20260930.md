# Longevity Weekly
> **Fourteen randomized trials showed resistance training raised handgrip strength in older adults with sarcopenia.**

**What You Need to Know:** Human randomized trials and meta-analyses demonstrate that resistance training improves muscle strength and physical performance measures such as gait speed in older adults with sarcopenia. These effects appear in both early and later stages of frailty. No regulator has approved any specific resistance training protocol for aging-related indications.

### Mechanism of the Week: Muscle, Strength and Aging
This week's mechanism examines how resistance training and related interventions influence sarcopenia and physical function in older adults. Sarcopenia describes the progressive loss of skeletal muscle mass, strength and physical function that occurs with advancing age and raises risks of frailty, falls and loss of independence. In one randomized controlled trial, sixty-eight community-dwelling older adults aged sixty-seven point two years on average with hamstring tightness and possible sarcopenia were randomly assigned to high-volume static stretching for thirty minutes daily, low-volume static stretching for three minutes three days per week, eccentric exercise or a non-intervention control for six weeks. Assessments conducted at baseline, post-intervention and three-month follow-up included muscle thickness, range of motion, musculotendinous unit stiffness, isometric knee flexor strength, cross-education effects on contralateral strength, Y-Balance performance and function outcomes. Significant group by time interaction effects were observed for range of motion and isometric strength. Eccentric exercise improved strength more than the control condition, whereas stretching-related changes remained below the minimal detectable change. No statistically detectable difference in range of motion was identified between high- and low-volume stretching. Low-volume stretching required substantially less weekly exercise time than high-volume stretching. A 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis of fourteen randomized controlled trials in healthy older adults with sarcopenia found resistance training produced a standardized mean difference of zero point eight one for handgrip strength, one point two six for knee extension strength, one point two eight for gait speed and minus zero point nine three for the timed up and go test. A separate 2021 meta-analysis of twenty-five randomized trials with two thousand two hundred sixty-seven participants reported benefits on handgrip strength with an effect size of zero point five one, lower-limb strength with an effect size of zero point nine three, gait speed with an effect size of zero point seven five, functional performance with an effect size of zero point seven six and muscle mass with an effect size of zero point two nine. Resistance training during early stages had positive effects in all variables, being particularly effective in improving gait speed with an effect size of zero point six three and functional strength with an effect size of zero point five three. The PROVIDE study, a multicenter randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of three hundred eighty sarcopenic primarily independent-living older adults, showed that a vitamin D and leucine-enriched whey protein nutritional supplement taken twice daily for thirteen weeks improved chair-stand time by one point zero one seconds and appendicular muscle mass by zero point one seven kilograms compared with an iso-caloric control. One meta-analysis found no statistically significant effect on appendicular skeletal muscle mass or skeletal muscle mass overall. No regulator has approved resistance training as a therapy for aging-related muscle loss. The effects on muscle mass were not consistent across studies. The mechanism acts on the hallmark of altered intercellular communication. The PROVIDE study was a completed randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial.

### Worth Knowing
- Randomized trials establish that resistance training improves strength and gait speed in older adults diagnosed with sarcopenia.
- Meta-analyses of human data show these strength gains occur in both early prevention and later treatment stages of frailty.
- Observational evidence connects higher muscle power to longevity, consistent with trial findings on physical performance.

### Evidence Ledger
- Resistance training produced a standardized mean difference of 0.81 for handgrip strength in a meta-analysis of fourteen randomized trials; evidence level is randomized human trial; source https://europepmc.org/article/MED/34763651.
- Resistance training improved handgrip strength with effect size 0.51 and lower-limb strength with effect size 0.93 across twenty-five randomized trials; evidence level is randomized human trial; source https://europepmc.org/article/MED/33921356.
- A vitamin D and leucine-enriched whey protein supplement improved chair-stand performance and appendicular muscle mass in a randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of 380 sarcopenic adults; evidence level is randomized human trial; source https://europepmc.org/article/MED/26170041.
