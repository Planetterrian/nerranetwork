This week's mechanism examines how resistance training and related interventions influence sarcopenia and physical function in older adults.

Sarcopenia describes the progressive loss of skeletal muscle mass, strength and physical function that occurs with advancing age and raises risks of frailty, falls and loss of independence.

In one randomized controlled trial, sixty-eight community-dwelling older adults aged sixty-seven point two years on average with hamstring tightness and possible sarcopenia were randomly assigned to high-volume static stretching for thirty minutes daily, low-volume static stretching for three minutes three days per week, eccentric exercise or a non-intervention control for six weeks.

Assessments conducted at baseline, post-intervention and three-month follow-up included muscle thickness, range of motion, musculotendinous unit stiffness, isometric knee flexor strength, cross-education effects on contralateral strength, Y-Balance performance and function outcomes.

Significant group by time interaction effects were observed for range of motion and isometric strength.

Eccentric exercise improved strength more than the control condition, whereas stretching-related changes remained below the minimal detectable change.

No statistically detectable difference in range of motion was identified between high- and low-volume stretching.

Low-volume stretching required substantially less weekly exercise time than high-volume stretching.

A 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis of fourteen randomized controlled trials in healthy older adults with sarcopenia found resistance training produced a standardized mean difference of zero point eight one for handgrip strength, one point two six for knee extension strength, one point two eight for gait speed and minus zero point nine three for the timed up and go test.

Resistance training during early stages had positive effects in all variables, being particularly effective in improving gait speed with an effect size of zero point six three and functional strength with an effect size of zero point five three.
