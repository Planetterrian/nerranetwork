inside the industry because every new project starts from the same template of least space and
purchased fixtures. Field agriculture by contrast receives its photons at zero direct cost and
sheds heat to the open air. So it's cost structure never encounters this double burden.
The current price of indoor produce therefore reflects the accumulated decisions to treat lighting
and climate control as purchase services rather than as variables to be minimized at the level of
fundamental energy balance. Even when operators adopt more efficient LEDs or variable speed fans,
the underlying requirement to generate and then reject the same quantity of photons persists.
Incremental component swaps rarely alter the dominant cost centers. Building codes written for
human occupancy rather than photosynthetic reactors further dictate ceiling heights, fire suppression,

across every kilogram of output whether or not the crop actually needs that level of climate
precision. These interlocking choices compound because each new facility is permitted, financed,
