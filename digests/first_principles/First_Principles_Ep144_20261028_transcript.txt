Formulation development adds a layer, stability testing adds another, then fragrance creation,

They apply equally to the concentrated surfactant before it is diluted and bottled. Repeating the

chemistry. Another objection notes that pumps and attractive bottles improve convenience.
The convenience is genuine. The pump mechanism alone can cost more than the entire surfactant
load inside the bottle. The same convenience could be delivered by a standardized refill
pouch at far lower material cost. A redesign would start by collapsing the product into a concentrated
surfactant base, sold in bulk or in minimal packaging. Taking the water out at the plant
cut shipping weight. At home, the buyer dilutes with tap water. The next move would standardize the
surfactant blend itself across brands. Nearly identical alkyl sulfates would not each need a
fresh safety dossier. A third step would replace custom pump dispensers with simple refill pouches
or returnable containers. That cut in plastic and mechanism cost is more than half. Three conditions
have to hold before any of this can reach the material floor. Regulators must accept a single master
file for the core surfactant system.
