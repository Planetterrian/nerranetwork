The Raptor story begins with the choice of cycle itself.
SpaceX chose the full-flow stage combustion cycle for Raptor
because it extracts more energy from the same propellant mass than simpler cycles.
The cycle allows higher chamber pressure
and better specific impulse without enlarging the engine.
Methane and liquid oxygen were selected in part
because both fluids can be produced on Mars.
Methane avoids the coking problems of kerosene
while remaining easier to handle than hydrogen.
These decisions set the thermodynamic floor,
yet they also created new integration challenges
that had to be solved through geometry rather than added hardware.
Early Raptor prototypes carried hundreds of separate valves, lines, and brackets
because each function had been added incrementally.
Later versions combined propellant manifolds.
They eliminated entire valve families by changing the sequencing logic.
They moved from machined forgings to cast or additively manufactured parts
where the geometry permitted.
Each consolidation removed mass, leak paths, and assembly hours.
The target production costs per engine fell by more than half
