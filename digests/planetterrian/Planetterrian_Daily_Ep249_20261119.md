### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Potatoes complete life cycle in lunar soil with organic amendment — Phys.org**
   Potato plants grown in five different lunar regolith simulants representing mare and highland regions reached full growth and yield when 30 percent vermicompost was added. Plants without the amendment showed stunting and underdeveloped roots. Those grown with 5 to 15 percent vermicompost showed only partial recovery. The organic matter binds heavy metals in the regolith, limiting toxicity while preserving nutritional quality of the tubers. No statistically significant rise in glycoalkaloid toxins was detected. The work compared results against high-yield Earth soil from Hermiston, Oregon, and supports future in-situ food production for lunar missions by recycling crew waste into more compost. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-potatoes-grown-simulated-lunar-soil.html)

2. **Gut bacteria convert plant sterols into circulating sulfated metabolites — Phys.org**
   Phytosterols from vegetable oils, nuts and seeds normally see only about 5 percent enter circulation. Researchers traced how intestinal microbes transform the compounds into sulfated metabolites that reach tissues. The study clarifies a previously unknown route of sterol metabolism that may influence systemic cholesterol handling. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-gut-microbes-dietary-sterols-circulating.html)

3. **Hypothalamus atlas reveals selective vulnerability in ageing and Alzheimer’s — Nature**
   High-resolution MRI from 458 adults and single-nucleus RNA sequencing of 614,403 nuclei produced a detailed map of hypothalamic subregions. Subregions tied to metabolism, stress and circadian rhythms showed the largest volume loss and transcriptional shifts. Microglia adopted a pro-inflammatory state while inhibitory neurons in sleep- and circadian-regulating areas displayed the strongest gene-expression changes. The atlas links these cellular patterns to the sleep, metabolic and neuroendocrine disturbances seen in patients. Source: [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11305-w)

4. **Pan-cancer atlas charts tumour microenvironment across 42 cancer types — Nature**
   Integration of 3,381 human and mouse single-cell samples covering more than 15 million cells identified conserved stromal and immune transcriptional programs. The resource, released as an interactive web atlas at tmeatlas.stanford.edu, highlights subpopulations linked to poorer outcomes and supplies software for further TME interrogation. Source: [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11270-4)

5. **Arts education linked to better attendance and writing in students with disabilities — Phys.org**
   University of Missouri researchers found that greater access to arts classes correlated with fewer disciplinary incidents, higher school attendance and improved writing performance among students with disabilities. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-art-success-arts-benefits-students.html)

6. **Climate and habitat pressures may raise bat-virus spillover risk — Phys.org**
   Adelaide University modelling indicates that rising temperatures, extreme heat events and habitat loss could increase the likelihood of deadly viruses carried by bats reaching human populations. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-climate-habitat-loss-fuel-deadly.html)

7. **Prehistoric grazing patterns offer clues for reducing European wildfires — Phys.org**
   An international team examined how reintroducing large herbivores might help maintain open landscapes and lower wildfire risk in rural Europe, drawing on ecosystem-service models published in Ecosystem Services. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-prehistoric-ecosystems-clues-future-wildfires.html)

8. **Canadian antibiotic sales in food animals fell 11 percent since 2018 — Phys.org**
   Federal policy changes were not the main driver; sales declined steadily through voluntary industry and veterinary actions. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-policy-canada-effect-sales-antibiotics.html)

9. **Ancient calcite deposits revise Türkiye mountain-building timeline — Phys.org**
   Fault-motion dating in the Central Taurides identified an earlier phase of lateral crustal movement between the Early Eocene and Early Miocene, pushing the onset of regional extension back to roughly 25 million years ago. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-ancient-minerals-reveal-chapter-trkiye.html)

10. **115-million-year-old algae fossils shift evolutionary timeline — Phys.org**
    Micropaleontologists working in Portuguese sea cliffs uncovered an unexpected “algae rebellion” that pushes back the known appearance of certain algal lineages. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-millionyearold-algae-rebellion-portugal-sea.html)

### Science Deep Dive: How Gut Microbes Reshape Plant Sterols
Most people picture plant sterols simply blocking cholesterol uptake in the intestine before the sterols themselves are excreted. In reality, gut bacteria actively convert a fraction of these compounds into sulfated metabolites that enter circulation and reach distant tissues. The process begins when specific bacterial enzymes add sulfate groups after the sterols have been partially metabolized, creating forms that the host can absorb. Only about five percent of ingested phytosterols normally reach the bloodstream, yet the microbial route accounts for a measurable share of that circulating pool. This microbial transformation adds a previously under-appreciated layer to how dietary plant sterols influence systemic lipid metabolism. One practical step is to maintain dietary fiber diversity, which supports the bacterial species capable of these conversions and may therefore affect how much of the sterol metabolites appear in blood.
