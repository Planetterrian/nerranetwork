Researchers at the Institute of Zoology of the Chinese Academy of Sciences mapped male and female human embryos at Carnegie stages fifteen, nineteen, and twenty-three, in work published in Science and reported by Phys.org.

They combined Stereo-seq spatial transcriptomics with single-cell multi-omics, tissue protein imaging, and human embryonic stem cell models.

Stereo-seq profiling resolved major gonadal, adrenal cortical, renal, mesenchymal, and neural populations and classified the dataset into thirty-three cell types.

At stage fifteen the gonadal primordium receives those signals from the adjacent mesonephros and adrenal primordium.

Treating human embryonic stem cell-derived intermediate mesoderm with BMP7, NCAM1, and CHIR99021 increased early gonadal marker expression and shifted transcriptomes toward the in vivo early gonadal state.

Later stages showed PAX8-expressing rete cells more similar to mesonephric mesenchyme than to gonadal somatic cells, and computational perturbation plus in vitro PAX8 knockdown indicated that PAX8 helps maintain rete cell identity.

Three transcriptionally distinct steroidogenic populations appeared in the first trimester, with fetal Leydig cells in the male gonad and two adrenal fetal-zone states.

Fetal-zone state two expressed SCARB1, LDLR, and cortisol-synthesis genes, while fetal-zone state one showed stronger de novo cholesterol synthesis marked by SREBF2.

That spatial work supplies a native-context map of how mesonephric and adrenal cues shape gonadal primordium fate, and the stem-cell tests turn WNT, BMP, and NCAM into inputs for directed differentiation aimed at functional gonadal tissue.

The rete-cell data raise the possibility that a subset of gonadal supporting cells arises through a mesonephros-linked route rather than solely from the gonadal somatic lineage.

If that route proves reproducible in culture, it could expand the cell sources available for regenerative approaches to disorders of sex development or fertility preservation.

One immediate experimental step is to test whether targeted modulation of the three-factor cocktail improves yield or stability of induced gonadal cells in longer-term cultures.

The steroidogenic data further indicate that adrenal-gonad coordination of cholesterol utilization and cortisol synthesis begins in the first trimester, including in programs that can diverge between male and female embryos.

The SmartPE system, built at the Institute of Genetics and Developmental Biology of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, raised prime-editing efficiency in dicot crops, according to a study in Nature Plants.

Inserting the START cis-regulatory element upstream of Cas9 raised transcript levels two point one-fold and lifted editing efficiency at four Arabidopsis targets by about one point eight-fold.

Complementary mismatched bases in the reverse transcriptase templates of dual pegRNAs then lifted average editing activity more than sixfold, to roughly ten percent at those four Arabidopsis targets.

Continuous mismatches, or mismatches spaced three base pairs apart, yielded the highest efficiencies, reaching twenty percent, while intervals of six base pairs or more sharply reduced performance.

In tomato protoplasts, six of nine targets exceeded ten percent efficiency; in soybean, nine of eleven targets exceeded five percent; and five rapeseed targets reached or exceeded about ten percent.

Using synonymous substitutions to create reverse-transcriptase-template mismatches, the team generated single amino-acid substitutions in soybean and tomato at efficiencies one point six to eleven point one times higher than standard prime editing.

Stable transformation produced homozygous edits at the SlOR2 locus in tomato hairy roots at seven point three percent, versus only one point zero percent heterozygous edits with standard prime editing, and seamless FLAG-tag insertions reached up to thirty-two percent efficiency.

University of Melbourne researchers integrated cross-sectoral trade monitoring data and estimated at least one hundred seven thousand three hundred fifty-six species are used and traded worldwide, in an analysis published in Science Advances.

That figure more than doubles the previous estimate of approximately fifty thousand species.

Legal trade is worth roughly ten times the value of illegal trade.

Only the United States systematically records and publicly shares trade data across wide taxonomic ranges, and equivalent records are absent for most other countries.

Something most people get wrong is that wildlife trade is a narrow illegal market in elephants, tigers, or pangolins, when the pooled records run across plants, animals, and fungi in both legal and illegal channels.

For some groups, seventy-five percent of traded species appear solely in United States datasets, leaving the remaining trade invisible to global assessments.

The absence of quantity, origin, and population data for the great majority of these species prevents any reliable judgment of whether current harvest levels threaten survival.

Because legal commerce already outstrips illegal commerce in economic value, the scale of pressure on biodiversity is larger than enforcement statistics alone suggest.

Improved customs and zoosanitary recording systems could close the largest gaps without new infrastructure, yet no coordinated international effort currently exists to standardize those records.

The global biodiversity framework target on trade accounts for only around two or three percent of species in trade and misses the other ninety-seven percent.

Repurposing existing customs processes could create a monitoring system, but that requires further engagement to implement at scale.

Until such data exist, risk assessments and sustainability claims rest on an incomplete foundation, even as legal trade in timber, seafood, medicinal plants, pets, cut flowers, ornamental fish, and fungi already operates at a volume most monitoring systems do not capture.

Researchers from the Center for Carbon Research in Tropical Agriculture at the University of São Paulo tested varied crop rotations with continuous soil cover in Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil.

The approach reduced damage from both droughts and floods while raising overall productivity.

Continuous vegetation cover and rotation diversity were the two practices that delivered the measured protection.

FIU biologist Alessandro Catenazzi and colleagues formally named four previously undocumented frog species collected in Peru.

The descriptions add concrete records to the country’s amphibian inventory.

Verified species identities are what future ecological and conservation assessments require.

A University of Massachusetts Amherst team measured erosion rates across mountain landscapes and found weaker rock erodes three times faster than harder rock even on gentler slopes.

The finding revises models that link erosion rates to carbon dioxide release from rock weathering.

A study measured significantly higher blood levels of prestin in people with chronic tinnitus compared with controls, as reported via r/science.

Prestin is a protein expressed in cochlear outer hair cells.

The protein is proposed as a candidate circulating biomarker, though the authors note further validation is required.

Late-life administration of a three-component senolytic vaccine lowered senescence markers in female B6 mice, according to Lifespan.io.

The treatment produced a twelve percent increase in median lifespan in those female animals.

Short-lived control groups complicate direct interpretation of the longevity effect.
