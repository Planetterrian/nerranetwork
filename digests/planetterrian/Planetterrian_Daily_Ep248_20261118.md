### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries

1. **Sudden heat shocks disrupt bee physiology more than sustained heat — Phys.org**
   Washington State University researchers exposed newly emerged honey bees from six colonies to three temperature regimes in enclosures. Sudden heat shock changed gut microbiome composition and increased mortality, while bees under continuous elevated temperature adapted with lower damage. The team tested both optimal and supplemented diets across groups of 150 bees each. Hormesis appears to explain why rapid temperature change produced greater effects than steady stress. The study was published in Scientific Reports. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-sudden-spikes-honey-bees-sustained.html)

2. **Endocrine-disrupting chemicals pose global health threat — Phys.org**
   A United Nations Environment Programme and World Health Organization report estimates annual health and environmental costs from EDC exposure exceed one trillion dollars. The assessment reviewed thousands of studies on bisphenols, phthalates and parabens and found strongest links to neurological conditions and reproductive problems including sperm decline. Evidence also connects EDCs to rising rates of type 2 diabetes and asthma. Workers in chemicals and recycling industries face highest exposure, with one 2024 wristband study detecting over twenty hazardous chemicals in Thai and Kenyan participants. Roughly one thousand EDCs have been identified, though many remain untested. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-scientists-health-threats-chemicals.html)

3. **Eagles prey on baboons heavier than prior raptor limits — Phys.org**
   University of St Andrews researchers documented talon punctures on skulls and characteristic shoulder bone marks on adult male baboons in Chad’s Ennedi plateau. One individual weighed over 26 kilograms, within the range of some hominin species. The finding challenges the assumption that primates above 13 kilograms escaped aerial predation for the past 2.5 million years. Baboons responded with alarm calls and dropped to safety specifically when Verreaux’s eagles flew overhead. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-eagles-preying-human-sized-baboons.html)

4. **Climate pressure widens income gaps in emerging economies — Phys.org**
   Researchers built a composite climate pressure index from greenhouse gas emissions, particulate exposure, temperature volatility and precipitation volatility. Analysis of 23 emerging market economies from 2002 to 2022 linked higher climate pressure to increased inequality across Gini coefficient, Palma ratio and Theil index measures. Per capita income and institutional quality correlated with lower inequality, while unemployment correlated with higher inequality. The study appeared in Sustainable Development. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-climate-worsen-income-inequality.html)

5. **Synthetic peptides now reach longer chains with greater control — PSU Connect**
   An MIT machine assembled a 164-amino-acid protein chain through 327 sequential reactions in hours. Researchers can now order exact sequences from suppliers and receive them within weeks, enabling side-by-side tests of single-amino-acid variants. Stapled peptides use hydrocarbon bridges to lock alpha helices that otherwise unwind, raising helical content in one MDM2/MDMX-binding sequence from 11 percent to 70 percent. β-amino acid substitutions in GLP-1 analogues shifted receptor signaling balance between G protein and β-arrestin pathways in cultured cells. RADA16-I peptide forms a fully defined gel that supports three-dimensional cell growth and alters chemotherapy response in ovarian cancer spheroids compared with flat cultures. Source: [psuconnect.in](https://www.psuconnect.in/health/synthetic-peptides-are-expanding-opportunities-in-biotechnology-research)

6. **Photon fluctuations reveal electron dynamics inside crystals — Phys.org**
   High-harmonic generation produces new frequencies many times higher than the driving laser beam. Researchers showed that pulse-to-pulse fluctuations in emitted light carry additional information beyond brightness and spectrum about how electrons move in solids. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-photon-fluctuations-probe-electron-dynamics.html)

7. **AI adoption correlates with more managerial roles — Phys.org**
   A Strategic Management Journal analysis of U.S. job postings from 2010 to 2022 found firms increasing AI use posted more managerial vacancies rather than fewer. The study linked AI adoption to shifts in required managerial skills. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-ai-managerial-skills.html)

8. **Invasive plants remain sparse in Andalusian mountains — Phys.org**
   University of Córdoba researchers surveyed Sierra Nevada and Sierra de los Filabres and found invasive plants concentrated near road edges, declining sharply at higher elevations. The study highlights vulnerability of these ecosystems to future climate-driven spread. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-invasive-limited-andalusia-mountains.html)

9. **Gender value differences stable across Europe — Phys.org**
   A recent study found no overall widening of gender differences in values despite media focus on young men’s conservatism. The pattern appears driven by social and conventional media coverage rather than population-level trends. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-gender-differences-values-widening-europe.html)

10. **Owner lifestyle shapes dog gut microbiome composition — Phys.org**
    A study of hundreds of dogs across four nations found that pet owners’ daily habits influence the microbial communities inside their dogs. The research underscores how shared environments link human and canine health. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-human-lifestyles-affect-gut-microbiome.html)

11. **Quantum-gas microscope images individual strontium atoms — Phys.org**
    Researchers at ICFO developed a microscope that resolves single atoms in strontium quantum gases in one shot. Earlier work imaged bosonic strontium-84; the new system extends capability to additional atomic species. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-quantum-gas-microscope-individual-atoms.html)

12. **Engineered gel delivers particles to injured spinal cord cells — Phys.org**
    FAMU-FSU College of Engineering scientists created a gel that releases therapeutic particles at sites of spinal cord injury. The material aims to support recovery of damaged cells in laboratory models. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-gel-particle-spinal-cord-cells.html)
