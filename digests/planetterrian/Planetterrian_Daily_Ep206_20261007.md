### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Petal windows alter light and heat in flowers — Phys.org**
   Some flowers have small semitranslucent sections in their petals. Field experiments showed these windows transmitted more ultraviolet and infrared radiation than pigmented tissue. Internal floral temperature predicted microbial numbers, with warmer flowers harboring fewer microbes. Microbial communities inside flowers can affect nectar quality. This structure may influence how pollinators interact with flowers. Corresponding author Jessica Williams noted that a small floral trait can change the physical environment inside a flower. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-petal-windows-affect-abundance-microbes.html)

2. **Nanoparticles improve gibberellic acid delivery in grapes — Phys.org**
   Gibberellic acid is highly sensitive to heat and light. Loading it into biodegradable nanoparticles improved grape yield and quality. Field applications showed treatment increased grape cluster weight by 104 percent and length by 67 percent compared with free gibberellic acid. Berry weight, length and width increased by 15 percent, 4 percent and 13 percent respectively. The approach offers more efficient delivery of plant growth regulators. The study appeared in the Journal of the Science of Food and Agriculture. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-biodegradable-nanoparticle-technology-agricultural-production.html)

3. **Ridesharing linked to lower crime rates in cities — Phys.org**
   Data from the staggered rollout of ridesharing services in U.S. cities since 2010 were analyzed. Services were linked to a 4.6 percent reduction in violent crimes and a 5.6 percent reduction in property crimes. Burglaries fell by 10.5 percent. No significant effects appeared on larceny, motor vehicle theft or arson. Improved employment opportunities generated by the platforms may have contributed. Study author Emtiaz Hossain Hritan is at the University of California, Irvine. The work was published in Economic Inquiry. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-ridesharing-safety-benefits.html)

### Planetterrian Spotlight
The oyster reef study assigns a gross ecosystem product value of twelve trillion dollars annually to these habitats. That figure captures services ranging from water filtration to coastal protection and fisheries support. Restoration would be required to realize the full amount, because many reefs have already been lost or degraded. The calculation treats ecological output the way economists treat gross domestic product, giving planners a single comparable metric. One open question is how quickly restored reefs could scale those benefits in different ocean basins.

### Science Deep Dive: How petal structure shapes internal flower conditions
Most people assume flower color exists only to attract pollinators from a distance. In reality the semitranslucent windows also change the physical environment inside the bloom itself. Light and heat that pass through those windows raise internal temperature enough to limit microbial growth. Warmer flowers in the field experiments carried measurably fewer microbes than cooler ones. The temperature shift occurs because the windows transmit more infrared and ultraviolet radiation than surrounding pigmented tissue. That change in turn affects nectar quality and the microbes that live in it. One concrete number from the work is the consistent prediction that internal temperature alone explained microbial abundance across the tested species. Restoring or breeding for petal windows could therefore become a practical lever for managing pollinator interactions without chemical inputs.
