### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries

1. **Brain waste-clearing waves weaken with age — MIT Health**
   MIT researchers scanned adults aged 18–39 and 60–84 during sleep with EEG and specialized MRI. Slow waves during non-REM sleep normally drive larger cerebrospinal fluid pulses that clear debris. Older participants showed significantly smaller cerebrospinal fluid waves even when sleep duration was comparable. Reduced neurovascular coupling and weaker blood-flow responses to breath-holding tasks accompanied the change. The team plans to test whether boosting cerebrospinal fluid flow could alter neurodegenerative risk. Source: [news.mit.edu](https://news.mit.edu/2026/waste-clearing-waves-in-brain-during-sleep-are-impaired-in-older-adults-1113)

2. **Tremella mushroom compounds extend nematode lifespan via SKN-1 — Bioengineer.org**
   Hydrocolloids extracted from Tremella mushrooms lengthened lifespan in nematodes through the SKN-1 gene pathway. The study measured survival curves and identified the transcription factor as the required mediator. Source: [bioengineer.org](https://bioengineer.org/tremella-mushroom-hydrocolloids-extend-nematode-lifespan-through-skn-1-gene-pathway/)

3. **Crigler-Najjar gene therapy holds efficacy at four years — Bioengineer.org**

Source: [bioengineer.org](https://bioengineer.org/gene-therapy-for-crigler-najjar-syndrome-sustains-efficacy-at-four-years/)

4. **Menopausal hormone therapy reshapes gut and vaginal microbiomes — Nature Medicine**
   A six-month study tracked perimenopausal women receiving hormone therapy and documented multi-site microbiome shifts. Vaginal communities moved toward a reproductive-age Lactobacillus-dominant state. Baseline gut and vaginal community types predicted speed of symptom relief. Clinical plus microbiome features together forecasted rapid remission. Source: [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04789-6)

### Science Deep Dive: How slow brain waves move fluid to clear waste
Most people assume deep sleep mainly restores energy and consolidates memory, yet the same slow waves also generate rhythmic cerebrospinal fluid pulses that flush metabolic debris from brain tissue. The mechanism is immediate: each large, low-frequency EEG wave coincides with a measurable drop in blood volume followed by an influx of cerebrospinal fluid that carries waste toward venous drainage. In the MIT study, older adults produced markedly smaller fluid waves even when total sleep time matched younger participants, showing the clearance step itself declines with age rather than simply tracking shorter sleep. One concrete observation is that the average cerebrospinal fluid wave amplitude dropped substantially in the 60–84 group compared with the 18–39 group under identical scanning conditions. This matters because sustained waste accumulation is one hypothesized contributor to later neurodegeneration. What changes for a listener is the practical reminder that protecting deep-sleep architecture—through consistent schedules, light exposure timing, and treatment of sleep apnea—directly supports the fluid-clearance process whose impairment the study documented.
