### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Sweeteners alter gut bacteria growth in lab tests — ScienceDaily**
   Researchers tested 39 sweeteners and recorded more than 100 instances where the compounds changed microbial growth when mixed with medications, caffeine, or flavorings. The combination of isosteviol and the antidepressant duloxetine stood out for cutting beneficial bacteria and lowering overall diversity. The work shows sweeteners can act directly on microbes rather than only through host metabolism. Follow-up experiments will test whether these shifts appear in human gut communities.

2. **Single-block detector reconstructs particle paths in 3D — ScienceDaily**
   The PLATON detector uses a light-producing material, a light-field camera, and sensitive photon sensors plus AI to map particle trajectories. Simulations indicate performance equal to or better than current systems built from millions of separate components. The design simplifies scaling and could improve resolution in PET medical imaging. The team plans prototype construction and validation against existing detectors.

3. **EP2 receptor blockade preserves macrophage function in aged mice — Science**
   Blocking the prostaglandin EP2 receptor maintained the ability of tissue-resident macrophages to clear senescent cells in multiple mouse organs. The intervention limited mitochondrial decline and other age-related tissue changes. Restored clearance of senescent neutrophils was one measurable outcome. Researchers will next examine whether similar signaling changes occur in human aging tissues.

4. **Engineered bacteria enable self-destructing plastic — ScienceDaily**
   A living plastic material incorporates bacteria that trigger complete breakdown within six days once activated. The process leaves no microplastic fragments. The material targets single-use applications where rapid, clean degradation is needed. Further work will optimize bacterial strains and polymer compatibility for different use cases.

5. **Golf produces nearly double its direct spending in societal value — Phys.org**
   Finnish players spend about €330 million yearly on the sport, generating an estimated €630 million in total societal benefits according to a Frontiers in Sports and Active Living analysis. The study quantified health, social, and economic returns beyond direct expenditures. Findings support continued public investment in accessible courses.

6. **UN report maps path to sustainable ocean economies — Phys.org**
   The document outlines practical steps for governments to balance marine resource use with pollution control, climate adaptation, and biodiversity protection. It draws on the $2.2 trillion global ocean economy and the billions of people who rely on these ecosystems. Authors emphasize measurable targets for pollution reduction and habitat restoration.

7. **Water plays complex role in older women’s well-being — Phys.org**
   Austrian research found that water-related activities and access provide physical, social, and psychological benefits that extend beyond exercise alone. The study highlights how daily water interactions support mental health and community connection in later life. Results suggest urban planning should prioritize safe water access for aging populations.

8. **Rare mussels rediscovered in Rhode Island river — Phys.org**
   University of Rhode Island surveys located living freshwater mussel populations in the Moosup River for the first time in over thirty years. Several native species sensitive to water quality were present. Their return serves as a biological indicator of improving river conditions.

9. **GPR40 activation delays thymic aging in mice — Lifespan.io**
   Targeting the GPR40 receptor preserved thymic structure and function in older animals, countering normal involution. The thymus produces T cells critical for immune defense. The Aging Cell study identifies a receptor-level intervention that could maintain immune capacity with age. Next steps include testing receptor agonists in additional models.

10. **Graphene nanoribbons withstand gamma radiation — Phys.org**
    University of Arizona tests showed the material retains electronic properties after exposure levels relevant to fusion reactor environments. The nanoribbons could serve as durable sensors inside extreme radiation fields. Researchers will evaluate long-term stability under combined heat and radiation stress.

11. **Climate change shifts waterborne disease patterns globally — Phys.org**
    A Nature Reviews Microbiology review synthesizes evidence that warming and altered precipitation change pathogen survival, transmission routes, and geographic ranges. Different pathogens respond variably, complicating uniform predictions. The analysis calls for region-specific surveillance and adaptation strategies.

12. **Bacteria in river sediment reveal hidden pollution — Phys.org**
    University of Seville researchers mapped microbial communities along the Guadalquivir River to detect contamination not captured by standard chemical tests. Shifts in bacterial composition flagged pollution sources and gradients. The approach offers a sensitive, low-cost monitoring tool for large river systems.

13. **Wildfire smoke reaches unhealthy levels across Midwest and East — Phys.org**
    Smoke from distant fires reduced visibility and triggered air-quality alerts from the Great Lakes to the Atlantic coast. Officials advised staying indoors during peak periods. The event underscores how distant wildfires can affect population centers far from the burn sites.

14. **MDCalc introduces ratings for clinical calculators — STAT News**
    The platform will score more than 800 tools used for risk assessment, eligibility decisions, and diagnosis on bias and validity criteria. The system aims to help clinicians choose reliable calculators. Initial ratings will cover widely used scores with plans for ongoing updates.

15. **Stem cell therapy shows zero mortality in early newborn trial — Fierce Biotech**
    Axiom’s Wharton’s jelly-derived cells produced no deaths over two years in infants with severe neurological conditions. The phase 1 data support the company’s approach to targeted brain delivery. Further trials will test efficacy in larger groups.

### Planetterrian Spotlight
The EP2 receptor findings build directly on earlier work tracking how tissue-resident macrophages lose clearance capacity with age. In the mouse model, blocking this single lipid signal restored senescent neutrophil removal and protected mitochondrial function across organs. The result points to a concrete molecular handle on one driver of chronic inflammation. Human tissue studies will be the clearest next indicator of whether the pathway behaves similarly.

### Science Deep Dive: Sweeteners and the Gut
Most people assume non-caloric sweeteners pass through the body without affecting anything except taste receptors. In reality they reach the colon and interact directly with resident microbes. Right now, as you digest a diet drink, certain bacterial species are already adjusting their growth rates in response to those compounds. One lab survey found over 100 distinct interaction patterns when sweeteners were combined with common drugs or flavorings, including sharp drops in microbial diversity. A single memorable example: the pairing of isosteviol with duloxetine cut beneficial populations more than either substance alone. The practical takeaway is to notice how your own digestion or bowel patterns shift when you change sweetener intake, and to watch for larger human feeding studies that will test whether these lab shifts translate to measurable microbiome changes.
