3. **Sphingosine one phosphate drives stem cell differentiation into cardiomyocytes** — dongascience.com
   Korea Research Institute of Bioscience and Biotechnology team led by Son Mi young and Lee Mi ok published in Experimental and Molecular Medicine. Application of S1P or S1PR1 selective agonist SEW2871 to human pluripotent stem cells yielded more than ninety percent differentiation into cardiomyocytes. The cardiomyocytes showed increased cell size densely aligned sarcomeres and more regular pacing signals. Bovine serum is avoided because the specific component responsible for growth was unknown until this work. Mature cardiomyocytes transplanted into animals with myocardial infarction engrafted into cardiac tissue. Source: [dongascience.com](https://www.dongascience.com/en/news/80145)

### Planetterrian Spotlight
The KRIBB work identifies sphingosine one phosphate as the serum component that drives over ninety percent differentiation of stem cells into functional cardiomyocytes. Earlier methods left cells immature with weak contraction and irregular electrical properties. Adding the S1PR1 agonist produced larger cells with aligned sarcomeres and robust pacing. When transplanted the cells integrated into damaged heart tissue in animal models. This removes the need for bovine serum and improves consistency across cell lines. The approach points toward scalable production of mature heart muscle for regenerative use. What remains to be tested is whether similar maturation signals operate in other stem cell derived tissues.

### Science Deep Dive: How Bone Responds to Reduced Loading
Most people assume bone loss from periods of low activity reverses quickly once normal movement resumes. In reality bone adapts to the loads it experiences and stays reduced in density when those loads stay low for extended periods. Right now as you sit or stand your skeleton constantly senses mechanical stress through osteocytes that signal formation or breakdown. A memorable number from astronaut data shows that flights longer than ninety days produce measurably higher hip fracture rates years later compared with shorter missions. The same principle applies on Earth during prolonged bed rest or reduced mobility after injury. The practical takeaway is to include high impact or resistance movements several times a week to maintain the loading signals bones need.
