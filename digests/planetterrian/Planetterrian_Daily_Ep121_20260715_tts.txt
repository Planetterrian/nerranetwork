Biodiversity delivers the largest productivity gains to drier grasslands precisely during extreme drought.

A global synthesis of seventy five biodiversity experiments found that drier grasslands gain the greatest productivity benefit from higher species diversity when extreme drought hits.

Forests showed no comparable context-dependent pattern.

Researchers at Yokohama National University published the results in Nature Ecology and Evolution.

The pattern indicates diversity stabilizes biomass production specifically where water stress is already high.

Follow-up work will test whether the same relationship holds under combined heat and drought.

Most people assume biodiversity mainly preserves rare species for their own sake.

In reality, diverse plant communities buffer ecosystem productivity most strongly when conditions turn harsh.

Right now, as grasslands experience longer dry spells, the extra species present convert limited water into biomass more reliably than monocultures.

One memorable number is that the synthesis drew on seventy five separate experiments spanning multiple continents.

The practical takeaway is to favour mixed plantings in restoration projects or farm margins rather than single-crop or single-grass solutions.

Those mixtures are the ones most likely to maintain output when drought arrives.

Field trials by Vrije Universiteit Brussel and UCLouvain demonstrated that nature-inclusive farming practices can maintain food production while restoring species diversity. The Biological Conservation study quantified both yield and ecological metrics. Soil health and pollinator abundance improved without external inputs. Larger farm networks will test scalability across European regions.

The approach is termed agriwilding.

Captive chimpanzees consistently chose crystals and examined them with prolonged attention, suggesting an innate attraction to the same visual features that drew early humans. The behaviour appeared before any practical use for the objects. Researchers propose the preference predates tool-making and may reflect sensory bias. Further tests will compare responses across primate species.

The observations were reported by ScienceDaily.

Now we turn from ecosystem buffering under stress to how social and cultural factors may buffer human aging.

Older adults who regularly attended cinema, museums, or concerts showed body-function measures equivalent to people roughly three years younger.

Stronger social connections, improved mental health, and healthier behaviours are proposed as linking mechanisms.

The observational study could not establish direct causation.

Researchers call for trials that test whether increasing cultural engagement slows functional decline.

From individual health behaviours we move to population-level environmental exposures that also shape birth outcomes.

Analysis of seven hundred thirty five thousand eight hundred thirty one singleton births in New Zealand linked higher nitrate concentrations at maternal residence to elevated preterm delivery, even below current regulatory limits.

The Environmental Research study covered births from two thousand eight to two thousand twenty one.

No safe threshold was identified within the observed range.

Public-health follow-up will examine whether treatment upgrades reduce the association.

Public-health findings on water quality lead us to another early-life mechanism, this time in amphibians facing a deadly pathogen.

Survivors of the chytrid fungus develop robust immune defences while still tadpoles, before metamorphosis exposes them to the pathogen.

The work also catalogued a large set of previously unknown antimicrobial peptides.

University College London, ZSL and Imperial College London led the study.

The peptides may offer templates for new anti-infective compounds.

Next steps include testing whether early-life immune training can be replicated in captive breeding programs.

Hydrogen-oxidizing bacteria produced more poly[(R)down three-hydroxybutyrate] when carbon dioxide was reduced under non-flammable gas conditions. The process enables safer, more efficient gas fermentation for the bioplastic. The study demonstrates a route to couple carbon recycling with lower energy input. Scale-up trials are planned to confirm industrial viability.

The findings were published in Phys dot org.

Southampton and Singapore researchers developed an electric-field and water-based assembly technique that avoids solvent residues in atomically thin stacks. The ultra-clean interfaces are intended for quantum devices and advanced electronics. The approach bypasses conventional transfer steps that introduce defects. Prototype quantum sensors are the first targeted application.

The method was described in Phys dot org.

Current carbon emissions are altering ocean chemistry at a rate that connects present-day outputs to deep-time geological records. The signal integrates atmospheric CO2 with long-term carbon-cycle memory. Researchers argue this framing expands the variable beyond local ecosystem impacts. Monitoring networks will track whether the signal strengthens predictably with cumulative emissions.

The analysis appeared in Phys dot org.

Brown University scientists produced new thickness estimates for the Moon’s loose surface layer using orbital and sample data. The maps refine landing-site selection and resource-utilization models. Thinner regolith zones may expose more bedrock for construction. Additional orbital datasets will refine the global model. The estimates were reported by Phys dot org.

From immune mechanisms in nature we shift to a simple dietary interaction that alters human nitrite levels.

Participants who chewed sugary gum after nitrate-rich vegetables or beetroot juice generated more nitrite and showed a transient drop in blood pressure.

The effect stems from enhanced oral nitrate reduction.

Researchers aim to develop sugar-free alternatives that preserve the benefit.

Larger trials will test sustained cardiovascular outcomes.

Oral biology gives way to an enzyme that acts inside human cells to clear long-lived protein damage.

The enzyme removes accumulated damage from long-lived proteins in human tissue samples.

Treated cells showed reversal of molecular aging signatures spanning decades.

The work remains at the in-vitro stage.

In-vivo delivery and safety studies are required before any therapeutic consideration.

A wearable that activates the vagus nerve increases blood-clot formation and may offer a non-invasive option for life-threatening hemorrhage. Preclinical data support further device optimization. Human safety studies are the immediate next step. The approach could complement existing tourniquet and pharmacologic methods. The device development was covered by Fierce Biotech.

Patients with Parkinson’s, multiple sclerosis, dementia or traumatic brain injury showed cognitive gains after sessions that fed back real-time brain electrical activity.

Larger controlled trials will determine durability of the effect.
