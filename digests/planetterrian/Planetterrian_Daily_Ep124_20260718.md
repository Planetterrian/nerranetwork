### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **South African genomics projects allocate 10% of budgets to community benefits — Phys.org**
   The approach uses community-led decision-making to translate ethical principles into action. Ngoni Ngwarai from the SAMRC/Wits Rural Public Health and Health Transitions Research Unit presented the blueprint at the World Congress of Bioethics. Researchers, universities, funders and industry partners can now apply the operational model in practice. The framework addresses how to embed benefit sharing directly into genomics studies.

2. **New 'super silk' maintains shape after repeated wetting — Phys.org**
   Conventional silk shrinks and loses form with moisture exposure despite its strength and biocompatibility. Researchers produced a modified version from silkworm cocoons that resists these changes. The material remains lightweight and suitable for clothing and medical uses. The improvement targets a long-standing limitation in natural silk performance.

3. **New scale measures university students' AI self-efficacy — Phys.org**
   Researchers at Koç University School of Nursing adapted the Artificial Intelligence Self-Efficacy Scale into Turkish. Associate Professor Remziye Semerci Şahin and Assistant Professor Seda Güney led the psychometric evaluation. The study appeared in the International Journal of Human–Computer Interaction. The tool assesses perceived confidence in using artificial intelligence technologies among students.

4. **Several UK mosquito species identified as potential avian malaria carriers — Phys.org**
   The Plasmodium parasite causes avian malaria in wild birds worldwide, including Britain. Researchers examined which local mosquito species transmit the parasite. Limited prior data existed on mosquito vectors for bird diseases compared with human pathogens. The work clarifies transmission routes in UK ecosystems.

5. **High school track experience provides overlooked edge for MLB players — Phys.org**
   A University of Florida sport management study challenges assumptions about optimal youth athlete training. Researchers found that track participation develops skills MLB teams have not fully valued. The analysis suggests teams may miss competitive advantages in player selection. Findings question traditional pathways into professional baseball.

6. **Gravity from Entropy theory links thermodynamics to cosmic structure — Phys.org**
   Professor Ginestra Bianconi at Queen Mary University of London proposes a new framework for structure formation. The model addresses how increasing complexity can occur while obeying the second law of thermodynamics. It connects entropy principles with the emergence of ordered cosmic features. The perspective offers a fresh angle on a core physics question.

7. **Optical chip design allows real-time light speed control — Phys.org**
   A Seoul National University team led by Professors Namkyoo Park and Sunkyu Yu developed the photonic integrated circuit. The device works with Professor Xianji Piao at the University of Seoul. Simulations show the circuit can slow light on demand. The advance targets precise manipulation within photonic systems.

8. **Large Hadron Collider enters long shutdown for Higgs boson upgrades — Phys.org**
   CERN's LHC has stopped operations after years of proton collisions. The upgrade aims to unlock further details of the Higgs boson. Work proceeds beneath the French-Swiss border on the world's largest scientific instrument. The shutdown follows extensive data collection at near-light speeds.

9. **Underwater oxygen loss pushes aquatic systems toward unsafe space — Phys.org**
   A review in Limnology and Oceanography led by UC San Diego's Scripps Institution of Oceanography examined oxygen decline. Rapid deoxygenation affects oceans and other aquatic ecosystems. Consequences may prove irreversible on human timescales. The analysis warns of broader Earth system stability risks.

10. **Wild snapdragons fine-tune petal shades to attract bees — Phys.org**
    Flowers adjust subtle color blends to gain competitive advantage in pollination. Slight shade differences can determine gene survival in the contest for bee visits. The study shows precise visual tuning beyond simple color categories. Researchers documented the effort plants expend on these adjustments.

11. **River bacteria consume methane yet fall short against rising emissions — Phys.org**
    Alberto Borges at the University of Liège compared microbial methane oxidation in Belgian and African rivers. Certain bacteria oxidize the greenhouse gas before atmospheric release. African rivers showed higher activity than Belgian ones. The biological filter remains insufficient to counter expected increases from warming and nitrate pollution.

12. **Multiple origin points drive most large Southeast Asian fires — Phys.org**
    Researchers traced the 2015 equatorial fires to identify starting conditions. Most large fires began at several points rather than single locations. Ecological, climatic and human factors were linked to ignition sites. The findings aim to improve prevention strategies under climate change.

13. **Prototype bicycle tire replaces nylon with silk for better performance — Phys.org**
    Thai researchers bonded silk fabric between natural rubber sheets using resorcinol-formaldehyde-latex coating. Laboratory and pilot-scale tests compared the silk composite against conventional nylon. The silk version showed gains in strength and endurance. Full tires were manufactured to validate the formulation.

14. **AI-designed proteins improve imaging of molecules inside living cells — Phys.org**
    Current tools struggle to resolve specific molecules at high detail within cellular environments. Researchers created designed proteins to address this limitation. The proteins help target and visualize chosen molecular residents. The approach expands options for studying dense cellular systems.

15. **AI-designed synthetic enzymes expand CRISPR gene-editing options — Phys.org**
    A Science paper describes SynTnpBs, new AI-created TnpB enzymes. The synthetic versions outperformed the natural reference enzyme. Traditional CRISPR relies on naturally evolved tools. The work targets creation of gene editors with properties absent in existing systems.

### Science Deep Dive: Thermodynamics and Cosmic Structure
Most people picture the second law of thermodynamics as a slow slide into total disorder where nothing new can form. In reality, local pockets of order routinely emerge even as overall entropy rises, because energy flows through open systems. Right now, as you sit reading this, gravitational collapse in distant gas clouds is converting potential energy into structured stars and galaxies while radiating waste heat outward. One memorable number: the observable universe contains roughly 10^80 atoms yet has produced highly ordered structures across 13.8 billion years without violating the law. The practical takeaway is to watch for future papers that quantify how much entropy export is required for any given level of biological or cosmic complexity.
