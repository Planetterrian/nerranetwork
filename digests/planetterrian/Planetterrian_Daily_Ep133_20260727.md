### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Virtual reality reveals artificial light effects on reef fish — Phys.org**
   A new VR platform combined metabolic measurements from the University of Glasgow with an immersive system developed at the Institut de Neurosciences de la Timone and deployed at CRIOBE. Researchers tested how light at night alters predator responses in coral reef fish. The setup allowed controlled exposure while tracking both behaviour and energy use. The work marks the first integration of these methods for this question. Follow-up studies will examine additional species and light wavelengths.

2. **Genetic analysis highlights value of ancient Tigray grapevines — Phys.org**
   Researchers examined grapevines in Ethiopia’s northern highlands, a region with some of Africa’s oldest continuous agriculture. The vines have persisted through centuries of environmental and social pressures. Genetic data revealed distinct lineages tied to local cultivation history. The findings add to understanding of crop origins alongside teff and other regional staples. Further mapping will clarify how these populations can support future breeding.

3. **Framework proposed to test medical AI superintelligence — Nature Medicine**
   Existing benchmarks for medical AI performance are described as misleading and insufficient. The commentary calls for a rigorous, task-based system to define and measure superintelligence in clinical contexts. Authors argue current tests fail to capture real-world diagnostic or therapeutic demands. Development of such a framework would allow clearer comparison across models. Work on the criteria is expected to begin with input from multiple clinical domains.

4. **Plasma proteins predict ALS onset in carriers — Nature Medicine**
   Longitudinal proteomic profiling tracked plasma from unaffected carriers of ALS-linked genetic variants. Early protein shifts appeared before clinical symptoms emerged. Trajectory mapping allowed prediction of when symptoms would begin. The approach identifies candidate biomarkers for timing of phenoconversion. Validation in larger cohorts will determine clinical utility.

5. **Evolutionary view reframes fever treatment decisions — Nature**
   Natural selection offers guidance on when suppressing fever helps or hinders recovery. The analysis links immune response patterns to historical pathogen pressures. Understanding these trade-offs can inform both individual care and broader health policies. The piece extends similar logic to crop management and social interventions. Ongoing work will test predictions in additional disease models.

6. **Ethical gap noted in brain organoid computing research — Nature**
   Tissue donors often remain unaware that their cells may contribute to biocomputing systems. Current consent processes focus on traditional biomedical uses. The commentary highlights the need to address this specific downstream application. Researchers building these systems have not yet established consistent disclosure standards. Revised consent language is under discussion in several labs.

7. **Engineered enzymes produce tunable recyclable plastics — Nature Biotechnology**
   Polyketide synthases were redesigned to generate sustainable polymer building blocks in microbes. The resulting materials allow property tuning while supporting recycling. The microbial chassis replaces petroleum-derived routes for certain plastics. Performance testing confirmed structural consistency across batches. Scale-up trials will assess industrial feasibility.

8. **GCaMP8 indicators evaluated for spike inference accuracy — Nature Methods**
   Multiple algorithms were tested against calcium imaging data collected with GCaMP8 variants. Indicator linearity emerged as a key factor in reliable spike timing reconstruction. The analysis compared performance across recording conditions and cell types. Results clarify which indicator-algorithm pairs minimise timing errors. Updated guidelines for experimental design are provided.

9. **Twisted laser light sorts mirror-image molecules by fragment patterns — Phys.org**
   Enantiomers that appear identical can produce different fragment counts when exposed to structured light. The method exploits handedness differences without requiring chemical labels. Experiments demonstrated separation based on fragment yield rather than traditional spectroscopy. The approach may simplify quality control in pharmaceutical synthesis. Refinements will target higher throughput.

10. **Teen decision-making reflects designed brain priorities — Phys.org**
    Risk-taking in adolescence aligns with heightened sensitivity to social feedback and novelty. This pattern supports skill acquisition and independence rather than indicating simple immaturity. The perspective reframes common adult interpretations of teenage choices. Data from developmental studies show the same circuits later support adult goal pursuit. Longitudinal tracking continues to map how these priorities shift with age.

11. **Rare molecules act as keystone drivers in ecosystems — Phys.org**
    A small number of scarce compounds can shape community structure far beyond their concentration. The review draws examples from freshwater and terrestrial systems. These molecules influence species interactions and nutrient cycling. Identification relies on combining metabolomics with ecological surveys. Field experiments will test predicted effects of targeted removal.

12. **Engineered enzyme reverses one aging hallmark in human tissue — r/longevity**
    A previously considered irreversible cellular aging feature was restored in cultured human samples. The intervention used a modified enzyme targeting the specific mark. This builds on ongoing work in aging biomarkers and intervention translation. Human tissue results provide a new data point for testing which changes can be undone. Additional models will examine functional outcomes.

13. **Airborne DNA sampling maps wildlife distribution — Phys.org**
    A DNA vacuum collected genetic traces from air in three Danish natural areas. The method detected multiple species without direct observation. Two studies confirmed reliable local biodiversity mapping. The technique offers a non-invasive complement to camera traps and field surveys. Expansion to new habitats is planned.

14. **Prescribed burns improve giant sequoia survival rates — ScienceDaily**
    Analysis of roughly 26,400 trees showed those in treated areas were nearly four times more likely to survive recent wildfires. Controlled burns prevented an estimated 1,900 deaths in the studied groves. The benefit stems from reduced fuel loads that limit crown fire intensity. Untreated groves experienced higher mortality despite the trees’ natural fire tolerance. Additional groves are being evaluated for treatment priority.

15. **Loss of functional fat tissue linked to metabolic disruption — ScienceDaily**
    Damaged fat cells lose lipid storage capacity, become inflamed, and disappear. This process impairs whole-body metabolism even without overall fat gain. The findings separate effects of excess fat from loss of healthy adipose function. Mouse models traced the sequence from cell damage to systemic glucose dysregulation. Human tissue studies will test whether similar dynamics occur in type 2 diabetes.

### Science Deep Dive: Teenage Brains and Risk
Most people assume a teenager’s willingness to retaliate against an insult or chase a risky thrill reflects an incomplete brain still learning to weigh consequences. The actual picture is different: the circuits that heighten sensitivity to social feedback and novelty are operating exactly as evolution shaped them to support rapid skill acquisition and eventual independence. Right now, while you listen, your own adult prefrontal networks are modulating those same pathways that once drove stronger exploration. One memorable feature is the speed of adolescent reward signalling, which can reach peak responsiveness before the slower regulatory systems fully balance it. The next time you watch a young person make a choice that seems short-sighted, consider that the behaviour is part of a temporary window optimised for learning rather than a defect. A practical takeaway is to channel that heightened sensitivity through structured challenges—sports, creative projects, or skill-building tasks—rather than attempting to suppress it outright. Future studies will clarify how long this window remains open and whether targeted experiences can extend its benefits into early adulthood.
