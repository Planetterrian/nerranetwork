### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Colonoscopy intervals of five years match three-year results after polyp removal — NEJM**
 A randomized trial directly compared surveillance colonoscopy performed at three years versus five years after initial polyp removal. The five-year interval met the criteria for non-inferiority on the primary outcome of colorectal cancer incidence measured across five years of follow-up. Participants assigned to more frequent examinations had additional lesions identified during the extra procedures. Those extra detections did not translate into a meaningful reduction in colorectal cancer diagnoses over the study period. The findings were published in NEJM and shared through recent expert commentary on the trial design and endpoints.

2. **DNA-based computer demonstrated in new Nature paper — Nature**
 Researchers constructed the first functional DNA computer using molecular components that operate as logic gates. DNA strands were engineered to carry out computational steps through specific binding and release reactions. The system completed defined calculations without relying on electronic hardware. Full technical details of the molecular architecture appear in the current Nature publication.

3. **Patient-specific neural networks from pre-operative scans improve surgical precision — Nature**
 Teams at MIT CSAIL and collaborating institutions trained neural networks on individual patient medical imaging data collected before surgery. The resulting models generated personalized predictions that guided intraoperative decisions. Integration of the networks into surgical workflows aimed to reduce complications and increase accuracy. The complete study is reported in Nature.

4. **Paper2Agent system lets research papers interact conversationally — Nature**
 A new computational framework converts static research papers into interactive agents capable of answering questions and exchanging information with other agents. Each agent retains the content and logic of its source paper while enabling cross-paper queries. The method was developed to change how scientific knowledge is accessed and synthesized. Implementation details are provided in the latest Nature issue.

5. **COSMOS trial shows multivitamin shifts metabolomic aging markers younger — Rhonda Patrick analysis**
 Two years of daily multivitamin supplementation produced measurable shifts in seven distinct metabolomic aging clocks toward younger values. The observed change corresponded to approximately 0.5 to 1 year of reduced metabolomic aging relative to placebo. Participants also showed lower metabolomic risk scores associated with major cardiovascular events, kidney disease, dementia, asthma, cataracts, glaucoma, liver disease, skin cancer, and prostate cancer. Prior COSMOS publications had already reported modest cognitive improvements and slower epigenetic aging with the same intervention. The newest clock and risk-score results lost statistical significance after correction for multiple comparisons.

6. **Japan reaches 100,000 centenarians — Scientific American**
 Government records now confirm that Japan has 100,000 residents aged 100 years or older. The milestone results from decades of incremental gains in life expectancy across the population. Earlier thresholds, such as the first recorded centenarian, once generated widespread media attention that has since become routine.

7. **Some bird species use geothermal sites as natural saunas — Phys.org**
 These locations appear to serve thermoregulatory functions similar to health spas. Visits may assist with feather condition or control of external parasites through elevated ambient temperatures.

8. **Mice engineered with human brain cortex cells show altered behavior — MIT Technology Review**
 Stanford investigators generated xenocortical mice in which up to 92 percent of cortical tissue consists of human cells. Multiple synchronized cameras recorded the animals’ position and velocity while they explored controlled arenas. The resulting behavioral data allow direct comparison of human-derived neural circuits with typical mouse cortex function. The model provides a platform for examining human-specific aspects of brain development and disease.

9. **Parental longevity to age 100 improves offspring lifespan odds — Washington Post**
 A population study identified a measurable longevity advantage among individuals whose parents reached age 100. The benefit persisted after statistical adjustment for some shared environmental and lifestyle variables. The finding adds to evidence that familial factors influence lifespan beyond obvious behavioral inheritance.

10. **Wealthy neighborhoods face 30 percent higher urban fire risk — Phys.org**
 UC Riverside analysis of urban fire patterns showed that higher-income neighborhoods located on hillsides and in canyons experience 30 percent greater fire incidence. These same areas demonstrate faster recovery times after fire damage compared with lower-income zones, likely due to differences in resources and rebuilding capacity.

11. **Dungeons & Dragons groups boost sense of belonging and well-being — Phys.org**
 University of Canterbury researchers surveyed participants in regular tabletop role-playing sessions. Regular involvement correlated with stronger reported social connections and an increased sense of belonging. These social outcomes were linked to measurable support for mental health indicators in the studied groups.

12. **Natural mortality estimates for Atlantic menhaden overstated in prior models — Phys.org**
 A new assessment of Atlantic menhaden populations determined that natural mortality rates had been substantially overestimated in earlier models. The corrected figures indicate the stock may support lower levels of fishing pressure than previously calculated. The species serves as a critical forage base for multiple predators along the U.S. East Coast.

13. **Single snapshot method estimates pace of biological aging — Technology Networks**
 Investigators developed an analytical approach that derives an estimate of biological aging rate from a single biological sample. The method bypasses the need for repeated measurements over time. Potential applications include faster evaluation of interventions intended to modify aging trajectories.

14. **Black and green Japanese rice contain unusual fats digested more slowly — ScienceDaily**
 Laboratory analysis identified distinct lipid profiles in black and green Japanese rice varieties that differ from those in standard white rice. These varieties also exhibited slower digestion rates in model systems. The combination of lipid composition and digestion kinetics may contribute to more stable post-meal blood glucose responses.

15. **Experimental drug disables PRX3 antioxidant defense in mesothelioma cells — ScienceDaily**
 The compound targets the PRX3 antioxidant pathway that mesothelioma cells rely on to manage oxidative stress. In an early-phase clinical trial the agent achieved disease control in 67 percent of enrolled patients. Survival outcomes in the initial cohort were described as encouraging relative to historical expectations for this cancer type.

Because the intervention is already widely available, these data lower the barrier for longer-term studies that track actual clinical events rather than signatures alone. ### Science Deep Dive: How Your Kidneys Filter Blood Continuously
Most people assume the kidneys only become active when fluid intake increases or when waste builds up noticeably. In reality the kidneys maintain continuous filtration regardless of immediate fluid status. Right now, as you listen, roughly one million nephrons in each kidney are forcing plasma through specialized capillaries while reclaiming water, ions, and nutrients before the filtrate reaches the bladder. The entire blood volume passes through this system approximately every 30 minutes under resting conditions. That produces a memorable number: about 180 liters of filtrate are generated daily, yet only 1 to 2 liters of urine are excreted after extensive reabsorption. The constant workload explains why even modest reductions in blood flow can alter electrolyte balance quickly. One practical takeaway is that consistent daily fluid intake supports the reabsorption processes without requiring extreme hydration practices; future kidney-function studies may focus on how this baseline filtration rate changes with age or chronic conditions.
