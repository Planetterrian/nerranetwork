### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries

1. **Quantum algorithm matches classical accuracy on DNA-derived molecules — Phys.org**
   Kvantify tested its BEAST-VQE algorithm on dispersion interactions in a pair of molecules taken from a DNA structure. The calculation used Quantinuum's System Model H2 quantum computer and produced results that matched the established CCSD(T) method. Researchers reduced the number of active orbitals required from 236 to 53. Hardware runs at two molecular geometries yielded outputs nearly identical to ideal simulations. The work demonstrates integration of quantum and classical methods for studying molecular stacking relevant to drug discovery. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-quantum-software-classical-benchmark-molecular.html)

2. **Microplastics generate reactive oxygen species when interacting with water — ScienceDaily**
   Columbia University and University of California Berkeley researchers published the findings in Nature. Tiny plastic particles increase surface area as they fragment, allowing water interactions that produce high-energy radicals. Wei Min noted that these reactive oxygen species can damage animal and human tissue. The team previously measured an average of approximately 240,000 detectable plastic fragments per liter of bottled water. A 2026 federal initiative allocated $144 million to study toxic microplastics in the body. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261120075549.htm)

3. **Two new armored tardigrade species found on Atlantic seamount — Phys.org**
   Researchers from Senckenberg am Meer examined sediment from Senghor Seamount off Cape Verde. They described Megastygarctides hekatae sp. nov. and Parastygarctus potercha sp. nov. from 219 specimens, with 212 collected from the seamount summit. The plateau yielded 20 tardigrade taxa while a deep-sea reference site 100 kilometers away yielded only two. Megastygarctides hekatae possesses rare genital spines and was found at about 100 meters depth. Parastygarctus potercha exhibits pedomorphism with reduced body size and fewer claws. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-seamount-armored-tardigrades-atlantic.html)

4. **RNA organizes protein condensates at neuronal synapses — Phys.org**
   Researchers at Duke University and Charité Berlin University Institute of Biochemistry published the work in Advanced Science. RNA acts as a scaffold to organize synapsin-1 into condensates at sites of neurotransmitter release. The study examined live neurons and a reconstituted system showing localized translation. Amy Gladfelter and Dragomir Milovanovic led the team that included Branislava Rankovic and Zachary Geisterfer. The findings establish a structural role for RNA beyond its information-carrying function. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-broadening-role-rna-messenger-condensate.html)

5. **Precise optical properties of barium titanate measured across broad spectrum — ScienceDaily**
   KAIST and Caltech researchers used Mueller matrix spectroscopic ellipsometry on single-crystal samples. They determined refractive index and extinction coefficient values from 190 nanometers to 40 micrometers. In the 2-10 micrometer range, extinction coefficients reached approximately 10^-5, indicating very low absorption. The team developed a method to predict behavior in crystals containing multiple domains. The measurements support design of optical modulators that rely on the material's Pockels effect. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261119194544.htm)

The achievement improves spatial resolution for material studies. Researchers demonstrated reliable operation at this energy on the European XFEL facility. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-ray-laser-reliably-generates-wavelengths.html)

It requires less computing power than some existing AI methods while maintaining accuracy. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-mushrooms-ai-potential-crop-stress.html)

The work clarifies how mitochondria produce the machinery needed for their own protein synthesis. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-parasite-protein-mitochondria-ribosomes.html)

### Science Deep Dive: How Microplastics Produce Reactive Molecules in Water
Most people assume plastic remains chemically inert after it fragments into microscopic pieces. The new measurements show otherwise. When microplastics and nanoplastics increase their exposed surface area, the material interacts with surrounding water to generate reactive oxygen species. These radicals form because hydrophobic plastic surfaces alter the behavior of water molecules and charged particles at the boundary, allowing electrons to transfer between species. In the human body the same process can occur once particles reach blood or tissues that contain abundant water. Columbia University researchers documented average concentrations of roughly 240,000 plastic fragments per liter in bottled water, most of them nanometer scale. The 2024 laser-microscopy study that produced those counts already indicated far higher particle numbers than earlier estimates. Wei Min's group traced the radicals directly to the plastic-water interface rather than to any additive or contaminant. The finding supplies one concrete mechanism that could connect documented microplastic exposure to reported associations with cardiovascular and neurological conditions. What remains uncertain is the quantitative contribution of this pathway to actual tissue damage in people. Further independent measurements of radical production rates inside living systems would clarify whether the chemistry scales to meaningful biological effects. One practical step is to reduce unnecessary plastic contact with food and water while those measurements are completed.
