Kyushu University researchers in a Fizz.org report showed that the H3MM-15 sub variant

Something most people get wrong about proteins is that a close sequence match can always take
another place inside a living cell.
A pair of histone proteins can differ at only three amino acids and still refuse to stand
in for one another.
That gap is small enough to look trivial on a sequence map and large enough to create
functional roles that are not interchangeable.
The cell is not averaging those three positions away.
It is reading them as a different job.
Miner sequence changes of that kind can lock a protein into a role no nearby variant can
fill, even when the rest of the chain looks the same.
Today's other findings sit on the same scale, a small molecular or environmental detail
that reshapes a much larger biological or geological process.
The point is not that every tiny difference matters equally.
It is that a difference of three residues can be decisive when the job is specific.
Substitution fails when chromatin needs one version and the standard version cannot
supply that behavior.
Sequence similarity is not the same thing as replaceability, and that is the assumption
the three residue gap undercuts.
