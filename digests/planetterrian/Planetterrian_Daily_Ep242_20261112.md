### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Light tool blocks autophagy to map rapid cell recycling targets — Phys.org**
   Researchers at the Salk Institute engineered ASAP, an optogenetic protein that relocates after light exposure to halt autophagy reversibly in any cell type. In human cell cultures, blocking autophagy for five to sixty minutes caused immediate accumulation of known autophagy substrates plus previously unknown targets including nuclear pore complexes. The tool confirmed that autophagy selectively clears only partially assembled nuclear pores while sparing intact ones. Collaboration with the D'Angelo lab at Sanford Burnham Prebys identified the specific signal on nuclear pores recognized by autophagy machinery. Protein condensates also emerged as novel autophagy substrates in the screen. The study appears in Nature Cell Biology. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-tool-halts-cellular-recycling-disease.html)

2. **Higher PFOA and PFOS levels tied to elevated ER-negative breast cancer rates — ScienceDaily**
   An NIH analysis of blood samples collected years before diagnosis in the Sister Study cohort linked elevated perfluorooctanoic acid and perfluorooctanesulfonic acid to higher incidence of estrogen receptor-negative breast cancer. Women in the highest exposure group showed a 67 percent higher rate for PFOA and a 93 percent higher rate for PFOS compared with the lowest group. No association appeared with overall breast cancer incidence. The findings were confirmed by meta-analysis of prior studies and published in the Journal of Clinical Oncology. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261112025527.htm)

3. **Electric fields and applied potential separately shape catalyst surface reactions — Phys.org**
   Researchers at the Interface Science Department demonstrated that applied potential drives electrochemical reactions at solid-solution interfaces while surrounding electric fields alter the ensembles that set activation parameters. The two factors act through distinct physical routes even though both involve electric fields. Experiments distinguished thermodynamic driving force from modulation of transition-state ensembles. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-potentials-electric-fields-reactions-catalyst.html)

4. **Vibrating microscopic elements store quantum information like guitar strings — ScienceDaily**
   A quantum computing architecture stores data in microscopic mechanical vibrations rather than conventional superconducting circuits. The system performed advanced quantum calculations while demonstrating improved coherence times. The approach may allow smaller devices with better error resistance. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261109205416.htm)

5. **Genetic mapping shows regional differences in bovine tuberculosis strains across Wales — Phys.org**
   Whole-genome sequencing of Mycobacterium bovis isolates revealed distinct evolutionary patterns in different Welsh regions. The analysis provides the most detailed view to date of how the pathogen varies spatially. Findings can inform targeted control strategies for cattle and farming communities. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-genetic-landscape-bovine-tb-wales.html)

6. **Fungus that parasitizes powdery mildew proves more widespread than museum records suggested — Phys.org**
   Screening of 150-year-old herbarium specimens showed Ampelomyces fungi occur commonly inside powdery mildew hosts. Many historical labels misidentified the parasite. The work from North Carolina State University expands understanding of hyperparasite distribution. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-parasites-fungus-infects-powdery-mildew.html)

7. **Explosive directional sports raise bone-formation markers more than walking or steady lifting — ScienceDaily**
   High-intensity activities with rapid changes in direction increased markers of bone formation by up to 46 percent in study participants. Conventional walking, jogging, and weight training produced smaller responses. The pattern suggests that impact and shear forces provide stronger osteogenic stimuli than repetitive linear loading. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261110042601.htm)

8. **First land vertebrates evolved stiff necks for limb-driven locomotion, not head turning — ScienceDaily**
   Fossil analysis of early tetrapods from nearly 380 million years ago shows rigid neck joints that stabilized the head during forelimb propulsion. The finding overturns the prior assumption that necks primarily evolved to increase head mobility on land. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261108233101.htm)

9. **All of Us project identifies thousands of new variants that alter drug response across diverse populations — STAT News**
   Sequencing of nearly 100,000 Americans uncovered thousands of previously unreported genetic variants in pharmacogenes. The variants affect how individuals metabolize or respond to prescription medications. Inclusion of underrepresented ancestry groups expanded the catalog beyond prior European-biased datasets. Source: [statnews.com](https://www.statnews.com/2026/11/11/all-of-us-study-finds-gene-variants-drug-responses/?utm_campaign=rss)

10. **Fructose released by surviving ovarian cancer cells weakens cell-cell adhesion and promotes spread — ScienceDaily**
    Chemotherapy-surviving ovarian cancer cells secrete fructose that disrupts junctions between neighboring tumor cells. Elevated dietary fructose and statin use further enhanced this effect in preclinical models. The mechanism offers a new angle on how metabolic changes facilitate metastasis. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261111221501.htm)

### Planetterrian Spotlight
Researchers at Johannes Gutenberg University Mainz, the Max Planck Institute for Biophysics, and Wuhan University used fluorescent probes to show that nuclear pores in living human cells contain fluid-like selective barriers rather than rigid sieves. The barriers allow rapid yet specific passage of molecules while maintaining the nucleus-cytoplasm boundary. The work resolves long-standing debate about the physical state inside the pore channel. How might these fluid barriers change with age or disease states that alter nuclear transport? Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-fluorescent-probes-reveal-fluid-barriers.html)

### Science Deep Dive: How cells decide what to keep and what to recycle
Most people picture a cell as a stable factory that only dismantles components when they are obviously broken. In reality, cells continuously sort healthy from damaged parts through autophagy, a process that runs even in unstressed conditions. The misconception arises because the default view treats autophagy as an emergency cleanup rather than routine quality control. A new optogenetic system called ASAP now lets researchers turn this sorting on or off with light in any cell type, revealing which proteins disappear first when recycling stops. Within five minutes of blocking autophagy, certain nuclear pore proteins and protein condensates accumulate, showing they are normally cleared on a fast timescale. This rapid turnover matters because nuclear pores control everything that moves in and out of the genome, and their partial assemblies are selectively removed while complete pores remain untouched. One practical implication is that future work can test whether altering these clearance rates changes outcomes in neurodegeneration or infection models where autophagy is already known to be disrupted. The next measurements that would settle remaining questions are direct comparisons of ASAP-blocked cells against genetic autophagy mutants to confirm which proteins are primary versus secondary targets.
