United States and United Kingdom researchers, according to Phys.org, examined a rock collected between the eighteen seventies and the eighteen nineties from the Dakota Formation in Kansas and held at the Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History.

Micro computed tomography, or micro-CT, scanning revealed a void left by a decomposed flower that measured one point five millimetres in height and one point two millimetres in diameter.

The fossil shows three sepals, three petals, six stamens in two sets of three, and three free carpels, a layout that most closely matches an extinct fan-palm relative.

Monocots make up roughly twenty percent of flowering plants and include cereals, bananas, yams and orchids.

Paleobotanist Professor Steven Manchester found the fossil by chance while scanning for another specimen, and botanist Walter Judd and paleobotanist Bruce Tiffney confirmed the monocot identity during weekly online meetings.

A cytochrome P four hundred fifty enzyme named CcCYP9Z273 in the leaf beetle Chrysolina coerulans, reported on bioRxiv, converts pregnenolone to caraumbellogenin through C fourteen-hydroxylation.

Gene silencing of CcCYP9Z273 blocked cardenolide formation in the beetles, and isotope tracing confirmed caraumbellogenin as an on-pathway intermediate.

Mutagenesis at one active-site residue switched the enzyme’s product selectivity between C fourteen-hydroxylation and C fifteen-hydroxylation.

Cardenolides serve as chemical defenses in these beetles and are used medically, as with digitoxin.

Most people assume that once a course of antidepressants, blood-pressure medication or anxiety treatment ends, its influence on the body stops within days or weeks, but an analysis of more than two thousand five hundred people, according to ScienceDaily, found those drugs still alter gut microbiome composition years after participants stopped taking them.

Some anxiety medications produced shifts in microbial composition on the same scale as broad-spectrum antibiotics, even though the drugs target the nervous system rather than bacteria.

A few episodes back, wastewater bacteria from eight Ohio cities serving one point three million people showed associations between gut patterns and local rates of obesity, high blood pressure, asthma and depression; this cohort adds the medications themselves as a lasting driver of those communities.

The altered microbes sit inside the intestines that line the digestive tract and help regulate nutrient absorption and immune signaling every day.

Those persistent changes can affect how efficiently food is broken down and how inflammatory signals are generated long after the prescription bottle is empty.

One practical step is to keep a dated list of past medications when discussing digestive or metabolic symptoms with a clinician, because the microbial record may still carry their imprint.

Post-mortem brain tissue from people with dementia, according to Fierce Biotech, contained micro- and nanoplastics at levels high enough to force re-examination of existing models of protein aggregation and clearance.

The particles were absent or far lower in age-matched control brains without dementia diagnoses.

Researchers note that the contamination may interfere with normal proteostasis pathways long before clinical symptoms appear.

Grim2AA, a DNA methylation clock tracked in a large cohort followed for incident events, raised the hazard of cardiovascular disease events by six to eleven percent for every one-year increase in the clock.
