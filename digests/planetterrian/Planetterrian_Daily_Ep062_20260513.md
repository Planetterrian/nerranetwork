### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Plant Enzymes Decoded for Rare Anti-Cancer Compound — Science Daily**
   Scientists at UBC Okanagan identified the two enzymes that assemble mitraphylline’s unusual structure in plants such as kratom and cat’s claw. The work removes a long-standing barrier to producing the compound sustainably instead of harvesting trace amounts from tropical species. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/05/260512213836.htm)

2. **Enteroviruses Share a Common Replication Weak Spot — Science Daily**
   University of Maryland researchers mapped how enteroviruses, including polio and common-cold strains, recruit both viral and host proteins to copy their RNA. The detailed mechanism functions like a molecular switch that decides between replication and protein production. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/05/260512202320.htm)

3. **New Approach Targets Senescent Cells in Cancer and Aging — Science Daily**
   Researchers showed that senescent “zombie” cells surviving chemotherapy rely on the protein GPX4 to avoid iron-triggered death. Removing this protection caused the cells to self-destruct, shrinking tumors and extending survival in mouse models. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/05/260509210646.htm)

4. **Blood Test Shows Early Warning of Heart and Kidney Damage — Fierce Biotech**
   Damage to microscopic blood vessels precedes overt heart and kidney disease, yet current imaging methods are impractical for routine screening. A new blood-based assay detects these early microvascular changes with potential for wider clinical use. Source: [fiercebiotech.com](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/blood-based-approach-could-detect-early-heart-kidney-disease-study-finds)

5. **European Approval for Second Alzheimer’s Blood Test — Fierce Biotech**
   Roche received CE mark clearance for an additional blood assay that can both confirm and rule out Alzheimer’s disease. The test builds on an earlier Eli Lilly-partnered assay already in use. Source: [fiercebiotech.com](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/roche-nabs-european-approval-second-eli-lilly-partnered-alzheimers-test)

6. **AI Tool Shortens Pathology Turnaround by at Least One Week — Fierce Biotech**
   Charles River Laboratories launched a digital pathology platform that uses artificial intelligence to accelerate slide analysis and reporting. The system targets routine workflows in toxicology and safety studies. Source: [fiercebiotech.com](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/charles-river-debuts-ai-diagnostic-tool-cut-down-pathology-timelines)

7. **Films Can Serve as Accurate Science Gateways — Phys.org**
   The movie Project Hail Mary demonstrates how complex topics in astrophysics and linguistics can be conveyed without sacrificing accuracy or entertainment value. Researchers note that well-crafted films reach audiences that traditional science communication often misses. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-05-cinema-effect-gateway-science.html)

8. **Studies Test Strategies to Maintain Weight Loss After GLP-1 Drugs — Nature Medicine**
   Two randomized trials examined switching from injectable GLP-1 agonists to either an oral agent or a microbiome-targeted supplement. Both approaches showed potential for preserving weight loss achieved during initial treatment. Source: [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04386-7?error=cookies_not_supported&code=ab35816e-86aa-415f-8a3b-fb61f5144f20)

### Planetterrian Spotlight
The discovery that two specific enzymes create mitraphylline’s distinctive twisted ring system opens a route to microbial or plant-cell production of the compound. Because the molecule occurs naturally in only trace quantities, scalable synthesis could finally allow researchers to test its activity against multiple cancer types in meaningful quantities. The same enzymatic logic may apply to other rare plant alkaloids whose structures have remained inaccessible. If production barriers fall, mitraphylline could move from a botanical curiosity to a practical lead compound. ### Science Deep Dive: Senescent Cells Are Not Just “Old and Tired”
Most people picture every damaged cell either repairing itself or dying quickly, yet a third fate is far more common than once assumed. Right now, as you read this, a small fraction of your cells have entered a permanent arrest where they neither divide nor clear out, instead secreting inflammatory signals that affect neighboring tissue. These senescent cells accumulate steadily with age and after certain treatments such as chemotherapy. Their persistence helps explain why tissue function declines even when the original injury has long passed. One practical takeaway is that future therapies may focus less on preventing all cellular damage and more on clearing the lingering senescent population once it forms. Watch for clinical trials that combine standard cancer treatments with drugs designed to eliminate these cells afterward.
