Most people assume thunderstorms are mainly about rain, wind and the occasional lightning bolt we see from the ground. In reality, right now inside any active storm cell, charge separation between ice particles and water droplets is building electric fields that can reach hundreds of millions of volts.

The GRAPES-3 telescope in India detects four billion muons every day; those muons bend measurably when they pass through these fields, giving physicists a remote readout of voltage differences far larger than anything measured by weather balloons.

One memorable number is that the potential differences inside thunderclouds exceed one hundred million volts, roughly ten times higher than the strongest fields recorded by aircraft instruments. This changes how we think about lightning initiation and also about radiation exposure for high-altitude flights.
