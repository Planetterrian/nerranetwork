### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries

1. **X-rays expose 100-million-year-old monocot flower in museum rock — Phys.org**
   Researchers from the U.S. and UK examined a rock specimen collected in the 1870s–1890s from the Dakota Formation in Kansas and held at the Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History. Micro-CT scanning revealed a void left by a decomposed flower that measured 1.5 mm in height and 1.2 mm in diameter. The fossil shows the classic monocot arrangement of three sepals, three petals, six stamens in two sets of three, and three free carpels. The structure most closely matches an extinct fan-palm relative. Monocots make up roughly 20 percent of flowering plants and include cereals, bananas, yams and orchids. The find supplies the oldest record of the standard monocot flower ground plan. Paleobotanist Prof. Steven Manchester found the fossil by chance while scanning for another specimen. Botanist Walter Judd and paleobotanist Bruce Tiffney confirmed the monocot identity during weekly online meetings. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-hidden-million-years-rays-reveal.html)

2. **Beetle cytochrome P450 enzyme drives cardenolide production — bioRxiv**
   A cytochrome P450 enzyme named CcCYP9Z273 in the leaf beetle Chrysolina coerulans converts pregnenolone to caraumbellogenin through C14-hydroxylation. Gene silencing of CcCYP9Z273 blocked cardenolide formation in the beetles. Isotope tracing confirmed caraumbellogenin as an on-pathway intermediate. Mutagenesis at one active-site residue switched the enzyme’s product selectivity between C14- and C15-hydroxylation. Cardenolides serve as chemical defenses in these beetles and are used medically, as with digitoxin. Source: [biorxiv.org](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.11.19.767920v1?rss=1)

3. **Medications leave lasting microbial fingerprints in the gut — ScienceDaily**
   Researchers analyzed data from more than 2,500 people and found that common drugs including antidepressants, blood-pressure medications and anxiety treatments altered gut microbiome composition. Some anxiety medications produced effects comparable in scale to broad-spectrum antibiotics. The microbial changes persisted years after participants stopped taking the drugs. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261120071501.htm)

4. **DNA methylation clock tracks rising cardiovascular risk — academic.oup.com**
   Every one-year increase in the Grim2AA DNA methylation clock raised the hazard of cardiovascular disease events by 6 to 11 percent. The association was measured in a large cohort followed for incident events. Source: [academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/ehjopen/article/6/6/oeag199/9027717)

5. **Type 2 diabetes raises Alzheimer’s incidence 53 percent — ScienceDaily**
   A review of 40 studies found people with type 2 diabetes face a 53 percent higher risk of developing Alzheimer’s disease. Researchers calculated that nearly five of every 1,000 people with type 2 diabetes develop Alzheimer’s each year. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261121221124.htm)

6. **Silver forms rare crystal structure under brief conditions — Phys.org**
   Researchers at Hokkaido University produced a previously unknown crystal structure of silver that exhibits unusually high chemical reactivity. The structure was observed only transiently before reverting. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-silver-briefly-rare-crystal-radioactive.html)

7. **Atmospheric organic nitrogen supplies major ocean nutrient — Phys.org**
   A research team quantified the contribution of atmospheric organic nitrogen to the global nitrogen cycle and established it as a significant nutrient source for marine ecosystems. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-atmospheric-nitrogen-emerges-major-nutrient.html)

8. **DNA component converts UV damage into repairable form — ScienceDaily**
   A key structural feature of DNA turns potentially destructive ultraviolet damage into a form that cells can repair more easily. The mechanism may explain how early life survived intense solar radiation on Earth. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261121221128.htm)

9. **New genetic eye disease impairs night and peripheral vision — ScienceDaily**
   Clinicians identified a previously unrecognized inherited retinal condition that progressively damages peripheral and night vision while sparing central vision for years. Difficulty adjusting to darkness serves as an early clinical sign. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261119194547.htm)

10. **194-year-old tortoise carries anti-aging DNA variant — ScienceDaily**
    Genetic analysis of a 194-year-old tortoise uncovered sequence features linked to exceptional longevity and cellular maintenance. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261121221130.htm)

### Science Deep Dive: Lasting Drug Effects on the Gut Microbiome
Most people assume that once a course of medication ends, its influence on the body stops within days or weeks. Analysis of more than 2,500 individuals shows that antidepressants, blood-pressure drugs and certain anxiety treatments continue to shape gut bacterial communities for years afterward. The altered microbes sit inside the intestines that line your digestive tract and help regulate nutrient absorption and immune signaling every day. Some anxiety medications produced shifts in microbial composition on the same scale as broad-spectrum antibiotics, even though the drugs target the nervous system rather than bacteria. Those persistent changes can affect how efficiently food is broken down and how inflammatory signals are generated long after the prescription bottle is empty. One practical step is to keep a dated list of past medications when discussing digestive or metabolic symptoms with a clinician, because the microbial record may still carry their imprint. Further longitudinal sampling will clarify whether the altered communities can be steered back toward a pre-treatment state.
