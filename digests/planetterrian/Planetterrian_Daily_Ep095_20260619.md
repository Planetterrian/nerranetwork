### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Full APOBEC3B structure shows viral neutralization mechanism — bioRxiv**
   The cryo-EM structure captures wild-type human APOBEC3B in complex with the Epstein-Barr virus protein BORF2. The amino-terminal and carboxy-terminal domains bridge a BORF2 dimer, creating contacts that differ from the related factor APOBEC3G. Mutational work indicates the NTD-CTD interaction normally regulates deaminase activity; BORF2 binding locks the enzyme into large aggregates that prevent cytosine deamination. The structure supplies a template for designing molecules that could block either viral evasion or A3B-driven mutagenesis in tumors. Source: [biorxiv.org](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.18.733170v1?rss=1)

2. **Yeast Warburg model links nutrient levels to aging and fitness — bioRxiv**
   Deletion of SNF1 in Saccharomyces cerevisiae alters chronological lifespan in a nutrient-dependent manner. Under low glucose and amino acids the mutant shortens lifespan; under 5 % glucose plus 3× amino acids lifespan lengthens and competitive fitness rises by roughly two orders of magnitude in co-culture with wild-type cells. The findings parallel metabolic reprogramming seen in cancer cells and show that the same genetic change produces opposite aging outcomes depending on nutrient status. Source: [biorxiv.org](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.18.733183v1?rss=1)

3. **Suburban street layout, not distance alone, drives post-war emissions — Phys.org**
   Analysis of U.S. suburban development since 1945 shows neighborhood design features explain more vehicle miles traveled than simple distance from city centers. Street patterns that force longer local trips and reduce walking account for a substantial share of household carbon output. The study reframes sprawl policy around street networks rather than density metrics alone. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-suburban-street-driven-emissions-wwii.html)

4. **Asteroid Donaldjohanson rotates on two axes, Lucy data show — Phys.org**
   Southwest Research Institute measurements indicate the inner-belt asteroid completes one end-over-end rotation every 10.5 Earth days while its spin axis wobbles every 26.5 days. The dual-axis motion was detected during the Lucy spacecraft flyby. The finding adds to understanding of how small bodies dissipate rotational energy over time. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-asteroid-donaldjohanson-rotates-lucy-flyby.html)

5. **Antioxidants clear select senescent muscle cells via mTOR — Lifespan.io**
   Work published in Aging Cell demonstrates that certain antioxidants reduce the burden of senescent cells in muscle by changing mTOR signaling. Senescent cells lose normal nutrient sensing; the altered pathway appears to push them toward clearance while sparing healthy cells. The selectivity offers a route to lower senescent load without broad cytotoxicity. Source: [lifespan.io](https://lifespan.io/how-antioxidants-can-selectively-remove-some-senescent-cells/)

6. **Cajon Pass may act as earthquake gate under record stress — Science Daily**
   The intersection of the San Andreas and San Jacinto faults at Cajon Pass now carries the highest stress recorded in the last 1,000 years. Researchers mapped stress accumulation and identified the pass as a structural feature that can either stop or allow rupture to propagate across both faults. Conditions match those preceding several of the region’s largest historical events. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/06/260617032155.htm)

7. **Digital overuse harms mental health without literal brain damage — Science News**
   Emerging studies link heavy device use to measurable declines in attention and mood, yet no evidence supports the idea that brains physically deteriorate from screen time. The phrase “brain rot” captures real cognitive and emotional costs rather than structural decay. Researchers emphasize distinguishing correlation from direct causation in future work. Source: [sciencenews.org](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/brain-rot-digital-online-mental-health)

8. **EU deforestation rules show implementation gaps in Brazil — Phys.org**
   University of Manchester analysis of the EU Deforestation-Free Products Regulation finds mismatches between regulatory requirements and actual beef supply chains in Brazil. Traceability and enforcement shortfalls could limit the policy’s impact on tropical forest loss. The study highlights challenges the UK would face if adopting similar import rules. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-major-gaps-eu-anti-deforestation.html)

9. **Wearables show mixed results for cardiovascular-disease management — STAT News**
   A review of device data in patients with established cardiovascular disease finds inconsistent effects on clinical outcomes. Some studies report improved medication adherence while others show no change in hospitalizations or mortality. Larger trials are needed to clarify which patient groups benefit. Source: [statnews.com](https://www.statnews.com/2026/06/18/do-wearables-actually-help-people-with-cardiovascular-disease/?utm_campaign=rss)

10. **Poll finds fewer Americans can afford quality care — STAT News**
    A new survey indicates only about half of U.S. adults had both affordable and high-quality health care last year. The share reporting access difficulties has increased compared with prior years. Cost barriers remain the dominant reason cited. Source: [statnews.com](https://www.statnews.com/2026/06/18/how-many-americans-can-afford-their-healthcare/?utm_campaign=rss)

11. **Proposed FDA pathway targets shared aging biology across diseases — r/longevity**
    The Alliance for Longevity Initiatives white paper outlines a Multi-Disease Therapeutic Designation that would allow sponsors to pursue one approval covering multiple age-related conditions sharing a mechanism. Section 6 addresses potential regulatory objections. The approach aims to reduce the need for sequential indication expansions. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/longevity/comments/1u9fxfh/the_multidisease_therapeutic_designation_an_fda/)

12. **R programming language team receives major statistical award — Phys.org**
    University of Auckland researchers who developed the R language have been honored with an award described as the Nobel Prize for statisticians. The recognition highlights R’s role in enabling reproducible analysis across biomedical and environmental research. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-zealand-scientists-major-global-award.html)

### Planetterrian Spotlight

This mechanism explains both viral evasion of innate immunity and a route by which A3B contributes to cancer mutagenesis when unregulated. Future drug efforts can now target either the BORF2 interface or the enzyme’s own NTD-CTD interaction. ### Science Deep Dive: How Antioxidants Can Clear Specific Cells
Most people assume antioxidants simply shield every cell from oxidative damage in the same way. In reality they can selectively push certain damaged cells toward removal while leaving healthy ones untouched. Right now, as you move, your muscle fibers contain a small population of senescent cells that have lost normal nutrient sensing; antioxidants appear to exploit that difference by shifting mTOR signaling and tipping those cells into clearance. One memorable detail is that this selectivity occurs without the broad cell death seen with many experimental senolytics. A practical takeaway is to watch for trials that test whether particular antioxidant doses or timing produce measurable drops in senescence markers rather than general antioxidant claims.
