### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Ancient Microbial Conflicts Shaped Human Immune Defenses — Science Magazine**
   Researchers traced key immune system components to ancient battles between microbes. The work maps molecular defenses that emerged from those early conflicts. Visual reconstructions show how these systems integrated into modern immunity. The findings clarify evolutionary steps that produced current innate responses. Further studies will test how these origins affect contemporary immune disorders.

2. **Sleep Physiology Linked to Dementia Pathways — Science Magazine**
   A new review examines connections between sleep processes and dementia development. It covers glymphatic flow, blood volume shifts, heart rate variability, and neuromodulator activity. Mai Nedergaard outlines how disrupted sleep may impair waste clearance in the brain. The analysis integrates multiple physiological systems rather than focusing on single factors. Follow-up work will test targeted sleep interventions in at-risk groups.

3. **First Baseline Data Established for Pediatric Deep Fascia Thickness — Charles University**
   Researchers measured deep fascia thickness in healthy children's legs using ultrasound. These reference values provide the initial standard for comparison in clinical settings. The data support non-invasive diagnosis of musculoskeletal conditions. Doctors can now identify deviations earlier without invasive procedures. Validation across larger age groups is planned next.

4. **Sperm DNA Marker Predicts Fertility Recovery After Chemotherapy — Charles University**
   Aggressive BEP chemotherapy for testicular cancer damages sperm production. Scientists identified H4K12ac as a reliable indicator of overall sperm health. Higher levels of this marker correlate with better recovery potential after treatment. The finding offers a practical way to monitor fertility restoration. Longitudinal tracking in patients will refine its clinical use.

5. **Red Blood Cell Waste Products Flag Alzheimer's Disease — Charles University**
   Specific oxidative compounds appear elevated in red blood cells of people with Alzheimer's. These markers differ from patterns seen in other dementias. The discovery supports development of a simple blood-based test for early detection. Researchers note the approach could distinguish Alzheimer's from related conditions. Larger cohort studies are underway to confirm diagnostic accuracy.

6. **Teacher Guidance Improves ChatGPT Grammar Learning Outcomes — Czech Technical University**
   Students using ChatGPT for grammar showed greater gains when a teacher provided oversight. Independent AI-only study produced inconsistent results across participants. The study tracked performance metrics in a controlled classroom setting. Structured human input helped learners apply corrections more effectively. Expanded trials will examine other language skills.

7. **GLP-1 Hormone Levels Low in Arthritic Joints — Science Daily**
   Researchers measured reduced GLP-1 concentrations inside joints of arthritis patients. The finding suggests direct anti-inflammatory effects from GLP-1 drugs beyond weight loss. High-dose medications could reach joint tissues and modulate local inflammation. This mechanism opens a potential new treatment route for arthritis. Clinical studies will test joint-specific outcomes in patients.

8. **AI Analysis Reveals Persistent Flaws in Clinical Trial Design — Phesi Report**
   Fewer than one in three trial protocols connect to documented patient data and outcomes. The report shows flawed decision patterns risk being repeated at larger scale through AI tools. Researchers examined protocol structures across multiple therapeutic areas. Better integration of real-world data is recommended to improve future trials. Regulatory bodies are reviewing the implications.

9. **U.S. Government Secures Experimental Ebola Antibody Doses — Fierce Biotech**
   A San Diego biotech was selected to supply an investigational antibody amid a worsening Ebola outbreak in central Africa. The therapy targets the virus directly during active transmission. Deployment focuses on treatment rather than prevention at this stage. Health officials continue monitoring case numbers and strain characteristics. Additional supply agreements may follow depending on epidemic trajectory.

10. **TROP2-Directed ADC Combo Improves Lung Cancer Progression Outcomes — ASCO Abstract**
    Combining a TROP2 antibody-drug conjugate with Keytruda reduced progression or death risk by 65 percent versus Keytruda alone. The data come from treatment-naïve patients with PD-L1-positive non-small cell lung cancer. The abstract was released ahead of the 2026 ASCO meeting. Tolerability and overall survival data remain under evaluation. Phase 3 confirmation is expected later this year.

### Science Deep Dive: How Sleep Clears Brain Waste
Most people assume the brain rests completely during sleep with little active maintenance occurring. In reality, sleep triggers a specialized clearance system that removes metabolic byproducts accumulated during waking hours. Right now, as you rest tonight, your brain's glymphatic pathways expand to allow cerebrospinal fluid to flush through tissue more efficiently. One memorable detail is that this flow can increase by up to tenfold compared with wakefulness, moving proteins and other debris toward drainage routes. The process also involves coordinated drops in heart rate variability and shifts in blood volume that support fluid exchange. Without adequate time in deeper sleep stages, these clearance mechanisms slow and waste products build up. A practical step is to protect consistent sleep timing, since even modest schedule disruptions can measurably reduce overnight clearance efficiency. Watch for upcoming studies that link specific sleep metrics directly to long-term cognitive outcomes.
