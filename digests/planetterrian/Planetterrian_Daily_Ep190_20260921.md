1. **AI Now Functions as Health Determinant** — Nature
   A new paper in Nature argues that artificial intelligence now operates as a determinant of health.
   The authors frame AI systems as active influences on health outcomes through data interpretation and decision support.
   The argument rests on how AI models process clinical information at scale across large populations.
   The paper calls for treating AI as a distinct factor alongside traditional determinants such as genetics and environment.
   It positions AI alongside established categories like socioeconomic status and lifestyle behaviors in health models. Source: [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44360-026-00188-w?error=cookies_not_supported&code=f6c98e88-9a8a-44d3-bf14-f08afcb6a702)

2. **AI Model Predicts Cancer From Chest X-Rays** — Cell Press
   A multimodal AI model named Oncoformer examined electronic health records and chest X-rays from 3.67 million individuals.
   The model predicted cancer at least one year before clinical diagnosis across multiple cancer types.
   Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve reached 0.869 for all cancers combined.
   Performance reached 0.905 for cervical cancer and 0.896 for colon cancer specifically.
   The study appears in an open-access Cell Press journal and includes detailed validation metrics. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/EricTopol/status/2101706576595931258)

6. **Cooling Liquids Show Particle Clusters in Glass Transition** — Phys.org

   These clusters explain why certain liquids resist crystallization even at low temperatures.
   The findings connect everyday observations of glass behavior to underlying molecular physics mechanisms. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-cooling-liquids-reveal-limiting-particle.html)

   Quiet periods feature reduced magnetic turbulence and fewer sunspots visible on the surface.
   Accurate prediction of cycle intensity affects satellite operations and power-grid stability worldwide.
   Observations since the 1800s have documented the repeating pattern with increasing precision over time. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-solar-sun.html)

### Science Deep Dive: How Fossil Chemistry Reveals Ancient Body Temperatures
Most people assume dinosaurs behaved like modern reptiles and could not maintain steady internal heat. Fossil tooth enamel tells a different story. Isotope ratios in T. rex teeth match temperatures near those of living elephants, indicating active metabolic heat production. Right now, every mammal listener maintains core temperature through mitochondrial activity in muscle and organ tissue. The same chemical signatures that distinguish warm from cold metabolisms in living species can be read in teeth that are millions of years old. One memorable detail is that the measured temperatures sit within a few degrees of 37 °C. Future isotope work on additional dinosaur species will show whether this pattern was widespread or limited to large predators.
