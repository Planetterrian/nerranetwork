Genomic variants shape engineered T-cell safety and a mouse study shows lifelong valine restriction extends lifespan.

Lifelong dietary restriction of the branched-chain amino acid valine increased both median and maximum lifespan in a mouse model. The intervention preserved physical function and reduced age-related tissue changes without the severe caloric deficit required by traditional restriction protocols.

Researchers tracked metabolic and inflammatory markers across multiple tissues and found sustained improvements in mitochondrial function and reduced senescence signals. The effect appears tied to altered nutrient-sensing pathways that respond specifically to valine levels. Follow-up work will test whether short-term or late-life restriction produces similar benefits.

If cell identity can be reset through epigenetic maintenance, similar principles may soon apply to engineered immune cells.

A study in Science Immunology examined how inherited genomic variants influence the performance of engineered T cells used in cancer and autoimmune treatments. Researchers identified specific variants that modify both therapeutic potency and the risk of adverse effects in patient cells.

The work analyzed T-cell products from multiple donors and linked variant status to differences in expansion, persistence, and cytokine release. This mechanism points to future screening steps that could match patients to safer cell products. Next steps include prospective validation in ongoing trials to test whether pre-treatment genotyping improves outcomes.

Moving from clinical tools to fundamental physics, a newly recognized magnetic state may reshape how we build devices.

The 2026 Europhysics Prize recognizes the discovery of altermagnetism, a previously unrecognized fundamental magnetic state with properties distinct from ferromagnetism and antiferromagnetism. Theoretical and experimental work demonstrated that altermagnetic materials combine zero net magnetization with strong spin-polarized currents.

The finding opens routes to new spintronic devices that avoid stray fields while retaining useful magnetic responses. Several candidate materials have already been synthesized and characterized. Device prototypes are the next milestone.

Researchers measured metastable states in ytterbium ions that persist far longer than previously quantified. These states maintain coherence suitable for quantum logic operations and optical clock references. The measurements resolve a decades-old question about the practical lifetime of the states under realistic conditions.

Both quantum-computing architectures and next-generation atomic clocks stand to benefit from the improved stability data. Engineering teams are now incorporating the states into prototype systems.
