### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries

1. **Sleep disorders tied to thalamus shrinkage and attention changes — Science Daily**
   Researchers at Florida International University combined results from 57 brain imaging studies of adults with sleep disorders. Both dyssomnias such as insomnia and sleep apnea and parasomnias such as sleepwalking showed consistent reductions in thalamus volume. The pulvinar subregion of the thalamus, which directs attention and supports cognitive control, was particularly affected. People with parasomnias also showed structural differences in the posterior cingulate cortex linked to motivation and emotion regulation. These patterns help explain slower reaction times and decision-making difficulties reported in sleep disorders. The analysis did not find a consistent structural signature unique to dyssomnias. Senior author Matthew Sutherland noted that the shared patterns clarify how sleep problems affect focus and everyday functioning. Lead author Katharine Crooks stated that looking across many studies revealed shared brain patterns tied to reaction times and errors. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261104012303.htm)

2. **Two to three cups of caffeinated coffee daily linked to lower dementia risk — ScienceDaily**
   A long-term study spanning up to 43 years found that drinking two to three cups of caffeinated coffee each day was associated with markedly lower dementia incidence. The same intake correlated with better cognitive performance across the follow-up period. Benefits appeared regardless of genetic risk factors for dementia. Decaffeinated coffee showed no comparable association with reduced risk or improved cognition. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261105224550.htm)

3. **New jellyfish species Copula lucentia described off Alicante — Phys.org**
   Researchers at the University of Alicante formally described Copula lucentia, a jellyfish only a few millimeters long, from specimens collected near El Campello. The species was identified through work at the university's Ramón Margalef Multidisciplinary Institute for Environmental Research. The find adds to evidence that the Mediterranean still harbors undescribed marine life. Source: [phys.org](https://www.phys.org/news/2026-11-scientists-jellyfish-species-alicante-spain.html)

4. **Insects sustain vertebrates through habitat and health roles beyond food — Phys.org**
   At least 14 million insect species provide ecosystem services that include pollination, waste decomposition, and creation of physical structures used by other animals. These contributions extend to supporting animal health and habitat complexity in ways that cannot be replaced by simple prey biomass. Source: [phys.org](https://www.phys.org/news/2026-11-hidden-ways-insects-animal-life.html)

5. **Medieval sanitation measures increased roundworm prevalence in Trondheim — Phys.org**
   Introduction of deliberate public health interventions in medieval Trondheim coincided with higher rates of roundworm infection in the population. The sanitation changes appear to have created conditions that favored parasite spread despite their intent to improve health. Source: [phys.org](https://www.phys.org/news/2026-11-introduction-medieval-health-exposed-population.html)

### Science Deep Dive: How sleep disorders reshape attention circuits
Most people assume sleep disorders only produce daytime tiredness that lifts after a good night’s rest. The pooled imaging data show instead that several disorders coincide with lasting reductions in thalamus volume, a structure that filters sensory information and sustains focus. This volume loss appears in the pulvinar, the portion that helps the brain spotlight relevant signals while ignoring distractions. The same region participates in networks that support working memory and error monitoring, so its shrinkage aligns with the slower reaction times and higher mistake rates reported by patients. One concrete observation from the analysis is that both insomnia-related and behavior-related sleep problems produced measurable pulvinar differences, suggesting a common pathway even when the original triggers differ. For anyone whose sleep is fragmented, the finding reframes routine grogginess as a possible signal that attention circuits are already changing. Tracking personal sleep consistency with a simple diary or wearable and discussing persistent issues with a clinician therefore becomes a practical step while researchers test whether early intervention can limit further structural shifts.
