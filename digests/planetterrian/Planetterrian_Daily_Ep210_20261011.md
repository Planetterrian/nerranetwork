### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Hidden Drainage Pathway Found at Back of Eye** — ScienceDaily
   Researchers at the University of British Columbia and the University of Toronto identified the posterior ocular lymphatic outflow pathway in mice. The pathway drains fluid and metabolic waste from the retina into the body's lymphatic network. The retina is one of the most metabolically active parts of the body, constantly generating byproducts that need to be cleared. Dr. Neeru Gupta, professor and head of UBC's department of ophthalmology and visual sciences, said the discovery helps explain how the eye flushes this waste. The system was demonstrated through direct tracing of outflow routes in the animal model. The finding challenges long-standing assumptions about how the eye functions. The work could open new approaches for treating glaucoma and macular degeneration. The team named the route the posterior ocular lymphatic outflow, or POLO pathway. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261008005254.htm)

2. **Self-Stabilizing Nuclear Clock Built with Thorium** — ScienceDaily
   A team at TU Wien constructed the first nuclear clock that maintains stability without continuous reference to an atomic clock. The device uses transitions between nuclear energy states in thorium atoms triggered by laser light. Researchers showed steady operation for more than twenty-four hours in the initial tests. The small energy gap between thorium nuclear states allows laser control that is not feasible with most other nuclei. Earlier work in 2024 established the laser excitation of thorium nuclei. The clock could eventually exceed the precision of current optical atomic clocks for metrology applications. The breakthrough relies on thorium's unusual nuclear property that permits laser-driven transitions. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261008102701.htm)

3. **Polyethylene Microplastics Linked to Fatty Liver Changes** — ScienceDaily
   Texas A&M researchers exposed mice to polyethylene microplastics and observed increased markers of fatty liver disease. The effect occurred even without an added high-fat diet. When combined with a diet high in fat, fructose, and cholesterol, liver damage indicators rose further. Polyethylene accounts for roughly one-third of global plastic production and appears in food packaging and containers. Dr. Adi Joshi stated that these particles affect the liver's natural defense and repair mechanisms. The study measured fat accumulation inside liver cells as the primary outcome. The findings suggest environmental pollutants and poor diet may reinforce each other's effects on the liver. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261008102718.htm)

4. **Birch Tar Shows Antibacterial Activity Against Wound Pathogens** — ScienceDaily
   Researchers at the University of Cologne, University of Oxford, University of Liège, and Cape Breton University produced birch tar using methods inferred from Neanderthal sites. Every sample inhibited growth of bacteria that cause wound infections. The material was previously understood mainly as an adhesive for hafting stone tools. The team tested the tar directly on relevant bacterial strains in laboratory assays. The findings appear in PLOS One. The work suggests Neanderthals may have recognized additional uses for the substance beyond tool construction. The tar was obtained by heating birch bark in controlled conditions that match archaeological evidence. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261008005256.htm)

5. **New Sauropodomorph Dinosaur Identified in Zimbabwe** — Phys.org
   Paleontologists described Musango matusadonaensis from fossils recovered along the Nyamasango River. The subadult specimen measured approximately four point five meters long and weighed two hundred twenty kilograms. Bone growth marks indicated it was still growing at death. The skeleton shows bipedal posture and belongs to the group that later produced long-necked giants. The find demonstrates that Zimbabwean dinosaur faunas contain species distinct from those in South Africa. Environmental differences are suspected as the driver of regional variation. The incomplete skeleton lacked a skull, leaving diet uncertain though relatives were largely herbivorous. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-zimbabwe-dinosaurs-south-africa-species.html)

6. **Seed-Sized Wireless Device Controls Nerve Activity** — ScienceDaily
   Engineers at NYU Abu Dhabi and Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi developed an injectable device roughly the size of a seed. The implant delivers programmable electrical stimulation near targeted nerves without batteries or wires. Power reaches the device wirelessly from outside the body. Placement can be guided and verified with ultrasound or CT imaging. Early tests showed the device can alter nerve firing patterns in real time. The work was published in Science Advances. The technology aims to treat chronic pain and movement disorders through precise, adjustable stimulation. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261008005253.htm)

7. **Personalized Gait Adjustment Reduces Knee Osteoarthritis Pain** — ScienceDaily
   A year-long randomized trial at the University of Utah, New York University, and Stanford University tested foot-angle retraining in people with knee osteoarthritis. Participants who adopted a personalized walking angle reported pain reductions comparable to medication effects. MRI scans showed slower loss of cartilage integrity in the adjusted group. The approach alters mechanical loading on the knee without drugs or surgery. The trial was the first placebo-controlled study of gait retraining maintained over twelve months. Researchers measured both pain scores and imaging markers of joint health. The method changes how the foot strikes the ground to shift pressure away from damaged cartilage areas. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261009025434.htm)

8. **Short Immunotherapy Course Prevents Bowel Cancer Recurrence** — ScienceDaily
   The NEOPRISM-CRC trial gave pembrolizumab for up to nine weeks before surgery to thirty-two patients with a specific genetic subtype of stage two or three bowel cancer. After nearly three years of follow-up, no patient experienced recurrence. The result held even for participants who retained small traces of disease after treatment. The regimen replaced standard post-operative chemotherapy in this group. The trial focused on mismatch-repair-deficient tumors. Outcomes were tracked through imaging and clinical monitoring. The approach targets tumors with high mutation burdens that respond strongly to immune checkpoint blockade. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261007042115.htm)

### Planetterrian Spotlight
The newly identified posterior ocular lymphatic outflow pathway offers a concrete route for studying how metabolic waste leaves the retina. Earlier models treated the back of the eye as largely isolated from lymphatic clearance, which left unexplained how fluid and inflammatory material accumulated in glaucoma and macular degeneration. Direct tracing in mice now shows fluid moving from retinal tissue into lymphatic vessels that connect to systemic drainage. This changes the experimental questions researchers can ask about pressure regulation and waste buildup. Clinicians may eventually test whether enhancing flow along this route alters disease progression in specific patient groups. One open measurement is whether the pathway operates at different rates across age or disease states in humans.

### Science Deep Dive: How Personalized Foot Angles Change Knee Mechanics
Most people assume knee arthritis pain stems only from cartilage loss that medication or surgery must address, yet the trial shows a simple shift in foot position during walking measurably reduces load on damaged areas. The knee joint receives different forces depending on the angle at which the foot contacts the ground with each step. One practical detail from the year-long study is that participants maintained the adjusted gait without ongoing supervision after initial training. When the foot angle moves pressure away from the inner knee compartment, cartilage experiences less compressive stress over thousands of daily steps. Future imaging could confirm whether this mechanical change slows structural decline beyond the one-year mark already measured. People with knee osteoarthritis can consult a physical therapist to test a personalized foot angle during walking assessments.
