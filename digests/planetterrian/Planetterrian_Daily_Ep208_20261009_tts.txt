Something most people get wrong is the idea that plants simply draw nitrogen from the air the way they draw carbon dioxide.

Atmospheric nitrogen gas stays inert until specialized microbes convert it into ammonia through nitrogen fixation.

The enzyme nitrogenase performs this conversion inside root nodules of legumes or in free-living soil bacteria, and the process requires substantial energy from the microbes.

A recent study identified a molecular off switch that bacteria use to halt fixation when fixed nitrogen is already abundant nearby.
