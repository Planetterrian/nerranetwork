### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries

1. **Crystals record shared trigger for Andean eruptions — Phys.org**
   University of Queensland researchers examined thin cross-sections of 28 rocks from seven volcanoes and two ancient super-eruptions across a 230-kilometer stretch of the Central Andes. Ph.D. candidate Daniela Parra-Encalada analyzed the chemical composition of the smallest plagioclase microcrysts, each roughly the width of a human hair. These newest crystals formed during the final stages of eruption and carried the same chemical signature across samples of different ages and eruptive styles. Modeling linked the signature to very hot magma rising from deeper underground and intruding into a shallow reservoir. The team named the pattern the “plagioclase eruptibility field.” Senior author Terese Ubide noted the pattern also appears in other Pacific Ring of Fire datasets. The work is published in Nature Communications. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-crystals-hint-volcano-ability-erupt.html)

2. **Miro1 protein buildup drives muscle insulin resistance in type 2 diabetes — ScienceDaily**
   Scientists found that accumulation of the protein Miro1 impairs insulin signaling in muscle tissue. Preclinical removal of Miro1 restored insulin responsiveness in the affected muscles. Exercise lowered Miro1 levels in people with diabetes. The findings point to a previously unrecognized pathway that contributes to insulin resistance. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261106174543.htm)

3. **Radiocarbon analysis detects previously unknown Nankai Trough earthquakes — Phys.org**
   University of Tokyo researchers applied a new radiocarbon method to a sediment core from the Kumano Sea. The analysis identified multiple hidden earthquake traces in the Nankai Trough. Reconstructing these events improves forecasts of future geophysical activity in the region. The study appears in Radiocarbon. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-radiocarbon-analysis-uncovers-hidden-earthquakes.html)

4. **Three-amino-acid difference makes histone variant essential for mouse sperm development — Phys.org**
   Kyushu University researchers showed that the H3mm15 subvariant of histone H3.3 cannot be replaced by standard H3.3 during spermatogenesis. The proteins differ at only three amino acids yet produce distinct behavior inside chromatin. H3mm15 helps regulate gene expression required for normal sperm formation. The work demonstrates that minor sequence changes can create non-interchangeable functional roles. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-small-histone-differences-gene-sperm.html)

5. **Clinical simulation model adds ethics and emotional skills for nursing training — Phys.org**
   Researchers developed a simulation approach that incorporates care ethics, relational presence with patients, and emotional management alongside technical skills. Earlier methods focused mainly on compliance and procedural standards from an interprofessional viewpoint. The new model addresses aspects of nursing practice previously overlooked in standard assessments. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-clinical-simulation-empowerment-autonomy-nursing.html)

6. **Lambayeque Valley survey finds low violence during El Niño events — Phys.org**
   Archaeologists examined social responses to extreme climate episodes in northern Peru. Despite resource stress from catastrophic flooding, violence remained extremely rare across the surveyed periods. The results challenge assumptions that ecological upheaval necessarily increases conflict or ritual sacrifice. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-sacrificing-people-catastrophic-el-nio.html)

7. **New killifish species discovered in three Brazilian forest pools — ScienceDaily**
   Researchers identified Laimosemion laranja, a tiny orange-patterned fish less than three centimeters long. The species occurs only in three small pools threatened by mining, deforestation, and pollution. Its restricted range raises immediate conservation concerns. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261104084019.htm)

8. **Theoretical gravitational reconnection identified in Einstein equations — ScienceDaily**
   Columbia University and Universidad Adolfo Ibáñez researchers described structures within gravitational fields that can break and reconnect. The process transfers energy into matter. The finding emerges directly from analysis of the existing equations. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261104084011.htm)

9. **Liquid-assisted growth reduces defects in perovskite films for solar cells and LEDs — Phys.org**
   A team led by Seong Sik Shin at Sungkyunkwan University developed a method that produces perovskite semiconductor thin films with fewer defects. The same platform yielded both high-performance solar cells and LEDs. International collaborators included institutions in Korea, the United States, and Japan. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-liquid-crystal-growth-perovskite-defects.html)

10. **New ground-dwelling bird species identified in Liang Bua cave on Flores — Phys.org**
    Researchers recovered a 190,000–120,000-year-old leg bone initially set aside as unremarkable. Re-examination established it as the largest known member of its bird group. The find comes from the same cave that yielded Homo floresiensis remains. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-species-ground-bird-largest-kind.html)

11. **Dundee biotech receives £2 million Gates Foundation grant for infectious-disease work — streamlinefeed.co.ke**
    A Dundee-based firm secured funding to advance early-stage research on pathogens affecting low- and middle-income countries. The grant supports integration into the Gates Foundation’s Global Health Discovery Collaboratory network, providing access to shared assays and compound libraries. Source: [streamlinefeed.co.ke](https://streamlinefeed.co.ke/news/dundee-biotech-firm-secures-2m-gates-foundation-grant-for-infectious-disease-research)

12. **Magnesium supports vitamin D activation and breakdown enzymes — Rhonda Patrick**
    Magnesium participates in the liver and kidney conversions that produce active calcitriol. It also aids parathyroid hormone secretion that helps regulate vitamin D balance. Nearly half of Americans fall short of estimated magnesium requirements from food and beverages. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/foundmyfitness/status/2119478647547433412)
