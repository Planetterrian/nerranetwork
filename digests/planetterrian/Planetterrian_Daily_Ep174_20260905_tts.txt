Now, here's something most people get wrong about why D N A uses exactly four bases.

Most people assume the four D N A bases are chemically fixed because any change would break replication or transcription.

In reality, the enzymes that read D N A evolved flexible active sites that can accommodate variations when the geometry fits.

Right now, inside every cell, R N A polymerase grips each base pair through hydrogen bonds and shape complementarity rather than recognizing the exact atoms of A, T, C, or G.

The UC San Diego imaging shows the enzyme uses the same contacts for synthetic letters, provided the overall size and pairing pattern remain similar.
