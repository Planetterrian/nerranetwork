### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Four life stages guide women’s heart care across decades — STAT News**
   Four medical societies issued a framework that divides women’s cardiovascular health into four distinct phases from childhood through older age. The American Heart Association, American Academy of Pediatrics, Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine, and American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists developed the guidance to address gaps that persist even after female-only trial requirements began in 1993. Differences in heart-muscle chromosomes, symptom presentation, and disease causes between women and men are cited as reasons for the unified approach. Three supporting articles appeared in Circulation the same day. The societies aimed to unite clinicians and patients over a lifetime of care with the explicit goal of reducing cardiovascular disease as the leading cause of death in women. Wide gaps in care across specialties remain despite the chromosome and symptom differences already documented in trials. Source: [statnews.com](https://www.statnews.com/2026/10/26/heart-health-women-four-life-stages-qa-sadiya-khan/?utm_campaign=rss)

2. **Liquid halts tooth decay without drilling in children — ScienceDaily**
   A major clinical trial tested a simple liquid applied to teeth in seconds and found it stopped cavities in young children. The treatment requires no injections or anesthesia. Researchers noted the approach could reduce painful procedures and surgery for thousands of children while supporting wider regulatory review. The inexpensive treatment could spare thousands of children painful dental procedures and surgery while paving the way for broader FDA-approved use. The liquid is painted onto teeth in seconds during routine visits. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261025222339.htm)

3. **Crows decide visual choices faster than humans or monkeys — Phys.org**
   University of Tübingen researchers tested two carrion crows on a moving-dot direction task. The birds averaged 235 milliseconds to identify direction and respond while maintaining high accuracy. The result is roughly twice the speed recorded for humans and monkeys under comparable conditions. The experiment presented dot patterns moving in different directions and required the crows to indicate the perceived direction. High accuracy was preserved even at the faster decision speed. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-crows-decisions-fast-humans-monkeys.html)

4. **Two daily drinks carry one-in-25 lifetime mortality risk — ScienceDaily**
   A large study linked consumption of two alcoholic beverages per day to a 1-in-25 lifetime risk of death from alcohol-related causes. No significant overall health benefits appeared across consumption levels. Risk rose steadily as intake increased. The analysis challenged the common view that moderate drinking is harmless by isolating alcohol’s contribution to mortality. Researchers tracked outcomes across varying intake amounts and found the risk curve continued upward beyond two drinks. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261024222030.htm)

5. **Spinosaurus spent most of its life in water, fossils indicate — ScienceDaily**
   Yale researchers applied machine learning to thousands of fossil measurements from ancient reptiles. The analysis showed Spinosaurus lived predominantly underwater, while some previously assumed fully aquatic species spent substantial time on land. The machine-learning model compared skeletal features across many specimens to classify habitat use. Results indicated that Spinosaurus spent most of its life underwater while certain marine reptiles retained significant terrestrial activity. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261024221956.htm)

6. **Pancreatic cancer cells rewrite DNA to evade treatment — ScienceDaily**
   Laboratory experiments revealed how pancreatic cancer cells continually reshape their genetic makeup to become more aggressive and resistant. Blocking one key gene reversed several of these changes. The finding points to a potential route for new therapeutic strategies. The cells were observed altering DNA structure repeatedly during growth and exposure to treatment conditions. Researchers identified the gene block as sufficient to undo multiple resistance-related modifications in the lab setting. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261024221849.htm)

7. **Sea-cucumber-inspired gel may repair soft tissue then disappear — Phys.org**
   University of Waterloo scientists created a gel that mimics the sea cucumber’s ability to alter body stiffness. The material is designed to support damaged tendons and other tissues during healing before degrading. It leaves no permanent implant behind. The gel changes stiffness in response to local conditions in a manner modeled directly on the invertebrate’s natural tissue behavior. Development focused on matching mechanical support needs during the repair phase without leaving residual material. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-sea-cucumberinspired-gel-tissue-humans.html)

8. **Low-dose aspirin tied to 70 percent lower dementia risk in specific genotype — ScienceDaily**
   Older adults carrying a particular genetic profile showed a 70 percent relative reduction in dementia risk while taking low-dose aspirin. Earlier studies found no overall protective effect across broader populations. Genetics may therefore explain why benefits appear in some individuals but not others. The reduction was measured in a defined genetic subgroup within the larger cohort. No comparable effect emerged when the same dose was examined without genotype stratification. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261024222026.htm)

### Planetterrian Spotlight
The sea-cucumber gel stands out because it draws directly from an animal’s natural ability to change tissue stiffness on demand. The material is intended to provide temporary mechanical support exactly where tendons or other soft tissues are damaged, then break down once repair is complete. This approach could reduce complications associated with permanent implants while matching the mechanical needs of the healing site. University of Waterloo researchers modeled the stiffness transition on the invertebrate’s documented tissue response. One open question is how the gel performs under repeated mechanical stress in larger animal models before human testing begins. The design avoids leaving foreign material in the body after the support phase ends.

### Science Deep Dive: Moderate Drinking and Lifetime Risk
Most people assume two alcoholic drinks a day fall within a safe or even protective range. A recent large analysis found this level instead carries a one-in-25 lifetime chance of death from alcohol-related causes, with risk climbing steadily as intake rises and no broad health benefit detected. The finding applies directly to daily choices about evening routines or social habits that many treat as routine. The study tracked mortality across consumption levels and isolated the contribution of alcohol itself from other lifestyle factors. Alcohol exerts its effects through multiple routes inside the body, including direct damage to liver cells, disruption of heart rhythm regulation, and gradual elevation of blood pressure over years. These processes accumulate even when intake stays at moderate levels for most days of the week. The one-in-25 figure comes from population-level tracking that compared death records against self-reported drinking patterns, separating alcohol-attributable causes from other mortality. What changes for the listener is the concrete number attached to a common intake level, allowing a direct comparison against personal habits rather than vague guidelines. Follow-up work that separates genetic susceptibility from average population effects would clarify who faces the steepest part of that curve. The absence of any net health benefit across the full range of consumption levels further removes the earlier assumption that small amounts confer protection. People can therefore weigh the documented mortality increment against their own patterns without relying on outdated reassurance about moderation.
