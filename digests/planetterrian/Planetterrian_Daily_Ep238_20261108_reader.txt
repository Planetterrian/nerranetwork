Florida International University researchers, as Science Daily reported, combined adult imaging of dyssomnias such as insomnia and sleep apnea with parasomnias such as sleepwalking.

The pulvinar subregion, which directs attention and supports cognitive control, was particularly affected.

People with parasomnias also showed structural differences in the posterior cingulate cortex linked to motivation and emotion regulation.

The analysis did not find a consistent structural signature unique to dyssomnias, even as shared anatomy tracked problems of focus.

Senior author Matthew Sutherland noted that the shared patterns clarify how sleep problems affect focus and everyday functioning.

Lead author Katharine Crooks stated that looking across many studies revealed shared brain patterns tied to reaction times and errors.

Drinking two to three cups of caffeinated coffee each day was associated with markedly lower dementia incidence.

Follow-up in that work stretched up to forty-three years.

The same intake correlated with better cognitive performance across the follow-up period.

Those associations appeared regardless of genetic risk factors for dementia.

Decaffeinated coffee showed no comparable association with reduced risk or improved cognition.
