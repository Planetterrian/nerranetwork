### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **New framework reframes wildlife hazards as climate-style risks — Phys.org**
   An international team from the University of Plymouth, University College London, University of Washington and six other institutions published the framework in Nature Ecology & Evolution. Every year over 700,000 people die from animal-transmitted diseases, snakebites kill up to 500,000 people, and crop losses from pests cost around $220 billion. The approach treats harms as the intersection of hazards, exposure and vulnerability, mirroring IPCC methods. Dr. Matthew Faith noted the structure supports evidence-based policy without prescribing specific species management. Poor health-care access and socioeconomic inequality raise vulnerability to mosquito-borne disease, snakebite and crocodile attacks. The authors call for collaborative management with local communities to incorporate local knowledge. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-rethink-threats-posed-hazardous-species.html)

2. **Six measures could cut 1.6 million deaths and trillions in costs — Phys.org**
   The World Health Organization report lists heat-health warning systems, climate-resilient drinking-water and sanitation infrastructure, improved hand hygiene, cleaner household energy and efficient fossil-fuel pricing. Implementation could avoid 16.7 billion tonnes of carbon dioxide emissions each year. The measures could generate up to $5.4 trillion in fiscal benefits annually plus nearly $4 trillion in broader economic gains from saved lives and reduced health-care costs. WHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus stated that scaling proven actions could deliver the greatest gains in countries that need them most. Projections rest on ambitious scenarios with acknowledged data gaps. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-million-year.html)

3. **Neutrino telescope sets new limits on Lorentz invariance — Phys.org**
   The KM3NeT/ORCA detector recorded 5,828 atmospheric neutrino candidate events over 1.4 years using its first six detection units. Analysis found no evidence of Lorentz invariance violation yet established tighter bounds, including the first direct constraints on certain muon-tau oscillation effects. Corresponding author Alba Domi stated the results remain competitive with longer-running experiments despite the partial configuration. Future data from the expanding array and higher-energy ARCA site are expected to probe smaller effects. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-einstein-laws-bottom-mediterranean-sea.html)

4. **Tumor cells tricked into producing their own cancer drugs — ScienceDaily**
   Researchers engineered a system that uses carbon monoxide naturally generated by tumor cells to assemble potent therapeutic compounds inside those same cells. Early laboratory tests indicate the approach can selectively target cancer cells while sparing healthy tissue. The strategy aims to reduce the systemic toxicity associated with conventional chemotherapy. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261101010552.htm)

5. **Continuous sensors track corn growth responses in real time — Phys.org**
   The platform allows plant breeders and agronomists to download ongoing data on stalk and ear development. It replaces broad general answers with field-specific measurements of how individual plants respond to fertilizer and other inputs.

6. **College students report widespread climate anxiety and limited coping resources — Phys.org**
   University of Utah researchers found that undergraduates across disciplines feel powerless and frustrated about Earth's future. Few institutional supports are available to manage the distress. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-anxiety-climate-affects-college-students.html)

7. **One in 15 adults has experienced AI-generated image-based abuse — Phys.org**
   A survey of more than 7,200 adults across the United States, United Kingdom and Australia found that 7 percent had encountered at least one form of AI-generated image-based abuse since turning 18. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-adults-experienced-ai-generated-image.html)

8. **Varying message content boosts social-media engagement without changing personality — Phys.org**
   Case Western Reserve University research shows that public figures increase engagement by changing topics and wording while preserving a consistent psychological voice. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-social-media-engagement-message-personality.html)

9. **Lab-grown neurons keep embryonic 3D genome features — Phys.org**
   Skoltech-led researchers compared more than 300 3D genome maps from 62 human samples and 34 mouse samples. The maps included embryonic stem cells, induced pluripotent stem cells, neural progenitors, fetal neurons, postmortem adult neurons and neurons differentiated in vitro. Stem-cell-derived neurons retain organizational traits characteristic of embryonic development rather than matching adult brain neurons.

10. **NIH Common Fund Data Ecosystem links 18 program datasets — Phys.org**
    The platform makes research data from 18 NIH programs easier to discover, connect and analyze across studies that previously remained isolated. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-ecosystem-scientists-explore-biological.html)

11. **Nelore cattle gut microbiota linked to methane output and feed efficiency — Phys.org**
    EMBRAPA researchers are mapping genetic influences on the rumen microbiome of Brazil's dominant beef breed. The work aims to identify strategies that lower methane emissions while improving feed conversion. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-nelore-cattle-microbiota-methane-emissions.html)

12. **Nasal microbiome influences persistent S. aureus colonization — Phys.org**
    The study examines why some individuals carry Staphylococcus aureus in the nose indefinitely while others do not. It focuses on interactions among resident bacterial species rather than single-pathogen factors. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-nasal-microbiome-potentially-bacteria.html)

### Planetterrian Spotlight
Researchers introduced a magnetoelectric particulate system that delivers non-invasive vagus nerve stimulation through the gut after oral administration. The nickel-ferrite core and barium-titanate shell generate electrical pulses under magnetic fields that activate vagal afferents, increase norepinephrine in the locus coeruleus, and boost dopamine synthesis in the substantia nigra while engaging the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway. In experimental Parkinson's models the approach reduced motor symptoms and anxiety-like behavior. The work appears in Nature Biomedical Engineering. Source: [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01829-x?error=cookies_not_supported&code=0bbb165a-447a-4fec-917b-71cae7083292)

### Science Deep Dive: How the nasal microbiome shapes S. aureus persistence
Most people assume that whether Staphylococcus aureus stays in the nose depends mainly on the bacterium itself or on a person's immune system alone. In reality the surrounding community of nasal bacteria plays a decisive role in allowing or preventing long-term colonization. The mechanism involves direct interactions among resident species that either compete for space and nutrients or produce compounds that inhibit S. aureus growth. One number that stands out is the finding that roughly one in three adults carries S. aureus persistently, yet the factors determining who falls into that group remain only partly understood. This matters for anyone who has ever dealt with recurrent skin infections or wondered why some household members test positive while others do not. The concrete next step the research points toward is mapping specific microbial interactions that could be shifted through targeted microbiome interventions rather than broad antibiotics.

That's the science shaping our understanding of health and the natural world today.
