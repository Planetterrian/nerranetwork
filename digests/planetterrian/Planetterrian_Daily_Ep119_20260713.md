### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Cell-type transcriptional maps recovered from bulk samples — Nature Biotech**
   DeepDETAILS performs cross-modality deconvolution on bulk sequencing data to reconstruct cell-type-specific regulatory processes. The method integrates multiple data layers to separate signals that normally blend together in standard assays. It supplies higher-resolution views of gene regulation without requiring single-cell isolation for every experiment. Researchers can now apply the approach to existing bulk datasets to extract cell-specific patterns that were previously inaccessible.

2. **Epigenetic editing advances to rewrite gene expression — Nature**
   Researchers are now altering chemical tags on DNA and chromatin to control gene activity without changing the underlying sequence. The techniques target specific marks that influence transcription rates across cell types. Early applications show precise tuning of expression levels that persist through cell divisions. Follow-up work will test durability and off-target effects in more complex tissues.

3. **Superconducting circuit runs first cyclic quantum heat engine — Phys.org**
   Aalto University researchers built the engine inside a superconducting circuit that cycles through thermodynamic states. The demonstration shows how quantum thermodynamics principles can be harnessed for practical devices. Improved understanding of these processes is expected to support larger quantum computers. The work also feeds back into classical thermodynamics by clarifying quantum-scale behavior.

4. **Harvestmen reveal separate evolutionary routes for maternal and paternal care — ScienceDaily**
   Citizen scientists contributed iNaturalist observations that more than doubled documented cases of egg-guarding in harvestmen. Analysis showed maternal and paternal care followed distinct evolutionary trajectories rather than one preceding the other. The rapid data collection completed the core dataset in days. The project illustrates how public observations can resolve long-standing questions in behavioral evolution.

5. **Plant engineered to produce five psychedelic compounds at once — Phys.org**
   Weizmann Institute researchers combined biosynthetic pathways for five mind-altering substances into a single organism. The compounds normally occur across distant branches of the tree of life. The engineered plant assembles the full set through coordinated metabolic steps. Future work will examine yields and stability of the multi-compound production system.

6. **Spacecraft exhaust threatens lunar records of early Earth chemistry — ScienceDaily**
   A new study models how rocket exhaust can contaminate the Moon’s most scientifically valuable sites. The contamination could obscure chemical signatures linked to the origin of life on Earth. Researchers recommend new landing protocols and monitoring before widespread missions begin. The findings highlight an immediate preservation issue for lunar science.

7. **Northern tree swallows face tighter climate timing constraints than southern populations — Phys.org**
   Cornell researchers analyzed nearly 95,000 nests over five decades across the species range. Northern birds experience greater risk because breeding windows compress under rising temperatures even though their temperature responses match those of southern populations. The mismatch affects chick survival and population stability. Continued monitoring will track whether adaptive shifts occur.

8. **New planning tool incorporates community input for urban tree planting — Phys.org**
   Researchers worked with residents and officials in five UK cities to create guidance for tree selection and placement. The approach balances human benefits with ecological needs under hotter conditions. Local knowledge shaped criteria that standard models often overlook. Cities can now apply the framework to meet both canopy and biodiversity targets.

9. **Machine learning improves biosensor accuracy for freshwater toxin detection — Phys.org**
   Screen-printed carbon electrode biosensors were calibrated with machine learning to detect microcystin-LR at low concentrations. The toxin, produced during algal blooms, damages liver tissue and raises cancer risk. The World Health Organization guideline stands at 1 microgram per liter in drinking water. The updated sensors offer faster, lower-cost field monitoring.

10. **Drone-based laser system maps volcanic gas ratios for eruption forecasting — Phys.org**
    Technical University of Munich researchers developed a drone-reflected laser method that measures carbon dioxide and sulfur dioxide concentrations in real time. The CO2/SO2 ratio serves as a leading indicator of impending eruptions. The system generates concentration maps without placing instruments directly in hazardous zones. Operational testing will determine integration into existing monitoring networks.

11. **Corporate biodiversity pledges lack measurable targets in most cases — Phys.org**
    Oxford and Stockholm Resilience Centre analysis of 180 major companies found only 13 percent of commitments precise enough for external evaluation. The remainder use vague language that prevents progress tracking. The study appears in One Earth. Stronger standardization would allow better accountability.

12. **PBDE flame retardants persist in Middle East and North Africa environments — Phys.org**
    University of Sharjah researchers documented ongoing contamination from polybrominated diphenyl ethers in the region. The compounds, once used in electronics and furniture, continue to cycle through air, soil, and water despite bans. The work updates exposure assessments for populations still encountering legacy sources. Further sampling will clarify current risk levels.

13. **Heat waves linked to at least 2,700 deaths in England and Wales — Phys.org**
    A study released this week attributed the fatalities to May and June heat events. The findings quantify direct mortality impacts under current climate conditions. Public health agencies can use the data to refine heat-warning thresholds. Expanded surveillance will capture effects in subsequent seasons.

14. **New imaging technique identifies crystal defects in LED materials — Phys.org**
    University of Liverpool and University of Strathclyde researchers demonstrated a method to locate nanoscale defects that reduce LED efficiency. The approach reveals how defects form during manufacturing. Correcting these sites could improve device performance across lighting and display applications. Next steps involve scaling the imaging for industrial use.

15. **Blue-light method streamlines synthesis of complex drug molecules — Phys.org**
    Researchers upgraded a classic reaction with visible light to assemble intricate structures in fewer steps. The approach reduces solvent use and intermediate isolations common in traditional routes. It targets molecules needed for drug discovery programs. Further optimization will test compatibility with diverse functional groups.

### Planetterrian Spotlight
The Aalto University quantum heat engine demonstration places thermodynamic cycling inside a superconducting circuit for the first time. This concrete hardware step supplies a testbed for scaling quantum systems while also refining models of energy flow at the quantum level. The immediate next measurements will track efficiency limits and coherence times under repeated cycles. How quickly can similar circuits be adapted to perform useful computational tasks beyond proof-of-concept operation?

### Science Deep Dive: Separate Paths to Parental Care
Most people assume maternal care evolved first and paternal care followed later in animals. In harvestmen, however, the two forms of egg-guarding arose along independent evolutionary routes rather than one building on the other. Right now, across countless species, parental behaviors continue to diversify through distinct genetic and ecological pressures rather than a single linear sequence. Citizen-science observations more than doubled the known cases of this behavior in days, showing how public data can resolve questions that lab studies alone could not address quickly. One memorable detail: the project completed its core dataset expansion in a matter of days instead of years. Listeners can watch for similar citizen-science projects that target other long-standing questions in behavioral evolution, especially those using photo databases to map trait distributions across continents.
