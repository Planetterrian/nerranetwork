people assume every mosquito species keys off the same human scent, so anyone who gets bitten
often would draw them all in equal measure. In reality, according to Science Daily, each species
is tuned to its own narrow combination of skin odors, chemicals, and surface bacteria rather than
a single universal human signal, and that tuning decides which mosquitoes find you on a given
evening because the blend on your skin matches some species better than others.
The work documented those distinct profiles across multiple species and showed that the
differences hold even when the same people are tested again and again, which undercuts
the idea of one type of person who draws every mosquito equally.
Broad spectrum repellents may therefore miss the specific signals used by the mosquitoes
that carry particular diseases. Refining repellents around the exact compounds each target species
detects could improve protection without raising the overall chemical load. And the next step
the team identifies is mapping which bacterial or chemical signals matter most for the species
that transmit malaria or dengue, so new formulations can be tested against those precise cues.
