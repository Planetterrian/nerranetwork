The Malik Lab at the Fred Hutchinson Cancer Center, directed by HHMI investigator Harmit Malik, tracks conflicts inside genomes that play out over a few generations rather than thousands of years, in a report carried by Phys dot org.

Postdoc Tamanash Bhattacharya found an opal stop codon inside alphaviruses, including Sindbis and chikungunya, that sets a kinetic Goldilocks balance for polyprotein production.

The virus can make sufficient protein to copy itself and still slip past immune surveillance in people and in mosquitoes.

Mosquitoes do not need that same balance because their temperatures are lower, yet the codon remains, since it still lets the virus build hidden replication compartments.

Graduate student Jeremy Hollis combined remote homology detection, AlphaFold structural modeling, and functional genetics to show that HJURP, a protein that marks centromeres at cell division, is present in most animals even though it changes too fast for ordinary search tools to catch.

Former postdoc Ching-Ho Chang found that the fruit fly protamine Mst77F evolves quickly, suppressing a meiotic drive that destroys X-bearing sperm and tilts broods toward sons.

Two papers in Science Advances lay out these mechanisms and supply rules that could guide development of new antiviral strategies.

Dynamic force applied to the tibia activated the Piezo1 mechanosensor receptor and produced measurable extensions in both healthspan and lifespan in mice.

Treated mice, in work published in Cell Metabolism, showed reduced systemic inflammation and decreased immunosenescence within bone marrow.

Genetic deletion of Piezo1 eliminated those benefits and reversed the protective effects.

Bone tissue here acts as an active regulator of whole-body aging through its ability to sense and transduce mechanical signals.

Most people assume the human brain became larger and more connected only through mutations that rewrote protein-coding genes in our lineage after it split from chimpanzees.

A retroviral infection roughly four million years ago left D N A that still influences neuron growth and wiring in one lineage but not the other.

Once a retrovirus inserts itself into germ-cell D N A, every descendant that inherits that stretch of sequence keeps it and hands it on.

In chimpanzees that leftover viral stretch sits beside a gene young neurons use to grow out processes and make connections.

The viral D N A holds that gene off during key developmental windows.

Human ancestors either never took on that insertion or changed nearby sequence enough to stop the silencing, so the gene stays on.

Lund University scientists used CRISPR to cut the viral segment out of chimpanzee stem cells.

Once the segment was gone, the gene switched back on, and neurons in the mini-brain models grew farther and connected more readily.

That cut cannot be tested in living animals, so how much this regulatory difference shapes adult brain size or circuit layout is still unknown.

The cell-level effect does show up consistently in the culture systems they used.

Protein pillars that stretch across the mitochondrial intermembrane space hold the geometry oxidative phosphorylation needs to run efficiently.

A mapping of those structures between the inner and outer membranes, carried by Phys dot org, shows they maintain that precise spacing.

Disruption of the pillars alters cristae organization and reduces cellular energy output.

The mapping fills in a layer of mitochondrial organization that had been under-appreciated.

University of Helsinki researchers compared species occurrence and abundance records against climate variables and against habitat metrics that included natural state and the share of land modified by human activity.

Habitat-related factors accounted for more variance in both presence and abundance across the studied animals than climate data alone.

Local land-management decisions provide a more immediate and actionable lever for biodiversity conservation than broad-scale climate projections in this system.

Solutions to mitigate biodiversity loss depend more directly on habitat quality than on temperature or precipitation trends here.

In Ayangue, Ecuador, the MCBAL project trained children ages seven to twelve to run monthly low-tide surveys over one thousand five hundred square meters of fixed transects.

Collectors built from reused PVC material gathered one thousand one hundred ninety-eight cigarette butts over eighteen months of operation along the boardwalk.

An exchange program that let residents and visitors trade collected waste for sports equipment such as snorkels or balls removed an additional nine hundred forty-one butts plus ninety-seven point nine kilograms of other trash across eight events.

The total number of butts collected directly from the sand declined fifty-three point two percent between twenty twenty-three and twenty twenty-four.

Data went into open repositories including Mendeley Data, and eighty-five point seven percent of participants shared their findings with families and social circles, which prompted the local Cabildo to fold the program into official community governance channels.

The initiative aligns with Sustainable Development Goals four, eleven, twelve, and fourteen, and showed no significant gender differences in outcomes.

Specific intronic splicing events in the STAT2 gene have percent-spliced-in values that correlate with response rates and overall survival in patients with non-small cell lung cancer treated with checkpoint inhibitors, in a paper published in Science.

The discovery reveals a previously unrecognized regulatory layer that influences immunotherapy efficacy.

The work adds a new biomarker dimension to existing prediction models for treatment outcomes.

Scientists at the MRC Laboratory of Medical Sciences engineered glowing biological reporters that keep their accuracy when imaging gene activity in living plants across days and weeks, in work carried by Phys dot org.

The dual-color system separates immune-linked transcriptional responses from background signals.

The approach supports longitudinal observation of plant reactions to disease, pests, and environmental stress without repeated tissue sampling or destructive harvesting.

Single photons absorbed in a superconductor generated individual magnetic flux quanta that researchers at AIST observed directly.

The number of quanta produced provides a direct readout of the photon count.

That observation establishes a new operating principle for photon-number-resolving detectors that does not rely on existing energy-threshold methods.

The same AIST group performed simple chemical modifications on common sugars including sucrose and fructose.

The altered sugars self-assembled into carbonaceous materials that displayed either controlled nanoporosity or ordered layered nanostructures.

The process operates without complex templating agents or high-temperature catalytic steps and opens routes to tailored carbon materials from inexpensive, abundant feedstocks.

Tohoku University researchers identified specific molecular structural motifs that enhance binding affinity and selectivity for per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances, or P fas, in aqueous environments.

The analysis supplies concrete design criteria for synthesizing next-generation adsorbent materials.

Existing adsorbents frequently exhibit limited capacity or poor performance under realistic water-chemistry conditions.

A validated panel of marker genes now tracks differentiation stages of pluripotent stem cells automatically, from systematic work at the IUF–Leibniz Research Institute for Environmental Medicine and Heinrich Heine University Düsseldorf.

The team released a web-based application that applies the panel to new expression datasets for automated classification.

The tool reduces dependence on subjective morphological judgments during the earliest phases of lineage commitment.
