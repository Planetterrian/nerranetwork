### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries

1. **Rising ill health drives increase in young people out of work or education — Phys.org**
   University College London researchers analyzed data from more than 15,000 people ages 16 to 24 in the UK Household Longitudinal Study between 2009 and 2023. The proportion of young people reporting a health problem or disability rose from about 4 percent in 2014–18 to about 9 percent in 2019–23. Psychological distress reports increased from about 18 percent to about 21 percent over the same periods. These prevalence shifts accounted for a greater share of young people not in work, education or training. The likelihood that a person with a health problem would be out of work, education or training remained unchanged between the two periods. Levels of clinical depression rose and fell in step with NEET rates across 2022 and 2023. The study concluded that interventions focused only on the labor market are unlikely to reverse the trend without concurrent investment in mental health prevention and treatment access. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-young-people-largely-driven-ill.html)

2. **Single-cell method tracks CRISPR edits and their gene-expression effects — Phys.org**
   Salk Institute scientists developed Superb-seq to read all on-target and off-target edits plus resulting changes in gene activity at single-cell resolution. The method combines Cas9 editing, a short edit-reporting DNA sequence, and single-cell RNA sequencing. In tests on 34,000 cells from three cell lines, Superb-seq identified 43 edit sites in one group of 10,000 human leukemia cells, 36 of which were off-target, and detected 5,761 edited cells. The approach distinguishes edits likely to be benign from those that disrupt genes protecting against cancer or other disorders. The study was published in Nature Methods on November 16, 2026. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-tool-tracks-unintended-crispr-effects.html)

3. **Vitamin C may reduce cancer-linked chemical formation in the stomach — ScienceDaily**
   A mathematical model indicates that vitamin C taken after meals can lower the formation of potentially cancer-causing chemicals from nitrates and nitrites in food. The model focuses on reactions that occur in the stomach after nitrate- and nitrite-containing meals. Researchers suggest the timing of vitamin C intake could offer a strategy for lowering cancer risk. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261114202236.htm)

4. **Osteoporosis drug prevents spinal mineral buildup in zebrafish model — ScienceDaily**
   An existing osteoporosis drug blocked damaging mineral accumulation in zebrafish spines after researchers identified a genetic link to spinal degeneration. The finding points to a possible route for slowing or preventing a major contributor to chronic back pain. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261113232105.htm)

5. **Wastewater bacteria patterns linked to community health conditions — r/science**
   Analysis of wastewater bacteria from eight Ohio cities serving 1.3 million people revealed associations between gut microbiome patterns and local rates of obesity, high blood pressure, asthma, and depression. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/science/comments/1xwzwvb/wastewater_offers_clues_to_community_health/)

6. **Giant rodent fossils found in southern Thailand mine — Phys.org**
   An international team including McGill University researchers recovered remarkably large rodent fossils from a mine pit in southern Thailand. The fossils, published in Scientific Reports, show that rodent gigantism began earlier and occurred across a wider geographic range than previously recognized. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-giant-prehistoric-rodent-fossils-southern.html)

7. **Mesolithic skeleton records shift to agriculture in Denmark — Phys.org**
   Radiocarbon dating and isotope analysis of a well-preserved skeleton from Mesolithic Denmark show the individual lived through the introduction of agriculture and changed diet accordingly. The remains provide a direct record of one person's adaptation during the Neolithic transition. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-archaeologists-uncover-witness-neolithic-revolution.html)

8. **School segregation shapes parents' education expectations for children — Phys.org**
   In areas with high segregation, the specific school a child attends is closely tied to how many years of education parents expect that child to complete. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-school-environment-plays-key-role.html)

9. **Temperature response after emissions cuts depends on prior warming — Phys.org**
   A study led by the Met Office Hadley Centre found that the zero-emissions commitment temperature change varies with the amount of warming that has already taken place. The response is not uniformly close to zero once emissions stop. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-climate-response-previous-difference.html)

10. **Lab experiments clarify how volcanic flows generate tsunamis — Phys.org**

The work addresses gaps in understanding the complex dynamics that determine whether a volcanic flow will trigger a tsunami and how large any resulting wave may be. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-volcanic-tsunamis.html)

### Science Deep Dive: How prior warming changes the temperature response after emissions stop
Most people assume that once human carbon dioxide emissions cease, global temperatures will stabilize or begin to fall within a short time. In reality the temperature change that follows, known as the zero-emissions commitment, varies with the amount of warming that has already occurred. A study led by the Met Office Hadley Centre examined this relationship across multiple climate models and found that the response is not uniformly close to zero. When substantial warming has already taken place, the committed temperature change can remain positive even after emissions reach net zero. The finding comes from comparing model runs that differ in the level of warming reached before emissions are cut. Listeners can track updates on how observational records align with these model patterns as new temperature and emissions data are released each year.
