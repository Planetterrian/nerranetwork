Human cardiac organoids were the testbed in two studies in Nature Cardiovascular Research on what happens when contraction is briefly switched off.

Transient myosin inhibition with mavacamten or aficamten reactivated the cardiomyocyte cell cycle.

Sarcomere remodeling followed that drop in force, and metabolic shifts through AKT1 and PDK accompanied the remodeling that reopened division programs.

The design paired those human organoids with direct pharmacological control of contraction force.

Something most people get wrong about heart muscle is the idea that cardiomyocytes simply become permanently specialized and old, when adult human cardiomyocytes renew at under one percent per year.

That rate is why muscle lost to infarction is rarely replaced by new cardiomyocytes.

The de-differentiated state reverses within hours once contraction resumes.

Any future regenerative approach will need a temporary window of reduced mechanical load without compromising overall cardiac output.

Helmholtz Institute for RNA-based Infection Research and JMU Würzburg scientists, in a Phys.org report, identified a key RNA-based regulator that lets a common gut bacterium handle nutrient scarcity inside the intestinal mucus layer.

The same work ran in collaboration with Vanderbilt Health and also tracked survival under harsh conditions in that mucus niche.

The regulator controls gene-expression programs used for nutrient scavenging.

It also controls programs for stress resistance and for staying put in this specialized layer.

The findings appear in the current issue of a peer-reviewed microbiology journal.

Common cold viruses activate a distinct signaling route in airway cells that precipitates asthma exacerbations, a path mapped in Science Translational Medicine.

The mechanism was dissected in human samples and in model systems.

Charité and TWINCORE researchers, in a Phys.org summary, found that a virus isolated from West African fruit bats bound and entered human cells in laboratory assays.

That entry happened despite genetic distance from known respiratory syncytial virus, or RSV, strains.

Pre-existing human antibodies neutralized the virus in the same experiments.

University of Hong Kong chemists identified a previously unrecognized cellular structure that sequesters certain nucleic-acid therapeutics inside compartments away from their intended sites of action.

The finding was published in Nature Cell Biology.

DNA insulators, in a Science Advances study from St. Jude covered by News-Medical, altered local gene expression across three hundred twenty-nine mapped lentiviral integration sites.

The insulators also changed chromatin state and three-dimensional genome contacts in ways that lower oncogenic potential.

A machine-learning model trained on those features predicted lower risk for insulated vectors when applied to patient genomic data.

No treatment-linked leukemia has occurred in the X-SCID trial cohort.

Qatar hosts the World Innovation Summit for Health, or WISH, on November twenty-ninth and thirtieth, Times Kuwait notes, under the theme The New Health Span.

Flagship reports will cover longevity science, artificial intelligence integration, fertility, and healthcare financing.

Discussions will examine how advances in cellular biology and epigenetics can extend the period of healthy life.

Those sessions also take up equitable access and ethical questions.

Doctor David Sinclair is scheduled to deliver a keynote on ageing biology.

The meeting’s stated aim is narrowing the gap between lifespan and healthspan.

Southern China produced fossil teeth from two caves, in a Nature paper noted by Phys.org.

One set shows Denisovan affinities.

A second, more primitive lineage has an exact placement that remains unresolved.

The four specimens date to the Middle and Late Pleistocene.

The smartJAN prototype from the University of Deusto combines near-infrared spectroscopy with machine-learning algorithms to identify lipid-transfer-protein allergens.

Those allergens include Pru p 3 in peach and Ara h 9 in peanut.

The system produces a spectral fingerprint in seconds.

Earlier tests also showed detection of gluten and meat bacteria.

The study is published in Food Analytical Methods.

Engineers developed an implantable device that harvests ultrasound energy for operation.

It emits an optical signal to indicate its structural integrity.

The approach removes the need for batteries and allows external inspection of device condition after implantation.

Job satisfaction in small and medium-sized enterprises, in the International Journal of Economics and Business Research, showed a stronger statistical association with perceived work-life balance than with salary level.

Flexible working arrangements were also weaker predictors than that balance measure.

Along the Pacific coast, researchers use drones to locate southern resident orcas.

A trained detection dog locates floating fecal samples for hormone and contaminant analysis.

The method has been applied each fall in recent years and avoids direct animal handling.

A team at the University of Hong Kong and Tsinghua University quantified the maximum elastic deformation between layers in graphite and hexagonal boron nitride before irreversible sliding occurs.

The measurements appear in Nature Communications.
