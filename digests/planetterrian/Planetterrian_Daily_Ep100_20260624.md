### Science Deep Dive: Temperature and Sex in a Warming Ocean
Most people assume that once temperature sets the sex of a fish early in life, the outcome is locked for that individual and its population. In reality, experiments show the initial bias can be offset across generations even when heat stress continues. Right now, as ocean temperatures rise, species like European seabass are already demonstrating this flexibility in controlled multi-year trials. In one study spanning ten years and more than 3,000 fish, the strong male bias seen in the first hot-water generation had reversed by the third, producing more females again. That generational rebound supplies a concrete number worth remembering: three generations were enough for measurable compensation. The practical takeaway is that conservation models should incorporate rapid evolutionary responses rather than treating temperature-sex links as permanent population threats; future monitoring programs will watch whether wild stocks show the same pattern.
