Most people assume mitochondria function only as power plants that produce ATP when needed.

Inside muscle and liver cells the organelles instead monitor nutrient signals and route carbon through pyruvate carriers.

The carriers direct material toward ATP synthesis or toward building new biomass depending on signals such as glucagon levels during fasting.

Under stress the system gives priority to neurons and heart tissue over other functions.

When carrier activity drops cells increase lactate output even while oxygen remains available.

That switch preserves carbon atoms for biosynthetic routes that support growth when energy production is curtailed.
