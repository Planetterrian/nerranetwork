### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Wearable patch tracks brain fluid flow in sleep — [@EricTopol](https://x.com/EricTopol) (X)**
   A new device published in Science Advances measures glymphatic clearance through brain water dynamics rather than motion alone. Current sleep trackers rely on movement algorithms that miss fluid exchange during rest. The patch offers a direct window into clearance processes tied to waste removal overnight. Researchers see it as a step toward objective markers of restorative sleep. Next steps include validation against established imaging methods in larger cohorts.

2. **Fossil shows possible right-handedness 550 million years ago — Phys.org**
   Researchers examined Spriggina floundersi specimens from the Ediacaran Period. The fossils display consistent asymmetry that may represent the earliest known lateral bias in any animal lineage. The finding pushes evidence of behavioral handedness back more than half a billion years. No prior Ediacaran organism had shown comparable directional preference. Further specimens will test whether the pattern holds across the species.

3. **Silicon chip writes DNA sequences using electricity and water — Science Daily**
   Harvard researchers built a chip that assembles dozens of DNA strands simultaneously with electric fields and enzymatic reactions. The method replaces organic solvents used in conventional synthesis with a water-based process. It produces clean output suitable for portable devices and potential data-storage applications. Current output remains limited by chemistry scale-up needs. The team plans refinements to increase sequence length and throughput.

4. **Silica nanoparticles trigger prostate tumor cell death in mice — Science Daily**
   Engineered particles selectively entered prostate cancer cells and activated self-destruction pathways. In mouse models the treatment also boosted immune recognition of the tumors. When paired with checkpoint inhibitors, multiple animals achieved complete remission. The particles exploit cancer-specific surface features for targeting. Follow-up work will examine safety margins and dosing schedules in additional models.

5. **Probe maps Alzheimer's-linked lipids at single-cell resolution — Phys.org**
   University of Osaka scientists developed a stable probe that captures lipid profiles cell by cell within intact tissue. The approach reveals chemical differences between neighboring cells that bulk methods average out. It targets lipids associated with Alzheimer's pathology. The technique maintains precision across repeated measurements. Application to human brain samples is the planned next phase.

6. **Gene therapy for congenital deafness tested in second ear — Nature Medicine**
   The inner ear's immune-privileged status allowed sequential bilateral dosing without strong immune interference. Patients with one form of inherited deafness received the therapy in both ears across separate procedures. Early data show preserved safety and measurable hearing gains. The study adds to evidence that the approach can be repeated. Longer-term auditory and vestibular monitoring continues.

7. **Updated McDonald criteria aim for earlier multiple sclerosis diagnosis — Nature Medicine**
   The 2024 revision incorporates new biomarkers to shorten time to confirmed diagnosis. Earlier identification expands eligibility for disease-modifying therapies. Realizing the criteria's potential requires refined imaging and fluid markers for accurate stratification. Clinical trials will need updated enrollment standards. Biomarker panels are under active evaluation.

8. **Real-world evidence now used to emulate clinical trials — Nature Medicine**
   Regulatory agencies increasingly accept data collected outside traditional randomized studies. These datasets can support approvals and even run prospective analyses in real time. The shift raises questions about endpoint definitions and control populations. Guidance documents are being revised to standardize methods. Several ongoing programs test the framework on existing registries.

9. **Review examines biological aging clocks across health and disease — Nature Medicine**
   The article surveys epigenetic, proteomic, and metabolomic clocks now in use. It outlines how clock readouts track disease risk and healthspan trajectories. Validation against longitudinal cohorts remains a priority. Integration with clinical decision tools is discussed as a future direction. The authors note gaps in clock performance across ancestries.

10. **Meta-analysis summary statistics bias heritability estimates — bioRxiv**
    LDSC regression applied to meta-analyzed Alzheimer's datasets underestimated heritability compared with individual-level data. Imputed variants with high INFO scores also lowered estimates relative to array-genotyped markers alone. The study compared results across multiple LD reference panels. Ancestry mismatch between studies contributed to the downward bias. Authors recommend restricting analyses to directly genotyped variants when possible.

11. **Varroa mites threaten Tasmanian crop pollination — Phys.org**
    A Tasmanian Institute of Agriculture assessment modeled impacts if the parasite reaches the state. Managed hives can receive treatment, but feral colonies lack protection and could collapse. Loss of unmanaged pollinators would reduce services for several crops. Monitoring programs are expanding at entry points. Economic modeling of yield effects is underway.

12. **Economic expectations influence fertility and marriage rates — Phys.org**
    University of Barcelona researchers used Spain's 2004 election surprise to isolate shifts in outlook. Improved expectations correlated with higher birth rates and marriage numbers in the following period. Abortion rates moved in the opposite direction. The design separated economic sentiment from policy changes. Similar natural experiments are proposed for other settings.

13. **PALACE improves phage genome assembly from metagenomes — Nature Biotechnology**
    The tool combines homology detection with deep learning to resolve fragmented viral sequences. It outperforms prior methods on complex community samples. Accurate phage genomes support studies of microbial ecology and potential therapeutics. Benchmarking used both simulated and real metagenomic datasets. Release of the software is planned for wider testing.

14. **Digital twins forecast disease and guide development — Nature Biotechnology**
    Models range from virtual patients to simulated organs-on-chips. Validation standards and regulatory pathways are still forming. Early applications focus on dosing predictions and toxicity screening. Cross-disciplinary teams are defining data requirements for credible simulations. Pilot projects continue in oncology and metabolic disease.

15. **Goblin shark filmed alive in natural habitat for first time — Science Daily**
    Researchers captured footage at previously undocumented depths and locations. The sightings extend the known range of the 125-million-year-old species. Behavior observed includes jaw protrusion mechanics not fully documented before. The data refine understanding of deep-sea predator ecology. Additional surveys are scheduled in under-sampled basins.

### Science Deep Dive: How DNA synthesis actually works
Most people picture DNA manufacturing as a purely chemical process that requires toxic solvents and complex purification. In reality, enzymes already perform precise assembly inside every living cell using only water and nucleotide building blocks. Right now your own cells are adding nucleotides at rates up to 1,000 per second during replication. The new silicon chip simply recreates a controlled version of that enzymatic step on a surface. One memorable detail: conventional synthesis still generates roughly 100 times more waste solvent than the amount of DNA produced. The practical takeaway is to watch for future devices that could let researchers or clinicians print short DNA sequences at the bench rather than ordering from distant facilities.
