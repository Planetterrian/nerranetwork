### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Epstein-Barr virus may protect against Type 1 diabetes** — Nature Reviews
   A review in Nature Reviews Endocrinology outlines how Epstein-Barr virus, already linked to higher multiple sclerosis risk, appears to confer protection against autoimmune Type 1 diabetes. The opposite directional effect on two autoimmune conditions points to virus-specific modulation of immune tolerance. Researchers note the established multiple sclerosis association provides a useful comparator for dissecting mechanisms. Further human cohort studies are needed to confirm the protective association and identify involved pathways.

2. **Nanobubbles clean water pollutants including algae** — Phys.org
   Nanobubbles roughly 100 nanometers across generate reactive species that break down oil, algae, and other contaminants without leaving chemical residues. Wen Zhang’s team at New Jersey Institute of Technology is testing the approach in field-scale water treatment systems. The bubbles remain suspended for long periods and deliver oxidative power directly at pollutant interfaces. Next steps include scaling generation methods for municipal and agricultural use.

3. **Chinese import competition raises global CO₂ emissions** — Phys.org
   University of Copenhagen economists tracked Danish firms facing low-cost Chinese imports and found that while offshoring reduced local emissions, overall global emissions increased. The net rise occurred because production shifted to regions with higher carbon intensity per unit output. The study used firm-level data matched to trade and emissions records. Policymakers are examining whether carbon border adjustments could alter this pattern.

4. **Soil bacteria adapt when phosphorus runs low** — Phys.org
   Caltech researchers showed that stressed soil bacteria remodel gene expression and metabolism to cope with phosphorus scarcity, altering interactions with plant roots. The changes affect nutrient cycling and plant growth under nutrient-limited conditions. Experiments combined controlled microcosms with transcriptomic profiling of bacterial communities. The findings matter for predicting soil responses to changing climate and fertilizer use.

5. **Prion proteins contain candidate antibiotics** — Phys.org
   University of Pennsylvania scientists used AI to scan prion and prion-like sequences and identified short “prionin” peptides with antibacterial activity against resistant strains. The peptides appear embedded within proteins best known for causing neurodegeneration. Structural modeling suggests the antimicrobial segments are normally masked until proteolytic release. Follow-up work will test efficacy and safety in animal infection models.

6. **15-atom iridium clusters outperform commercial catalysts** — Phys.org
   An international team from Tohoku University, Tokyo University of Science, Vanderbilt, and Adelaide developed an ambient-air method to synthesize stable 15-atom iridium nanoclusters. The clusters delivered 1.5 times the mass activity of standard iridium catalysts and ran without degradation beyond 20 hours. Precise size control was achieved without high-temperature or vacuum equipment. Researchers aim to extend the approach to other precious-metal catalysts.

7. **Distant galaxy cluster shows unexpected maturity** — Phys.org
   JWST observations led by IPAC at Caltech revealed a massive galaxy cluster acting as a strong gravitational lens at a redshift where such structures were not expected to be fully assembled. Three papers detail the cluster’s concentration and lensing strength. The data challenge current models of structure formation during cosmic noon. Additional JWST pointings are planned to map member galaxies and dark-matter distribution.

8. **Arabidopsis meristem mapped to 18 cell clusters** — Phys.org
   Researchers generated a high-resolution atlas of the inflorescence meristem in Arabidopsis, identifying 18 transcriptionally distinct cell populations that give rise to stems and flowers. Single-cell RNA sequencing traced lineage trajectories from stem-cell niches outward. The map clarifies how positional information is translated into organ identity. The dataset will serve as a reference for crop species with similar meristem organization.

9. **Twisted boron nitride layers control quantum light** — Phys.org
   University of Technology Sydney physicists demonstrated that rotating atomically thin hexagonal boron nitride flakes lets them tune the emission properties of embedded quantum emitters. The twist angle alters local strain and dielectric environment, shifting photon energy and coherence. Experiments combined optical spectroscopy with precise mechanical rotation stages. The method offers a route to integrable quantum light sources for photonic circuits.

10. **H5N1 hid in cattle udders rather than lungs** — Phys.org
    Early U.S. cattle infections with H5N1 produced severe udder disease while largely sparing the respiratory tract, delaying recognition that the virus had jumped species. Diagnostic sampling focused on milk rather than nasal swabs proved more sensitive. The altered tissue tropism differs from patterns seen in other mammals. Surveillance programs have since adjusted sampling protocols accordingly.

11. **Soil fungi underpin carbon storage and crop stability** — Phys.org
    Freie Universität Berlin analysis emphasizes that fungal networks move carbon through soil food webs and stabilize plant communities under stress. Mycorrhizal and saprotrophic guilds together influence both agricultural yields and long-term carbon sequestration. The review synthesizes field and laboratory evidence across ecosystems. Future work will quantify how management practices alter fungal contributions to soil carbon.

12. **Cytokinins show activity in mammalian cells** — Phys.org
    Until recently considered plant-specific, cytokinins are now being examined for roles in human metabolism and stress responses. Early cell-culture experiments indicate these compounds can modulate signaling pathways shared with plant systems. Researchers are mapping endogenous cytokinin levels in human tissues. Expanded studies will test whether dietary or microbial sources influence mammalian physiology.

13. **Root microbes help maize and sorghum tolerate heat** — Phys.org
    Rhizosphere microbiome surveys suggest certain bacterial and fungal taxa correlate with improved crop performance under elevated temperatures. Inoculation trials are testing whether selected consortia can be transplanted to boost resilience. The work focuses on microbes that influence water uptake and hormone signaling in roots. Field validation across multiple growing seasons is underway.

14. **Excessive Instagram use alters body ownership perception** — Phys.org
    Psychological experiments link heavy platform use to weakened sense of body ownership, with participants reporting reduced alignment between visual appearance and felt bodily self. The effect appears tied to repeated exposure to edited images rather than simple screen time. Researchers measured proprioceptive drift and explicit ownership ratings after controlled viewing sessions. Longitudinal studies are planned to assess reversibility.

15. **Young adults show declining knowledge of local species** — Phys.org
    A survey of 463 German adults found that familiarity with plants and birds typical of agricultural landscapes drops sharply in younger cohorts compared with older respondents. Common species such as dandelions remain widely recognized, while meadow specialists do not. The pattern points to reduced direct contact with farmland biodiversity. Educational programs targeting regional species identification are being developed.

The finding reframes prions as potential reservoirs of innate defense molecules rather than solely pathogenic entities. It opens a route to mine neurodegeneration-linked proteins for new antibiotic scaffolds. Next, researchers will synthesize and test the most promising prionins in live infection models. ### Science Deep Dive: Cytokinins Beyond Plants
You've probably heard that hormones such as cytokinins regulate growth only in plants. In reality, these same molecules are now detected in mammalian cells and appear to influence stress signaling and metabolism. Right now, as your cells manage daily oxidative and nutrient stress, trace cytokinins from diet or resident microbes may be modulating the same pathways plants use to slow aging under drought. A single cytokinin molecule can alter gene expression across dozens of targets, illustrating how a small concentration produces outsized effects. The next time you drink green tea or eat certain vegetables, consider that you are also ingesting compounds once thought irrelevant to human biology. Watch for upcoming studies that measure cytokinin levels in human blood and link them to measurable changes in inflammatory markers.
