Most people assume the body simply sweats to cool down when temperatures rise.

In reality, right now your skin blood vessels are widening to release heat while sweat glands activate in coordinated waves across different body regions.

A single square meter of skin can evaporate roughly one liter of sweat per hour under peak conditions, moving heat away far more efficiently than radiation alone.

The process begins in the hypothalamus, which compares core temperature against a set point and sends signals through the autonomic nervous system.

When humidity blocks evaporation, the same system ramps up heart rate to push more blood toward the skin surface.
