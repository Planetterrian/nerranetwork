Researchers at the Rowett Institute at the University of Aberdeen tested an adeno-associated virus, or AAV, carrying leptin instructions in mice with generalised lipodystrophy. Hunger fell as well, on top of the liver and diabetes changes.

The disorder limits the body's ability to store fat and brings a rare form of diabetes, fatty liver disease, persistent hunger, and chronic pain. Today's treatment means daily leptin shots for life, painful when there is little subcutaneous fat to inject into, expensive, and dependent on refrigeration.

Doctor Nadine Sommer, who led the work, noted the effects match daily leptin shots in patients. Lower doses are now under test before any human translation, which would require a different AAV vector.

Support came from the East of Scotland Bioscience Doctoral Training Partnership, BBSRC, and Diabetes UK, and Lipodystrophy UK and Lipodystrophy United welcomed the result as a path to a lighter treatment load.

Epstein-Barr virus infects about ninety percent of people, and a PNAS experimental model shows that specific immune-system gene variants determine why only a small fraction of those infections progress to multiple sclerosis.

The model identifies how those variants allow the virus to trigger autoimmune damage rather than remaining harmless. It is the immune-gene background, not infection alone, that lets Epstein-Barr start that damage in susceptible people.

Insect-foraging finches in forested Galápagos areas, in a Phys.org report, carry genetic mosaics that help account for the range of beak forms suited to different food sources. The team studied those forest birds rather than the more-studied seed-eating ground finches.

Paclitaxel, a widely used cancer drug, had its biosynthetic steps mapped so production might move from slow yew trees to faster-growing tobacco. The work targets cheaper, more scalable manufacturing as cancer diagnoses rise globally.

Damaged mitochondria in bone received engineered nanoparticles that restored function and improved regeneration in diabetic rat models, where standard healing is impaired by the disease. The particles go at cellular energy deficits directly rather than at downstream symptoms.

Ben-Gurion University and Volcani Institute researchers showed that prickly pear cactus activates two distinct physiological modes, one to survive long droughts and one to exploit brief rains. The dual strategy lets the plant take carbon efficiently while water is present and conserve resources when it is not.

Disrupting one gene in petunias altered both pigment pathways and the blend of volatile scent compounds. Some vanilla- and clove-associated volatiles increased, which shows the same cellular machinery shapes petal color and scent together.

Institute of Science and Technology Austria scientists imaged the internal structure of poxvirus cores. Gene-copying enzymes sit in dormant pairs beside export portals in the shell, ready to transcribe viral genes once the virus enters a host cell.

Fundamental fluid equations paired with machine learning at the University of California, Irvine reconstructed complete flow fields from sparse or noisy measurements. The method improves accuracy for medical imaging of blood movement where sensors capture only partial data.

Patients undergoing coronary angiography with or without percutaneous coronary intervention, or PCI, entered a randomized trial highlighted by Eric Topol that assigned one hundred people to music before and during the procedure. The music group showed a thirteen millimetre lower post-procedure systolic blood pressure and a reduced heart rate.

Something most people get wrong is that a violin physically improves with use, as if regular playing loosens wood fibers or alters resonances so the instrument literally opens up over months or years.

A CNRS researcher ran a controlled six-month trial of that belief with a new violin played daily by a professional soloist. At the end, the player reported the instrument felt easier to play and its tone had improved.

Repeated physical and acoustic measurements found no measurable change in the wood, varnish, or vibrational properties that could explain the difference. The gap between what the player heard and what the instrument did points to adaptation in the player's own touch, bow pressure, and auditory expectation.

Claims of breaking in an instrument therefore rest on subjective experience rather than detectable material shifts. Future work could test whether longer periods or different playing styles produce measurable effects, or whether listener panels blind to playing history reach the same judgments, and players might focus practice time on technique rather than expecting the wood itself to change.

Heriot-Watt University researchers, in a Phys.org report, interviewed Scottish food producers and wholesalers and ran six case studies that ranged from family-run producers to island wholesalers.

Small and medium-sized enterprises, or SMEs, make up around ninety-five percent of Scotland's food and drink sector, and the study found a mismatch between available grants and the upfront investment those small firms need to decarbonise their cold chains.

In brief, researchers from Australia and the United States, in a BiotechDispatch report, are pooling expertise to develop therapies for several rare pediatric genetic disorders.

An experimental gene therapy, reported by La Voz de Madrid, showed measurable auditory gains after treatment in children with profound congenital deafness.
