### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Fungal networks mapped across global soils for first time — Science Daily**
   Researchers compiled data to estimate the total length of mycorrhizal fungal filaments at 110 quadrillion kilometers. The networks move roughly 4 billion tons of carbon dioxide into soils annually. They support plant growth and influence climate regulation through carbon storage. The mapping provides a baseline for tracking how these systems respond to environmental change. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/06/260614011845.htm)

2. **Honey bees follow individual flight routes with centimeter precision — Science Daily**
   Drone tracking of wild honey bees showed each insect maintains its own consistent path between foraging sites. Some routes repeated so exactly that bees passed within centimeters of prior positions. Trees and other landmarks stabilized the paths, while open fields like corn increased variation. The findings highlight how insects use visual cues for navigation in natural settings.

3. **Complete wiring diagram published for adult fruit fly nervous system — Phys.org**
   An international team produced the first full connectome of all neuron connections in the central nervous system of an adult fruit fly. The map covers both brain and ventral nerve cord regions. Multiple labs at Harvard Medical School and Princeton University led the effort. This resource enables direct testing of how specific circuits control behavior. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-publish-connectome-fruit-fly-brain.html)

4. **Blood proteins reveal cell-type-specific aging patterns linked to disease — Nature Medicine**
   Plasma proteomic analysis assessed biological age across more than 40 cell types. Accelerated aging in particular cell populations correlated with higher risk for specific diseases. The signatures also identified individuals showing slower aging in certain cell types. This approach offers a way to measure heterogeneous aging rates from a single blood sample. Source: [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-026-04446-y)

5. **Plasma markers track cellular aging and predict future health outcomes — Nature Medicine**
   Researchers identified proteomic signatures that reflect the biological age of individual cell types. These markers forecasted disease risk and resilience across multiple conditions. The study linked distinct aging profiles in different cell populations to later clinical events. The work expands aging assessment beyond whole-organism measures.

6. **Adaptive deep brain stimulation reduces falls in Parkinson’s patients — Nature Medicine**
   A randomized crossover trial tested gait-phase-synchronized stimulation against continuous deep brain stimulation. The adaptive approach proved feasible and safe while lowering fall rates. Neural decoding drew on principles of locomotor encoding to adjust stimulation in real time. Participants showed improved gait control during daily activities.

7. **Genetic screen in worms identifies protectors against alpha-synuclein toxicity — bioRxiv**
   A forward genetic screen in Caenorhabditis elegans atfs-1 mutants uncovered genes that limit dopaminergic neuron loss from alpha-synuclein overexpression. Loss of jmjd-1.2, jmjd-3.1, or twk-14 increased neurodegeneration in this background. The jmjd genes encode H3K27me3 demethylases, while twk-14 encodes a potassium channel ortholog. The results point to chromatin and membrane potential pathways that normally buffer protein toxicity. Source: [biorxiv.org](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.11.731199v1?rss=1)

8. **Imaging platform tracks epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition in stem cells — Nature Methods**
   A quantitative imaging system captured cellular dynamics during EMT in human induced pluripotent stem cells. Progression differed between 2D and 3D culture formats and tied to basement membrane integrity. The platform integrates multiple readouts to follow state transitions in real time. It supports systematic comparison of EMT regulators across geometries.

9. **Hi-C foundation model enables cross-species chromatin analysis — Nature Methods**
   HiCFoundation was pretrained on a large collection of Hi-C datasets for 3D genome studies. The model supports single-cell and multiomics applications across species. It captures chromatin architecture features that generalize beyond the training data. Researchers can apply it to new datasets for epigenomic interpretation.

10. **Iron Age skeleton shows careful reassembly after brain removal — Nature**
    Archaeologists examined a female skeleton whose brain had been extracted after death. The remaining bones were arranged deliberately for burial. Some bones had been shaped into tools. The find illustrates specific mortuary practices in the period.

11. **Plant-stem nesting bees face higher heat risk than ground nesters — Phys.org**
    Australian researchers compared thermal tolerance across bee nesting strategies. Stem-nesting species showed greater vulnerability to rising temperatures in the short term. Ground-nesting bees can shift depth to avoid extreme heat. The difference arises from the limited thermal buffering in plant stems. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-bees-tiny-homes-crisis-harder.html)

12. **Warming shifts frost weathering patterns and rockfall risk in Spain — Phys.org**
    Climate models and field data show frost-driven rock breakdown moving to higher elevations. Lower elevations experience fewer freeze-thaw cycles as temperatures rise. The redistribution alters where and when rockfalls are most likely. The study maps changing hazards for mountain landscapes. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-climate-reshapes-spain-rockfall-frost.html)

13. **Self-sacrifice viewed as more heroic across U.S. respondents — Phys.org**
    Kelley School of Business experiments found consistent elevation of self-sacrificial acts in judgments of heroism. Participants rated sacrificial actions higher even when outcomes matched non-sacrificial alternatives. The pattern held across different scenarios and demographics. The work examines how observers weigh intent versus result. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-explores-sacrifice-judgments.html)

14. **Mineral removal improves whey protein texture and reduces bitterness — Science Daily**
    Researchers traced off-flavors in concentrated whey to residual minerals. A new processing step removes these minerals while preserving the smoother mouthfeel from prior concentration methods. The approach targets the source of bitterness without compromising functional properties. It aims to enhance consumer acceptance of protein beverages.

15. **First plain-language summary published for veterinary research — Phys.org**
    Taylor & Francis released the initial Plain Language Summary of Publication in a veterinary journal. The format translates technical findings into accessible language for broader audiences. It targets animal health practitioners and interested non-specialists. The initiative extends open-access practices already used in human medicine. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-plain-language-summary-veterinary-science.html)

### Science Deep Dive: Underground Fungal Networks
Most people picture soil as a mix of minerals, water, and plant roots with only scattered microbes. In reality, mycorrhizal fungi form continuous living filaments that link plants across entire landscapes. Right now, as you stand or sit, these threads are exchanging nutrients and carbon between roots that may be meters apart. One memorable scale: the total length reaches 110 quadrillion kilometers worldwide. The networks move 4 billion tons of carbon dioxide into soils each year, a flux comparable to major geological processes. Next time you walk across grass or forest floor, remember the hidden connections beneath are actively redistributing resources and storing carbon. Watch for field studies that measure how drought or warming alters the density of these filaments in different ecosystems.
