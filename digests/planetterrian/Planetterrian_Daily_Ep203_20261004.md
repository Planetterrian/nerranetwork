### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Ancient Herb Polysaccharide Slows Aging via Gut and Brain — Bioengineer.org**
   Researchers examined a polysaccharide extracted from an ancient Chinese herb and its effects on aging markers. The compound altered gut microbial communities in ways that influenced neural pathways linked to aging processes. Experiments tracked changes in both intestinal bacteria composition and downstream brain signaling. The work highlights interconnected routes between microbiome activity and central nervous system responses during aging. Source: [bioengineer.org](https://bioengineer.org/ancient-chinese-herb-polysaccharide-slows-aging-through-gut-and-brain-pathways/)

2. **Vagus Nerve Stimulation Shows Immune Modulation Progress — Eric Topol Substack**
   Kevin Tracey reviewed advances in vagal neuromodulation and its effects on inflammatory pathways. The approach targets the vagus nerve to alter cytokine release and immune cell activity. Clinical observations indicate reduced systemic inflammation in targeted conditions. The review details device refinements that improve precision of nerve activation. Source: [erictopol.substack.com](https://erictopol.substack.com/p/vagus-nerve-stimulation-and-the-immune)

3. **Group Attachment Security Boosts Dissent Expression in Teams — Phys.org**
   A KAIST team led by professors Sujin Lee and Seungwon Jeong tested group attachment security in decision scenarios. Participants primed with attachment security produced more detailed and persuasive minority opinions. Majority members showed greater willingness to incorporate those views when attachment security was present. Two experiments used hypothetical decision tasks to measure both expression and reception of dissent. The findings appear in Scientific Reports. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-group-dissenters-stronger-arguments.html)

4. **Emissions Cuts May Limit Antarctic Ice Loss This Century — Phys.org**
   An international team including Rutgers scientist Robert Kopp modeled Antarctic ice response under different emission pathways. The analysis gave at least an 89 percent probability that Paris Agreement low-emission targets would reduce ice loss by 2100 compared with high-emission scenarios. Even under ambitious cuts, the probability of net ice loss remained at least 92 percent. Higher emissions risk triggering faster ice flow through weakened ice shelves. The study was published in Nature Geoscience. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-today-emissions-choices-limit-antarctic.html)

5. **Study Identifies Factors Aiding Recovery From Intimate Partner Violence — Phys.org**
   Researchers examined recovery trajectories among people experiencing intimate partner violence in the Netherlands. More than one million individuals aged sixteen and older encounter domestic violence annually. The work focused on elements that support psychological and physical recovery after partner-perpetrated abuse. Data collection addressed both intimidation and physical harm patterns. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-recovery-intimate-partner-violence-reveals.html)

6. **Rigorous Tests Find Visible Light Does Not Accelerate Water Evaporation — Phys.org**
   Experiments with hydrogels and water droplets tested claims that visible light increases evaporation rates without added heat. Direct measurements showed no measurable acceleration attributable to light exposure alone. Earlier proposals lacked confirmation that light acts directly on water molecules. The results clarify that temperature remains the dominant driver. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-visible-evaporation-rigorous.html)

7. **Drone and Seafloor Cameras Improve Coral Reef Monitoring — Phys.org**
   University of Miami researchers deployed drones above waves and cameras near the seafloor to track reef changes. The combined approach captures structural shifts that were previously costly or slow to document. Repeated imaging allows quantification of coral cover and habitat complexity over time. The methods reduce reliance on labor-intensive diver surveys. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-imaging-technologies-clearer-view-coral.html)

8. **Squid Body Surface Holds Hundreds of Additional Hair Cells — Phys.org**
   Case Western Reserve University scientists mapped hair cells across the entire squid body surface. Prior knowledge placed these mechanosensory cells only on the head and arms. The expanded distribution may offer a model for studying hair cell function and loss in humans. The cells resemble those in the mammalian inner ear. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-newly-hair-cells-squid-clues.html)

9. **Leucine Supports Cellular Energy Production Beyond Muscle Building — ScienceDaily**
   Research examined leucine's role in mitochondrial function within cells. The amino acid participates in pathways that maintain energy output in addition to its known effects on protein synthesis. Experiments isolated leucine's contribution to metabolic activity separate from general protein intake. The findings expand understanding of branched-chain amino acid actions at the cellular level. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261001095319.htm)

10. **Chang’e-6 Samples Reveal New Magnetic Mineral on Moon — ScienceDaily**
    Analysis of lunar soil returned by Chang’e-6 identified nanoscale metallic iron in the gamma phase inside impact-glass particles. This form of iron can retain stable magnetic records over long periods. The discovery provides a potential archive of the Moon’s ancient magnetic field. The mineral had not been documented previously in natural lunar material. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261001214015.htm)

### Planetterrian Spotlight
The KAIST experiments on group attachment security provide measurable conditions under which minority opinions receive fuller expression and consideration. In the first experiment, participants who received relational support within their assigned group produced longer, more structured dissenting arguments. The second experiment showed that majority members were more likely to integrate those arguments into final decisions when attachment security cues were present. These patterns emerged in controlled hypothetical scenarios rather than real organizational settings. The results point to a psychological lever that organizations could test through structured feedback protocols. What practical adjustments in team environments might shift attachment security levels enough to change decision quality?

### Science Deep Dive: How Visible Light Interacts With Evaporating Water
Most people assume visible light can speed up water evaporation on its own, even without raising temperature. Rigorous tests with hydrogels and isolated droplets show this does not occur. Right now, as moisture leaves a surface or your skin after washing, the rate depends on heat transfer and humidity gradients rather than photons in the visible range. Earlier proposals lacked direct confirmation that light itself alters molecular escape from the liquid phase. The experiments recorded evaporation under controlled illumination and found no statistically detectable increase attributable to light alone. A memorable detail is that any apparent effect in prior work traced back to unintended heating rather than a distinct light-driven mechanism. When evaluating claims about light and evaporation in future reports, check whether temperature was held constant and measured at the liquid surface.
