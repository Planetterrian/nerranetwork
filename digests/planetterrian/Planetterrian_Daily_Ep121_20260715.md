### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Biodiversity lifts grassland output most under extreme drought — Phys.org**
   A global synthesis of 75 biodiversity experiments found that drier grasslands gain the greatest productivity benefit from higher species diversity when extreme drought hits. Forests showed no comparable context-dependent pattern. Researchers at Yokohama National University published the results in Nature Ecology & Evolution. The pattern indicates diversity stabilizes biomass production specifically where water stress is already high. Follow-up work will test whether the same relationship holds under combined heat and drought.

2. **Cultural participation linked to younger biological age — ScienceDaily**
   Older adults who regularly attended cinema, museums, or concerts showed body-function measures equivalent to people roughly three years younger. Stronger social connections, improved mental health, and healthier behaviours are proposed as linking mechanisms. The observational study could not establish direct causation. Researchers call for trials that test whether increasing cultural engagement slows functional decline.

3. **Tadpole immune priming explains amphibian fungus survival — ScienceDaily**
   Survivors of the chytrid fungus develop robust immune defences while still tadpoles, before metamorphosis exposes them to the pathogen. The work also catalogued a large set of previously unknown antimicrobial peptides. University College London, ZSL and Imperial College London led the study. The peptides may offer templates for new anti-infective compounds. Next steps include testing whether early-life immune training can be replicated in captive breeding programs.

4. **Nitrate in drinking water tied to preterm birth risk — Phys.org**
   Analysis of 735,831 singleton births in New Zealand linked higher nitrate concentrations at maternal residence to elevated preterm delivery, even below current regulatory limits. The Environmental Research study covered births from 2008 to 2021. No safe threshold was identified within the observed range. Public-health follow-up will examine whether treatment upgrades reduce the association.

5. **Chimpanzees prefer crystals over ordinary stones — ScienceDaily**
   Captive chimpanzees consistently chose crystals and examined them with prolonged attention, suggesting an innate attraction to the same visual features that drew early humans. The behaviour appeared before any practical use for the objects. Researchers propose the preference predates tool-making and may reflect sensory bias. Further tests will compare responses across primate species.

6. **Lower CO2 improves microbial biodegradable-plastic yield — Phys.org**
   Hydrogen-oxidizing bacteria produced more poly[(R)-3-hydroxybutyrate] when carbon dioxide was reduced under non-flammable gas conditions. The process enables safer, more efficient gas fermentation for the bioplastic. The study demonstrates a route to couple carbon recycling with lower energy input. Scale-up trials are planned to confirm industrial viability.

7. **Chewing gum after beetroot raises nitrite and lowers blood pressure — ScienceDaily**
   Participants who chewed sugary gum after nitrate-rich vegetables or beetroot juice generated more nitrite and showed a transient drop in blood pressure. The effect stems from enhanced oral nitrate reduction. Researchers aim to develop sugar-free alternatives that preserve the benefit. Larger trials will test sustained cardiovascular outcomes.

8. **New stacking method yields cleaner 2D material heterostructures — Phys.org**
   Southampton and Singapore researchers developed an electric-field and water-based assembly technique that avoids solvent residues in atomically thin stacks. The ultra-clean interfaces are intended for quantum devices and advanced electronics. The approach bypasses conventional transfer steps that introduce defects. Prototype quantum sensors are the first targeted application.

9. **Ocean acidification now functions as a planetary-scale signal — Phys.org**
   Current carbon emissions are altering ocean chemistry at a rate that connects present-day outputs to deep-time geological records. The signal integrates atmospheric CO2 with long-term carbon-cycle memory. Researchers argue this framing expands the variable beyond local ecosystem impacts. Monitoring networks will track whether the signal strengthens predictably with cumulative emissions.

10. **Lunar regolith thickness mapped for exploration planning — Phys.org**
    Brown University scientists produced new thickness estimates for the Moon’s loose surface layer using orbital and sample data. The maps refine landing-site selection and resource-utilization models. Thinner regolith zones may expose more bedrock for construction. Additional orbital datasets will refine the global model.

11. **Agriwilding combines crop yields with biodiversity gains — Phys.org**
    Field trials by Vrije Universiteit Brussel and UCLouvain demonstrated that nature-inclusive farming practices can maintain food production while restoring species diversity. The Biological Conservation study quantified both yield and ecological metrics. Soil health and pollinator abundance improved without external inputs. Larger farm networks will test scalability across European regions.

12. **Ear-worn device stimulates clotting to control bleeding — Fierce Biotech**
    A wearable that activates the vagus nerve increases blood-clot formation and may offer a non-invasive option for life-threatening hemorrhage. Preclinical data support further device optimization. Human safety studies are the immediate next step. The approach could complement existing tourniquet and pharmacologic methods.

13. **Lawnmower-like enzyme reverses molecular aging markers — r/longevity**
    The enzyme removes accumulated damage from long-lived proteins in human tissue samples. Treated cells showed reversal of molecular aging signatures spanning decades. The work remains at the in-vitro stage. In-vivo delivery and safety studies are required before any therapeutic consideration.

14. **Brain-computer interface training improves cognition in neurological disease — Fierce Biotech**
    Patients with Parkinson’s, multiple sclerosis, dementia or traumatic brain injury showed cognitive gains after sessions that fed back real-time brain electrical activity. The study measured performance on standardized neuropsychological tests. Larger controlled trials will determine durability of the effect. Device refinement focuses on wearable form factors.

15. **Generative AI use prompts questions about skill retention — Phys.org**
    Widely available chatbots now handle writing, coding and planning tasks, raising concern that reduced practice could erode human capabilities. No longitudinal data yet quantify the effect. Cognitive scientists are designing studies that track skill trajectories with and without AI assistance. Early findings will inform guidelines for education and professional training.

Because exposure occurs through everyday residential supply, the finding directly implicates municipal treatment infrastructure. Upgrading nitrate removal in affected regions offers a concrete, population-level intervention. Regulators will now weigh whether to lower the permitted threshold. ### Science Deep Dive: Biodiversity Under Stress
Most people assume biodiversity mainly preserves rare species for their own sake. In reality, diverse plant communities buffer ecosystem productivity most strongly when conditions turn harsh. Right now, as grasslands experience longer dry spells, the extra species present convert limited water into biomass more reliably than monocultures. One memorable number: the synthesis drew on 75 separate experiments spanning multiple continents. The practical takeaway is to favour mixed plantings in restoration projects or farm margins rather than single-crop or single-grass solutions; those mixtures are the ones most likely to maintain output when drought arrives. Future reports on drought-resilient agriculture will show whether this pattern scales to working landscapes.
