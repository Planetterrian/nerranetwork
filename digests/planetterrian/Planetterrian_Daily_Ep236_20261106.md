### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Greater estrogen exposure tied to less brain vessel damage in women — Neurology**
   A study published in Neurology examined lifetime estrogen exposure in women. Greater endogenous and exogenous exposure correlated with lower cerebrovascular pathology. Among APOE-e4 carriers, it also associated with greater cortical thickness in regions vulnerable to Alzheimer’s disease. The work drew on data linking reproductive history and hormone use to brain imaging markers. Source: [neurology.org](https://www.neurology.org/doi/pdf/10.1212/WNL.0000000000218706)

2. **Cancer-linked gene uses shortcut to start cell division — Phys.org**
   Researchers identified a mechanism where a gene tied to cancer bypasses normal checkpoints to trigger replication. Unregulated division from this shortcut can promote tumor growth when controls fail. The finding clarifies how cells normally restrict division to injury repair or tissue development. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-cancer-linked-gene-shortcut-trigger.html)

3. **TACO1 protein helps mitochondria finish tricky protein sequences — Phys.org**
   Karolinska Institutet researchers showed that TACO1 assists mitochondrial ribosomes when translation stalls on difficult amino acid stretches. The study, published in Nature Communications, details how this protein prevents incomplete chains in energy-producing organelles. Disruption of the process appears in certain mitochondrial diseases. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-mitochondria-protein-production.html)

4. **Placenta-on-a-chip tracks toxin effects on pregnancy — Phys.org**
   Engineers built a microfluidic model that exposes placental tissue to metals such as cadmium. The platform lets researchers measure changes in barrier function and cell signaling without direct human testing. Cadmium exposure has previously been linked to miscarriage and low birth weight. Exposure to toxic metals like cadmium has been associated with adverse pregnancy outcomes such as miscarriage, preterm birth and low birth weight. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-placenta-chip-effects-environmental-toxins.html)

5. **Antibiotic runoff may drive honeybee colony losses — Phys.org**
   A Journal of the American Veterinary Medical Association article argues that agricultural antibiotic pollution represents an overlooked threat to pollinators. Bees encounter residues that can alter gut bacteria and immunity. The authors note this factor has received less attention than pesticides or pathogens. High losses of honeybee colonies have caused concern around the world. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-antibiotic-pollution-key-factor-honeybee.html)

### Science Deep Dive: How river barriers shape fish migration success
Most people believe that simply installing fish passes at dams and weirs will let migrating fish reach their spawning grounds without further effort. Research from Wageningen University shows that these structures often fail when maintenance lapses or water flows are not coordinated across multiple sites. The fish moving through streams near you depend on consistent water levels and clear passages that change with daily management decisions by operators. Ph.D. research by Panos Panagiotopoulos tracked migration outcomes across Dutch waterways and found that isolated fixes left many fish unable to complete their routes. Maintenance crews must regularly clear debris and adjust gate settings so that the passes remain functional during peak migration periods. When water management plans treat the entire river system as one network rather than separate barriers, passage rates improve measurably. Future monitoring that combines fish counters with real-time flow data could identify which specific adjustments produce the largest gains in successful migrations.
