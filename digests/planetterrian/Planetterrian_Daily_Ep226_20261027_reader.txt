A record-breaking hot drought from two thousand eighteen to two thousand nineteen caused widespread plant mortality in Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park, according to work covered by Phys.org.

Researchers surveyed nine species over a mild spring and an extreme summer, measuring leaf traits, water content, and carbon-nitrogen ratios.

Anangu traditional owners co-developed the research aims, selected the species, conducted the fieldwork, and interpreted the results with plant physiologists.

Wanari, tjanpi, and kurkara were among the culturally significant plants chosen because they supply food, habitat, and tool materials.

Anangu knowledge of Country, fire, and landscape change was woven directly into the Western plant-physiology framework throughout the project.

Something most people get wrong is the idea that desert plants have foolproof defenses against heat and drought simply because they live in arid zones.

Plants that keep growing during dry spells maintain carbon uptake but risk running out of water when conditions intensify.

Plants that shut down growth conserve water, yet they can exhaust stored reserves if the drought lasts too long.

The team found that maintaining stable water levels inside leaves was the single best predictor of survival after comparing multiple physiological traits, including carbon-nitrogen ratios and overall leaf morphology.

Leaf relative water content emerged as that strongest statistical predictor.

Plants that experienced large reductions in leaf water content suffered the most severe dieback, regardless of their primary drought strategy.

Species that lost this internal water balance suffered the highest dieback once temperatures and water deficits reached the levels recorded in two thousand eighteen to two thousand nineteen.

Neither drought-tolerance nor drought-avoidance conferred reliable protection at those combined extremes.

One practical step that follows is supporting community nurseries that propagate the more resilient drought-avoiding species, while also advancing cultural burning practices that reduce fuel loads and competition.

Future work will test whether restoring traditional fire regimes can reduce competition and improve water availability for those drought-avoiding species identified in the survey.

Continued monitoring will show whether these local actions can buffer the next extreme drought, or whether only larger reductions in global emissions will keep the current range of desert plants viable.

The researchers note that sustained reductions in anthropogenic climate forcing will ultimately determine whether these desert ecosystems retain their current diversity of keystone plants.

University of Queensland researchers found that saturated free fatty acids contribute to both energy production and signalling required for synaptic plasticity, in work circulating via r/science.

The team challenged prior assumptions about brain bioenergetics by demonstrating that these lipids directly support neuronal communication processes.

Experiments tracked how the fatty acids participate in the metabolic and signalling pathways that enable synapses to strengthen or weaken.

The work focused on how these molecules supply ATP while also acting as signalling intermediates at the synapse.

This dual role was measured across conditions that alter synaptic strength in living tissue.

Earlier models treated saturated fatty acids mainly as structural components or secondary metabolites, and the new measurements overturn those models that downplayed them in brain energy dynamics.

The measurements link their abundance directly to the metabolic demands of long-term potentiation and long-term depression.

The experiments isolated the contribution of saturated species from other lipid classes to confirm their dual energetic and messenger roles.

That separation clarifies why previous studies that grouped all fatty acids together may have missed the functional specificity now observed.

Because synaptic plasticity underpins learning and memory, the finding reframes how shifts in fatty-acid profiles could influence cognitive flexibility.

Oxford, Newcastle, and Cambridge researchers examined more than four thousand nine hundred people with inflammatory bowel disease and found roughly three point five percent carried antibodies that neutralize interleukin-ten, according to Science Daily.

These autoantibodies disable a key regulator of inflammation and associate strongly with the HLA-DRB1*01:03 genetic variant previously linked to severe disease.

The same antibodies were absent in healthy controls.

The antibodies were detected in both Crohn’s disease and ulcerative colitis patients.

The discovery clarifies how one of the strongest known genetic risk factors for inflammatory bowel disease produces intestinal inflammation.

Researchers estimate that fifteen thousand to twenty thousand people in the United Kingdom alone may carry these neutralizing antibodies.

Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew researchers analyzed historic specimens and DNA sequences to distinguish Ganoderma lucidum, described in seventeen eighty-one, from Ganoderma lingzhi used in traditional East Asian medicine, in a Phys.org report.

The two species diverged at least sixteen million years ago.

Conventional DNA barcoding correctly identified only one hundred forty-nine of one thousand one hundred thirty public sequences assigned to Ganoderma lucidum.

A mislabeled hazel-tree specimen collected near London supplied the first usable genetic material matching the original seventeen eighty-one description.

The study shows that accurate species identification requires multiple genomic fragments plus evolutionary modeling rather than single-barcode approaches.

The team proposes calling Ganoderma lucidum false lingzhi to reduce misidentification in pharmacological research and consumer products.

Spiders displayed distinct sleep stages that parallel patterns seen in other animals, in behavioral observations summarized on r/science.

The study recorded periods of reduced responsiveness followed by a second phase with different physiological markers.

The two phases were distinguished by changes in arousal threshold and movement patterns during rest.

Researchers observed these stages across multiple individuals under controlled laboratory conditions.

The findings add spiders to the list of invertebrates displaying organized sleep architecture.

Functional imaging examined how individuals with higher narcissistic traits process social situations that require empathy.

The work focused on brain responses during complex interpersonal scenarios rather than isolated tasks.

Participants with elevated grandiose narcissism scores showed distinct activation patterns when interpreting others’ mental states in dynamic social contexts.

Results highlight reduced engagement in networks typically linked to perspective-taking.

The study design emphasized ecologically valid stimuli to capture real-world social cognition demands.

Face-to-face interactions with friends emerged as the strongest predictor of mental health outcomes among college students.

A study of nearly eighty thousand students found that exercise, sleep duration, and alcohol avoidance showed weaker associations once social contact was accounted for.

The pattern held across multiple campuses and demographic groups.

Researchers collected data through repeated surveys that captured both social behavior and self-reported well-being measures.

The large sample size allowed statistical separation of social contact effects from other lifestyle variables.

Leading researchers in the well-being field, reviewing that same ranking of social connection, report prioritizing time with loved ones after looking at accumulated evidence.

One investigator stated that relationships with other people represent the single strongest contributor to happiness.

Population-level data consistently place social connections above income or achievement metrics in predictive models.

The survey captured how prominent happiness scientists personally apply their own research findings in daily life.

Multiple independent datasets converged on the same ranking of relationship quality as the dominant predictor.

Sixteen years of monitoring data show that changes in freshwater inflow reshape algal species composition in South Australia’s Coorong lagoon system.

Shifts in salinity and nutrient delivery track directly with river management decisions.

Continuous sampling captured both seasonal cycles and multi-year trends linked to upstream water releases.

The long-term record supplies a baseline for testing adaptation strategies under changing climate conditions.

The dataset now supports modeling of how altered flow regimes may affect primary producers and dependent food webs.

Field measurements of energy expenditure in wild Panamanian bats demonstrate how individuals operate near their physiological limits during nightly foraging.

Direct observations of flight duration and foraging intensity were paired with physiological sampling to quantify daily energy budgets.

The data link specific activity budgets to population persistence under habitat pressure.

The study highlights that bats routinely approach their maximum sustainable metabolic rate when securing food.

Researchers propose incorporating these energetic ceilings into future conservation assessments.
