Michigan’s MiOncoSeq program launched in two thousand ten as the first national effort to sequence patient tumors by analyzing both DNA and RNA from normal tissue and tumors.

Researchers applied long-read nanopore sequencing, capable of reading thousands to tens of thousands of letters in one stretch, to seven hundred sixty-eight patients with prostate, breast, or ovarian cancer, according to a report in The Good Men Project.

Conventional short-read sequencing examines only a few hundred DNA letters at a time and often fails to show how mutations are arranged or whether both copies of a protective gene have been disabled through complex rearrangements and chemical modifications.

In a broader analysis of four thousand four hundred ninety-six tumor samples from three thousand six hundred eighty-eight patients, the team used nanopore sequencing on selected cases to map mutation arrangements in genes including NOTCH1, PDGFRB, KIT, and PIK3CA.

One woman with a strong family history of breast and ovarian cancer had tested negative on a commercial multigene panel, yet nanopore sequencing of her blood sample revealed a disease-causing DNA insertion in BRCA2.

A separate report in the New England Journal of Medicine traced a rare spinal tumor in a three-year-old boy, twenty-six months after gene therapy for Krabbe disease, to a fragment of modified adeno-associated virus, or AAV, DNA that inserted into the GLI1 gene and drove its overexpression.

Arul Chinnaiyan stated that identifying these mutations can help clinicians understand why certain patients develop drug resistance and how to make better treatment choices, and that understanding complications like the GLI1 insertion can guide safer gene-therapy design.

WIF1-positive basal cells sit packed around the urethra in the regions where age-related prostate growth begins, according to Johns Hopkins researchers in a report on r/science.

The team identified this previously unknown cell type in the exact zones linked to enlargement.

The cells are proposed to explain the slow, interrupted urination many men experience after age fifty.

Their location suggests they may respond to local mechanical or hormonal signals that accumulate with age.

The finding offers a potential cellular target for more localized therapies.

If the cells prove causal, treatments could shift from broad hormonal suppression toward interventions that spare other tissues.

What remains unknown is whether these cells change in number or activity in men who never develop symptomatic enlargement, or whether they interact with known inflammatory pathways in the prostate.

Older adults whose eating habits most closely matched the Mediterranean diet had significantly higher concentrations of the mitochondrial microproteins humanin and SHMOOSE, USC Leonard Davis researchers found.

Science Daily reported that the team examined blood samples from older adults with varying degrees of diet adherence.

Higher consumption of olive oil, fish, and legumes tracked with increased humanin levels.

Olive oil intake combined with lower refined-carbohydrate consumption tracked with higher SHMOOSE levels.

Participants with stronger diet adherence also displayed lower indicators of oxidative stress, the process in which reactive oxygen species overwhelm cellular defenses and damage proteins and DNA.

Roberto Vicinanza noted that these microproteins may act as molecular messengers that translate what people eat into how their cells function and age.

Pinchas Cohen, the study’s senior author, added that humanin and SHMOOSE could serve as biomarkers for adherence to the Mediterranean diet and hold clinical significance.

Psychedelics can sustain antidepressant-like effects after the drug has cleared, and a Nature Neuroscience study mapped the intracellular pathway that allows it, according to a report on r/science.

The work isolates beta-arrestin recruitment at the five-HT2A receptor as a key mechanism.

That pathway operates separately from classical G-protein signaling.

Rolling snakes keep their heads steady relative to the body axis through specific kinematic adjustments during locomotion.

Researchers documented the locomotor patterns and head-movement control that maintain visual stability during the rolling gait.

Seabird ancestry predicted plastic accumulation better than diet, feeding method, or body size.

An analysis of ninety-three thousand two hundred eighty-one seabirds from two hundred forty species recorded plastic in one hundred eighty-two species.

Phylogenetic relationships can help identify seabirds at elevated risk.

Older adults who napped most days or every day carried a forty-eight percent elevated risk of developing dementia compared with rare or never-nappers.

The association held after adjustment for demographics, health conditions, and nighttime sleep.

It persisted across multiple sensitivity analyses.

Reduced sugar intake in early life linked to modestly slower brain aging and lower dementia incidence decades later.

A fifteen-year follow-up tracked participants from childhood into later adulthood.

Ultra-processed foods carried a twelve percent higher likelihood of digestive cancer in cohort studies and a forty-two percent higher likelihood in case-control studies.

Ultra-processed meat and protein products showed the strongest subcategory association at thirty-three percent elevated risk.

The meta-analysis combined results across multiple populations.

A cuprate superconductor long assumed to have uniform structure actually contains regions of two different atomic arrangements separated by unusually wide boundary zones.

University of Warwick researchers used scanning three-dimensional X-ray diffraction at the European Synchrotron Radiation Facility, according to Science Daily, in a result that challenges a forty-year assumption about these crystals.

The boundaries are hundreds of times broader than expected and may actively interfere with superconductivity rather than simply coexisting with it.

Mark Senn said the finding means existing bulk measurements and theoretical models will need reinterpretation because the crystal is fundamentally patchworked and textured.

The team suspects similar hidden structural patterns may be widespread among other cuprate superconductors and related materials studied under extreme pressure.

The measurements were made possible by a recent one hundred fifty million euro upgrade to the synchrotron that provides extremely intense X-ray beams for studying materials in detail.

In brief, scientists writing in Science Daily propose that polarized-light detection paired with specially designed fluorescent molecules could distinguish hypothetical mirror-image organisms from ordinary life if such organisms were ever synthesized.

A single X-ray pulse imaging method, reported by Phys.org, captures instabilities and turbulence structures in plasmas ranging from nuclear-fusion to laser-plasma regimes in one exposure.

Something most people get wrong about the Mediterranean diet is that its health effects come only from antioxidants or from changes in blood lipids and inflammation.

An additional route runs through two tiny proteins produced inside mitochondria.

Humanin and SHMOOSE originate from short open reading frames within mitochondrial DNA.

Those segments were once considered non-functional.

They are now known to encode biologically active molecules.

Humanin was first identified in two thousand three by Pinchas Cohen and colleagues.

That identification sits inside more than twenty years of work on small proteins encoded by mitochondrial DNA.

Later work linked humanin to improved insulin sensitivity.

It has also been tied to better cardiovascular outcomes, increased longevity, and protection against cognitive decline.

Mitochondria generate most of the cell’s energy.

They also regulate stress responses and aging processes.

The microproteins appear to function as internal messengers that convert dietary information into adjustments in mitochondrial output.

Clinicians might one day monitor humanin or SHMOOSE levels to assess whether dietary interventions are producing the expected cellular effects, rather than relying solely on self-reported food logs.

Open questions include whether other eating patterns or lifestyle factors influence the same mitochondrial signals.

Another is whether genetic variation in mitochondrial DNA affects an individual’s response to the Mediterranean diet.
