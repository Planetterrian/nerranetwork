### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Ancient DNA Confirms Early Cacao Use in North America — Phys.org**
   Researchers examined residues from the Mississippian city of Etowah and detected cacao DNA in artifacts dated to the 11th and 12th centuries. The finding pushes back the known timeline for cacao consumption in eastern North America by several hundred years. Cacao, the source of chocolate, was previously documented mainly in Mesoamerica during that era. The study appears in PLOS One and relied on targeted ancient DNA recovery from ceramic vessels. Next steps include broader sampling across other Mississippian sites to map trade routes.

2. **Degraded DNA Identifies Two Extinct Galapagos Tortoise Lineages — Phys.org**
   Yale researchers combined new computational tools with ancient DNA from museum specimens to recover sequences from two previously unknown giant tortoise populations. Both lineages disappeared after human arrival on the islands. The method improves recovery from highly fragmented samples that standard approaches miss. The work clarifies historical biodiversity on the archipelago before modern extinctions. Further application to other island systems is planned.

3. **AI Improves Early Forecasts of Tropical Cyclone Formation — Phys.org**
   A study tested machine-learning models on Atlantic Ocean data and showed they can flag developing disturbances several days before traditional systems detect them. The AI draws on large-scale atmospheric patterns that precede storm organization. Forecasts gain lead time without replacing existing dynamical models. The approach was validated against historical storm seasons. Operational testing with weather agencies is the next phase.

4. **Intrinsic Capacity Emerges as Key Aging Metric — The New York Times**
   Researchers define intrinsic capacity as the composite of physical, cognitive, sensory, and psychological reserves that determine functional aging. Longitudinal cohorts show that declines in this measure predict disability and mortality more reliably than single biomarkers. The World Health Organization has incorporated the concept into its healthy-aging framework. Clinicians can track it through simple batteries of tests rather than advanced imaging. Ongoing trials are testing interventions that preserve multiple domains simultaneously.

5. **AI Identifies Low-Energy Settings for NASA Alloy Printing — ScienceDaily**
   Engineers used an algorithm to evaluate more than 100 million parameter combinations for 3D-printing GRCop-42, a copper alloy used in rocket engines. After 40 physical trials the system located six viable settings, including one at 500 watts. Lower power requirements expand the range of printers that can produce the material. The work reduces cost barriers that previously limited adoption. Follow-on tests will assess mechanical performance of the new prints.

6. **Altered Gravity Effects on Female Physiology Under Study — Phys.org**
   Begum Mathyk and collaborators are extending earlier spaceflight data to examine how microgravity and radiation interact with reproductive and endocrine systems in women. The project builds on samples collected two years ago and adds new assays for hormone signaling and tissue response. Longer missions increase the need for sex-specific countermeasures. Data will inform habitat design and medical protocols for deep-space travel. Results are expected to guide selection criteria for future crews.

7. **Toronto Elementary Phone Bans Show Academic Gains — Phys.org**
   Schools that removed student phones during instructional hours recorded measurable improvements in attention and test performance. High schools saw smaller or inconsistent effects, suggesting developmental differences in susceptibility to distraction. The study compared matched schools before and after policy changes. Teachers reported fewer behavioral interruptions once devices were stored. Provincial data collection continues to assess longer-term mental-health outcomes.

8. **Food-Waste Compost Improves Tomato Flavor Compounds — Phys.org**
   University of Queensland trials added green-bin compost to commercial tomato fields and measured both chemical profiles and sensory panels. Treated fruit scored higher in sweetness and overall aroma intensity. The effect traces to increased availability of certain soil micronutrients that influence sugar and volatile production. Control plots without compost produced blander fruit under identical irrigation. Larger-scale adoption could divert organic waste while raising crop value.

9. **First Full Reptile Checklist for Turks and Caicos Completed — Phys.org**
   After twenty years of fieldwork, a University of North Carolina Asheville researcher compiled the initial comprehensive inventory of the islands’ native terrestrial reptiles. The illustrated guide covers distribution, habitat preferences, and conservation status for each species. Several taxa are endemic and face pressure from habitat loss and invasive predators. The reference supports both scientific surveys and local protection planning. Digital versions will allow citizen scientists to contribute sightings.

10. **Bird Flu Poses Emerging Threat to Australian Mammals — Phys.org**
    Researchers warn that highly pathogenic avian influenza could spill over into iconic marsupials that lack prior exposure. Bait-delivered vaccines are under consideration as a proactive shield for vulnerable populations. Modeling shows transmission risk rises with longer migration routes of infected birds. Field trials of oral vaccines in related species are already underway. Surveillance networks are expanding to detect early cases in wildlife.

11. **Ultra-Processed Food Intake Linked to Higher Prostate Cancer Risk — ScienceDaily**
    Analysis of more than 17,000 U.S. men found that higher consumption of soft drinks, packaged snacks, and processed meats corresponded to 24–31 percent elevated prostate cancer incidence after adjustment for age, smoking, and socioeconomic factors. The association held across racial and ethnic groups. Mechanisms under investigation include chronic inflammation and disruptions to metabolic hormone balance. The study used nationally representative dietary recall data. Replication in other cohorts is planned.

12. **Envy Shapes Voting More Than Individual Decisions — Phys.org**
    Linköping University experiments demonstrated that participants weighed envious comparisons more heavily when resources were allocated by majority vote than when they chose alone. The effect arises because personal responsibility diffuses across the group. When votes are unlikely to be decisive, individuals focus more on relative outcomes. The finding helps explain polarization in collective resource debates. Follow-up work will test interventions that restore accountability.

13. **Extended Mean-Field Theory Refines Neural-Network Models — Phys.org**
    Physicists showed that classical mean-field approximations break down in densely connected networks where higher-order interactions dominate. An extended formulation captures fluctuation effects and improves predictions of collective dynamics. The approach was validated on both simulated and empirical neural datasets. It offers a middle ground between full simulation and overly simplified models. Applications to other many-body systems in biology are being explored.

14. **Overconfident CEOs Reduce Corporate Lobbying — Phys.org**
    University of Queensland analysis linked higher CEO overconfidence scores to measurably lower lobbying expenditures. Firms with such leaders therefore face greater exposure to regulatory shifts. The pattern held after controlling for firm size and industry. Reduced engagement leaves policy outcomes more dependent on competitors’ advocacy. Governance reforms that calibrate executive self-assessment are under discussion.

15. **Bonnie Bassler Honored for Quorum-Sensing Research — Princeton University**
    The Royal Society awarded Bassler its highest honor for decades of work mapping how bacteria communicate via chemical signals to coordinate group behaviors. Her discoveries revealed density-dependent gene regulation across diverse microbial species. The findings underpin new strategies for disrupting pathogenic biofilms without traditional antibiotics. Current extensions explore quorum-sensing interference in chronic infections. The award highlights the translation of basic microbiology into therapeutic concepts.

### Science Deep Dive: How Ultra-Processed Foods May Raise Cancer Risk
Most people assume any diet-cancer link comes down to excess calories or missing nutrients. In reality, ultra-processed foods appear to act through additional routes that alter inflammation and hormone signaling even when total energy intake is controlled. Right now, as you digest a packaged snack, emulsifiers and additives can shift gut-barrier permeability and circulating inflammatory markers within hours. One memorable number from recent U.S. data: men in the highest intake group showed 24 to 31 percent higher prostate cancer incidence after standard risk-factor adjustments. The next time you reach for a ready-to-eat meal, notice how many ingredients on the label have no direct culinary counterpart; that list length often tracks with the strength of the observed associations. Watching future trials that swap whole foods for matched ultra-processed versions will clarify whether the risk signal shrinks when those additives disappear.
