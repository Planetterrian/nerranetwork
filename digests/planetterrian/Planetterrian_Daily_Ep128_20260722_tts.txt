Now moving from psychedelic-induced network reorganization to everyday behaviors that also shape circadian timing.

Most people assume any daylight exposure or even a quick glance outside resets their internal clock, yet the actual mechanism requires sustained activation of intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells.

Right now, as you move through your morning, these cells integrate blue-wavelength signals over several minutes to suppress melatonin and advance your circadian phase.

A memorable number is that the response saturates only after roughly 10 to 30 minutes of outdoor light, far longer than the few seconds many expect.

The low UV index at dawn further means the benefit arrives without meaningful skin-risk trade-offs.
