### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Miniature Cas12m proteins cleave DNA after all — Phys.org**
   An international team led by Hayato Morinaga, Satoshi N. Omura and Osamu Nureki at the University of Tokyo identified active Cas12m variants that cleave DNA. Cryo-electron microscopy revealed atomic-scale differences between active and inactive forms. Researchers pinpointed specific amino acid changes that switch UnCas12m4a from inactive to active. The work supplies design rules for compact genome-editing tools. Results appear in Nature Chemical Biology. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-miniature-cas12m-proteins-dna.html)

2. **Dragonflies consume diverse migrating insects — Phys.org**
   Researchers at the University of Exeter sampled two dragonfly species at a Pyrenees mountain pass. DNA metabarcoding of gut contents identified 87 prey species. Flies made up the largest share of the diet in every sample. Ninety percent of lesser emperors and seventy percent of darters had eaten known migrants. Some lesser emperors had consumed smaller darter dragonflies. The study documents an aerial food web among migrants and was published in Ecography. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-dragonflies-feast-insects-autumn-migrations.html)

3. **Robotic knee replacement shows no added benefit — ScienceDaily**
   A large clinical trial compared robot-assisted knee replacement with conventional surgery. Patients in both groups reported equivalent pain, mobility and recovery at one year. The technology improves surgical precision yet produced no measurable clinical difference after twelve months. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261124055904.htm)

4. **Aversion to AI leaves decision influence intact — Phys.org**
   Researchers at City St George's, University of London tested whether dislike of artificial intelligence reduces its effect on choices. Participants who expressed aversion still altered decisions when AI input was provided. The study concludes that negative attitudes do not block the technology's sway over behaviour. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-ai-doesnt-deter.html)

5. **Protein organ clocks improve disease forecasts — Science Translational Medicine**
   Adding measured and inferred protein organ clocks to electronic health records raised five-year prediction accuracy for multiple diseases. The improvement appeared in two large cohorts and was not captured by records alone. The approach uses RABIT technology to infer protein levels from routine data. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/EricTopol/status/2125674086567428480)

6. **Drug raises energy use without brown-fat activation — ScienceDaily**
   A tested compound increased whole-body energy expenditure yet left brown-fat activity unchanged. The finding indicates an additional, still-unidentified route for calorie burning. Researchers note the result complicates efforts to harness brown fat for obesity treatment. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261124055856.htm)

7. **DNA cubes self-assemble into 3D nanostructures — Phys.org**
   Associate professor Petr Sulc's team created a programmable system of DNA cubes that form complex three-dimensional shapes. The method draws on principles similar to Minecraft block placement. It opens routes to nanorobots, layered electronics and advanced nanoscale devices. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-minecraft-dna-cubes-complex-3d.html)

8. **Artificial photosynthesis yields edible protein fungus — Phys.org**
   A system combining waste carbon dioxide, light and microbes produced a protein-rich edible fungus within seven days. The approach bypasses traditional crop requirements for land and water. It addresses projected shortfalls in agricultural protein output by 2050. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-artificial-photosynthesis-protein-rich-edible.html)

9. **Fungi induce water-conducting cells in plants — Phys.org**
   An international team led by the University of Göttingen found that certain fungi trigger formation of new water-carrying cells. Infected plants displayed greater tolerance to drought stress. The mechanism operates through a single fungal protein and was reported in Cell Host & Microbe. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-fungi-specialized-cells-protein.html)

10. **Carbon nanotube electrons follow quantum model — Phys.org**
    An international group from Lithuania, Finland and Israel measured electron behaviour inside carbon nanotubes. Experiments and theory together showed that a quantum model, rather than a classical one, governs collective electron motion. The work supplies the first direct demonstration of the governing physical framework. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-carbon-nan) nanotube-reveal-quantum-electron.html

11. **Small-molecule inhibitor revives antibiotic against MRSA — Phys.org**
    Imperial College London researchers identified a compound that restores ciprofloxacin activity against methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus. The inhibitor targets a resistance mechanism and re-sensitises the bacteria in laboratory tests. The finding offers a route to extend the useful life of existing antibiotics. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-inhibitor-antibiotic-drug-resistant-bacteria.html)

12. **Peregrine falcon habitat could expand sharply — Phys.org**
    Modelling under a high-emissions scenario projects a 74 percent increase in suitable UK territory for peregrine falcons by 2080. The gain stems from shifting temperature and precipitation patterns. Other raptors, including white-tailed eagles, face substantial range contraction under the same projections. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-world-fastest-animal-livable-territory.html)

### Planetterrian Spotlight
**Heaven scent: Study shows link between a partner's body odor and sexual intimacy — Phys.org**
Partners who find each other's natural body odour pleasant report higher levels of sexual intimacy and satisfaction. The study suggests odour compatibility forms part of the sensory basis for long-term pair bonding rather than a signal to be masked. Participants rated odour pleasantness separately from visual or personality traits, isolating its contribution. The work points to an under-appreciated role for olfaction in human relationship maintenance. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-heaven-scent-link-partner-body.html)

### Science Deep Dive: Migrating insects form aerial food webs
Most people picture animal migrations as orderly streams of individuals moving in parallel with little interaction among species. Dragonfly research at a Pyrenees pass shows the opposite: migrants concentrate in space and time, creating layered predator-prey relationships in flight. The mechanism becomes personal when you realise that the same insects crossing your garden or farmland in autumn are simultaneously feeding and being fed upon by other migrants. DNA metabarcoding revealed 87 distinct prey species inside the dragonflies, with flies present in every gut sample examined. This number illustrates how a single narrow pass funnels enough biomass to support an entire aerial food web rather than isolated travellers. What changes for the listener is recognition that protecting migratory corridors also safeguards the hidden trophic links that move with the insects; losing those corridors would remove both the prey base and the predators that rely on it during seasonal passage.
