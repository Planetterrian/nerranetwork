### Top 12 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Land policy mix lowers climate transition costs while protecting food security — Phys.org**
   A Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research model integrated economy, energy, land use and climate to test scenarios for limiting warming to 1.7°C. Without land-based measures the required carbon price reached $400 per ton of CO₂ by 2040 and produced a 3.5% consumption loss from 2025 to 2100. Adding all feasible land measures reduced the price to $130 and the consumption loss to 1.3%. The land area needed for biomass ranged from 66 million to more than 300 million hectares depending on the mix. A balanced policy using residual biomass, capped methane and nitrous oxide pricing, and global forest conservation achieved the target at $180 per ton and a 1.5% consumption loss. The study notes that land-based potential is concentrated in the Global South where food, water and biodiversity pressures already exist. Lead author Leon Merfort stated that a transformation in the land sector allows a slower energy transition to reach the same climate target. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-scarce-resource-important-climate.html)

2. **Polymetallic nodules form three-dimensional habitats for unique deep-sea organisms — Phys.org**
   Researchers from MARUM and NIOZ dissected 244 nodules collected by box corers and diving robots in the Clarion-Clipperton Zone. They recovered 4,139 individual animals living inside cracks and pores, averaging 17 per nodule and reaching 245 in one case. Genetic analyses showed the nodule communities were similar across distances up to 1,000 km yet differed markedly from surrounding sediment communities. Roundworm abundance inside nodules was about 10% and copepod abundance about 25% of sediment levels. The single-celled species Thurammina sp. 1 was especially common inside nodules and appears adapted to the sheltered spaces. Nodules grow only a few millimeters per million years at depths of 4,000 to 6,000 meters. Sabine Gollner noted that commercial mining may result in irreversible loss of undescribed biodiversity associated with nodules. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-deep-sea-destroy-unique-habitats.html)

3. **German VAT cut during COVID boosted private consumption especially among lower-wealth households — Phys.org**

Lower-wealth households and younger and middle-aged households showed the largest increases in consumption regardless of financial literacy. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-covid-added-tax-significantly-boosted.html)

### Planetterrian Spotlight
A randomized trial tested whether adding thrice-weekly resistance exercise to tirzepatide treatment would limit lean-mass loss in adults with overweight or obesity. One hundred twenty participants received tirzepatide and were assigned to resistance training or usual activity for 40 weeks. Both groups lost similar total weight and fat mass, yet the exercise group lost 1.09 kg less total lean soft tissue mass and showed gains rather than losses in upper- and lower-body strength. Adherence to the exercise protocol reached 91%. The findings indicate resistance training can serve as a practical adjunct that preserves muscle function during incretin-based weight loss. What specific strength or mobility outcomes would you want measured in longer follow-up of this approach? ### Science Deep Dive: How mosquito species differ in what attracts them to humans
Most people assume mosquitoes respond to one shared set of human cues, so anyone labeled a “mosquito magnet” would attract every species equally. In reality each species is tuned to its own narrow combination of skin odors, chemicals and surface bacteria. That tuning directly affects which mosquitoes you encounter on a given evening because the blend on your skin matches some species better than others. The study documented these distinct profiles across multiple species and showed that the differences persist even when the same people are tested repeatedly. One practical consequence is that broad-spectrum repellents may miss the specific signals used by the mosquitoes that carry particular diseases. Refining repellents around the exact compounds each target species detects could therefore improve protection without increasing overall chemical load. The next step researchers identify is mapping which bacterial or chemical signals matter most for the species that transmit malaria or dengue so that new formulations can be tested against those precise cues.
