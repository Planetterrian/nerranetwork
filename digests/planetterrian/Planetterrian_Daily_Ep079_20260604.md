### Top 15 Science & Health Discoveries
1. **Physical Fitness Tied to Brain Health in Young Adults — r/science**
   The study examined links between fitness measures and brain structure in younger adults. Effects differed between males and females. No specific sample size or p-values appear in the summary. The finding adds to evidence that aerobic capacity influences neural metrics early in life. Future work may clarify which fitness components drive the sex-specific patterns.

2. **Hantavirus Response Tools Show Early Promise — Phys.org**
   Researchers developed candidate treatments and vaccines after a cruise-ship outbreak exposed gaps in options. No approved therapies or vaccines existed at the time of the incident. The work targets a rare but high-mortality rodent-borne virus. Limited funding has slowed further testing. Next steps depend on sustained support for preclinical and early clinical evaluation.

3. **Male Puberty Timing May Predict Later Health Risks — STAT News**
   Onset age of puberty in boys remains far less studied than in girls. Early or late timing could flag elevated long-term disease risks. Data gaps stem from historical research focus on female development. The report calls for expanded longitudinal tracking in males. Such data could improve risk-stratification models.

4. **Beluga Whales Switch Mates Across Lifetimes — ScienceDaily**
   DNA analysis of more than 600 belugas in Alaska’s Bristol Bay spanned 13 years. Both males and females produced offspring with multiple partners. The flexible system contrasts with more rigid mating patterns in some other mammals. Researchers suggest this behaviour may aid genetic diversity. Continued monitoring could reveal how the pattern affects population resilience.

5. **Cancer Cells Lose MHC I Yet Become Vulnerable to CD4+ T Cells — ScienceDaily**
   Downregulation of MHC I normally lets tumours evade killer T cells. The same change increases susceptibility to helper CD4+ T cells. The study overturns the assumption that MHC I loss is an unmitigated escape route. Experiments showed the shift redirects immune attack rather than blocking it entirely. Follow-up work will test whether this vulnerability can be exploited therapeutically.

6. **AAV8 Gene Therapy Tested in Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia — Nature Medicine**
   Three patients received AAV8-mediated LDL receptor gene therapy in a phase 1 trial. Preclinical data supported the approach before human testing. Early signals indicated both safety and some efficacy on lipid parameters. The trial marks an initial step toward in-vivo gene correction for this genetic disorder. Longer-term follow-up will assess durability and dosing requirements.

7. **Epidural Spinal Cord Stimulation Improves Post-Stroke Arm Function — Nature Medicine**
   Seven participants with chronic hemiparesis underwent cervical epidural stimulation. Strength, motor function, and spasticity all showed measurable gains. The feasibility trial reported no major safety issues. Stimulation parameters were adjusted individually. Larger controlled studies are needed to confirm the magnitude and persistence of benefit.

8. **Tactile Robot Added to Safety Planning for LGBTQ+ Youth — Nature Medicine**
   A randomized trial tested the Purrble robot alongside standard safety planning. Participants showed lower scores on the Difficulties in Emotion Regulation Scale and fewer depressive symptoms. The device provides tactile interaction during moments of distress. The study focused on youth at elevated self-harm risk. Replication in broader populations is planned.

9. **First FDA-Approved PROTAC Validates Targeted Protein Degradation — Nature Biotechnology**
   The approval demonstrates that proteins can be degraded therapeutically rather than only inhibited. The milestone does not yet prove superiority over existing modalities. It confirms the degradation mechanism can reach clinical use. Additional PROTACs are advancing through pipelines. Observers will watch whether the class expands to new targets.

10. **Great Apes Demonstrate Flexible Problem-Solving in Virtual Tasks — Phys.org**
    Chimpanzees at Leipzig Zoo navigated virtual forests using touchscreen controls. The tasks mirror foraging strategies observed in the wild. Two decades of research at the site have mapped cognition, cooperation, and curiosity. Results highlight continuity between captive and natural behaviours. Ongoing studies track how these skills develop across individuals.

11. **Javan Leopard Relies on Prey Diversity and Recovering Forest — Phys.org**
    Camera-trap and spatial modelling data show the leopard uses a wide prey base. Recovering forest patches prove as important as formal reserves. The work was conducted on densely populated Java. Findings suggest conservation must extend beyond protected-area boundaries. Habitat connectivity efforts are now being prioritised.

12. **Extreme Arguments Can Reduce Support for Controversial Positions — r/science**
    The study tested paradoxical thinking interventions. Presenting extreme arguments in favour of a topic lowered overall endorsement. The effect emerged across multiple contentious issues. Sample details and exact effect sizes are not reported in the summary. Researchers plan to examine boundary conditions for the approach.

13. **Patient-Facing AI Scribes Enter Clinical Testing — STAT News**
    Ambient AI tools first assisted clinicians with documentation. Developers now adapt the same systems for patients to record and summarise visits. Early pilots focus on note accuracy and privacy safeguards. The shift aims to improve patient engagement after appointments. Regulatory and integration questions remain open.

14. **Gold-Palladium Catalyst Mechanism Supports Bio-Based Chemical Production — Phys.org**
    Researchers identified how the bimetallic system converts renewable feedstocks. The work targets replacement of petroleum-derived building blocks for plastics and consumer goods. Temperature-dependent signal behaviour allows tuning of output. The mechanism could lower reliance on fossil inputs. Scale-up testing is the reported next phase.

15. **Stonehenge Altar Stone Likely Moved Hundreds of Kilometres — Phys.org**
    New geological and transport modelling traces the stone’s origin. Ancient communities navigated challenging terrain to relocate it. The study highlights logistical capabilities of Neolithic groups. No direct health implications are discussed. The findings refine understanding of prehistoric mobility networks.

### Science Deep Dive: How Tumours Can Invite a Different Immune Attack
Most people assume that cancer cells shutting down MHC I molecules simply hide from the immune system. In reality, that same step can expose them to a separate arm of immunity. Right now, as your body’s T cells patrol tissues, MHC I levels on any given cell determine which immune subset can recognise it. When MHC I drops, killer CD8+ T cells lose their primary signal, yet CD4+ helper T cells gain an opening through alternative antigen presentation routes. One memorable number: CD4+ T cells can comprise up to 60–70 % of the T-cell pool in some tumour microenvironments, giving them substantial reach once the MHC I barrier falls. The practical takeaway is to watch for trials that combine MHC I–lowering agents with CD4+-boosting strategies rather than assuming MHC I restoration is always the goal.
