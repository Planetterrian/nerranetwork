**What You Need to Know:** Four Gulf states face double-digit GDP drops as the IMF says war will shrink the region’s economy. The Fund’s October outlook, reported today, ties a Gulf downgrade to the prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz, puts the UAE on course for a 3.9 percent contraction this year, and calls for cash transfers to Nigeria’s poorest while living costs stay high. It also says oil prices are up 32 percent this year and global inflation is 4.7 percent.

### Lead
**Middle East and Central Asia’s economy to shrink in 2026 amid war, IMF says: Al Jazeera**
The International Monetary Fund says the Middle East and Central Asia economy will shrink in 2026 amid war, and Al Jazeera reports that Iraq, Bahrain, Kuwait and Qatar face double-digit declines in GDP amid the fallout from the US-Israel war on Iran. The National reports that the Fund has sharply downgraded its Gulf forecasts because of the prolonged closure of the Strait of Hormuz and projects a 3.9 percent contraction in the UAE economy this year. In the October World Economic Outlook, the IMF said an energy-supply shock linked to that conflict pushed oil prices up 32 percent this year and global inflation to 4.7 percent, reversing two years of progress on disinflation. It put global growth at 3 percent in 2026 and said growth could rebound to 3.4 percent in 2027 if the strait reopens fully by March next year. For Nigeria, the Fund urged targeted, temporary cash transfers for the most vulnerable households; Economic Counselor Silvana Tenreyro projected growth of 4.3 percent by 2027, and the Fund said the Central Bank should return inflation to target within two to three years alongside a medium-term fiscal framework. The IMF said Nigeria’s growth has held up as an energy exporter but living costs remain very high, and that heavy investment in artificial intelligence is helping keep global growth afloat. **South Africa: Road Accident Fund to Receive No Additional Govt Funding: AllAfrica**
Transport Minister Barbara Creecy has ruled out additional government funding for the Road Accident Fund and rejected its proposal to raise the fuel levy to R3 a litre, EWN reports. She said motorists are already under financial pressure and that the priority is the fund’s management and financial challenges. A new chief executive has been appointed, and the process of appointing a permanent board is underway. Creecy said legislative changes are needed to establish a clear schedule of benefits, with concerns continuing over the fund’s ability to meet claims.
