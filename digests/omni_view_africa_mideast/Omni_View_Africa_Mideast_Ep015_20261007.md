### Lead
**Kenya: Ruto Orders Enhanced Ebola Response As Kenya Traces 27 Passengers: AllAfrica**
President William Ruto has directed the National Security Council Committee to assess Kenya's Ebola situation and recommend further measures after the country's first confirmed imported Ebola Virus Disease case died. The patient had travelled from the Democratic Republic of Congo through Uganda to Nairobi and died on Monday evening at The Nairobi Hospital, two days after admission to its isolation unit; the hospital said the patient tested positive for Bundibugyo Ebola virus disease. State House spokesperson Hussein Mohamed said that as of October 6 the government had screened 652,584 travellers and tested 267 samples in five laboratories, with only the deceased patient's sample positive and no further cases detected; the deceased has been buried under public health protocols. Eight family members and 21 healthcare workers are quarantined and monitored daily, and 27 passengers from the same flight have been identified and contacted. Chief executive Felix Osano said the hospital remains open, that its isolation unit is physically separate from the main facility, and that identified contacts are being monitored with the Ministry of Health for 21 days; he said being in the building is not, by itself, exposure. Designated capacity stands at eight beds at Kenyatta National Hospital, 50 at Moi Teaching and Referral Hospital, 49 at National Police Hospital and 143 at The Nairobi Hospital, and the government said 4,971 healthcare workers have been trained. **Russia-backed Mali retakes rebel stronghold as Tuareg fighters withdraw under air pressure: Business Insider Africa**
Mali says its forces entered Kidal on October 6 with support from Russia's Africa Corps, reversing a government withdrawal from the northern city in April. The Azawad Liberation Front said it left in a "strategic and controlled withdrawal" to preserve its forces, citing pressure from aerial operations by Malian forces and their Russian partners. Two Malian officers told AFP that troops entered ahead of schedule and met no resistance. Government spokesman Brigadier General Issa Ousmane Coulibaly said air and ground forces carried out the operation with Africa Corps assistance, and Sahel researcher Rida Lyammouri described the recapture to the Associated Press as "a major symbolic victory to the central government." Africa Intelligence reports that, against a backdrop of heavy casualties, the military high command is formalising ties with militias already fighting jihadist groups and their allies. **Zimbabwe: Mnangagwa Declares State of Disaster As 8,500 Homes Lose Roofs in Masvingo Hailstorm: AllAfrica**
President Emmerson Mnangagwa has declared a State of Disaster after a hailstorm hit Masvingo on Monday. Preliminary assessments say about 8,500 households had their roofs completely blown off, and 90 percent of 17,000 households assessed had shattered windowpanes. Damaged sites include Great Zimbabwe University's Mucheke Campus, Masvingo Polytechnic, Masvingo Teachers College, the Passport Office, Masvingo Airport and Runyararo Clinic's maternity ward. ZESA power lines were brought down, and the resulting outages have affected water pumping, municipal drainage and sewer networks, which New Zimbabwe, carried by AllAfrica, calls an immediate public health risk. ### Both Sides: Should the reworked food-warning draft stay closed?
South Africa's health department says it has finished reviewing proposed warning labels on sugary, salty and fatty foods and is now debating the new text behind closed doors at Nedlac, more than three years after the draft was published. No one outside government has seen the reworked version. The decision in front of the department is whether that text stays closed until the minister publishes it, or whether submissions and the new draft are opened now.

The department's case, set out by spokesperson Foster Mohale, is that the review is complete and the remaining path is a legal one. The department says it received substantive submissions from 179 companies and organisations; once captured and reviewed, the material exceeded 6,000 pages, and a four-member technical team worked through them. Mohale says the draft was reworked partly to spare the industry unnecessary costs. He says the next steps are a discussion with the department's legal services, a feedback session with the food industry, academia, civil society and consumer advocacy groups, processing by legal services, and submission to the health minister for publication.

The case for opening the file now is put by legal researcher Dr Aisosa Omoruyi of Priceless SA. Daily Maverick notes that under the draft regulations, 80 percent of packaged products in South Africa would need warning labels.

Both sides are working from the same process facts: a formal public consultation has already been held, the new draft has not been shown outside government, and it is now at Nedlac. On Mohale's account, what happens next is the legal-services discussion, then the feedback session, then submission to the health minister for publication. ### Progress Watch
**Harvestfield strengthens Nigeria's healthcare manufacturing capacity with local introduction of next-generation mosquito nets: Realnews Magazine**
Harvestfield Healthcare FZE has started commercial production of Synera DuoForte, a dual-active-ingredient long-lasting insecticidal net, at its free-trade-zone plant in Ogun State, with operations begun on 13 September 2026. Realnews Magazine reports the wholly Nigerian-owned site is already supplying a World Bank-funded programme reaching 31 states. The nets, made under agreement with GDM Health Products and combining a pyrethroid with chlorfenapyr, are a WHO-prequalified product; the Ogun site is an approved location under that prequalification and is certified by NAFDAC and to ISO standards. The World Health Organisation recommends this class of net where pyrethroid resistance is established.
