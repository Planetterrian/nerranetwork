### Lead
**Houthis push for Taiz as fighting intensifies around strategic Yemeni city: The National**
Fighting has intensified around Taiz as the Houthis try to break into the city. Yemeni Armed Forces spokesman Col Majed Al Nazili said government warplanes carried out 15 air strikes on Houthi positions on the outskirts, and he said those strikes killed dozens of Houthi fighters and reinforcements and destroyed the group's equipment and vehicles. The rebels clashed with government forces in residential neighbourhoods east of the city, about 100 kilometres from Mokha, which the Houthis seized last month. Taiz links the Houthi-held north, the government-held south and the Red Sea coast, so whoever holds it controls the movement of weapons and supplies between the fronts and Aden. Yemen's Defence Ministry warned residents late on Thursday to stay clear of combat zones and dozens of roads. The UN says renewed fighting has displaced more than 133,000 people, mostly from Taiz and Lahj, and that the figure is expected to rise. UN Special Envoy Hans Grundberg said he was alarmed that fighting had significantly intensified and urged the Houthis to halt the offensive, noting that Taiz is Yemen's third-largest city and that the governorate is hosting most of the newly displaced. The Saudi-led coalition said a drone struck the Taiba electricity station in Medina at 5:11 a.m. on Tuesday, 29 September, damaging one transformer that supplies the Prophet's Mosque without cutting the wider network. Coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki said an examination of debris led the coalition to conclude the Houthis planned and carried out the attack. The Houthis have denied responsibility for targeting Islamic holy sites, Agence France-Presse reported. GCC Secretary-General Jasem Albudaiwi said the strike had crossed "all red lines" and that a threat to Saudi Arabia and its holy sites is a threat to every GCC member. Al-Maliki said the coalition will take the necessary measures to neutralize the Houthi militias. **Gulf oil infrastructure at risk as US rushes Patriot missiles to Saudi Arabia, Qatar amid Iran tensions: The Tribune**
The United States is sending the USS Theodore Roosevelt and its strike group toward the Middle East, where two other US carriers are already deployed, as President Donald Trump weighs further military action against Iran. The US military has also positioned two extra Patriot batteries in Saudi Arabia and Qatar, Axios reported, citing two American officials and a regional insider, after Saudi and Qatari authorities worried Tehran could hit their energy assets if Washington restarts major offensives. One battery was sent to guard a major oil installation in Saudi Arabia, and the second to protect a primary gas facility in Qatar. In August, when Trump contemplated a significant offensive that included strikes on Iranian energy assets, the heads of Saudi Arabia and Qatar petitioned him against that course over fears of retaliatory strikes on their petroleum and gas sites. A Center for Strategic and International Studies assessment found US Patriot interceptor reserves had dropped below 1,000 after heavy use in the conflict with Iran. The Makin Island Amphibious Ready Group, carrying thousands of US Marines, has arrived as the Pentagon adds naval vessels, combat jets and personnel. ### Both Sides: Whether Egypt and Ethiopia should let Thursday's expulsions stand
The decision already taken is whether Ethiopia and Egypt will let Thursday's expulsions stand as the answer to fighting in Ethiopia's north. Ethiopia moved first. The strongest case on Addis Ababa's side is the accusation it has already made: that Egypt, together with Eritrea and Sudan, is supporting an opposition alliance led by the Tigray People's Liberation Front, which has battled troops loyal to the federal government since last week. Egypt's strongest case is a flat denial. State media in Cairo, citing an unnamed source, called the expulsion unfortunate and unjustified, and said Egypt has consistently rejected interference in other countries' internal affairs. Egypt, Eritrea and Sudan all deny supporting the alliance. Cairo then ordered an unnamed counsellor at Ethiopia's embassy to leave within two days. Both governments are carrying out the expulsions. They are also already locked in a decade-old dispute over the Nile, in which Cairo says a huge Ethiopian dam will likely cut Egypt's share of the river. The African Union Commission says it is following the tensions among Ethiopia, Eritrea and Egypt with concern. Arab News reports that the Union urges the three to refrain from actions that could undermine stability. The clock already running is Soliman's 48 hours from Thursday's order, and two days for the Ethiopian counsellor.
