### Lead
**Iraq transports its oil past Hormuz for first time in decades as war with Iran chokes exports: The National**
The state-run Iraqi Oil Tankers Company shipped two million barrels of crude on a very large crude carrier to a point outside the Strait of Hormuz, director general Ali Qais told the Iraqi News Agency. Qais said the task was assigned for the first time in decades, shifting part of Iraqi sales from free-on-board loading at Basra to delivery outside the strait. The National reports that oil provides at least 90 percent of state revenue, and that most exports — roughly 3.3 million barrels a day before the war — left southern Basra terminals through Hormuz. It reports the waterway has been effectively closed by Iran since the war broke out in February, with shipping disrupted by missile and drone attacks, insurance spikes and naval confrontations. Buyers have demanded delivery outside the strait, and the State Oil Marketing Organisation has offered discounts for Basra loading. The National also reports that Iranian-aligned factions launched drones toward Gulf states from Iraqi territory, and that Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain and Jordan held Baghdad responsible. Oil minister Bassim Khudair said last month, according to the state news agency, that Iraq has exported three million barrels a day since the beginning of September; talks on the Iraq-Turkey pipeline via Ceyhan and on trucking crude to Syria cover only a fraction of that volume. ### Across the Region
**Ethiopia's Catholic Bishops issue pastoral appeal for peace and reconciliation: Vatican News**
The Catholic Bishops’ Conference of Ethiopia, in a letter issued on Thursday, said the Church grieves over armed conflict in Tigray, Afar, Amhara, Oromia and other regions. The bishops said war deepens divisions, takes lives, devastates communities and property, and sows hatred that may last for generations. They echoed Pope Leo, who at his Wednesday General Audience said he was following renewed hostilities in Tigray and neighbouring Afar and Amhara with deep concern, and asked all parties to choose dialogue. The bishops said peace is a shared responsibility, and that when violence erupts the greatest suffering falls on children, women, the elderly and the sick. Source: [vaticannews.va](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/church/news/2026-10/ethiopia-bishops-issue-pastoral-appeal-for-peace-reconciliation.html)

**Metier re-ups on South Africa's Frogfoot and Vox for fibre push: Dealroom**
Private equity firm Metier has reinvested in fibre operator Frogfoot and internet provider Vox through its Metier Capital Growth Fund III, leading a consortium of co-investors. The two privately held companies share a shareholder base: Frogfoot builds and operates open-access fibre, and Vox sells internet, voice, cloud and cybersecurity to households, enterprises and public-sector clients. The capital is meant to speed rollout into South Africa’s underserved regions, pairing Frogfoot’s trenching with Vox’s services on those lines. Grant Howarth, a principal at Metier, said the firm is renewing a commitment after twenty years and will work with new partners on operational synergies in communities’ everyday lives. ### Both Sides: Whether Israel’s football association should file a Uefa racism complaint
The decision in front of the Israel Football Association is whether to lodge a racism complaint with Uefa over remarks allegedly made by Republic of Ireland players in last week’s 3-0 Nations League win. The rematch is on Sunday in Serbia.

An Israeli journalist put the case for a complaint in the pre-match press conference. He accused Ireland’s players of racist remarks, insisted the allegations were true, and refused to give up the microphone when Football Association of Ireland officials tried to take it. The Israel Football Association has not filed. It said it would consider a Uefa complaint after Sunday’s match.

The case against treating the allegation as established came in the same room. An Irish press officer said, “That is completely untrue,” and the exchange ended manager Heimir Hallgrimsson’s conference. Hallgrimsson later told Irish media he had not read the claims and did not want to get involved. He said something was reported by both teams, that it goes to a process in the Irish department and with Uefa, and that he hoped the focus could stay on the game.

Both sides accept that reports exist and that Uefa is the body that would handle them. The next step the Israel Football Association named is its own: consider whether to complain after Sunday’s match.
