### Lead
**Pezeshkian pitches a 'CIS + Iran' bloc at the Turkmenistan summit: Middle East Eye**
Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian, in his first appearance as a special guest at the Commonwealth of Independent States summit in Turkmenistan, called for a new "CIS + Iran" format and pitched Tehran as the region's gateway to open waters. He said war, external pressure and disrupted trade routes make regional resilience more urgent, called Iran a "natural route" to the sea for Central Asia, and said southern ports, notably Chabahar, could link the region to South Asia, Africa and Europe. "Our common interests lie not in competition among routes, but in connecting them," he said. He backed Turkmenistan's UN proposal for a Caspian Sea transport corridor linking Central Asia, South Asia and Europe, and said it could be built using Iranian infrastructure. He condemned US and Israeli attacks on Iran, said Tehran remained open to dialogue, and set one condition: "Negotiation under the shadow of force is not negotiation; it is imposition and coercion." Sanctions, he warned, would harm not only Iran but regional trade, energy and connectivity. **UAE prosecutor says flydubai co-pilot aimed at Tel Aviv airport: IraqiNews**
The UAE's top prosecutor said Friday that the co-pilot who attacked his captain on a flydubai jet last month had planned to crash the plane into Tel Aviv's international airport. Findings published by state media say the Omani co-pilot, now in custody and identified in reports as Hamam al-Hammami, drew on Al-Qaeda's September 11 attacks and sought the greatest possible loss of life. The prosecutor said he was radicalised while studying in Australia, that this was his third flight to Tel Aviv after two planning trips, and that licence, conduct and security checks had shown no sign of those views; he had previously worked for Oman Air and Royal Air Maroc. On the Dubai-Tel Aviv flight he assaulted Indian captain Smit Machchhar, who opened the cockpit door; passengers and crew overpowered him, and an off-duty pilot landed the aircraft in Saudi Arabia after passengers described a drop of about 5,000 metres. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has said the co-pilot must have got around rules barring crews from countries that do not recognise Israel, and last week called the case Islamist radical indoctrination. ### The Region and the World
**Fire-damaged HMM boxship seeks a South African port: Splash 24/7**
The 5,593 teu HMM Platinum, a Liberian-flagged ship built in 2013, is seeking a port of refuge in South Africa after a fire on a voyage from Kattupalli, India, toward Santos, Brazil. The blaze reportedly began in a container off southern Madagascar; cargo claims specialist WK Webster places it off Lavanono and says some cargo is temperature-sensitive. Claims specialist W.E. Cox said the ship was carrying about 2,300 containers and warned that damage could be substantial. Salvage firm SMIT has been engaged under Lloyd's Open Form, and a declaration of general average is considered likely. The ship had been expected in Durban on October 8; Gqeberha, formerly Port Elizabeth, is now identified as a possible refuge. ### Both Sides: Should Ghana treat moral formation as the answer to school indiscipline?
Ghana's minister of education, concerned about rising indiscipline, convened a National Conference on July 29 and 30 and invited stakeholders, including Church representatives, to discuss the causes. Bishop Matthew Kwasi Gyamfi, president of the Ghana Catholic Bishops' Conference and local ordinary of Sunyani, says that meeting should not have been necessary.

Gyamfi's case is that the cause is already known. Discipline and human formation do not happen by accident, he says; children must be taught values alongside academic excellence, with people deliberately guiding them. Academic education alone, he argues, does not make responsible members of society. If children are not taught those values and are left on their own, no one should be surprised when indiscipline follows. He said he found it disheartening that a minister would not know why indiscipline is rising, and he questioned inviting the Church and people who, he says, had been sent away after helping to instill discipline, to discuss a problem whose answer he calls obvious. He shared that reflection with ACI Africa on Friday, October 9.

The ministry's case is the step Gyamfi describes: the minister treated the rise as serious enough to call churches and other stakeholders together specifically to discuss the causes. That is a case for examining the problem with the people who work with students, rather than closing it before the conference.

Both treat rising school indiscipline as the problem in front of them. Gyamfi does not dispute the minister's concern; he disputes the ministry's grasp of the cause. Source: [aciafrica.org](https://www.aciafrica.org/news/24597/ghana-catholic-bishops-president-links-rising-school-indiscipline-to-neglect-of-moral-formation)
