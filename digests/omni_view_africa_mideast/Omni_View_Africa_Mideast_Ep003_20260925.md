### Lead
**France to protect key Saudi oil terminal as Houthis step up missile attacks: South China Morning Post**

President Emmanuel Macron said Thursday that France will send soldiers, radar systems and defence systems to protect the Saudi oil port of Yanbu on the Red Sea, and that French forces will not take part in combat. Saudi Arabia said it intercepted missiles fired toward Yanbu and the city of Taif. Saudi authorities reported intercepting six ballistic missiles, while the Houthis said they launched dozens of missiles and drones on Thursday. The kingdom had issued emergency warnings for Mecca, Jeddah and Yanbu. Saudi Arabia's foreign ministry said Friday that the kingdom, Turkey and Pakistan will hold an urgent meeting of their chiefs of staff on support for Riyadh. Under the Mecca Agreement, the ministry says an attack on one party is an attack on all. The three foreign ministers have already met, condemned attacks on Mecca and other civilian sites, and reaffirmed Saudi Arabia's right to self-defense. Saudi Arabia's Grand Mufti called on the military to be ready to give their lives until the Houthis are removed from power, the state news agency said late Thursday. Source: [scmp.com](https://www.scmp.com/news/world/middle-east/article/3368772/france-protect-key-saudi-oil-terminal-houthis-step-missile-attacks)

### The Region and the World
**Colombia formally cuts ties with Iran: Middle East Eye**

Colombia's foreign ministry said Thursday that the country has formally severed diplomatic relations with Iran, with the decision taking effect on Saturday. The ministry cited national and hemispheric security concerns. It accused Iran of human rights violations and alleged links to international terrorist and narcoterrorist groups. It also cited confirmed human rights violations, attacks on countries not involved in the Middle East conflict, the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, and obstruction of International Atomic Energy Agency efforts to verify Iran's nuclear programme. The ministry said those facts had deteriorated Colombia's trust in the government in Tehran. Source: [middleeasteye.net](https://www.middleeasteye.net/live-blog/live-blog-update/colombia-formally-cuts-ties-iran)

### Both Sides: Whether talks wait on Washington meeting Iran's conditions

The question is whether formal negotiations to end the war, and any reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, wait until Washington meets Iran's conditions.

Iran's case, as its president states it, is that the choice sits in Washington. Masoud Pezeshkian told Fox News that America must choose whether it wants to end the war, after he was asked if it could end by the end of the year. The National reports that Tehran has hardened its position on the strait and insists Washington meet its conditions before formal negotiations resume. The Washington Post reports that Iran says it has given the Trump administration a new proposal for peace talks, and The New York Times reports that Iran has proposed a seven-day plan to end the war. The strongest reason on this side is sequence: Iranian officials are putting a proposal forward and describing an end to the fighting as America's decision, with conditions before formal talks.

The case for not waiting on that sequence comes from governments treating the closure as the emergency. The National reports that about 80 countries have backed a call for the urgent reopening of the waterway, and that diplomacy at the UN General Assembly has yet to produce a breakthrough. The United Arab Emirates has suspended all flights operated by Iranian airlines to and from the country until further notice. Colombia's foreign ministry, in cutting ties, cited the blockade of the strait, obstruction of International Atomic Energy Agency checks, attacks on countries not involved in the conflict, and human rights violations. The strongest reason on this side is that a wide group of states is asking for the waterway to reopen now, while some governments are already cutting flights and relations over the blockade and the war.

Iran's stated conditions and the eighty-country call both treat the strait as not open in the ordinary way, and both sit against talks that The National says have not broken through. The proposal is now with the Trump administration, and the Emirati suspension runs until further notice.
