**Report: Trump rejects Iran's ceasefire proposal, may resume bombing after midterms: Middle East Eye**
Yesterday Iran said it had given Washington a new proposal. Tehran's proposal would have reopened the Strait of Hormuz and resumed nuclear talks in return for the United States lifting its blockade of Iranian ports. That blockade, the report says, is causing deep damage to Iran's economy. ### Across the Region
**UN expands list of companies operating in West Bank settlements: Middle East Eye**
The United Nations human rights office on Friday added 61 companies to its database of firms operating in Israeli West Bank settlements, which the UN considers illegal. First released in 2020, the database now lists 214 firms, mostly from Israel, and also from 10 other countries including France, Germany, China and the United States. Newly added names include Israeli pesticides maker ADAMA, part of Chinese-owned Syngenta Group, the Israel-based real estate holding company Alony Hetz, and Spain's Salvat Logistica. Those firms did not immediately respond to a request for comment. They join companies already listed, including Expedia and Airbnb, on a database the UN human rights office describes as highly sensitive because listed firms could face boycotts or divestment over settler expansion in territory home to more than 3 million Palestinians. Source: [middleeasteye.net](https://www.middleeasteye.net/live-blog/live-blog-update/un-adds-61-new-israeli-companies-operating-illegal-west-bank-settlements)

### The Region and the World
**Chinese VP Han Zheng pledges to deepen ties with Ghana, Cyprus: CGTN**
Chinese Vice President Han Zheng told Ghanaian President John Dramani Mahama in New York on Friday that China is ready to steadily deepen the China-Ghana strategic partnership. The meeting was held on the sidelines of the 81st session of the United Nations General Assembly. Han said China will advance cooperation in mining, green energy, vocational training and youth development, and he asked Ghana to keep a favorable environment for Chinese firms and to use China's zero-tariff policy. Mahama said Ghana adheres to the one-China policy and wants stronger ties in trade, investment, mining and manufacturing, and more people-to-people exchanges. In a separate meeting the same day, Han told Cypriot President Nikos Christodoulides that China is also ready to deepen that strategic partnership. ### Both Sides: Further force after launches toward Saudi cities
The contested question is whether Saudi Arabia should answer the latest launches with further force.

Gulf Cooperation Council Secretary General Jasem Al Budaiwi called missile and drone attacks on Riyadh and Khamis Mushait a serious breach of the kingdom's sovereignty and a direct threat to civilians and regional stability. His case for a hard response is that duty to protect: he said member states fully support any measures Saudi Arabia takes to protect its security, sovereignty and territorial integrity, and he asked the international community to take a firm stance to end repeated Houthi attacks. Coalition spokesman Turki Al-Maliki said air defences shot down two drones launched toward the Riyadh area and two ballistic missiles targeting Khamis Mushait.

The Houthis' stated case is that the war is already being fought on Yemeni ground. They said on Friday that Saudi warplanes launched 14 airstrikes and missile attacks in the previous 24 hours on Taiz, Amran, Marib and Saada. Yahoo News reported that Saudi return strikes have not stopped the Houthis from seizing large stretches of territory in Yemen this month. The United Nations said the escalation has displaced more than 122,000 people, and that between 6 August and 18 September, 674 people died and 2,998 were injured.

Each side's account includes the other's fire: launches toward Saudi cities, and Saudi strikes inside Yemen. The Associated Press said it could not immediately independently verify the latest interception claim, and that no Houthi statement of responsibility had yet appeared. No article sets a date for talks. The step named now is the Council's call for an international response.
