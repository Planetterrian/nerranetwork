### Lead
**Economic fire — Interest rate adjustment makes for interesting braai chat: Daily Maverick**
The Monetary Policy Committee of the South African Reserve Bank voted unanimously to raise the repo rate by twenty five basis points from seven percent to seven point two five percent. The prime lending rate rose automatically from ten point five percent to ten point seven five percent. Headline inflation reached four point four percent in August while the central bank projects it will peak at five point seven percent in November. Services inflation stood at five point one percent in August. The economy contracted zero point two percent in the second quarter of twenty twenty six, prompting the bank to lower its full year GDP growth forecast to one point two percent. Market analysts viewed the move as a preventive step to anchor expectations rather than the start of a longer tightening cycle. Chief Economist Johann Els at PSG Financial Services noted the unanimous vote was more hawkish than expected. Supply side price shocks are initially inflationary but ultimately deflationary for demand and growth according to Els. The adjustment marks the second such increase since the government ended austerity measures. The decision came the day before Heritage Day when many South Africans gather for braais. Archbishop Desmond Tutu had earlier linked the day to braai celebrations which boosted retail sales. The bank cited elevated long term inflation expectations above its three percent midpoint target as a key factor. ### Across the Region
**CAR Rebel Commander Convicted of Crimes Against Humanity: AllAfrica**
The International Criminal Court convicted Central African Republic Seleka rebel commander Mahamat Said Abdel Kani on four counts including torture and persecution for acts during the twenty thirteen conflict. Prosecutors said Said ran a Bangui prison where suspected supporters of former president François Bozizé were beaten and held in a windowless cell beneath his office floorboards. Central African Republic Justice Minister Arnaud Djoubaye Abazène welcomed the ruling and said no victim will be left behind. The twenty thirteen seizure of power by predominantly Muslim Seleka rebels triggered opposition from mainly Christian anti Balaka militias. Detainees testified about beatings and harsh conditions in the prison. The conviction covers events from the height of the conflict that ousted President Bozizé. **Drone strikes kill 10 at Sudan court and college: Business Day**
Sudan's paramilitary Rapid Support Forces carried out drone attacks on government held towns in North Kordofan state. One strike hit a crowded courtroom in Um Ruwaba on September eight and killed about ten people while injuring dozens according to Emergency Lawyers. A second strike the next day hit a college in al Obeid killing a security guard and damaging eight classrooms. The dean of the college said the entire faculty felt destroyed. A student who had taken exams the previous day described her future as destroyed. The United Nations reported that drone attacks by both sides killed more than one thousand civilians in the first half of the year. Residents described the strikes as part of a new campaign after weeks of reduced activity. The Rapid Support Forces said it does not intentionally target civilians. A hospital patient in Um Ruwaba who fled earlier bombardment distinguished between strategic and suicide drones. ### Both Sides: Ghana maritime surveillance effectiveness
Ghana's opposition and the Ghana Maritime Authority disagree on whether the Vessel Traffic Management Information System remains fully operational for monitoring the country's waters. Miracles Aboagye of the New Patriotic Party argues the system has been down for months at multiple coastal sites and that reliance on the Skylight alternative leaves smaller vessels and activities outside port limits undetected increasing risks of narcotics movement. The Ghana Maritime Authority states its systems remain operational for monitoring activities within Ghana’s maritime space. Both sides accept that continuous detection capability matters for intercepting illicit consignments. The next step is continued public statements from both the authority and opposition figures without a scheduled resolution date. Aboagye specifically named sites at Ada Winneba Axim Keta and Half Assini as affected. He said the CCTV cameras at those sites were also not functioning. The authority issued a statement on September twenty three rejecting the claims of downtime.
