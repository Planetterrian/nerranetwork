### Lead
**Wong thanks ‘heroes’ who thwarted flydubai pilot’s alleged attack on co-pilot as Australians confirmed safe: The Guardian**
On 30 September, Flydubai flight FZ1073, travelling from Dubai to Tel Aviv, diverted to Saudi Arabia after one pilot allegedly stabbed the other and the aircraft plunged about 17,000 feet in two minutes, The Guardian reported; Israeli officials said the accused pilot was an Omani citizen. Passenger Yaniv Hayoun told Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu he put the attacker in a chokehold and dragged him from the cockpit, saying the man was trying to destroy the instruments to disable the plane; the BBC named the stabbed pilot as Indian captain Smit Machchhar, whom Israel’s prime minister hailed. Flydubai said flights to and from Israel are suspended while the investigation continues and that the motive remains unclear. Netanyahu told CNN Israel had information that Iran’s proxies were planning attacks but no specific warning about this flight, and that it was too early to say who was behind it. After speaking with Netanyahu, President Donald Trump said the co-pilot “was perhaps a terrorist, or perhaps a whack-job, and he stabbed the pilot,” while France 24 reported that Israel’s defence minister labelled the incident a thwarted terror attack. The US State Department said four Americans were aboard and that US diplomats in the kingdom are in contact with Saudi investigators. Foreign Minister Penny Wong said at least two Australians are safe and thanked the “heroes” who thwarted the attack; Israel’s embassy in Australia said they are a Jewish couple from Sydney, and ambassador Hillel Newman called the incident an attempt to hit the Abraham Accords, which normalised UAE-Israel relations in 2020. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/01/australian-couple-on-flydubai-plane-to-israel-ntwnfb)

### Across the Region
**Tinubu: Nigeria’s Decades-Long Economic Problems Cannot Be Erased In Four Years: Arise News**
President Bola Tinubu, marking 66 years of independence on 1 October, said hardship since his reforms is real, but that problems accumulated over decades cannot be erased in four years, Arise News reported. He said his administration inherited a deeply distressed economy, that the period of emergency treatment is over, and that Nigeria has entered an “age of prosperity.” He called advocates of a return to petrol subsidies “influential but regressive voices.” Arise News reported petrol at about 195 naira a litre when he took office in May 2023 and around 1,500 naira now, a rise of more than 650 percent, and the dollar from about 460 naira to around 1,500. Source: [arise.tv](https://www.arise.tv/tinubu-nigerias-decades-long-economic-problems-cannot-be-erased-in-four-years/)

**Zimbabwe tycoon Wicknell Chivayo and wife killed in helicopter crash: BBC Africa**
Businessman Wicknell Chivayo and his wife, Lucy Muteke, were killed when a helicopter crashed on Wednesday evening in the Marondera rural area, about 70 kilometres east of Harare, the BBC reported. Police said all six people on board died; Commissioner Paul Nyathi put the time at about 17:00 local time, government officials said two of the six were pilots, and spokesman Nick Mangwana said there were no survivors. Family spokesperson Herbert Chivayo confirmed the deaths to ZBC, which said the group left Gandami in Chikomba, where the couple were building a rural home, and crashed at Kudenga Farm about 40 minutes after take-off. Police said the cause was not yet known and that investigations were under way. Chivayo, managing director of Intratrek Zimbabwe, had denied allegations of wrongdoing over dealings that included a 172 million dollar solar project in Gwanda, awarded by the state-owned Zimbabwe Power Company and disputed in court for years, and scrutiny of materials procured for the disputed 2023 elections. **Women Entrepreneurs: 60 Join China-Backed South Africa Training: Grit Daily News**
A two-week training programme for women entrepreneurs opened in Johannesburg on Tuesday with more than 60 participants, Grit Daily News reported, citing Xinhua; it is part of the Global Female Entrepreneurs Empowerment Program, led by Beijing Foreign Studies University. ### Both Sides: Whether Botswana should kill elephants for an independence feast
Botswana’s government has authorised the killing of 23 elephants, and the hunting of other wildlife including kudu, impala and wildebeest, so meat can be distributed across eight districts for celebrations of the country’s 60th anniversary of independence. The decision under debate is whether that cull should stand as a national feast.

Supporters of Botswana’s hunting policy say international criticism does not fully account for rural communities living beside large elephant populations. Their strongest reason is harm close to home: elephants can destroy crops and property and endanger people, especially as expanding settlements push communities closer to wildlife. The government defends regulated hunting as a way to manage that human-wildlife conflict and to generate funds for communities and conservation. AllAfrica reported that Botswana has more than 130,000 elephants.

The Elephant Protection Society warns that using elephants for celebratory feasts could undermine conservation efforts and encourage poaching. That is the core case against: a state-authorised feast, on this view, treats elephants as festival meat and can signal that killing them is acceptable. Alongside that warning, the decision has raised concerns over hygiene and disease during a foot-and-mouth outbreak, and local officials are calling for the meat to be processed and distributed more safely.

Both sides are working from the same official act: the government approved the culling of 23 elephants ahead of the anniversary, with the meat intended for distribution. The reporting names no court date and no reversal. What it records next is the officials’ call for safer processing, set against the society’s warning that a celebratory kill can weaken conservation and feed poaching. Source: [allafrica.com](https://allafrica.com/stories/202610010097.html)
