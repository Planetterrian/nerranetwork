### Across the Region
**Nigeria: How Nigeria's Population Commission Mismanaged N245 Billion On Undelivered Products, Other Controversial Contracts - Auditor-General: AllAfrica**
The Auditor-General for the Federation says the National Population Commission awarded and paid for census-related contracts without adequate evidence of delivery, inflated some prices, and breached procurement rules. The findings, in the Auditor-General's 2024 Annual Report on Non-Compliance, submitted to the National Assembly in July, cover the 2023 financial year and involve more than N245 billion in contracts and payments. The largest item is N131.56 billion paid for 800,000 personal digital assistants and accessories for the planned 2023 population and housing census; contracts worth N131 billion went to six contractors between 1 December 2022 and 2 May 2023. The office blamed weaknesses in the commission's internal controls and recommended that the director-general account to the National Assembly's Public Accounts Committees and recover billions of naira for the Treasury. In several cases the commission did not answer the queries, and the auditor-general said the findings stay valid until the recommendations are carried out. Ojogun Osifo, appointed director-general by the late former President Muhammadu Buhari in August 2022, left when his tenure expired in July 2026; President Bola Tinubu then appointed Abuh Muhammed. Source: [allafrica.com](https://allafrica.com/stories/202610080094.html)

**Nigeria: Nigeria's Economic Growth Insufficient to Reduce Poverty - Report: AllAfrica**
The World Bank's October 2026 Africa Economic Update, "Building AI Readiness," says growth in Sub-Saharan Africa is expected to rise to 4.3 percent in 2026, helped by external demand and favourable prices for oil, minerals and other commodities. Resource-rich economies such as Nigeria are expected to benefit, but the bank cautions that extractive growth often has weak links to jobs and household incomes. Per capita GDP growth is projected at 1.8 percent in 2026, which the bank says is too modest to mark a real gain in living standards. Using a poverty line of 3 US dollars a person a day at 2021 purchasing power parity, the regional poverty rate is projected at 47.8 percent in 2026 and 47.1 percent in 2027. The bank says poverty stays broadly stagnant over 2022–27, while food, energy and transport costs keep squeezing poor households. ### The Region and the World
**EAM Jaishankar Calls Ghana, India "Champions of Global South": Open Magazine**
India's external affairs minister, S. Jaishankar, met Ghana's foreign minister, Samuel Okudzeto Ablakwa, in Accra and said the visit followed Prime Minister Modi's trip last year. He called India and Ghana historic partners and "champions of the Global South," and said the two should find new areas inside an established comprehensive partnership. During the visit the two sides signed agreements on agricultural research and training and on biodiversity. They are also looking at pharmaceuticals and healthcare, mining, defence, railways and the digital sector. Jaishankar pointed to Ghana's Accra Reset health programme beside India's Ayushman Bharat, Feed Ghana beside India's farmer and millet programmes, and Ghana's target of one million coders beside Digital India and Startup India. ### Both Sides: Should Washington prefer Iran's collapse to a deal?
The question is whether the United States should treat Iran's collapse as better than a negotiated end to the war. President Donald Trump said envoy Steve Witkoff is "doing very well" in discussions with Iran, but that a deal with Tehran is not his preferred outcome. He said the war "ends" and "it's going to be very soon," and called Iran "a failed nation." A White House official told Al Jazeera that Trump will not let Tehran "manipulate" him as he says it did previous presidents, and is "comfortable letting Iran's inevitable collapse take its course," because the United States "controlled the Strait of Hormuz and Iran's economy was collapsing."

Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson, Esmaeil Baghaei, said Tehran has "clearly and firmly" set conditions for ending what he called the imposed war and for restoring security to the Persian Gulf and the Strait of Hormuz, and will answer US proposals through mediators. Ali Mohammadi, deputy political commander of the IRGC Navy, rejected US claims that oil and ships are moving through the strait, saying the oil market is not reacting and "understands that you are lying." He said the force has "full oversight" of the Gulf, the strait and the Gulf of Oman, and asked why US troops stayed "600 to 700km" away if the IRGC Navy had been destroyed. US Central Command said Tehran's control of the strait is "false," that the United States and regional partners "clearly control" it, and that traffic is flowing "right now."

Trump and Baghaei both speak of ending the war, and both treat the strait as central; they do not agree who controls it, or whether a deal should come before collapse.
