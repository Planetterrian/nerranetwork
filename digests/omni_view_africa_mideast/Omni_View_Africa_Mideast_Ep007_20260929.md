### Across the Region
**Kenya: Court Halts Dangote Refinery Groundbreaking in Lamu: AllAfrica**
A Malindi Environment and Land Court has temporarily stopped planned construction of the Dangote refinery in Lamu County. Justice Jane Onyango certified as urgent a petition by Salim Tima Swale and 132 others and ordered that the status quo on L.R. No. 13061, in the Hindi/Manda Magogoni area, be maintained until October 14, 2026. The order bars the respondents from clearing, excavating, fencing, demolishing, constructing on, transferring, disposing of, or otherwise interfering with portions occupied by the petitioners. The 133 petitioners say they are long-standing occupants, cultivators and farmers with homes, crops and trees, and that their interests arise from occupation and use, not only from formal registration, and should be recognised in any compulsory acquisition. They had asked the court to restrain the Office of the President, Dangote Industries, the Lamu County Government and other respondents from a groundbreaking ceremony scheduled for Wednesday, September 30, 2026. The orders stay in force pending further directions. Source: [allafrica.com](https://allafrica.com/stories/202609290058.html)

### The Region and the World
**FAO urges more funding for Sudan as El Niño threatens food security in the region: China Daily**
The Food and Agriculture Organization is urging more money for agriculture and emergency food production in Sudan, where needs far outstrip available funding, director Rein Paulsen told China Daily in Nairobi after visiting Sudan last week. FAO is concentrating on staple crops, livestock health and animal feed; Paulsen said a programme costing about 17 million dollars last year generated more than 500 million dollars' worth of sorghum and about 22 percent of Sudan's national sorghum production. A nationwide livestock vaccination campaign is under way, with feed concentrates and mineral supplements, and Paulsen called for irrigation to be rebuilt so farmers depend less on unreliable rain. He described the anticipated El Niño as a food-security risk multiplier across the Horn. In Sudan, FAO expects rainfall significantly below normal, potentially by 30 to 40 percent, and possibly at times unsuitable for planting; in Somalia the concern is excessive rain and flooding in the Jubba and Shabelle basins. ### Both Sides: Whether Serbia's court deposit is enough after extraditing a Bahraini asylum seeker
Ahmed Jaafar Mohamed Ali has been in Bahrain's Jau Prison since January 2022, after Serbia extradited him while he was seeking asylum. The Council of Europe's Committee of Ministers is pressing Serbia to carry out a March 2025 European Court of Human Rights judgment. What is being decided is whether placing the damages in a domestic account discharges Serbia, or whether Belgrade must still act so the money reaches Ali and his situation after extradition is faced.

The case for further action is put by the Committee of Ministers and by Sayed Ahmed Alwadaei, director of advocacy at the Bahrain Institute for Rights and Democracy. The court held that Serbia violated Article 3 of the European Convention on Human Rights, which bars torture and inhuman or degrading treatment, by extraditing Ali in defiance of an interim measure and without examining his complaint that he would face ill-treatment in Bahrain. The committee says Serbia should cooperate with Ali's representatives and the Secretariat so the 9,800 euro award, plus any tax and default interest, can be released without delay. Alwadaei says Serbia cannot take Bahrain's assurances at face value and must do what it can to protect Ali. Ali, in an account Middle East Eye says came from a recent call, describes beatings, an overcrowded cell in Building Two, hunger strikes and solitary confinement. His lawyers say the Interpol red notice used to arrest him in November 2021 was unlawfully issued. Campaigners say a private plane chartered by UAE-based Royal Jet took him to Bahrain three days after the interim measure.

The step Serbia has taken, as the committee records it, is that a sum for Ali has been placed in a deposit account in a domestic court. The committee also records the lawyers' point that releasing those funds is difficult because of Ali's situation in Jau Prison. He is held there on two life sentences. He fled Bahrain in 2011, lived in Iran, and travelled to Serbia to seek asylum.

Both records accept the January 2022 extradition, the two life sentences, the March 2025 finding against Serbia, and the 9,800 euro award. The committee's stated next step is cooperation so the funds can be released without delay. Source: [middleeasteye.net](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/council-of-europe-serbia-implement-judgement-bahrain-political-prisoner)
