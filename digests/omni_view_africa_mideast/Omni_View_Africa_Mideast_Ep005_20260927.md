### Lead
**27 killed in two mass shootings in South Africa: UA.NEWS**
Twenty-seven people were killed and another twenty-six injured in two mass shootings west of Johannesburg and in Cape Town, UA.NEWS reports, citing Deutsche Welle and South African police. In a bar west of Johannesburg, armed men killed seventeen visitors and wounded fifteen on Saturday at about 9:30 p.m. local time, and emergency services pronounced all seventeen dead at the scene. Deutsche Welle, citing South African police, reports that the attackers searched several visitors, took their mobile phones and left in two unidentified vehicles, and that the suspects may have been armed with AK-47 assault rifles and pistols. At an entertainment venue in Lwandle, Cape Town, armed men killed ten people and wounded eleven; police said five of those killed died at the scene, and the shooting began at 1:46 a.m. Police are searching for the attackers. Source: [ua.news](https://ua.news/en/world/u-par-u-dvokh-masovikh-strilianinakh-zaginuli-27-liudei-deutsche-welle)

**President Sheikh Mohamed holds talks with senior Uzbek official in Abu Dhabi: The National**
President Sheikh Mohamed held talks in Abu Dhabi on Saturday with Saida Mirziyoyeva, head of the administration of the president of Uzbekistan, aimed at advancing ties, The National reports. They explored ways to expand cooperation in support of shared development goals. Ms Mirziyoyeva delivered Uzbekistan’s address at the UN General Assembly in New York this week, stressing that the UN should work more effectively with Central Asia and stating that Uzbekistan was committed to peace and global cooperation. The Abu Dhabi meeting was attended by Sheikh Saif bin Zayed, deputy prime minister and minister of the interior; Sheikh Hamdan bin Mohamed, deputy chairman of the Presidential Court for Special Affairs; and Sheikh Mohammed bin Hamad, adviser to the UAE president. In January 2025 the two countries formalised a strategic partnership, and Etihad Airways launched daily direct flights between Abu Dhabi and Tashkent last month; the airline said the inaugural service was sold out. ### Both Sides: Whether Israel should open a West Bank war before the election
Whether Israel should go to war in the West Bank, and annex land, before next month’s election is a question Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich has pressed in public. Israel’s attorney general has, separately, moved to keep one settlement step off the campaign calendar.

Smotrich’s case, made on a Ynet podcast and reported by Middle East Eye, starts from his claim that he has solutions. He said Israel should go to war in Judea and Samaria, do there what it did in Gaza, and dismantle the Palestinian Authority, which he called a terror authority. He also called for annexing southern Lebanon and the areas of Gaza still under Israeli control. He has previously urged annexation of more than 80 percent of the West Bank, and his campaign for next month’s election has often demanded a large expansion of settlements. The reason he gives is that the Authority is, in his description, a terror authority, and that the approach used in Gaza should be repeated there.

The strongest contrary step in today’s reporting is procedural. Middle East Eye reports that Israel’s attorney general ordered a one-month postponement of an E1 settlement construction tender in the West Bank so it would not be used for electioneering. That order does not, in the reporting, answer the war call; it delays a settlement tender so it cannot serve the campaign.
