**Gaza hostage deal could have been secured a year earlier, Israeli general says: Middle East Eye**
Israeli Reserve Major General Nitzan Alon, the former government point person on hostage negotiations, said Israeli hostages in Gaza could have been brought home up to a year earlier than their release in late 2025. Speaking on Friday in a conversation hosted by the Washington Institute for Near East Policy, he said political and personal considerations got in the way. He said Israel could have secured the release of all the hostages a year before the final deal without any impact on the outcome of the war. Alon has accused the government several times over the past year of delaying or preventing hostage releases, and he has clashed publicly with ministers over responsibility for the deaths of hostages in captivity and the prolonging of the war in Gaza. He called for new, realistic Israeli leadership. ### The Region and the World
**Gulf nations urged to form ecological union as study suggest Iran war caused 'unprecedented' damage: The National**
A study by Dr Devaraj Asir Ramesh of Anna University in Chennai, in the International Journal of Research and Innovation in Social Sciences, describes unprecedented ecological disruption across the Gulf from the Iran war, which the report says began at the end of February when the United States and Israel launched strikes on Iran. The research, covering tanker strikes, port attacks and refinery explosions, says the Arabian Gulf's sensitive ecosystems were severely impacted, with fewer species in some areas. Dr Ramesh writes that Iranian strikes on the Emirates were likely to have contaminated seagrass beds with hydrocarbons and caused auditory damage to cetaceans, and that oil from attacks on tankers at Fujairah could have caused further harm; he says oil spills, chemical leaks and acoustic disturbances have impaired biodiversity, fisheries, aquaculture, desalination and eco-tourism. He urges coral regeneration at Musandam, mangrove planting at Ras Al Khor in Dubai and seagrass restoration off Abu Dhabi, and suggests Gulf nations form an ecological union. Dr Manjula Nair of Heriot-Watt University Dubai says oil leakages infiltrated Iran's Siri and Qeshm islands, home to coral reefs, mangrove forests and sandy beaches, that ongoing military activity has made the spills hard to contain, and that the pollution reaches water security. ### Both Sides: Whether Saudi Arabia should sack Georgios Donis after a loss to Qatar
Saudi Arabia meets Qatar in the semi-final of Gulf Cup 27, and Goal.com treats that match as the moment the Saudi Arabian Football Federation may have to decide the future of Greek coach Georgios Donis, not merely as a ticket to the final.

The case for removing him if the team loses starts with the stakes that piece sets out. A defeat, Goal.com says, would leave the federation facing a difficult decision, and sacking Donis would become a very real possibility because of how much rides on the upcoming phase. To some, the analysis says, a dismissal might look like a direct fix for the crisis on the bench.

The case against a change is the risk Goal.com places beside that fix. Changing the coach now will not necessarily drag Saudi Arabia out of its crisis. It could return the team to what it lived through recently, when Frenchman Hervé Renard walked away a short time before the World Cup and left Donis to pick up the pieces. Every shake-up, the piece says, hands the players fresh ideas and a different approach to absorb, and that churn could delay the form needed at major tournaments. A dismissal would not simply swap one name on the bench. It would launch a new project at the most sensitive time, while Saudi Arabia still needs a clear system before its biggest occasion.

Both readings accept that this semi-final is pivotal for Donis, and that a win would give him far more room to continue his project, with the title itself widening that room further. The next step the article names is the match: beat Qatar and the final becomes the next chapter; lose, and the question of whether he stays is back with the federation.
