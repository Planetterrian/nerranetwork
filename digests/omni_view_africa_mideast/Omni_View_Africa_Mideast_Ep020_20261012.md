### Lead
**Airstrike kills 72 Sudanese workers at Egypt-Sudan border gold mine: Pajhwok Afghan News**
An airstrike on Saturday killed at least 72 Sudanese workers at a gold mine near the Egypt-Sudan border, Pajhwok Afghan News reported, citing Agence France-Presse. Emergency Lawyers, which documents abuses in Sudan’s war, accused the Egyptian military of carrying out the attack. AFP said it had been unable to independently verify that allegation, and the Egyptian government had not issued an official comment. Two survivors said many workers were at the site, that people fled toward the mountains, and that they had seen bodies and feared others were still under the rubble. The report called this the second attack this year on an informal gold-mining site in Jabal al-Uqaydat; a similar attack in June killed 23 workers. ### Across the Region
**As tensions soar in the African horn, Egypt prepares to deploy troops to Somalia: Hiiraan Online**
Technical military teams from Egypt, including advisers, trainers and officers, are reported to be preparing the first deployment of Egyptian army troops to Somalia since a joint military cooperation agreement in 2024. The move is described as coming under bilateral security cooperation and the African Union mission AUSSOM. The troops are reported to be headed for Ceelcade, site of the former Cisiley Airfield on the northern outskirts of Mogadishu in Middle Shabelle, about 400 kilometres from the nearest point on Ethiopia’s border with Somalia. Somali analyst Hassan Muhammad told The New Arab he hopes the deployment strengthens Somali security institutions, and that success depends on clear goals, full coordination with Somali institutions, and respect for Somalia’s sovereignty. An Egyptian delegation met Defence Minister Ahmed Moallim Fiqi in Mogadishu on 20 September. **Kenya approves $350 million rescue to get grounded planes flying as national airline faces $941 million debt overhaul: Business Insider Africa**
On 9 October the Cabinet approved $350 million in shareholder financing for Kenya Airways, to meet urgent obligations and return grounded aircraft to service. The money is to be released in instalments under National Treasury oversight, repaid over up to 10 years, and could later be converted into equity if the required approvals are given. A separate proposal would convert about $941 million in existing government loans into an equity-qualifying tradable instrument, not a further cash injection. The government says the carrier contributes more than $1.3 billion a year through tourism, trade and regional connectivity; corporate, shareholder and regulatory approvals are still required. Kenya Airways reported a first-half 2026 pre-tax loss of about $123 million, and said fuel costs rose 72 percent and accounted for as much as half of total costs after the Middle East conflict disrupted fuel markets and delayed maintenance and spare parts. **World Bank mobilising $6 billion for Nigeria targeting agriculture, MSMEs, 850,000 jobs: Peoples Gazette Nigeria**
The World Bank Group is mobilising $6 billion for Nigeria, aimed at agriculture, micro, small and medium enterprises, and 850,000 jobs. AGROW is expected to mobilise about $220 million from agribusiness firms and a further $22 million from certified seed producers. ### Both Sides: Should the Security Council deter Ethiopia, as Eritrea asks?
Eritrea has formally asked the UN Security Council to take deterrent measures against Ethiopia, accusing Addis Ababa of declaring war, according to FTL Somalia’s account of the standoff. Residents of the Tigrayan town of Adigrat told the BBC that Eritrean forces had entered northern Ethiopia, moving toward areas where clashes are ongoing. Fighting in the region began two weeks ago after months of tension between the Ethiopian government and the Tigray People’s Liberation Front. Each side accuses the other of violating the 2022 agreement that ended a two-year civil war. Ethiopia has accused Eritrea of supporting rebel groups, particularly in Tigray; Eritrea has repeatedly denied those allegations. Addis Ababa has rejected what it describes as political rhetoric, coercive measures, or acts of destabilization from regional actors.

Somalia’s deputy foreign minister, Hassan Mohamed Ali Nadara, told BBC Somali that Mogadishu’s position is one of peace and neutrality, so that Somalia does not add another problem across security, the economy and movement while protecting its own objectives and unity. Asked about President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud’s participation in the Alamein summit with Egypt, Eritrea and Sudan, Nadara rejected the suggestion that the summit shows Somalia is not neutral.

Eritrea’s case for deterrent measures is that Ethiopian conduct amounts to a declaration of war and that its own forces are already moving toward active fighting. Ethiopia’s case against is that pressure of that kind is coercive rhetoric and destabilization, not grounds for UN punishment, while the charge that Eritrea backs rebels is denied in Asmara. Nadara’s case for staying out is that shared borders, historical and religious ties, and existing agreements make a new front more costly than a posture of reducing the crisis. The reporting names Eritrea’s Council request as the step already taken, not a later session date. Source: [ftlsomalia.com](https://www.ftlsomalia.com/somalia-tells-bbc-it-is-neutral-in-ethiopia-eritrea-crisis-analysts-disagree/)
