AllAfrica reports the Department of Home Affairs pulled that intake measure after violent anti-immigration protests in Durban and Soweto.

It had followed a July Constitutional Court judgment in the Scalabrini Centre of Cape Town case.

The court struck down Refugees Act provisions that had stopped some asylum seekers on procedural grounds before anyone weighed their claims on the merits.

People may apply for protection no matter how they entered. The ruling does not itself hand them refugee status or permanent residence.

Home Affairs says the director-general issued the directive without the minister or other political office-bearers knowing.

Refugee Reception Offices were overwhelmed within days. The department points to officials’ safety and public order after vehicles were torched and businesses vandalised.

Gauteng Premier Panyaza Lesufi calls the withdrawal a relief and a chance for further consultation.

The court ruling still stands. The department says it will build a revised operational approach that follows the judgment and deals with those strains.

The case on both sides of ending the war turns on whether Iran must cut enrichment.

U S Vice President JD Vance told Reuters that Iran has to make a meaningful reduction in enrichment capacity to meet American demands and end the war.

His reason is the fuel level. If Iran does not want a nuclear weapon, he asked, why does it need 60 percent enriched fuel, and why build highly enriched fuel if it wants to show it is not building one.

He calls that a basic threshold. Washington stays open to an agreement, he said, but will not trade words for actions, and wants concrete Iranian nuclear concessions.

Iranian lawmaker Alaeddin Boroujerdi told NourNews that Tehran is not aiming to produce nuclear weapons, and that the programme is pursued under the nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty.

His reason for refusing to treat enrichment as a concession to Washington is that those activities are unrelated to the United States, and that Washington should meet its own duties on nuclear arms reduction.

Both treat enrichment as the live issue in whether the war ends by agreement. Neither describes an agreement as already reached.

Vance has set a cut in enrichment, including highly enriched fuel, as the price of satisfying Washington. Boroujerdi has rejected the claim that Iran seeks a weapon and has pointed back to the treaty and to American arms-reduction obligations.
