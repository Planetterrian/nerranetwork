### Lead
**Home Affairs withdraws asylum directive after protests in Durban and Soweto: AllAfrica**
The Department of Home Affairs withdrew a directive on the intake of asylum applications after violent anti-immigration protests in Durban and Soweto. The directive, issued on 28 September, followed a July Constitutional Court judgment in the Scalabrini Centre of Cape Town case. That ruling struck down Refugees Act provisions that had stopped some asylum seekers on procedural grounds before their claims could be assessed on the merits. The judgment lets people apply for protection regardless of how they entered South Africa, but it does not automatically grant refugee status or permanent residence. Home Affairs said the director-general issued the directive without the knowledge of the minister or other political office-bearers, and that Refugee Reception Offices were overwhelmed within days. The department cited officials’ safety and public order after vehicles were torched and businesses vandalised. Gauteng Premier Panyaza Lesufi welcomed the withdrawal as a relief that opens room for further consultation. The withdrawal does not overturn the court ruling, and the department says it will develop a revised operational approach that complies with the judgment while addressing those challenges. ### Both Sides: Should Iran cut enrichment to end the war?
US Vice President JD Vance and Iranian lawmaker Alaeddin Boroujerdi are arguing, in public, over whether Iran must cut nuclear enrichment before the war can end. Vance’s case, given to Reuters, is that Iran must make a meaningful reduction in enrichment capacity to meet US demands and end the war. His stated reason is the fuel level itself: if Iran does not want a nuclear weapon, he asked, why does it need 60 percent enriched fuel, and why build highly enriched fuel if it wants to show it is not building a weapon. He called that a basic threshold, said Washington remains open to an agreement, and said the United States will not trade words for actions. It will require concrete Iranian nuclear concessions.

Boroujerdi’s case, given to NourNews, is that Tehran is not aiming to produce nuclear weapons and that Iran’s programme is pursued under the nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty. His stated reason for refusing to treat enrichment as a concession to Washington is that Iran’s enrichment activities are unrelated to the United States, and that Washington should meet its own obligations on nuclear arms reduction.

Both treat enrichment as the live issue in whether the war ends by agreement, and neither describes an agreement as already reached. Vance has set a reduction in enrichment, including highly enriched fuel, as the price of satisfying Washington. Boroujerdi has rejected the claim that Iran is seeking a weapon and has pointed back to the treaty and to US arms-reduction duties.
