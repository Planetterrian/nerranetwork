## Mira's field note

A study just out in Nature Ecology & Evolution followed threespine stickleback in a wetland formed by the 2011 tsunami on Japan’s northeast coast. Freshwater and marine forms hybridized there, yet within ten generations the marine-derived stretches of the genome had largely vanished, leaving only a few key regions tied to survival in fresh water and the urge to migrate seaward. The work supplies one of the clearest real-time records yet of how reproductive barriers reassert themselves after a sudden environmental jolt.
