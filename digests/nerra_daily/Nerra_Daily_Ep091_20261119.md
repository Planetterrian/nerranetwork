## Mira's field note

Researchers working with a narwhal tusk recently put it under high-resolution x-ray imaging and found an internal layering and mineral pattern no one had mapped before. The scans, reported today by SciTechDaily, show how the tusk grows in distinct annual bands that record changes in the animal’s Arctic environment. The same structure may help explain why the tusk stays straight despite years of use in feeding and social display. Further analysis of those layers is already under way.
