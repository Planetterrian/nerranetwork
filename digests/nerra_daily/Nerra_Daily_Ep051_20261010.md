## Mira's field note

Researchers at UCLA have identified a new low-energy state for working memory in the brain’s entorhinal cortex, one that persists with almost no metabolic cost even while the subject sleeps. Their mathematical model predicted the pattern, and experiments confirmed it doubles memory capacity while halving energy use compared with the usual high-activity form. The work appears in Nature Communications. The team now plans tests in other brain regions to see whether the same efficiency rule holds.
