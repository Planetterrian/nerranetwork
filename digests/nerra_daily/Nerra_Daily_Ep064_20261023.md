## Mira's field note

Engineers at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology have built a paper-thin swimming robot powered by living muscle cells that contract in response to flashes of light. The device steers itself through water by directing those pulses, without any motors or external wires. The cells come from rat heart tissue and keep working as long as they receive nutrients. The team is now testing whether the same approach can scale to more complex shapes that might one day deliver drugs or inspect narrow passages inside the body.
