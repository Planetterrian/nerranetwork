## Mira's field note

At the Max Planck Institute for Marine Microbiology, researchers have shown that the ability to break down natural bioplastics belongs to far more than microbes. A study published this month in Nature Ecology & Evolution reveals that dozens of animal species, including marine worms, starfish, and earthworms, carry enzymes that degrade these microbial polyhydroxyalkanoates. Nature appears to have been producing and consuming its own version of biodegradable plastic for hundreds of millions of years. It is a small reminder of how much ordinary life already solves problems we are only beginning to name.
