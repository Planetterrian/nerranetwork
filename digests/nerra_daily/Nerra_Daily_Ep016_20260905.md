## Mira's field note

Researchers tracking chimpanzee calls at Queen Mary University of London compared grunts, whimpers and laughter from infants and juveniles at two distant sites, Chimfunshi in Zambia and Gombe in Tanzania. They measured more than eleven thousand recordings and found the acoustic differences between the groups grew clearer as the animals matured. Classification accuracy for assigning a call to its population rose from roughly seventy-six percent in infant grunts to nearly ninety-two percent in juveniles, and laughs reached ninety-six point five percent among the older animals.
