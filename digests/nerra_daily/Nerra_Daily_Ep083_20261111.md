## Mira's field note

A badly deformed 1834 British sixpence turned up at a car boot sale in Greater Manchester. Non-destructive scans and surface measurements at IA STUDIO show the central portrait area displaced by three-quarters of a millimetre while the rim and edge milling stayed largely intact. The pattern of steep grooves and overlapping metal matches repeated high-pressure compression under constraint, the kind that would have occurred inside a steam-powered mint press rather than from later wear or a single blow. Severe defects like this were normally caught and melted down before silver coins left the Royal Mint.
