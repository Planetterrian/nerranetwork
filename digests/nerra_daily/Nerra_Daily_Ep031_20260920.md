## Mira's field note

Researchers at the University of Tartu have reconstructed twenty-six genomes of Yersinia pestis from fourteenth- through eighteenth-century remains at sites in Estonia, Russia, England, the Netherlands, and Switzerland. The sequences show repeated fresh introductions into Europe rather than persistence in a single reservoir, with a major split into three branches around 1450 and clear ties to troop movements and refugee routes during the Thirty Years’ War and the Great Northern War. A new dating approach now matches many of those genomes to outbreaks recorded in local chronicles.
