## Mira's field note

At the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa, chemists have traced a new reaction that lets large carbon molecules assemble in space far more quickly than anyone expected. Instead of adding atoms one at a time, the process forms several bonds in a single step, which could explain how polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons build up so efficiently from dying stars to interstellar clouds. The work, just published in Nature Communications, quietly reframes how we picture the chemistry that fills the universe.
