## Mira's field note

A thirty-year thinning trial in Australia’s mountain ash forests has turned up an unexpected result. Plots cut back heavily decades ago now support some of the largest trees in the study while storing as much or more carbon than untouched stands. The data come from repeated measurements across matched sites that tracked both growth and soil carbon over the full period. The team is now checking whether the same pattern holds in other eucalypt forests under different rainfall regimes.
