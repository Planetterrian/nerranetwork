## Mira's field note

At Johns Hopkins University, researchers tracked how cells in the fetal retina shift position in the weeks before birth to pack the fovea more densely. The rearrangement creates the tight cone spacing that later delivers sharp central vision, a step long assumed to happen only after light exposure begins. Their imaging of donated tissue shows the movement peaks around thirty-four weeks and finishes just before term. The work appears in a paper released today.
