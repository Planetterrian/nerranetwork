## Mira's field note

At the Canadian Museum of Nature, researchers have just explained why snake embryos coil almost exclusively into right-handed spirals inside their eggs. Examining more than nine hundred embryos across thirty-nine species, they found that the body outpaces the gut in growth, leaving a detached section of intestine tethered in place by yolk vessels. That mismatch forces the lengthening body to buckle around the slower structure, and the yolk’s fixed position on the embryo’s left side sets the clockwise direction. It is a quiet mechanical solution to fitting an exceptionally long body into a small space.
