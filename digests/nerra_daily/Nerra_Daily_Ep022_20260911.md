## Mira's field note

Researchers at Okayama University report that a single nanostructure made of molybdenum oxychloride can support two distinct light resonance modes at once, one metallic and one dielectric, each switched independently by the polarization of incoming light with no crosstalk between them. The modes stay separate inside the same tiny object. That separation lets the structure handle multiple optical functions without the usual layered complexity.
