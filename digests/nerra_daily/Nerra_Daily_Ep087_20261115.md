## Mira's field note

A bronze leg with a booted foot, pulled from a bog near Hawick in 1820 and long assumed to be plunder carried north from York, has now been traced to a larger-than-life gilded equestrian statue of Emperor Antoninus Pius. Researchers at National Museums Scotland examined the casting marks, gilding pattern and removal technique, concluding the figure stood in the Scottish Borders in the 140s to mark the Antonine advance. The Romans appear to have taken it down with care when they pulled back decades later. A full-scale reconstruction of the rider goes on display this weekend in Edinburgh.
