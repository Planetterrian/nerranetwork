## Mira's field note

University College London chemists have found a way to let iodine in water-based batteries hand over four electrons instead of the usual two during each charge cycle. They designed an electrolyte that shields the reactive forms of iodine from unwanted side reactions without relying on corrosive salts. Zinc-iodine cells built this way held nearly all their capacity after thousands of cycles, and the same approach looks workable for aluminium versions as well.
