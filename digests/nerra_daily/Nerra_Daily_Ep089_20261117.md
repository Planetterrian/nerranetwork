## Mira's field note

UCLA bioengineers have fitted caps onto their nanovials, the tiny hydrogel bowls designed to hold one or two cells and their secretions. The lids keep bacteria and yeast trapped long enough for small colonies to grow, six hours for the bacteria and twelve for the yeast, while nutrients still pass through. Cells can now be sorted by growth rate using standard lab equipment, and researchers can watch one cell alter another’s behavior inside the same compartment. The study is published in Science Advances.
