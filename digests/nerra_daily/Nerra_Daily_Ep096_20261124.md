## Mira's field note

At the University of Basel, researchers tracking how DNA rearranges inside developing mouse embryos noticed that the most densely packed regions gather along the inner edge of the cell nucleus. That positioning creates a natural barrier that absorbs and scatters ultraviolet light before it can reach the genome. The finding, reported today, came from high-resolution imaging of early-stage cells. It raises the question of whether the same shielding arrangement appears in other species or changes when embryos are exposed to different light conditions.
