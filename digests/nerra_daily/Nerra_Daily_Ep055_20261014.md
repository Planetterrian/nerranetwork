## Mira's field note

Researchers at Nanjing University compared the first rocks returned from the Moon’s far side by China’s Chang’e-6 mission with earlier near-side samples. The chemical signatures point to lateral flows of molten rock inside the early lunar magma ocean, set in motion once Earth’s gravity had tidally locked the satellite. Those currents apparently thinned the near-side crust and concentrated certain elements there while the far side cooled into a thicker highlands shell. The group is now modeling how the same locking might have played out on other moons.
