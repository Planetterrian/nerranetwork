## Mira's field note

Johns Hopkins engineers have built robots the size of a pencil tip that can navigate inside a beating heart to deliver medicine directly to infections. The devices aim to treat endocarditis without open surgery or broad IV antibiotics, threading through blood vessels to reach the site with precision. The team built them from materials that respond to magnetic fields for steering and release. Early tests show they can carry and deposit payloads in simulated heart conditions. The next stage is animal trials to check how the bots hold up in real blood flow and tissue.
