## Mira's field note

At Singapore’s Centre for Quantum Technologies, researchers have built an atomic clock that keeps time with record accuracy, published this week in Nature. The device uses a cloud of laser-cooled lutetium atoms held in an optical lattice and measures a narrow optical transition that yields stability better than one part in 10 to the 18th. That precision lets it resolve differences equivalent to one second across the age of the universe. The team is now testing how the clock performs when moved between laboratories to check for environmental shifts.
