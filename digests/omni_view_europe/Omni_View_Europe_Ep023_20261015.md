# Omni View Europe
> **Three hundred sixty billion euros in China deficit opens two days of Brussels talks short of a budget deal.**

**What You Need to Know:** A China trade deficit of three hundred sixty billion euros is on the table as European Union leaders open two days of talks in Brussels that are not expected to settle the next long-term budget. Energy prices and Friday's session on Ukraine sit beside that argument. Elsewhere, student protests in Tirana turned violent, and a Guardian series measures how far Polish incomes have climbed since the Soviet era.

### Lead
**EU summit to focus on China, Ukraine and bloc budget: DW**
European Union leaders opened talks in Brussels on Thursday on competition from China, energy prices and the bloc budget for 2028 to 2034, with support for Ukraine due on Friday and President Volodymyr Zelenskyy invited. DW reports the deficit with China reached €360 billion ($400.4 billion) last year and is set to exceed that this year; the European Commission agreed with Beijing last week to curb hybrid-car imports, and trade chief Maroš Šefčovič's preliminary deal also covers access to rare earths. The EU accuses China of unfair state subsidies, industry groups say the import surge is closing factories, and Chancellor Friedrich Merz and President Emmanuel Macron want measures against market distortion, including a Franco-German "kill switch" that would cut China's market access if the deficit stayed unsustainable. Euronews reports oil remains above $100 a barrel despite a coordinated G7 release, and the International Energy Agency warned on Wednesday of renewed wholesale diesel price rises in Europe; Italian and Greek leaders want fiscal rules eased for temporary price measures, while more fiscally conservative states warn that would add to deficits and volatility. The seven-year budget needs unanimous approval; European Council President António Costa wants a deal by the end of the year so the law can be finished before January 2028, an Irish compromise has left both frugal governments and supporters of a larger budget unhappy, and a senior diplomat said leaders are not in a hurry and want a good deal rather than a fast one. DW says this summit is not expected to produce a breakthrough, a special summit is set for November, and pressure is rising ahead of elections next year in several member states; Euronews adds that Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez's call for snap elections in Spain adds uncertainty. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas told Euronews the US-Russia diesel deal is a "gift to Putin," that "the majority of the financing of this war comes from oil," and that strikes on Russian refineries fit "defence of Ukraine" under international law. Zelenskyy told Piers Morgan he feels "betrayed" and that Ukraine is losing American support "not because of us"; Euronews, citing the Financial Times, reports US negotiators proposed that European countries unfreeze some immobilised Russian state assets into a vehicle controlled by Washington, a plan European delegates rejected in Miami last week, with a loan to Ukraine due to be raised in Brussels on Friday. ### Across the Region
**Student protests in Albania turn violent as demonstrators clash with police: The Guardian**
Tens of thousands of students marched in Tirana over education costs and corruption, the Guardian reports, setting a government building and two banks alight and throwing stones at police. Officers used water cannon and tear gas; a fire started at the education ministry, a nearby branch of Raiffeisen Bank caught fire, bins were overturned, shopfronts were smashed, and dozens of people were detained. France 24 reports the protests turned violent for a second night on Wednesday, with several government buildings damaged and at least one ablaze. Thousands demanded that Prime Minister Edi Rama resign and that university conditions improve. **Rising Violence is a Sign that Serbia’s Ruling Party is Panicking: Balkan Insight**
Balkan Insight reports a run of street incidents in the weeks before Serbia's parliamentary elections on October 25, and links them to nervousness in the ruling Serbian Progressive Party about the vote. In Užice, the paper says party supporters beat citizens during a protest against a visit by Aleksandar Vučić while police stood by; in Belgrade, men attacked and injured an observer from the Anti-Corruption Agency at a ruling-party stand. In Novi Sad, supporters led by a party candidate confronted the opposition student-led list and tried to seize a banner. Mayor Aleksandar Šapić was reported to have assaulted a female opposition councillor; he denied it. An N1 journalist was attacked in Belgrade, a journalist in Niš was injured while filming a party event with police reportedly refusing to step in, and Momčilo Marjanović told Balkan Insight he spent a decade providing party "security" that included buying votes and beating protesters. ### The Closer Look
**Poland looks to economic heights in a country transformed since Soviet days: The Guardian**
The Guardian closes a series on Poland today with people in Warsaw describing wages that have risen along with the cost of living. Above the city, the Varso tower, finished four years ago and the tallest building in the European Union, carries a 310-metre spire over the Palace of Culture and Science, Joseph Stalin's gift. Cezary Błaszczyk, managing director of HB Reavis in Poland, which owns the tower, said "We look down on it," and that standing taller than the palace means the country got over communism and now has a strong economy. His colleague Natalia Kasperska said a slackline walker who crossed the 500-metre gap between the buildings a few months ago made "a symbolic walk from the past to the future."

The paper sets that view against four decades since Poland left Soviet-dominated rule. Excluding oil-exporting states and financial hubs, it says incomes have converged with western levels at the fastest rate in the world, from 40 percent of the EU average in the mid-1990s to 81 percent last year. National income passed $1 trillion, or £755 billion, last year, ranking Poland among the world's 20 largest economies. International Monetary Fund projections show income per person will soon overtake Japan and Spain. Deputy foreign minister Marcin Bosacki recalled queueing after school in the 1980s for up to five hours for butter and meat while the communist government imposed martial law. The Gdańsk shipworkers' Solidarność movement and Pope John Paul II's pilgrimage in 1979 preceded democracy in 1990, Nato membership in 1999 and EU accession in 2004.

The direction of migration has turned. For years Poles left to earn more abroad, and Polish plumbers became shorthand in Britain during the fights over free movement before the Brexit vote. In each year since 2021, twice as many Polish nationals left the United Kingdom as arrived. In 2024, Germany recorded more non-Germans moving to Poland than heading the other way for the first time on record. Architects Jedrzej Kolesinski and Krzysztof Gornicki, who moved to London in their twenties and led the Foster + Partners team behind the Varso, have come back and are designing Warsaw's new airport, which the paper calls the biggest greenfield infrastructure project in Europe. Gornicki said he can do global work from Warsaw and wanted to raise a family near his parents in a city safer, greener and more affordable than London. Electric trams, cranes and dockless e-scooters fill the streets below; orders at the oldest milk bar, Prasowy, now go through a touchscreen; Parade Square, once an asphalt expanse for Soviet tanks, has been planted as Warsaw bids for the 2040 summer Olympics. The Guardian also writes that incoming capital has driven up living costs and squeezed poorer Varsovians, that income inequality is among the highest in Europe, and that political divisions are widening ahead of a general election next year. ### Progress Watch
**Garda numbers set to reach record levels but GRA says growth ‘nowhere near enough’: The Irish Times**
The Irish Times reports that a class of about 200 recruits passing out on Friday at the Garda College in Templemore is set to push sworn Garda membership above 14,800 for the first time, from 14,683 at the end of August, a year-on-year rise of 500, past the previous record of 14,750 in March 2020. Garda headquarters says that growth has supported high-visibility policing in Dublin, Cork, Limerick and Galway, and that Commissioner Justin Kelly intends to meet a government target of 5,000 additional gardaí by 2030, about 16,500 members, broadly in line with Police Scotland. The Garda Representative Association says the pace is "nowhere near fast enough," notes that Kelly told the Public Accounts Committee the force will rely on overtime until it reaches 16,500, and says 400 members have left so far this year, against an earlier target of passing 15,000 in 2021 and 18,000 by the end of the decade. Minister for Justice Jim O'Callaghan's department says he is confident the highest-ever number of serving gardaí will be reached by the end of this year.
