### Lead
**Orange Order to march down Garvaghy Road for first time in almost 30 years: The Irish Times**
Late on Saturday, Belfast's Court of Appeal overturned a High Court injunction and allowed the Orange Order to parade down the Garvaghy Road in Portadown, County Armagh, at 8am on Sunday. The Irish Times reports that 35 members are to walk the largely nationalist road for the first time in 28 years, after the Parades Commission on Friday granted an application it had refused since 1998. Residents challenged that ruling; on Saturday evening, after a five-hour hearing, Judge Patricia Smyth suspended the parade, said the long-running dispute had the potential to escalate across Northern Ireland, and urged all sides to step back from the abyss. At 11.30pm Judge Siobhan Keegan said the High Court judge had erred in law; in the early hours of Sunday, Judge Michael Humphreys dismissed a fresh application, saying the commission had taken evidence, weighed the issues and reached a reasoned decision, and that the threshold for striking it down had not been met. Demonstrators gathered from the early hours to block the march, and the Police Service of Northern Ireland told them that organising or taking part in the protest was an offence liable to arrest and prosecution. The Irish Times states that five people were killed in incidents linked to the Drumcree tensions of the 1980s and 1990s. ### Across the Region
**Poland is racing ahead with military spending: The Guardian**
Since Vladimir Putin ordered Russian troops into Ukraine almost five years ago, Poland's defence spending has risen from 2.2 percent of GDP to 4.8 percent this year, about 53 billion dollars, the fourth-highest cash total in the EU after Germany, France and Italy, The Guardian reports. North of Warsaw, in Czosnów, a hi-tech weapons facility that MBDA Polska opened this month stands on ground that was a cornfield two years ago. Jim Price, the subsidiary's managing director, said Poland is shifting from importing weapons to making them at home, and that the firm invested in the country so work for Nato's eastern flank could be done quickly, with Polish jobs. Deputy foreign minister Marcin Bosacki said the build-up benefits Polish security, defence and the economy, and that solidarity and power are what would discourage attacks on Nato states by a man he called a traditional Russian imperialist. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/sep/27/poland-military-spending-economic-growth-economy-defence-boom)

### The Region and the World
**EU unlocks 710 mn euros' aid for crisis-hit communities: RFI**
Part of a new European Union aid package is marked for Ukraine to prepare for winter, Commission president Ursula von der Leyen said on Saturday., in a statement to the UN General Assembly. The statement puts 380 million euros into sub-Saharan Africa for protecting vulnerable migrants, supporting voluntary return and reintegration, strengthening migration governance and responding to forced displacement. A further 252 million euros from the emergency aid reserve is for communities hit by conflict, forced displacement, epidemics and natural catastrophes, including in sub-Saharan Africa, the Palestinian territories and Lebanon as well as Ukraine; smaller sums are for stability in the Great Lakes and for the Ebola outbreak in the east of the Democratic Republic of Congo. ### Both Sides: A decree to stop evictions in Spain
Spain's government is under pressure to issue a decree stopping further evictions after Maricarmen Abascal was removed from her home.

The case for acting now is the one made by marchers and by parties to the left of the prime minister. The Guardian reported that Abascal, 87, was taken on a stretcher from the Madrid home she had lived in for seven decades, protesters and police beside her. An estimated 10,000 people then protested in Madrid on Wednesday, the paper said, with signs reading Shame and Out with the vulture funds. RUSSPAIN reported a later occupation of Puerta del Sol. Organisers said 300,000 people filled the streets; official numbers were 25,000. Protesters demanded a decree to stop more cases like Abascal's. Irene Montero, of Podemos, told the march that this time the concession had to be made by rentiers and speculators, because houses are for living, and she warned that talks with Junts could betray people fighting for housing rights. Sumar threatened to skip the next Council of Ministers if that decree stalls.

The other side, in these reports, is the conduct protesters assign to office-holders and one purchase the coverage describes. Marchers blamed Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez, Madrid regional president Isabel Díaz Ayuso and mayor José Luis Martínez-Almeida for what they called a total neglect of their duties. They chanted at Housing Minister Isabel Rodríguez that she stood with owners. RUSSPAIN reported that the Madrid regional government bought a luxury penthouse in Chamberí, and that 15,000 people had protested outside that building days earlier.

Both accounts accept that Abascal was evicted and that people then took to the street. The next step the reports name is the coming Council of Ministers, which Sumar has tied to whether the decree moves.
