### Lead
**EU halts support to Sierra Leone over drug lord Bolle Jos: Belga English**
The European Commission has immediately suspended payments to Sierra Leone over the fugitive Dutch national Jos Leijdekkers, known as Bolle Jos, Belga English reported from statements by Dutch Justice Minister David van Weel. The Sierra Leone government stands to lose 18 million euros in budget support. The Netherlands hopes a further 20.5 million euros in aid can be halted next year. The European Parliament has adopted a resolution calling for stronger judicial cooperation with countries outside the EU, including in West Africa, so convicted drug criminals cannot evade their sentences; the text names Leijdekkers, who is believed to be hiding in Sierra Leone. Belga reported that courts in Belgium and the Netherlands have sentenced him to more than 100 years in prison. Van Weel told De Telegraaf he was pleased with the suspension, and that it is frustrating for EU funds to go to a country that refuses to cooperate in an extradition. ### Across the Region
**With Jansa’s Return, Slovenia’s Media Landscape Is Being Reshaped: Balkan Insight**
Slovenia’s ruling coalition, led by the Slovenian Democratic Party and dependent for its thin majority on the Resni.ca party, which Balkan Insight describes as pro-Kremlin, tabled amendments in August to the law on public service media. Critics fear the changes would give the government more control over Radio Television Slovenia and narrow its editorial independence, and the European Federation of Journalists has called for that independence to be protected. The public comment period ended on September 9, clearing the way for a parliamentary vote. The coalition has also called a non-binding referendum for October 11 on abolishing the broadcaster’s license fee, which would leave Radio Television Slovenia funded from the state budget. Marko Milosavljevic, a media studies professor at the University of Ljubljana, told Balkan Insight that Janez Jansa is moving fast; the same report says media experts cite Hungary’s 2004 abolition of its license fee as a warning about political dependence. **Filming in England awarded over £2m UK Government funding: Filming in England**
Filming in England, part of Creative UK, has secured £2.1 million over three years from the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport, with the money administered through the BFI. The grant continues a coordinated production-support service for feature film and high-end television in English regions outside London. It is drawn from the government’s £75 million Screen Growth Package in the Creative Industries Sector Plan, and it sits under an Industrial Strategy that names the creative industries a priority growth sector. The agency says the service has backed work including Hamnet, Spider-Man: Brand New Day and Peaky Blinders: The Immortal Man, plus upcoming releases including Pride and Prejudice in 2026 and Narnia: The Magician’s Nephew. It names five production hubs: the South East, the South West around Bristol, the West Midlands around Birmingham, the North West around Manchester and Liverpool, and Yorkshire around Leeds. ### Both Sides: What is driving the US diesel surge
Washington is pressing a shortage that it wants European supplies to ease, not a European law. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright said the administration expected European countries to announce additional diesel supplies soon.

President Donald Trump, speaking from the Oval Office on Wednesday, September 30, argued that Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries are the main cause of rising US diesel prices, ahead of the war in the Middle East. He said those refineries are being knocked out at an alarming rate, and that without that damage the United States would not have a diesel problem. He tied the rise to the war in Ukraine, including attacks by both sides that disrupted energy production and exports, and he said a possible US ban on diesel exports could lower domestic diesel prices while also hurting gasoline prices.

Wright’s account is wider. He said the United States has lost some diesel exports from the Middle East, though those are being restored, and has lost diesel exports from China, which he called a lot of interruptions. He pointed to extra European supply as the next step the administration expects.

Both treat supply as tighter and US prices as up. Kyiv Post, citing Reuters, reported US gasoline prices up by more than 40 percent over the past year, and diesel at a record $6.53 a gallon a week ago, according to AAA. Reuters said the surge has been driven largely by tighter supplies linked to the war with Iran and by refining disruptions, including Ukrainian attacks on Russian facilities. Wright’s next marker is an announcement of additional diesel from European countries; Kyiv Post does not report that Trump has ordered an export ban. Source: [kyivpost.com](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/85803)

### Progress Watch
**Sweden Conducted More Food Inspections in 2025, but Inconsistencies Remain: Food Safety Magazine**
Swedish authorities carried out more than 60,000 planned food inspections in 2025, about 4,000 more than in 2024, and met the national annual target, the Swedish Food Agency said in its report on food controls, as covered by Food Safety Magazine. They completed 91 percent of planned risk-based controls, above the 90 percent target, and 88 percent of planned controls at high-risk operations. Consistency in how authorities assess businesses was measured at 53 percent, compared with 49 percent in the figure the agency cites. Traceability checks stood at 70 percent, short of a 90 percent target, and 69 percent of identified deviations were confirmed corrected within a year, short of a 75 percent target. The agency has started a standardised control process, and it set four priorities for 2026 to 2029: risk-based and consistent controls, effective controls, traceability, and preparedness. Source: [food-safety.com](https://www.food-safety.com/articles/11877-sweden-conducted-more-food-inspections-in-2025-but-inconsistencies-remain)
