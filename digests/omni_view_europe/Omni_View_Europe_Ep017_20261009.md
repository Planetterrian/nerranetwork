### Lead
**Russia slashes cancer care as defence spending hits post-Soviet record: Euronews**
Euronews reports that Russia’s federal cancer programme budget falls from 44.9 billion roubles to 3.87 billion roubles in 2027, a nearly forty-fold reduction against 2025 levels. Defence spending reaches 35 percent of all federal expenditure, the highest share since the Soviet Union collapsed. The cut is the budget figure Euronews publishes for the cancer programme, not a claim that treatment has already stopped. Patients who depend on that federal programme are the people the smaller 2027 line reaches. The same account ties the reduction to defence taking that record share of the federal budget. ### Across the Region
**Tens of thousands march in Madrid to mourn pensioner whose eviction sparked housing protests: France 24**
Tens of thousands marched in Madrid on Thursday after 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal died on Wednesday, two weeks after eviction from the flat she had lived in for 70 years, the September removal that had already fed housing protests and a call for early elections. A provisional estimate by the central government’s delegation in the Madrid region put the crowd at 20,000. Abascal, who used a wheelchair, died in hospital after the company that owned the flat raised the rent sharply, and mourners walked from Puerta del Sol, where hundreds are camping, about three kilometres to her former home in Retiro. Teacher Rosa Marin told AFP that Abascal “will be an icon of struggle,” and marchers chanted “they aren’t deaths, they are murders.” Photography teacher Anton Alexander told AFP the protests have put housing at the heart of the 29 November election. **Commission fines Kingspan €40 million for providing incorrect and misleading information during merger investigation: The European Sting**
The European Commission has fined Kingspan 40 million euros for incorrect and misleading information during its 2021 review of Kingspan’s planned acquisition of Trimo, a deal Kingspan later abandoned. Both firms make mineral fibre sandwich panels for industrial and commercial buildings. The Commission opened the information investigation in November 2022 and, on 19 March 2024, sent a statement of objections alleging six infringements, then dropped two. It now finds four infringements, committed at least negligently, including misleading information on how Kingspan tracks penetration rates for those panels, incorrect or misleading information on bidding data, and incorrect information on board members’ involvement in the Trimo plan, other acquisitions, industry trends and strategy. Source: [europeansting.com](https://europeansting.com/2026/10/09/commission-fines-kingspan-e40-million-for-providing-incorrect-and-misleading-information-during-merger-investigation/)

**China rejects EU hybrid car export limits as Brussels considers new tariffs: EU Today**
China has rejected a European Union request to restrict hybrid-car exports to Europe during talks in Beijing by Trade Commissioner Maroš Šefčovič, EU Today reports, and the Commission is considering safeguard limits or extra tariffs once shipments pass thresholds still under discussion. EU countervailing duties imposed in October 2024 covered Chinese battery-electric vehicles and not hybrids, after which Chinese makers expanded in Europe’s hybrid market. On Wednesday the European Parliament adopted a non-binding call for a tougher China policy, prepared by Hilde Vautmans, by 454 votes to 86 with 110 abstentions, citing trade practices, support for Russia’s war, and pressure on Taiwan, while the merchandise trade deficit with China is put at about 360 billion euros. European leaders meet in Brussels on 15 and 16 October; EU Today notes safeguard measures could also affect suppliers in Japan, South Korea and Britain. ### Both Sides: Abortion-pill deceit after UK decriminalisation
What is being stated is how far last year’s parliamentary amendment goes. MPs voted through a change that decriminalises abortion up to birth for any woman acting in relation to her own pregnancy, including sex selection, and this week the government published an Equalities Impact Assessment on removing women from that criminal law. The assessment, as quoted by Pregnancy Help News from Christian Concern, says it would never be a criminal offence for a pregnant woman to terminate her pregnancy at any gestation, including beyond 24 weeks. It also says it would not be a criminal offence for a woman to deceive a registered medical practitioner about gestation in order to obtain abortion pills by post beyond the 10-week limit.

The government’s case, in that assessment, is that once a woman is outside the criminal offence for ending her own pregnancy, deceiving a doctor to get those pills is not itself a crime for her. The case against treating deception as lawful comes from Christian Concern and health expert Kevin Duffy, who say the 10-week limit exists because the pills are not approved beyond it and carry serious safety risks. The same article says the World Health Organisation recommends that medical abortion at 12 weeks or more be managed by doctors in a healthcare setting, that the Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists endorses that position, and that BPAS, MSI-RC and NUPAS no longer provide medical abortion in clinic beyond 10 weeks. Christian Concern adds that decriminalising abortion does not necessarily mean deception stops being an offence, and that phone-based supply cannot check coercion or gestation.

Both the assessment and its critics are talking about the same voted amendment and the same 10-week pills-by-post limit, which the article calls widely accepted. The latest step in the record is this week’s publication of the assessment. Source: [pregnancyhelpnews.com](https://pregnancyhelpnews.com/uk-government-claims-abortion-pill-deceit-is-not-a-crime)
