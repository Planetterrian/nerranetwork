### Lead
**Ukraine hits Moscow with one of its largest drone attacks in two years: France 24**
France 24 reports that Ukraine launched an overnight drone attack on Moscow that Russian authorities, speaking on Tuesday, called one of the largest on the capital in two years. The Russian state agency TASS said the strike was the second-largest drone attack on Moscow and the surrounding region in that period. Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said Russia downed most of the 650 drones sent toward the capital, including 105 downed close to the city. Regional governor Andrei Vorobyov said a 20-year-old man was killed in a village on the outskirts and that several homes were damaged. The Ria Novosti agency reported that the strikes wounded six people. France 24 notes that both Ukraine and Russia have stepped up long-range strikes on civilian and industrial targets, including energy, transport, ports and ships in the Black Sea, and that Russia has said it plans to increase infrastructure strikes, including in Kyiv, as winter approaches. Source: [france24.com](https://www.france24.com/en/europe/20261006-massive-ukrainian-drone-attack-targets-moscow-russian-officials-say)

### Across the Region
**Commission finds Polish support for MAN Trucks factory expansion incompatible State aid: The European Sting**
The European Commission has concluded that Poland’s plan to support extra capacity at a MAN Trucks factory in Niepołomice, in the Małopolskie region, breaks EU state-aid rules, so Poland cannot pay the money. The aid, about €26 million, or PLN 116 million, was opened to an in-depth investigation in July 2025. The Commission’s early view was that the project would help jobs in a less advantaged region, but it doubted the plan met the Guidelines on Regional State Aid. After the full inquiry, the Commission said Poland had not proved the aid decided MAN Trucks’ choice of Poland, and the evidence did not show the company would have built elsewhere without it. The Commission also said Poland had not shown the sum was the minimum needed, so the support had no real incentive effect and was not proportionate. Source: [europeansting.com](https://europeansting.com/2026/10/06/commission-finds-polish-support-for-man-trucks-factory-expansion-incompatible-state-aid/)

**Government backs recommendations of NHS doctors-led AI Commission: GOV.UK**
On 6 October 2026 the UK government said it will accept all 44 recommendations of the National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare, published on 10 September, and set out how they will be delivered across the UK. The Commission found that rules must move to a more proportionate, lifecycle-based system, with AI medical devices watched through their working life rather than judged once. Alongside that reply, the MHRA opened applications for phase three of AI Airlock, its sandbox for AI medical devices, to test post-market surveillance with developers, regulators and health partners. A webinar for applicants is set for 22 October. The MHRA has committed to draft guidance by December 2026 on how changes to AI devices are handled, and to begin next year by consulting on how those devices are qualified and classified. Source: [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-backs-recommendations-of-nhs-doctors-led-ai-commission)

### Both Sides: Limits on new EU members
What is being decided is how the European Union would stay workable if Ukraine and other candidates join. The European Commission is set to unveil proposals on Tuesday, Kyiv Post reports, after months of delay while governments pushed different ideas.

The case for safeguards starts with money, votes and past blockages. Diplomats expect steps to stop new members disrupting decisions or drawing too many resources, including a promise not to use the veto in key areas and limits on Common Agricultural Policy subsidies. Enlargement commissioner Marta Kos said in August, “We must make sure we do not let in Trojan horses,” and that such measures are “a must-have, not a nice-to-have,” so the Union can accept new members with confidence. The fear stated in the reporting is that new members, especially Ukraine, could strain the budget and make decisions unmanageable, with Hungary’s repeated blockages under Viktor Orban treated as the lesson.

The case against a lesser status starts with the risk of a permanent waiting room. Chancellor Friedrich Merz earlier this year floated associate membership, under which countries could attend EU meetings but not vote. That plan received short shrift from a raft of EU countries and from Ukraine, who saw it as a way of trapping applicants in a halfway house. There has also been pushback against speeding the process up.

The shared ground is the queue. Russia’s 2022 invasion gave fresh impetus to enlargement. Ukraine and Moldova are among four frontrunners, Montenegro is pressing to become the 28th member next year, and Albania is not far behind. Brussels insists enlargement is crucial for the EU’s future. The next step is the Commission proposal Kyiv Post says is due Tuesday. Source: [kyivpost.com](https://www.kyivpost.com/post/86205)
