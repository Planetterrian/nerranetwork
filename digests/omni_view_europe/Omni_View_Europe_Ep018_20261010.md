**Scotland to develop 'War Book' with UK as military chiefs warn of conflict by 2030: The Herald**
Scotland is to draft a new War Book with the UK as military leaders warn of a growing threat of large-scale conflict by 2030. The Herald reports the format, first developed early last century and used in the Cold War for a nuclear attack, was shelved after that threat faded and is being revived because of an increased threat to Nato from Russia. It is separate from armed-forces plans and is meant as a whole-of-society home-defence document; defence stays reserved to Westminster, but ministers recognise devolved health, transport and infrastructure would be hit in a war. A senior Scottish Government civil servant said on Thursday in Edinburgh that Scotland will work with the UK Government to influence the book, led by the Cabinet Office, and that this is the first time the Scottish Government has been involved in drafting it. ### Both Sides: A US license for Russian diesel
The decision in front of Europe is whether a US license letting Russian diesel onto world markets is an energy fix partners should live with, or a crack in the sanctions wall around Moscow's war.

Donald Trump's case, in his own post after Friday's call with Vladimir Putin, is price. He said Russia will supply diesel immediately and in November, and that prices for Americans, farmers, ranchers and truckers will come down fast. EA WorldView reports US diesel hit a record $6.52 a gallon last month, more than double a year ago, and that Trump tied the spike to his war on Iran and control of the Strait of Hormuz ahead of November's midterm elections. The US Treasury said it is issuing a temporary general license so that supply can reach the global market. The UK government recognises that conflicts can move energy prices and the cost of living, and says it fully supports US-led efforts toward a just and lasting peace.

The case against comes from Kyiv and from London's sanctions line. Volodymyr Zelensky said the agreement is not honest, is unworthy of partners, and is a weak decision, and that it looks like a birthday present for Putin. Al Jazeera reports he called it a gift to Putin, and that US lawmakers and European allies criticised the deal. The UK government said it will maintain the toughest sanctions regime it has ever imposed, raise the cost of Putin's aggression, and keep military and financial support so Ukraine can defend itself. It said Ukraine has shown it is serious about peace, Russia has rejected full and partial ceasefires while striking civilians and energy, and Russia could end the war today.

Both sides are acting on the same link between the war and fuel: Trump is moving supply because diesel is expensive, and the UK says it is monitoring energy markets even as it refuses to loosen its own sanctions. Next, on the record, is immediate licensed supply, a further 500,000 tons in November, and 1,000,000 tons after that. Britain says it remains in close contact with the US, Ukraine and other allies.
