> **Nearly 200 years on, Britain keeps its Jerusalem building open under a new name after Israel moved to shut it.**

**What You Need to Know:** Britain says its Jerusalem consulate building stays open from today as the UK Mission, Jerusalem, after talks with Israel, which Sky News reported had confirmed a closure. The UK government says it does not accept that Israel has the legal right to close that mission, has withdrawn some staff and families, and will not take reciprocal action while talks continue. Danish intelligence says sabotage against defense suppliers has moved from planning into operations, and students in France are back in the streets.

### Lead
**Foreign Secretary statement on the UK's diplomatic presence in Jerusalem: GOV.UK**
A Foreign Secretary statement said that, after discussions with the Government of Israel, the Consulate General building will not close and will be known from today as the UK Mission, Jerusalem. The UK government said it does not accept that Israel has the legal right to close the British Consulate General, there for nearly 200 years, and it restated its long-standing view that East Jerusalem is occupied territory. It said the UK Mission, Jerusalem is independent and operational and will keep delivering services and work with the Palestinian people, so Britain maintains its presence and its role in support of a two-state solution. Without an understanding, the statement said, Britain would not have the assurances it needs for staff and premises, so it has temporarily withdrawn a number of staff and families and will work in the coming months to build that presence back up. The UK said it will not take reciprocal action while those discussions are underway and credible, and that it stands by measures taken on 8 September with international partners in support of a two-state solution. Sky News reported that Israel has confirmed it is closing the British consulate in East Jerusalem after a UK ban on trade with West Bank settlements, which Sky described as illegal. ### Across the Region
**Russia Steps Up Sabotage Attacks on Danish Defense Firms, Intelligence Says: Kyiv Post**
Josefine Christensen, a lead analyst at Denmark's Security and Intelligence Service, said Wednesday in Copenhagen that Russia has begun sabotage against Danish firms supplying military equipment to Ukraine, after concluding that broader efforts to disrupt Western support had failed. She told a homeland security conference that spring saw a lot of planning and very little carried out, while early summer saw many more operations actually run, in remarks Kyiv Post attributed to Reuters. Christensen said Moscow is now trying to hit weapons production because earlier broad targeting did not deter NATO from supporting Ukraine, and she described the shift as a significant escalation in hybrid warfare. She cited an April list from Russia's Defense Ministry naming European drone and drone-component manufacturers as potential military targets, and PET says Russia is using untrained proxies recruited through Telegram, including homeless people and people addicted to drugs, moving from reconnaissance to sabotage. The Russian Embassy in Copenhagen rejected the allegations Wednesday as unfounded claims, and Russia has repeatedly denied involvement in sabotage or hybrid warfare. ### Both Sides: Genetically modified crop imports
The European Commission has approved or renewed authorizations for seven genetically modified crops, and the question is whether those import approvals should stand when governments in the member states would not vote them through.

The case for the decisions is the one the Commission acted on: favorable assessments by the European Food Safety Authority. UkrAgroConsult reports that the Commission approved or renewed seven events in June and September 2026 — four soybean events and three corn events — for food and animal feed. The expansion is particularly important, that report says, for the European feed sector, which relies heavily on imported soybeans and corn. The new and renewed approvals broaden the shipments that can enter the EU market for processing and use in animal feed. The list is soybean MON 94637, GMB151 crossed with DAS-44406-6, and MON 94313; corn DP202216 crossed with NK603 and DAS-40278-9; and renewals for corn NK603 crossed with T25, corn MON 87460, and soybean MON 87705. The Commission adopted the decisions after member states failed to reach a qualified majority either for or against.

The case against is the one member states put on the record in those votes: public opposition to genetically modified crops, the precautionary principle, scientific concerns, and political considerations. They did not gather a qualified majority to stop the authorizations. They also did not gather one to pass them.

What both the approvals and the objections leave in place is the scope of the decisions themselves: imports and processing only, with no authorization to grow the crops in the EU, a term of 10 years, and a requirement that products comply with EU labeling and traceability rules. Those authorizations now run for that 10-year term. Source: [ukragroconsult.com](https://ukragroconsult.com/en/news/european-commission-authorizes-imports-of-seven-new-gm-soybean-and-corn-events/)
