### Lead
**Ukrainian soldier to go on trial over sabotage of Nord Stream gas pipelines: The Guardian**
A Ukrainian special forces officer, Serhii Kuznetsov, went on trial in Hamburg on Wednesday, accused by German prosecutors of taking part in the blasts that damaged the Nord Stream gas pipelines on 26 September 2022. Prosecutors accuse him of being an accomplice to war crimes by attacking civilian infrastructure, causing an explosion, damaging property and disrupting public services. He denies any involvement, and Ukraine denies involvement. He will be identified in court as Serhii K under German privacy laws, though an extradition order used his full name. Prosecutors allege he prepared and carried out the detonation of at least four incendiary devices of up to 27 kilograms at a depth of about 80 metres under the Baltic Sea, near the Danish island of Bornholm, and that six accomplices — four deep-sea divers, an explosives expert and a ship’s captain — used a rented yacht and timer-controlled explosives. The blasts burst Nord Stream 1, Russia’s main gas supply route to Germany before Moscow cut supplies in August 2022, and damaged Nord Stream 2, which Germany had suspended before Russia’s full-scale invasion and which was never put into operation. In July, German state prosecutors accused Ukrainian state authorities of ordering the attack, arguing the aim was to halt gas supplies and stop Russia using gas revenue to finance the war; President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has denied his country was involved. Kuznetsov was arrested in August 2025 on a family holiday in Italy while travelling on a false passport, and extradited to Germany last November. A further suspect, Volodymyr Zhuravlyov, was arrested in northern Croatia in August, is fighting extradition after applying for asylum there, and denies any role. The German government has declined to comment. The Alternative für Deutschland has used the case to argue that German aid for Ukraine must cease and the pipeline be reinstated. A Russian request to participate as co-plaintiffs was rejected; Gazprom, which owns Nord Stream 2, had sought that role. Kuznetsov is expected to remain silent, and his lawyers are expected to offer affidavits from two military superiors that he is prohibited from revealing his actions as a soldier. ### Across the Region
**Ukraine live: Russia's 'targeted' strikes on energy leave 600,000 without power, Zelensky says: France 24**
Two days after Donald Trump announced an energy ceasefire, Ukrainian authorities said heavy overnight Russian strikes killed at least six people, and President Volodymyr Zelensky said the attacks left some 600,000 people without power. Zelensky wrote that a targeted ballistic strike hit energy infrastructure covered by the ceasefire announced by the American president, and accused Russia of deliberate, ostentatious disrespect toward the United States. Russia’s defence ministry said its forces struck seven Ukrainian energy facilities and a data centre in Kyiv which it said was used to gather intelligence on Russia’s armed forces, naming heat and power plants in Kyiv and Kharkiv among the targets, and said the attack used ground-launched missiles and long-range strike drones. Kyiv’s air force said Ukraine downed four of 39 missiles. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said two people were killed and 21 injured in a missile strike on an apartment block; Zelensky said Darnytska CHP-4, which supplies heating and electricity, was hit with no early warning from partners. A technology adviser to Zelensky, Sergiy Beskrestnov, said SpaceX introduced a speed limit for Starlink terminals in motion overnight, that defence forces were not warned, and that missions were disrupted. Trump said the United States is still sharing intelligence and that Ukraine hit many sites the day before; he did not say Russia had broken a ceasefire. Russia’s foreign minister Sergey Lavrov said Ukraine does not deserve the respite a ceasefire would bring. Zelensky is due to meet Prime Minister Andy Burnham at Downing Street on Wednesday to discuss air defence supplies. Russia’s defence ministry, cited by Interfax, said it had downed 902 drones over Russian regions overnight. ### The Closer Look
**How Europe’s most wanted cocaine trafficker turned Sierra Leone into the world’s first true narco state: The Guardian**
The price of cocaine in Europe is the stake in a Guardian investigation published on Wednesday. Intelligence assessments cited by the paper credit a 35-year-old Dutch national, Jos Leijdekkers, with importing volumes large enough to crash the European market, and say that last year he halved the drug’s wholesale price. Those sources say he is also the son-in-law of Sierra Leone’s president, Julius Maada Bio, and that he ordered the abduction of a Freetown man, Alan Jalloh. A western official told the Guardian: “It is state capture by a criminal network on a scale never seen.”

The account of that capture, as the paper’s police and intelligence sources tell it, runs from a supermarket forecourt to a boarding at sea. Witnesses described a black Toyota Rav4 forcing through rush-hour traffic outside Goodies supermarket in Freetown. Men the witnesses called burly and white pulled on balaclavas, and dragged Jalloh, 40, and two friends into the vehicle. None of the three has been seen since. Two days later, on 25 October 2025, another three men disappeared. Within a week, nine people had been seized from the city’s streets, most in daylight. Police sources were told they were dismembered and that at least one was beheaded. No bodies have been found. An officer investigating Jalloh’s abduction said: “They were exterminated to hide the truth.” Intelligence sources told the paper that at least 14 people have been killed inside Sierra Leone on Leijdekkers’ orders, and that high-ranking police are believed to have been murdered on his say-so. Officers have fled the country, those sources said.

Five months after Jalloh disappeared, the cargo ship Arconian left Freetown. By May, Spanish civil guards boarded it about 100 miles off Morocco. A guard the Guardian names as Lucas Fernández described six gunmen in the hold and, nearby, 1,279 bales of cocaine weighing between 30 and 45 tonnes, which the paper calls the largest amount of the drug ever seized. Seventeen days later, Bio addressed Oxford University. The Guardian describes him as chair of Ecowas, a region it says is home to more than 400 million people, and as someone the EU has treated as a reliable ally, with aid to Sierra Leone stretching back more than 45 years. The paper says that continued even as intelligence detailed cocaine volumes bound for Europe, and that the UN overlooked its own evidence.

The same sources warn the network is expanding. A Dutch official said the entire west Africa region is at risk. A European police source said dozens of Leijdekkers’ gunmen descended on a Dutch village last month intent on retribution, and that cocaine disputes in Europe had entered a new era. The first recorded sighting of Leijdekkers in west Africa, the investigation says, was a six-vehicle convoy in Freetown in October 2022. **Trump says 'massive' oil, gas and mineral exploration to begin in Greenland: France 24**
President Donald Trump said on Wednesday that a massive oil, gas and mineral exploration was getting ready to begin in Greenland, without giving details of prospective American involvement.
