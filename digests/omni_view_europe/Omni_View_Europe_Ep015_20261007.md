### Lead
**Finland orders halt to work on Google data centre sites: Al Jazeera**
Finland’s Supervisory Agency, the LVV, has ordered Tuike Finland, the company representing Google, to suspend by 23 October 2026 all preparatory work that would significantly alter the environment at planned data-centre sites in Muhos and Kajaani, until compulsory environmental impact assessments are completed. Google said in September it would invest 13 billion euros, about 14.6 billion dollars, in Finnish digital infrastructure over two years, and called the projects in Muhos, Vaala, Kajaani and Hamina its single biggest investment in Europe. The LVV is investigating whether hundreds of hectares of forest were cleared without a mandatory assessment; environment-department head Tommi Muilu said trees have been removed, topsoil stripped, site roads and storage areas built, and ditches altered. Hanna Halmeenpaa, chair of the Finnish Association for Nature Conservation, told AFP that nature sites which should be preserved had been logged across a deforested area of more than 300 hectares. Tuike Finland must explain how it intends to proceed by 14 October, and the LVV said it could face enforcement proceedings if it does not comply. Google spokesman Sondre Ronander told AFP the company acknowledged shortcomings, would study the findings and follow the guidance, and said landscaping plans include planting trees across 130 hectares at Muhos; opposition parties last month called for a national permit system to prevent power shortages and soaring energy prices. ### Across the Region
**Norway Plans to Spend $63.6 Billion from Wealth Fund in 2027: ASHARQ AL-AWSAT**
Norway’s minority Labour government said on Wednesday it plans to withdraw 608.4 billion Norwegian crowns, about 63.61 billion dollars, from the country’s 2.3 trillion dollar sovereign wealth fund in 2027, up from a revised 583.4 billion crowns in 2026, and must now negotiate the budget with four centrist and left-wing parties. The government said the proposal is estimated to have a neutral effect on economic activity next year. It put the structural non-oil deficit for 2027 at 2.7 percent of the fund’s projected value at the end of 2026, in line with the current year. Mainland GDP is now expected to grow 1.1 percent this year, against 1.7 percent seen in May, and 1.7 percent in 2027, up from 1.6 percent previously; core inflation is seen at 3.1 percent in 2026, down from 3.2 percent in May, and at 2.8 percent in 2027, against 2.6 percent seen previously. **Commission seeks 15-year safeguards on future member states: EUobserver**
The European Commission on Tuesday proposed up to 15 years of post-accession safeguards that could punish new member states for bad behaviour, in a pre-enlargement policy review the European Council had asked for in June 2024 and had wanted ready by spring 2025. The review was delayed several times because of political sensitivities in EU capitals. It is aimed at membership frontrunners Montenegro, Albania, Moldova and Ukraine, each of which is to receive an accession roadmap this month. Commission president Ursula von der Leyen has called enlargement a geopolitical imperative; the Commission suggests new members promise, for an unspecified time, not to oppose EU decisions on future enlargements because of bilateral disputes, while enlargement commissioner Marta Kos said that commitment would be voluntary. ### The Region and the World
**Spat over Israeli flag removal engulfs the European Parliament on October 7 anniversary: Euronews**
On the third anniversary of the October 7 attacks — which Euronews said killed around 1,200 people in Israel, with 251 taken hostage, and were followed by an Israeli offensive in Gaza that has killed over 70,000 Palestinians — the European Parliament became the scene of a clash over an Israeli flag. Euronews reported that on Monday evening Rima Hassan, a France Unbowed MEP, knocked over an Israeli flag at an exhibition for victims of the attack; photos on social media showed the Israeli and EU flags on the floor. Hassan acknowledged removing the Israeli flag, denied intentionally toppling the EU flag, and her office said neither flag was stepped on or damaged; she framed the act as a response to Israeli state support for the exhibition and claimed speeches there denied the existence of Palestine. Centre-right MEP François-Xavier Bellamy called the act a form of complicity with those who want to reintroduce antisemitic hatred in Europe, and Israeli foreign minister Gideon Sa’ar called the scenes shameful, outrageous, but sadly not surprising. ### Both Sides: Age bans or school lessons for young consumers online?
The question is whether the European Union should rely on age limits to protect minors online, or whether schools must teach consumer rights as well. The strongest case for limits is the one set out in the Commission President’s State of the Union address on 16 September 2026: upcoming EU KIDS legislation would put the protection of young people in the digital environment at the core of the Union’s agenda, and would include strict rules such as a ban on social media before the age of 13. The strongest case for classrooms comes from Tiziana Toto, head of national consumer policies at Cittadinanzattiva APS, and Cinzia Pollio, a senior project manager there. They argue that without education on awareness and the responsible use of new technologies, an approach built only on limits and restrictions postpones the problem rather than solving it, and that consumer literacy should be an integral part of school curricula across Europe. Cittadinanzattiva is making that case with consumer associations EEKE in Greece and InfoCons in Romania, with support from iliad and co-funding from the European Union’s Single Market Programme, through the CoRe Consumer Regeneration project. Italian MEP Denis Nesci of the ECR Group is hosting the project’s closing event on 14 October 2026, from 11:00 to 13:00 Central European Summer Time, in Room 1H1 of the European Parliament in Brussels and online. The fact both sides share is that minors face real risks online and that those risks now belong on the Union’s political agenda. The next step dated in the reporting is that 14 October meeting, which the organisers describe as the place for lessons from 16 schools and hundreds of students and for policy recommendations.
