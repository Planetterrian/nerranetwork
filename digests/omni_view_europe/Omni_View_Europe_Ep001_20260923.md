### Lead
**Italian parliament backs Meloni’s plan to restart nuclear power: The Mighty 790 KFGO**

Italy’s Senate gave final approval on Wednesday to legislation that lets the government restart nuclear power generation almost 40 years after a 1987 referendum closed the country’s reactors. The law requires the government to adopt implementing decrees within 12 months and creates an independent regulator while setting rules for decommissioning old facilities. Energy Minister Gilberto Pichetto Fratin said sustainable nuclear power together with renewables will make Italy a safe, independent and competitive country. The government estimates nuclear could reach at least 11 percent of the energy mix and save €17 billion in decarbonisation costs by 2050, with the national plan projecting a possible rise to 22 percent. Centre-left opposition parties opposed the measure and called for any new plants to be sited only on already developed or degraded land. A June poll by Demopolis found 51 percent support for restarting nuclear generation overall, though a majority opposed construction of a plant near their homes. The government argues that technological and safety advances have rendered the 1987 vote obsolete and that the move will shield the economy from energy-price surges linked to conflicts in the Middle East. Anti-nuclear campaigners maintain that safety concerns and lengthy construction times outweigh the benefits, and Italy currently relies heavily on energy imports including electricity from nuclear plants in neighbouring countries such as France. Five Star Movement senator Elena Sironi said any new plants should be located primarily in areas already developed, degraded or affected by decommissioned industrial activities without consuming additional land. Source: [kfgo.com](https://kfgo.com/2026/09/23/italian-parliament-backs-melonis-plan-to-restart-nuclear-power/)

### Across the Region
**£84 million to boost Plymouth's defence growth plans: GOV.UK**

The UK government confirmed release of £50 million through the Defence Growth Deal for Plymouth, unlocking up to £34 million more from industry and local partners to create a National Centre for Marine Autonomy. The package is expected to create nearly 1,000 jobs, support 500 people into work and benefit more than 400 UK businesses in robotics, artificial intelligence and autonomous systems. Defence Minister Lord Vernon Coaker approved Team Plymouth’s strategic business case and announced the investment at Market Hall in Plymouth. The National Centre for Marine Autonomy is expected to create 120 jobs and attract more than 30 businesses including start-ups and scale-ups. A separate £1.5 million Get Plymouth Working scheme will help 500 people into jobs in defence, advanced manufacturing, engineering and construction with a focus on veterans and young people aged 18 to 25. Around 750 people will gain new technical skills and qualifications while more than 7,000 young people will benefit from STEM training. Lord Coaker said the investment strengthens national security while backing British jobs, businesses and communities. Source: [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/84-million-to-boost-plymouths-defence-growth-plans)

**Germany commits for first time to phasing out fossil fuels by 2045: The Guardian Europe**

Germany published a roadmap that for the first time sets an explicit target of transitioning away from fossil fuels by 2045, alongside raising renewable electricity from 55 percent to 80 percent by 2030 and cutting methane emissions 30 percent by 2030. The plan reaffirms the coal exit by 2038 with a possible earlier date of 2035 and adds 12 GW of onshore wind plus a 215 GW solar target by 2030. Andreas Sieber of 350.org called the explicit phase-out commitment a notable shift but warned that the economic ministry continues to push for greater use of gas. Former climate envoy Jennifer Morgan said the strategy must be paired with faster rollout of green electrification in transport and heating. The push for heat pumps to replace gas boilers faced attacks from the far-right after a 2023 heating law that would have banned most oil and gas installations. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2026/sep/23/germany-commits-for-first-time-to-phasing-out-fossil-fuels-by-2045)

### Both Sides: Restarting nuclear power in Italy
Italy’s Senate approved legislation that authorises the government to restart nuclear power generation. The government argues that advanced modular reactors will help decarbonise industry and strengthen energy security while shielding the economy from price surges caused by conflicts in the Middle East. Energy Minister Gilberto Pichetto Fratin stated that sustainable nuclear power together with renewables will make Italy a safe, independent and competitive country, and the government estimates the move could save €17 billion in decarbonisation costs by 2050 with the share potentially rising to 22 percent under the national energy and climate plan. The administration maintains that technological and safety advances have made the 1987 referendum obsolete and that reduced dependence on foreign energy supplies will protect households and businesses. Anti-nuclear campaigners counter that safety concerns and the long construction times outweigh any benefits, noting that a 1987 referendum led to the closure of existing reactors and that many Italians still oppose building plants near their homes. A June Demopolis poll found 51 percent support for restarting nuclear generation overall but a majority opposed to local construction. Centre-left opposition parties, including the Five Star Movement, urged that any new plants be limited to already developed or degraded land without consuming additional territory. Senator Elena Sironi said new facilities should be located primarily in areas already developed, degraded or affected by decommissioned industrial activities. The law gives the government 12 months to adopt the necessary implementing decrees and to establish an independent regulator. Both sides accept that Italy currently relies heavily on energy imports, including electricity from nuclear plants in neighbouring countries such as France.
