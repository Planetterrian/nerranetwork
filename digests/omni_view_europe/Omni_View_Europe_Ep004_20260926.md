### Lead
**Russia targets Ukraine's digital nerve centres as war reaches data infrastructure: The Times of India**
Russian strikes have widened onto data centres and telecommunications sites, disrupting internet service for around 100,000 households in Kyiv and the surrounding region. The Times of India, citing the BBC and Ukrainian officials, reported that the strikes damaged data centres run by internet providers in the capital. On Friday a Russian drone hit the Inkom business centre, which housed a Datagroup data centre; Ukrainian officials said four people were killed and the facility was damaged. Datagroup said its services continued from backup sites. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia had widened attacks onto data centres and internet providers to cause maximum disruption, and that the strikes affect people’s ability to stay connected, study and work. Russia said it struck the data centre because Ukrainian military intelligence was using it. The Russian defence ministry confirmed the Datagroup strike and said its forces had also targeted New-Telco, United DC, Kyivstar and Parkovyi, alongside continued strikes on energy and railway infrastructure. ### Across the Region
**European Commission proposes efficiency labels for data centres: Cyprus Mail**
The European Commission has proposed labels showing how efficiently data centres use energy and water, for operators of sites of 500 kilowatts or more. Those operators would disclose energy and water efficiency, the link between water use and local water stress, and whether they can serve the local energy system, including by reusing waste heat. The scheme sets no cap on electricity or water use and does not require disclosure of total electricity consumed. European Union data put data centres at about 2.5 percent of EU electricity, with combined capacity expected to rise from 12 gigawatts last year to 28 gigawatts by 2030, while the EU aims to triple capacity over seven years. Countries and lawmakers have two months to object; otherwise the rules enter into force, and the labels could later become a condition for mandatory minimum standards. **Drones could speed up getting defibrillators to people having cardiac arrests, study suggests: The Guardian**
Matthieu Heidet and colleagues mapped 28,349 out-of-hospital cardiac arrests from 2011 to 2024 in the Greater Paris area, excluding central Paris, against 1,893 fixed automated external defibrillators. Hillary Minka, first author, said only 30 percent of those cases fell within 500 metres of the nearest recorded device. The team found 910 extra fixed devices would be needed for 84.5 percent of the recorded cases to sit within 500 metres, and 1,712 for full coverage, while drones flying within 3,900 metres could raise coverage with fewer machines. In the United Kingdom, the British Heart Foundation counts more than 30,000 out-of-hospital cardiac arrests a year in which emergency services try resuscitation, and fewer than one in ten people survive; each minute without CPR and defibrillation cuts the chance of survival by 10 percent. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/society/2026/sep/26/drones-could-speed-up-getting-defibrillators-to-people-having-cardiac-arrests-study-suggests)

### Both Sides: Faster returns from Spain to Morocco
Spain’s government is drafting a real decreto ley that could let some people who reached Ceuta be returned to Morocco before every administrative and judicial appeal has ended. The text is not in force. It would cover citizens of countries the European Union classifies as safe, including Morocco, nearly two months after a mass arrival and as authorities handle thousands of arrivals from Ceuta.

The government’s case is speed. Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska has said people with no right to remain will be returned, and that applicants for international protection will be returned only after their claims have been examined and rejected. The stated aim is to stop an asylum request from freezing a removal for months. Territorial Policy Minister Ángel Víctor Torres has described roughly 150 to 200 voluntary returns to Morocco a day, and 160 returns in one recent 24-hour period. Officials are already processing returns and are not waiting for the decree.

The case against that timetable, as the draft is set out, is that the shorter path would fall on people whose cases are not finished. The Comisión Interministerial de Asilo could lose its preliminary review. Rejected applicants might have five days to appeal, with their cases put first. Very few asylum applications from Morocco succeed, according to the same account, yet those applicants would still face a different process before their cases were fully settled. A separate disputed provision could archive the applications of people who leave reception centres and expose them to expulsion, while investigations into suspected ill-treatment at some facilities remain open.

The government and the account of the draft both treat the decree as not yet law, and both record that returns to Morocco are already under way. The next step is whether the government adopts the real decreto ley. Source: [russpain.com](https://russpain.com/news/government-targets-faster-migrant-returns-to-morocco-99251/)
