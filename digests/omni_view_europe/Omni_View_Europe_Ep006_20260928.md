> **Four EU hopefuls, including Ukraine, could get timed road maps to membership.**

**What You Need to Know:** The European Commission is drawing up timed accession road maps for Ukraine, Montenegro, Moldova and Albania, the four it treats as closest to joining. Officials say the plans would set reform duties, cluster dates and, if the reforms continue, a path to admission, while other candidates would not get the same offer now. Current member states would still have to approve each step.

### Lead
**The EU is preparing "road maps" for accession for Ukraine and three other countries: Українські Національні Новини**
The European Commission is drawing up targeted accession road maps for Montenegro, Ukraine, Moldova and Albania, two senior Commission officials working on the drafts told Politico, in an account carried by Українські Національні Новини. The four are treated as the leaders of the accession queue, but they still have slow progress and no clear date for formal entry. Politico writes that the road maps would be the first time those four are offered a guarantee of admission if the required reforms continue, plus indicative deadlines for finishing the talks. One official said the plans would spell out what each candidate must do, create duties for current members who must approve each step, and set approximate dates for completing negotiating clusters. A Commission spokesperson said the maps will list priorities, timelines and outcomes so the most advanced candidates can finish the reforms. Officials confirmed that Kosovo, North Macedonia and Bosnia and Herzegovina will not be offered maps at this stage, nor will Georgia, Turkey or Serbia, whose bids have stalled over concerns about democratic backsliding and the judiciary. Ursula von der Leyen promised targeted support for the candidates that have made the most progress in her State of the Union address in early September. **Pope to speak on European unity in France's Metz: Kuwait Times**
Pope Leo is due to speak on Monday in Metz on the future of Europe and its integration, closing a four-day visit to France, Kuwait Times reports. The northeastern city, of about 120,000 people, changed hands between France and Germany in past wars and is tied to Robert Schuman, a founder of the European Union, who is buried just outside it. The first US pope is expected to speak on Europe's roots and on unity, with Russia's war on Ukraine in its fifth year and nationalist movements gaining strength, the paper says. President Emmanuel Macron is also due to speak, and a handful of European heads of state and government are expected; the message comes seven months before French voters choose Macron's successor, with the eurosceptic, anti-immigration far right seeking power. The Vatican says more than 1.3 million people saw him in Paris and Lourdes from Friday to Sunday, including a Saturday mass for about 800,000 on the Champs-Elysees; in Metz he is to ride an open-top popemobile and say mass in St Stephen's Cathedral, after a security sweep that included bomb-disposal experts on the facade. **Rosetti Marino Wins €250–300M Hejre EPCI Contract in Denmark: imarinenews**
Italian contractor Rosetti Marino has won a contract worth 250 million to 300 million euros from INEOS E&P A/S for a platform on the Hejre development in the Danish North Sea, imarinenews reports. The Ravenna-based group will handle engineering, procurement, construction, load-out, transport and installation. The company said the reservoir runs at 759 barg and 170 degrees Celsius, and called Hejre one of Europe's most important energy-security projects. Chief executive Oscar Guerra said the award confirms the firm's commitment and competitiveness in highly technical European offshore work. Preliminary work has started and is expected to run to completion in 2028; the report puts estimated reserves at about 170 million barrels of oil equivalent and describes a water depth of five to six kilometres. Greenpeace and other environmental groups opposed the project over lifecycle carbon emissions; the Danish Energy Appeals Board revoked the existing permit in 2025 because the impact assessment did not count downstream climate effects, and the Danish Energy Agency reapproved a revised plan in May 2026. ### Both Sides: Cutting EU research partnerships
What is being decided is how far the European Commission may shrink the research partnerships it funds in the next framework programme. Its proposal for the 2028–34 tenth programme, FP10, says public-public and public-private partnerships should be radically simplified and streamlined, and that work is already under way. The EU now funds about 60 of them, from brain health to robotics, so Union money can be joined with national budgets and contributions from participating organisations.

Five MEPs who help design FP10, including Christian Ehler and René Repasi, the Parliament's leads on the legislation, published a joint statement last week. Their strongest case is that measurement science is shared infrastructure, not a niche: the metrology partnership underpins health, industry, energy, digital work and defence, so it must continue. They said simplification is welcome but crude reduction is not, and they objected to shrinking the number of partnerships and reorganising the rest along sectoral lines. They asked the Commission for a well-founded, reasoned proposal and said the absence of metrology will not be accepted.

The Commission did not answer Research Professional News before publication. The Council of member-state governments said the winnowing has advanced substantially in recent months. Its case for going on is practical: portfolio reviews are testing strategic coherence, critical mass, room to merge efforts, fit with other instruments, and whether a dedicated partnership still adds value.

Both sides accept that the partnership system should be simplified. The reporting does not show agreement on how many partnerships remain, or on metrology.
