### Lead
**32 Countries Condemn Iran’s State-Backed Threats, Cite RAF Fairford Incident: Kurdistan24**
Foreign ministers from 32 countries, including the United States, the United Kingdom, France, Germany and Australia, published a joint statement on Monday condemning what they described as Iran’s use of violent attacks, covert networks and proxy actors. The ministers recognised the United Kingdom’s attribution of Iran’s involvement in an incident at RAF Fairford on 27 September, and its assessment that the event showed the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps’ continued willingness to act beyond Iran’s borders through covert networks and proxy actors. They listed alleged activity from lethal plotting and arson to sabotage, cyberattacks and other hybrid operations, and tied their concern to a recent campaign of attacks across Europe against Jewish communities, Iranian journalists and US interests, which the statement said was claimed by Harakat Ashab al-Yamin al-Islamiya, or HAYI. The statement said any attempt by Iran to threaten, silence, intimidate, harass, harm or coerce people on their territories through proxies or any other means is unacceptable, and warned that people acting for Iran or any other state would face efforts to identify, investigate, disrupt and prosecute them. The ministers pledged to hold those responsible accountable and to strengthen coordination. The Guardian reported that Andy Burnham said the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps were “almost certainly” behind threats to RAF Fairford, and described that as the firmest attribution of responsibility made so far by the UK. ### Across the Region
**What is holding back Cyprus's Schengen ambitions?: DW**
The European Commission said in July that Cyprus is technically ready to join the Schengen Area after implementing 120 recommendations from evaluations dating to 2021, covering border controls, visa policy, access to European security databases and police cooperation. On 1 October, Deputy Minister for Migration and International Protection Nicholas Ioannides said all of those recommendations are now considered implemented, and that the next step is a political agreement; with Ireland, Cyprus is one of only two EU members outside the zone. Joining still requires a unanimous decision by the EU Council after consultation with the European Parliament. According to information obtained by DW, Austria and the Netherlands have informally expressed reservations over security concerns linked to the Green Line and the British Sovereign Base Areas, without officially threatening a veto; both previously objected to Bulgaria and Romania joining. DW describes the Green Line as the roughly 180-kilometre ceasefire line dividing the Republic of Cyprus from the Turkish-controlled north since Turkey’s military intervention in 1974, not an internationally recognised state border, with the UN buffer zone monitored by UNFICYP and crossings at nine points under a special EU regulation. **Norway Details Funding Allocation for Second Andøya Launch Pad: European Spaceflight**
In its proposed state budget, published on 7 October, the Norwegian government allocated 323.3 million kroner, about 30 million euros, in 2027 toward a second orbital launch pad at Andøya Spaceport. Of that sum, 223.5 million kroner would come from the Ministry of Defence and 99.5 million kroner from the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Fisheries. The project’s total budget is 646 million kroner, with the remainder expected in the 2028 budget, and Norway is targeting 2028 for completion, though parliament still has to approve the 2027 money before construction can begin. Under a revised model, the state would build and own the infrastructure, while majority state-owned Andøya Space would operate the site and lease it on terms not yet confirmed. The Norwegian Armed Forces are expected to take over security of the area around the spaceport; today Andøya Space handles safety during launches and the police handle public security.
