### Lead
**‘This is terrorism’: daytime Russian strike hits science academy in Kyiv: The Guardian Europe**
A Russian drone hit the National Academy of Sciences in central Kyiv on Monday, killing at least one person, in the latest daytime attack on the capital. Kyiv mayor Vitali Klitschko said three others were wounded and taken to hospital. Flames tore through the 19th-century building as people climbed from first- and second-floor windows; it stands near the German embassy, the National Opera, St Volodymyr’s Cathedral and several government buildings. Academy president Anatoliy Zagorodny said the blast struck while many staff were inside, called it an attack on science and terrorism, said the academy is not connected with the military, and feared colleagues could still be trapped. The Guardian reports the institution has longstanding ties to Ukraine’s defence sector, and that first vice-president Volodymyr Horbulin, a former high-ranking security official, was among the injured. Another Russian drone struck a nearby private medical centre, killing at least one person. Foreign minister Andrii Sybiha accused Russia of cynicism for targeting a scientific institution during the working day, and said every attack left without a strong response only encourages the next. The Guardian reports that missiles and increasingly sophisticated jet-powered drones over the past week have hit apartment blocks, offices, warehouses and datacentres across the capital. **Europe’s AI gap is becoming a productivity problem: EU Today**
In 2025, 20 percent of EU companies with at least 10 employees were using artificial intelligence, up from 13.5 percent a year earlier, and the share among large companies was 55 percent. Denmark led at 42 percent, Finland was at almost 38 percent, and Sweden and Belgium were both at about 35 percent. IMF managing director Kristalina Georgieva told EU finance ministers in Dublin on 21 September that US companies are increasingly using AI to overhaul entire business processes, while European companies tend to apply it more narrowly. The IMF estimates AI could eventually add between 0.1 and 0.8 percentage points to annual global potential growth. The 2026 Stanford AI Index puts US private AI investment at 285.9 billion dollars in 2025, more than 23 times the 12.4 billion dollars recorded for China, and says American generative-AI investment exceeded the combined total for China and Europe. A European Commission-backed examination found European investors provide most funding for EU AI start-ups in rounds below 10 million euros, but their participation falls to 26 percent in deals above 25 million euros.
