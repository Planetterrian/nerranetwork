### Lead
**Spanish police remove over 3,000 migrants from Ceuta beach camp: Euronews**
Spanish police began dismantling a migrant settlement on Trampolín Beach in Ceuta on Sunday, escorting people onto buses while officers in riot gear moved through the crowded site. Euronews reported that more than 3,000 migrants had reportedly been living there for weeks in hundreds of tents, often sleeping on cardboard sheets. The operation brings in the National Police, the Civil Guard, local police and the Ceuta General Command. People taken off the beach are going by bus to the CATE temporary reception centre for initial police processing, so the camp is being replaced by formal processing rather than left in place. **UK Green Party votes to define Zionism as racism: The Business Standard**
Britain’s Green Party voted at its annual conference to define Zionism as a form of racism and an “ethnonationalist political project.” The motion passed by 1,022 votes to 813. It calls on the UK government to end all arms trade with Israel, backs the Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions movement and a full arms embargo, and supports a single democratic state throughout historic Palestine plus the Palestinian right of return. Leader Zack Polanski, who did not vote, and other members then pledged to oppose “genocide, ethnic cleansing, apartheid and the illegal occupation” and criticised the Netanyahu government; co-deputy leader Mothin Ali tied the vote to the casualty toll of Israel’s war on Gaza. Israel’s Foreign Ministry said the policy went “far beyond the boundaries of legitimate political debate,” accused the party of adopting “the elimination of the State of Israel through armed struggle,” and said it now considers the Greens as legitimising terrorism; President Isaac Herzog called the motion “a disgrace and a shame,” and the UK’s Movement for Progressive Judaism said the party had “made antisemitism party policy.” Source: [tbsnews.net](https://www.tbsnews.net/worldbiz/europe/uk-green-party-votes-define-zionism-racism-1563041)

**Russia's invasion of Ukraine on track to be one of the most expensive wars of the century: The New Voice of Ukraine**
The Moscow Times, as reported by The New Voice of Ukraine, says the Kremlin has earmarked a record 35 percent of its 2027 budget for the military, a proposed 17.1 trillion rubles, or 203 billion dollars. Janis Kluge of the German Institute for International and Security Affairs estimates Russia has spent 58.3 trillion rubles, about 692 billion dollars, on the military and weapons procurement over the first five years of the war. The draft projects accumulated military spending of 907 billion dollars by 2028, above the central bank’s 743 billion dollars in gold and foreign-currency reserves, and earmarks 50 trillion rubles, 593 billion dollars, for defense over three years. For 2027 the draft cuts economic support by 1.76 billion dollars, housing and utilities by 1.98 billion dollars, healthcare by 700 million dollars and social support by 5.38 billion dollars. Kiril Rogov of Re: Russia said the budget shows Moscow “will keep pouring funds into the war while forcing Europe to decide if it is prepared to sustain its support” for Ukraine. ### The Region and the World
**Former European leaders urge EU sanctions over Israel’s E1 plan: Philenews**
Around 500 former European leaders, diplomats and senior officials have asked the European Union to act at once against Israel’s E1 settlement plan in the occupied West Bank, in an article published Monday in Libération. The plan provides for more than 1,200 homes near Jerusalem; the authors say Israel is expected to award contracts within weeks for a first phase, after the judiciary on September 27 delayed tenders by a month so as not to affect parliamentary elections at the end of October. They want the European Commission to propose sanctions on financial institutions and other service providers involved in E1 and in settlements they call illegal, a ban on all EU trade with those settlements, and suspension of the trade provisions of the EU-Israel association agreement, for adoption when EU foreign ministers meet on October 12. Signatories include former French prime minister Alain Juppé, former French foreign minister Dominique de Villepin, former Swedish prime minister Stefan Löfven and former European Parliament president Josep Borrell, who accuse Germany and Italy of systematically blocking EU-level measures. ### Both Sides: Overriding Scotland’s nuclear ban
The question is whether Westminster should pass a law to set aside Scotland’s block on new nuclear plants. Shadow energy secretary Andrew Bowie is to announce at the Conservative conference today that a Tory government would legislate so “no part of Great Britain misses out on plans for new nuclear renewal.” The pledge applies if the Conservatives win the next general election. It is a proposal, not a statute.

Bowie’s case is that the SNP’s effective ban “flies in the face of common-sense,” costs jobs, harms energy security and ends what he calls Scotland’s association with the nuclear industry. He says the plans would deliver thousands of jobs, and he told First Minister John Swinney to “bring it on” if he wants a fight. The Labour UK government already wants small modular reactors in Scotland. Suggested sites with grid connections are Torness in East Lothian, Hunterston in North Ayrshire and Dounreay in Caithness. Torness, the last plant still running, is scheduled to close by 2030. Hunterston A shut in 1990 and Hunterston B in 2022. Nuclear industry leaders say the UK has paid almost twice as much to turn off wind farms in Scotland as it plans to spend on small modular reactors by the end of this decade.

The Scottish Government’s stated case is its effective ban itself: it says it is focusing on renewable energy instead, and it holds the planning powers that have blocked the UK plan. A law of the kind Bowie describes would be written to override that choice.

Both sides are arguing from the same facts: Holyrood’s planning powers have blocked new nuclear, the UK government wants reactors in Scotland, and Torness is due to close by 2030. The next step named in the reporting is Bowie’s announcement today. Any statute would follow only if the Conservatives win and then legislate. Source: [scotsman.com](https://www.scotsman.com/news/politics/tories-vow-to-change-law-to-override-snp-on-new-nuclear-power-9264927)
