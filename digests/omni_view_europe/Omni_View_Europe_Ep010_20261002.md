> **France's fifty-four-billion-euro budget plan rests on growth its own watchdog calls optimistic.**

**What You Need to Know:** France's fifty-four-billion-euro budget plan rests on growth its own watchdog calls optimistic. Ministers want the 2027 deficit at 5.0 percent of GDP, down from 5.4 percent, while the High Council for Public Finance calls the 1.0 percent growth assumption optimistic and notes the budget still has to pass. Separately, Brussels and Kyiv say Ukraine needs no further outside money in 2026 after a new tranche.

### Lead
**French government forecasts 1% growth in 2027: what do experts think?: Euronews**
The High Council for Public Finance said the draft budget and social security financing bills, presented Thursday to the Council of Ministers, rest on a 1.0 percent growth forecast it calls optimistic. The council, chaired by Amélie de Montchalin, First President of the Court of Audit, also cites significant uncertainty, including over whether the 2027 budget is adopted at all. The draft aims to bring the public deficit to 5.0 percent of GDP in 2027, from 5.4 percent this year, at the cost of an effort the government puts at 54 billion euros — which Hadrien Camatte of Natixis CIB says would be a fifth straight year at or above 5 percent. New measures total 43 billion euros, 25 billion in spending cuts and 18 billion in extra revenue, plus 12 billion euros already decided this year. Camatte says the central government deficit would still widen to 5.0 percent of GDP from 4.8 percent in 2026, on 10.3 billion euros more debt servicing, 6.5 billion more for defence and 2.8 billion more for the EU budget, while social security is aimed at a 0.2 percent surplus, from a 0.1 percent deficit, including about 4 billion euros from a partial pension freeze and 500 million from a freeze on family benefits. ### Across the Region
**EU says Ukraine no longer requires additional funds in 2026: Pravda**
Paula Pinho, chief spokesperson of the European Commission, said on Friday that a joint meeting on 1 October found Ukraine has no need for additional international funding in 2026 and no external financing gap. She said the Commission had never stated that a gap existed, and that the conclusion came after sitting down with Ukrainian partners. A joint statement on 1 October said the two sides had found ways to fund Ukraine's budget and defence for 2026. On 2 October the Commission allocated a regular 2.9 billion euro tranche under the Ukraine Facility, with part of the money provided through a 90 billion euro support loan. On 29 September, Enlargement Commissioner Marta Kos said the EU was meeting its funding commitments and called on Kyiv to unite for a reform drive in return. ### The Region and the World
**Kremlin Says US-Russia Economic Deals Can Start Before Ukraine Peace: Kyiv Post**
Any US-Russia economic opening still runs through the war in Ukraine: Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said on Thursday that Moscow wants mutually beneficial projects to start before a settlement, while, as he put it, Washington insists the settlement come first. His comments followed Washington talks by President Vladimir Putin's envoy Kirill Dmitriev, which a US official called constructive and described as focused on ending the war and on possible energy cooperation only after the fighting stops. Reuters reported that Dmitriev met representatives of the US Treasury and Energy departments; Peskov said Dmitriev had briefed Putin and that dialogue was continuing. The Kremlin has also said that in September President Donald Trump urged Putin to end the war in order to unlock trade Moscow called large-scale, and that Putin answered with his reading of the battlefield and of Russia's war aims. On Wednesday Peskov dismissed reports that Moscow was discussing the release of Russian political prisoners in exchange for an easing of US sanctions. ### Both Sides: Must Google share search data with rivals?
The European Commission has ordered Google, under the Digital Markets Act, to give rival search engines and AI companies access to search data, and Google has asked the General Court to block that order. In July 2026 the Commission required Google to share rankings, entered queries, click-through rates and how often answers are viewed. The Commission's case is that anonymization and other safeguards protect users, and that the data lets competitors, including firms such as OpenAI, improve their services against Google Search. It says opening those services would also let a user call up a rival assistant by voice, for example to book a taxi or look something up. If the order is upheld, eligible search providers would receive the data by January 2027, and Android changes meant to benefit users would follow by July 2027. Google's case is that sharing may violate European privacy law and put millions of users at risk. It has filed at the General Court, Europe's second-highest court, and sought an interim measure in Luxembourg, arguing the order risks serious harm if it takes effect first. Google also rejects the Commission's view of how far the Digital Markets Act reaches. Both sides are arguing over an order the Commission has already issued and that Google has formally contested. The next step is the court's handling of that challenge and of the request to pause the handover. Source: [cybernews.com](https://cybernews.com/news/google-eu-competition-lawsuit/)
