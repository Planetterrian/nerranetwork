### Lead
**EU Governments Spend €17.9bn Shielding Consumers from Energy Price Shock: EU Today**
Twenty-five of the EU’s twenty-seven member states have adopted some form of energy support during 2026, and the latest assessment puts the cost at 17.9 billion euros. More than two-thirds of the spending directly suppresses energy prices rather than giving targeted income support. Measures adopted or sufficiently detailed by 4 May were then estimated at 14.5 billion euros, about 0.07 per cent of EU GDP, and the Commission calculated that extending them to the end of 2026 could raise the cost to 38.6 billion euros. The Commission has urged governments to concentrate assistance on vulnerable households and particularly exposed industries; its spring assessment found that roughly three-quarters of the support then in force was not aimed at those groups. EU Today reports that broad price measures can cut household bills at once, but cost more and weaken the incentive to use less energy when supplies are tight. The present shock began with oil and gas disruption from the Middle East conflict. Brussels says Europe does not currently face an immediate security-of-supply problem for the winter of 2026–27, because supplies have been diversified and strategic reserves remain available, while EU Today reports that the petroleum import bill rose 55.8 per cent in the second quarter from a year earlier against a 1.2 per cent rise in volume. Source: [eutoday.net](https://eutoday.net/eu-energy-support-17-9-billion-price-shock/)

### Across the Region
**‘Tsunami’ of Net Restrictions Leaves Turkey’s Online Media Fearing for Future: Balkan Insight**
An Istanbul court on 30 September ordered access blocked to the news site T24 and to its social media accounts under Article 8/A of Turkey’s Internet Law, citing prevention of crime and protection of public order. The order covered 118 items that allegedly weaken the “Turkish family structure” and negatively affect young people’s development on sexual orientation and gender identity. T24, founded in 2009 and described as one of Turkey’s most-read quality journalism platforms, said it removed all 118 pieces inside the four-hour deadline it was given. Access to the entire website was still blocked. Journalists and rights advocates describe the wider wave of blocks, which has spread from LGBTQ+ groups to independent media and to posts about a stock-market scandal, as an unprecedented crackdown. **By Skoda and By Bike: Serbia’s Not Completely Even Election Race: Balkan Insight**
Serbia votes on 25 October after Aleksandar Vucic stepped down as president, a little over a week after a 19 September Instagram video, to lead the Serbian Progressive Party into the contest. In that video he drove an old Skoda and said, “Today, for the first time, I’m doing it all myself – both as a driver and as a man of the people.” He and the party have been in power since 2012. Balkan Insight reports that, despite the rules, they regularly deploy state resources in elections that observers say have not been free or fair for a long time, and that the line between party and state is heavily blurred. Crowdfunding, donations and volunteers are carrying cycling students toward the same vote while Vucic tours by car. ### The Region and the World
**EU irritated by Dutch Min.'s claim that it halted Sierra Leone subsidies over Bolle Jos: NL Times**
A day after the aid halt was tied in public to an extradition fight, the European Commission says Dutch Justice Minister David van Weel is presenting misleading information. Van Weel told Dutch media that Sierra Leone’s development aid was stopped because the country refuses to extradite Jos Leijdekkers, known as Bolle Jos, who faces around 80 years in prison in the Netherlands and Belgium over drug trafficking and related crimes. European Commissioner Magnus Brunner said the suspension was made for technical reasons, administrative errors, and that Sierra Leone had been set to receive another 18 million euros from the EU this year. Brunner also said the EU believes action is required against non-EU countries that harbor major drug lords, while the Commission says a political halt is not legally permitted because extradition was not a condition of the budget-support contract. A Commission spokesperson told ANP that the EU and its member states are examining what measures they can take, and sources close to the Dutch government told NOS that van Weel expects the aid to stay halted until Leijdekkers is extradited. ### Both Sides: Opening Android and search data under the Digital Markets Act
The contested question is whether Google must open Android features to rival artificial-intelligence services and share search data with competitors. Google has appealed two European Commission orders under the Digital Markets Act to the EU General Court in Luxembourg. Oliver Bethell, the company’s senior director of competition, says people use Google Search for personal questions and that the data order would force those queries to be shared without sufficient anonymisation. Google’s strongest stated case is privacy and security: it argues the orders would harm users, because the sharing would not anonymise personal queries enough. The Commission says it will defend the orders in court. It says the orders take into account the integrity and security of the features involved and ensure protection of end users’ personal data. The Commission’s reason is access. It found in July that third-party AI services did not have the same Android access as Google’s Gemini, including activation by a voice keyphrase, and it ordered Google to open eleven specific features from the next platform release in July 2027. The second order requires Google to share search data with other search providers and to include AI chatbots that provide search, which the Commission says were previously ineligible. The Commission says that change would make data-sharing effective and the field with Google Search more level. DuckDuckGo says the EU anonymisation framework is robust and that an appeal only buys Google time. Both sides accept that the Commission issued the two orders in July and that Google is challenging them in the General Court. The orders tie the Android changes to the platform release in July 2027.
