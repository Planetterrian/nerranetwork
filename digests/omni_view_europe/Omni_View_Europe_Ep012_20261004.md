### Lead
**Germany’s Merz visits Kyiv amid fears of ‘hardest’ winter in war: Politico Europe**
German Chancellor Friedrich Merz arrived in Kyiv on Sunday morning for an unannounced one-day visit and is planning to meet President Volodymyr Zelenskyy on air defense, long-range weapons and peace initiatives, Politico Europe reported. The visit is expected to bring new military, financial and economic support pledges. Kyiv has warned it is running out of money to buy weapons and has pointed to a €24 billion defense funding gap; Politico reported that the European Union has answered by accelerating disbursement of a €45 billion loan. Germany is the biggest contributor of financial and military support to Ukraine. This is Merz’s first bilateral visit to Kyiv and his second trip as chancellor, after a May 2025 visit with leaders from France, the United Kingdom and Poland. In Münster on Thursday he said that if Moscow is not stopped, the Russian war machine will sooner or later turn against NATO, and that the fifth winter of the war, beginning in a few weeks, will likely be the hardest yet. The Guardian reported that he arrived with chief executives of German defence companies, said Russian forces are bombing towns, residential buildings and heating plants, and pointed to German help that includes new interception systems against jet drones and repairs for bombed power plants. Zelenskyy told Reuters that Ukraine will increase strikes on Russian oil refineries, and not on civilian objects, because Ukrainian intelligence has documents he says show Vladimir Putin issued a doctrine allowing attacks on infrastructure, logistics, roads, schools and hospitals to pressure people to leave cities. He also told Reuters that a counteroffensive called Vivaldi had recaptured about 140 square kilometres in the Donbas. ### Across the Region
**Bosnians vote as pro-Russian politician looms large over election and ethnic tensions simmer: France 24**
Polls opened Sunday morning in Bosnia and Herzegovina for a general election, France 24 reported, with about 3.4 million voters choosing the three-member presidency, the national parliament, and the heads of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and of Republika Srpska. Associated Press journalists saw polling stations that could not start on time because of trouble with new digital voting technology, introduced over earlier concerns about vote tampering. Milorad Dodik, the former president of Republika Srpska, is banned from public office after a conviction for defying the international envoy who oversees the 1995 peace agreement, and he does not appear on the ballot. Back from a meeting with Vladimir Putin in Moscow, he told a Friday rally in Banja Luka that Bosnia “is dead” and “an impossible country”; Russia’s ambassador, Igor Khalabukov, was there. **Calls grow for Andy Burnham to reverse UK aid cuts as Malawi funding drops to £5m: The Herald**
The SNP and the Scottish Greens are urging Andy Burnham to reverse UK aid cuts after a Center for Global Development analysis of Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office accounts found aid to Malawi is set to fall from £50 million in 2024-25 to £5 million by 2028-29, The Herald reported. That analysis also found £1.6 billion in UK support since 2009 prevented thousands of maternal and child deaths, an estimated 525,000 unsafe abortions and essential healthcare for more than three million people, while humanitarian programmes reached more than 3.5 million people and more than 400,000 children gained access to education. International Development Minister Simita Kumar, SNP MSP Pauline Stafford and Scottish Greens MSP Kate Nevens all called for a rethink. Kumar said similar reductions are planned for Rwanda and Zambia, asked the prime minister to change course, and said Scotland will raise its International Development Fund by 25 percent this parliament and grow its Climate Justice Fund. ### Both Sides: Spain's rejected housing decrees
Spain’s Congress of Deputies on Friday rejected two housing decrees from Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez’s minority government, and on Saturday tens of thousands marched in more than 50 cities, EU Today reported. The government had approved emergency measures whose stated aim was stronger protection against eviction for vulnerable households, changes affecting property investment companies, and a larger supply of affordable rental homes. A second decree included automatic renewal of some rental contracts. Campaigners have treated the eviction of 87-year-old Maricarmen Abascal from the Madrid flat where she had lived for decades as the case for those protections. In Madrid, the government delegation estimated the crowd at about 70,000; organisers put it considerably higher. In Valencia, clashes broke out near a property investment conference at the City of Arts and Sciences. Protests elsewhere were largely peaceful.

Junts opposed the decrees on the ground that they would not resolve the underlying shortage of housing and risked discouraging owners from putting homes on the market. The Popular Party and Vox also voted against them. The government and Junts both treat a shortage of homes as the problem, in cities where rising demand has met limited supply, strong tourism and a large short-term rental market. Spain has already blocked more than 65,000 Airbnb listings that authorities said did not meet the rules, and Barcelona has announced plans to end about 10,000 tourist-apartment licences by 2028 so those homes can return to long-term rent. The two decrees did not pass. Source: [eutoday.net](https://eutoday.net/tens-of-thousands-march-across-spain/)
