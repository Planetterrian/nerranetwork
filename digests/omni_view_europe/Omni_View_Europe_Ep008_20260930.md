### Lead
**UK’s Burnham says he’ll consider push to rejoin EU: Politico Europe**
British Prime Minister Andy Burnham told Labour’s annual conference in Liverpool on Tuesday that the Brexit settlement struck by the Conservatives had done “more harm than good,” and said he would spell out options for Britain’s long-term relationship with European partners at a UK–EU summit later this year. In a BBC interview broadcast Wednesday, he said the government could look at a customs union, as former chancellor George Osborne has suggested, at the single market, as the Liberal Democrats have argued, “or we could go all the way,” judging each by what is doable and by the pros and cons. Politico reports his government will stick, before a general election, to predecessor Keir Starmer’s promise not to rejoin the single market or customs union, while the comments tee up a post-election push a decade on from the Brexit referendum. The Conservatives accuse him of trying to “reopen old wounds by restarting the battles of the Brexit years.” As a candidate in the Makerfield by-election he had said he was “not proposing that the UK considers rejoining the EU”; Labour’s 2024 manifesto pledged to “make Brexit work” while ruling out the customs union and the single market. Sir Keir Starmer’s departure delayed a summer EU summit until later this year, when Burnham has also said he wants to publish a 10-year plan for the UK economy. **EU accused of hiding environmental impact of data centers: Politico Europe**
Europe’s data centers used 20.7 terawatt-hours of electricity in 2025 — about as much as Croatia — and more than 8 million cubic meters of water, Commission figures cited by Politico show, a 26 percent rise in electricity and a 52 percent rise in water from 2024. Those totals are incomplete and published only in aggregate, after a 2024 law barred Brussels and EU capitals from disclosing individual data-center indicators amid tech lobbying that cited commercial interests. Lighthouse Reports says the Commission holds the primary responsibility for denying public knowledge of the build-out, in breach of the Aarhus Convention, which the EU has been a party to since 2005. Energy chief Dan Jørgensen told Politico that if these large power users are not in line with EU objectives, they risk undermining the bloc’s ability to meet its own targets. Conservative MEP Diego Solier said transparency should be the starting point, but should not require disclosure of genuinely commercially sensitive information. Source: [politico.eu](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-complaint-data-center-energy-water-lighthouse-reports/)

### The Region and the World
**World’s seas heated by a record 23 zettajoules last year: The Guardian**
In Europe, heating twice as fast as the global average, the Mediterranean and Black Seas have been hit by record marine heatwaves, with extreme heat lasting more than a month, as a record 23 zettajoules of heat entered the world’s seas last year — almost 40 times the energy humanity used that year, the 10th Copernicus ocean state report found. Sea levels have risen by about 3.8 millimetres a year since 1999, and by 4.2 millimetres a year between 2012 and 2025, and the global ocean has become 17 percent more acidic in the last 40 years. The Mediterranean has warmed by 0.32 degrees Celsius per decade, with a clear acceleration of sea-level rise in the west. Pierre Bahurel, director general of Mercator Ocean International, which published the report, said warming, rising seas and ecosystem degradation are interconnected signs of long-term change. Western Europe is in an unusually warm September after a fiery summer, with temperatures hitting 40 degrees Celsius in parts of Spain and Portugal last week; hot coastal water adds heat on nights that would otherwise cool. ### Both Sides: Lethal conditions on the Polish-Belarusian border
The contested question is whether deaths in the Polish-Belarusian borderlands since 2021 are the result of policies that knowingly exposed migrants to lethal conditions, as a report published on September 8 argues, or whether that account distorts the picture, as Poland’s Interior Ministry says.

The case for the report is led by Katarzyna Czarnota, who researched that border for the Helsinki Foundation for Human Rights from 2021 to 2024 and is a lead researcher on The Death Zone, published by Border Forensics with two research projects. She says hypothermia describes how a person died, not why they were exposed to lethal conditions, and recalls a dispatcher refusing an ambulance for three boys, aged 16 or 17, in the Bialowieza forest after days of eating leaves and drinking from a puddle, because they were “illegal migrants.” Charles Heller, the report’s director of research, says people crossing remain trapped for weeks or months in cycles of push-forwards and pushbacks. Drawing on testimonies, medical data, field research, maps and geostatistics, the researchers examined 102 documented deaths and 214 fragmentary reports of potential disappearances, and allege that policies, infrastructure and enforcement on both sides “knowingly exposed” migrants to conditions already shown to be lethal.

The Interior Ministry’s stated case is that the report distorts the picture. Official records, as Czarnota describes them, stop at physiological causes — hypothermia, drowning, cardiac arrest — which in ordinary language make the cold or the river the culprit rather than the person who left someone there.

Both sides are speaking about deaths that have occurred on that border; the report’s documented count is 102. The article names no dated official review. Source: [balkaninsight.com](https://balkaninsight.com/2026/09/30/poland-belarus-borderlands-how-a-forest-is-made-lethal/rd/)
