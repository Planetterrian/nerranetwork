Here is how it works when a cheap contract and an expensive one do not earn the same return.

In a parimutuel market, the favorite-longshot bias is a pattern in returns, not a slogan about who is clever.

Expected return on an outcome tends to rise as that outcome takes a larger share of the bets.

Longshots, on that definition, are the side where return has historically been worse. Favorites are the side where return has historically been better.

A twenty-ten paper in the American Economic Journal: Microeconomics treats the pattern as a claim to test.

It ties both the direction and the size of the bias to one ratio: private information against noise.

The paper says that ratio should show up in things you can count.

How many people are betting. How many outcomes are listed. How much private information is in the market. How much of the crowd is there for recreation. Whether a bet can be split into small pieces. Whether noise shows up after the event. Whether the outcomes were already unequal before the betting started.
