> **Two tribes won a court order barring Kalshi sports contracts on their California lands.**

**What You Need to Know:** Two tribes won a court order barring Kalshi sports contracts on their California lands. U.S. District Judge Jacqueline Scott Corley entered that preliminary injunction on November 4, limited to the tribes' Indian lands, after the Ninth Circuit found the contracts were likely Class III gaming under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission, in a November 5 interpretation, told designated contract markets to end domestic geographic access restrictions within 30 days or explain why they cannot.

### Top Story
U.S. District Judge Jacqueline Scott Corley of the Northern District of California granted a preliminary injunction on November 4 in Blue Lake Rancheria v. Kalshi Inc., and the order enjoins Kalshi Inc. and KalshiEX from offering sports contracts on the Indian lands of Blue Lake Rancheria and the Chicken Ranch Rancheria of Me-Wuk Indians. The barred contracts are those that facially involve, relate to, or reference baseball, tennis, pickleball, soccer, basketball, football, golf, chess, esports, hockey, motorsports, boxing, wrestling, and Ultimate Fighting Championship events, and the injunction does not bar sports contracts elsewhere in California. A Ninth Circuit panel on September 16 found the tribes likely to succeed on the claim that those contracts are Class III gaming under the Indian Gaming Regulatory Act when entered from tribal lands, held that the Commodity Exchange Act does not displace that claim, reversed Corley's November 2025 denial of an injunction, and upheld dismissal of a Lanham Act false-advertising claim. The tribes must give Kalshi maps of their land boundaries by November 13, and Kalshi must file a report on the steps it has taken to comply within 21 days of the November 4 order. Sports and gaming attorney Daniel Wallach called the ruling the first preliminary injunction obtained by tribes against Kalshi nationwide and "a major development in tribal gaming litigation." Kalshi said in an October joint status report that it had geofenced the plaintiff tribes' lands and that the injunction request was moot; the tribes said a member of the Blue Lake Rancheria Gaming Commission later placed a trade on the app while on the reservation. Robinhood is a defendant in the suit, and the preliminary injunction does not expressly impose the same restrictions on the Robinhood entities. ### The Ecosystem
**CFTC tells contract markets to end domestic geographic restrictions: Commodity Futures Trading Commission**
The Commodity Futures Trading Commission on November 5 issued an interpretation of Commission Regulation 38.151(b), the impartial-access requirement for designated contract markets. Chairman Michael S. Selig said derivatives markets "must operate on a uniform basis at a national scale in order to achieve the purposes set out in the Commodity Exchange Act," and that "Geographic access restrictions are inconsistent with those purposes and the Act's impartial access requirements." Designated contract markets must cease domestic geographic restrictions that violate those requirements within 30 days, or explain to the Division of Market Oversight why compliance is not feasible, and within 30 days they should ensure intermediaries are not impeding that access or explain why that is not feasible. Gambling Insider reports the direction could conflict with court orders requiring geofencing in Nevada, Washington, and Michigan, and that the Second Circuit on Tuesday denied Kalshi and Coinbase motions for an injunction pending appeal. Kalshi has said it was being put in an "impossible position, looking to follow state court orders that may contradict our federal regulatory obligations." ### How It Works: Forecasting an Outbreak With a Market
Seasonal influenza forecasting starts from a running count of infections and asks two different questions: when the wave crests, and how big it is. The systems in today's papers do that by fitting a model to past weeks and then updating it as new observations arrive, the way a weather model takes in a fresh reading. They are not exchanges. No abstract here describes a contract, a trader, or a payout.

The worked example with a stated lead time is the New York City study in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences. For the 2003 through 2008 seasons, researchers assimilated weekly web-based estimates of local infection rates and, after each update, produced a retrospective ensemble of forecasts rather than a single line. They report that skillful real-time predictions of peak timing could be made more than seven weeks before the actual peak. Confidence was not a separate slogan; it was the spread of the ensemble. A narrow spread meant the timing runs agreed. A wide spread meant they did not.

Melbourne shows the split between timing and size. Using Google Flu Trends for 2006 through 2014, a bootstrap particle filter and a mechanistic infection model called the epidemic peak accurately four to six weeks ahead. The magnitude of the peak and the overall burden were much harder to predict. The team chose among hundreds of observation models by treating forecasts like receiver operating characteristic curves. In Fuzhou, SARIMA, Prophet, Holt-Winters, and XGBoost were fit to cases from January 2013 through December 2021 and tested on 2022. XGBoost had the lowest mean squared error, root mean squared error, and mean absolute error on the training set and the test set. The case series was seasonal and cyclical, with an upward trend and large swings. Dante, described in Nature Communications, learns spatial, temporal, and surveillance structure instead of prescribing it, and it issues forecasts that stay coherent across state, regional, and national scales. It beat a Dynamic Bayesian Model for nearly all spatial units, flu seasons, geographic scales, and targets in a retrospective comparison, and it placed first in the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention's prospective 2018/19 FluSight challenge in the national competition, the regional competition, and the state competition.

Where the work is disputed is in what a good score is allowed to decide. The Melbourne authors say peak size and total burden do not come along for free when timing is skillful. CDC scientists, in a BMC Public Health paper on FluSight challenges hosted since the 2013-14 season, write that federal, state, and local officials need to understand how a forecast was made, how to interpret it, and how it performed in the past before it is built into a response. Heterogeneous surveillance, which Dante's authors flag, means a clean national curve can hide a thin state.

A listener reading a market on an outbreak can borrow that discipline without pretending these papers scored a venue. Name the target: peak week is not peak height, and peak height is not total burden. Name the lead time: more than seven weeks in the New York exercise is a different claim from four to six weeks in Melbourne. And do not treat one percentage as a measurement unless an ensemble, a past error, or both are visible. A contract price collapses a crowd into one number. These studies say that number is usable only for the question it actually answered, on the horizon where it actually had skill.
