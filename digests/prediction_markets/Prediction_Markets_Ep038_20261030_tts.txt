Locked collateral is how it works between a trade and a payout: the dollar cannot leave until an oracle settles the claim.

A paper on settlement discounting says a near-certain dollar, in that setup, is a delayed dollar.

The price then holds a belief about the outcome and a separate discount for the wait until cash.

The authors pull that second piece from contracts that stay near certain, using the settlement times those contracts actually realized.

They summarize the curve as an annualized settlement wedge.

The wedges they recover are positive, they change with maturity, and they change over time.

Take the raw prices of those near-certain claims across horizons.

The horizon gradient is how far those prices drift as the wait until settlement gets longer.

Adjust the prices by the recovered curves.

The paper finds that step cuts the near-certainty horizon gradient by roughly forty-eight to eighty-eight percent.

On that measure, most of the raw pattern across maturities is a priced wait, not forecast error alone.

What is left is the slice the wedge does not explain.

Neg-risk conversion compresses the discounts by recycling part of the position into synthetic collateral, so less capital sits idle for the whole wait.

Yield-bearing collateral flattens the term structure by cutting the opportunity cost of the lock-up. The dollar that cannot be withdrawn can still earn something while it waits.

In both designs the price moves because the funding terms moved, not because the event became more or less likely.

The paper does not say the wedge erases the gradient. A cut of forty-eight to eighty-eight percent leaves a remainder.

The measurement is also tied to collateral locked until an oracle redeems the claim. A venue that pays from a different custody setup, or that turns capital over sooner, sits outside these curves.

The limit is the one the measurement states.

A price near certainty means something different once you know when the dollar can actually leave.

A contract that is already sure, but whose collateral cannot move until a later oracle date, is not the same object as a contract that settles at once.

Part of the gap can be the wait, what the collateral earns, and whether the market recycles capital, rather than a different belief about the event.
