Senators passed S. 4035, the DEATH Bets Act, on Thursday, November nineteenth, on a voice vote, and the measure now sits with the House. DeFi Rate and RG.org both carry the floor account.

A registered entity could not list or clear a contract that expressly references terrorism, assassination, war, U.S. military operations, or similar activity the CFTC writes into a rule, and it could not list a contract that, when listed, is reasonably expected to resolve principally because a person has died.

John Curtis of Utah and Adam Schiff of California introduced a wider bill in March, one that would have reached contracts that involve, relate to, or reference those events, or that could be construed as tracking closely to a death.

The substitute they passed adds a rule of construction: this statute does not rewrite the law on other event contracts, gambling included, does not touch the sports-contract lawsuits already in court, and does not hand the CFTC power over sports contracts.

Mike Levin's companion, H.R. 7942, has been parked since April in the House Agriculture subcommittee that covers commodity markets and digital assets. A stated probability and the weight a person puts on that outcome come apart under rank-dependent utility, including prospect theory, and that bend is how it works in the research on probability weighting. The weight need not equal the chance. Small stated chances can count for more than their face value; chances near certainty can count for less.

The shape estimated again and again is an inverse S, concave first, then convex.

Wu and Gonzalez, in Management Science in 1996, refused to bake that shape into the estimate. Older studies had already pointed to an inverse S, but fitting it had required a guessed formula for value, for weights, or for both.

They gave two preference conditions that are necessary and sufficient for concavity, and two's partner for convexity, and that can be checked without assuming how value is shaped. The tool was a preference ladder, a run of questions that differ only by a common consequence. Those ladders lined up with an S, concave up to a probability below zero point four zero and convex after that.

Nonlinearity showed up away from the endpoints, zero and one, not only at the edges. The same ladder data were fit to functions Tversky and Kahneman proposed in 1992 and Prelec proposed in 1995.

Abdellaoui, in Management Science in 2000, split the job and kept it free of a preset formula. Utility comes out first, decision weights second, under rank-dependent expected utility and under cumulative prospect theory. For money, utility was concave on gains and convex on losses. The weights satisfied upper and lower subadditivity and matched earlier parametric estimates.

In that sample the weighting function on losses sat higher, more elevated, than the one on gains.

A 2010 paper in Strategic Management Journal argued that strategy scholars had inferred the wrong predictions from prospect theory. The value function, it said, implies negative risk-return links for high performers and positive links for low performers, the opposite of that literature.

Isolation means most firm choices should look like mixed gambles, gains and losses together, while earlier strategy work had treated them as unmixed. And the theory does not spit out one general forecast; the forecast depends on parameters and on features of the choice that research had ignored.

A 2005 review in the Annual Review of Political Science noted a different cold shoulder: security scholars in international relations had used the theory a lot, while Americanists, comparativists, and political economists mostly had not.

That review blamed discomfort with behavioral assumptions, not a defect unique to the theory, and left the jump from the lab to political decisions in the field as an open problem.

Hold the market price as a traded price, not as that laboratory weight. A contract implying a small probability sits where the 1996 ladders found stated chances overweighted. A price near certainty sits where the curve had turned convex and the weight came in under the stated chance. Abdellaoui's split between gains and losses says the bend need not match on both sides.

None of this is a reason to buy or sell anything, and none of it says a venue has already run the laboratory curve through its price. The screen and the choice experiment are different measurements.
