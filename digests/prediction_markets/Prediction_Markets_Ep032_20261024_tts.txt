Quoting both sides of an order book is how it works for a market maker: post a buy and a sell, collect the spread, and hold the inventory until someone takes the other side. A finished trade is the moment those two interests meet. Until then, the person who posted the quote still carries the position. That is the job a research paper studies on Polymarket.

The authors call those books thin. On a thin book, the maker's own quote may be the only price a later trader can hit. They name three snags. A policy trained on real fills may simply stop quoting, because the trader who hits it can know more than the quote. Profit or loss from a quote can show up many steps later, so a learner has trouble tying the result to the earlier post.

Level-two feeds do not identify single orders, and a simulator needs that detail.

Their fix was to rebuild order-level records in LOBSTER format from Polymarket and to train agents in JaxMARL-HFT on four hundred seventeen real markets. They gave the baseline maker a wider view, the book plus recent flow, and they changed its actions and its inventory controls.

They also split the desk into a quoter and a risk manager, trained with recurrent Independent Proximal Policy Optimisation. To connect a late result to an early quote, they tried blended rewards and a post-fill markout, a score of how price moves after a fill.

The redesigned single agent quoted more often and lost less on held-out markets than the retrained baseline. Observations, actions, and risk limits were changed together, so the paper cannot say which change did the work. A split desk with no careful rewards did poorly. Shaped rewards beat that unshaped split and gave the authors their best result.

Scoring was end-of-episode portfolio value on markets kept out of training. No configuration showed a positive average portfolio value on those periodic checks. Each setup used one seed. The authors call the environment, the data, and the protocol a base for quoting many linked markets at once. They do not claim that base already keeps a positive average once the markets are new.

Read the number on a thin screen as one quote that still has to be held. A print has found a counterparty. A resting quote has not. Reward design changed whether the split desk kept posting. More quotes and smaller held-out losses still did not produce a positive average portfolio value on the checks.
