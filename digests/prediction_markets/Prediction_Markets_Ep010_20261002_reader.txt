Here is how it works when a price is supposed to gather what separate people know. The trade is the message. A person with a private signal buys or sells, the quote shifts, and the next arrival meets a number that already holds that signal. An automated market maker can keep posting the quote with no human on the other side.

A twenty fourteen paper on arXiv, Information Aggregation in Exponential Family Markets, shows those makers can be built from exponential family distributions, a standard statistical template. It links the pooling inside a price to the way learning agents pool beliefs when they use that same family.

Where traders are risk averse in the form of exponential utility, the paper works through market behavior and the price equilibrium. It also takes up traders limited by a budget rather than by that utility alone.

A last print is a thinner record than it looks. A twenty twenty-five arXiv paper, Are Final Market Prices Sufficient for Information Aggregation?, looks at parimutuel horse-race betting, where a wager cannot be written against the final odds. Interim odds show expected returns depend on the path, not only the finish.

Horses whose odds fell in the final five minutes earned higher realized returns than horses with similar final odds. A two-period extension of the Ottaviani and Sørensen information model accounts for that, with the late drop coming from informed bettors putting money on private signals.

The paper adds that patterns between final odds and returns, on their own, may not split real information aggregation from distorted probabilities.

So the last price is not a full file of what people knew, or of when they acted. The path into the number matters, and so does whether anyone could contract on the price that eventually printed. That racing result describes those markets. It is not a method for any contract.
