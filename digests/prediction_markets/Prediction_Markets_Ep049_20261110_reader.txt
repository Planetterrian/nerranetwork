A noise trader is someone whose order is not a clean read of the facts, and that is how it works in the papers that treat this flow as part of the price, not a leftover.

In that model, governance improves liquidity only when the market also has noise traders the authors call semi-informed. The strength of the effect depends on how much those traders participate. Governance and that participation have what the model calls a synergistic effect. Pull the semi-informed flow out, and the liquidity channel disappears.

The step underneath is the counterparty. Someone with a better signal can trade only if someone else will take the other side. If everyone waits on the same information, there is no trade, and the improvement never shows up as liquidity. This paper models stocks, not event contracts. The piece it isolates still travels: money that is uninformed, or only partly informed, is not only error.

In this account it is a condition for anyone else to trade at all.

A twenty fifteen study in the International Journal of Financial Management looks for that mix in Indian equities. The authors use daily trades plus intra-day prices, volumes, and trade times at one-day and five-day intervals for the Nifty-Fifty names listed on both the National Stock Exchange and the Bombay Stock Exchange, and market returns from the NSE Nifty and the BSE Sensex.

They ask whether return variation includes noise-trader risk, and whether short-run arbitrage by informed traders pushes them into long-short hedges. Noise, they argue, has a systematic piece and a firm-specific piece, and both move through time. Those pieces include idiosyncratic shocks and noise shocks.

At lags, a long-short book across these markets can hedge the fundamental systematic shock and the fundamental firm-specific shock, and may peel the noise shock off them. Once the same stocks are held on long-short horizons, that long-short return is what exposes noise running across names.

Set beside the same exercise two years earlier, the abstract's pattern is directional, not a published coefficient. Intra-day returns from the one-day and five-day data impound significant noise. Daily returns show high exposure. Weekly returns show moderate exposure.

In the GARCH models, conditional volatility of those long-short returns shows the time-varying idiosyncratic noise highly persistent when noise traders are present. During the trading day, the study says, prices impound information and noise together.

A twenty sixteen paper in Qualitative Research in Financial Markets challenges the habit of calling every individual trader a dumb noise trader. Experienced researchers and practitioners rated the math and verbal demands of investment tasks from earlier studies.

On that rating, individual investors can do a number of complex cognitive actions, especially higher-order verbal reasoning, and they look more limited where the math gets harder. The authors note that the math-heavy tasks are the ones considered more relevant to performance. They also fence the result. The measure is indirect, so they will not claim a definite score for retail competence.

A direct test would be a lab experiment, and they say that data was not available for the retail investors in view. Ten tasks is all they could analyze, which limits statistical power and blocks a deeper deductive test.

Their notes stay on disclosure: put key investor documents mostly in words, tell people what studies like these found, and offer online math practice built for individual investors. That is not a trading rule.

The twenty twenty piece is a model, and its noise trader is semi-informed, not blank. The twenty fifteen study finds noise that stays persistent inside the day in one equity market during a recovery. It does not size that effect on an event contract.

The twenty sixteen authors reject a blanket label, then mark how narrow their own rejection is: ten tasks, no direct performance test. What you can still do with a contract price is smaller than a slogan and more useful. A move can be information, or it can be the other side of an order that was never about the fact you are watching.

Liquidity, in the twenty twenty account, is partly supplied by that second group. Persistence, in the twenty fifteen account, means the noise need not be gone by the close. The twenty sixteen assessment says the people in that flow are uneven, stronger on verbal reasoning than on the math performance is said to require, not uniformly lost.

None of this names which side of a live contract is the informed side. A price is a trade, and a trade needs a counterparty who may not share your reason for being there.
