A federal funds futures price is a traded rate, and that is how it works as a forecast of policy only after someone does extra arithmetic. The quote prices the average federal funds rate over a future month. Getting from there to the chance of a decision at one meeting means stripping a term premium, then fitting what is left onto the discrete choices the committee actually has.

A twenty sixteen note in the Federal Reserve’s FEDS Notes series studies those front-end premiums with futures plus the Desk’s Survey of Primary Dealers and its Survey of Market Participants, and it treats the premium you subtract as an assumption, not as a figure the futures price states on its own. Divide the remainder by the size of the step on the menu. Swap the premium and the probability moves while the futures price sits still.

A two thousand nine paper in the Journal of Money, Credit and Banking says many premium estimates assume average forecast errors are zero, or that surveys stand in for expectations. An unanticipated slide in the funds rate breaks the first assumption. Strategic answers from survey respondents break the second. Premiums built that way can be biased.

Their alternative method finds premiums often negative since two thousand, generally in line with the negative betas of that decade. A negative premium puts the futures rate below the expected future rate, so treating the raw quote as the expectation understates the chance of higher settings.

A two thousand five paper in the Journal of Futures Markets uses options on those same futures, which began trading in March two thousand three. Because Federal Open Market Committee choices are discrete, ordinary least squares can recover the implied density, unlike the heavier tools used when equities, foreign exchange, or commodity futures are modeled as continuous.

That fit can cover one meeting or several at once, and it can force the probabilities to respect restrictions inside a meeting and across meetings. The authors then use those probabilities to see how data releases and Fed communication shift the perceived likelihood of actual outcomes. The two thousand nine objection is aimed at the shortcuts, not at the quote itself.

The twenty sixteen note does not pick a single true premium. It asks which assumptions look plausible next to futures and those two surveys.
