### How It Works: Hedging With Event Contracts
An event contract pays if a stated outcome occurs and pays nothing if it does not. Using that payoff as a hedge means the gain is meant to offset a loss on something the holder already has, so the contract is doing a job closer to insurance than to a standalone wager. The price is what the other side will accept to take the remaining risk.

Good Authority, citing Marieke de Goede’s Virtue, Fortune, Faith: A Genealogy of Finance, says nineteenth-century regulators separated bucket-shop gambling from legitimate futures by whether losses could be quantified and modeled. In that account, a gambler faces the house. A hedger shifts risk toward someone with a different appetite for it.

A 2020 paper in the journal Risks works the arithmetic of a second trade: after the price moves, take the opposite position to the first. The result it derives is that a profit can be locked in without a forecast of the final outcome. Two frictions break that result in practice. An exchange charges a fee. Some markets have no lay option, so there is no way to take the against side at all.

A 2026 paper on arXiv uses Bitcoin as a proof of concept and treats Kalshi contracts on the coin’s future price as option-like hedges, in bullish, bearish, and flat regimes. The authors report that a dynamic mix of those contracts and the spot coin beat simpler portfolios across all three regimes. They also say fees can erode the result. That is a research claim about those constructions, not a plan to copy.

Regulators still have to decide which use is the real one. Good Authority reports that roughly 90 percent of Kalshi’s trading volume is sports contests; that Nevada, Minnesota, New York, and Washington have tried to ban those bets; and that the CFTC has filed suits asserting that it alone regulates futures markets. The Third Circuit sided with the CFTC in April. The Sixth and Ninth Circuits have ruled for the states on at least some of these markets. The Supreme Court may eventually hear some of the cases on appeal. A price is easier to read as insurance, or harder, once the fee, the right to take the other side, and the court that claims the contract are all in view.
