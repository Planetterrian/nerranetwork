Here is how it works when two prices on one question refuse to line up. A winner-take-all set is meant to cover every outcome and let only one of them pay, so the related prices should add to a dollar.

Miss that dollar, on either side, and you have a bound that comes from the settlement payoff. You do not automatically have a trade you can finish while the book is still open.

A twenty twenty-five paper on arXiv looks at Polymarket and splits those gaps in two. Rebalancing sits inside a single market. Combinatorial gaps run across related markets.

From on-chain order-book history, the authors put extracted profit near forty million dollars. Checking every linked condition does not scale, so they cut the search by timing, topic, and how the contracts combine.

A twenty twenty-six arXiv paper draws a second line. Payoff-space no-arbitrage is what the terminal payoffs imply. Protocol-executable no-arbitrage is what a trader can actually force with the conversions the venue offers before settlement.

On Polymarket's negative-risk markets, linked binaries stand for outcomes that cannot all be true, but the NegRisk Adapter only makes the no-to-yes direction usable before settlement.

Rebuilding depth-aware portfolios and on-chain conversion traces, the authors estimate one point one two million dollars of arbitrage profit. One point zero eight six million came from converter-enabled strategies, and thirty-two thousand from baskets held through to settlement.

In the order-book sample, positive violations piled up on the yes side the adapter does not support. No-side violations the adapter does support were less common and died faster.

Their reading is that a conversion you can use before settlement tightens the bound, because capital is not locked up and inventory can be recycled. They also built a prototype that would make the reverse path tradable before settlement.

The same constraint shows up when two prices disagree. The gap is enforceable only if a trader can turn one position into the other before resolution. If that conversion is missing, the bound can just sit there until the contract pays.
