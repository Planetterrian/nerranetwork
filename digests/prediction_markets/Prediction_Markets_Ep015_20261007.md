### Top Story
On Wednesday, four tribes in California and Oklahoma launched prediction apps with Kalshi, CNN reports, among them the Kletsel Dehe Wintun Nation in an isolated part of northern California. Administrator Eric Wright told CNN the reservation does not have potable drinking water and that he hopes revenue from the tribe's new Kalshi-backed app will fund water projects. Wright said the tribe is not a large gaming tribe with money lying around, and that the decision is about tribal sovereignty and economic self-determination: other tribes may disagree, but they do not have veto power over that economic future. Hundreds of other tribes claim Kalshi and Polymarket are illegally encroaching on gaming rights and are pushing in court to ban sports prediction markets, which the report says make up over 80 percent of the business. ### The Ecosystem
**Gaming regulators seek Supreme Court review: Indian Gaming Magazine:** After appellate courts split on preemption, the International Association of Gaming Regulators and the North American Gaming Regulators Association are supporting U.S. Supreme Court review in Flaherty v. KalshiEX, LLC, Indian Gaming Magazine reports. Their joint amicus brief, prepared with counsel from the International Masters of Gaming Law, addresses whether the Commodity Exchange Act preempts state gambling laws when sports wagers are offered as event contracts on exchanges registered with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission. In preliminary-injunction proceedings, the Third Circuit concluded that federal law likely preempts application of New Jersey's sports-wagering laws to the challenged contracts; the Ninth Circuit reached a contrary conclusion concerning Nevada's laws. The brief is a request that the Court resolve that conflict, not a judgment. It notes that Murphy v. NCAA in 2018 invalidated the Professional and Amateur Sports Protection Act and left states to authorize and regulate sports wagering or keep their prohibitions. Ben Haden, president of the International Association of Gaming Regulators, said sports wagering through prediction markets can leave consumers without the protections gaming regulators provide, including safeguards against underage access and harm for people with gambling problems, and can put the integrity of sport at risk. ### How It Works: Superforecasters and Forecasting Tournaments
The method that won a U.S. Intelligence Community geopolitical forecasting tournament was to cull top performers each year and assign them to elite teams. A 2015 paper in Perspectives on Psychological Science calls those teams' members superforecasters. They maintained high accuracy across hundreds of questions and a wide array of topics, defying expectations of regression toward the mean two years in a row. The authors find support for four mutually reinforcing explanations: cognitive abilities and styles, task-specific skills, motivation and commitment, and enriched environments. Superforecasters, they write, are partly discovered and partly created. The high-performance incentives of tournaments, the paper says, highlight aspects of human judgment that would not come to light in laboratory paradigms focused on typical performance.

A 2014 paper in Psychological Science describes the setting. Five university-based research groups competed to recruit forecasters, elicit predictions, and aggregate those predictions into probabilities for events in a two-year geopolitical forecasting tournament. One group found support for three psychological drivers: training, teaming, and tracking. Probability training corrected cognitive biases, encouraged the use of reference classes, and supplied heuristics, such as averaging when multiple estimates were available. Teaming let forecasters share information and discuss the rationales behind their beliefs. Tracking placed the highest performers — the top 2 percent from year one — on elite teams that worked together. Training, team collaboration, and tracking improved both calibration and resolution. Statistical algorithms, reported elsewhere, improved the accuracy of the aggregation. Putting statistics and psychology to work produced the best forecasts two years in a row.

Those abstracts do not show that an event-contract price automatically inherits that record. They do show that accuracy moved when selection, training, teams, and a statistical aggregation rule changed. A price is easier to read as a forecast when the pool and the combination rule are visible, and harder when they are not.
