How a price gets set is the marginal trader: the account willing to buy the next contract, or sell it, not the crowd that merely holds a view. Everyone else can watch and leave the quote alone. If that willing trader is active and careful, the number can look like a forecast even when most accounts never update a belief. If the book is quiet, the number can be one desk.

The Iowa Electronic Markets show why thickness matters. On September twenty-ninth, a thinly traded vote-share market forecast a nine-point Democratic popular-vote margin for the twenty twenty-four presidential election. The same day, a thickly traded winner-takes-all market priced an eighty-five point seven percent chance the Democrat would get more votes than the Republican.

Blend the two, as the paper in PS: Political Science and Politics does, and the margin forecast falls to six to seven points while the win chance edges to eighty-seven percent. The thin book was the decisive-looking margin. The thick book barely moved the win chance, and the authors still say significant uncertainty remains.

A second result from those markets shows how large one trader can be. A market-making program written for one set of their securities traded thirty-four percent of total volume and posted a Sharpe ratio of nine point nine. In a small real-money market, one system was about a third of the tape. The price was whoever kept a bid and an offer up, not the median person who opened an account and left.

That paper is also the argument against reading volume as accuracy. It presents the program as profiting from inefficiencies. A trader can pin the quote and still be harvesting a quote that was off. Dominance of the tape and a good forecast are different facts.

Combining the thin vote-share book with the thick winner-takes-all book changed the margin and did not, in the authors' words, remove the uncertainty.

Read a price as a trade, then ask who had to be willing for it to print. On that September night the thin contract and the blend disagreed by points, nine against six to seven. A book where one program is a third of volume can change hands all day and still be one trader's inventory. Play money and dollars are different stakes. The quote still moves only when someone hits it.
