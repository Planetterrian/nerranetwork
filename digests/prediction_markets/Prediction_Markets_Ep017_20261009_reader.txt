Here is how it works when a contract is used as a hedge rather than as a standalone wager.

The contract pays if a stated outcome occurs and pays nothing if it does not.

The hedge is the idea that this payoff offsets a loss on something the holder already has. The price is what the other side will take to accept the leftover risk.

Good Authority, citing Marieke de Goede’s book Virtue, Fortune, Faith, says nineteenth-century regulators split bucket-shop gambling from legitimate futures by asking whether losses could be quantified and modeled.

In that account, a gambler faces the house. A hedger shifts risk to someone whose appetite for that risk is different.
