Sean Casten, a House member from Illinois, introduced a whistleblower bill on Thursday, and with Ted Lieu of California announced it Friday as the Policing Insider Trading in Prediction Markets Act of twenty twenty-six, RG dot org reported, with referral to the Judiciary Committee and the Committee on Education and Workforce.

The attorney general would pay people who voluntarily supply original information that leads to a successful covered case, at least ten percent and at most thirty percent of the monetary sanctions, out of the Justice Department's Assets Forfeiture Fund.

A covered case is a criminal investigation, action, or settlement the attorney general brings over a federal fraud offense involving a prediction-market platform, and it has to produce sanctions above one million dollars, counting penalties, disgorgement, interest, and forfeited money.

The bill treats a platform as any website, app, exchange, or protocol in interstate or foreign commerce that lets people trade contracts tied to an event or a contest, and it withholds awards from anyone convicted or found civilly liable in that case. A weather derivative pays on a measured index, which is how these contracts work, rather than on proof that one field failed. Cumulative average temperature adds up the season's average. Growing degree-days add up how far temperature sits above a base. Product-design basis risk is the miss between that index and actual yield.

Geographical basis risk is the miss between the station in the contract and the farm.

A twenty nineteen paper on arXiv, on hedging crop yields against weather uncertainties, uses a machine-learning ensemble to see which weather variables move maize yield, and reports that average temperature mattered most.

The temperature model it proposes is mean-reverting, with a speed of reversion that can change over time, a seasonal level to revert toward, and local volatility that depends on the local average temperature.

The authors stretch that into several locations at once, correlated with each other, and then write prices for futures, options on futures, and basket futures, on both cumulative average temperature and growing degree-days.

Follow their build without inventing a temperature or a premium, because the abstract gives neither. The ensemble picks average temperature as the driver. The path is pulled toward a seasonal mean, faster or slower as the speed changes, with volatility tied to the local average. A futures price is then the price of the cumulative average of that path, or of the degree-days accumulated from it.

A basket lets the buyer weight the stations that sit nearer the crop, and the paper says that choice reduces geographical basis risk. An option is only a right to that index exposure. The ensemble step is their attempt to eliminate product-design basis risk.

A twenty eleven paper on arXiv, on pricing weather derivatives for extreme events, models extremes as a max-stable process, drawing on spatial statistics and extreme-value theory, then simulates payments across a general set of contracts. Those simulations keep the spatial dependence, so a bad outcome in one place is not treated as independent of a bad outcome next door.

Catastrophe-ratemaking methods then supply risk loads and premiums for contracts that are renewal-additive, so the load can be added across renewals.

The twenty nineteen paper says most smallholder farmers and agricultural stakeholders will not pay weather-derivative prices because both kinds of basis risk sit in the models. The ensemble and the basket are the authors' reply to that refusal, and the text does not say the refusal then disappears.

The twenty eleven premiums still depend on the max-stable description of the tail and on those spatially linked simulations, so a bad description moves the risk load with it. The abstract names no outside critic of the fit.

So a temperature price is a price of an index. Cumulative average temperature and growing degree-days accumulate heat and cold differently. A basket is a weighted mix of places, a cut in geographical basis risk in that twenty nineteen account, not a promise the field matches.

An extremes contract transfers the tail, and in the twenty eleven account the premium includes a catastrophe risk load, not only an expected payout. Knowing that tells you what risk the price is for, and what gap remains if the crop, the place, or the tail does not match. It is not a judgment that any contract is cheap or rich, and it is not a hedge to copy.
