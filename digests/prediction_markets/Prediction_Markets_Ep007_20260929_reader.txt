How it works, when the question is who decides the answer, starts with a lock, not a vote.

In a crypto-settled contract, collateral stays put until an oracle brings in a result and winning claims can be redeemed.

An ACM Computing Surveys paper calls that the oracle problem: the contract can see on-chain data, but the real event sits off-chain, so something has to carry the outside result onto the chain.

That survey splits the designs in two.

Voting-based systems finalize an outcome mainly from what participants have staked.

Reputation-based systems mix reputation and performance metrics with authenticity proofs meant to support correctness and integrity.

Both are ways to hand a smart contract a final result, and the paper points to remaining challenges and later research rather than a finished method.

A separate paper on arXiv looks at the wait itself.

If collateral is locked until oracle settlement, a near-certain dollar is a delayed dollar.

The price then holds a maturity-dependent settlement discount on top of beliefs about the outcome.

The authors build an implied discount curve from persistent near-certain contracts, using the settlement times that actually occurred, and they summarize it as an annualized settlement wedge.

Those wedges are positive, they change with maturity, and they move through time.

Repricing along those curves cuts the near-certainty horizon gradient by roughly forty-eight to eighty-eight percent.

The paper reads much of that raw maturity pattern as priced settlement friction, not forecast error alone.

negRisk conversion squeezes the discounts by recycling part of a position into synthetic collateral.

Yield-bearing collateral flattens the curve because the locked money is less costly to hold.

Pricing quality, on this account, depends on settlement mechanics, how productive the collateral is, and whether capital can be recycled.

A price near certainty is a belief, and it is also a claim on cash that cannot move until an oracle report, a stake-weighted vote, or a reputation-weighted check closes the question.
