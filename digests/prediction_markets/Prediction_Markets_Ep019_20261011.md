> **Google banned prediction-market ads in Connecticut, the fifth state where platforms lose that reach.**

**What You Need to Know:** Google banned prediction-market advertising in Connecticut on October 7, effective the same day, cutting a fifth U.S. state out of the ad program it opened in January. Connecticut's consumer-protection regulator had spent September ordering nine companies to stop offering sports contracts there.

### Top Story
Google prohibited ads for prediction-market contracts and related products in Connecticut on October 7, 2026, PPC Land reports, citing a change-log entry in Google's Advertising Policies Help Center. The ban took effect the day it was posted, with no window between notice and enforcement. The cut makes Connecticut the fifth U.S. state removed from a program Google opened in January to federally regulated prediction markets, alongside Nevada, Ohio, Michigan and New York. Connecticut's consumer-protection regulator spent September ordering nine of these companies to stop offering sports contracts in the state, and Google's ad rule there now tracks that position rather than a federal licence alone. ### How It Works: Futarchy and Decision Markets
Futarchy picks a policy by running markets that are conditional on the choice. A group first agrees on a measurable result it cares about. Traders then price that result under each option. The option whose conditional price looks better is the one the rule selects.

A study in Frontiers in Blockchain asks whether that arrangement can govern decentralized science. The authors examined governance data from 13 DeSci DAOs and replayed proposals from VitaDAO. Under a deterministic model, every historical decision pointed the same way as the futarchy-preferred outcome. They call that latent compatibility, and they say a real version still needs measurable indicators and a diverse set of traders.

Decision markets are the narrower tool inside that idea. They pull private information out of people who have their own interests and fold it into a forecast that depends on the action. A 2023 paper on arXiv says the standard design pays for honesty with a random decision rule. Sometimes the principal must do the thing the forecast has already marked as worse.

That paper sketches three mechanisms that keep a fixed decision instead. If the principal already holds a signal, she can score everyone else against it, then choose using the combined forecasts plus her own signal. If she does not, she can offer one agent a share of the profit and take that agent's signal as the stand-in, or she can score two agents against each other with a proper peer-prediction rule and use those signals the same way. A test of the first design, in a multi-agent bandit system, trained agents to a level similar to a Bayesian model that could see every signal at once.

For a reader, a conditional price is not a plain forecast. It prices an outcome only if a choice is made, and the papers tie its usefulness to the score, the stand-in, and the indicator the group picked.
