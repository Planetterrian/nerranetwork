> **A robot hand just learned to copy human moves using sound waves instead of cameras.**
Researchers at MIT found a way to make robot hands way more skilled by using ultrasound scans that see under the skin. This matters because better robot hands could one day help with delicate tasks like surgery or helping people with disabilities. Today we’ll also look at OpenAI’s big developer event and new rules for stopping harmful deepfake images.

### The Big Story
Scientists at MIT have figured out a new way to help robot hands move more like real human hands. Instead of relying only on cameras that watch from the outside, they used ultrasound imaging—the same kind of sound-wave scan doctors use to look at babies before they’re born—to see what’s happening inside the hand while it moves.

Think of it like this: your hand has 34 muscles, 27 joints, and over 100 tendons working together every time you pick up a pencil or wave hello. Cameras can only see the outside, so robots often get confused about exactly how those parts are working. Ultrasound lets the system “listen” to the moving tendons and muscles in real time, giving the robot much richer information.

This is a big deal because robot hands have always been clumsy at fine tasks. With better sensing, future robots could help in hospitals, factories, or even at home with things like cooking or fixing small objects. For students interested in careers, this shows how AI and robotics are combining medical tools with machine learning in creative ways.

If you’re someone who likes building things or playing with robotics kits, this kind of breakthrough could lead to more affordable and capable robot helpers in the next few years. It also raises interesting questions about how much “feeling” we want machines to have.

Right now there isn’t a public demo you can try, but you can watch the research video on the MIT Technology Review site to see the hand in action. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re texting a friend, your phone sometimes guesses the next word by looking at what you usually type? Now imagine instead of guessing words, the system is trying to guess exactly how your hand muscles and tendons are moving at every single moment.

Step one: doctors already use ultrasound to see inside the body with sound waves that bounce back like echoes in a cave. Step two: researchers attached an ultrasound probe to a person’s wrist and recorded what the inside of the hand looks like while it performs different gestures. Step three: they fed all those moving images into an AI so it could learn the hidden patterns of muscles and tendons. Step four: the AI then tells a robot hand exactly which motors to turn on to copy the same motion.

And that’s basically what the ultrasound system is doing when it helps the robot hand become more skillful. So next time someone says “sensing under the skin,” you can tell them—it’s basically using sound echoes the same way your phone predicts text, except it’s predicting movement instead of words. Not so scary, right?

**Meta gets advice on stopping creepy deepfake pictures**
Meta’s Oversight Board told the company it needs better ways for regular people to report sexualized AI-generated images of themselves. Deepfakes are fake photos or videos made by AI that can look completely real, and they’re being used to harass people online. The suggestions include making reporting easier and removing the images faster.

This matters because anyone with a phone can now be targeted, and knowing the rules are improving helps keep social media safer. You can read the full recommendations on Engadget’s site to understand what changes might be coming to Instagram and Facebook.
