The biggest news is that Gemini 3.5 Flash can now operate your computer on its own. This opens the door to AI that handles real tasks instead of just chatting. ### The Big Story
Google just added a feature called "Computer Use" straight into its Gemini 3.5 Flash model. This lets the AI look at your screen and actually move the mouse, click buttons, and type for you. Think of it like giving your phone's assistant a pair of virtual hands so it can finish tasks instead of just telling you how. The model can now open browsers, fill out forms, or test software without a human guiding every step. On a test called OSWorld, it scored 78.4, matching the level of other top models at handling these real-world actions. For students, this means future homework helpers could one day organize your files or run practice quizzes on actual websites. For anyone curious about future jobs, it shows how AI is moving from answering questions to doing work alongside people. The change matters because it turns AI from a conversation partner into something that can complete longer projects on its own. Developers can already start building simple agents with the Gemini API for things like testing apps or automating repetitive clicks. If you're not a coder, the best way to see the direction is to try Gemini on your phone or computer and notice how it already understands images and context better than older versions. Over time, features like this will make everyday tools feel more like having a patient helper who can just do the thing. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/google-bakes-computer-control-directly-into-gemini-3-5-flash-letting-the-model-see-and-operate-your-screen/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you're texting a friend, your phone guesses the next word based on what you've typed before? Now imagine that same idea, but instead of guessing words, the system guesses what action comes next on a screen. First, the AI takes a picture of everything open on your computer. Then it breaks that picture into small pieces it can understand, like spotting a button or a text box the same way you spot a door or a light switch. Next, it predicts the most useful next move, such as "click the search bar" or "type this address," just like autocomplete predicts a whole sentence. Finally, it sends that action back to the computer so the cursor actually moves and clicks. And that's basically what computer-use AI is doing when it operates your screen. So next time someone says an AI can "control your device," you can tell them it's really just super-powered screen autocomplete that learned from millions of examples. Not so scary once you see the pattern.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**ChatGPT just got a friendlier upgrade**
OpenAI released a new version of GPT-5.5 Instant that feels more natural in conversation. It understands what you're really asking even when your question is a bit messy, and it gives better answers for shopping ideas or local suggestions. The model also handles tricky instructions more reliably, like "plan a weekend trip under $200 that includes hiking." Paid users can try it right now by opening ChatGPT and starting a new chat. Free users will get it tomorrow. A fun thing to test: ask it to recommend three movies based on a show you like, then add the constraint "nothing with subtitles" and see how it adjusts. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2069843083701915755)

**Figma just added AI that makes moving graphics and shiny effects**
At its yearly design conference, Figma showed new tools that let you create motion graphics and special shader effects with AI help. Instead of drawing every frame by hand, you describe what you want and the AI fills in the movement or lighting. This is perfect for anyone who makes TikTok videos, school presentations, or game art. You can try it by going to figma.com, creating a free account, and opening a new design file. Then search for the new AI motion tools and type something like "make this circle bounce like a basketball" to see what happens. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/tech/955831/figma-code-design-tools-config-2026-announcements)

### Quick Bits
**Want to stop Google from using your searches to train AI?**
Google now keeps images and searches you upload so its models can learn from them. You can turn this off in your Google account settings under Search history and data controls. It only takes a minute and keeps your activity more private. Source: [wired.com](https://www.wired.com/story/how-to-opt-out-of-google-search-new-ai-data-training/)

**AI chatbots lean left in their answers**
A new report found that tools like ChatGPT give answers that match left-leaning views about 80 percent of the time on political topics. Researchers tested several models and noticed the same pattern across different questions. It's a reminder that AI learns from the huge amount of internet text it was trained on.
