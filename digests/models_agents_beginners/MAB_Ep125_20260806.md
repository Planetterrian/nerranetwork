### The Big Story
A solo developer just built a history podcast that feels more like a conversation than a lecture. You pick any topic, the AI writes and narrates a full two-host episode with sound and artwork, and then you can jump in at any moment. The system uses large language models combined with text-to-speech to generate the full episode in just a couple of minutes, complete with narration and custom artwork. 

Think of it like watching a movie with friends where you can pause and ask “Wait, did that really happen?” and the characters actually answer you before the story continues. The hosts stop, respond using real sources, and then pick up right where they left off. The live demo focuses on the real history behind the Odyssey, and the entire experience runs without requiring any account creation. 

This matters because history class often feels like memorizing dates, but this turns it into something active and curious. If you’re a student prepping for a test or just someone who wonders about the Trojan War while listening, you get answers without leaving the experience. The creator built in strict grounding rules so every claim stays tied to verifiable sources instead of letting the model invent details. 

It also shows how AI can make learning feel personal instead of one-way. The creator made sure every claim links back to real sources and added a quiz at the end so you can check what you learned. The interrupt feature works by letting you tap a microphone button during playback, speak your question out loud, and receive an immediate answer before the episode resumes. 

You can try it right now at historai.ca — no sign-up needed. Pick the Odyssey demo, start the episode, and when something surprises you, tap the mic button and ask your question out loud. The hosts will pause and reply. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/artificial/comments/1vgurp1/i_built_an_history_podcast_you_can_interrupt/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when your phone’s voice assistant hears “turn on Bluetooth,” it opens the settings app and flips the switch for you? 

Now picture the same idea, but instead of one simple command, the AI looks at the whole situation: it sees that Bluetooth is off, knows your mouse stopped working, figures out the exact menu to open, and clicks the right button without you telling it every single step. The developer in this case had accidentally disabled Bluetooth on a Windows machine, which immediately cut off the wireless mouse with no easy keyboard workaround available. 

It does this by treating your computer like a set of tools it can reach — menus, buttons, and settings become things it can “touch” the same way you would with your finger. The model plans a short sequence: open settings, find Bluetooth, turn it on. Then it carries out that plan one action at a time, checking what changed after each click. In the actual test, the AI used computer controls to open the settings window and completed the task with a single click. 

The surprising part is that it can recover if something goes wrong, like if the first menu it tried didn’t work. It just tries the next logical step. That’s why the same kind of system can also handle bigger tasks like booking a ride or organizing files. 

Once you see it that way, AI agents stop feeling like magic and start feeling like a very fast, very patient digital helper that knows how your apps are laid out. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/emollick/status/2085212700561285460)

### Quick Bits
**Google is replacing its old Assistant with Gemini this September**
Starting September 4, 2026, Google Assistant will stop working on Android phones, tablets, watches, and cars. Gemini, Google’s newer AI, will handle voice commands instead. The change affects every device running Android or Wear OS, including cars with Android Auto. 

It’s a big switch because Gemini can understand more natural questions, but it also means simple commands might feel different at first. If you use “Hey Google” every day, you’ll start seeing the change soon. The move tests whether a large language model can match the reliability of the older, rule-based assistant for everyday tasks. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/google-will-shut-down-google-assistant-starting-september-2026-as-gemini-takes-over-on-android-and-wear-os/)

**LLMs sometimes give better money advice than most humans**
A study from MIT and Stanford found that many people would make smarter money choices if they followed suggestions from models like GPT-5.2 or Gemini 3 Flash. The research showed that following the models’ advice would leave most participants financially better off than their current habits. The quality still depends on how clearly you ask your questions. 

It’s a reminder that the way you phrase things to an AI really matters, even for everyday decisions like saving or budgeting. People who asked more specific questions received noticeably stronger guidance in the study.
