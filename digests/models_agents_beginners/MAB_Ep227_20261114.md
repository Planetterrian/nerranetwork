# Models & Agents for Beginners
> **A complete beginner just built a working racing game on his MacBook using only ChatGPT.**
One person with zero coding experience turned after-work chats with ChatGPT into a full racing game complete with coastlines, engine sounds, and police chases. Today we explore how that happened, why AI safety checks matter more than ever, and a brand-new open-source tool that turns video AI into robot brains. You’ll leave with two things you can try right now on your phone or laptop.
---
### The Big Story
A guy who had never written a single line of code decided in August to build a racing game he could play on his MacBook. He asked ChatGPT for help, and the AI suggested the free Godot game engine because it supports both desktop and mobile platforms. Over the next two months the project grew from weekend experiments into a daily hobby that now eats six to eight hours after work. The coastline alone took two full days of back-and-forth: run the game, describe what looked wrong, get a new version, repeat. The water uses shaders, the foam comes from generated textures, and the splashes were animated in Blender then brought into Godot as animated textures. Engine sounds started as a real-car recording but didn’t feel right in the game, so ChatGPT helped build a custom synth instead. Ambient sounds came from ElevenLabs or Adobe Firefly. The car itself is a free stock model with a free license. Buildings and props were made from text prompts or generated reference images, and a couple of the environment assets were generated as complete 3D models using Tripo AI. Codex then helped optimize both the meshes and textures for use in Godot. The project started with GPT-5.6 Sol on a $20 subscription and moved to GPT-6 Astra on a $500 subscription in November. The game is being tested on an M1 Pro MacBook Pro and an iPhone 15 Pro, with parallel builds because the iPhone version needs tighter optimization. Right now the focus is on how the car handles, how it interacts with the world, and how it all looks and sounds in motion. Sprints, circuit races, and rivals are planned, with a story to follow once those modes work. First up is a police chase. This story matters because it shows that the barrier to making something real has dropped dramatically; you no longer need years of classes or a team to turn an idea into a playable game. If you’ve ever thought “I wish I could make my own game,” this is proof the door is open. Go to chatgpt.com, start a new chat, and type: “I’ve never coded before. Help me make a simple 2D racing game in the free Godot engine. Give me the first three steps.” Save your progress in a notebook so you can keep going tomorrow. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how your phone sometimes asks you to do a quick fingerprint or face scan before it lets an app do something important? That little check is basically a safety test making sure the right person is in charge. Now imagine a much bigger version of that check, but instead of protecting your phone it’s protecting the whole world from an AI that might give bad advice or cause harm. Researchers at Anthropic just shared ten practical rules for building these checks on their Engineering Blog. The rules say you need experts from different fields, including subject-matter and technical experts, to design the tests, you have to write down exactly what you’re testing before you start, and you need to make sure the test can’t be tricked by clever wording. They also recommend running the same test multiple ways because the results can change depending on whether the questions are multiple-choice or open-ended. The post draws on psychology’s replication reforms from a decade ago, such as preregistration of hypotheses and analysis plans, to make measurements more trustworthy. The goal is simple: before a new AI model is released, you want clear proof it won’t help someone hurt themselves or others. When the tests are done right, everyone can see the evidence instead of just trusting the company’s word. That’s the part that makes these safety checks actually trustworthy. Source: [anthropic.com](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-strong-safety-evaluations)

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Robots that learned to move by watching videos**
Runway just released an open-source AI model called Praxis-1 that was trained on millions of videos and can now control robots. The model uses knowledge from video training for robot control and leads the World Action Model category on Nvidia’s RoboLab-120 benchmark. It comes with open weights and source code under the Apache 2.0 license, but it can’t yet plan complex action sequences on its own. Instead of writing thousands of lines of robot code, the model uses what it learned from watching things move to tell a robot arm or body what to do next. It’s completely free to download and experiment with if you have a decent computer. This matters because it shows one path from “AI that makes cool videos” to “AI that can help robots do real tasks.” Head to the Decoder article linked below, click the link to the Hugging Face page, and download the model weights. Even if you don’t run it yourself, scroll through the examples to see a robot arm stacking blocks after only watching video. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/runways-open-source-praxis-1-turns-video-ai-into-a-robot-controller/)

**ChatGPT can now shop for you (with your permission)**
OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on November 13, 2026, a feature that lets special AI agents inside ChatGPT actually complete purchases after you approve them with Face ID or a fingerprint. The feature is available for ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States who hold eligible US-issued American Express, Chase, Mastercard or Visa cards. Your card details never go to the AI or the store; after approval, the payment network generates a token with its own card number, security code and expiration date, and charges that token to the card. OpenAI built the feature with American Express, Mastercard and Visa. At checkout, ChatGPT Wallet shows the merchant, the items, the total and the selected card. Users keep existing card rewards and dispute protections for eligible claims. Right now it’s only for ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US, but it shows where agent-style AI is heading. OpenAI plans to let users delegate purchases to an agent in a future version that would use spending limits, purchase categories and time periods. If you have access, open ChatGPT, look for the Dots agent, and try asking it to find a specific item like “a blue hoodie under $30” to see how the approval flow works. Source: [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet)
