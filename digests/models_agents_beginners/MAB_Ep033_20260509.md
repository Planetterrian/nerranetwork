### The Big Story
Google just announced a new health coach inside its Google Health app that uses Gemini to pull together data from your wearables, fitness apps, and medical records. 

Instead of giving generic tips, it creates proactive wellness guidance that matches your actual schedule and habits. Think of it like having a helpful friend who knows your calendar, your step count, and your doctor’s notes, then suggests small changes that fit without feeling like homework.

The coach can spot patterns across all those sources and remind you about things like hydration or movement before you even notice you’re off track. This matters because most people already track some health data on their phones but rarely connect it all into useful advice.

For students or anyone juggling school and activities, it could mean gentler suggestions that respect busy days instead of pushing impossible goals. You might see it recommend a short walk between classes based on your real sleep data rather than a generic “exercise more.”

Right now the feature is rolling out in the Google Health app on Android and iOS, so open the app, connect any wearables or fitness trackers you already use, and start a conversation with the coach about your typical day. It’s free to try if you have a Google account. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/GoogleAI/status/2052789253038592466)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re texting a friend, your phone’s keyboard guesses the next word before you even finish typing? 

Now imagine that same autocomplete could look ahead and guess the next three or four words all at once instead of one at a time. That’s basically what multi-token prediction does inside models like Gemma 4. 

By planning several steps ahead, the AI finishes its answers faster without losing quality, which Google says can speed up workflows by up to 3X. The model still checks its work, but it spends less time going back and forth on every single word. 

So next time someone says “multi-token prediction,” you can tell them it’s just smarter autocomplete that thinks a few words ahead. Not so scary, right?

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Turn any weird idea into a labeled textbook diagram:** Reddit (r/ChatGPT)
ChatGPT’s image generator can now create clean, educational cross-section drawings even when you don’t attach a real photo. The trick is a clever prompt that asks it to “restore” an imaginary image into a scientific illustration with labels and layers. 

It’s perfect for school projects or just seeing how everyday objects look on the inside. Go to chatgpt.com, start a new chat, pick “Create image,” and paste this prompt: “Restore the attached photo into a clean educational cross section on a white background… Preserve the most distinctive visible features… Add neat labels with pointer lines.” 

Try it with something random like “a bicycle” or “a backpack” and watch it build a neat diagram you can screenshot for notes. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1t7py24/the_nonexistent_photo_restoration_prompt_but_it/)

**See how realistic AI images have gotten:** Reddit (r/ChatGPT)
People are sharing AI-generated photos that look almost indistinguishable from real ones, and the comments are full of guesses about what’s fake. It’s a fun way to train your eye for what current image models can and can’t do yet. 

Head to reddit.com/r/ChatGPT and search for “levels of realism in the image generation” to scroll the latest examples. Pick one and try describing it in your own words to ChatGPT or another free image tool to see how close you can get. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1t7qbf3/the_levels_of_realism_in_the_image_generation_are/)
