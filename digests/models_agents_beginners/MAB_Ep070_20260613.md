> **Your phone's photos are about to get a magic "expand" button that adds new scenery on any side.**
Apple is adding a simple tool to stretch photos outward by up to 25 percent on any edge. This is part of iOS 27 and feels like giving your pictures extra room to breathe. We'll also look at how AI actually fills in those new areas, plus a free tool that keeps your private info out of chatbots.
---
### The Big Story
Imagine you're editing a photo on your phone and you wish the sky went a little higher or the table stretched a bit wider. That's exactly what the new "Extend" feature in iOS 27 lets you do. You pick any edge of a picture and the phone can add up to 25 percent more space, inventing what should be there based on the rest of the image.

Think of it like when your phone suggests the next word while you're texting, except this time it's guessing what the next part of a picture should look like. The AI looks at the colors, shapes, and patterns already in the photo and continues them outward in a way that usually feels natural. It works on any side—top, bottom, left, or right—so you can fix a cropped horizon or make a group photo feel less crowded.

This matters because almost every teen takes and shares photos every day. Instead of deleting a shot because something important got cut off, you can now rescue it in seconds. It also opens up creative experiments, like turning a regular selfie into a wider scene or making a small drawing fill an entire screen. For anyone who posts on social media or works on school projects with images, this removes a tiny but annoying limit.

The feature arrives with iOS 27, so if your phone supports the update you'll be able to try it the moment the software lands. Open the Photos app, pick an image, and look for the new Extend option along the edges. You can drag the handles and watch the AI fill in the blank space right away—no extra apps or accounts needed. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you're texting a friend, your phone guesses the next word before you finish typing? Now imagine that same autocomplete works on pictures instead of words, and it has to invent brand-new parts of the scene that never existed.

First the system looks at the pixels right next to the edge you want to expand. It notices patterns like "this area is blue sky with white clouds" or "this is wooden table with grain lines." Then it predicts what those patterns would look like if they kept going, almost like continuing a pattern on graph paper. Because the AI has seen millions of photos during training, it has a good sense of what usually comes next in similar situations.

Next it checks the whole image for context so the new part doesn't clash—like making sure the lighting matches and the new grass lines up with the old grass. Finally it blends the new pixels smoothly into the old ones so you can't see the seam. And that's basically what outpainting (the technical name for extending images) is doing when it adds space to your photos. So next time someone says "AI image extension," you can tell them—it's basically autocomplete for pictures. Not so scary, right?
