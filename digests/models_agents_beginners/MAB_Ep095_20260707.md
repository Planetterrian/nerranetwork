A handful of new AI schools are letting kids learn entirely with AI instead of classrooms. This matters because it shows how AI is moving from homework helper to full-time tutor. ### The Big Story
A small number of wealthy families are choosing AI-powered schools over regular ones for their kids. Companies like Forge Prep and Alpha are building programs where students learn through AI systems instead of traditional teachers and classrooms. Think of it like having a personal tutor who never gets tired, can explain the same math problem ten different ways, and adjusts the lesson the moment you look confused. These schools use AI to create custom learning paths for each student, tracking what they understand and what still feels tricky. The big shift is that education moves from one-size-fits-all to something that changes with every learner. For students, this could mean school feels less frustrating and more like getting help exactly when you need it. It also raises questions about who gets access to these tools first. If AI tutors become really good, they might eventually help more kids, not just those whose families can pay. Right now the programs are expensive and limited, but the idea shows how AI could reshape homework, test prep, and even how you explore new subjects you’re curious about. You can’t sign up for these specific schools yet, but you can already use free AI chatbots to get the same kind of patient, step-by-step help on schoolwork. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/961505/wealthy-ai-schools-alpha-forge-prep)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re playing a single-player video game, the computer-controlled characters don’t wait for you to tell them what to do — they just walk around, attack, or explore on their own? Now picture that same idea but with an AI that can open apps, click buttons, and follow a whole plan without anyone sitting at the keyboard. First it gets a goal, like “find this file and make a copy.” Then it breaks the goal into small steps it can actually do on a computer. Next it checks what happened after each step and decides what to try next, almost like following a checklist but rewriting the checklist as it goes. The surprising part is that the AI isn’t just guessing randomly — it’s using patterns it learned from millions of examples of people doing similar tasks. So when the AI reaches the end of the plan, it has actually completed real work instead of just chatting about it. That’s the core idea behind what people call an AI agent: software that can carry out a full job instead of needing a human to click every single button. ### Cool Stuff & Try This
**ChatGPT tricks that feel like secret superpowers**
Most people only use ChatGPT for quick questions, but it can do a bunch of other useful things once you know the right prompts. It can turn your messy notes into clean study guides, rewrite an email so it sounds more polite, or even help you plan a weekend project step by step. These features are already built in — you just have to ask in a slightly different way. Go to chat.openai.com on your phone or laptop, start a new chat, and try typing: “Turn these three bullet points into a short, friendly paragraph I can send to a teacher.” Or ask it to “explain photosynthesis like I’m ten years old and then like I’m fifteen.” Both experiments take less than a minute and show how the same tool can switch between homework help and creative brainstorming. ### Quick Bits
**An AI lab is circling Earth right now**
A British startup just launched a small lab into orbit that will send protein data back to Earth so AI models can study how aging diseases like Alzheimer’s work. The zero-gravity environment changes how proteins behave, giving scientists new information they couldn’t easily get on the ground. Source: [wired.com](https://www.wired.com/story/british-space-startup-launches-longevity-lab-into-orbit/)

**Amazon is closing its famous “human task” website**
Mechanical Turk, the site where companies paid real people to do small jobs like label photos or answer surveys, is shutting down for new customers at the end of July. Many of those same tasks are now handled by AI instead. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/amazon-sunsets-mechanical-turk-the-original-artificial-artificial-intelligence/)
