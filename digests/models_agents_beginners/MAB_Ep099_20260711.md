# Models & Agents for Beginners
> **Imagine walking into any real street you’ve seen on Google Maps and instantly turning it into your own explorable 3D world.**
Google just showed off a new prototype called Project Genie that builds interactive 360-degree worlds straight from Street View photos. It turns a simple text prompt into a living environment you can move through frame by frame. We’ll also look at an eco-friendly chatbot that runs on way less energy and check a few other quick AI moments worth knowing.

### The Big Story
Google shared a research prototype called Project Genie that creates interactive 360-degree virtual worlds using real locations from Google Maps Street View. You type a prompt or pick a starting place, and the system builds a full environment you can explore in any direction.

Think of it like having a super-smart friend who can instantly turn a single photo into an entire playable scene, complete with what’s behind you and beside you. Instead of a flat picture, it fills in the whole space so you can turn around and keep moving.

The system solves the “blank space” problem by anchoring everything to real Street View data at the start, then keeps the world alive by generating each new frame based on what you do next. That means your actions—like swimming across water or climbing a hill—actually change what appears on screen in real time.

This matters because most virtual worlds today are either pre-built by big teams or feel empty when you try to explore freely. Genie shows a path where anyone could start from a real place they know and turn it into something new without needing game design skills.

For a student or creator, it opens the door to quick experiments in storytelling, history projects, or just fun “what if” worlds. You could stand on your own street and imagine it underwater, or turn a famous landmark into a puzzle game level.

Right now the tool is still a research prototype, so there isn’t a public demo link yet. Keep an eye on the Google DeepMind and Google Labs accounts on X for updates—the team has been sharing early examples there. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/GoogleAI/status/2075609303029776872)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how your phone’s keyboard guesses the next word while you’re texting, making the whole sentence feel natural even though it’s just predicting one piece at a time?

Now picture the same idea but with pictures instead of words. The AI looks at the current view and guesses what the next frame should show based on where you’re trying to move. It does this over and over, building the feeling of walking or swimming through a space.

Because it works frame by frame instead of trying to create one giant finished world all at once, the environment can react to your choices—like what happens when you turn left or jump. The starting point comes from real Street View photos so the first view already feels grounded and believable.

That guessing process is what lets the world stay consistent even when you explore behind the camera or move in unexpected ways. The model has learned patterns from tons of video so it can keep the lighting, textures, and movement looking right.

The result is a world that feels alive because every new second is freshly imagined rather than pre-recorded.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Eco-friendly chatbot that plants trees while it chats**
Ecogpt is a chatbot built to use about 10 percent of the computing power most other AI models need. It answers questions the same way ChatGPT does, but with a much smaller environmental footprint. The team behind it has already donated enough to plant over 34,000 trees through two charities.  

Anyone curious about everyday AI use can try it right now—no coding required. Go to the Ecogpt site (search “Ecogpt chatbot”), start a conversation, and ask it something simple like “Tell me a short story about a robot that recycles.” Watch how it responds while knowing it’s running on far less energy than usual models. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/artificial/comments/1utdlkb/ecogpt_is_a_chatbot_that_aims_to_be_more/)

**Testing an AI on a daily video-game challenge**
One researcher is using a model called Codex to play the daily challenge in the game Slay the Spire. The AI has already beaten the first boss on a tough randomized run, which shows how these tools are getting better at handling strategy games with changing rules.  

If you play Slay the Spire or similar games, this is a fun reminder that AI can sometimes act like a practice partner. You can try the same kind of experiment yourself by opening ChatGPT or Claude and describing a simple game scenario to see how it reasons through choices. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/emollick/status/2075823098868142502)

### Quick Bits
**What do those big benchmark numbers actually mean?**
When a new model claims “80 percent on this test,” it usually means the AI got 80 percent of the questions right on a specific set of problems. Even a perfect score wouldn’t mean the model is finished—new, harder tests keep getting created. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/artificial/comments/1ut87cn/what_is_the_meaning_of_ai_benchmarks/)

**Hugging Face CEO says open-source AI keeps growing fast**
Roughly half the Fortune 500 companies now use open models and datasets shared on the Hugging Face platform. That means more free tools and examples are available for students and hobbyists to explore without paying for access.
