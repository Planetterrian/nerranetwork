> **AI can now watch you do a task once on your Mac and then repeat it forever on its own.**
OpenAI just added a "Record Replay" feature to its Codex app that learns your workflows by watching you work. This matters because it moves AI from answering questions to actually doing repeatable tasks for you. We'll also break down how this kind of learning works under the hood, share prompt tips you can try today, and look at a fun new AI vanity search tool.

### The Big Story
Imagine finishing a boring computer task once while an AI watches, then having it handle that exact task for you every time after. That's what OpenAI's new Record Replay feature in the Codex app does. You perform a workflow on your Mac, the system records your clicks and actions, turns them into a reusable "skill," and then runs that skill automatically whenever you want.

Think of it like teaching a helpful robot assistant by showing it your morning routine just one time. After that single demonstration, the robot can repeat the whole sequence without needing step-by-step instructions again. The feature works by capturing what you do on screen and converting those actions into something the AI can replay on its own later.

This changes how people might use AI in everyday life. Instead of typing prompts over and over for the same report formatting or file organizing job, you show it once and let the AI take over. Students could demonstrate organizing notes or pulling data for a project, then reuse that process weekly. Creatives might show how they resize and tag batches of images, freeing time for the fun parts of making art.

For you specifically, this could mean less repetitive clicking if you're using AI for schoolwork, side projects, or content creation. It turns the AI from a question-answer machine into something that handles routines you set up yourself. The feature is rolling out first on macOS, though it's not yet available in the EU, UK, or Switzerland.

You can try the idea right now by going to the OpenAI website, signing up for ChatGPT (free tier works), and practicing clear step-by-step instructions for a task you do often. Later, when Record Replay reaches more users, you'll already know how to give good demonstrations. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you're showing a friend how to make your favorite sandwich, you just do it once in front of them and they remember the steps? Now imagine the friend is an AI that turns your exact movements into a saved recipe it can follow perfectly every single time without asking questions.

First, the AI watches every click, drag, and keystroke you make while doing the task on your computer. Next, it breaks those actions into small, repeatable chunks it can store as a "skill." Then it saves that skill so you can trigger it later with one simple command instead of doing everything again. Finally, when you ask it to run the skill, the AI replays the exact sequence on its own, just like following the sandwich recipe without you standing there.

And that's basically what Record Replay is doing when it turns your one-time demonstration into an automatic workflow. So next time someone says "AI agents learning from demonstration," you can tell them — it's basically recording your sandwich-making once so the robot can make it forever. Not so scary, right?
