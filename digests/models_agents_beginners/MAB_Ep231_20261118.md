# Models & Agents for Beginners
> **AI just learned to turn your song into a short music video that follows the beat and lyrics.**
Suno added a feature that pairs its AI songs with matching AI video clips. Google now lets you peek inside and edit what Gemini remembers about you. An AI-generated 3D space elevator scene shows how far creative tools have come. Today we break down what these mean and exactly how you can try them.
---
### The Big Story
Suno, the AI music generator, just launched a new feature called Visuals that creates short video clips to go with the songs it makes. Instead of just hearing the music, you now get images or video that match the lyrics, rhythm, and mood. Think of it like having an automatic music-video editor that watches the song structure and adds lip-sync or scene changes at the right moments. The clips can start from a photo you upload, a text prompt, or preset characters, and they run up to 15 seconds long. Visuals runs on H3 Move, a customized and safety-filtered version of MiniMax’s video model H3. Lyric videos let you tweak fonts and colors, extend clips on a timeline, and pick the length and aspect ratio. Two quality tiers are available: Fast and Standard. Filters block prompts and uploads that reference specific artists, celebrities, or copyrighted works, and the platform adds an embedded watermark before any content is shared outside Suno. This matters because making even a simple music video used to require expensive software, filming, and editing skills. Now a student writing a song for a school project or a teen posting on social media can get visuals in the same app where they create the track. It lowers the barrier between an idea and a finished piece you can share. Visuals works on the web, iOS, and Android and requires its own credits, though Pro and Premier subscribers receive a temporary allotment to try it out. You can try it right on the web at suno.com, or open the Suno app on iOS or Android. Start by generating a song, then tap the new Visuals button and choose “Fast” mode for a quick clip or “Standard” for higher quality. The site gives you a few free credits to test it before asking for a paid plan. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/sunos-new-visuals-feature-pairs-ai-generated-music-with-ai-generated-video/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re texting, your phone sometimes guesses the next word before you finish typing? Now picture that same idea stretched across time and pictures. An AI video model looks at the song’s lyrics and beat the way your phone looks at the words you’ve already typed. It predicts what scene or movement should come next, just like your phone predicts the next word. Then it checks the timing so the picture changes when the music changes, the same way your phone keeps the sentence flowing smoothly. The model also watches for things like a singer’s mouth shape so the video looks like it’s actually singing along. All of this happens in a few seconds because the AI has seen millions of music videos during training and learned the patterns. The system follows the song’s structure, picks up on lyrics, and supports lip sync while running clips up to 15 seconds. So when you hit “create visuals,” the system is really just doing a super-fast version of the same word-guessing trick, except it’s guessing what the next frame of video should show.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**See exactly what Google Gemini remembers about you**
Google Gemini keeps a running summary of things you’ve told it so it can give better answers later. You can now open that summary, read it, and delete or edit anything you don’t want it to keep. This gives you real control over your data instead of wondering what the AI has stored. Editing the AI’s memory is one way you can shape its responses. Go to gemini.google.com on your phone or laptop, click your profile picture, then choose “Gemini Apps Activity” and look for the memory summary section. Try deleting one old fact and then ask Gemini a question that used to rely on it—you’ll see the difference right away. Source: [engadget.com](https://www.engadget.com/2315475/google-gemini-memory-summary-see-what-remembers-about-you/)

**Walk around inside an AI-built 3D space elevator**
An AI created a fully explorable 3D scene of a realistic space elevator where every object follows real physics and lighting. You can walk through the scene, look around, and watch how light and shadows change as you move. It shows how far AI has come at building whole worlds instead of just flat pictures. The scene is dynamically lit and every single object was coded by hand, yet the result still feels like a real place you can explore. If you have a decent computer, open the web version at the link in Ethan Mollick’s post (search “Upwell Loading Car 07” on itch.io or use the direct demo link). Use WASD keys to walk and the mouse to look around—try riding the elevator car and watching other cars pass while guided speeds adjust the climb time. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/emollick/status/2122983239883317335)
