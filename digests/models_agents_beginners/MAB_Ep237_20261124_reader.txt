Researchers, via their public live page, are calling these groups agent swarms, which just means several AI systems share one job.

One swarm is aimed at a famous unsolved math question called P equals NP, a puzzle that has stumped people for decades.

Another swarm is trying to take the Xbox version of GTA six and turn it into a computer game without permission.

Treat it like a school group project, where one student hunts facts, one writes, and one checks. Except these AIs never sleep, and they can run twenty-four hours a day.

The GTA swarm is looking at how to shift game files from one platform to another while skipping the usual rules.

The live math page shows agents floating an idea, testing it, and switching tactics when they hit a wall.

For classwork, it hints at helpers that could split tough homework across several smart assistants. For games, it hints at help with mods or remixes, though skipping permission raises hard questions about rules and fairness.

Open the P equals NP live site on your phone or laptop right now. Watch the latest attempts even if you do not follow every math step. The page shows the current state of the problem and the newest tries, with no coding required.

Passing work from one AI to another is the part worth slowing down for.

Soccer already trains this muscle, because a striker does not also play goalkeeper, and they pass.

Treat each AI in the swarm the same way. The thing they pass is a chunk of the problem or a bit of code.

One AI looks at where the problem stands right now. Another suggests the next move. A third checks whether that move is even allowed.

They keep sending that information back and forth until the group hits the goal.

If one member gets stuck, the others keep working, so the whole group does not freeze.

Over time the swarm learns which passes work best, the way a real team gets sharper with practice.

That pass, suggest, and check loop is the swarm's real engine. Once you see it as positions and passes, coordinated AI starts to feel ordinary, which is the point.

Google just shipped two changes you can try on a phone without writing any code.

Google's chatbot Gemini, Online Tech Tips reports, can now pull in your conversation history from ChatGPT and other AI chats so those threads live in one place.

That helps if homework help or creative writing ideas are scattered across apps and you do not want to start over. Anyone with a Google account can try it on a phone or laptop.

Open the Gemini website or app, look for the import option in settings, and follow the steps to connect your ChatGPT account.

You can select specific chats or whole histories, and the original context stays intact so Gemini can pick up exactly where the old chat left off. Then ask Gemini to continue a conversation you started somewhere else.

Search itself can now look at your Google Drive files and Wallet information, TechCrunch reports, inside a view called AI Mode, to give more personal results.

Drive holds your documents, and Wallet holds payment details. Ask about a trip, and it might check saved documents or payment details to suggest options that actually fit you.

This works inside the normal Google Search page or the Gemini app if you have those accounts set up.

Try searching something like plan a weekend trip under two hundred dollars, and see if it pulls from your own data.

The update brings Personal Intelligence features, meaning answers that can use your own saved stuff, straight into AI Mode so responses can reference Drive files or Wallet entries without extra steps.

North Korea, the South China Morning Post reports, just showed off its first ChatGPT-style chatbot, and it will not answer anything it treats as uncultured.

Officials present it as a national tool, with filters that block whole categories of questions from the start.

One made-up blog post, in a test described by Shattered, was enough to fool three different AI systems inside twenty-four hours.

The article was designed to look real enough that the AIs pulled it into their overviews without spotting the fabrication.
