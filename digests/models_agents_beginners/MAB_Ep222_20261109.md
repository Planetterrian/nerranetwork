> **Apple just dropped a hint that ChatGPT and Claude could soon live inside your smart home display.**
The biggest news is that Apple’s upcoming HomeView smart hub may soon let you talk to third-party AI chatbots like ChatGPT right on the device. We’ll also look at how one famous AI researcher built an entire video platform in a weekend using Claude, plus a fun everyday way people are already using ChatGPT to pick better wine at the store. Stick around for a simple breakdown of how these chatbots actually work under the hood.
---
### The Big Story
Apple is preparing its new 7-inch HomeView smart home display to support outside AI chatbots in addition to its own Siri. Early test software for the upcoming iOS 27.3 update contains a brand-new section labeled “Conversational Apps.” That section appears designed to let the screen show and run chat services from companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic. The same beta code also lists two-way audio routing, conversation session management, and the ability to handle interruptions so voice chats can feel natural. Security features are being added too: after three failed face-verification attempts the system will ask for iPhone authentication, and repeated lockouts will force waits that grow to eight hours. HomeView itself is scheduled to reach stores on November 13 in selected countries including the United States, Britain, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, India, Singapore, and South Africa, with a starting price of $299 in the U.S. The feature that would actually run third-party chatbots is still described as “not fully wired up,” so no one can say exactly when or whether it will appear. For families this could mean choosing the AI voice that explains homework clearly or that remembers the right smart-light settings, instead of being limited to one company’s style. Students might ask one chatbot for step-by-step science help while parents ask another for quick calendar summaries. You cannot try HomeView yet, but you can already compare how different chatbots answer the same question by opening both ChatGPT and Claude in your browser and typing something like “explain photosynthesis like I’m 12.” Source: [analyticsinsight.net](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/amp/story/news/apple-homeview-chatgpt-claude-support-hinted-in-ios-273)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how your phone keyboard watches the last few words you typed and guesses the next one before you even finish? Now picture that same trick stretched across millions of sentences instead of just one text message. The AI looks at huge collections of books, websites, and conversations to learn which words usually follow which other words. When you ask it a question, it doesn’t “think” the way a person does—it just keeps picking the most likely next piece until it builds a full answer. Each little chunk it reads or writes is called a token, roughly one word or part of a word. The more tokens it can handle at once, the longer the conversation or document it can keep track of without forgetting earlier details. That’s why sometimes a chatbot suddenly seems to lose the thread if your chat gets really long. Picture it as an autocomplete engine that got so good at guessing it can write essays, answer science questions, or even help plan a project.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Andrej Karpathy built a whole video site in one weekend**
OpenAI co-founder Andrej Karpathy used the AI chatbot Claude to sort through 43 million old Hacker News comments and turn them into a clean list of 6,454 recommended videos. The result is a hand-picked feed that he says beats YouTube’s own suggestions for finding interesting tech talks and tutorials. It shows how one person with no coding team can create something useful by chatting with an AI instead of writing code from scratch. If you want to see the spirit of the project, go to the-decoder.com and read the full story, then try asking Claude or ChatGPT to help you organize a list of your own favorite YouTube videos into categories. Open the chatbot on your phone or laptop, paste a short list of video titles you like, and type “group these into beginner, intermediate, and advanced tech talks and give each group a short reason why it fits.” The AI will sort them and explain its choices, giving you a tiny version of the same weekend project. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/andrej-karpathy-built-a-curated-video-platform-in-a-weekend-using-claude-and-20-years-of-hacker-news/)

**Using ChatGPT to pick better supermarket wine**
One Reddit user takes a quick photo of the entire wine shelf and asks ChatGPT which bottle would go well with curry or whatever they’re cooking. The chatbot reads the labels, prices, and grape types from the picture and gives a recommendation plus reasons why it might taste good. It works even when the photo is a little blurry and often matches what a wine expert would pick, but much faster. Next time you’re at the store, open the ChatGPT app on your phone, snap the shelf, and type “Which of these would taste good with pasta?”—you might discover a new favorite without guessing. The same trick works for any meal: just add the dish you’re planning and let the chatbot suggest based on the actual bottles in front of you. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1xrm6lk/my_favourite_use_of_chatgpt_is_buying_supermarket/)
