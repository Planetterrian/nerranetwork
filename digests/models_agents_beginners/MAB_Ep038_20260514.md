### The Big Story
Microsoft just upgraded the AI helper inside its Edge browser so it can see and understand everything across all the tabs you have open right now. Instead of asking the chatbot about one page at a time, you can now ask it to compare two products you're looking at in different tabs, pull facts from multiple articles, or even turn your research into a quick podcast summary. Think of it like having a super-organized friend who can glance at every open window on your screen and instantly connect the dots between them. The update also adds a memory feature that remembers details from earlier conversations and a quiz mode that can test you on what you just read. For students or anyone doing school projects, this means less time copying and pasting between tabs and more time actually understanding the material. It could change how people research online by making the browser itself part of the thinking process rather than just a window to information. If you're already using Edge on your laptop or phone, this feature is rolling out now and works with the free version of Copilot. To try it, open Edge, start a chat with Copilot in the sidebar, and ask something like "compare the phones in my other tabs" while you have a few product pages open. The more tabs you have, the more useful it gets for real tasks like planning a purchase or studying a topic from several sources. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you're texting a friend, your phone's keyboard guesses the next word before you even finish typing? Now imagine that same autocomplete got way better at noticing tiny patterns across millions of old messages and books. First it learns that certain punctuation marks like the long dash often appear in polished writing, even if most people rarely use them in casual chats. Then it starts copying that style because the pattern shows up a lot in the training data from professional articles and essays. Next, when you ask it to write something, it reaches for those same polished patterns because they feel like the "smart" way to sound. And that's basically what a language model is doing when it fills its answers with em-dashes even though regular internet comments are full of typos and short sentences. So next time someone says AI writing looks weirdly formal, you can tell them it's just autocomplete on steroids that learned from the fanciest examples it could find. Not so mysterious once you see it that way. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1tcpjje/if_ai_was_trained_on_the_internet_where_the_hell/)

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Meta's new private AI chats that no one can read later:** The Decoder
Meta just added an Incognito Chat mode to its AI on WhatsApp and the Meta AI app where conversations get processed without being saved anywhere, even on Meta's own servers. This is exciting because it gives you a way to ask questions or brainstorm without worrying that your words stick around forever. Anyone who uses WhatsApp already can try it right away by starting a chat with Meta AI and switching to the private option. Go into WhatsApp, open a conversation with the Meta AI bot, and type something like "help me plan a weekend trip" then look for the incognito toggle to keep it from saving. It's a simple way to test private AI use without signing up for anything new.

**Poppy, the AI that remembers your whole digital life:** TechCrunch
Poppy is a new app that connects your calendar, email, and messages to notice what you need to do next and surface reminders automatically. It feels like having a helpful assistant that knows your schedule without you typing everything in. Students or anyone juggling school and hobbies should try it because it can pull tasks from real messages instead of making you copy them over. Head to the Poppy website or app store listing, connect your email and calendar accounts, then ask it to show reminders based on what's coming up this week. You may need a parent's help to sign up if you're under 18, but the setup is straightforward and free to start. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/13/poppy-debuts-a-proactive-ai-assistant-to-help-organize-your-digital-life/)
