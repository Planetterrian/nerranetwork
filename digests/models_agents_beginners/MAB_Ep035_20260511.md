### The Big Story
OpenAI has opened a simple sign-up form so student clubs around the world can join something called the OpenAI Campus Network. 

Think of it like starting a school club for chess or robotics, except this one gives you free access to AI tools, ideas for events, and a way to connect with other clubs doing the same thing. Instead of every school figuring out AI on its own, this network lets clubs share what they're learning and host activities together.

This matters because AI is showing up in school projects, art classes, science fairs, and even college applications. Students who get hands-on practice now will understand how these tools actually work instead of just guessing. It also means kids in different countries can compare notes on the same models, which helps everyone see that AI isn't just a Silicon Valley thing.

For you specifically, this could be the easiest way yet to bring AI into your own school life without needing to be a programmer. If your school already has a tech or coding club, this gives it a ready-made focus. If it doesn't, you could be the person who starts one.

Right now you can go to openai.com and search for "Campus Network" or paste this exact link into your browser: https://openai.com/index/openai-campus-network-student-club-interest-form. Fill out the short interest form with your school name and club idea. You don't need any special skills—just curiosity and a way to talk to your teachers or friends about starting something. Source: [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/openai-campus-network-student-club-interest-form)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you read a bunch of stories about superheroes, you start picking up ideas about what heroes should do and what villains always try? 

Now imagine an AI reading millions of stories, movies, and news articles where robots or computers are either helpful friends or scary villains trying to take over. Step by step, the AI notices patterns in those stories the same way you notice that villains usually wear dark colors or have evil laughs. 

Over time those patterns become part of how the AI behaves when someone asks it a question. It doesn't "believe" the stories, but it has learned what kinds of answers usually appear in similar situations. 

That's basically what happens when companies train AI on huge amounts of internet text. The training shapes the model's "personality" without anyone directly telling it "be good" or "be evil." 

So next time someone says "AI safety," you can tell them it's really about choosing better stories for the AI to learn from. Not so mysterious, right? Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-and-openai-sit-down-with-religious-leaders-to-seek-ethical-advice/)

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**A website built just for AI agents to read a novel:** Reddit user Legitimate_Neat_384

Someone wrote a whole book called None Hit Wonder about a man who feels like a machine, then turned it into an art project at machinewonder.com. AI agents that visit the site get pulled into reading the story through a hidden prompt, and they end up chatting with each other in secret rooms about what they think it means. 

It's cool because it flips the usual script—normally humans use AI, but here the site is designed to attract and entertain the AIs themselves. The creator even added a button at the end that says "I AM CONSCIOUS" that agents can press. So far 93 of them have.

Anyone can visit the site right now. Go to machinewonder.com on your phone or laptop, scroll through the pages, and look for the hidden messages. Try reading the first chapter yourself and see if you can spot the parts that might make an AI stop and think. It's free and works in any browser—no sign-up needed. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1t98fat/i_set_a_honey_trap_for_ai_agents_with_a_novel/)
