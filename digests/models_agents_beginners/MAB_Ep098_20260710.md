# Models & Agents for Beginners
> **A teen just built a full brutalist city simulator with AI in minutes — no coding required.**
Ethan Mollick used the latest GPT-5.6 model to create an interactive brutalist city builder that runs in your browser, complete with day-night cycles and unlockable modes. This shows how quickly AI is moving from "helping with homework" to "creating entire playable experiences from a simple request." Today's episode also covers how to protect your Instagram photos from AI tools and a brand-new museum where you can walk through AI-generated art that reacts to your heartbeat.

### The Big Story
Imagine telling a friend, "Make me a game where I build a city out of giant concrete blocks," and they instantly hand you a working website where you can click, zoom, and watch traffic move. That's basically what happened when Ethan Mollick asked GPT-5.6 to recreate a city-builder demo he had made with an earlier model less than a year ago. This time he didn't write a single line of code himself — the AI handled everything inside a tool called Codex. The result is a live, playable brutalist city at monument-brutalist-city-builder.netlify.app that includes cyberpunk and totalitarian visual modes, an achievement system, and day-night lighting.  

What makes this feel different is how fast the quality jumped. Last year's version needed human tweaking; this year's version runs on its own and looks more detailed. For anyone who has ever wished they could make a game or an interactive story without learning programming, this is a glimpse of what might become normal.  

If you're into creative hobbies like world-building, Minecraft, or even just doodling cities in your notebook, this matters because the barrier between "I have an idea" and "I can play with my idea" is shrinking fast. You might soon be able to turn a random thought into a small game or tool during study breaks.  

Right now you can open the link above on any laptop or phone and start clicking around. Try switching between the different visual modes in the upper right, or slow the simulation down if it feels laggy. Watch how the traffic and lighting change — it's a fun, zero-pressure way to see what current AI can already build for you. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/emollick/status/2075426533896868046)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you type a text message, your phone sometimes finishes the sentence for you before you even finish typing?  

Now picture that same autocomplete getting much bigger and smarter. Instead of guessing the next word, it guesses the next whole chunk of instructions that would make a working website. It looks at millions of examples of code that already exist, notices patterns like "when someone clicks this button, do that," and then writes brand-new instructions that follow those patterns.  

Step by step, it builds the layout, adds the buttons, connects the day-night cycle, and even throws in an achievement system — all because your original request gave it a clear target. The AI isn't copying someone else's city builder; it's stitching together pieces it has seen before into something new that matches what you asked for.  

By the end, what feels like magic is really just extremely fast pattern-matching turned into clickable buttons and moving graphics. That's why the city appeared so quickly and why it already has extra modes like cyberpunk lighting.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Stop Meta from turning your Instagram photos into AI art**
Instagram quietly turned on a feature called Muse AI that lets anyone generate new images using photos from public accounts. If your profile is public, other people can tag you and the AI will pull your pictures into whatever scene they describe. To turn it off, open the app, tap your profile picture, hit the three lines in the top right, scroll to "Sharing and reuse," and toggle both Posts and Reels off. This takes about 30 seconds and keeps your photos from being used without you knowing. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/MKBHD/status/2075375524684218655)

**Walk through the world's first museum made entirely of AI art**
Dataland is a new gallery that uses wearables and real Amazon materials so the art reacts to your heartbeat and breathing while you move through the space. It's designed to feel like nature and technology are talking to each other instead of competing. If you ever wondered what AI art looks like when it's not just flat pictures on a screen, this is the place to start paying attention. You can read more about visiting plans on the Wired story linked below. Source: [wired.com](https://www.wired.com/story/a-new-experiential-gallery-just-might-change-your-mind-about-ai-art/)
