> **# Models & Agents for Beginners**
ChatGPT just dropped unlimited free chats and a “Think” button that lets the AI slow down and reason harder.
OpenAI made its latest models way more accessible to everyday users, including free ones. This means you can now chat as much as you want without hitting limits, and you get tools that help the AI work through tricky questions step by step. Today we’ll break down what changed, how the “thinking” actually works, plus creative tools you can try right away.
---
### The Big Story
OpenAI updated how ChatGPT works for millions of people, especially those using the free version. Starting now, free and “Go” users get unlimited text chats with a model called GPT-5.6 Luna, plus a new “Think” button that gives the AI extra time to reason on harder questions. Plus and Pro users also received an improved version of the stronger GPT-5.6 Sol model along with a slider that lets them choose how much effort the AI puts into each answer. The update gives GPT-5.6 Sol more focused responses while keeping the version that powers Work and Codex unchanged. Free users will switch to the smaller GPT-5.6 Luna as their default starting next week, and that model still falls short of its bigger siblings in capability. The reasoning slider is described as easier to use after listening to feedback.

Think of the slider like asking a friend to either give you a quick opinion or really sit down and work through a tough problem with you. The free tier change removes the old daily message caps that used to cut conversations short, so students and casual users can now ask follow-up questions without worrying about running out. This matters because ChatGPT is already used for homework help, brainstorming story ideas, and answering random curious questions—removing limits makes it feel more like a reliable study buddy you can turn to anytime. For teens and beginners, it lowers the barrier to experimenting with AI without needing a paid account. You can now test longer projects, like planning a school presentation or exploring a hobby topic in depth, all for free. To try it yourself, open the ChatGPT app or go to chatgpt.com on your phone or laptop, start a new chat, and look for the “Think” button on tougher questions. Just type something like “Help me understand how photosynthesis works” and tap Think to see the difference in how detailed the answer becomes. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re deciding what to eat for dinner, you sometimes just grab the first thing in the fridge, but other times you stop and list out what’s in the house, check what you had yesterday, and think about what you feel like? That extra pause is basically what the new “Think” button and reasoning slider do inside ChatGPT. Instead of rushing to the most likely next word, the model now spends more steps checking its own work, comparing options, and building a short chain of thoughts before answering. It’s like the AI is quietly writing a tiny scratch paper list in the background—testing one idea, seeing if it fits, then adjusting—rather than blurting out the first guess. The slider simply tells the model how long that scratch-paper session should last: short for quick facts, longer for math or planning problems. This approach helps on questions where one wrong step would ruin the whole answer, like figuring out a multi-part science problem or writing a clear explanation. The result feels more careful and less likely to miss something obvious. The same idea shows up when the model is asked to solve something step by step instead of jumping straight to a final sentence. You can picture it as the difference between guessing the end of a story after reading the first page versus reading a few chapters and then deciding how it should continue. That built-in pause is what turns a fast guess into a more reliable answer.

### Quick Bits
**Google’s free AI model for spotting hurricanes earlier**
Google open-sourced an AI called WeatherNext that can predict a storm’s path and strength up to 15 days ahead. This could help communities prepare sooner for big weather events, and because it’s free for anyone to use and improve, students and weather fans can explore how it works. The model focuses on delivering forecasts that include both track and intensity details over that two-week window. Source: [engadget.com](https://www.engadget.com/2232141/google-open-source-ai-model-can-help-with-earlier-hurricane-warnings/)

**Suno fights spammy AI music with watermarks**
The AI music tool Suno is adding invisible watermarks and new download rules so people can tell when a track was made by AI. This keeps creative tools from being flooded with low-quality spam while still letting real musicians experiment. The company outlined the changes in a blog post that also shares its principles for responsible use going forward.
