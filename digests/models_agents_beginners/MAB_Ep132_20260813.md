### The Big Story
A popular post on the ChatGPT subreddit breaks down OpenAI’s tools into three easy categories that finally feel logical. ChatGPT is the version you talk to for questions, brainstorming, or uploading files. Codex is different because it gets its own computer-like space where it can read files, run commands, and actually edit things on a machine. Work is the newest piece: an agent that runs in the cloud and connects to your email, documents, and apps so you can give it tasks from your phone without needing a full computer nearby. 

The post explains that ChatGPT equals the AI you talk to, Codex equals an agent plus a computer that hogs RAM and CPU while running terminal commands or tests, and Work equals an agent plus cloud plus integrations that can reach Gmail, Slack, Google Drive, or Dropbox. The original poster notes that Codex runs on an actual computer somewhere, which creates a problem if you are sitting on the couch with only an iPhone. Work solves that by operating through existing integrations instead of taking over your local machine. 

Think of it like having three different helpers. One is a smart friend you text with, one is a coworker who sits at a desk with tools and files, and one is a remote assistant who can pull information from all your online accounts. This matters because many people get confused when companies release multiple similar-sounding products, and this simple split shows exactly when you might reach for each one. For students or anyone juggling school and part-time work, it means you can pick the right helper depending on whether you’re on your laptop or just your phone. 

You can read the full explanation yourself right now at the Reddit link in the show notes and see how the original poster uses real examples like summarizing Slack threads or working with local files. The post is written by a regular user, not a company, so it skips the marketing language and focuses on what actually changes for everyday people. The mental model also points out that Work exists on desktop too, but thinking about it mobile-first is what finally made the product lineup feel coherent to the poster. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re texting, your phone suggests the next word based on what you’ve already typed? Now imagine that same idea, but the AI can also reach out and use other apps or files the way a helpful assistant would. 

First it reads your request in full. Then it decides which tool or document it needs, like checking your calendar or pulling up an email. After that it takes the action in the background and brings back a finished result instead of just giving advice. The key difference is that a regular chatbot stops at suggestions, while an agent actually carries out steps across different services. 

That separation shows up clearly when you compare the three OpenAI tools. ChatGPT stays inside the conversation window. Codex needs a real computer environment with local files and terminal access. Work skips the local computer and works through cloud connections instead. The result is that you can hand Work a task like “find the relevant emails, check the documents, look through the Slack discussion and create a summary” even when you only have your phone in your hand. 

That’s what separates simple chat from the newer agent-style tools: one talks, the other does.

**Twitch streamers can now opt out of AI training**
Amazon updated its rules so people who stream on Twitch can choose whether their videos help train future AI models. The opt-out covers streams, VODs, clips, stream chats, and pictures or text on the channel, and it applies only to future training of models meant to generate or synthesize text and other media.
