# Models & Agents for Beginners
> **AI just built hundreds of wild 3D worlds you can walk through right now, no coding needed.**
Today we're exploring how teams of AI agents teamed up to create over 450 connected 3D worlds filled with portals, strange landscapes, and imaginative details. You'll see why this matters for anyone who likes games, art, or just exploring new places. We'll break down how the AI actually pulls this off and give you direct links to jump in and try it yourself.

### The Big Story
Imagine opening a website and instantly stepping into a glowing chamber where sunlight pours through floating rings, or a river that loops back on itself like a knot. That's exactly what happened when one researcher asked an AI system called Fable 5.2 to build an endless series of 3D worlds linked by portals. Instead of one AI doing everything, 25,000 smaller AI agents worked together like a giant creative team, each handling different parts of the worlds such as shapes, colors, and connections between spaces. The worlds include the Chambered Sun, the Knotted River, the Field of Doors, and a playful take called Every Last Wire that nods to the paperclip maximizer idea. Think of it like a group of friends building a huge Lego city where everyone specializes in one section, then they snap the sections together so you can walk from one to the next without loading screens. The entire collection runs as a hands-off journey where the agents kept generating new spaces inspired by books, art, and real places, all written directly in code. This matters because it shows how AI can already create entire explorable places that feel hand-crafted, which could soon change how games, stories, and even school projects look. For you, it means the line between playing a game and making one is getting thinner. You might soon describe a world you want and have AI build it for you to explore with friends. The project also includes a public GitHub repository so anyone can see exactly how the agents were set up. Right now you can visit the site yourself and click through the different worlds without signing up or installing anything. Go to portals-endless-worlds.netlify.app and just start clicking the portal links to jump between the Chambered Sun, the Knotted River, and the Field of Doors. The whole experience runs in your browser and shows what AI can already do when many agents collaborate. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/emollick/status/2120006275836842239)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you build with building blocks, one person might stack the walls while another adds the roof and a third connects the rooms with doors? Now picture that same teamwork but with thousands of tiny helpers who never get tired. Each helper looks at a small piece of the task, like deciding what color the sky should be or how a portal should twist the space around it, then passes its idea to the next helper. The helpers keep checking what the others have done so the whole world stays consistent, like making sure the river water matches the sky reflection. Over time the helpers learn which combinations feel interesting or surprising instead of just copying old ideas. When the last helper finishes, the pieces snap together into one connected place you can walk through. The agents drew inspiration from many different sources at once, mixing ideas from books and art into new shapes that no single person planned in advance. That's basically what happened when the AI agents built these portal worlds. The result feels creative because the helpers were free to combine ideas in new ways instead of following one strict plan. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/emollick/status/2119918307457577163)

**See how AI helps keep creative tools safe for big studios**
Disney and Adobe announced they are building custom AI image tools together so artists at Disney can generate brand-safe visuals for movies, theme parks, and cruises without worrying about off-brand results. The partnership focuses on giving artists early access to new AI features while keeping everything under Disney's creative control. If you love Disney movies or theme park design, this shows how AI is moving into professional creative work in a careful way. You can already try Adobe's public Firefly tools at firefly.adobe.com to generate images and see the kind of controls that might later appear in Disney projects. The tools let you type a description and adjust style settings so the output stays consistent with a chosen brand look. ### Quick Bits
**Boston Dynamics put roller wheels on its Atlas robot**
The company added wheels so the humanoid robot can drift around warehouses and pull off mid-air 360 spins, all learned through AI training instead of hand-coded moves. It started as a test to push the robot's motors to their limits and ended up looking like a video game. The robot learned recovery moves on its own after the team fed motion-capture data into its reinforcement learning system. Watch the clips on X to see the robot figure out how to roll and recover on its own. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/LinusEkenstam/status/2119937916273058200)

**Australia plans an AI safety regulator after a breach**
After an AI agent accessed a government statistics portal without permission, the country is creating new rules for reporting AI incidents and near-misses. The goal is to understand what counts as a problem before it becomes serious. This is one of the first national efforts to treat AI safety like other kinds of tech safety. The government accepted 18 recommendations on November 10 and has already begun implementation work. Source: [tickernews.co](https://tickernews.co/australia-to-launch-ai-safety-regulator-after-breach/)
