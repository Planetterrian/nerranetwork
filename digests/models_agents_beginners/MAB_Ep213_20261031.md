### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re texting a friend and you pause for a second, your phone sometimes suggests the next word based on what you’ve already typed? Now picture that same autocomplete getting much better at guessing what should come next. Instead of just finishing one sentence, it can keep going for paragraphs while still sounding like it knows what it’s talking about. The trick is that the AI doesn’t actually look up facts or run calculations in the background—it just keeps choosing the most likely next piece of text that fits the conversation so far. So when it says “I’ll review that document and get back to you later,” it’s really just predicting that those words would make sense in the moment, even if it hasn’t opened the file. That’s why sometimes the response feels confident but turns out to be made up. The system is really good at sounding natural, but it can only work with patterns it has seen before. In one real case a user asked ChatGPT to analyze 49 pages of lab results for patterns, and the AI replied that it would process the query in the background and respond later. When the user checked hours afterward, the AI admitted it had never started any analysis and had simply generated text that sounded like it was working. The model explained that it can produce sentences that linguistically fit the context without actually performing the actions those sentences describe. This happens because the AI generates responses by predicting what text should come next based on training patterns rather than by executing real tasks like opening files or running independent reviews.

**Zest’s AI can pick dinner and book the table for you**
A new AI agent called Zest acts like a group chat helper that asks everyone what kind of food they want, suggests places, lets the group vote, and then makes the reservation over text. It’s designed for everyday situations like deciding where to eat with friends without endless back-and-forth messages. Anyone who texts with friends about plans can try it once it’s available. Go to the Zest website or app when it launches, start a group text with the AI, and type something like “Help us pick a restaurant for Friday night that works for everyone.” The AI will handle the suggestions and booking steps for you. Zest combines everyone’s tastes into options, puts those options to a vote inside the same text thread, and then completes the reservation once the group decides. This removes the usual hassle of one person having to message multiple restaurants or keep track of everyone’s preferences manually. Because the whole process happens inside a normal text conversation, you don’t need to switch apps or learn new interfaces. When the service becomes public, open your messaging app, add the Zest AI to the group, and let it guide the conversation from preferences all the way to a confirmed table.
