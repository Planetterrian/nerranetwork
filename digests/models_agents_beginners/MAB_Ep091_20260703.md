### The Big Story
Meta has quietly released an experimental app called Pocket that lets people create and share small interactive games using simple text prompts. Instead of learning coding or game design software, you type what you want the game to do and the AI builds it for you right away. Think of it like asking a friend to invent a quick game on the spot, except the friend is an AI that can instantly turn your idea into something you can play on your phone. The app focuses on short, fun experiences rather than big polished titles, so you might make a tiny puzzle, a silly racing game, or a quick trivia challenge in seconds. This matters because it lowers the barrier for creative play — you no longer need expensive tools or years of practice to experiment with game ideas. For students or anyone who loves Roblox or mobile games, it opens a door to making instead of just consuming. You might discover you enjoy designing game mechanics, which could spark interest in future creative or tech careers. The biggest personal takeaway is that AI is moving from answering questions to building experiences you can interact with immediately. Right now you can search for "Meta Pocket" in your app store or on the web to see if it's available in your region and start typing prompts like "a simple endless runner with a cat character." The app is still experimental, so features may change quickly. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/02/meta-quietly-launches-vibe-coded-gaming-app-pocket/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you order food through a delivery app, the app doesn't just show you a menu — it also handles finding a driver, tracking the order, updating you if something changes, and making sure the food actually arrives? Now imagine that same idea but for digital tasks instead of dinner. An AI agent is like a helper that can take a goal you give it, break it into smaller steps, use different tools along the way, and keep going until the job is done. For example, if you asked it to research and write a short report on your favorite band, it might first search for recent news, then pull facts from a couple of sites, then organize everything into paragraphs, and finally check its own work for mistakes. The agent keeps track of what it has already done and what still needs to happen, just like the delivery app keeps updating your order status. What makes this different from a regular chatbot is that the agent can act over time and switch between different skills instead of answering one question and stopping. Recent measurements show these agents are getting better fast — they can now finish about 16 percent of real freelance tasks at a professional level, up from just 2.5 percent only eight months ago. The progress comes from giving the AI clearer ways to plan and check its own steps instead of guessing everything at once. So when you hear people talk about AI agents, picture a helpful assistant that can carry out a whole project instead of just chatting. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/ai-agents-can-now-complete-16-percent-of-freelance-jobs-at-pro-quality-up-from-2-5-percent-eight-months-ago/)
