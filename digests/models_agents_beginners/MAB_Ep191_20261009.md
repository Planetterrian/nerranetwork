# Models & Agents for Beginners
> **Claude can now turn simple text into animated videos and working dashboards.**
Anthropic just added two new tools to Claude that let anyone create animated explainers and live data visuals straight from words. This matters because it lowers the barrier for students, creators, and curious beginners who want to make professional-looking content without learning complex software. Today we’ll explore how these features work, see a wild browser-based 3D project, and check out practical ways people are already using AI for everyday tasks.
---
### The Big Story
Anthropic added two beta features to Claude called Motion and Dashboards. Motion creates short animated videos from text descriptions and images, while Dashboards pulls information from data sources and turns it into interactive charts and displays. Think of it like describing a scene to a helpful friend who then draws moving pictures and arranges them into a clear story, except the friend is an AI that works instantly. The tools now work on free accounts too, so you don’t need a paid plan to experiment. This changes things for school projects because instead of spending hours learning video editing apps, you can type what you want and get a finished animation in minutes. It also helps with understanding data—if you’re tracking grades or sports stats, you could ask for a live dashboard instead of staring at spreadsheets. For anyone thinking about creative careers, this shows how AI is becoming a co-creator rather than just a chatbot. You might use it to explain a science concept for class or turn a story idea into a short video. To try it, go to claude.ai, start a new chat on a free account, and type something like “create an animated explainer about how rain forms” or “make a dashboard showing weekly study time.” The system will generate the video or dashboard right in the conversation. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/claude-can-now-generate-animated-explainer-videos-and-live-data-dashboards-from-text-prompts/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you describe a scene to someone over the phone, they can picture it and even sketch a quick version based on your words? Now imagine that person also knows thousands of short video clips and can combine pieces of them to match exactly what you described. The AI looks at your text one small chunk at a time, decides what should appear first, then what should move next, and builds each frame by predicting the most likely next image that fits the story. It does this over and over, checking that the motion stays smooth and the objects stay consistent, almost like flipping through a flipbook where every page is drawn to match the one before it. When you add an image as a starting point, the system uses that picture as the first frame and then generates the rest of the movement around it. The result feels like a tiny movie because the model has seen millions of real videos during training and learned the patterns of how things actually move in the world. That’s why the animations can look natural even though nothing was filmed by a camera.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**A 3D creek that runs right in your browser**
Someone built a peaceful flowing creek scene using GPT-6 Astra and the Three.js graphics library, with every rock, plant, and ripple created in Blender. The whole thing loads and moves smoothly inside a regular web browser without any special software. It’s a great example of how AI can help generate 3D assets that used to require teams of artists. If you enjoy games or world-building, this shows what’s possible with current tools. Visit the Reddit post in r/ChatGPT titled “A 3D creek running in your browser” and click the link in the post to load the scene yourself—try rotating the view or just watching the water move. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1x1dt3b/a_3d_creek_running_in_your_browser/)

**Letting ChatGPT handle repetitive job applications**
One student got tired of typing the same name, address, and work history into dozens of forms, so they connected ChatGPT to their email and browser to scan listings and fill out matching applications. The AI compares job descriptions to the resume, opens the forms, and fills the repeated fields automatically. It’s not perfect—CAPTCHAs still stop it sometimes—but it cuts down the boring parts dramatically. If you’re applying for summer jobs or internships, this workflow could save hours. Open ChatGPT, upload your resume, and ask it to compare a job description you paste in; then try the browser tool to see how it helps with the next steps. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1x1dejg/i_made_chatgpt_do_the_worst_part_of_job_hunting/)

This hints at how AI might one day help with quick health questions, though it’s still not a replacement for real medical care. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/emollick/status/2108414247558316508)
