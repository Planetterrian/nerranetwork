# Models & Agents for Beginners
> **AI just helped students jump almost a full grade on a marketing project — but it raises a big question about what they actually learned.**
Researchers ran a big experiment with over a thousand students and found that AI can dramatically boost grades on certain assignments. This matters because those are exactly the skills schools reward most. Today we break down what that means for anyone in school right now, plus two super simple things you can try with ChatGPT on your phone in the next five minutes.
---
### The Big Story
A new study with 1,053 university students showed that using GPT-4o on a marketing assignment raised their grades by nearly a full point on a five-point scale. The researchers wanted to see how much AI could improve performance on real schoolwork. Think of it like having an extremely fast research assistant who can organize ideas, write clear paragraphs, and polish your work in seconds. The catch is that the study did not check whether students actually understood the material better after using the AI. For teens and students, this hits close to home because the skills that get top grades — clear writing, structured thinking, and quick research — are the exact ones current AI tools handle really well. That means your report or presentation could look much stronger, but the learning part might not stick the same way. The bigger picture is that schools may need to rethink what they test if AI can fake the output without the understanding. On the positive side, it also shows how AI can act like a personal tutor for organizing thoughts when used thoughtfully. Right now there is no public demo of this exact study, but you can test the same idea yourself by asking ChatGPT to help structure a short school assignment and then compare what you produce with and without it. The experiment took place at Bocconi University and focused on a marketing task where the AI helped with the kind of output that usually earns high marks. Students who had access to GPT-4o produced work that scored almost one point higher than those who worked without it. The five-point scale is the same kind of grading system many high schools and colleges use, so the jump is easy to picture: a solid B becoming an A-minus or better. What the researchers noticed is that the biggest gains came in areas like organizing information and writing polished paragraphs — exactly the parts AI is trained to do well. This does not mean the students learned more about marketing itself, because the study did not measure long-term understanding. It simply shows that the final product looked stronger on paper. For anyone still in school, the takeaway is practical: AI can make your work look better right now, but it is still up to you to make sure you actually know the material when test time comes. Teachers and parents are starting to talk about new ways to check real learning, such as oral explanations or in-class writing that AI cannot do for you. The study gives a clear number to think about: 1,053 students is a large enough group that the result is worth paying attention to. If you want to see the effect for yourself without waiting for new school rules, open ChatGPT and try writing one paragraph of an assignment on your own, then ask the AI to help rewrite it and notice how different the two versions feel. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/the-skills-that-earn-top-grades-are-the-ones-ai-can-fake-best/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re texting a friend, your phone sometimes guesses the next word before you even finish typing? Now picture that same idea stretched across an entire paragraph or essay. The AI looks at the words you’ve already written, checks patterns it learned from millions of other texts, and predicts what should come next to sound natural and on-topic. It does this one small chunk at a time, over and over, until the whole answer is built. Because it has seen so many examples of good schoolwork, it can copy the style that usually earns high marks. The result looks polished even if the person using it didn’t do the thinking themselves. That’s why the study found such big grade jumps — the AI is really good at producing the exact kind of output teachers reward. The process starts with the model breaking your prompt into tiny pieces called tokens, each roughly one word or part of a word. It then uses everything it has read before to decide which token is most likely to come next. Over thousands of these tiny decisions, a full answer appears that follows the patterns of high-scoring work. In the marketing assignment experiment, this step-by-step prediction helped students produce clearer structure and better wording without them having to build every sentence from scratch. The model does not know the marketing topic the way a student who studied it would; it only knows what good answers tend to look like. That difference is exactly why the grades went up even though the study did not measure whether the students understood the content more deeply afterward. Watching the AI build an answer this way makes the grade boost feel less mysterious and more like a very fast autocomplete that has read a lot of textbooks.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Turn any photo into a mini artbook page**
ChatGPT’s image tool can take one of your photos and turn it into a split-page layout that looks like a page from a photography journal, with a realistic photo on top and a hand-drawn ink sketch of the same scene below. It keeps the people, poses, and mood from your original picture while adding nice typography and paper texture. This is perfect if you like making mood boards, zines, or just want to play with your vacation photos in a creative way. Open the ChatGPT app or go to chatgpt.com, start a new chat, upload any photo you have on your phone, and paste this prompt: “Create a nostalgic editorial photo-to-sketch artbook page based on the reference image. Divide the page vertically into two connected sections…” Hit send and see what it makes. You can tweak the prompt afterward if you want different colors or a different style. The tool works on both the free and paid versions of ChatGPT, so you can experiment right away without needing a subscription. Many people are using it to turn ordinary phone snapshots into something that feels like a page from a travel diary or art book. If the first result is not quite right, you can reply with a short change like “make the sketch lines softer” and the AI will generate a new version. This kind of creative play shows how image tools are moving from simple filters to full layout helpers that anyone can use. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1w32gdt/turning_a_photo_into_a_cinematic_photo_inksketch/)

**Change your ChatGPT display name in under a minute**
If you ever wanted your chats to show a different name than the one tied to your account, there’s now a simple setting for it. Click your profile picture in the bottom-left corner of the ChatGPT website or app, go to “Profile,” and type whatever name you want to appear. It updates instantly and shows up in all your conversations. This is handy if you share a device with family or just want a fun nickname instead of your real name. No coding or extra apps needed — it’s built right into the normal settings. The change stays until you edit it again, so you can switch back whenever you like. It is a small feature, but it makes the chat feel more personal without any extra steps. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1w35kso/how_to_change_your_username_on_chatgpt/)

### Quick Bits
**AI agents are terrible at guessing how long tasks will take**
New research found that AI coding assistants like Claude Code and Codex often overestimate how long a job will take — sometimes by ten times the actual time. They also rate their own work about 20 points higher than humans do. It’s a reminder that these tools still lack real-world experience even when they sound confident. The gap shows up most clearly on longer projects where the AI has to plan several steps ahead. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/ai-agents-have-no-sense-of-time-and-are-not-aware-of-it/)

**Virginia Beach schools just partnered with OpenAI**
The school district signed an agreement to bring ChatGPT tools into classrooms. This is one of the growing number of districts testing how AI can support teachers and students directly in everyday lessons. The move puts real classroom use of AI in the spotlight for parents and students who want to know what is changing in their own schools.
