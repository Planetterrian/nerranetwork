We’ll also break down exactly how large language models turn simple math into writing essays and code. ### The Big Story
OpenAI released a new feature called Appshots for its Codex coding assistant. With one button press on a Mac, you can send whatever is currently visible in any app window straight to the AI so it understands the exact task you’re working on. Think of it like handing your AI helper a live screenshot of your screen instead of typing out long descriptions of what you need. This matters because coding help usually requires you to copy and paste tons of details, which is slow and easy to mess up. Now the AI can see your actual project files, designs, or error messages without extra steps. For students learning to code or creators building small apps, this could make getting help feel more like chatting with a friend who can look over your shoulder. It might speed up homework projects or hobby coding experiments by removing the friction of explaining everything in text. If you have a Mac and access to Codex through ChatGPT, try opening a coding project in one window and pressing the Appshot shortcut to see how the AI responds with context from your screen. The feature is rolling out now for Mac users. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/openai-appshots-turn-any-mac-window-into-context-for-codex/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re texting a friend, your phone suggests the next word before you finish typing? Now imagine that same autocomplete got millions of times smarter because it studied billions of sentences instead of just your recent chats. It doesn’t “think” like a person. Instead it turns every word into a number (called a token) and uses math to figure out which number is most likely to come next based on everything it has seen before. When you type a sentence about an investor going to the bank, the model checks patterns from its training and predicts “vault” instead of “river” because the surrounding words about money make that the strongest match. Layers inside the model help by first turning words into vectors (basically arrows in space that capture meaning), then using attention to notice which words relate to each other, and finally a special part called the LM Head turns all that math back into actual word choices. There is no magic moment where it suddenly becomes conscious. It is just an enormous probability machine that keeps picking the best next token from its vocabulary. So next time someone says “large language model,” you can tell them it is basically supercharged autocomplete that got really good at guessing what comes next. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/artificial/comments/1tl59ha/llms_are_just_giant_probability_machines/)

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**AI that spots security bugs faster than people can fix them: The Decoder**
Anthropic shared that its Claude Mythos Preview model, working with about 50 partners, discovered over 10,000 serious security holes in important software. The problem is these bugs are appearing quicker than teams can patch them, creating a risky window where the issues could be misused. This matters because the same kind of AI that helps with homework could also be used to find weak spots in apps you use every day. It is a reminder that powerful tools need strong safety rules, and no company has figured out perfect protections yet. While you cannot try the preview model yourself, you can stay aware by keeping your own apps and devices updated whenever patches appear. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-warns-claude-mythos-preview-finds-bugs-faster-than-developers-can-patch-them/)

**When Google’s AI search stops listening to you: The Verge**
Google’s AI Overviews sometimes ignore the actual words you typed and give a generic chatbot-style answer instead. One example showed that searching the word “disregard” made the AI respond as if you were chatting with it rather than summarizing search results. This is interesting because millions of students and researchers rely on search for quick facts, and small glitches like this can send you down the wrong path. It shows that even big AI tools still have rough edges when handling real user requests. You can test it yourself right now by going to google.com and trying a simple one-word search like “disregard” to see what the AI Overview shows. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/tech/936176/google-ai-overviews-search-disregard)
