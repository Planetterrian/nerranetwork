> **ChatGPT just learned to remember way more about you without you asking.**
OpenAI rolled out a smarter memory system that tracks important details automatically for ChatGPT Plus and Pro users. This changes how the AI can help with school projects, creative work, and daily tasks by keeping context across chats. Today we’ll also explore how AI cracked an 80-year-old math puzzle, check out a new image model from Egypt, and see what happens when an Uber driver discovers his playlist is 100% AI-generated.

### The Big Story
OpenAI announced that ChatGPT is getting a new memory system that automatically keeps track of important details from your conversations. Instead of you having to remind it about your preferences or past topics every time, the AI now notices and saves key information on its own. Think of it like a really good friend who remembers you prefer studying in the morning and that you’re working on a history project about ancient Egypt. The update also doubles the amount of memory available, so longer projects stay organized without losing details. This matters because most people use AI for homework help, brainstorming stories, or planning things over multiple days. When the AI forgets what you told it yesterday, it feels like starting over. With better memory, it can build on your earlier ideas and give more useful suggestions. For students, that could mean an AI that remembers your writing style and helps improve it across assignments. For creators, it could track themes you like in your art or videos. The rollout started today for Plus and Pro users in the US. To try it, open the ChatGPT app on your phone, make sure it’s updated, and start a conversation about something you care about. Ask it to remember details like your favorite subjects or a project you’re working on, then check back tomorrow to see what it recalls. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2062567561276100809)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re texting a friend, your phone sometimes remembers the last few messages so the conversation makes sense? Now imagine the AI keeps a running notebook of everything important you’ve said across many chats, not just the last few lines. It looks for patterns like “this person always asks about science homework on weeknights” or “they’re building a story about dragons.” Then it uses that notebook to make future answers fit what it already knows about you. The system doesn’t store every single word forever. It picks out the useful parts the same way you might highlight key sentences in your notes instead of copying the whole textbook. That’s basically what the new memory feature is doing. So next time someone says “context window” or “long-term memory in AI,” you can tell them it’s just the AI keeping a smart notebook of your important details. Not so mysterious, right?

**A new image model built in Egypt**
A team in Egypt just released Horus Lens 1.0, their first text-to-image model made locally. It’s part of a bigger effort to grow AI tools in the region and shows that cool AI work is happening all over the world, not just in big tech hubs. The model focuses on generating images from descriptions, and early examples look promising for creative projects. If you enjoy making art or experimenting with visuals, this is worth watching as it develops. Check out the announcement and sample images shared on Reddit’s r/artificial to see what it can do.
