> **OpenAI is folding its separate Work mode back into regular ChatGPT — and your usage limits are about to share one pool.**
Starting soon, ChatGPT will test a single chat window where everyday conversations and bigger projects live together, with one shared usage allowance instead of separate counters. This matters because millions of students and hobbyists use ChatGPT daily for homework, creative writing, and quick questions. We'll also look at how AI image-editing agents actually pick tools, plus a serious safety story about hidden deepfake features in ordinary-looking apps.

### The Big Story
OpenAI is testing a change that removes the separate “Work” tab inside ChatGPT and brings everything into one chat window. Right now, people who pay for ChatGPT Plus or Pro have different usage counters for regular chatting, the Work area, and the coding tool Codex. The new setup will combine the first two so you can start a normal conversation and then ask the same chat to build a website or presentation without switching tabs. Think of it like your phone’s Messages app suddenly letting you open a full spreadsheet or edit a photo without leaving the thread — convenient, but everything now draws from the same monthly allowance of tokens. That means a long, complex project could use up the allowance faster than before, leaving less room for quick questions later in the month. For students using ChatGPT to brainstorm essays or practice language skills, this could feel like the meter running quicker on bigger tasks. OpenAI says most people probably won’t notice a difference and that the fast “Instant” model stays unlimited, but the company is rolling the test out slowly and asking for feedback first. If you’re on a paid plan, you can watch for an email or in-app notice about joining the small test group. You can also check your current usage numbers right now under Settings → Usage to see how the separate counters work before the change arrives. Source: [trendingtopics.eu](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/chatgpt-merges-chat-and-work-following-claudes-playbook/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how your phone has a data plan with a monthly limit, and streaming a whole movie uses way more data than sending a few texts? AI chatbots work on something similar called a usage allowance. Every time you type a question or the AI writes a long answer, it breaks the text into small pieces called tokens — roughly one token per word. More advanced models or longer, more complicated tasks burn through tokens faster, just like HD video eats your phone data quicker than a simple message. When OpenAI merges the chat and Work areas, those different kinds of conversations now share one total token budget instead of having separate buckets. The company is trying to make the experience feel smoother so you don’t have to decide “am I chatting or working?” but the trade-off is that one big project can leave less room for everything else until the allowance resets. The unlimited fast model stays free to use, so quick questions won’t be affected. It’s basically the same idea as a family phone plan where everyone shares the same data — convenient until one person starts downloading lots of videos.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Adobe’s new AI that edits photos like a real Photoshop pro**
Adobe Research built an AI agent called EditAgent that doesn’t just apply one filter over and over. Instead, it looks at your photo, picks the right specialized tool for the job (like the clone stamp or adjustment brush), tries a few options, and then lets a regular language model choose the best result based on actual image measurements. This is exciting because it shows how future creative tools could feel more like having a helpful assistant who knows which button to press instead of you guessing. If you like editing photos for school projects, social media, or art, this points to tools that could save hours of clicking around. The research is still early, but you can already play with similar ideas in the free version of Photoshop Express on your phone or the web at photoshop.com. Try this: upload a photo, use the “Healing” or “Adjust” tools, and notice how the app suggests specific fixes — that’s a tiny taste of what the smarter agent version will do. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/adobe-is-testing-how-well-ai-agents-can-use-editing-tools-like-a-photoshop-professional-would/)

**Hundreds of everyday-looking AI apps secretly offered deepfake abuse tools**
A Wired investigation found roughly 400 apps and websites, mostly tied to Chinese-language developers, that looked like normal AI image or chat tools but hid features letting users create violent or non-consensual deepfake images and videos. The apps used regular AI models but added secret prompts or extra buttons that bypassed normal safety filters. This matters because it shows how hard it is to keep powerful image tools safe once they’re released — and why checking where an app comes from and reading recent reviews is worth the extra minute. If you ever download an AI app that promises “uncensored” or “anything goes” image generation, that’s a red flag. You can stay safe by sticking to well-known tools like the official ChatGPT app, Google’s Gemini, or Claude at claude.ai, all of which have visible safety settings and clear reporting options. Source: [wired.com](https://www.wired.com/story/hundreds-of-mundane-ai-apps-are-hiding-abusive-deepfake-features/)

**Parents sue OpenAI after daughter’s death linked to medical advice from ChatGPT**
A 31-year-old woman with epilepsy reportedly followed a tapering schedule suggested by the chatbot, stopped her prescribed medication, and later died from a seizure. Her family is now suing, arguing the AI should have included stronger warnings when giving health-related answers. The case highlights why it’s important never to treat chatbots as doctors.
