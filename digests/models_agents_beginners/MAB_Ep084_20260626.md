### The Big Story
A company called General Intuition just raised money to train AI using video game footage instead of real-world data. The idea is simple: millions of hours of gameplay contain patterns of movement, decision-making, and quick reactions that mirror how humans handle everyday tasks. Think of it like learning to drive by watching expert gamers navigate complex tracks instead of sitting in a real car. The AI watches how players dodge obstacles, team up, or react to surprises, then practices those same moves in digital worlds that feel like practice arenas. This matters because many future AI tools will need to understand physical actions, not just answer questions. If the training works, it could lead to helpers that plan routes for you, suggest better strategies in games you play, or even assist with hands-on projects like building something in shop class. For students interested in game design or robotics careers, this approach opens doors without needing expensive real robots. Right now there is no public demo, but you can explore similar ideas by trying free game-based AI experiments on sites like Hugging Face, where some models show how game data teaches basic navigation skills. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/25/general-intuitions-2-3b-bet-that-video-games-can-train-ai-agents-for-the-real-world/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you play a video game like Fortnite or Minecraft, you quickly learn to spot patterns without thinking, like when an enemy is about to attack or the best spot to build cover? Now picture an AI doing the exact same thing, but instead of playing once, it watches millions of recorded matches from thousands of players. Step by step, it notices tiny details like how fast characters turn, when they jump, or how teams coordinate, storing those moves as useful habits. Next, the AI practices those habits inside safe digital copies of the game worlds, trying millions of variations until the best ones stick. Over time those habits start to feel like real intuition, the kind that helps in new situations the AI has never seen before. The surprising part is that these game-trained habits can transfer to non-game tasks, like guiding a robot or helping plan a route through a crowded hallway. So when you hear about AI learning from games, it is basically borrowing the same quick-thinking skills you already use while playing.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Get an AI to show its thinking with one clever prompt**
Ethan Mollick shared a simple prompt that makes models like GLM-5.2 or Opus 4.8 reveal how they reason step by step instead of jumping straight to an answer. The prompt asks the AI to suggest two poems that fit the current state of AI models, but only after thinking hard and avoiding popular choices it can just copy. This is cool because most chatbots hide their reasoning, yet seeing the process helps you understand why they pick certain ideas. Anyone curious about how AI works should try it. Go to a chatbot that supports these models, paste the exact prompt "I want you to suggest two poems that you think apply very well to the current state of GenAI models like you. Don’t just pick popular poems and back justify. Think hard about options first," and watch the thinking trace appear before the final answer. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/emollick/status/2070384714691584239)

**Notion is replacing its email tool with AI agents**
Notion decided to stop work on a new email app because most users now prefer AI agents that can read, sort, and reply to messages automatically. The agents act like smart assistants that learn your habits and handle routine inbox tasks so you spend less time clicking through emails. This matters for students and anyone juggling school, clubs, and friends because it frees up time for creative work instead of sorting messages. You can already try similar agent-style help inside Notion itself by turning on its AI features for summarizing notes or drafting replies. Head to notion.so, open any page or database, and ask the built-in AI to organize information the way an inbox agent would. Source: [arstechnica.com](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/06/notion-killing-skiff-influenced-email-app-since-most-users-use-ai-agents-instead/)
