### The Big Story
DoorDash just launched a chatbot called Ask DoorDash that lets you search and order using normal words or even photos instead of tapping through restaurant lists. Think of it like talking to a helpful friend who already knows every menu in town and can build your cart for you in seconds. You might say something like “I want spicy noodles and something sweet under ten dollars” or send a picture of a burger you saw online, and the chatbot finds matching options right away. This works because the AI reads your request the same way it reads any other message and matches it to real items in the app. For students or anyone who orders food after a long day, it removes the annoying part of hunting through categories and filters. It also opens the door to more natural conversations with apps we use every day, which could make future services feel less like filling out forms and more like chatting. If this kind of tool spreads, you might soon order almost anything online just by describing what you need. Right now you can open the DoorDash app on your phone, tap the new Ask DoorDash button at the top, and try typing a casual request like “something cold and fruity for under five dollars” to see what it suggests. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/11/doordashs-new-ai-chatbot-lets-you-order-with-prompts-and-photos/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you're texting a friend, your phone sometimes guesses the next word before you finish typing? Now imagine that same autocomplete got much better at understanding the whole sentence you are trying to write, even if you use casual or incomplete words. First it breaks your message into small pieces it has seen millions of times before. Then it checks what usually comes next in similar messages from its training. Finally it looks at the whole request at once to guess what you really want, like turning “spicy noodles cheap” into actual menu items. And that's basically what a chatbot like Ask DoorDash is doing when it turns your casual words or photo into a food order. It is not reading your mind, but it has seen so many examples of people asking for food that it can make a smart guess about what matches. So next time someone says “natural language understanding,” you can tell them — it's basically super-powered autocomplete that learned from billions of everyday messages. Not so scary, right?

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Save your Codex rate limits for later**
OpenAI now lets Plus, Pro, and Business users bank their Codex rate limit resets instead of losing them when they expire. Codex is the coding helper inside ChatGPT that can write or fix code for you. This change means you can save a reset for a busy day instead of feeling rushed to use it right away. If you already have one of those plans, go to your account settings in ChatGPT and look for the new option to store resets. Try asking Codex to explain a simple Python script you found online, then save the reset for next time you need help with homework or a small project.

**Check whether your playlists contain AI-generated songs**
A free tool from Deezer now scans playlists on Spotify, Apple Music, and other services to spot tracks made by AI. It is useful because more and more music online is created by computers, and you might want to know what is human-made versus machine-made. Anyone with a phone can try it without signing up for Deezer. Go to the Deezer website, find the AI detector tool, paste a playlist link from another service, and see what it flags. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2065225362544726371)
