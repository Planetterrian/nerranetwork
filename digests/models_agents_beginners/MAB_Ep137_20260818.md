# Models & Agents for Beginners
> **Two kids, ages 4 and 6, made their first $100 — and asked AI to help run the stand**
A lemonade stand run by two little kids just became a crash course in business — and they used a free AI tool to track sales, costs, and inventory. That is the kind of everyday win this show lives for: not a lab demo, a real stall, real customers, real lessons. We will also look at a dad who had not owned a laptop in years and let an AI set one up, why creative AIs keep giving you the same idea in different hats, and a courtroom reminder that a chatbot is not a secret diary.

### The Big Story
Sky Lou and Poppy, ages 4 and 6, ran a lemonade stand, sold out the drinks, moved a stack of hand-painted flower cards, and made their first $100. The money was nice. The real prize, their dad wrote, was inventing the stand, gathering ideas, and figuring out *how* to actually run it — customer service, where money comes from, and the fact that you can build something of your own instead of only taking a job. They also learned the hard way: one stretch meant three hours of work and only four sales. A helium balloon that cost €6 suddenly looked different when they realized it equaled about four cups of lemonade. That is a money lesson a lot of adults still have not learned.

Here is the AI twist. The family “vibecoded” a tiny shop dashboard with Perplexity (a free AI you talk to in plain English, a bit like a search engine that answers in full sentences). Vibecoding just means you describe what you want in regular words and the AI helps build it. Their tracker watched sales and costs, inventory (how many cups and cards they still had), customer notes, and adding or removing products. Grandpa gave leftover wood, they painted the stand, and some supplies — cups, the jar, decorations — will work again next year.

Why should you care? Because this is what AI looks like when it leaves the homework tab. It is not replacing the kids’ ideas. It is the clipboard, the calculator, and the “did we actually make a profit?” notebook, so their brains can stay on the fun parts: the plan, the signs, the talking to customers. If you have ever wanted a bake sale, a sticker shop, a tutoring side hustle, or a club fundraiser, that is the same shape of problem.

Try it right now. Open a browser, go to perplexity.ai (you may need a parent’s help to sign up), and type: “I want to run a one-day bake sale at school. Give me a supply list, prices that could make a profit, and a simple table I can use to track what I sell, what it cost, and what is left.” Then ask it to make the table even simpler, as if a 10-year-old has to fill it in. You will see the same kind of helper those kids used — no coding, no fancy setup. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/LinusEkenstam/status/2089627117864550831)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how if your whole class gets the same story prompt, you end up with vampires, space hamsters, *and* a sandwich that solves crimes? That spread of totally different answers is called variance — basically, how mixed and surprising the results are. Now picture asking an AI the same prompt five times. A lot of the time you get five stories that feel like cousins: same mood, same plot beats, same “dark past, heart of gold” energy. The model has read so many “good” examples that it aims for the middle of the target, the answer that usually scores well, instead of the weird arrow that lands in the next county.

Ethan Mollick, who writes about how people actually use these tools, argued that AI labs need to care more about that spread on creative work. If it takes a lot of extra poking to get real variety out of a smart model, then the model is less useful for brainstorming, problem-solving, and art — the jobs where the fifth idea is supposed to surprise you. So when your chatbot keeps handing you three “different” slogans that all sound like the same slogan in a new hoodie, that is low variance, not you being picky. Ask it on purpose: “Give me five options that would disagree with each other. Make one of them slightly unhinged.” You are not breaking it. You are asking it to step off the bullseye. ### Cool Stuff & Try This
**The family IT department just got replaced by a phone chatbot**
Ethan Mollick’s father got a new MacBook after years with no laptop and did not know how to set it up. He followed some advice, opened ChatGPT on his phone, asked how to install Codex (an AI helper that can actually do tasks on the computer, not just talk about them), and then told Codex to take over: fix setup problems and install what he needed. Mollick, who had been the family’s unofficial tech support for decades, basically watched that job disappear. If a parent or grandparent in your life still texts you “the Wi-Fi looks angry,” this is the future of that text thread. You do not need Codex for a first taste. On your phone, open ChatGPT and try: “I have never done this before. Walk me through changing my laptop’s screen brightness and setting up a simple folder for school files, one tap at a time, and wait for me to say done before the next step.” Source: [x.com](https://x.com/emollick/status/2089512978575950080)

**A 24/7 tutor that writes you practice problems**
One student who had only used the free websites for ChatGPT and Claude started using an AI helper as a teacher, not a vending machine for answers. It planned a whole project, chopped it into 40 exercises, checked the work, and explained sloppy habits versus solid ones — without just handing over something to copy. They said it felt like learning a new idea in a single day with a patient coach who never gets tired. You can steal the method for any subject, no coding required. Go to chatgpt.com and type: “Turn the causes of World War I into 8 practice questions that get harder each time. Do not give answers until I try. Then tell me what I missed and why.” Swap in Spanish verbs, cell biology, or whatever is on tomorrow’s quiz. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1vr6er8/the_amount_of_things_i_learn_thanks_to_ai_is/)

### Quick Bits
**TikTok’s parent company hits pause for Hollywood**
ByteDance, the company behind TikTok, signed a written agreement with the Motion Picture Association to pull back how its AI models use Hollywood movies, characters, and stories. If you care about the films and shows you love — and about AI that can clone a famous universe in two clicks — this is the entertainment world drawing a line around “please do not eat our homework.” Source: [engadget.com](https://www.engadget.com/2238384/bytedance-agrees-to-reel-in-its-ai-models-to-protect-hollywood-ip/)

**A juror typed the trial into ChatGPT. The trial fell apart.**
Someone on a jury put details from a real trial into ChatGPT, and the whole trial collapsed. Treat that as a flashing neon sign: a chat box is not a private notebook. If something is secret, someone else’s business, or would get you in trouble on a poster in the hallway, do not paste it into an AI.
