YouTube is rolling out an AI system that lets anyone over 18 scan their face once so the platform can automatically watch for fake videos pretending to be them. This matters because deepfakes are showing up more often in everyday places like social media and school projects. ### The Big Story
YouTube is expanding its AI likeness detection program so every user 18 and older can sign up for free. The tool works by having you record a short selfie-style video of your face. YouTube's AI then uses that scan to keep an eye on new uploads and alerts you if it finds videos that look like you but aren't. Think of it like giving the platform a fingerprint of your appearance so it can shout "hey, someone made a fake of you" before the video spreads. This is a direct response to the growing problem of deepfake videos being used to harass or trick people online. For teens and students, it means one more layer of protection when sharing creative work or just existing on the internet. If someone tries to make a fake video of you for a prank or something worse, you'll get notified early instead of finding out later from friends. The feature is already rolling out, so you can go to YouTube's creator tools section on the website or app, look for "likeness detection," and follow the simple steps to record your scan. No coding or special equipment is needed—just your phone camera and a few minutes. Source: [engadget.com](https://www.engadget.com/2174282/youtube-likeness-detection-ai-deepfakes-expansion/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you're doing a big group project at school, you don't make every single person do every single job? Instead you split up: one person handles research, another builds the slides, and someone else presents. Now imagine an AI model works the same way. It has different "experts" inside it—specialized parts that each get really good at one kind of task, like answering science questions or writing stories. When a new question comes in, the model quickly picks only the experts that are actually useful for that question instead of waking up the entire team. The new EMO model takes this idea further by training those experts to focus on whole topics rather than tiny word patterns, so you can turn off most of them and still get almost the same quality answers. That means the AI can run on phones or laptops that don't have tons of memory, because it's not dragging every expert along for every task. So next time someone says "mixture-of-experts," you can picture it as a smart study group that only calls in the right teammates instead of making everyone show up. Not so mysterious after all. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/researchers-train-ai-model-that-hits-near-full-performance-with-just-12-5-percent-of-its-experts/)

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Osaurus puts both local and cloud AI on your Mac in one simple app:** TechCrunch
Osaurus is a free Mac app that lets you run AI models that live on your own computer alongside bigger models that run in the cloud, all without switching between different websites. It keeps your files and conversations private on your device when you want, but can still reach out to stronger cloud models when you need extra power. This is perfect for students who want to experiment with AI for homework or creative projects while staying in control of their data. Download Osaurus from the TechCrunch article link or search for it on the Mac App Store, then try asking it to summarize a PDF you have saved locally first, then switch to a cloud model for a more creative writing task. No terminal commands or setup headaches—just pick a model from the menu and start chatting. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/15/osaurus-brings-both-local-and-cloud-ai-models-to-your-mac/)

**Runway wants to turn video generation into something anyone can use:** TechCrunch
Runway started as a tool for filmmakers and is now pushing hard to make AI video creation as easy as typing a prompt. Their models can generate short video clips from text descriptions, and the company believes this path leads to bigger "world models" that understand how scenes and physics work. If you like making TikToks or YouTube shorts, this could become a fun way to create visuals without filming everything yourself. Head to runwayml.com, sign up for the free tier (you may need a parent's help if you're under 18), and try generating a 5-second clip of "a cat skateboarding through a neon city at night." Experiment with different styles and see how the motion looks. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/15/runway-started-by-helping-filmmakers-now-it-wants-to-beat-google-at-ai/)

### Quick Bits
**AI radio stations run entirely by popular models:** The Verge
A lab called Andon Labs built four radio stations where AI models like Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, and Grok pick the music, write the scripts, and host the shows with zero human help. It's a wild experiment that shows how far agent-style AI has come, but also highlights the weird, sometimes off-topic results you get when no one is steering. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/931479/andon-labs-ai-radio-companies)

**Google now treats AI manipulation as spam:** The Verge
Google updated its search rules so that anyone trying to trick its AI Overviews or AI Mode results by stuffing content with hidden keywords or fake signals will get treated like traditional spam. It means cleaner, more honest answers when you search, especially as more people rely on the AI summaries at the top of results. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/tech/931416/google-ai-search-spam-policy)
