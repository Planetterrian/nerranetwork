# Models & Agents for Beginners
> **ChatGPT just learned to talk and control your whole computer with your voice.**
OpenAI rolled out voice control that lets you speak to multiple AI agents at once on your desktop. This turns everyday devices into hands-free helpers for school projects or creative work. We'll also look at how open-source AI actually works, plus new tools for making videos and learning the basics yourself.
---
### The Big Story
OpenAI added ChatGPT Voice to its desktop app on both Mac and Windows. The feature uses a new system called GPT-Live that can listen, speak, and manage several AI agents running at the same time inside apps like ChatGPT Work or Codex. 

Think of it like having a helpful friend who sits next to you, watches what you're doing on screen, and jumps in when you ask out loud. Instead of typing every instruction, you can just say what you want and the voice system handles the back-and-forth with the other agents. 

This matters because it removes the keyboard barrier that still slows a lot of people down. A student could describe a history report idea while the AI pulls sources, organizes notes, and even suggests images without the user ever clicking away from their work. For anyone who finds typing tiring or prefers talking through ideas, the change makes AI feel more like a conversation than a search box. 

You can try it right now if you have a Plus, Pro, Business, Edu, or Enterprise plan: open the ChatGPT desktop app on your computer, look for the voice option in the interface, and start speaking. The rollout is happening globally today, so the feature should appear for eligible users without any extra steps. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2080378182469857576)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you text a friend, your phone sometimes guesses the next word before you finish typing? 

Now picture that same guessing game happening with sound instead of letters. The AI listens to your voice, breaks the sounds into tiny pieces it has heard before, and predicts what word or phrase is most likely coming next. At the same time it checks the words against everything else happening on your screen so the answer actually fits the task you're working on. 

When you speak again, it repeats the loop in reverse: it turns its planned reply into sounds that feel natural, almost like a real person talking back. The whole process runs continuously, so the AI can keep track of several different tasks at once without you having to repeat yourself. 

That back-and-forth loop is basically what powers the new voice feature in the desktop app.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Make short videos that already have sound built in**
Flux 3 from Black Forest Labs can now create videos up to 20 seconds long that include matching audio from the start. Earlier tools usually made silent video and then added sound later, which often felt off. Teens who like making TikTok-style clips or short stories can use this to bring characters to life with voices and effects in one step. The model is still new, so independent tests are limited, but the company says it performs slightly ahead of current leaders. You can check the latest examples on The Decoder site or search for Flux 3 demos on YouTube to see what it can do right now. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/flux-3-generates-videos-with-native-audio-up-to-20-seconds-long-a-first-for-black-forest-labs/)

**Learn how an entire language model is built, step by step**
A Reddit user who trained a small GPT-style model on their own PC is turning the whole process into a free website with code, math explanations, and visuals. The goal is to show exactly how each piece connects instead of leaving beginners to piece together scattered tutorials. No coding experience is required to follow along at first; you can just read and watch the diagrams. If you're curious about what actually happens inside ChatGPT, this is one of the clearest beginner paths available. Send a private message to the poster on r/ChatGPT if you want to join the first group testing the site. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1v56l56/i_built_and_trained_a_small_gptstyle_llm_from/)

**Try Google's expanding Gemini Spark assistant**
Gemini Spark is now open to more people and acts as an agentic helper that can take on multi-step tasks. Beginners can use it for planning school projects, brainstorming stories, or organizing notes without needing any special setup. The expansion means you may already have access through your Google account on phone or computer. Go to gemini.google.com, start a new chat, and ask it to help with something like "plan a weekend study schedule that includes breaks and fun activities" to see how it breaks the request into steps. Source: [engadget.com](https://www.engadget.com/2222303/google-expanding-access-gemini-spark/)
