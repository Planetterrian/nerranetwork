Google is folding its Gemini AI into the Waze navigation app, giving drivers new ways to customize trips with simple voice commands. ### The Big Story
Waze, the navigation app owned by Google, is adding new features powered by Gemini, Google's AI assistant. The updates let drivers use voice commands to personalize routes, report road conditions more naturally, and switch into modes like "Motorcycle" or "Less Chatty" that change how the app talks to you.

Think of it like having a friend in the passenger seat who already knows your usual shortcuts and can adjust the route on the fly when you say something casual like "avoid highways today." The AI listens to your words, understands the request in context, and then changes the map and spoken directions without you tapping anything.

This matters because navigation is one of the most common ways people interact with technology while moving through their day. For students heading to school or practice, or anyone running errands, a smarter app could mean fewer frustrating reroutes and more control over how much the voice interrupts music or podcasts.

It also shows how AI is moving from chat windows into the background of apps you already have on your phone. Instead of opening a separate AI tool, the help shows up right where you need it.

You can try it yourself right now by opening the Waze app on your phone, starting a route, and saying something like "make this trip less chatty" or asking about specific road conditions. The new voice features should respond directly in the app. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/transportation/964132/waze-gemini-ai-voice-commands-less-chatty)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how your smartwatch or phone tracks steps, heart rate, and sleep without you thinking about it, but all that raw data just sits there as numbers?

Now picture someone collecting those numbers from millions of devices over years, turning every tiny reading into a long string of information the computer can study.

The AI learns patterns across all that data the same way you might notice that your own heart rate jumps after certain meals or that you sleep better on weekends, except it spots patterns across millions of people at once.

When a new reading comes in, the model compares it against everything it has seen before and guesses what it means for health or behavior, like spotting when someone might be getting sick or changing activity levels.

That is basically what a foundation model for sensor data does: it turns messy streams of numbers from wearables into useful insights without needing a human to label every single reading first.

The payoff is that future apps could give you personalized health tips based on your own patterns instead of generic advice. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/sensorfm/)

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Instagram is stepping back from an AI tool after users pushed back**

Meta added a feature that let people turn public photos into AI-generated images, but quickly removed it after complaints about how it used other people's content. The company said the goal was creative control, yet listeners made it clear they did not want their photos feeding the system without stronger choices.

This is worth noticing because it shows users can influence how AI appears in the apps they use every day. If you post on Instagram, your content is no longer being pulled into that particular AI tool.

You can check your own settings by opening Instagram, going to your profile, tapping the menu, and looking under Settings and privacy for any AI or content references options. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/10/meta-removes-controversial-ai-feature-on-instagram-after-backlash/)

**A company wants to put small AI computers in people's homes**

Sunrun, known for home solar panels, is testing a program that would place compact AI computing units in customers' houses and pay them for the electricity and space used. It is still early, but the idea is to spread out the computing power instead of building giant data centers.

This connects to everyday life because the same homes already running on solar could one day help power the AI tools people use for homework or creative projects.

If the pilot expands, you might see offers through solar installers in your area, though right now it is mainly a wait-and-see development. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/963930/sunrun-distributed-ai-data-center)

### Quick Bits
**LinkedIn leads in AI-written posts**

A study found that about 41 percent of longer posts on LinkedIn appear to be fully generated by AI, far higher than other platforms. It is a reminder that when you scroll social media, some of what you read may not come from a real person. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/linkedin-is-the-undisputed-king-of-long-form-ai-slop-according-to-a-study-spanning-five-platforms/)

**Apple's old car project still helps today's devices**

Even though Apple never released a self-driving car, the work on powerful chips for that project helped create the fast on-device AI processing now found in iPhones and Macs. The lessons from trying to run complex AI without constant cloud connections carried over to the chips you use every day. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/tech/964519/apple-silicon-self-driving-car-ai-m7-ultra)
