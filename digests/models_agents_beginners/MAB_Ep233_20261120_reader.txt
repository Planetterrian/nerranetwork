Google, The Verge reports, added a feature called computer use to its Gemini app on Mac computers. Gemini is Google's artificial intelligence, or AI, assistant. Computer use lets it steer the cursor, launch programs, change documents, and surf the web for you.

Picture a helpful friend at your keyboard who follows instructions one step at a time. You stay in control, and you can halt it or give a yes before a bigger step.

It runs in the background, so your own screen and pointer remain yours. It has its own background window with a live preview in your chat, and you choose which apps it may touch.

You no longer have to copy information from one app to another by hand. You can ask it to sort files into folders, fill out forms, or repeat the same chore across a batch of files. It can build a slideshow from material in different places, or list items for sale.

For school, that can mean turning notes into a presentation without hopping between tabs.

The feature is rolling out now in English in the United States. You need the Gemini app and a paid Google AI Plus, Pro, or Ultra plan.

OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta already let their AIs work inside desktop apps, so Google is arriving a little late.

Download the Gemini app for Mac from Google, open a chat, and ask it to organize my desktop files by date. Watch the preview window, and you can stop it anytime.

Training on millions of computer sessions is what lets the AI pick each click. In a video game, your character walks around and taps objects while you just press buttons.

You start by telling it the job you want done. Then it studies what is on the screen, the way your eyes would.

After that it matches that view to habits it learned, like opening a folder and dragging files into it. Last, it chooses the next click or the next keys to type.

Your button presses move a game character, and here your instruction plus those past examples move the cursor. Huge pattern matching, pointed at ordinary screen chores, is really all that is going on.

Your hands can stay busy, because ChatGPT, the chatbot from OpenAI, now types while you talk. OpenAI added Live Dictation, which turns your spoken words into text as you go, instead of waiting until you finish recording.

That helps when you think faster than you type, or when you like voice notes. It is rolling out now globally on every ChatGPT plan, on the web, the desktop app, and the iPhone and Android apps.

Go to the ChatGPT website or open the app, tap the microphone icon in a new chat, and start speaking. Watch the words appear as you talk, then ask it to continue or edit what it wrote.

Students and artists who want a clean workspace got a new switch inside Canva, the graphic design platform. One toggle disables every generative AI tool at once, meaning the ones that create new images or extend photos.

That includes Magic Expand, which stretches photos using AI, and the chatbot that makes images. It does not shut down every AI-powered feature.

Open Canva in your browser, go to your account settings, and look for turn off AI features. Flip it, and those tools disappear until you turn them back on.

Simple text prompts make Grand Theft Auto six scenes, or GTA six scenes, in Gemini and ChatGPT, the Times of India says, for fun experiments. Those include five ready-to-copy prompts that work right now for Instagram posts.

Open the Gemini website or the ChatGPT website and type a prompt for a cinematic game-style scene. Tweak the words to add your own characters or locations.
