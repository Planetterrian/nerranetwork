The design platform Canva just released its first in-house image model, called Canva Craft, ProPakistani reports.

In-house just means Canva built this one itself, instead of borrowing someone else’s picture maker.

Millions of students and creators already use Canva for posters and social graphics, so this lands right where people already work.

Most image A I's jump straight to colored pixels, like dumping paint on a canvas and hoping the poster looks organized.

Craft starts with a layout pass that decides where objects sit, how big they should be, and where text belongs.

That two-step approach keeps each piece separate, instead of baking everything into one flat picture.

Think of stickers on a locker versus one painted mural. You can peel one sticker without wrecking the rest.

If you have ever tried to move one object in an A I picture and watched the whole thing fall apart, that change feels huge.

The model supports resolutions up to two K, which is a sharp, high-detail picture, and more than thirty different aspect ratios.

Aspect ratio is just the shape of the frame, like square versus wide.

So the same description can become a square Instagram post or a wide desktop wallpaper without extra work.

Canva trained Craft on thousands of human-created designs from its own template library, instead of relying mainly on photographs.

That library includes posters, social media graphics, presentations, infographics, menus, and layouts packed with lettering.

The model is learning how text, pictures, and other pieces should be arranged, the way a yearbook staff learns from old yearbooks.

Because those templates already follow real design rules, the output tends to place readable text correctly.

It also tends to keep objects from overlapping in awkward ways.

If you make YouTube thumbnails, TikTok graphics, or school presentations, you can generate a picture and then tweak just the text or one character without starting over.

Craft also connects to Canva Magic, the company’s existing image-to-design tool, so a generated picture can become editable layers inside the same project.

Layers are like separate sheets of clear plastic stacked together. You can move the title sheet without smearing the background.

Canva is already building two faster versions called Craftfast and Craft-ultrafast, aimed at lowering generation costs and speeding up results.

Craft runs inside Canva’s free Dream Lab section, so you do not need a separate app.

Canva plans to let other A I assistants such as Chat G P T and Google’s Gemini call Craft through something called Model Context Protocol.

Model Context Protocol, or M C P, is a shared handshake that lets different A I tools pass jobs to each other.

That would let you ask those chatbots for a Canva-style graphic and then open it directly in Canva for more editing.

Craft works in two clear steps that match that habit. First it reads your words and builds an invisible blueprint.

That blueprint lists every object, its size, its position, and even where the wording should sit, like a seating chart for a group project.

Only after that plan is finished does the second step turn the blueprint into actual colored pixels.

Because the plan exists on its own, the system can catch problems early, such as text covering an important object, instead of guessing the whole poster at once.

Picture freehand drawing versus putting a ruler and light guidelines down first. The guidelines do not show in the final art, but they keep the letters straight.

Training on real Canva templates helps that blueprint follow the same spacing and hierarchy rules human designers already use.

Hierarchy here just means what should shout and what should whisper, like a huge title and a smaller date.

Each planned piece stays on its own layer afterward, so you can drag one element later without the rest of the picture shifting or breaking.
