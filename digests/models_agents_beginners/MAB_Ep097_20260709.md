### The Big Story
OpenAI just finished rolling out GPT-Live to everyone who pays for ChatGPT Go, Plus, or Pro. Free users will get it soon too. 

Think of GPT-Live like turning a text message conversation into a live video call where the other person can see what you're showing them and reply instantly with their voice. Instead of waiting for typed answers, the AI can now watch your camera, listen to you speak, and respond right away while the conversation keeps flowing naturally.

This matters because most of us already use ChatGPT for homework help, brainstorming story ideas, or figuring out game strategies. Adding live voice and video makes those moments feel less like typing into a search box and more like having a patient tutor or creative partner sitting next to you. It could change how students practice presentations or how friends collaborate on creative projects without needing to be in the same room.

For you personally, this means the AI you already have on your phone can now help in new ways—like walking you through fixing a bike while you point the camera at it, or practicing a language conversation out loud. The update is already live, so open the ChatGPT app on your phone, make sure it's the newest version, and tap the new live button to start a voice or video chat. No extra sign-up is needed if you already have a paid plan. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2075019750569378007)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you're on a group video call with friends, everyone can talk at the same time and the app keeps the conversation moving without long pauses? 

Now picture the AI doing the same thing, but instead of just passing audio back and forth, it has to understand what you're saying in the moment, decide what to say next, and speak it out loud while also watching anything you show on camera. It does this by breaking your words into tiny pieces it can process quickly, then choosing the best reply based on everything it has seen and heard so far. The system keeps a short memory of the last few seconds so the flow stays natural instead of restarting every time you speak. 

That back-and-forth speed is what makes the experience feel alive rather than like sending messages and waiting. Once you try it, the difference between typing and speaking becomes obvious right away.

**Grok 4.5 is here and ready to chat**
xAI just released Grok 4.5, a new version that Elon Musk says aims to match the power of top models while staying lighter and cheaper to run. It can handle creative writing, answer questions, and help with everyday tasks the same way other chatbots do. Anyone with an X account can try it right now for free on the X app or at grok.x.ai. **Google's tool caught a fake photo of a senator**
Earlier this week a scary-looking photo of Senator Mitch McConnell spread online, but it turned out to be completely made up by AI. Google's deepfake detection system helped prove it was fake by spotting the telltale signs that human eyes miss. This kind of tool matters because fake images can spread fast on social media and cause real confusion. While the full detector isn't open to everyone yet, you can still practice spotting fakes yourself by looking closely at lighting, shadows, and background details in photos you see online. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/08/googles-deepfake-detector-system-used-to-debunk-mcconnell-hoax-pic/)

### Quick Bits
**Mistral's new robot brain uses just one camera**
The company Mistral released a small 8-billion-parameter model called Robostral Navigate that can guide robots through new spaces using only a single camera feed. It was trained mostly in video game simulations before being fine-tuned with reinforcement learning. This approach could make it easier for future robots to explore places they've never seen before. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/mistral-enters-robotics-with-robostral-navigate-an-8b-model-that-steers-robots-using-just-one-camera/)

**Amazon is building a smarter Alexa**
Amazon is working on a new version of Alexa, nicknamed Moonraker, that can handle longer, multi-step tasks instead of just answering one question at a time. The goal is for it to plan and carry out things like booking a trip or managing a shopping list without you giving every single instruction. It is still in development, so it is not available yet. Source: [engadget.com](https://www.engadget.com/2210614/amazon-is-reportedly-working-on-a-more-powerful-agentic-alexa-assistant/)
