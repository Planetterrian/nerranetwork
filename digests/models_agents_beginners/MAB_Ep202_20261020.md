> **AI agents are quietly stripping ads and sluggish menus from smart TVs, turning them fast and simple again.**
Today’s biggest beginner-friendly story is about people using free AI tools to fix their own smart TVs by removing bloatware and ads. We’ll explain exactly how that works and what you can try yourself. Then we’ll look at a new ChatGPT feature perfect for school projects and a fresh video tool that lets you create short clips with sound.

### The Big Story
Imagine your smart TV running slow because it keeps showing ads, tracking what you watch, and forcing you to use apps you never wanted. A growing number of people are fighting back by letting simple AI agents take control and clean everything up. These agents connect to the TV over the same Wi-Fi network your phone uses, then explore the TV’s menus the way a curious friend might poke around a new app. Once inside, the agent can block ads, speed up the interface, and even add features like pulling videos straight from the internet without extra apps. The result feels like turning a complicated gadget back into a plain screen that just plays what you want. This matters because almost every teen has dealt with a laggy TV during movie nights or family streaming, and it shows how AI can give regular people more control over the devices they already own instead of waiting for companies to fix them. If this trend grows, future TVs might come with fewer forced features because users now have an easy way to remove them. You can try this yourself right now by putting your computer and TV on the same Wi-Fi, then asking an AI like Claude to help reverse-engineer the TV’s controls with one short prompt. Many people report it takes about five minutes and works on popular brands like Samsung. The agents work by sending requests that mimic normal remote control presses, reading the on-screen responses, and repeating the process until every unwanted menu item is disabled. Owners say the TV feels noticeably faster once the tracking and ad services are turned off. The same method also lets users install apps from outside the official store, something the TV makers usually block. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/19/people-are-using-ai-to-make-their-smart-tvs-dumb-again/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re at home and you want to change the Wi-Fi password on your router, you just open a browser on any phone or laptop that’s already connected to that network? The device doesn’t need special cables because everything on the same Wi-Fi can talk to each other. Now picture an AI agent doing the exact same thing, except instead of changing a password it quietly explores every menu and setting on your smart TV. It starts by sending simple requests like “show me the settings page,” then follows the on-screen buttons the way you would with a remote. Because the agent and the TV share the network, the AI can see responses instantly and keep going until it finds the hidden options that control ads or speed. The whole process feels like having a tiny, patient helper sitting next to the TV who never gets bored clicking through menus. Once the agent learns the layout, it can remember the steps and repeat them whenever you want the TV cleaned up again. That’s basically how these reverse-engineering agents work: they use the everyday connection you already have in your house to reach devices that normally only talk to their own apps. The agent keeps a running list of every screen it visits so it can return to any point without starting over. It also notices when a menu changes after a button press and adjusts its next move accordingly. This back-and-forth continues until the agent has mapped the entire system and can carry out the exact sequence of clicks needed to remove bloatware. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/LinusEkenstam/status/2112459982331687223)

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**ChatGPT now builds and edits slides with friends in real time**
OpenAI added live collaboration to ChatGPT Slides so multiple people can work on the same presentation at once. You type or speak your ideas, ChatGPT turns them into slides, and your classmates or teammates can jump in, edit text, move pictures, or leave comments while everyone watches the changes happen. This is perfect for group school projects where one person used to do all the work because sharing files was annoying. Users can also click directly on any slide to change text, update charts, or resize images without leaving the chat. ChatGPT or its helper agent can turn feedback comments into actual edits automatically. To try it, open ChatGPT, type “create ChatGPT slides” followed by your topic, then share the link with friends. Everyone needs a Pro, Business, or Enterprise plan, but the free version still lets you build single slides to test the idea. The finished deck can be presented inside ChatGPT or exported straight to PowerPoint or Google Slides. Source: [digit.in](https://www.digit.in/news/general/openai-adds-real-time-collaboration-to-chatgpt-slides-introduces-other-new-features.html)

**Kling AI version 4.0 adds sound and longer clips**
The video tool Kling just released version 4.0 that can now create 30-second clips with stereo sound and lip-sync in multiple languages. You give it a prompt or a few keyframe images, and it generates smooth video that matches the audio. This opens up quick experiments for short stories, music videos, or meme clips without needing expensive software. The new version also accepts up to ten keyframe images and understands prompts up to 8,000 tokens long. Go to the Kling website, sign up for the free tier, and try typing a simple prompt like “a cat dancing on a windowsill with upbeat music.” The new Flash version runs lighter if you want faster results on a regular laptop. Comparisons with other tools show it keeps pace but does not pull far ahead, suggesting the rapid gains in AI video quality may be slowing. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/kling-ai-drops-version-4-0-with-stereo-audio-multilingual-lip-sync-and-a-lighter-flash-variant/)
