> **Imagine turning a simple chat into a real desktop app you can actually install and use.**
Anthropic just made it possible to turn AI-created projects into standalone apps that run on your Mac or Windows computer—no coding required. This opens up a whole new way for anyone to build and share their own tools, from games to study helpers. We'll also look at how you can already start vibe-coding entire projects inside Slack, shop Black Friday deals with an AI agent, and give parents more control over what teens see online. Plus, a quick tip on keeping your chats private.

### The Big Story
Anthropic has turned its Claude Artifacts feature into real desktop apps you can install on Mac and Windows computers. Artifacts are the interactive projects that Claude builds while you chat with it, such as simple games, calculators, or study tools. Now those projects can become standalone programs that sit on your desktop, complete with menu bars, notifications, and even transparent windows. You build one in chat without writing code, and it can add menu bar items, send notifications, and create transparent windows. Think of it like turning a doodle you made in a notebook into a framed picture you can hang on the wall and look at anytime, without needing the notebook open. This matters because it lowers the barrier between “I had an idea in a chat” and “here’s something I can actually use every day.” A student could chat with Claude to build a flashcard app for history class, then install it as a real program that pops up reminders during study time. Someone into creative hobbies could make a quick drawing tool or music mixer and keep it on their computer like any other app. The best part is that a free account is enough to get started—no paid plan required. To try it, open Claude in your browser, ask it to build something simple like “a timer that tracks study sessions,” then look for the option to turn that Artifact into a desktop app. Once installed, you can use it even when you’re not chatting with Claude. The feature works on both macOS and Windows, making it accessible no matter which computer you have at home or school. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-turns-claude-artifacts-into-standalone-desktop-apps-for-macos-and-windows/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re texting a friend and you say “let’s make a plan for the weekend,” your phone just shows the words you typed? Now picture the AI remembering every single thing you said in the whole conversation and using that memory to build something new. It doesn’t just reply—it keeps a running picture of what you want, like a sketch that gets more detailed every time you add a detail. When you ask for changes such as “make the timer bigger” or “add a sound,” the AI updates that sketch in its mind and shows you the new version right away. Over many back-and-forth messages, the sketch turns into a working little program that can do real things like count time or show buttons you can click. The surprising part is that the AI isn’t following a pre-written recipe; it’s figuring out the steps on the fly from everything you’ve told it so far. That’s why the final result can feel like something you and the AI built together instead of something copied from a template. The same memory process lets the program keep working after you close the chat, because the AI has already turned your instructions into a complete set of rules the app follows on its own.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Vibe-code an entire tool without leaving Slack**
Viktor is an AI that lives inside Slack and can build real software projects just from your messages. You describe what you want—like turning a messy list into a working tracker—and it handles the rest, including fixing problems on its own. One user turned a simple Google Sheet into a full tool that now operates PRs, merges changes, checks in on the work, and corrects any issues without further help. It’s free to start, so anyone curious about making things can experiment right away. Open Slack, add the Viktor app, and try typing something like “turn my copied Google Sheet into a simple tool that tracks homework due dates.” Watch what it creates and how it updates itself when you give feedback. The whole process happens inside your regular Slack workspace, so you never need to switch to another website or download extra software. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/LinusEkenstam/status/2125523487834165368)

**Shop Black Friday deals with an AI helper**
Walmart teamed up with Meta so you can ask the Muse AI agent to find deals without switching between apps. Muse can look at products, compare prices, and suggest what fits your budget. If you already use Instagram or Facebook, you can reach Muse directly in those apps. Go to the Meta AI section in Instagram, type “show me Black Friday deals on headphones under $50,” and see what it suggests. The agent stays inside the same chat window you use for messaging friends, so shopping feels like a normal conversation rather than a separate task. This setup works especially well on phones, where jumping between store apps can feel slow during busy sale days. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/walmart-teams-up-with-meta-to-bring-black-friday-deals-to-ai-agent-muse/)

**Bring your AI helper straight into Instagram DMs**
Meta now lets you chat with its Muse AI agent inside Instagram direct messages, so you don’t have to open a separate app. This makes it easy to ask quick questions or get help while you’re already scrolling or messaging friends. Open Instagram, start a DM with yourself or a friend, and try asking Muse something simple like “help me plan a study schedule for next week.” It can keep the conversation going right there in your messages. The integration removes the need to toggle between Instagram and the Muse app when you want to interact with the AI agent. Because the chat stays inside your existing DMs, you can switch back to talking with friends without losing your place. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/meta-brings-its-muse-ai-assistant-directly-into-instagram-dms/)

### Quick Bits
**Parents can now limit AI-generated images teens see on Pinterest**
Pinterest added a setting so parents can reduce how much AI-made content appears in their teen’s feed. It’s a direct response to worries about fake or unrealistic images showing up in searches and recommendations. If you use Pinterest, check the family settings to see the new controls that let parents decide how much generated content reaches younger accounts. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/pinterest-will-now-let-parents-limit-the-ai-generated-content-their-teens-see/)
