# Models & Agents for Beginners
> **ChatGPT now handles one in five searches that used to go straight to Google.**
People are choosing AI chatbots over traditional search engines at a record pace. This shift shows how natural it feels to ask questions in conversation instead of typing keywords. Today's episode also looks at how the same kind of AI is starting to help engineers design real physical objects like cars and circuit boards, plus a simple way to understand what these models are actually doing under the hood.

### The Big Story
More and more people are typing their questions into ChatGPT instead of Google. Recent reports show ChatGPT search volume has reached about 20 percent of Google's total. That means roughly one out of every five searches that might once have gone to the classic search engine is now happening inside an AI chat.

Think of it like the difference between flipping through a big index at the back of a textbook versus raising your hand and asking a helpful friend who already knows the material. The friend can understand the full question, remember what you asked before, and give a direct answer instead of a list of links.

This matters because searching used to feel like a separate skill you had to learn. Now it feels more like talking. For students doing homework, creators looking for ideas, or anyone curious about something at 10 p.m., the experience is faster and more natural.

It also changes what companies have to compete on. Google used to win by showing the best links. ChatGPT wins by giving the best single answer. If this trend continues, the way we find information could keep moving toward conversation instead of lists.

You can try it right now on your phone or laptop. Open chatgpt.com, type any question you would normally Google, and notice how the answer feels different from a list of blue links. Compare the two side by side for the same question and see which one helps you faster. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how your phone keyboard sometimes guesses the next word while you're texting a friend? It looks at the last few words you typed and picks what usually comes next based on patterns from millions of other texts.

Now picture that same system but much larger. Instead of just guessing one word, it guesses the next chunk of text, then the next, over and over, until it has built a full answer. The model has seen so many examples during training that it has learned which ideas tend to follow which other ideas.

When you ask it a question, it treats your words as the start of a sentence and keeps extending that sentence in the most likely direction. It does not look up facts the way a search engine does. It simply continues the pattern it thinks fits best.

That is why the answers can feel surprisingly natural and why the same model can switch between writing a story, explaining a math problem, or helping with a recipe. The core trick stays the same: keep predicting what comes next.

The process begins with your exact words as the starting point. The model then calculates probabilities for thousands of possible next tokens based on everything it learned from books, websites, and conversations. Each new token is chosen and added, creating a chain that grows into paragraphs. Because the training data was so huge, the probabilities often line up with what a helpful human would say next. This single mechanism of repeated next-token prediction is what lets one model handle homework help, creative writing, or quick troubleshooting without needing separate programs for each task.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**OpenAI's AI is now helping design real cars and circuit boards**
OpenAI shared updates on how ChatGPT and Codex are being used to create mechanical parts, electrical layouts, and even full vehicle designs. Engineers at places like Mazda and a racing team are letting the AI turn 3D scans into editable models and run simulations. The AI suggests shapes and connections, then humans check the results for safety and real-world fit.

This is exciting because it shows AI moving beyond text and images into the physical world. One day the same tools could help students design simple robots or custom parts for school projects.

You can explore the same kind of workflow yourself without any special software. Go to chatgpt.com, describe a simple object you wish existed (like a phone stand that clips to a laptop), and ask it to describe the parts and how they would connect. Then ask it to suggest changes based on materials you have at home. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/OpenAI/status/2117692475775258785)

### Quick Bits
**A free userscript turns ChatGPT chats into a walkable pixel-art world**
Someone built a browser script that lets ChatGPT generate an entire explorable game world with characters, stories, and furniture you can rearrange. The project, called Elsewhere, runs right on chatgpt.com with no separate API key needed. It supports two modes: Adventure for solving stories through talking to characters and using objects, and Hangout for creating a personal space where you chat with ChatGPT as an in-world character. You can redesign appearances, save worlds, and export files to continue later. The script is available on Greasy Fork and requires a userscript manager like Violentmonkey. World creation can take around 30 minutes and uses part of your normal ChatGPT usage allowance, but once finished you can walk around freely and interact without regenerating everything. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1xnjob6/nobody_said_i_couldnt_so_i_built_a_game_right_on/)

**GPT-6 helped fix a broken phone over USB without a factory reset**
One user connected their phone to a computer and let GPT-6 Astra read the logs and carrier settings. The phone was an OPPO A5 2020 on the One NZ network where calls went straight to voicemail. The AI spotted a single hidden setting that was blocking calls and applied a temporary fix. The phone started working again, though the change resets on restart. It shows how AI can already dig into technical problems when given the right access. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1xnjd0z/gpt6_astra_ultra_fixed_my_phone_over_usb_one/)
