# Models & Agents for Beginners
> **Google just gave its AI the power to click, type, and organize files on your Mac while you watch.**
Today’s biggest update lets Google’s Gemini AI actually work inside your Mac apps and files to finish multi-step tasks for you. This matters because it turns AI from a chat window into something that can handle real work like filling forms or sorting folders. We’ll also look at a new way to talk to ChatGPT without typing, a simple toggle that lets you turn AI off when you want, and a fun way to make game-style photos with free tools.
---
### The Big Story
Google just added a feature called “computer use” to its Gemini app on macOS. This means the AI can now move your mouse, open apps, edit files, and browse the web on your behalf while you watch a live preview in the chat window. Think of it like having a helpful friend sitting at your computer who can follow your instructions step by step, but you stay in control and can pause or approve anything before it happens. The AI works in the background so your screen and cursor stay free for other tasks. This changes everyday workflows because instead of copying information between apps yourself, you can ask Gemini to organize files into folders, fill out forms, or build a slideshow from data in different places. For students, it could mean faster homework help like turning notes into presentations without switching tabs. For anyone who finds repetitive computer tasks annoying, it removes the boring parts. The feature is rolling out now in English for users in the US who have a Gemini app and a paid Google AI subscription. Google suggests the feature might be useful for things like speeding up workflows, filling in forms, making presentations, and listing items for sale. Users will be able to decide which of your apps Gemini can access, and be able to follow along as Gemini works because it has its own background window that shows a live preview in your chat. The company is a little late to the game with this launch, as other leading AI model makers like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta have already been offering agentic features in their desktop applications for some time. To try it, download the Gemini app for Mac from Google, open a chat, and give it a task like “organize my desktop files by date” while watching the preview window. You can stop it anytime. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1037902/google-gemini-macos-ai-computer-use)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re playing a video game, your character can walk around and interact with objects on screen while you just press buttons? Now picture an AI that does the same thing but on your actual computer instead of inside a game. It sees what’s on your screen the way you do, decides what to click or type next, and shows you a small window of its actions so you can follow along. The AI doesn’t take over completely — it waits for your okay on bigger steps and stops if you tell it to. This works because the model was trained on millions of examples of people using computers, learning patterns like “open this folder then drag files there.” The result feels like the AI has its own little robot hands inside your Mac. What used to feel like magic is really just pattern matching at a huge scale applied to everyday screen actions. After the user instructs the AI what they want to do, they’ll see the agent working in a live preview in their chat, while still keeping their screen and cursor free to continue working on other things. As with other AI agents offering computer use, users can pause, stop, and approve Gemini’s actions before they happen. Google suggests the feature could be useful for tedious tasks and other workflows, like filling out forms, repeating the same task over and over across a batch of files, organizing files into folders based on specific criteria, creating slideshows from files or information in online dashboards, and more. The feature is initially available in English in the U.S. and requires the Gemini app for Mac and a Google AI Plus, Pro, or Ultra subscription.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**ChatGPT now types while you talk**
OpenAI added Live Dictation so ChatGPT can turn your spoken words into text in real time instead of waiting for you to finish recording. This is useful when your hands are busy or you think faster than you type. Anyone who likes voice notes or needs quick help with writing can try it. It works on the web, desktop app, and phone apps for all ChatGPT plans. ChatGPT can receive audio prompts from your microphone faster now with a Live Dictation feature that transcribes as you speak, rather than waiting until you finish recording. It’s rolling out now globally on all ChatGPT plans on the web, desktop app, and iOS and Android apps. While using dictation in ChatGPT, you can tap or click the microphone and watch the words appear instantly as you speak. Go to chatgpt.com on your browser or open the app, tap the microphone icon in a new chat, and start speaking — watch the words appear as you talk and then ask it to continue or edit what it wrote. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1037634/openai-chatgpt-live-dictation)

**Canva now lets you turn AI completely off**
Canva added a simple toggle that disables every generative AI tool at once, including Magic Expand and the image-making chatbot. This gives people who prefer traditional design tools full control without AI suggestions popping up. It’s great for students or artists who want a clean workspace. With this change, you can disable the graphic design platform's array of AI tools, such as its AI photo-extending Magic Expand feature, as well as turn off the ability to generate images with its Canva chatbot. This toggle doesn't turn off every AI-powered feature, but it removes the generative ones that create new images or extend photos. Open Canva in your browser, go to your account settings, and look for the new “turn off AI features” option — flip it and the AI tools disappear until you turn them back on.
