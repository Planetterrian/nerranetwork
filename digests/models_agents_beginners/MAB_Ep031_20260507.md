### The Big Story
Perplexity just made its Personal Computer AI assistant available to every Mac user, no paid subscription required. Before today it was locked behind a Max plan, but now anyone with a Mac can try it. Think of it like having a super-organized friend who can sit at your computer, open apps, search the web, and handle tasks while you watch. The agent works inside your actual Mac environment, so it can pull information from your files or browser and turn that into answers or summaries. This matters because most AI tools stay stuck in their own chat windows, but this one steps into the same space where you already do homework, edit videos, or scroll social media. For students it could mean asking it to gather sources for a project without switching tabs constantly. For creators it might help organize notes or pull quick facts while you're in the middle of making something. The biggest shift is that AI is moving from "talk to me in this box" to "let me work alongside you on your real computer." If you have a Mac, head to the Perplexity website or app, look for the Personal Computer feature, and try asking it to research a topic you're curious about right now. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/07/perplexitys-personal-computer-is-now-available-everyone-on-mac/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you're planning a group project at school, you don't just pick one tool and hope it works? You grab your laptop for research, your phone for quick messages, and maybe a notebook for ideas, switching between them as needed. Now imagine an AI that does the exact same thing: when you ask it to handle something on your Mac, it looks at the task and decides which "tool" fits best. One step might need a web search, so it opens the browser. Another step might need to read a file on your desktop, so it uses a different tool for that. It keeps going, picking the right helper for each small piece until the whole job is finished. That's basically what these new AI agents are doing under the hood — they're not magic, they're just really good at breaking big requests into smaller steps and choosing the right digital tool for each one. So next time someone says "AI agent," you can picture it like a student juggling apps and tabs to finish a project without getting stuck on any single part.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Spotify now lets AI agents build personal podcasts for you:** Engadget
Instead of listening to the same shows everyone else hears, you can now tell an AI agent exactly what you want — like a 10-minute episode about your favorite game or a story mixing two topics you love — and it creates the whole thing. This is exciting because it turns passive listening into something you shape yourself, almost like directing your own mini radio show. Teens who love niche hobbies, music, or learning random facts will probably enjoy experimenting with it most. Go to Spotify on your phone or computer, search for the new AI podcast tools, and try prompting it with something like "make a short podcast about how AI helps artists." You may need a parent's help to sign up if you're under 18, but the feature is open to regular accounts. Source: [engadget.com](https://www.engadget.com/2166997/spotify-now-lets-ai-agents-like-openclaw-generate-personal-podcasts/)

**You can turn off Google's built-in Gemini AI in Chrome if it feels weird:** Wired
A 4-GB AI model from Google now runs inside Chrome by default, which surprised a lot of people and raised privacy questions. The good news is you can remove it in just a few clicks without breaking anything else. This matters because it gives you control over what runs on your own device. Open Chrome, go to Settings, search for "Gemini," and follow the steps to disable or uninstall the model. It's a quick way to see exactly what AI features are active on your browser and decide what you actually want. Source: [wired.com](https://www.wired.com/story/you-can-disable-gemini-in-chrome-if-its-freaking-you-out/)

**New AI model catches pancreatic cancer up to three years early in tests:** Live Science
Doctors often miss the earliest signs of pancreatic cancer on CT scans because the clues are tiny. A new AI tool was trained to spot those hidden patterns and flagged the disease years before human experts noticed anything. Early detection like this could save lives by catching the illness when treatment works best.
