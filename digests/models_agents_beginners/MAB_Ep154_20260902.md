### The Big Story
Google is testing a new AI tool called Google Pics that lets you create designs just by typing what you want. Instead of learning buttons and menus in apps like Canva, you describe your idea in plain words and the AI builds it. Think of it like telling a friend “make a birthday poster with balloons and a cake” and watching them sketch it instantly — except the friend is an AI that handles colors, layout, and style automatically. The tool pushes deeper into creative software by focusing on prompts first, which means beginners skip the learning curve of traditional design programs. This matters for students making presentations, teens designing stickers or thumbnails for videos, or anyone who has ideas but feels stuck by complicated interfaces. It could change how creative hobbies start, letting people experiment faster without buying expensive software or taking classes. For you personally, it means your next school project or social media post could look polished in minutes instead of hours. Right now you can try similar prompting tools by going to any free AI image generator and typing a detailed description of a poster or graphic you want to make — experiment with adding style words like “in cartoon style” or “with bright colors” to see how the results change. The more specific your description, the better the output usually gets. Google is entering a space long dominated by Canva and Adobe, but it is doing so with an approach that starts from text prompts rather than manual editing tools. That shift removes many of the first hurdles people face when they want to make something visual but lack design training. Over time this could mean more people try creative projects they would have skipped before because the software felt too hard to learn. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you're using your phone, some apps work completely on the device itself while others send your request to big computers far away? Now picture an AI chat app that decides on the spot which way is better for each question. For simple things like quick facts, it might use a small model that lives right on your phone so the answer comes back instantly and stays private. For harder questions that need more brainpower, it quietly sends the work to powerful cloud servers and brings the answer back without you noticing the switch. This hybrid approach keeps things fast when possible and smart when needed, instead of forcing every request down one path. The result feels smoother because the app isn't stuck using only one kind of model. It also gives users more control over privacy since some work never leaves the phone. That's the idea behind the recent Perplexity update that added local model support — the system is learning to blend both worlds so you get the best of speed and capability without extra effort. The update arrives just before a major Apple event, suggesting companies see value in showing users they can choose where their data goes. When the local model handles easy tasks, the cloud only gets used for the tough ones, which can save time and reduce how much information travels over the internet. Users who care about keeping certain chats private now have a clearer path to do so while still accessing stronger answers when they need them. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/LinusEkenstam/status/2095049424682483995)

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Sonos speakers that can chat with you using ChatGPT**
Sonos just updated its speakers so they can pull in ChatGPT and let you create custom AI voices or responses through your music system. This turns a regular speaker into something that can answer questions, tell stories, or play personalized audio without you touching your phone. It's exciting because it brings AI into everyday spaces like living rooms or bedrooms where people already listen to music. Anyone who has a Sonos speaker and wants to experiment with voice AI should try it. Go to the Sonos app, look for the latest update, and ask the speaker to generate a short custom greeting or playlist description using the new ChatGPT features. The update makes the speaker feel more like a helpful companion than just a music player. You can now ask it to create a bedtime story in a voice you choose or describe a playlist in a fun way that matches your mood. Because the AI lives in the speaker itself, the experience stays hands-free while you move around the room. Source: [smartprix.com](https://www.smartprix.com/bytes/sonos-27-update-brings-chatgpt-and-custom-ai-to-your-speakers/)

**Perplexity adding easy local model access**
Perplexity now lets users run a local AI model alongside its cloud version, making it simple to switch between private on-device answers and more powerful online ones. This is cool because it gives people a taste of hybrid AI without needing technical setup. Students or anyone worried about privacy can try keeping some chats on their device. Download the Perplexity app and look for the option to enable local model mode, then ask it a simple question to see how the two systems compare in speed and detail. The change is meant to feel transparent so you do not have to think about which model is answering. It also points to a future where more apps let you decide how much of your data stays on your phone versus going to the cloud. Trying both modes on the same question shows you the trade-off between quick private replies and deeper answers that need more computing power.
