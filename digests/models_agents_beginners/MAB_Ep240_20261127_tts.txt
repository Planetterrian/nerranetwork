Q.ANT has created, Wired reports, a computer chip that does the hardest artificial intelligence, or A I, math as light waves.

The aim is cutting the energy those models need, because ordinary chips turn every calculation into on and off switches that heat up and gulp power.

This design turns the same work into light patterns moving through tiny channels with almost no heat, like a flashlight flashing millions of patterns at once.

If these chips spread, homework and art assistants could run on cheaper, smaller devices without draining batteries so fast, even on older phones or where electricity is limited.

Q.ANT is still in early testing but showing it can handle A I math, and their website is where to watch for early demos.

Devices could stay cooler during long study sessions or creative projects, and schools with expensive power could one day run the same helpers wealthier districts use.

Long video calls make a phone warm because electrical signals dump leftover energy as heat.

A photonic chip, meaning one that works with light, sends those yes and no decisions as flashes down a glass path thinner than a hair.

Different colors or angles of light slide through each other without a clash.

Hair-thin glass trails let beams cross without slowing down.

Each beam can carry a different slice of the calculation at the same time, so the whole job finishes in fewer steps.

Light moves so quickly that it needs less energy to stay organized, and the chip stays cool even during heavy A I work.

See the beams sharing one path, and the cool chip starts to make sense.
