> **# Models & Agents for Beginners**
Teens are using AI to outsmart expensive World Cup ticket scalpers and build their own tools.
Fans on Reddit are teaming up with Claude to create custom software that helps them find affordable tickets for the 2026 World Cup. This shows how everyday people can use AI to solve real problems without being programmers. We'll also explore how AI can double-check its own work for safety and look at a brand-new open-source agent you can try.
---
### The Big Story
A group of soccer fans on Reddit got tired of sky-high prices for 2026 World Cup tickets and decided to fight back with AI. Instead of just complaining, they asked Claude—an AI from Anthropic—to help them write simple programs that scan ticket sites and spot better deals faster than regular browsers. 

Think of it like having a super-organized friend who never gets tired of refreshing websites and comparing prices for you. The fans shared their AI-built tools in private chats, letting others copy and tweak the ideas without starting from zero. 

This matters because big events like the World Cup usually favor scalpers with bots, but now regular people have the same kind of helper on their side. For students or anyone who loves sports or concerts, it means AI can turn frustration into action on things that actually affect your life and wallet. 

You don’t need to be a coder to get started—many of these fans simply described what they wanted in plain English and let the AI do the heavy lifting. If you have access to Claude or a similar chatbot, try describing a small everyday problem you face and ask it to suggest a simple solution or even a basic web tool. 

The experiment shows AI isn’t just for big companies anymore; it’s becoming a practical sidekick for regular people solving real-world headaches. Source: [wired.com](https://www.wired.com/story/redditors-are-using-ai-to-beat-obscene-fifa-world-cup-ticket-prices/)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re texting a friend, your phone suggests the next word before you finish typing? Now imagine that same autocomplete got millions of times smarter and could also suggest entire paragraphs, fix mistakes, and even write whole short programs if you just describe what you want. 

Step two: instead of stopping at suggestions, picture the AI actually running those ideas for you—like turning your description of “a tool that checks ticket prices” into working code you can test right away. 

Step three: the AI can then look at what it created, spot problems, and offer fixes, almost like having a helpful editor who keeps improving the draft until it works. 

And that’s basically what tools like Claude are doing when they help regular people build simple apps or scripts without years of coding classes. 

So next time someone says “AI agents,” you can tell them—it’s basically supercharged autocomplete that turns your ideas into working helpers. Not so scary, right?

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**An open-source AI helper that runs on your own computer**
Nous Research just released Hermes Desktop, a free AI agent app you can download and run locally on Windows, Mac, or Linux. It works like a personal assistant that stays on your device instead of sending everything to the cloud, which some people prefer for privacy. 

Anyone curious about AI agents can try it without needing coding skills—just download the app and start chatting with it about tasks like organizing files or answering questions. Go to the Nous Research site or GitHub page linked in tech coverage, grab the installer for your computer, and ask it something simple like “help me plan my study schedule for this week.” You may need a parent’s help if you’re under 18 for the download. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/nous-research-releases-hermes-desktop-an-open-source-ai-agent-for-every-platform/)

**Getting smarter about when to trust ChatGPT at school or work**
One user shared how they use ChatGPT daily but still double-check outputs because the answers can look perfect while hiding small errors. This is a great reminder that AI is a powerful starting point, not the final word. 

Try this: open ChatGPT (or Claude) and ask it to explain a topic you’re studying, then compare its answer to your textbook or a trusted site like Khan Academy. Notice where it adds helpful examples versus where it might miss details. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1tvlkuv/using_chatgpt_at_work_daily_but_still_not_sure/)
