### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re texting a friend, your phone guesses the next word before you finish typing? Now imagine that same autocomplete works on pictures instead of words, and it has studied millions of photos to learn what usually comes next in a scene. First it breaks your text prompt into small pieces it understands. Then it starts with random noise, like static on an old TV, and slowly removes the noise piece by piece while checking against everything it learned about shapes, colors, and objects. Each step is basically asking, “Given this prompt, what should this tiny part of the picture look like?” After many quick passes, the static turns into a clear image that matches your description. And that’s basically what image-generating AI is doing when it creates pictures from your words. So next time someone says “diffusion model,” you can tell them — it’s basically super-powered autocomplete for pictures. Not so scary, right?
