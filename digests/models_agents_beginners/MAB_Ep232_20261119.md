### The Big Story
Google released Gemini 4 Neon today, a new AI model built for complex software engineering, knowledge work, and science workflows. It delivers fast end-to-end task completion across those areas and rolls out immediately to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers inside the Gemini app, to paid Pro and Ultra users inside Antigravity, through the Gemini API paid tier in Google AI Studio, and inside Gemini for business. The model handles text, images, video, audio, and PDFs together inside a one-million-token context window and turns those mixed inputs into structured deliverables such as reports or code fixes. It offers four adjustable thinking levels so users can trade speed for deeper reasoning on the fly. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its xhigh thinking level it scores 61 and ranks third overall while sitting ahead of comparable models. It reaches second place on the Vals Index and lands on the Pareto frontier. It also ranks second on steerability inside the new Agent Arena Index, meaning it adapts cleanly when users correct it mid-task. Neon records the lowest fabrication rate yet in Google’s internal Antigravity testing and excels at multi-step instruction following. In enterprise and analytical work it synthesizes spreadsheets, PDFs, charts, audio, and video in one pass and earns first place on both the Legal Research Bench and the Tax Agent Bench. It leads most categories on Surge AI benchmarks for finance, accounting, spreadsheet modeling, and complex constraint following, scores 73.5 percent on Chartography for reading dense professional charts, and reaches 75.8 percent on Agents’ Last Exam v1.1. It takes first place overall on Zapier’s AutomationBench with 56.3 percent and on Andon Labs’ Vending-Bench 2 with a score of $16,843 in a long-horizon business simulation. The model is priced at three dollars per million input tokens, fifteen cents per million cached input tokens, and fifteen dollars per million output tokens. For students and creators already using AI for homework, art projects, or learning new skills, these capabilities shrink the gap between a fun toy and an actually useful helper that can remember an entire project at once. You can try Gemini 4 Neon right now with a paid Google AI Pro or Ultra subscription by opening the Gemini app or visiting Google AI Studio and selecting it from the model dropdown. Start with a simple multi-step request like “Read this PDF and turn the key points into a study guide with quiz questions at the end” to see how it handles longer context. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you’re texting with a friend, your phone only shows the last few messages on screen, so you sometimes lose track of what you were talking about earlier in the day? Now imagine the AI can keep the entire conversation—including every photo, link, and side topic—open in its “working memory” at once. That working memory is called a context window, and Gemini 4 Neon’s version holds up to one million tokens, which is roughly the length of a full novel. When the model receives your prompt, it doesn’t just look at the newest sentence; it can scan back through everything that came before to stay consistent across the whole project. This is why it can take a giant stack of PDFs, charts, and notes and still produce one clean deliverable instead of forgetting details halfway through. The bigger the window, the less you have to repeat yourself or break projects into tiny pieces that fit inside a smaller memory. In practice the model uses that full window to synthesize spreadsheets, PDFs, charts, audio, and video in a single pass before turning them into structured outputs like reports or code fixes. Because it also tracks four different thinking levels, it can decide on the fly whether to move quickly or slow down for deeper checks. The result is an AI that stops feeling like it has short-term memory loss and starts feeling like a teammate who actually remembers the whole plan from start to finish.
