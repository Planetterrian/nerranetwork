You'll also see a new chatbot that gives you more freedom than ChatGPT, plus a teen who built a whole game using AI helpers. ### The Big Story
Imagine typing one short paragraph about a mysterious, enormous presence slowly arriving in an ordinary neighborhood, and an AI instantly creates a playable game that captures that exact feeling without making it scary or sad. That's what happened with a new AI system called Argon. It took a prompt describing "Imminence" — something very big and strange happening in a suburb — and built a game that lets players experience the slow, inevitable arrival of that presence. The exact prompt used was: "A game about Imminence. Something very big, very strange is happening. A suburb & the arrival of a vast & unknowable presence. Not horror, invoke the feeling of the end of all things coming, inevitably, but also not sad or scary."

Think of it like giving a friend a one-sentence movie idea and having them instantly hand you a finished short film you can watch and interact with. Argon doesn't just write text or draw pictures; it assembles the whole experience so you can explore the world it created. The result is hosted at links such as rigee-sycamore-court-2608.netlify.app and imminence-afternoon.netlify.app, where anyone can open the game in a browser. This matters because game design used to require teams of artists, programmers, and writers working for months. Now a single idea can become something you can actually play.

For students and creative teens, this changes what "making a game" even means. You no longer need years of coding classes to bring a strange story idea to life. It opens doors for people who love world-building, storytelling, or atmospheric games but don't have the technical skills yet. The "so what" for you is that your next creative project could start with just a weird feeling you want to explore, and the AI helps turn it into something real you can share with friends or post online.

You can try something similar right now by going to the link in Ethan Mollick's post (rigee-sycamore-court-2608.netlify.app) and typing your own short description of a mood or scene. See what kind of interactive experience the AI builds from your words. The game stays true to the original feeling the whole time you play, showing how one model can handle story, rules, and visuals together. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/emollick/status/2110179213487534366)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you text a friend, your phone guesses the next word based on everything you've already written? Now picture that same guessing system, but instead of just suggesting one word, it guesses what the entire next scene should look like, what the characters might say, and even what rules the player should follow next. The AI starts by reading your description and splitting it into small pieces such as "suburb," "vast presence," and "not scary." It then checks against patterns it has seen in thousands of stories, games, and images to decide which pieces fit the mood you described.

Next it builds the actual parts you interact with — the rooms you can walk through, the objects you can click, and the quiet sense that something large is approaching. Because it keeps predicting what should come right after the last thing it created, the whole game stays consistent instead of jumping between unrelated ideas. The model does not follow a long list of hand-written rules for every possible action. It simply continues the pattern of "what comes next" across text, images, and game logic at the same time.

That steady prediction is why the finished game feels like one single atmosphere rather than separate pieces glued together. When you play, every new screen or sound continues the same slow, strange feeling you asked for in the first sentence.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**A chatbot that gives you fewer rules than ChatGPT**
Infernus AI is a new chat tool made for people who want more creative freedom and fewer automatic blocks when asking unusual questions. It works like other chatbots but is designed to let conversations go further without cutting them off. If you've ever felt limited by safety filters on mainstream tools, this is worth testing. Go to the Infernus AI site, start a free chat, and try describing a wild story idea or asking it to continue a scene in a style you like. You may need a parent's help to sign up if age verification appears. Source: [openpr.com](https://www.openpr.com/news/4660803/infernus-ai-offers-a-new-chatgpt-alternative-for-users-seeking)

**A full game built almost entirely with AI**
One person spent nearly a year creating a complete game called The Last Admiral using AI tools for most of the coding while they handled the story, visuals, and balance themselves. The game contains one million lines of code total, with 400,000 lines being the actual game code and about 40 percent of that made up of comments. The creator used Codex and Claude to write and clean the code, plus an agents.md file so the two AIs could talk to each other. They also built over 100 live editors inside the Godot engine to adjust visuals, audio, and mechanics without writing new code each time. The game has a free demo on Steam and will be part of Steam Next Fest starting October 19. Head to store.steampowered.com, search for "The Last Admiral," download the free demo, and see what a solo creator plus AI can make. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1x5hfnc/spent_nearly_a_year_building_my_game/)
