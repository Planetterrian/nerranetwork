Airbnb is building its own artificial intelligence, or AI, travel agent, The Verge reported from the company's third quarter twenty twenty-six shareholder letter.

An agent here means a helper that can take many steps in a row, not just answer one question and stop.

The tool is set to launch in the first half of twenty twenty-seven, and it will help people plan trips, compare homes, manage bookings, and get help during a stay.

Picture a travel buddy who already knows what you like, and can pull up maps, photos, and itineraries without you typing a long list of questions.

Instead of a regular chat that only answers in text, this agent builds a custom screen with the right visuals at the right moment, almost like a mini travel app that appears instantly.

Airbnb CEO Brian Chesky explained that today's AI agents handle simple tasks such as making a restaurant reservation or scheduling an appointment.

Planning a trip works differently, he said, because it is aspirational, which just means you are dreaming and browsing, not ticking off one tiny to-do.

The company noted that eighty percent of travelers say they enjoy spending time researching trips, and people want to look at photos, compare homes, and make decisions with the friends or family they are traveling with.

Think of it like a group chat for a weekend hangout. Nobody wants a giant wall of text. You want the photos, the map pin, and the calendar sitting there so everyone can weigh in together.

The new agent draws from a library of hundreds of travel-specific interface components, including maps, photos, comparisons, calendars, and itineraries, then composes them in real time to match what the user is looking for.

Interface components are just the building blocks of a screen, like Lego pieces for maps and photo grids, snapped together while you talk.

That matters because general-purpose chatbots are text-heavy, while travel benefits from visual browsing and shared decision making.

A chatbot is simply a program you type to, and it types back. Fine for a quick fact. Awkward when you are trying to fall in love with a place.

For a teen planning a school break or a family vacation, it could mean fewer tabs open and more time actually looking at cool places.

The company says it is making the agent because its experience matching hosts and guests, plus its role in existing reservations, gives it an edge over other AI tools.

The announcement also described the agent as having a personalized interface built specifically for travel, offering the right interface at the right time based on what guests are looking for.

You cannot try it yet, but you can follow Airbnb's updates on their site or app to see early previews when they appear.

You know how when you are texting about weekend plans, your phone sometimes guesses the next word, or suggests a whole reply based on what you just said?

Stretch that same idea across many steps. The AI does not just guess one word. It keeps track of your whole conversation and decides what task to do next, like pulling up a map or suggesting three places that match what you described.

It can also remember earlier details you mentioned, such as I like quiet spots, and use that memory later without you repeating yourself.

That memory is less like a locker where a fact sits forever, and more like a sticky note the helper carries from room to room as the plan changes.

The system works by breaking big goals into smaller actions it can handle one at a time, checking what it already knows and what it still needs to ask or look up.

It is the same move you use on a big school project. You do not write the whole essay in one breath. You pick a topic, then find three sources, then outline, then write, checking what you still need at each step.

Over time it gets better at choosing the right action because it has seen thousands of similar conversations before, the way you get faster at packing once you have packed for camp a few times.

Open your messages right now, start a text about weekend plans, and watch how your phone suggests the next word or a whole reply. That tiny guess is the seed. An agent is that seed, grown up, choosing the next job instead of the next word.

Once you have watched those suggestions pop up, you already understand the basic loop. Track the chat. Pick a next action. Check what is missing. Repeat until the plan is done.

A customizable animated character called Muse can now hold a real-time video conversation with you, after Meta opened the feature in its AI app.

The avatar can show expressions and respond with voice, making the chat feel more like talking to someone than typing at a screen.

It is fun if you like creative role-play, or just want a more natural way to practice conversations, because the face on screen actually reacts while you speak.

The feature rolled out first to a Verge writer on the iPhone app, where the avatar appears at the top of the screen during the call.

You can try it right now in the Meta AI app on iOS, which is Apple's iPhone software, by starting a voice chat and choosing the avatar option.

Ask it to act like a character from your favorite show, or help you brainstorm a story idea, and watch its reactions on screen while it talks.

The expressive avatar makes the interaction feel more alive because it reacts in real time to what you say, closer to a video call than a comment box.

ElevenLabs and SoundCloud teamed up so you can create music with AI and post it directly to the platform without downloading files first.

SoundCloud Artist Pro subscribers even get three months of free ElevenLabs access as part of the deal.

This is exciting if you make beats or songs for fun and want to share them quickly, because the extra step of exporting audio and uploading it separately is gone.

Think of it like posting a story without saving the photo to your camera roll first. Make it, send it, done.

Go to the ElevenLabs website or app, generate a short track, and look for the new SoundCloud publish button to test it out.

Once published, the track appears on your SoundCloud profile right away, ready for others to hear.

Aina's new ring, TechCrunch reported, has a tiny screen, a joystick, and a microphone so you can speak to AI helpers, take calls, or dictate notes without pulling out your phone.

It is designed to feel like a wearable remote control for AI tools you already use, a tiny clicker on your finger instead of another slab in your pocket.

The ring also works for dictating notes directly on a Mac, giving a hands-free way to interact with AI agents on the go.
