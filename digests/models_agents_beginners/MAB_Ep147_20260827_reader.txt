Gemini just dropped a dictation tool that turns messy voice notes into polished text while understanding what’s on your screen.

This is Models and Agents for Beginners, episode 147.

Students and creators often speak faster than they can type, leaving behind filler words and tangled thoughts.

The new Gemini 3.5 Transcribe model listens to spoken words and produces clean, ready-to-use writing instead.

It removes phrases like “um” or “like” automatically and arranges everything into proper sentences.

At the same time it glances at whatever sits open on the screen to grasp the full situation.

Think of it like a super-patient friend who writes everything down, erases the awkward pauses, and checks your open document for extra clues.

Another way to picture it is a helpful editor sitting beside you who knows the difference between a casual text and a formal email.

The system handles more than eighty-five languages and reaches a four percent word error rate while running seventy percent faster than the earlier Chirp 3 model.

That speed comes from passing harder tasks to other Gemini models through function calling, which works like handing off a complicated question to a specialist teammate.

For anyone drafting school papers or turning voice memos into emails, the result feels closer to talking than typing.

You no longer need to fix spelling or structure afterward, which frees mental energy for the actual ideas.

The same features appear in the Gemini app on macOS and through Gboard on Android devices right now.

If you want to experiment, open the Gemini app, start a chat, and tap the microphone to dictate a short paragraph for an assignment.

Watch how the text arrives already cleaned up and neatly formatted without any extra steps from you.

On Android you can also hold the microphone key in any text field while using Gboard and see the same cleanup happen live.

The model also supports voice commands that relate directly to the app or document already visible on screen.

That combination lowers the barrier between having an idea and finishing a polished piece of work.

Developers can explore the same capabilities through Google AI Studio without writing code, simply by testing a voice prompt in the browser.

You know how your phone guesses the next word while you text based on what came before.

Now picture that same idea expanded so the system guesses an entire cleaned-up paragraph while also peeking at the app open on your screen.

First the model listens to the raw audio and splits the sound into tiny recognizable chunks, the way you might break a long sentence into separate words when reading aloud.

Next it uses the surrounding conversation or on-screen content as extra clues, similar to noticing you are inside an email app and therefore should sound more formal.

Then it removes filler sounds such as “um” and “uh” because it has studied millions of examples of how polished writing usually looks.

Finally it rearranges everything into proper sentences and even suggests actions if you mention something like sending the note to a parent.

The four percent word error rate in streaming mode happens because the screen context helps it choose the right words even when the audio is unclear.

The seventy percent speed improvement comes from handing off complex tasks to other Gemini models through function calling, which keeps the main process moving quickly.

The result feels like the AI understood not just the sounds but the whole situation you are in, so the output arrives ready to use.

That is why the finished text needs far fewer fixes afterward than older dictation tools required.
