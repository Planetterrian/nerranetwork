> **ChatGPT just learned how to look at every tab you have open and actually do things for you.**
OpenAI rolled out an update that turns ChatGPT into a real browser helper that can read your tabs and fill out forms across them. This matters because it moves AI from just chatting to actually working alongside you on everyday tasks like shopping or research. Today we break down exactly how that works, explain the idea behind "computer use" with a simple analogy, and look at a new Gmail search tool plus a wild conversation about AI-designed viruses.
---
### The Big Story
OpenAI added new powers to its Chrome extension so ChatGPT can now see every tab you have open in your browser. It can read what is on those pages, summarize the content, and even take actions like filling out forms or comparing products across multiple tabs using a feature called "Computer Use" in Work mode. The extension is open to all ChatGPT plans, meaning both free and paid users can access the new capabilities right away. Think of it like having a super-organized friend sitting next to you who can glance at all your open windows at once and quickly pull together the information you need without you having to switch back and forth. This changes how people might handle school research projects or online shopping because instead of copying and pasting between tabs yourself, the AI can do the comparing and organizing in one place. It also raises questions about privacy since the AI is looking at whatever you have open, so users will want to be thoughtful about sensitive tabs before turning the feature on. For teens exploring future careers in tech or design, this shows how AI is moving from answering questions to becoming a hands-on helper that saves time on repetitive screen tasks. The update works by letting the model access the content of open tabs directly through the browser, then using that information to perform actions like typing into forms or pulling details from several product pages at the same time. You can try it right now by opening Chrome, searching the Chrome Web Store for the official ChatGPT extension from OpenAI, installing it, and then asking ChatGPT to summarize or compare information across your open tabs while in Work mode. The feature is designed to feel like a natural extension of regular chatting, so you simply type what you want done and the model handles the rest inside the browser window. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/chatgpt-becomes-a-full-chrome-browser-assistant-that-reads-and-acts-on-your-open-tabs/)

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Gmail just got a much smarter search bar**
Google added a live AI search feature to Gmail for people who subscribe to its AI plans. Instead of typing exact words and hoping the right email pops up, you can now ask questions in plain language and the AI looks through your messages to find what you mean. This is useful for anyone who has hundreds of old emails and can never remember where that one important message went. The feature turns the search bar into something closer to a conversation, where you describe what you are looking for and the system understands the intent rather than matching only the exact words you typed. Go to Gmail on your phone or computer, make sure you have a Google AI subscription active, then try typing something like "find the email about the school trip last month" and see what it surfaces. You may need a parent's help to set up the subscription if you are under 18. The update is meant to reduce the frustration that comes from basic keyword search that often misses the message you actually need. Source: [engadget.com](https://www.engadget.com/2288545/gmail-live-ai-search-feature-arrives-for-google-ai-subscribers/)

### Quick Bits
**What happens when AI designs brand-new viruses?**
A Stanford student used a generative AI model to create genetic blueprints for tiny viruses, and MIT Technology Review sat down with him to talk about what comes next. The conversation explores how close we are to AI helping design new life forms and what safety questions that raises. It is a fascinating listen if you like thinking about the future of biology and technology. The student’s work shows one early step where AI can propose structures that might one day be built in a lab, even though no actual living virus has been created yet. Source: [technologyreview.com](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/16/1146224/roundtables-a-conversation-with-the-creator-of-ai-designed-viruses/)

**Do we really need to worry about AI stealing our sense of taste?**
One writer argues that AI models tend to create things that feel familiar because they are trained on what already exists, while humans keep craving surprise. The post looks at research on how our brains react to new things and why that might protect human creativity even as AI gets better at making art and stories. It is a thoughtful take if you enjoy making music, writing, or drawing. The piece points out that people naturally move on from whatever becomes common, which could keep human creators one step ahead of what models are likely to produce.
