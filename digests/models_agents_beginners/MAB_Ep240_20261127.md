### The Big Story
A German company called Q.ANT has created a special chip that performs the hardest parts of AI math using light waves instead of the usual electrical signals. The goal is to cut the amount of energy required to run AI models by using light to perform the toughest calculations. Traditional chips turn everything into on/off switches that heat up and use lots of power. The new chip turns the same calculations into patterns of light that move through tiny channels with almost no heat. Think of it like sending messages with a flashlight that can flash millions of different patterns at once, rather than sending them one tiny electrical pulse at a time. This matters because training and running large AI models currently uses huge amounts of electricity, which affects everything from your phone bill to the climate. If light-based chips become common, the same smart assistants you use for homework or art could run on cheaper, smaller devices without draining batteries so fast. For students and creators, that could mean running helpful AI tools on older phones or even in places with limited electricity. Right now the technology is still in early testing, but the company is showing it can handle the kinds of math AI needs. You can follow Q.ANT's updates on their website to see when early demos might appear. The shift from electricity to light also opens the door to smaller devices that stay cooler during long study sessions or creative projects. Schools in areas with expensive power could one day run the same AI helpers that wealthier districts use today. The core idea stays simple: replace heat-making electrical switches with fast, cool light patterns that do identical math. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you text a friend, your phone sends the message as tiny electrical pulses racing through wires inside the phone? Now picture those same messages traveling instead as flashes of light inside a special glass path no thicker than a hair. The light can carry many different patterns at the same time without getting mixed up, and it barely warms up the way electricity does. AI calculations are basically millions of those same simple on/off decisions added together really fast. A normal chip turns every decision into an electrical switch that uses power and creates heat. The light version turns the same decisions into different colors or angles of light that pass through each other without fighting. Because light moves so quickly and doesn't need as much energy to stay organized, the chip can finish the same work while using far less electricity. That is the basic idea behind the new photonic chip: it swaps the electricity for light to do the exact same math more efficiently. The glass paths act like tiny highways where beams of light cross without colliding or slowing down. Each beam can represent a different part of the calculation at once, so the whole job finishes in fewer steps than electricity would need. Students who have watched their phones get warm during long video calls already know how much energy electrical signals waste as heat. Light avoids that waste entirely, which is why the new chip stays cool even while doing heavy AI work.

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**ChatGPT Wallet for Black Friday shopping**
OpenAI is testing a feature called ChatGPT Wallet that lets the chatbot help with actual purchases during the big sales week. Instead of just giving you links, the AI can walk you through checkout steps inside the same chat. It is aimed at people who already use ChatGPT for planning or comparing prices and now want help finishing the buy. Anyone with a paid ChatGPT plan can try it during the current test. Go to chatgpt.com on your phone or laptop, start a new chat, and type something like “Help me find the best headphones under $50 on sale right now and walk me through checkout.” The tool will show product options and guide you step by step without leaving the window. You can also ask it to compare two similar items side by side and explain which one has better reviews before you decide. The feature keeps everything in one conversation so you never have to copy links between tabs. Teens who shop for games, clothes, or school supplies during sales events can test how the AI handles real checkout flows. The test runs only for a limited group right now, so results may vary depending on your plan. Source: [news.designrush.com](https://news.designrush.com/openai-chatgpt-wallet-ai-agents-cyber-week)

**Five things experts say you should never share with ChatGPT**
Security researchers have listed five kinds of information that are risky to paste into any chatbot, including ChatGPT. The list covers things like passwords, medical details, and private photos. The warning is simple: once you type something, the company running the chatbot may store or review it. You can read the full list yourself at the HuffPost article linked below and decide what feels safe to share. If you have already sent something private, the article also explains how to ask the company to delete that conversation. The advice applies to any chatbot, not just ChatGPT, because most services keep logs of what users type. Teens who use AI for school essays or personal journaling should especially check the list before pasting anything that names friends or family. The article gives clear next steps if something sensitive has already been shared, including how to contact support teams directly.
