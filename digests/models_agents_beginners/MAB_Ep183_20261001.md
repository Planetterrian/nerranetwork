### The Big Story
Imagine finishing a long day and realizing you still have to sort through dozens of emails, check if a flight price dropped, or plan tomorrow's schedule. A new guide from Tom's Guide highlights how AI agents can take those repetitive chores off your plate by working on their own after you give them instructions. Unlike a chatbot that waits for one question and answers it, an agent can keep working in the background, checking things repeatedly and coming back with updates when something changes. Think of it like giving your phone a to-do list and letting it handle the steps while you do something else. The guide lists seven everyday tasks perfect for this, starting with sorting your inbox by summarizing new messages, separating FYIs from action items, and drafting routine responses that you still approve before sending. Another task is watching prices on flights, hotels, or products you want to buy, where you tell the agent the price you're willing to pay and it alerts you when it drops or finds a better option. A third job is preparing for the next day by pulling together relevant background from your calendar and other connected information to give you a short briefing on what deserves attention. For students or anyone juggling school and hobbies, this means less time on admin and more time on the fun parts like creative projects or gaming. It also connects to future careers because learning to direct these agents is becoming a real skill employers will value as more companies adopt them. You can start small by connecting an agent like Gemini or ChatGPT to your email and giving it a clear instruction about what to watch for. The key is writing prompts that tell it exactly what to do and when to report back, so it feels like having a helpful helper rather than just another app. OpenAI debuted its Dots at DevDay on Tuesday while Meta launched Muse earlier this month, showing how these tools are becoming available for everyday users. Source: [tomsguide.com](https://www.tomsguide.com/ai/7-boring-jobs-you-can-hand-to-an-ai-agent-tonight-number-4-is-my-personal-favorite)

### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you ask a friend to water your plants while you're on vacation, they check every couple of days, notice if something needs fixing, and text you only when there's actual news? An AI agent works the same way but with digital tasks instead of plants. It gets a standing instruction like "watch this price and tell me if it drops," then keeps looking on its own instead of stopping after one answer. The difference from a regular chatbot is persistence because the chatbot answers once and waits for your next message, while the agent can set reminders for itself and act across apps like email or shopping sites without you stepping in. It uses the same core prediction skills as chatbots but adds the ability to loop back and check again later without you reminding it every time. That turns a one-time helper into something that can manage small ongoing jobs like tracking multiple prices or sorting new emails as they arrive. The result feels less like talking to a search box and more like having a digital teammate that handles the boring follow-up work so you can focus on other things. Source: General concept

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Stay safe from fake ChatGPT tricks**
A security report from Huntress shows attackers are creating fake "Custom GPT" pages on the real chatgpt.com site that trick people into running harmful commands on their own computers. It looks like a normal upgrade prompt but sends you to a page on Google Sites that resembles a Cloudflare CAPTCHA verification screen and asks you to paste and execute a specified PowerShell command on Windows. This matters because it uses the trusted ChatGPT name to lower your guard, and the attack involves eight stages including downloading an obfuscated PowerShell script followed by a malicious MSI installer. The malware then uses DLL sideloading with a legitimate executable file signed by Canon to load a malicious DLL, registers settings to run when Windows starts, and ultimately executes a remote access malware called a RAT that can examine antivirus status, network settings, and download more files. If you ever see a message pushing you to a "backup domain" or asking you to run commands for verification, stop and close the tab right away. Always stick to the official chatgpt.com address and never paste system commands from unknown sources, even if the page looks official. Source: [gigazine.net](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20261001-custom-gpt-clickfix-rat/)
