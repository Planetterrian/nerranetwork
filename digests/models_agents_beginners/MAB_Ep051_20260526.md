### The Big Story
Pope Leo XIV released his first major document as pope, called Magnifica Humanitas, which focuses on protecting people in a world full of artificial intelligence. The letter talks about real risks like AI being used in wars, how it might change jobs, and the need to keep human dignity front and center. Think of it like a coach writing a playbook not just about winning the game, but about making sure every player still feels respected and safe while the rules keep changing. The Pope is pointing out that technology can grow so fast it starts deciding things for us instead of the other way around. This matters because AI is already showing up in school apps, social media feeds, and even creative tools you might use for art or music projects. If the systems shaping what you see and learn are built without thinking about people first, it could quietly limit your choices later. For someone just starting to explore AI, this is a reminder that the tools are here to help you, not replace your voice or judgment. The message encourages everyone, including teens and students, to stay curious and involved rather than just accepting whatever an AI says. You can read the full letter yourself on major news sites covering the Vatican to see the exact points he raises. It is written in a thoughtful way that invites regular people to think along with him instead of needing any technical background. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you're scrolling through your phone, the apps already seem to know what you'll want to watch or read next because they've watched everything you've done before? Now imagine that same idea, but instead of just guessing your next video, the system starts shaping what facts or ideas you even see in the first place. Step two: every time you ask a question, it gives an answer that feels confident, whether it's right or a little off, and you have no easy way to check the original sources the way you could with a textbook. Step three: a small number of companies decide how that system is trained—what counts as "balanced" or "safe"—so the same question can get very different answers depending on which company's AI you use. And that's basically what happens when AI becomes the main way people get information about the world. It starts acting like a new kind of library or news source that everyone relies on, except you can't flip through the pages yourself to see how the answers were chosen. So next time someone talks about AI becoming "epistemic infrastructure," you can tell them it's basically the phone's guesswork turning into the main map everyone uses to understand reality. Not so scary once you see the everyday version, right? Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/artificial/comments/1to0dmn/ai_is_becoming_epistemic_infrastructure/)

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Vibe coding could let way more people build with AI:** r/ChatGPT
Vibe coding is the idea that you can tell an AI what you want in plain English instead of writing computer code, and it turns your description into working software. This approach is already helping open-source AI projects grow fast because people who never learned programming languages can now join in. It matters for beginners because it lowers the wall between having an idea and actually making something with AI. Anyone curious about creating simple tools or games can start practicing by describing exactly what they want an AI chatbot to build. Go to a free chatbot like ChatGPT or Claude and try typing a clear request such as "Help me design a simple study planner that reminds me of homework due dates." You may need a parent's help to sign up if you're under 18, but the experiment itself takes just a minute and shows how human words are becoming the new way to program. Source: [reddit.com](https://www.reddit.com/r/ChatGPT/comments/1tnqn53/vibe_coding_will_increase_open_source_ai/)

### Quick Bits
**Turning everyday chores into robot training data**
Researchers are asking people to record themselves cooking, cleaning, and doing laundry so future humanoid robots can learn those same movements. The idea is that your normal routines could become the lessons that teach machines how to help around the house one day. It raises interesting questions about privacy and who benefits when your daily life becomes training material. Source: [wired.com](https://www.wired.com/story/household-chores-training-robots/)

**Entry-level jobs may be the first to feel AI pressure**
A new analysis warns that while overall employment numbers look steady, the starting jobs many students and new graduates rely on are quietly getting harder to find. AI tools are already handling some of the simple tasks that used to be first steps into a career. This shift could change how people get their first real work experience in the next few years. Source: [technologyreview.com](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/05/26/1137865/its-time-to-address-the-looming-crisis-in-entry-level-work/)
