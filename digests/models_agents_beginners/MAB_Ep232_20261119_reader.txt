Google, on its blog, released Gemini 4 Neon today, a model aimed at hard programming jobs, research-heavy work, and science processes that take many steps.

A context window is the working memory a model like Neon uses while you work with it.

Your phone chat usually keeps just the latest bubbles on screen, so this morning's plan has already scrolled away.

First, you send a question, maybe with files and notes attached.

Second, Neon does not only stare at the newest sentence.

Third, it can scan back through everything already in that window so the answer stays consistent with the whole pile.

Fourth, you do not have to repeat yourself or chop the project into tiny pieces that fit a smaller memory.

The chat bubbles that vanished on your phone are still sitting in Neon's window, which holds one million tokens, roughly the length of a full novel.

Once you picture that window as working memory, the giant-context claims sound like a bigger desk, not a magic trick.
