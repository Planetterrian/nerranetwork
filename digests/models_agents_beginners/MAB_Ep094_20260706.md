# Models & Agents for Beginners
> **A new AI called Fable rewards wild, over-the-top requests instead of safe small ones.**
Ethan Mollick just shared how to get the most out of Fable, a model that shines when you ask it for the biggest possible thing first. Today's episode also shows you an interactive demo where you can literally watch an AI's thoughts, plus a simple privacy setting that stops Google from training on your data. We'll end with a new open-source model that punches above its weight.

### The Big Story
Ethan Mollick, who writes about practical AI use, says most people are asking Fable for way too little. Instead of starting small like we did with older chatbots, he recommends beginning with the maximum request you can imagine. This helps you discover the outer edges of what the model can actually handle before you scale back. Think of it like testing how far a new bike can go on its first ride instead of just pedaling around the block. Once you see the limits, you can adjust your requests with much more confidence. The payoff is faster learning about what these systems are capable of, which matters if you're using AI for school projects, creative writing, or exploring future job skills. You stop wasting time on timid prompts that never show the model's real strengths. Mollick notes this approach works especially well because newer models like Fable keep getting cheaper, so experimenting costs less than it used to. To try it yourself, head to whatever chat interface hosts Fable and start with your biggest, boldest request first, then notice where it succeeds or falls short. ### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you text a friend, your phone sometimes guesses the next word based on what you've typed so far? Now picture an AI that does something similar but across its entire "brain" at once, tracking which parts light up while it's answering you. Researchers at Anthropic built a space called J-space that maps those active parts in real time, almost like putting little labels on the exact neurons firing during a conversation. This mapping works because the model is built in layers, and each layer handles a different slice of the task, such as understanding your question or choosing the next word. By watching which layers stay busy, the team can spot when the AI is about to say something unhelpful and gently steer it. The surprising part is how much this resembles the way our own brains seem to handle thoughts in separate regions. The result is a clearer window into why an AI makes the choices it does, rather than treating it like a mysterious black box. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2074185387577094398)

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Watch an AI think in real time**
Anthropic teamed up with Neuronpedia to build a live demo that lets anyone see inside open-weight models using the same J-space technique. You can click around and watch which parts of the model activate as it processes different prompts. This is perfect if you've ever wondered what actually happens between your question and the answer. Go to neuronpedia.org/jlens, pick one of the example prompts, and watch the colored highlights move as the model works. No account needed.

**Stop Google from using your data to train AI**
Google quietly updated its privacy settings so it can now train AI on more of the things you search and ask. The good news is you can turn this off in just a few clicks. Open your Google account settings on your phone or computer, find the section about data sharing for AI improvement, and toggle it off. This keeps your searches and conversations private while still letting you use Google normally. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/06/if-you-use-google-youre-training-its-ai-heres-how-to-opt-out/)

### Quick Bits
**New open-source model runs big but uses few parts at once**
Tencent released Hy3, a 295-billion-parameter model that only activates 21 billion parameters for any single task. It reportedly matches models two to five times larger while cutting mistakes in half. You can download and experiment with it if you have the right hardware. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/tencent-releases-hy3-open-source-model-that-allegedly-matches-models-up-to-five-times-its-active-size/)
