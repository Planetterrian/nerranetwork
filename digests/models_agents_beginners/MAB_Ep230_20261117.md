### Explain Like I'm 14
You know how when you open a notebook and start sketching, you can add little buttons, arrows, and pop-up notes that actually do something when you tap them? Now picture an AI that can build those same interactive pieces directly inside a drawing or graphic instead of just writing plain text. It starts by understanding your request in normal words, then decides what elements like buttons or 3D effects would make the idea come alive. Next it writes the actual code that makes those elements clickable or movable, all while keeping everything inside one canvas area. The result feels like a tiny website or game you can play with right away, instead of a static picture. This works because the AI has learned patterns from millions of existing web pages, so it knows how to combine text, graphics, and simple actions without you writing any code yourself. The payoff is that creative ideas stop being stuck on the page and become things you can actually click and explore in seconds. Instead of stopping at a description, the model turns the description into a working mini-experience you can test immediately. That shift changes how people brainstorm, because an idea no longer needs a separate coding step before you can see whether it feels right. You can ask for a simple choose-your-own-adventure scene, watch the AI place the choice buttons inside the illustration, and tap them to see what happens next. The same process works for more practical requests, like a small dashboard that updates numbers when you press different options. Because everything stays inside one canvas, the final output remains easy to share or embed without extra files. The approach revives an older style of interactive graphics that once required specialized tools, but now an everyday prompt can produce the same result. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/claude-now-builds-interactive-websites-inside-canvas-graphics-reviving-the-flash-era-with-real-html/)

### Cool Stuff & Try This
**Google Maps now plans your whole day for you**
Google added an AI feature inside Maps that can build a full itinerary for a single day or even several days. It looks at places you might like, adds real-time details like opening hours, and suggests the best order to visit everything. The update runs inside the Gemini-powered Ask Maps chatbot that already lives in the app, so you do not need to switch to another program. This is perfect for anyone planning a weekend trip or a fun day out with friends without spending hours researching. The feature can handle both short outings and longer multi-day trips, adjusting suggestions as real-time information changes. Open the Google Maps app on your phone, tap the Ask Maps button powered by Gemini, and type something like “plan a one-day trip to the city with coffee, a park, and lunch.” It will give you a ready-to-follow schedule you can tweak right away. You can also ask follow-up questions such as “add a bookstore stop” or “make sure we finish before 6 p.m.” and the itinerary updates on the spot. Because the suggestions come with live details, you see which locations are open when you arrive instead of discovering a closed door later. The whole process stays inside the familiar Maps interface, so the directions, estimated travel times, and saved places all stay in one place. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1034110/google-ask-maps-ai-trip-planning)
