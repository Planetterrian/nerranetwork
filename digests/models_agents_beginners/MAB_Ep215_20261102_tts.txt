Token counting sits underneath every Chat G P T reply, and a token is just a small chunk of text.

Picture the data plan on your phone. Streaming a full movie burns a lot more than sending a few texts.

Chatbots run on a usage allowance that follows that same spirit.

Hit send, and the system chops your words into tokens, roughly one token per word.

The reply gets chopped too, so a long answer costs more than a short one.

Treat those chunks like bricks in a building set. A tiny question is a handful of bricks. A huge project is a whole castle.

A more advanced model, or a messy multi-step task, burns through the pile faster.

High definition video eats a mobile plan quicker than a plain message, and hard A I jobs follow that pattern.

Adobe Research built an A I agent named EditAgent that edits photos the way a skilled Photoshop user would, The Decoder reports.

An agent here means software that can choose steps on its own, not a single filter that slaps the same look on every picture.

It studies the photo, then reaches for a specialized tool, such as the clone stamp, which copies pixels from a clean spot to cover a blemish, like patching a stain with matching fabric.

It can also grab an adjustment brush, which lightens or darkens just one area instead of the whole frame.

It tries a few passes, then a language model, the next-word predictor behind chatbots, picks the winner using real measurements of the picture, such as whether a scratch is actually gone.

Future editors could feel less like hunting through menus and more like a helper who already knows which button to press.

The research is still early, so you will not find EditAgent as a finished product in your pocket today.

You can still get a tiny taste in the free Photoshop Express app on your phone or on the web.

Upload a photo, tap Healing or Adjust, and watch which specific fixes the app suggests. That suggestion is a baby version of a tool-picking helper.
