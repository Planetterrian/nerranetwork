### Deep Dive: Federal Grid Funding and Connection Uncertainty
The federal budget allocates funds specifically for expanding power grids to support renewable energy integration. Previous federal approaches emphasized generation investments that have risen nearly 70 percent since 2015, yet grid spending has lagged. Annual investment in electricity generation such as building new power plants has risen by almost 70 percent since 2015 reaching one trillion dollars a year according to the International Energy Agency. Grid spending has risen at less than half that pace. The new allocations target upgrades but leave interprovincial connection frameworks unspecified creating uncertainty for project permitting and environmental assessments. The IEA calculates annual grid investment must increase by about 50 percent from today's 400 billion dollars per year. Any delays will substantially increase global emissions by prolonging reliance on fossil fuels the IEA warns. This directly affects transmission planning and environmental reviews for renewable projects seeking federal support. Practitioners handling impact assessments for grid projects should track subsequent federal announcements for connection policy. Power grids have not been readied enough for the scaleup of renewable electricity generation according to Jan Rosenow a professor of energy and climate policy at Oxford University. We have been complacent in the past and now the grid has become a real bottleneck.
