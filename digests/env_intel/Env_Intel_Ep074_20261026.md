### Deep Dive: La Rose Climate Rights Trial
The five-week proceedings in Vancouver feature fifteen plaintiffs, six of them Indigenous, testifying alongside climate scientists. Plaintiffs argue that inadequate federal climate action breaches section 7 life liberty and security of the person protections and section 15 equality rights because younger generations bear disproportionate future impacts. The case filed in 2019 challenges Canada's overall climate approach and could influence similar litigation elsewhere even if not precedent-setting outside Canada. Practitioners assessing long-term site risks should note how constitutional arguments may intersect with federal impact assessment and species at risk considerations on projects with multi-decade horizons. The trial is being closely watched by legal experts for its potential to shape future regulatory expectations. ### Practice Spotlight: Climate Projections in Phase II ESA Scoping
When scoping a Phase II ESA in coastal or northern jurisdictions factor in updated sea-level rise and permafrost thaw data from the Canada Energy Regulator scenarios rather than relying solely on historical groundwater contours. Adjust monitoring well placement and vapour intrusion modelling to account for projected changes in groundwater flow direction over the next twenty to thirty years. Cross-reference with provincial CSR Protocol 1 requirements for conceptual site models that incorporate climate variables where available. The most common oversight is treating climate as a future issue instead of adjusting current sampling density and analytical suites now.

### Practitioner Deep Dive: Integrating Climate Projections into Phase II ESA Scoping
A Phase II work plan for a coastal British Columbia site shows historical groundwater flow toward the northwest but recent modelling indicates potential reversal under twenty-fifty sea-level rise scenarios. The Canada Energy Regulator demand projections imply higher industrial water use that could further alter gradients. Experienced assessors adjust the conceptual site model early by adding an extra line of wells perpendicular to the projected future flow direction and by running sensitivity analyses on vapour intrusion pathways under elevated water tables. The nuance is that climate variables are not an add-on report but must be embedded in the initial CSM to avoid re-mobilization of contaminants that current monitoring would miss. The mistake I see most often is locking the monitoring network to a single historical flow vector and then discovering during the first five-year review that the plume has shifted off-site. The fix is straightforward: run at least one climate-adjusted scenario in the initial modelling and document the rationale in the Phase II report so the client and regulator both understand the adaptive sampling plan.
