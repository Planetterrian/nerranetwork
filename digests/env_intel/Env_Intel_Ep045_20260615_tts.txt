Now, speaking of the Newfoundland fish-sauce removal, here's something I wish someone had told me early in my career.

You arrive at a former fish-processing facility on the Newfoundland coast where 110 vats of concentrated fish sauce have sat for 25 years.

The Phase two shows elevated BOD, ammonia, and sulphide in shallow groundwater but the nearest receptor is a small harbour 300 meters away.

The science here is straightforward: anaerobic decomposition of proteins generates volatile fatty acids and hydrogen sulphide that migrate rapidly in sandy or fractured bedrock aquifers typical of Atlantic Canada.

In the field you notice that standard low-flow sampling underestimates peak concentrations because the plume is density-stratified.

The experienced practitioner therefore installs multilevel wells screened at 1 meters intervals and collects both filtered and unfiltered samples for sulphide and ammonia to capture the true mass flux.
