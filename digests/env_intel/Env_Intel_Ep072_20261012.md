### Deep Dive: Species at Risk Act Protection Delays
A CBC investigation identifies a procedural gap in the Species at Risk Act that allows listed species to remain without critical habitat orders or recovery strategies for extended periods. The study documents 153 unresolved cases out of 288 as of January 2025. Some species have waited multiple years after initial listing without further protection measures. This directly affects project screening under the Impact Assessment Act and provincial environmental assessment processes where SARA-listed species trigger additional review requirements. Consultants conducting Phase I and Phase II assessments in jurisdictions with known occurrences must verify current listing status and any pending habitat orders before submitting reports. The absence of a fixed timeline for ministerial decisions on protection creates uncertainty in permitting schedules. Federal-provincial coordination on enforcement remains inconsistent across provinces. The procedural gap means listing alone does not activate the full suite of obligations practitioners expect. Recovery strategies and critical habitat orders are the actual compliance triggers, yet publication of those instruments lags in more than half the cases examined. Project proponents therefore face variable risk depending on whether a particular species has reached the order stage. The study highlights that 153 cases remain open without resolution, leaving habitat protection incomplete for those species. Practitioners must therefore treat every SARA-listed species occurrence as potentially active until the registry confirms the order status. ### Practice Spotlight: Verifying SARA Listing Status in Assessments
When a site assessment identifies potential habitat for a listed species, confirm the most recent critical habitat order status through the federal Species at Risk Public Registry rather than relying solely on the initial listing date. The backlog means some species have recovery strategies published while others with identical listing dates do not, creating uneven compliance triggers. Cross-reference with provincial wildlife databases because enforcement of habitat protection orders varies by jurisdiction even when federal listing applies. Update internal checklists to flag any species with listing dates before 2023 that still lack published orders. The verification step prevents later regulator requests for additional surveys after submission.

### Practitioner Deep Dive: Confirming SARA Protection Triggers During Assessments
A Phase II report flags potential habitat for a species listed three years ago, yet the federal registry shows no critical habitat order published. The experienced practitioner checks the registry date first rather than assuming the listing automatically triggers review. The science here is that SARA protection obligations attach only when a critical habitat order or recovery strategy is actually in force, not at the moment of listing. Field teams sometimes submit assessments citing the listing date alone and receive regulator requests for additional surveys months later. The common mistake is treating the initial listing notice as the compliance trigger. The fix is to add a registry check step to every species screening protocol and document the order publication date in the report.
