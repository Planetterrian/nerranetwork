### Practitioner Deep Dive: Floodplain groundwater interaction during site assessments
A closure package for a former service station in southern Manitoba shows benzene concentrations stable in three wells but rising sharply in a fourth after spring flooding, despite no new release. The difference arises because the fourth well sits inside the active floodplain where the water table now intersects a residual LNAPL smear zone that was previously above the seasonal high. Under Manitoba Environment Act guidance and CCME Tier 1 protocols, practitioners must distinguish between plume migration and temporary hydraulic connection; the latter often produces transient exceedances that resolve once water levels drop. Experienced assessors cross-reference well hydrographs against river-stage data from the nearest gauge rather than relying solely on quarterly snapshots. The most common mistake is assuming any post-flood increase signals a new source and triggering unnecessary additional investigation; the fix is to collect one extra round of water-level and product-thickness measurements during the next low-water period before revising the conceptual site model.
