### Lead Story
British Columbia’s Ministry of Water, Land and Resource Stewardship announced that work is now underway on three conservation planning projects.  
The planning areas together cover approximately 127,000 hectares of ecosystems and wilderness.  
No new regulatory thresholds or amended standards were released with the announcement, but the plans will establish management objectives that can constrain future land-use and remediation activities.  
Consultants conducting Phase I and Phase II ESAs or preparing risk assessments near these blocks should confirm whether proposed investigation or remediation works intersect with the planning boundaries.  
Next steps include release of detailed planning terms of reference; practitioners should request copies directly from the ministry to identify any new information requirements. Source: [news.gov.bc.ca](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026WLRS0032-000798)

**Rusting Rivers: Alarm Grows Over Rising Acid in Arctic Waters: The Tyee**
Climate-driven permafrost thaw combined with increased rainfall is generating sulfuric acid in rivers and lakes across Arctic Canada. Resulting pH declines can mobilize metals and alter background water-quality conditions used in northern site assessments. ### Practitioner Deep Dive: Permafrost thaw and acid generation in northern site assessments
A routine groundwater sampling round in the Northwest Territories returns pH values 1.5 units lower than the previous year’s data at the same wells, with concurrent spikes in sulfate and dissolved metals. The change is not explained by site activities; instead, regional permafrost degradation is releasing previously sequestered sulfides that oxidize once exposed to increased infiltration and oxygen. Under the CCME framework and territorial equivalents, background conditions must be re-established before attributing concentrations to the site, yet many Phase II scopes still rely on a single pre-thaw baseline dataset. Experienced assessors now collect paired surface-water and shallow groundwater samples at reference locations outside the inferred plume to document the shifting regional signature before finalizing the conceptual site model. The most common mistake is assuming that historical background values remain valid; the fix is to schedule an explicit background re-characterization event whenever multi-year monitoring shows consistent directional shifts in pH or major ions.
