A monitoring dataset from last spring shows inconsistent dissolved oxygen readings across wells screened at similar depths in the same aquifer.

The variation often traces to differences in purge volume or stabilisation criteria rather than actual plume movement.

Experienced assessors cross-check field parameters against historical low-flow records before attributing changes to site conditions.

They also verify that the pump intake sits at the screen midpoint and that drawdown stays below ten percent of the screen length.

The mistake I see most often is accepting stabilisation after only three consecutive readings without confirming the well has been purged at least three well volumes.

The fix is straightforward: document the actual volume removed and compare it to the calculated well volume before recording final parameters.

Low-flow sampling requires careful attention to stabilisation parameters including pH conductivity temperature and dissolved oxygen.

Field teams must ensure the pump rate remains consistent and that the water level drawdown does not exceed the recommended threshold.

Historical data from the same wells provides the baseline for identifying whether current readings reflect true aquifer conditions or procedural variations.
