> **Alberta pipeline project now contingent on federal-provincial agreement on industrial carbon tax.**

**Executive Summary:** Alberta and federal officials have tied a new West Coast pipeline to resolution of industrial carbon pricing. British Columbia has advanced six clean electricity projects to cut emissions at industrial sites. Environment and Climate Change Canada is directing research funding toward plastic pollution effects on Indigenous communities. Practitioners should track carbon pricing outcomes for project economics and review new federal research priorities for site assessment protocols.

### Lead Story
Alberta and Canada have conditioned approval of a new pipeline to the West Coast on reaching agreement on the industrial carbon tax. The linkage directly affects project economics for any proponent advancing linear infrastructure under the Impact Assessment Act and Alberta EPEA. Prior pipeline applications treated carbon pricing as a separate compliance stream; the current arrangement folds tax outcomes into the overall permitting decision. Consultants preparing environmental impact statements for oil and gas corridors must now model multiple carbon price scenarios in their net-present-value and risk assessments. Field teams should update greenhouse-gas quantification protocols to align with the latest federal draft guidance on industrial emissions. Watch for the next federal-provincial meeting outcome, which will determine whether the project proceeds to detailed regulatory review or requires redesign. ### Industry & Practice
**Pipeline-Carbon Tax Linkage: The Narwhal**
The Alberta-Canada pipeline agreement introduces new uncertainty into project schedules and capital planning for any proponent relying on federal or provincial approvals. Environmental consultants should prepare sensitivity analyses that treat carbon tax levels as a variable input rather than a fixed compliance cost. Early engagement with both Alberta Environment and Protected Areas and the Impact Assessment Agency is now advisable for any linear project crossing provincial boundaries.

### Practitioner Deep Dive: Carbon Price Sensitivity in Remediation Cost Modelling
You arrive at a former upstream oil and gas battery in Alberta where the Phase II ESA has delineated a 1.2-hectare plume of PHC F2 and F3 in fine-grained till. The client’s internal model assumes a fixed $50/tonne carbon price for the thermal desorption option, yet the federal-provincial pipeline talks signal that industrial rates could rise sharply within the project timeline. In the field, this means the remedial options analysis must now run parallel cost curves at $50, $80, and $120 per tonne, because each increment changes the break-even point between excavation and in-situ chemical oxidation. The experienced practitioner notices that carbon pricing also indirectly affects disposal facility gate fees and transportation surcharges, items rarely flagged in standard CCME Tier 2 worksheets. The insight that separates senior from junior work is recognizing that the carbon tax applies not only to on-site energy use but to the embodied emissions of imported reagents and off-site treatment. The mistake I see most often is locking the remedial design to a single carbon price assumption in the initial options analysis; the fix is to require the cost model to carry three discrete carbon-price scenarios through to the final recommendation table submitted under EPEA.
