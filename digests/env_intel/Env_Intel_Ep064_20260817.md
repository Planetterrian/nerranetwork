### Practitioner Deep Dive: Interpreting Municipal Authority Limits Under EPEA for GHG Sources
A client submits a municipal development permit for a data centre expansion and the county returns the application without GHG review, stating the matter sits outside its EPEA mandate. The practitioner must decide whether to proceed with only municipal air-quality parameters or initiate parallel provincial filing. Under EPEA, GHG reporting obligations attach to specified industrial activities and thresholds; data centres fall into a grey zone where combustion or cooling loads may trigger federal output-based pricing instead. Experienced assessors cross-check the project’s expected annual emissions against the federal backstop applicability list before assuming municipal silence closes the file. They also examine whether any combustion sources at the facility meet the definition of a regulated source under Alberta’s industrial emissions rules. The most common mistake is treating a municipal “no jurisdiction” response as final clearance; the fix is to obtain written confirmation from Alberta Environment and Protected Areas on whether the facility meets any EPEA reporting trigger before the permit advances. This step prevents later enforcement exposure when provincial or federal reviewers examine the same project file.
