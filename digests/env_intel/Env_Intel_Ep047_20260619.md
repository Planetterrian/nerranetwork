### Lead Story
Tamara Davidson, Minister of Environment and Parks, approved an amendment to the Red Chris Porphyry Copper-Gold Mine environmental assessment certificate authorizing the transition from open-pit mining to underground block-caving. The change replaces surface extraction methods with underground techniques at the existing permitted site. This alters the physical footprint and potential contaminant release pathways compared with the original open-pit design. Consultants managing similar porphyry copper-gold projects under BC EMA certificates should assess whether analogous amendments are feasible or required for ongoing operations. No implementation timeline or additional monitoring conditions are stated in the announcement. Source: [news.gov.bc.ca](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026ENV0031-000725)

### Practitioner Deep Dive: Block-Cave Transition Risk Reassessment
A client submits a request to amend an existing EMA certificate for a porphyry deposit, citing improved geotechnical stability and reduced surface disturbance from shifting to block-cave methods. The original certificate and supporting documents were prepared under open-pit assumptions, so the hydrogeological model, seepage estimates, and waste-rock characterization no longer match the proposed extraction sequence. BC CSR Protocol 1 requires that any material change in mining method triggers re-evaluation of contaminant transport pathways and exposure scenarios before the amendment can be considered complete. Experienced assessors recognize that block-cave operations often create different fracture networks and dewatering demands than open pits, which can change groundwater flow directions and metal leaching rates even when total tonnage remains similar. The most common mistake is treating the amendment as a simple footprint reduction and reusing the original risk assessment without updating the source-term and receptor-pathway analysis; the fix is to rerun the fate-and-transport model with block-cave-specific parameters before submitting the amendment package.
