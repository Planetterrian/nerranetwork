### Lead Story
British Columbia issued an updated BC Hydro integrated resource plan that lowers projected capacity additions relative to earlier electrification scenarios. The plan replaces prior demand forecasts with more conservative growth assumptions tied to slower industrial load uptake. Previous iterations had assumed faster alignment with provincial net-zero pathways; the revision reduces the number of new clean-energy procurement tranches expected before 2035. Consultants preparing environmental assessments or permit applications for generation and transmission projects must now align submissions with the lower build-out schedule. Risk assessments for sites adjacent to planned corridors should re-evaluate cumulative impact pathways under the revised timeline. No formal comment period is attached, but BC Hydro will accept targeted technical feedback through existing utility liaison channels. ### Practitioner Deep Dive: Reconciling Inconsistent Benzene Detections Across Nested Well Networks
A Phase II report shows benzene above CSR standards in a deeper monitoring well while the up-gradient shallow well remains non-detect, even though both are screened in the same aquifer unit. The apparent reversal often traces to subtle vertical gradients and sorption differences rather than laboratory error. BC CSR Protocol 4 requires explicit consideration of vertical profiling when concentrations vary by more than an order of magnitude between adjacent screens; many submissions still default to horizontal plume mapping alone. Experienced reviewers cross-check historical water-level data for seasonal reversals and request low-flow sampling logs before accepting the data set as representative. The most common mistake is assuming the shallow well defines the source boundary; the fix is to install a short-screened well at the precise suspected release depth and re-sample under stabilized drawdown conditions before revising the conceptual site model.
