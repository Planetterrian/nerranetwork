### Practitioner Deep Dive: Interpreting Wildfire Smoke Effects on Site Monitoring Data
A routine quarterly groundwater sampling round returns benzene concentrations in a shallow well that exceed the prior two events by a factor of three, yet the up-gradient source zone shows no change in free-phase product thickness. Smoke from regional wildfires has deposited fine particulates across the site for several days, altering soil gas exchange and temporarily suppressing volatilization while increasing surface runoff infiltration. The experienced practitioner recognizes that wildfire-derived organic aerosols can sorb to soil organic matter and later desorb under changing redox conditions, producing transient spikes unrelated to the primary release. Cross-check field notes for smoke event timing against analytical holding times and request re-analysis of archived samples using the same method to isolate the effect. The most common mistake is attributing every concentration increase to source migration or failed remediation; the fix is to log concurrent air quality indices and meteorological data before revising the conceptual site model.
