### Practitioner Deep Dive: Pipeline Corridor Baseline Data Gaps
You arrive at a scoping meeting for a proposed linear project crossing multiple First Nations territories in northern BC and the Alberta border region. The client has preliminary route options but limited recent field data on groundwater flow directions and seasonal surface-water connectivity near proposed watercourse crossings. Under the Impact Assessment Act and BC environmental assessment requirements, baseline programs must capture both western science parameters and Indigenous knowledge on valued components. Experienced practitioners notice that publicly available surficial geology mapping often under-resolves fine-scale hydraulic conductivity variations that control contaminant migration or spill trajectory modelling in low-gradient boreal terrain. The insight that separates senior from junior work is recognizing when desktop aquifer data must be supplemented with targeted slug testing or packer testing before route alternatives are narrowed. The most common mistake is treating all route options as equivalent for hydrogeological risk screening; the fix is to run a rapid comparative vulnerability matrix using existing well records and LiDAR-derived drainage before committing field budgets.
