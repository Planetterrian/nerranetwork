### Practitioner Deep Dive: Interpreting Seasonal Variability in Background Groundwater Concentrations
You arrive at a former industrial site in southern Ontario for the first post-spring-thaw sampling round. MW-4 shows benzene at 18 µg/L while the same well read 4 µg/L in January, yet no new release is suspected. The difference is almost entirely driven by water-table fluctuation and temperature-driven biodegradation rates rather than plume migration. Under O. Reg. 153/04 and CCME Tier 1, background wells must be sampled at the same frequency and analytical suite as compliance wells; failure to capture this seasonal envelope often leads to unnecessary delineation or incorrect statistical trend analysis. Experienced assessors compare dissolved oxygen, redox, and temperature logs across events before attributing concentration shifts to site sources. The most common mistake is treating a single low-flow event as representative without checking whether the well screen intersects the seasonal high-water table; the fix is to schedule at least one additional event during the opposite hydrologic extreme before finalizing the conceptual site model.
