### Practitioner Deep Dive: Cumulative Air Emissions Assessment for New Industrial Facilities Near Existing Infrastructure
A consultant receives a client request to scope air quality work for a proposed concrete batch plant adjacent to an existing border crossing and rail corridor. The Phase I has flagged potential overlap with existing combustion sources, yet no formal cumulative assessment trigger appears in the initial permit checklist. Under Ontario EPA approvals, the ministry retains discretion to require combined effects modelling even when individual source contributions fall below screening thresholds. Experienced practitioners recognize that residential receptors within 500 metres often drive the need for refined dispersion runs using site-specific meteorological data rather than conservative defaults. The key insight is that regulators increasingly expect explicit justification for excluding nearby sources, particularly when public correspondence has already highlighted health concerns. The mistake I see most often is assuming that meeting individual source emission limits satisfies the cumulative obligation; the fix is to document the rationale for source inclusion or exclusion in the initial modelling plan submitted with the application.
