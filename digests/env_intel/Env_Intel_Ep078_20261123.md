> **University of Guelph research shows raising entire male pigs could cut Canadian swine GHG emissions by 11.2 percent per kg carcass weight.**

**Executive Summary:** Research from the University of Guelph Department of Animal Biosciences identifies measurable reductions in manure volume, nitrogen content and feed consumption when entire male pigs replace castrates. The findings carry direct implications for greenhouse gas accounting and nutrient management on Canadian swine operations. ### Deep Dive: University of Guelph Swine Production Efficiency Study
The University of Guelph Department of Animal Biosciences study examines how production practices affect greenhouse gas outputs and nutrient loads from Canadian swine operations. Worldwide pork production exceeds 100 million tonnes annually to meet demand. Canada directs approximately 75 percent of its pork output to export markets. In 2021 the country shipped more than 1.5 million tonnes valued at roughly 5 billion dollars and ranked third among global exporters according to USDA data. Swine production ranks as the second largest contributor to livestock sector greenhouse gas emissions at 707.3 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent worldwide. Canadian swine operations contributed 6.7 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent as of 2001. Manure accounts for 81 percent of total greenhouse gas emissions from swine systems. The study evaluates the shift from castrated barrows to entire males. Entire males show 13 percent faster growth, 20 percent higher lean yield, 14 percent better feed conversion and 9.5 percent lower feed intake. These performance shifts reduce manure volume and lower its nitrogen content. The resulting greenhouse gas reduction reaches an estimated 11.2 percent per kilogram of carcass weight. That reduction equals 0.75 million tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent annually in Canada and 39.6 million tonnes worldwide. Lower nitrogen in manure decreases the risk of leaching into water bodies. Ontario Ministry of Agriculture Food and Agribusiness data place feed costs during the grower finisher phase at approximately 224 dollars per tonne of pork produced. At current prices the feed intake reduction alone could deliver nearly 87 million Canadian dollars in annual savings for Canadian producers if half of the 12.6 million market hogs recorded in January 2026 are raised as entire males. The same shift would reduce feeding costs tied to the 1.5 million tonnes of exported pork whose associated feeding expenses reached 336 million dollars. The analysis notes that routine castration of male piglets occurs in many countries to prevent boar taint yet raises animal welfare concerns and lowers overall system efficiency. No specific provincial regulation or reporting threshold is cited in the source material. ### Practice Spotlight: Nutrient Management Planning for Swine Operations
Review current manure nutrient profiles against the efficiency metrics reported for entire-male production systems. Adjust nitrogen loading calculations where feed conversion and growth data indicate lower manure nitrogen output. Confirm whether provincial nutrient management plans require updates when production practices shift to entire males. Compare reported 9.5 percent feed reduction and 14 percent feed conversion improvement against existing operation records to determine whether revised nitrogen excretion factors are warranted. Verify that any change in manure volume aligns with current storage and land application permits in the relevant province. Document the basis for any updated calculations so that future compliance reporting can reference the University of Guelph performance data directly.
