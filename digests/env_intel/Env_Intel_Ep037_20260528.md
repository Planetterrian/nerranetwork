### Practitioner Deep Dive: Interpreting Apparent Concentration Variability in Nested Monitoring Wells
You arrive at a former service station to resample after the Phase II flagged benzene above CSR Schedule 6 standards in one well but not the adjacent one 8 m up-gradient. The difference is not random. Vertical hydraulic gradients and subtle layering in the fill unit often route dissolved-phase mass along preferential pathways that standard 2-inch wells with 1.5 m screens can miss or average out. BC CSR Protocol 4 and Ontario O. Reg. 153/04 both require documentation of screen placement relative to the water table and any observed stratification; experienced assessors log the exact elevation of the pump intake and compare it against previous low-flow stabilization data rather than assuming uniform aquifer conditions. The insight that separates seasoned practitioners is recognizing that a single non-detect does not disprove plume extent when the well screen straddles a low-permeability lens. The most common mistake is immediately declaring the plume delineated or reduced; the fix is to install a short-screened well or use a multilevel sampler at the same location before adjusting the conceptual site model or risk assessment assumptions.
