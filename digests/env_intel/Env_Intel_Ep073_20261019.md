### Lead Story
GlobalData's latest Canada Power Market report projects cumulative installed power capacity expanding at a compound annual growth rate of 2.1 percent between 2025 and 2035. Non-hydro renewable capacity, mainly wind and solar PV, is forecast to increase its share from 20.6 percent to 31.4 percent of total capacity as electricity demand grows at 1.7 percent CAGR. The report identifies interprovincial transmission bottlenecks as the principal infrastructure constraint, noting that surplus low-carbon generation in Quebec, Manitoba and British Columbia cannot readily flow to decarbonize systems in Alberta or Saskatchewan. Provincial networks remain more extensively connected to US markets than to neighbouring Canadian provinces. Attaurrahman Ojindaram Saibasan, Power Analyst at GlobalData, states that Canada already operates one of the world’s cleanest power systems anchored by vast provincial hydropower resources and an established nuclear fleet. The next decade will mark a significant evolution with non-hydro renewables driving the majority of net capacity additions led by onshore wind and utility-scale solar. Major nuclear life-extension programs and small modular reactor deployments will help maintain baseload stability. Practitioners assessing or remediating sites tied to new generation or transmission infrastructure should incorporate these coordination delays into project schedules. Canada is entering a pivotal phase of power-sector expansion as federal tax credits, the phase-out of coal-fired generation, and accelerating industrial electrification drive substantial capital into wind, solar, and nuclear power. Realizing the full potential of these clean additions will require overcoming persistent interprovincial transmission bottlenecks and modernizing regional grids according to the analysis. Electricity planning remains constitutionally divided across 10 distinct provincial jurisdictions. ### Regulatory & Policy Watch

**COP17 biodiversity summit opens in Yerevan:** RFI
Almost 200 countries will review progress on the 2022 nature pact that set 23 targets including conserving 30 percent of land and oceans. A draft UN review found countries off-track on 22 targets, with stronger progress on traditional conservation than on production and consumption changes. Environment ministries face funding cuts in both rich and poor countries, creating barriers to cross-ministry coordination on root causes of nature loss. Wildlife populations continue to shrink with monitored populations across nearly 6,000 species falling by an average of 73 percent between 1970 and 2022 according to the WWF Living Planet Report. Astrid Schomaker who heads the UN Convention on Biological Diversity notes that progress is moving in the right direction but way too slowly. Juliette Landry a biodiversity researcher at the Paris-based think tank IDDRI explains that barriers include lack of political will for some countries and lack of access to financial resources capacities and technologies for others. Cop17 runs in the Armenian capital Yerevan until 30 October and marks the first global review of progress since the adoption of the landmark nature pact in 2022. The agreement set targets from cutting pollution to saving threatened species and conserving 30 percent of the world’s land and oceans. Progress has been stronger on traditional conservation work than on changing the way goods are produced and consumed one of the root causes of nature loss. Source: [rfi.fr](https://www.rfi.fr/en/environment/20261019-nature-summit-confronts-the-money-still-driving-wildlife-destruction-cop17-yerevan)

### Practitioner Deep Dive: Spring Groundwater Monitoring Preparation
A monitoring dataset from last spring shows inconsistent dissolved oxygen readings across wells screened at similar depths in the same aquifer. The variation often traces to differences in purge volume or stabilisation criteria rather than actual plume movement. Experienced assessors cross-check field parameters against historical low-flow records before attributing changes to site conditions. They also verify that the pump intake sits at the screen midpoint and that drawdown stays below ten percent of the screen length. The mistake I see most often is accepting stabilisation after only three consecutive readings without confirming the well has been purged at least three well volumes. The fix is straightforward: document the actual volume removed and compare it to the calculated well volume before recording final parameters. Low-flow sampling requires careful attention to stabilisation parameters including pH conductivity temperature and dissolved oxygen. Field teams must ensure the pump rate remains consistent and that the water level drawdown does not exceed the recommended threshold. Historical data from the same wells provides the baseline for identifying whether current readings reflect true aquifer conditions or procedural variations. Calibration of field meters before each sampling event reduces the risk of instrument drift affecting results. Documentation of purge volumes allows later review of whether the well was adequately developed prior to parameter recording.
