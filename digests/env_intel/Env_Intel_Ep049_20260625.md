### Practitioner Deep Dive: Sediment Quality Characterization for Federal Dredging Projects
A proponent submits a dredging application for a BC coastal terminal and assumes standard Tier 1 sediment screening will suffice for federal approval. In practice, federal port projects trigger additional evaluation of contaminant bioavailability and transport pathways that provincial CSR protocols alone do not address. Experienced assessors recognize that historical industrial inputs in Burrard Inlet often produce heterogeneous contaminant profiles that single-point sampling misses, requiring targeted coring and elutriate testing to predict water-column impacts during dredging. The key distinction lies in distinguishing between total concentrations and the bioavailable fraction that actually drives Fisheries Act or CEPA risk determinations. The mistake I see most often is relying solely on bulk chemistry results without confirming whether the proposed dredge prism intersects known historical fill or spill zones. The fix is straightforward: integrate existing sediment quality data from prior port studies into the sampling design before submitting the application.
