# Nerra Voices

> **Inside a dual-certified facial plastic surgeon's practice where skin-cancer reconstruction and elective facelifts share the same anatomy and demand identical standards for natural function.**

*Episode 2 · October 10, 2026*

**What You Need to Know:** In his Nerra Voices interview, Dr. Michael Brandt states that skin-cancer reconstruction and elective facelifts demand identical precision in tissue handling, planning, and scar camouflage because both must meet the same functional and aesthetic standard. He describes this shared requirement through the “supermarket test,” where a successful result lets the patient pass unnoticed so that others see the person rather than the work. The discussion also covers how his MSc research on scars changed his daily evaluation of outcomes and why he treats fillers and surgery as distinct tools rather than substitutes.

### What we talked about

- Dr. Brandt notes that reconstruction after cancer removal often requires creative tissue movement to preserve surrounding structures, while aesthetic cases allow more control over incision placement, yet both receive the same attention to detail.
- His MSc work on scar assessment showed that the overall gestalt—how a scar sits relative to facial contours and affects expression—drives patient perception more than isolated factors such as color or texture.
- He applies the supermarket test to decide when a repair has failed: any distortion of eyelid position or unnatural tension means the result draws attention instead of blending in.
- Fillers can camouflage a nasal bump or restore volume but cannot reposition descended tissues or remove excess skin, so he does not use repeated injections as a bridge when structural correction is needed.
- Training and dual certification required years of delayed personal time; he now prioritizes evenings and family activities after health issues reminded him where his primary responsibility lies.
- Every case, whether reconstructive or cosmetic, creates an ongoing relationship because the surgeon continues to worry about long-term results rather than completing a one-time intervention.

Dr. Michael Brandt is a dual-certified facial plastic and reconstructive surgeon at FORM Face + Body in Toronto.
