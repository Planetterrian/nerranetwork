**Grammar Spotlight:**
Saying "it is [weather]" in Russian uses short adjectives that end in -о.  
- Сегодня тепло. (Today it is warm.)
- Сегодня холодно. (Today it is cold.)
English just says "it is cold," but Russian adds the special -о ending to show we are talking about the weather, not a person or thing.

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: Снег**
Did you know that the Russian word снег and the English word snow are actually the same ancient word? They both come from a very old language spoken thousands of years ago called Proto-Indo-European. This word started as *sniegwh- and travelled through many languages, slowly changing its sound until it became снег in Russian and snow in English. But be careful — the Russian word магазин sounds like "magazine" but actually means "shop"! Next time you see snow falling, remember it is cousins with снег — they both come from the same magical root. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, people love talking about the weather because winters are very long and cold. Spring (весна) is a special time when everyone celebrates the return of warmth and the first flowers after months of snow.
