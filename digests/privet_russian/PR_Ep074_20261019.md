Memory hook: Imagine a “lee-sah” fox leaping like a sly little acrobat — the sound even feels quick and clever!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
We learn how to say “the animal does something” using simple present-tense verbs. In Russian the verb changes a little depending on who or what is doing the action.
Examples: Лиса бегает. (The fox runs.) Тигр спит. (The tiger sleeps.) Слон ест. (The elephant eats.)
Unlike English, Russian verbs show the subject right in the ending, so you often don’t need extra words like “he” or “she.”

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: тигр**
This word started in ancient Persian as “tigra” meaning “arrow” because of the tiger’s swift stripes, then travelled through Greek and Latin before reaching Russian in the 18th century via European languages.
But be careful — the Russian “тигр” looks almost exactly like the English “tiger,” yet in some languages the same-sounding word can mean something completely different!
Here’s a memory trick: picture a tiger shooting forward like an arrow — that Persian “arrow” image helps the sound stick forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russian fairy tales the clever лиса often outsmarts bigger animals, teaching children that brains can be stronger than size. Kids grow up hearing stories like “The Fox and the Crane,” where the fox’s quick thinking becomes a favourite lesson.
