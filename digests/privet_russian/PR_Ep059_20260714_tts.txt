Now let us look at one grammar idea using these weather words.

In Russian, weather often uses short phrases with no subject at all.

Сегодня тепло simply means today is warm, with the it understood but never spoken.

Compare that to English, where we always say it is warm.

You can now say kholodno to mean it is cold.

Or dozh-d idyot to mean it is raining.

Or veter silnyy to mean the wind is strong.

That feels natural once you try it a few times.

Russians love talking about the weather because their huge country has dramatic changes.

Snowy winters in Siberia contrast with sunny summers in the south.

People often greet each other with kak pogoda to ask how the weather is, as a friendly way to start a chat.

Now here is something really cool about one of today's words.

Want to know a secret about veter?

Did you know that the Russian word ветер and the English word wind are actually cousins from the same ancient root?

They both come from an old language root meaning to blow.

That root travelled through Germanic tongues to become English wind and through Slavic branches to become Russian ветер.

Be careful though, because ветер sounds nothing like the false friend weather, which in Russian is actually pogoda.

Next time you feel the wind on your face, remember it is cousins with ветер.

They both come from the same blowing root, and that connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.
