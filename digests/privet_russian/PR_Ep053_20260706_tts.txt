Today we step into a cozy Russian kitchen. Our word of the day is хлеб.

The Russian word is хлеб.

Хлеб.

Khlyeb.

It sounds a little like loaf if you stretch your imagination. Russians often say хлеб when they mean the staff of life.

Repeat after me.

Khlyeb.

Here is a simple sentence.

Я ем хлеб.

Ya yem khlyeb.

I eat bread.

Now we build more kitchen words that go with хлеб.

The next word is молоко.

Молоко.

Malako.

Яблоко.

Remember дом from a few lessons ago?

Dom.

That means house. Now picture a kitchen inside that house where we eat our food.

Our last kitchen word is еду.

Еду.

Yedu.

In Russian every noun belongs to a team.

The teams are called masculine, feminine, or neuter.

These teams change how we say describing words.

Хлеб is masculine. That is why we say вкусный хлеб.

Яблоко is neuter. That is why we say вкусное яблоко.

English does not change adjectives for teams, but Russian gives every noun its own team colour.

In Russia bread is treated with great respect.

It is often placed in the centre of the table.

People say хлеб — всему голова.

Khlyeb — vsemu golova.

Bread is the head of everything.

Guests are welcomed with bread and salt.

That shows warmth and hospitality.

Do you remember what хлеб means?

Say it out loud.

Khlyeb. Bread.

Now here is something really cool about one of today’s words.

Want to know a secret about чай?

Did you know that the Russian word чай and the English word tea are actually cousins from the same ancient root?

This word started in China as cha.

It travelled along the Silk Road through Persian and Turkish traders.

It finally arrived in Russian in the seventeenth century when tea became popular at the royal court.

Be careful though.

The Russian word магазин sounds like magazine but actually means shop.

Next time you sip a cup of tea remember its cousin чай.

They both come from the same Chinese root.

That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.
