### Word of the Day
> **Дождь**
**Transliteration:** dozhd' (pronounced "dohzhd" with a soft "zh" like the "s" in "pleasure")
**English meaning:** rain

**Example sentence:** Сегодня идёт дождь. (Segodnya idyot dozhd'.) — It is raining today.
Imagine the word "dozhd'" sounding like a gentle "drip-drop" on the roof — the "zh" makes it feel splashy and wet!

**Repeat after me:** Say it slowly with me: dozh-d'... now at normal speed: dozhd'! Great job — repeat it once more: dozhd'!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
We are learning how to say "it is [weather]" using the simple pattern "Сегодня [weather word]." (Segodnya = today.)  
- Сегодня тепло. (It is warm today.)
- Сегодня холодно. (It is cold today.)
- Сегодня идёт дождь. (It is raining today.)

In English we often add "is" and change the verb, but in Russian the weather word itself carries the meaning and stays very simple — no extra verb needed for temperature words!

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: солнце**
Did you know that the Russian word солнце (solntse) and the English word "solar" are actually cousins from the same ancient root? They both come from the Proto-Indo-European word *sóh₂wl̥, which simply meant "sun." This word traveled through Latin as "sol," giving us "solar" and "sun," while in Slavic languages it became solntse.  

Along the way it picked up a fun false friend: in some old texts "sol" could also hint at "salt" because the sun was seen as preserving life like salt does — but be careful, the Russian word for salt is actually соль (sol')!  

Next time you see the word "solar" or feel the sun on your face, remember it's cousins with солнце — they both come from the same ancient word for the bright star above us. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians love talking about the weather because it changes dramatically across their huge country — from snowy Siberia to sunny Black Sea beaches. A popular saying is "Нет плохой погоды, есть неподходящая одежда" (There is no bad weather, only unsuitable clothing), teaching that you can enjoy any weather if you dress right!
