Let's warm up with Russian words for snow, ice, and winter weather that will help you chat about chilly days!

I'm Olya, and this is Привет, Русский! — episode seventy-eight.

Our word of the day is снег. Snyeg. Say it with me. Snyeg. It means snow. Think of the soft sound snow makes when it lands, like a quiet sneeze that covers everything in white. Here is a simple sentence. Снег белый. Snyeg belyy. The snow is white. Repeat after me. Снег. Snyeg.

Now we add more winter words that go with снег. The next word is лёд. Lyod. It means ice. Lyod rhymes with load, and you can picture a heavy load of slippery ice on the ground. Say it slowly. Lyod. The ice feels cold, so we say лёд холодный. Lyod kholodnyy. The ice is cold.

Next comes дождь. Dozhd'. This word means rain. It sounds a bit like dosed, but it brings water from the sky instead of medicine. Repeat after me. Dozhd'. Дождь идёт. Dozhd' idyot. It is raining.

Ветер comes next. Vye-ter. It means wind. Vye-ter is like better weather when a cool breeze helps on a warm day. Say it with me. Vye-ter. Ветер дует. Vye-ter duyet. The wind is blowing.

The sun often appears even in winter, and its word is солнце. Solntse. It means sun. Solntse starts like solar, our big sunny star in the sky. Repeat after me. Solntse. Солнце светит. Solntse svetit. The sun is shining.

Two useful phrases tell us how the air feels. Холодно. Kholodno. This means it is cold. Kholodno feels like cold with a Russian ending. Say it slowly. Kholodno. Сегодня холодно. Segodnya kholodno. It is cold today.

The opposite is тепло. Teplo. It means it is warm. Teplo sounds like tepid but means nicely warm and cozy. Repeat after me. Teplo. В доме тепло. V dome teplo. It is warm in the house.

Our last word is зима. Zeema. It means winter. Zeema carries a sense of zeal for building snowmen and playing outside. Say it with me. Zeema. Зима холодная. Zeema kholodnaya. Winter is cold.

Russian adjectives change their endings to match the noun they describe. Take холодный. Kholodnyy. It means cold and works with masculine words like ветер. When the noun is feminine, like зима, the ending becomes холодная. Kholodnaya. This agreement makes the sentence sound natural, unlike English where the adjective stays the same no matter the noun.

Russians often start conversations by mentioning the weather because winters last a long time and bring plenty of snow. A friendly phrase you can try is Какой холод. Kakoy kholod. It means what cold and works well on a snowy street.

For our practice time, kids can draw a snowy scene and label the снег, лёд, and солнце, then say each word out loud. Adults, look out the window and make one simple sentence about today's weather using two new words, such as Сегодня холодно и снег.

Before we finish, here is something really cool about one of today's words. Зима began long ago in an early Slavic form that shares an ancient root with the Sanskrit word hima for snow or frost. That same root gave the Himalayas their name, the abode of snow. The word traveled through early Slavic languages and kept almost the same sound in Russian.

A playful false friend appears in some other languages where a similar sound means earth instead of a season. Picture a Himalayan peak covered in never-melting зима snow to lock the connection in your mind.
