Now for a quick grammar bite.

In Russian every noun has a gender.

Most animal names that end in а or я are feminine.

Корова is feminine.

So we say она большая.

She is big.

Медведь ends in a consonant.

It is masculine.

So we say он любит мёд.

He loves honey.

Птица ends in а.

It is feminine.

Она летает.

She flies.

You do not need to memorize long rules.

Just listen for the ending sound.

In Russian fairy tales the медведь is often a strong but kind forest character.

Children grow up hearing stories like Masha and the Bear.

The bear sometimes helps and sometimes tricks the little girl.

It is a favourite tale across Russia.

Want to know a secret about one of today's words?

The Russian word медведь and the English idea of honey eater come from the same ancient root.

Both mean the one who knows honey.

Long ago people avoided saying the real name of the bear.

They worried it might bring bad luck.

So they used this polite word instead.

Over centuries it became медведь.

It sounds nothing like the English word bear, yet it carries the same sweet golden meaning.

Next time you hear honey, remember it is cousins with медведь.

That connection will help you remember the word forever.
