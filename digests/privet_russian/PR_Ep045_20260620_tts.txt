Now let's look at a simple grammar idea using these words.

We often start weather sentences with сегодня, which means today.

In Russian the adjective at the end changes its ending to match the noun.

Сегодня тепло stays neutral because it describes the overall day.

Сегодня жарко also stays neutral for the same reason.

Небо голубое shows how голубое matches the neutral word небо.

Unlike English, Russian makes the adjective agree with the noun.

This lets you say useful phrases like сегодня тепло right away.

Russians love talking about the weather because their huge country has Arctic snow in the north and southern heat in other places.

In many cities people greet each other with Как погода, which means how is the weather, before saying anything else.

It is a warm way to start any conversation.

Now here is something really cool about one of today's words.

Want to know a secret about солнце?

Did you know that the Russian word солнце and the English word solar are actually cousins from the same ancient root?

This word began in Proto-Indo-European as sóh₂wl̥, moved through ancient Slavic languages, and became солнце in Russian while Latin turned it into sol and later solar.

Be careful though, because the Russian word небо sounds a little like nebula yet simply means sky, not a space cloud.

Next time you see solar panels, remember they are cousins with солнце because both come from the same shining root.

That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.
