Russian nouns have gender. Most animal names ending in a are feminine like кошка and собака. We use моя for my with feminine words. Моя кошка means my cat. Моя собака means my dog. Compare that to English where my stays the same for everything. Мой медведь means my bear because bear is masculine.

In Russia many families keep cats as beloved pets. You will often see stray cats cared for in courtyards and parks. Russians have a special saying. Без кошки дом — не дом. It means a house without a cat is not a home. The phrase shows how Russians value these furry friends in daily life. Say bez KOSH-ki dom — ne dom slowly with me.

Want to know a secret about кошка? Did you know that the Russian word кошка and the English word cat are actually cousins from the same ancient root? They both come from a very old sound people made to call cats, something like katt or kadu thousands of years ago. This word traveled through Latin cattus into many European languages while in Russian it became кошка, keeping that soft k sound.

Be careful though, the Russian word мышь sounds a bit like mouse and means exactly the same thing, yet мышь can also trick you because it ends with a soft sign that changes the pronunciation. Next time you see the English word cat, remember it is cousins with кошка. They both come from that ancient calling sound. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.
