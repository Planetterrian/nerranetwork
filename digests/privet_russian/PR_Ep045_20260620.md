**Grammar Spotlight:**
Today we look at simple weather sentences using “сегодня” (today) and adjectives. In Russian, adjectives often change their ending to match the noun’s gender.  
Examples:  
- Сегодня тепло. (It is warm today.) — neutral form
- Сегодня жарко. (It is hot today.) — neutral form
- Небо голубое. (The sky is blue.) — “голубое” matches neutral “небо”
Unlike English, Russian makes the adjective “agree” with the noun; English keeps adjectives the same.

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: Солнце**
Did you know that the Russian word солнце and the English word “solar” are actually cousins from the same ancient root? This word started in Proto-Indo-European as *sóh₂wl̥, travelled through ancient Slavic languages, and settled in Russian as солнце while Latin turned it into “sol” and later “solar.”  
But be careful — the Russian word “небо” sounds a little like “nebula” yet simply means “sky,” not a space cloud!  
Next time you see “solar” panels, remember it is cousins with солнце — they both come from the same shining root. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians love discussing the weather because their huge country has everything from Arctic snow to southern heat. In many cities people greet each other with “Как погода?” (How is the weather?) before talking about anything else — it is a warm way to start any conversation.
