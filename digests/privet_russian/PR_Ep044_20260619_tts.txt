Now for a quick grammar bite.

Russian nouns have genders.

They can be masculine, feminine, or neuter.

Adjectives must match the noun.

Look at our animals.

Большой кит.

That is a big whale.

The ending changes for masculine.

Большая птица.

That is a big bird.

The ending changes for feminine.

Большое животное.

That is a big animal.

The ending changes for neuter.

It is like a matching game once you see the pattern.

Now here is something really cool about one of today's words.

Want to know a secret about медведь?

The Russian word медведь and the idea of a honey-eater share the same concept.

They both come from the ancient Slavic words for honey and to know.

People were afraid to say the real name of the bear out loud.

This word started in Proto-Slavic.

It travelled through old Russian folk tales.

It stayed in modern Russian as a gentle nickname.

The Russian word медведь sounds nothing like the English bear.

Yet both animals love the same sweet treat.

Next time you see honey, remember it is cousins with медведь.

They both come from the same honey-loving root.

That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

In Russian fairy tales animals often act like wise teachers or tricky friends.

Think of the clever fox or the strong bear in stories by Pushkin.

Kids grow up hearing these tales.

This makes animal words extra fun and memorable.

The tales help when you visit Russia or talk with Russian-speaking family.
