Let's look at one simple grammar idea.

In Russian we often skip the subject when talking about weather.

We just say тепло.

It means it is warm.

Or холодно.

It means it is cold.

Сегодня ветрено.

It is windy today.

Unlike English we drop the it and use the adverb directly.

Now you can say a full weather thought in Russian.

There's something really cool about one of today's words.

Ветер started in ancient Proto-Slavic as větrъ.

It shares a very old root with English wind that goes all the way back to the same source meaning to blow.

It travelled through centuries of Slavic languages without changing much.

English kept a similar sound but spelled it differently.

Be careful.

The Russian ветер sounds nothing like the English veteran even though they share some letters.

Picture a veteran sailor shouting VYEH-ter every time a gust hits the sails.

Russians love to talk about the weather because it changes dramatically across their huge country.

From snowy winters in Siberia to warm summers near the Black Sea.

People often greet each other with a quick comment about the temperature or wind.

It is a friendly way to start conversation.
