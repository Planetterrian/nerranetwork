The word of the day is школа. Школа. It means school. Listen again slowly: школа. The pronunciation is SHKO-la. It sounds almost exactly like the English word school, which makes it easy to remember right away. Here is a simple sentence with it: Я хожу в школу каждый день. That means I go to school every day. Our vocabulary builder adds words that fit together in a school setting. The first is учитель. Учитель. It means teacher. Say it slowly: oo-CHEE-tyel. Repeat after me: учитель. The teacher is the one who helps you learn, and a sentence could be Учитель говорит по-русски. That means the teacher speaks Russian. Next comes ученик. Ученик. This means pupil or student.

The pronunciation is oo-chee-NEEK. Repeat after me: ученик. An example is Ученик читает книгу, which means the student reads a book. Now we add урок. Урок. It means lesson. Pronounced oo-ROK. Repeat after me: урок. You might hear Урок начинается в девять, or the lesson starts at nine. Книга comes next. Книга. This is book, said KNEE-ga. Repeat after me: книга.

A sentence could be Я читаю книгу в школе, meaning I read a book at school. Two action words follow. First, писать. Писать. It means to write, and the sound is pee-SAT. Repeat after me: писать. Then читать. Читать. This means to read, pronounced chee-TAT. Repeat after me: читать. Класс is our next word. Класс. It means class or classroom, and it sounds like KLAS. Repeat after me: класс.

Our final vocabulary word is друг. Друг. It means friend, pronounced DROOK. Repeat after me: друг. A sentence could be Мой друг сидит рядом, or my friend sits nearby.

Now for grammar. Russian verbs change their endings depending on who is doing the action. Take the verb читать, which means to read. With я it becomes я читаю for I read. With ты it becomes ты читаешь for you read. With он or она it becomes он читает or она читает for he or she reads. These endings make it clear who is acting, much like English but with more visible patterns.

Once you know this, you can build sentences like она читает урок right away.

In Russia the first day of school on September first is called День знаний. Children bring flowers to their teachers and sometimes wear special clothes, turning the start of the year into a small celebration.

There's something really cool about one of today's words that shows how languages travel. The word школа began in ancient Greek as scholē, meaning free time for learning. It moved through Latin into Old Russian and then into modern Russian. English school followed almost the same path from the same root, so the two words are linguistic cousins that arrived centuries apart.

In some contexts Russians also use школа for any training course, just as we say driving school. Picture a Greek scholar sitting in a Russian classroom; the word travelled with him all the way to you.
