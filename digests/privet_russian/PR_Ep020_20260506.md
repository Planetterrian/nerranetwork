# Привет, Русский! — Episode Plan
- **Russian (Cyrillic):** животное  
  **Transliteration:** zhivotnoye  
  **English:** animal  
  **Example sentence:** Это большое животное.  
  **Example translation:** This is a big animal.  
> **"Zhivotnoye" comes from "zhivot" (belly or life) — picture a lively animal with a big belly!**

- **Russian (Cyrillic):** лягушка  
  **Transliteration:** lyagushka  
  **English:** frog  
  **Example sentence:** Лягушка прыгает в реке.  
  **Example translation:** The frog jumps in the river.  
Sounds a bit like "lagoon" — imagine a frog splashing in a lagoon!

- **Russian (Cyrillic):** большой  
  **Transliteration:** bol'shoy  
  **English:** big  
  **Example sentence:** Большой зверь живёт в лесу.  
  **Example translation:** A big beast lives in the forest.  
Think of the Bolshoi Theatre — it's the "big" famous theatre in Moscow!

- **Russian (Cyrillic):** древний  
  **Transliteration:** drevniy  
  **English:** ancient  
  **Example sentence:** Древнее животное имело зубы.  
  **Example translation:** The ancient animal had teeth.  
"Drevniy" sounds like "tree" (derevo) — ancient trees have been around forever!

- **Russian (Cyrillic):** кость  
  **Transliteration:** kost'  
  **English:** bone  
  **Example sentence:** У лягушки есть кости.  
  **Example translation:** The frog has bones.  
"Kost'" sounds like "cost" — bones cost nothing in a fossil hunt!

- **Russian (Cyrillic):** зуб  
  **Transliteration:** zub  
  **English:** tooth  
  **Example sentence:** У зверя большие зубы.  
  **Example translation:** The beast has big teeth.  
"Zub" is short and sharp — just like a tooth!

- **Russian (Cyrillic):** находка  
  **Transliteration:** nakhodka  
  **English:** discovery / find  
  **Example sentence:** Это интересная находка.  
  **Example translation:** This is an interesting discovery.  
"Nakhodka" sounds like "knock on the door" — you knock and find a surprise!

- **Russian (Cyrillic):** река  
  **Transliteration:** reka  
  **English:** river  
  **Example sentence:** Лягушка живёт в реке.  
  **Example translation:** The frog lives in the river.  
"Reka" sounds like "wreck a boat" — but rivers are calm homes for animals!

- **Russian (Cyrillic):** саламандра  
  **Transliteration:** salamandra  
  **English:** salamander  
  **Example sentence:** Саламандра — древнее животное.  
  **Example translation:** The salamander is an ancient animal.  
Looks and sounds almost exactly like the English word — easy to remember!

**Grammar Spotlight:**  
Adjectives in Russian change their endings to match the gender of the noun (masculine, feminine, or neuter). This is different from English, where adjectives stay the same. For example: большой зверь (big beast — masculine), большая лягушка (big frog — feminine), большое животное (big animal — neuter). Just look at the noun's ending to pick the right adjective form!

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: саламандра**  
Did you know that the Russian word саламандра and the English word "salamander" are actually the same word? They both come from the ancient Greek "salamandra," a mythical creature said to live in fire. This word started in Greek mythology, travelled through Latin into medieval European languages, and arrived in both Russian and English hundreds of years ago with almost no changes. But be careful — the Russian word "магазин" sounds like the English "magazine" but actually means "shop"! Next time you see a salamander (or even a lizard), remember it's cousins with саламандра — they both come from that fiery Greek root. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**  
In Russian fairy tales and folklore, animals are often wise teachers or funny characters. The bear, for example, appears as a strong but gentle giant in many stories that children still love today.
