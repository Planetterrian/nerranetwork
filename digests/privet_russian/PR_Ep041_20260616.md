**Grammar Spotlight:**
Noun gender in Russian is simple once you notice the patterns. Most animal names ending in -а or -я are feminine (like корова, кошка, мышь), while words ending in a consonant are usually masculine (like медведь, кот).  

Examples:  
- Корова (feminine) — Она большая. (She is big.)
- Медведь (masculine) — Он любит мёд. (He loves honey.)
- Птица (feminine) — Она летает. (She flies.)

Unlike English, Russian nouns always have a gender, but you don’t need to memorize rules — just listen for the ending!

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: Медведь**
Did you know that the Russian word медведь and the English idea of “honey-eater” are basically the same word? They both come from an ancient root that meant “honey-knower.”  

This word started in very old Slavic languages as a polite way to avoid saying the real name of the bear (people thought saying the real name might bring bad luck). It travelled through centuries of Russian speech and finally settled as медведь around the Middle Ages.  

But be careful — the Russian word “медведь” sounds nothing like “bear,” yet it literally means “the one who knows honey”!  

Next time you see the English word “honey,” remember it’s cousins with медведь — they both come from the same ancient root about sweet golden treats. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russian fairy tales, the медведь is often a strong but kind character who lives in the forest. Children grow up hearing stories like “Masha and the Bear,” where the bear helps (or sometimes tricks) the little girl Masha.
