### Word of the Day
**солнце** (SOLN-tse) — sun
Example sentence: Сегодня светит солнце. (Segodnya svetit solntse.) — Today the sun is shining.  
Memory hook: Picture a big yellow “sol” (like solar) shining down — “solntse” is your sunny shortcut!  
Repeat after me: солнце… солнце.

**Grammar Spotlight:**
Russian weather phrases often use “есть” (est’) for “there is” or just the adjective alone.  
Examples: Есть дождь. (There is rain.) / Холодно. (It is cold.)  
Unlike English, you don’t always need “it is” — the adjective or noun stands by itself.

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: дождь**
Did you know the Russian word дождь and the English word “drizzle” share an ancient root meaning “to drip”? This word started in Proto-Indo-European, travelled through Slavic languages, and settled in Russian as дождь. But be careful — the Russian word “дождь” sounds a bit like “drench” yet simply means rain, not “soak”! Next time you see drizzle, remember it is cousins with дождь — they both come from the same dripping root. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians love talking about the weather because winters are long and cold. People often say “Как погода?” (How is the weather?) when greeting friends, and sunny days in summer are celebrated with outdoor picnics and walks in the park.
