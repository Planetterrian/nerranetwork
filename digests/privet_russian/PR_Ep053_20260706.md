# Привет, Русский! — Episode Plan

### Word of the Day
**Хлеб**
Transliteration: khlyeb  
English meaning: bread  
Example sentence: Я ем хлеб. (Ya yem khlyeb.) — I eat bread.  
Memory hook: It sounds a little like “loaf” if you stretch your imagination, and Russians often say “хлеб” when they mean the staff of life.  
Repeat after me: khlyeb… khlyeb.

**Grammar Spotlight:**
Russian nouns have genders (masculine, feminine, neuter) that affect the words around them.  
- Хлеб is masculine, so we say “вкусный хлеб.”
- Яблоко is neuter, so we say “вкусное яблоко.”
- Compare to English: we don’t change adjectives for gender, but in Russian it’s like giving every noun its own “team colour.”

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: чай**
Did you know that the Russian word чай and the English word “tea” are actually cousins from the same ancient root? This word started in China as “cha,” travelled along the Silk Road through Persian and Turkish traders, and finally arrived in Russian in the 17th century when tea became popular at the royal court. But be careful — the Russian word “магазин” sounds like “magazine” but actually means “shop”! Next time you sip a cup of tea, remember its cousin чай — they both come from the same Chinese root. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, bread is treated with great respect — it is often placed in the centre of the table and people say “хлеб — всему голова” (bread is the head of everything). Guests are traditionally welcomed with bread and salt, showing warmth and hospitality.
