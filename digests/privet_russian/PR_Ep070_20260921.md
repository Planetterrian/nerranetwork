**Grammar Spotlight:**
In Russian, the verb “to eat” changes depending on who is doing the action. We say “я ем” (I eat), “ты ешь” (you eat), and “он/она ест” (he/she eats). This is different from English, where the verb stays almost the same. Example: Я ем сыр. (I eat cheese.) Ты ешь хлеб. (You eat bread.)

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: чай**
This word started in northern Chinese dialects as “chá,” traveled along the Silk Road through Central Asia, and entered Russian in the 17th century as the popular drink “чай.” But be careful — the Russian word “чай” sounds exactly like the English “chai” yet it simply means the same beverage, not a fancy latte! Here’s a memory trick: picture a steaming cup of “chay” arriving by caravan from China straight to a Russian samovar.

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians love sharing tea and simple snacks like bread with cheese or soup when guests visit. Offering food is a warm way to show hospitality and start a friendly conversation.
