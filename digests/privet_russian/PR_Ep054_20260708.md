### Word of the Day
**семья**
Transliteration: sem-YA  
English meaning: family  
Example sentence: Это моя семья. (Eto moya sem'ya.)  
English translation: This is my family.  
Memory hook: It sounds like “seminar” but means the people you gather with every day.  
Repeat after me: сем…ья… сем…ья… семья!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
In Russian, words for family members often change ending to show “my” or “your.” We add -а or -я for “my.”  
Examples:  
моя мама (moya mama) — my mom  
мой папа (moy papa) — my dad  
моя сестра (moya sestra) — my sister  
English does the same with “my,” but Russian makes the ending match the person’s gender.

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: мама**
Did you know that the Russian word мама and the English word mama are actually the same word? They both come from the very first sounds babies make all over the world. This word started as a natural baby sound, travelled through ancient languages, and settled into both Russian and English with almost no change. But be careful — the Russian word “папа” sounds exactly like English “papa,” yet in some languages it can mean something else entirely! Next time you hear “mama,” remember it’s cousins with Russian мама — they both come from the universal first word babies say. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, бабушки often play a very active role in raising grandchildren, helping with school and cooking big Sunday meals. Families love to gather around the table for long talks and tea, making “семья” feel like the heart of daily life.

For kids: Draw your семья and label each person with the new Russian words, then say each name out loud.  
For adults: Introduce your family in one sentence using “моя” or “мой,” for example: “Моя мама и мой папа живут в доме.” Remember дом from a few lessons ago? That’s the house where your семья lives!
