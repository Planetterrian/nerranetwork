### Word of the Day
**путешествие**
Transliteration: poo-tesh-ESS-tee-yeh  
English meaning: journey or trip  
Example sentence: Я люблю путешествие на поезде.  
English translation: I love a journey by train.  
Memory hook: It sounds a bit like “put a stitch” — imagine stitching together memories on a long journey!  
Repeat after me: путешествие… путешествие.

**Grammar Spotlight:**
Today we look at the preposition “в” when talking about going somewhere. In Russian you say “в Москву” (to Moscow) or “в отель” (to the hotel). It works like English “to” but the noun often changes its ending. Examples: Я еду в Москву. / Мы идём в отель. Unlike English, Russian uses this little word to show direction, so the city or place gets a special ending.  

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: паспорт**
Did you know that the Russian word паспорт and the English word “passport” are actually the same word? They both come from the French “passeport,” which literally meant “pass through the port.” This word started in medieval French, travelled across Europe with traders and sailors, and landed in Russian in the 1700s when Peter the Great opened Russia to the world. But be careful — the Russian word “магазин” sounds like “magazine” but actually means “shop”! Next time you see your English passport, remember it’s cousins with Russian паспорт — they both come from the idea of safely passing through a port. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.  

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians love long train journeys across their huge country. The famous Trans-Siberian Railway takes seven days from Moscow to Vladivostok, and families often bring big чемоданы full of food for the trip.
