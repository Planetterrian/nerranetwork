Our word of the day is хлеб.

Khlyeb.

It sounds a little like loaf, and both are the staff of life.

Repeat after me: khlyeb… khlyeb.

Now listen to it in a sentence.

Я ем хлеб.

That means I eat bread.

Let's build our food vocabulary.

Our first new word is яблоко.

YAblo-ko.

It sounds like apple with a friendly ko ending.

Repeat after me: YAblo-ko… YAblo-ko.

This is a big apple.

Это большое яблоко.

Next comes молоко.

Mo-lo-KO.

The last syllable pops like milk splashing into a glass.

Repeat after me: mo-lo-KO… mo-lo-KO.

I drink milk.

Я пью молоко.

Now we have сыр.

Syr.

It is short and cheesy, just like the English word sir, but it means cheese.

Repeat after me: syr… syr.

The cheese is delicious.

Сыр вкусный.

Our next word is мясо.

MYA-so.

Imagine a meaty lion saying my-a.

Repeat after me: MYA-so… MYA-so.

The meat is big.

Мясо большое.

Here is овощ.

O-voshch.

It sounds like oh, fresh, perfect for garden vegetables.

Repeat after me: O-voshch… O-voshch.

The vegetable is delicious.

Овощ вкусный.

Next is фрукт.

Frukt.

It is almost the same as the English word fruit, an easy win.

Repeat after me: frukt… frukt.

Now we learn two action words.

Есть means to eat.

Yest’.

It sounds like yes-t, so say yes to eating.

Repeat after me: yest’… yest’.

I eat bread.

Я есть хлеб.

Пить means to drink.

Peet’.

It rhymes with eat but means drink.

Repeat after me: peet’… peet’.

I drink milk.

Я пить молоко.

Remember моя from earlier lessons.

Ma-YA.

It is your personal word.

My apple.

Моя яблоко.

Remember большой too.

Bol’-SHOY.

Think of a big show of food on your plate.

Big cheese.

Большой сыр.

Now for a quick grammar bite.

Every Russian noun has a gender, masculine, feminine, or neuter.

Хлеб ends in a consonant, so it is masculine.

Яблоко ends in o, so it is neuter.

Молоко is also neuter.

Adjectives must match the noun, like большое яблоко for a big apple, but большой сыр for big cheese.

The words hold hands!

Russians love sharing food, especially bread and tea.

When guests arrive, hosts always offer чай with something sweet.

Before eating, people say приятного аппетита to show they care about everyone at the table.

For our practice challenge, draw your favourite food and label it with today’s Russian words.

Then say Я ем followed by the food name out loud.

Adults, name three items on your plate in Russian the next time you eat.

Want to know a secret about one of today’s words?

Did you know the Russian word чай and the English word tea are actually cousins from the same ancient root?

The word started in China as chá, travelled along the Silk Road through Persian and Turkish traders, and reached Russian in the seventeenth century.

Be careful though.

The Russian word магазин sounds like magazine but actually means shop.

Next time you sip tea, remember it shares the same Chinese root with чай.

That connection is your secret shortcut.
