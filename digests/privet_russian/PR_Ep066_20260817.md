### Word of the Day
**мама**
Transliteration: MA-ma  
English meaning: mom  
Example sentence: Моя мама любит чай.  
Example translation: My mom loves tea.  
Memory hook: It sounds almost exactly like the English “mama” — the first word many babies say everywhere!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
In Russian, family words often change slightly depending on who they belong to. We use “мой” for “my” with masculine words (мой брат) and “моя” for feminine words (моя сестра). It works like English “my,” but you must match the ending to the word’s gender. Example: Мой папа (my dad) vs. Моя мама (my mom). This small change makes your sentences sound natural and correct.

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: брат**
This word started in the ancient Proto-Indo-European language spoken thousands of years ago as *bʰréh₂tēr. It travelled through Slavic languages and arrived in modern Russian unchanged in its core sound. English “brother” comes from exactly the same ancient root, so the two words are true cousins! But watch out for the false friend — in some Slavic languages “brat” can also mean “buddy,” which might surprise English speakers. Here’s a quick memory trick: picture two brothers sharing a “brat”wurst at a family picnic — the shared sound helps you remember they mean the same thing.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, families often gather for big Sunday lunches where three generations sit together. Grandparents (бабушка and дедушка) are very involved in raising children, and it is common for adult children to visit their parents every weekend.

**Practice Challenge:**
For kids: Draw your family and label each person with the new Russian words (мама, папа, брат, etc.). For adults: Introduce your family out loud in Russian — “Это моя мама. Это мой папа.” — and say it three times. Remember поезде from a few lessons ago? Now try: Моя семья едет на поезде!
