Russian nouns have gender, and adjectives must match that gender.

Bread is masculine, so we say вкусный хлеб for delicious bread.

Food is feminine, so we say вкусная еда for delicious food.

Milk is neuter, so we say вкусное молоко for delicious milk.

English does not change adjectives this way.

Russian adds a small ending to show the match.

It works like a fun matching game once you hear the pattern.

In Russia bread receives great respect and is never thrown away.

Families often share one loaf at the table.

Offering bread and salt is a traditional welcome to guests.

Now here is something really cool about one of today’s words.

Want to know a secret about сахар?

The Russian word сахар and the English word sugar are actually the same word deep down.

They both come from ancient Sanskrit śarkarā meaning grit or gravel.

The word travelled through Persian and Arabic into European languages around the twelfth century.

It started in India, moved west with traders, and landed in Russian kitchens.

Be careful though, because the Russian сахар sounds nothing like the false friend saccharin in English, which is a completely different artificial sweetener.

Next time you see sugar on a packet, remember it is cousins with Russian сахар.

They both come from the same ancient grit, and that connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.
