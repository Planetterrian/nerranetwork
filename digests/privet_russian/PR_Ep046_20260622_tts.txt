Our word of the day is хлеб.

Khlyeb.

It means bread.

Think of it like the word loaf, stretched a little in your mind.

Russians often treat хлеб as something basic and essential, just like we say bread and butter.

Here is a simple sentence.

Я ем хлеб.

Ya yem khlyeb.

That means I eat bread.

Now for a quick grammar bite.

In Russian, adjectives must match the gender of the noun.

Хлеб вкусный uses the masculine ending.

Вода вкусная uses the feminine ending.

Яблоко вкусное uses the neuter ending.

English keeps the adjective the same no matter what.

Russian adds ый, ая, or ое so the words agree.

Now you can describe your food correctly.

Want to know a secret about one of today's words?

The Russian word чай and the English word tea are cousins.

Both started as chá in China.

The word traveled along ancient trade routes and reached Russian in the sixteen hundreds.

English took a different path through Dutch and Portuguese traders.

Be careful though.

The word магазин sounds like magazine but means shop.

Next time you sip tea, remember it shares the same root as чай.

In Russia, bread is treated with great respect.

Families often share one big loaf at the table.

Children learn early that throwing away bread is considered unlucky.

A traditional welcome gift is a loaf of bread with salt.
