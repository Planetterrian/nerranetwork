In Russian, family words often change slightly depending on who they belong to.

We use мой for my with masculine words like мой брат.

We use моя for my with feminine words like моя сестра.

It works like English my, but the ending must match the word’s gender.

Мой папа sounds natural.

Моя мама sounds natural too.

This small change makes your sentences sound correct right away.

There's something really cool about one of today's words.

брат started thousands of years ago in an ancient language as bʰréh₂tēr.

It travelled through Slavic languages and reached modern Russian almost unchanged.

English brother comes from the exact same ancient root, so the two words are true cousins.

In some Slavic languages brat can also mean buddy, which might surprise you.

Picture two brothers sharing a bratwurst at a family picnic.

The shared sound helps you remember they mean the same thing.

In Russia families often gather for big Sunday lunches where three generations sit together.

Grandparents бабушка and дедушка are very involved in raising children.

It is common for adult children to visit their parents every weekend.
