### Word of the Day
**Дом** (dom)
Pronunciation: dohm (rhymes with “home”)  
English meaning: house or home  
Example sentence: Мой дом большой.  
English translation: My house is big.  
Memory hook: Think of “dom” as the dome that shelters your whole family — your own little kingdom!  
Repeat after me: Дом… дом.

**Grammar Spotlight:**
In Russian, we use the preposition “в” (in) with the prepositional case to say where something is. For example: В комнате (in the room), на кухне (in the kitchen — note the special ending for кухня). Compare to English “in the room.” Russian changes the ending of the noun, while English usually stays the same.

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: окно**
This word started in ancient Slavic as “oko,” meaning eye, because a window lets light into the house just like an eye lets light into the body. It travelled through Old Russian and kept its simple form while English “window” came from a completely different phrase meaning “wind eye.” But be careful — the Russian “око” is an old poetic word for eye, not the everyday “глаз.” Here’s a memory trick: picture the window as the house’s watchful eye, always open to the world outside.

**Cultural Corner:**
In many Russian homes, the kitchen is the heart of family life where people gather for tea and long talks, even more than the living room. Guests are often welcomed straight into the kitchen, showing that warmth and hospitality matter more than fancy furniture.
