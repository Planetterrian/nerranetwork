**Grammar Spotlight:**
We are looking at simple present-tense weather sentences using "есть" (to be) implied or "идёт/дует" for actions. Russian often drops the verb "is" in descriptions. Examples: "Солнце светит" (The sun shines), "Дождь идёт" (Rain goes/It rains), "Ветер дует" (Wind blows). Unlike English which always needs "is raining," Russian uses action verbs directly — shorter and punchy!

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: Небо**
Did you know that the Russian word небо and the English word "nebula" are actually cousins from the same ancient root? They both come from the Proto-Indo-European word for "cloud" or "mist" that travelled through Latin and Greek into English scientific terms while staying everyday-simple in Russian. This word started in ancient roots describing the misty dome above, moved through Slavic languages unchanged for centuries, and kept its sky meaning while English cousins turned more starry. But be careful — don't mix it with "heaven" which is "рай" in Russian! Next time you look up at the небо, remember it's linked to nebula — your secret shortcut to picturing the cloudy sky forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia people love talking about the weather because winters are long and snowy while summers can be surprisingly hot. Russians often say "погода шепчет" when the weather is just right for a walk, showing how weather is part of daily chats and nature appreciation.
