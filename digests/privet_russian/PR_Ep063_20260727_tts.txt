Now for a quick grammar bite.

In Russian we use the little word в to show you are going somewhere.

It works like the English word to.

You say Я еду в Москву.

That means I am going to Moscow.

Or Мы идём в отель.

That means we are going to the hotel.

The ending of the place often changes a little, but the word в stays the same.

Want to know a secret about one of today's words?

The Russian word паспорт and the English word passport are actually the same word.

They both come from the French passeport, which meant pass through the port.

The word travelled across Europe with traders and sailors.

It reached Russian in the seventeen hundreds when Peter the Great opened Russia to the world.

Be careful though. The Russian word магазин sounds like magazine but really means shop.

Next time you hold your passport, remember it shares a story with Russian паспорт.

That connection started in medieval French and gives you a secret shortcut for remembering the word forever.

Russians love long train journeys across their huge country.

The famous Trans-Siberian Railway takes seven days from Moscow to Vladivostok.

Families often bring big чемоданы full of food for the trip.

The journey crosses many time zones and vast landscapes.

Passengers spend days watching the scenery change outside the window.

These long rides turn travel into a shared family experience with plenty of snacks along the way.
