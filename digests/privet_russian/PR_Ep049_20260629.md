### Word of the Day
**Хлеб**
Transliteration: khlyeb  
English meaning: bread  
Example sentence: Я люблю хлеб. (Ya lyublyu khlyeb.)  
English translation: I love bread.  
Memory hook: Imagine a “loaf” of bread sitting on the table — the “kh” sound is like a friendly cough before you take a bite!  
Repeat after me: khlyeb… khlyeb… now say it faster: khlyeb!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
Russian nouns have gender (masculine, feminine, neuter) and adjectives must match.  
- Masculine: вкусный хлеб (delicious bread)
- Feminine: вкусная еда (delicious food)
- Neuter: вкусное молоко (delicious milk)
English doesn’t change adjectives this way, so Russian adds a little ending to show agreement — once you hear the pattern it feels like a fun matching game!

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: сахар**
Did you know the Russian word сахар (SA-khar) and the English word “sugar” are actually the same word deep down? They both come from ancient Sanskrit “śarkarā” meaning “grit” or “gravel,” which travelled through Persian and Arabic into European languages around the 12th century. This word started in India, moved west with traders, and finally landed in Russian kitchens. But be careful — the Russian word “сахар” sounds nothing like the false friend “saccharin” in English, which is a completely different artificial sweetener! Next time you see “sugar” on a packet, remember it’s cousins with Russian сахар — they both come from the same ancient grit, and that connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, bread is treated with great respect — it is considered almost sacred and is never thrown away. Families often share a loaf at the table, and offering bread and salt is a traditional welcome to guests.
