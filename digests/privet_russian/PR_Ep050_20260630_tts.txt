The simple sentence is Я лечу на самолёте. Ya le-choo na sa-ma-LYOT-e. I am flying on an airplane. Repeat after me. Sa-ma-LYOT.

In Russian, many travel words change their endings depending on whether you are going to a place or already at it. This is the prepositional case. English uses in, on, or at with the same word. Russian adds endings like e or u. For example, в самолёте means in the airplane. В поезде means on the train. В отеле means in the hotel.

It feels like giving the word a little travel sticker so everyone knows where you are.

Russians love long train journeys across their huge country. The famous Trans-Siberian Railway takes seven days from Moscow to Vladivostok. Families often bring tea and snacks to share with new friends in the compartment, turning travel into a warm social adventure.

Now, here's something really cool about one of today's words. Want to know a secret about чемодан? Did you know that the Russian word чемодан and the French word valise are cousins from the same journey? This word started in Turkish as çanta meaning bag, travelled through French valise, and finally arrived in Russian in the eighteenth century as чемодан. But be careful.

The Russian word магазин sounds like magazine but actually means shop. Next time you see a suitcase, remember it is cousins with чемодан. They both come from a Turkish root. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.
