Now for a quick grammar bite that will help you use these words right away.

Russian nouns have three genders: masculine, feminine, and neuter.

This decides how small words like "this" change their endings.

For example, we say Эта звезда because звезда is feminine.

We say этот спутник because спутник is masculine.

And we say это небо because небо is neuter.

Unlike English, which uses the same "the" or "a" for everything, Russian shows the gender through the ending sounds.

Now you can point at things and say это for neuter words like the moon, and it will feel natural.

They both come from the idea of a traveling companion.

This word started in old Slavic languages meaning a fellow traveler or someone who goes along with you.

It rocketed into global fame in nineteen fifty-seven when the Soviet Union launched the first artificial satellite and simply called it Sputnik.

English speakers borrowed the word directly because nothing else captured that exciting new invention.

But be careful, because in everyday Russian, спутник can also just mean a human friend traveling with you, not only a space machine.

Next time you see the word satellite or hear about old space news, remember it is cousins with спутник.
