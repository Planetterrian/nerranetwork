**Grammar Spotlight:**
Noun gender in Russian. Every noun is masculine, feminine, or neuter.  
- хлеб is masculine (ends in consonant)
- яблоко is neuter (ends in -о)
- молоко is also neuter.
Unlike English, Russian adjectives must match the noun’s gender: большое яблоко (big apple), but большой сыр (big cheese). It feels like the words are holding hands!

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: чай**
Did you know the Russian word чай and the English word “tea” are actually cousins from the same ancient root? This word started in China as “chá,” travelled along the Silk Road through Persian and Turkish traders, and finally arrived in Russian in the 17th century. But be careful — the Russian word “магазин” sounds like “magazine” but actually means “shop”! Next time you sip tea, remember it’s cousins with чай — they both come from the same Chinese root. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians love sharing food, especially bread and tea. When guests arrive, hosts always offer чай with something sweet. Saying “приятного аппетита!” (bon appétit) before eating shows you care about the people at the table.
