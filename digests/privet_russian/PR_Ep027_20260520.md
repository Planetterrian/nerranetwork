**Grammar Spotlight:**
Russian nouns change endings depending on whether they are the subject or object of a sentence (called cases). For example, "хлеб" becomes "хлеба" when saying "I eat bread." Compare to English, where word order usually shows meaning instead of endings. This helps Russian sentences stay flexible while keeping the same idea.

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: магазин**
Did you know that the Russian word магазин and the English word magazine are actually the same word? They both come from the Arabic "makhzan," meaning a storehouse or treasury. This word started in Arabic, travelled through Persian and Turkish trade routes, and finally arrived in Russian in the 17th century as a place to keep goods. But be careful — the Russian word 'магазин' sounds like 'magazine' but actually means 'shop'! Next time you see a magazine, remember it's cousins with магазин — they both come from the same root for storing things. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, bread is more than food — it is a symbol of hospitality and respect. Guests are often welcomed with bread and salt on a special embroidered cloth. This tradition shows how sharing simple foods brings people together.
