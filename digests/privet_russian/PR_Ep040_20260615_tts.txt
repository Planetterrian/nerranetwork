In Russian we often use the word na when we talk about traveling by something.

Na means by or on for vehicles.

For example, na poyezde means by train.

Na samolyote means by airplane.

The ending of the word changes after na, but the idea stays simple.

Now you can say ya yedu na poyezde and we fly by airplane, or my letim na samolyote.

Now here is something really cool about one of today's words.

Want to know a secret about bilet?

Did you know that the Russian word билет and the English word ticket are actually cousins?

They both come from the same old French root that started as a little Latin label.

The word traveled through French as billet and reached Russia in the seventeen hundreds when Peter the Great was improving travel.

Watch out though, because билет can also mean an exam paper in school, so the sentence around it tells you if it is for a train or a test.

Next time you hold a ticket, remember it shares a story with билет, and that little Latin label is your secret shortcut to remembering the word forever.
