**Grammar Spotlight:**
Russian adjectives like "вкусный" (delicious) change their ending to match the noun they describe. For masculine nouns (like хлеб), it ends in -ый. For neuter nouns (like яблоко), it becomes "вкусное". This is similar to English where we just say "delicious bread" or "delicious apple" without changes, but Russian adds the matching ending so the words "agree" — like best friends dressing alike!

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: чай**
Did you know that the Russian word чай and the English word tea are actually the same word? They both come from the ancient Chinese word for tea, "chá." This word started in China thousands of years ago, then travelled along the Silk Road: one path went north through Central Asia into Russian as "чай" around the 1600s, while another path went west by sea through Portuguese and Dutch traders into English as "tea." But be careful — the Russian word "магазин" sounds like the English "magazine" but actually means "shop," a fun false friend that can surprise you in a grocery store! Next time you see the English word tea, remember it's cousins with the Russian чай — they both come from the same Chinese root. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, tea is more than a drink — it's a warm way to welcome guests and share stories. Families often gather around a samovar (a special tea urn) for hours, sipping чай with jam or cookies, showing how hospitality and conversation are at the heart of Russian daily life.
