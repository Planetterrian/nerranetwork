**Grammar Spotlight:**
The question word “где” (where) helps you find places while traveling. It usually comes at the start of a question.  
Examples: Где вокзал? (Where is the train station?) Где мой чемодан? (Where is my suitcase?)  
In English we also start with “where,” but Russian often drops the verb “is” — so “Где вокзал?” is shorter and snappier than “Where is the train station?”

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: билет**
Did you know the Russian word билет and the English word “ticket” are actually cousins from the same French root? They both come from the Old French “billet,” meaning a short note or document. This word started in Latin as “bulla” (a seal or document), travelled through French, and arrived in Russian in the 1700s when Peter the Great opened Russia to European travel. But be careful — a false friend lurks nearby: Russian “билет” can also mean an exam “ticket” or test paper, not just travel! Next time you buy a ticket, remember it’s cousins with билет — they both come from the same Latin seal, your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, long train journeys are a beloved tradition — people bring tea in special glasses with metal holders and chat for hours. The famous Trans-Siberian Railway crosses eight time zones, making it the ultimate adventure!
