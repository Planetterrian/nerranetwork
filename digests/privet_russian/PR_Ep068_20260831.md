# Привет, Русский! — Episode Plan

### Word of the Day
**Учитель**
Transliteration: oo-CHEE-tyel  
English meaning: teacher  
Example sentence: Мой учитель говорит по-русски.  
Example translation: My teacher speaks Russian.  
Memory hook: Imagine your teacher cheering “You can do it!” — the “cheer” sound in “учитель” helps you remember the friendly guide at school.  
Repeat after me: учитель… (pause) учитель.

**Grammar Spotlight:**
Today we look at the verb “читать” (to read) in the present tense. Russian verbs change endings depending on who is doing the action.  
- Я читаю (I read)
- Ты читаешь (you read)
- Он/она читает (he/she reads)
Unlike English, which often keeps the verb the same (“I read, you read”), Russian adds different endings so the listener instantly knows who is acting.

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: школа**
No way — the Russian word for school started life meaning “free time”! It began in ancient Greek as skholē, which first described leisure or spare time for thinking and discussion. Greek scholars passed it to Latin as schola, then it travelled into Old Russian around the 17th century through Western European languages. But be careful — in English we also say “school,” yet the Russian version kept the original Greek “skh” sound while English softened it. Here’s a memory trick: picture Greek philosophers relaxing in their skholē, then suddenly deciding to turn their free time into a classroom — that same peaceful spot became школа!

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, the first day of school on September 1 is called “День знаний” (Knowledge Day). Children bring flowers to their teachers and the whole school gathers for a special assembly to celebrate the start of the new learning year.
