Russian nouns have genders that change the words around them.

Most animal names ending in a consonant like кот are masculine.

Words like птица and рыба are feminine.

In English we rarely mark gender on nouns.

In Russian you say большой кот for big tomcat but большая птица for big bird.

Now you can say мой кот for my cat in the masculine form.

Or моя рыба for my fish in the feminine form.

That small change makes your sentences sound natural.

Want to know a secret about one of today's words?

Медведь and the English idea of honey-eater are secretly cousins.

This word started in ancient Slavic roots.

Мёд meant honey.

Ведать meant to know.

So медведь literally means the one who knows honey.

It was a polite way to avoid saying the real bear name and angering the animal.

The word travelled through old Slavic languages.

It never borrowed from Latin ursus like most European tongues.

Both cultures used sweet nicknames to talk about the scary forest giant.

Next time you see honey, remember it is cousins with медведь.
