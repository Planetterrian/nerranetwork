Planes land at аэропорт. Ah-eh-ro-PORT. Airport. The plane is flying to the airport is Самолёт летит в аэропорт. Remember летает from earlier lessons? It helps here because planes летает to the аэропорт.

You need a паспорт to travel far. PAS-port. Passport. I have a passport is У меня есть паспорт. Easy to remember because it sounds almost the same as the English word.

Your clothes and books go into a чемодан. Cheh-mo-DAN. Suitcase. I put things in the suitcase is Я кладу вещи в чемодан.

To reach the вокзал many people use a машина. Ma-SHEE-na. Car. We are going by car is Мы едем на машине.

A big trip is called путешествие. Poo-teh-SHEST-vee-ye. Journey or trip. The journey to Moscow was long is Путешествие в Москву было долгим.

Your things together are багаж. Ba-GAZH. Luggage. My luggage is heavy is Мой багаж тяжёлый. It sounds like baggage and carries the same idea.

You find your way with a карта. KAR-ta. Map. I am looking at the map is Я смотрю на карту.

Now for one grammar idea that makes travel talk much easier. Russian uses different verbs for different ways of moving. Я еду на поезде means I am going by train. Мы летим на самолёте means we are flying by plane. Ты идёшь пешком means you are walking. Each method gets its own verb, so your sentence tells the listener exactly how the trip happens.

There is something really cool about one of today's words. Вокзал began as the name of a London pleasure garden called Vauxhall. English engineers brought the sound to Russia when the first big train stations were built, and Russians made it their own word for any major railway station. It is a fun borrowing that now feels completely Russian. Just remember it is not a park anymore.

Picture a grand station with a big V that Russians pronounced their way, and the word sticks.

Russians enjoy long train rides, especially on the Trans-Siberian Railway that crosses the whole country. Families often pack big samovars of tea and play cards for days, so the journey itself becomes part of the fun.
