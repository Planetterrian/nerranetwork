### Word of the Day
**школа** (shkó-la)
English meaning: school  
Example sentence: Я иду в школу. (Ya idú v shkólu.) — I am going to school.  
Memory hook: Imagine a “school” of fish swimming together — the Russian word sounds almost the same and means the place where kids learn together!  
Repeat after me: shkó-la… shkó-la… now say it at normal speed: shkóla!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
In Russian, many verbs change their ending depending on who is doing the action. For “to read,” we say я читаю (ya chitáyu — I read) but он читает (on chitáyet — he reads). It is like English “I read” vs. “he reads,” but Russian changes the ending every time. Listen: я читаю… он читает. Try it: ya chitáyu… on chitáyet.

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: книга**
Did you know that the Russian word книга and the English word “book” are actually cousins through an ancient journey? This word started in ancient Greek as “biblion,” travelled through Old Church Slavonic, and finally settled in Russian centuries ago. Along the way it picked up the soft “kn” sound we hear today. But be careful — the Russian word “книга” looks nothing like “book,” yet both mean the same thing and share that hidden Greek root! Next time you open a book, remember its Russian cousin книга — they both come from the same ancient word for “papyrus scroll.” That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, children start school at age six or seven and often stay in the same class with the same teacher for the first four years. This creates a very close “family” feeling inside the classroom, and kids celebrate the first day of school, called “День знаний,” with big bouquets of flowers for their teachers.
