Now for a quick grammar bite.

Russian adjectives must match the gender of the noun they describe.

Большое дерево ends in oye because дерево is neuter.

Длинная река ends in aya because река is feminine.

Высокая гора also ends in aya.

Unlike English, where big stays the same, Russian makes the adjective agree so the sentence sounds natural.

Russians have a deep love for nature.

They often spend weekends in the лес or at an озеро.

Many songs and poems celebrate the beauty of the Russian landscape, especially the white birches in famous paintings and fairy tales.

For our practice challenge, kids can draw a лес with a река and three цветки, then label each in Russian and say the words out loud.

Adults, look out your window or take a short walk and describe three things using today's words, like Это дерево or Небо синее.

Now here's something really cool about one of today's words.

Want to know a secret about цветок?

Did you know that the Russian word цветок and the English word color are actually cousins?

They both come from the ancient root meaning to shine or be bright.

This word travelled through Slavic languages and became tsvet for color, then grew into tsvetok for flower because flowers are nature's brightest colors.

Be careful though.

Цвет sounds like it could mean color in English, yet it means both color and the blooming flower.

Next time you see a colorful flower, remember it is cousins with цветок.

They both come from that shining root.

That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.
