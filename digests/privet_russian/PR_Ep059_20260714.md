**Grammar Spotlight:**
In Russian, weather often uses impersonal constructions with “it” understood but not said. “Сегодня тепло” literally means “Today [is] warm” — no subject needed. Compare to English “It is warm today.”  
Examples:  
- Холодно. (It is cold.)
- Дождь идёт. (Rain goes = It is raining.)
- Ветер сильный. (Wind [is] strong.)

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: ветер**
Did you know that the Russian word ветер and the English word “wind” are actually cousins from the same ancient root? They both come from the Proto-Indo-European *weh- meaning “to blow.” This word started in that ancient language, travelled through Germanic tongues to become English “wind,” and through Slavic branches to become Russian ветер.  
But be careful — the Russian word “ветер” sounds nothing like the false friend “weather,” which in Russian is actually погода!  
Next time you feel the wind on your face, remember it is cousins with ветер — they both come from the same blowing root. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians love talking about the weather because it changes dramatically across their huge country — from snowy winters in Siberia to sunny summers in the south. People often greet each other with “Как погода?” (“How is the weather?”) as a friendly way to start a conversation.
