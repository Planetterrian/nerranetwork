Our Word of the Day is одежда. Одежда. Say it slowly with me. О-де-ж-да. Now at normal speed. Одежда. It means clothes or clothing. Think of “a-dye-zh-da” like “a-dye-zh” – you dye your clothes different colours! Here is a simple sentence. У меня есть новая одежда. That means I have new clothes. Repeat after me. Одежда.

Now let's build more vocabulary around clothes. The Russian word for shirt is рубашка. Рубашка. Repeat after me. Рубашка. It sounds like “roo-bash-ka” – imagine a rooster wearing a bashful shirt! Here is a simple sentence. Я ношу синюю рубашку. That means I am wearing a blue shirt. Repeat after me. Рубашка. Рубашка.

Next comes брюки. Брюки. Repeat after me. Брюки. This means trousers or pants. Think of “bree-oo-ki” – trousers you wear while brewing tea. Here is a simple sentence. Мои брюки чёрные. That means my trousers are black. Repeat after me. Брюки. Брюки.

A dress is платье. Платье. Repeat after me. Платье. It sounds like “plat-yeah” – a plate of pretty fabric! Here is a simple sentence. Девочка носит красивое платье. That means the girl is wearing a beautiful dress. Repeat after me. Платье. Платье.

Footwear or shoes is обувь. Обувь. Repeat after me. Обувь. Say “o-boov” like “oh, boov” – boots that make you boogie! Here is a simple sentence. Моя обувь удобная. That means my shoes are comfortable. Repeat after me. Обувь. Обувь.

Socks are носки. Носки. Repeat after me. Носки. “Noss-key” – they hold the key to warm toes. Here is a simple sentence. Я покупаю тёплые носки. That means I am buying warm socks. Repeat after me. Носки. Носки.

A jacket is куртка. Куртка. Repeat after me. Куртка. “Court-ka” – a jacket you wear to court or just to stay warm. Here is a simple sentence. Зимой я надеваю куртку. That means in winter I put on a jacket. Repeat after me. Куртка. Куртка.

A hat or cap is шапка. Шапка. Repeat after me. Шапка. It sounds like “shap-cap” – a shapely cap! Here is a simple sentence. Надень шапку на голову. That means put a hat on your head. Repeat after me. Шапка. Шапка.

Boots or shoes are ботинки. Ботинки. Repeat after me. Ботинки. “Ba-teen-ki” – teeny boots that make you teeny-tiny steps. Here is a simple sentence. Ботинки на ногах. That means boots are on my feet. Repeat after me. Ботинки. Ботинки.

Remember большая from before? It means big. Now for a grammar bite. In Russian adjectives change to match the noun. Look at these examples with our new words. Синяя рубашка means blue shirt. The ending on синяя matches the feminine word рубашка. Чёрные брюки means black trousers. The ending on чёрные matches the plural word брюки. Удобная обувь means comfortable footwear.

The ending on удобная matches the feminine word обувь. Unlike English, Russian adjectives agree with the noun. Once you spot the pattern it feels natural.

There's something really cool about one of today's words. Куртка began in French as jaquette and entered Russian in the eighteenth century when French fashion words arrived in big numbers. The surprise is that Russian borrowed many clothing terms from French during Peter the Great's era. Picture a French courtier handing you a “court-ka” jacket. That picture locks the sound in your mind.

Russians wear a шапка and warm felt boots in winter because the cold is serious. Bright scarves and gloves make popular gifts. Children hear their babushka say “Надень шапку!” which means put on your hat.

For the practice challenge, kids can draw themselves wearing three new items and label them рубашка, брюки, and шапка. Adults can look in the wardrobe and say out loud “У меня есть…” plus three clothing words they see.
