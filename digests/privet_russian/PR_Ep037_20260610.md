**Grammar Spotlight:**
Russian nouns have gender (masculine, feminine, or neuter). Most animal names ending in -а are feminine like кошка and собака. We use моя for “my” with feminine words: моя кошка. Compare to English where “my” stays the same for everything. Examples: Моя кошка (my cat), Моя собака (my dog), but Мой медведь (my bear — masculine).

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: Кошка**
Did you know that the Russian word кошка and the English word “cat” are actually cousins from the same ancient root? They both come from a very old sound people made to call cats — something like “katt” or “kadu” thousands of years ago in early Indo-European languages. This word traveled through Latin (cattus) into many European languages, while in Russian it became кошка, keeping that soft “k” sound. But be careful — the Russian word “мышь” sounds a bit like “mouse” and means exactly the same thing, yet “мышь” can also trick you because it ends with a soft sign that changes the pronunciation! Next time you see the English word “cat,” remember it’s cousins with кошка — they both come from that ancient calling sound. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, many families keep cats as beloved pets, and you will often see stray cats cared for in courtyards and parks. Russians have a special saying: “Без кошки дом — не дом,” which means “A house without a cat is not a home,” showing how much they value these furry friends.
