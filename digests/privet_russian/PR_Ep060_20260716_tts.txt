Russian nouns have genders that change word endings.

Яблоко ends in o and is neuter.

Сыр ends in a consonant and is masculine.

Молоко also ends in o and is neuter.

English does not change endings for gender.

We just say the red apple or the red cheese.

In Russian you say вкусное яблоко for the tasty apple.

You say вкусный сыр for the tasty cheese.

Now here's something really cool about one of today's words.

Want to know a secret about чай?

Did you know that the Russian word чай and the English word tea are actually cousins from the same ancient root?

They both come from the Chinese word chá.

The word travelled along the Silk Road to Persian and Turkish traders.

Then it reached Russian around the seventeenth century when tea became popular in Moscow.

But be careful.

The Russian word магазин sounds like magazine but actually means shop.

Next time you see tea on a menu, remember it is cousins with чай.

They both come from Chinese chá.

That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

In Russia, tea is more than a drink.

It is a daily ritual shared with family and friends.

It is often served with jam, lemon, or sweets in a special teapot called a samovar.

Offering tea to guests is a warm sign of hospitality that has lasted for centuries.
