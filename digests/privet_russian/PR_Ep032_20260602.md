### Word of the Day
**космос** (KOS-mos) — space
Example sentence: Я смотрю на космос. (Ya SMOT-ryu na KOS-mos.) — I look at space.  
Memory hook: It sounds exactly like the English “cosmos,” so you already know it!  
Repeat after me: космос… (pause) …космос!
- Russian (Cyrillic): звезда
  Transliteration: zvyez-DA  
  English: star  
  Example sentence: Звезда яркая.  
  Example translation: The star is bright.  
  Memory hook: It sounds like “star” with a fun “zvy” beginning.  

- Russian (Cyrillic): ракета
  Transliteration: ra-KYE-ta  
  English: rocket  
  Example sentence: Ракета летит в космос.  
  Example translation: The rocket flies into space.  
  Memory hook: Sounds like “rocket” — almost the same word!  

- Russian (Cyrillic): луна
  Transliteration: lu-NA  
  English: moon  
  Example sentence: Луна круглая.  
  Example translation: The moon is round.  
  Memory hook: Think of “lunar” — same root.

  Transliteration: pla-NYE-ta

  Example sentence: Земля — это планета.

  Transliteration: SOLN-tse

  Transliteration: kos-mo-NAVT

  Memory hook: “Cosmo” + “naut” = space sailor!  

**Grammar Spotlight:**
In Russian, many space words are feminine and end in -а or -я. For example: ракета (rocket), звезда (star), and планета (planet) are all feminine. We say “большая ракета” (big rocket) because the adjective must match the noun’s gender. English doesn’t change adjectives for gender, but Russian does — it’s like giving every word a tiny team jersey!

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: ракета**
Did you know that the Russian word ракета and the English word “rocket” are actually the same word? They both come from Italian “rocchetta,” meaning “little spindle,” because early rockets looked like small spools of thread. This word started in Italy in the 1500s, travelled through French and German, and arrived in both Russian and English around the same time. But be careful — the Russian word “ракета” can also mean a badminton shuttlecock in some contexts, creating a fun “gotcha!” moment! Next time you see a rocket launch on TV, remember it’s cousins with ракета — they both come from that little Italian spindle. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians are very proud of their space history. The first human in space was cosmonaut Yuri Gagarin in 1961, and every year on April 12, Russia celebrates Cosmonautics Day with special TV shows and museum visits. Many Russian children dream of becoming космонавты because space exploration is taught as a national achievement.
