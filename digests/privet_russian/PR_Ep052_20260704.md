**Grammar Spotlight:**
Adjectives must match the gender of the noun they describe. In Russian, “небо” (sky) is neuter, so we say “голубое небо” (blue sky). “Дождь” (rain) is masculine, so “сильный дождь” (strong rain). English does not change adjective endings, but Russian adds -ое, -ый, or -ая to show the match — a small ending that makes your sentences sound correct!

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: Небо**
Did you know that the Russian word “небо” and the English word “nebula” are actually the same word? They both come from the ancient Latin “nebula,” meaning cloud or mist. This word started in Latin, travelled through scientific writing into English as “nebula” for starry clouds, and stayed in Russian as the everyday word for the sky above us. But be careful — the Russian word “небо” also means “heaven,” creating a lovely double meaning that English keeps separate! Next time you look up at the sky, remember “небо” is cousins with “nebula” — they both come from the same misty Latin root. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians love talking about the weather because winters are long and cold while summers can be hot and sunny. People often greet each other with “Как погода?” (How is the weather?) and use it as a friendly way to start a conversation, just like saying hello!
