### Word of the Day
**Russian (Cyrillic):** жара
**Transliteration:** zha-RA
**English meaning:** heat (especially hot weather or a heatwave)
**Simple example sentence:** Сегодня сильная жара.
**English translation:** Today there is strong heat.
**Memorable hook:** Imagine the sun turning your backyard into a giant frying pan — that’s жара!
**Repeat after me:** zha-RA… zha-RA… (pause) Сегодня жара!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
Adjectives change slightly to match the noun they describe (gender agreement). In Russian we say “сильный ветер” (strong wind — masculine) but “сильная жара” (strong heat — feminine). It’s like giving each word a tiny matching outfit so everything sounds smooth together. Compare to English where adjectives stay the same: “strong wind” or “strong heat.”

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: температура**
Did you know that the Russian word температура and the English word temperature are actually the same word? They both come from the Latin “temperatura,” meaning a proper mixture or balance. This word started in ancient Latin, travelled through scientific writing in Europe, and was borrowed into both Russian and English in the 1700s when people began measuring heat with thermometers. But be careful — the Russian word “магазин” sounds like “magazine” but actually means “shop”! Next time you see the word temperature on a weather app, remember it’s cousins with температура — they both come from the same Latin root. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians love talking about the weather because it changes so dramatically — from freezing winters to sudden summer heatwaves. In many families, the first thing people say when they meet is “Как погода?” (How’s the weather?) as a friendly way to start a conversation.
