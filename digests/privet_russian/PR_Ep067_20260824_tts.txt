Our word of the day is пингвин. Пингвин. It sounds like PEENG-veen. This word means penguin. Imagine a penguin waddling like a little black-and-white tuxedo bird. Repeat after me. Пингвин. Here is a simple sentence with our word. Пингвин плавает в воде. The penguin swims in the water. You can picture the black-and-white bird moving through the ocean waves using this exact phrase.

The transliteration PEENG-veen helps English speakers say it correctly on the first try. Listeners often find the memory hook of a tuxedo bird makes the meaning easy to recall during practice.

Now let's build more vocabulary with animals you might see in stories or at the zoo. Remember корова from a few lessons ago. It moos ka-RO-va and means cow. Корова даёт молоко. The cow gives milk. This review word connects to the sound it makes when spoken slowly. The example shows how the animal provides something useful every day.

You can repeat the full sentence to practice both the noun and the action it performs.

Next comes птица. Птица. It sounds like PTEET-sa. This word means bird. Птица летает в небе. The bird flies in the sky. The memory hook that links it to the English word “tweet” fits the way birds communicate. Speakers use this term for any feathered creature moving overhead. Repeating the sentence helps lock in both the pronunciation and the action described.

Рыба comes next. Рыба. It sounds like RY-ba. This word means fish. Рыба плавает в озере. The fish swims in the lake. The shorter sound similar to “river” reminds learners that fish belong in water. The example sentence places the animal in a calm body of water. Practicing the transliteration RY-ba builds confidence with the soft ending sound.

Заяц sounds bouncy, just like the animal itself. Заяц. ZA-yats. This word means hare. Заяц прыгает быстро. The hare jumps quickly. The memory hook highlights the lively motion that matches the word’s rhythm. The sentence focuses on speed as the key trait of this animal. Listeners can say the phrase aloud to feel the energetic quality in the pronunciation.

Волк is our next animal. Волк. It sounds like volk. This word means wolf. Волк живёт в лесу. The wolf lives in the forest. The memory hook connects it to the idea of a folk group that stays together. The example shows the animal’s natural home among trees. Repeating the full sentence reinforces the location and the noun together.

Медведь follows right after. Медведь. It sounds like med-VYED'. This word means bear. Медведь ест мёд. The bear eats honey. The memory hook explains that “med” means honey, so the full word describes the one who knows where to find it. The sentence gives a clear action the animal performs. The transliteration med-VYED' captures the stress on the second syllable for accurate speech.

Собака is a friendly one. Собака. It sounds like sa-BA-ka. This word means dog. Собака бежит быстро. The dog runs fast. The memory hook of “so bark-a” matches the sound dogs make. The example highlights movement as a common dog behavior. Practicing the sentence helps combine the noun with a simple verb.

Кошка closes our list for today. Кошка. It sounds like KOSH-ka. This word means cat. Кошка спит на окне. The cat sleeps on the window. The memory hook describes the cozy curled-up position cats often take. The sentence places the animal in a common indoor spot. Repeating the phrase builds familiarity with the soft ending sound.

Russian nouns have three genders. Most animal words ending in a consonant are masculine, like волк. Words ending in а are usually feminine, like корова. Look at these examples. Волк большой. The wolf is big. That ending shows masculine. Корова большая. The cow is big. That ending shows feminine. This is different from English, where adjectives stay the same.

The pattern appears clearly when the same adjective changes its ending to match the noun.

Let me share something really cool about one of today's words. The word медведь started long ago in Slavic languages as a polite nickname. People once feared the real name of the bear, so they called it the one who knows honey. Мед means honey and вед means knower. English bear comes from a completely different root that means brown. That makes it a fun false friend in sound and meaning.

Picture a bear sniffing out honey like a detective who always finds the sweet stuff. That picture helps the word stick. The breakdown into two clear parts gives learners a reliable way to remember the origin each time they say it.

In Russian fairy tales the bear often appears as a strong but kind forest character. Stories like Masha and the Bear show the bear as big, a little clumsy, and friendly. The animal lives in the woods and interacts with the main hero in helpful or testing ways. Listeners recognize this figure from traditional tales passed down through generations.

The description fits the gentle yet powerful image Russians associate with the word.
