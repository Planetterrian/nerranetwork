Russian nouns carry gender, and that changes how we describe them. Кит is masculine, so the matching word for big becomes большой. Кошка is feminine, so the same idea becomes большая. A big whale is большой кит. A big cat is большая кошка. The ending shifts to match the noun. Большое works for neutral words like животное. This simple match lets you build correct descriptions right away.

In Russian fairy tales the giant whale often carries whole villages on its back. People long ago pictured the whale as a magical helper rising from the sea. That image still appears in stories today.

Want to know a secret about one of today's words? The Russian word кит and the English scientific term cetacean both trace back to the same ancient Greek root. That root meant sea monster. The word traveled through Latin into Russian as кит, while English kept it mainly in science. Watch out though.

Кит sounds a little like the English word kit, yet it names a giant whale instead of a small toolbox. Whenever you meet cetacean in English, recall its cousin кит from the Greek sea-monster root. That link gives you an instant memory shortcut.
