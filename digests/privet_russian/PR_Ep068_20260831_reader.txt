Let's look at one simple grammar idea using the word читать.

Russian verbs change their endings depending on who is doing the action.

Я читаю means I read.

Ты читаешь means you read.

Он читает or она читает means he reads or she reads.

The ending tells you right away who is reading.

That is different from English, where the verb often stays the same.

There's something really cool about one of today's words that takes us back in time.

The Russian word школа once meant free time.

It started in ancient Greek as skholē, describing leisure for thinking and talking.

Greek ideas moved into Latin as schola, then reached Old Russian in the seventeenth century.

English kept a softer sound while Russian kept the original skh sound.

Picture Greek thinkers turning their quiet free time into lively classrooms.

That same peaceful idea became the слово школа we use today.

In Russia the first day of school on September first is called День знаний.

Children bring flowers to their teachers.

The whole school gathers for a special assembly to welcome the new year of learning.
