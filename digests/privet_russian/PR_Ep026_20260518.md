# Привет, Русский! — Episode Plan
- Russian (Cyrillic): космос
  Transliteration: KOS-mos
  English: space
  Example sentence: Я вижу звёзды в космосе.
  Example translation: I see stars in space.
  Memory hook: Sounds exactly like the English word "cosmos" — the universe!
- Russian (Cyrillic): ракета
  Transliteration: ra-KE-ta
  English: rocket
  Example sentence: Ракета летит в небо.
  Example translation: The rocket flies into the sky.
  Memory hook: Imagine a rocket racing like a fast "racket" in a game!
- Russian (Cyrillic): звезда
  Transliteration: zvez-DA
  English: star
  Example sentence: Звезда яркая ночью.
  Example translation: The star is bright at night.
  Memory hook: Think of "zvezda" twinkling like a dazzling "star" in the sky.
- Russian (Cyrillic): планета
  Transliteration: pla-NE-ta
  English: planet
  Example sentence: Земля — это планета.
  Example translation: Earth is a planet.
  Memory hook: "Planet" sounds almost the same in English — easy to remember!
- Russian (Cyrillic): луна
  Transliteration: lu-NA
  English: moon
  Example sentence: Луна светит ночью.
  Example translation: The moon shines at night.
  Memory hook: "Luna" is like the name of the moon in stories and songs.
- Russian (Cyrillic): солнце
  Transliteration: SOLN-tse
  English: sun
  Example sentence: Солнце теплое.
  Example translation: The sun is warm.
  Memory hook: Picture the sun as a big, glowing "sol" like solar energy.
- Russian (Cyrillic): земля
  Transliteration: zem-LYA
  English: earth
  Example sentence: Мы живём на земле.
  Example translation: We live on Earth.
  Memory hook: "Zemlya" sounds like "zem" for the ground under your feet.
- Russian (Cyrillic): спутник
  Transliteration: SPUT-nik
  English: satellite
  Example sentence: Спутник летает вокруг Земли.
  Example translation: The satellite flies around Earth.
  Memory hook: The first satellite was called Sputnik — now it means any satellite!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
In Russian, nouns have genders (masculine, feminine, or neuter), which affect the endings of words. For space words, "космос" is masculine and "ракета" is feminine. This changes how we describe them, like adding "ая" for feminine words. Compare to English, where we don't change word endings for gender — Russian adds this extra pattern to make sentences flow nicely.

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: космос**
Did you know that the Russian word космос and the English word cosmos are actually the same word? They both come from the ancient Greek word "kosmos," meaning order and the universe. This word started in ancient Greece, travelled through Latin into European languages, and arrived in Russian in the 18th century as scientists began exploring the stars. But be careful — the Russian word "спутник" sounds like it could mean "sputnik" the satellite, yet it also simply means "companion" in everyday life, creating a fun gotcha moment! Next time you see the English word "cosmos," remember it's cousins with the Russian "космос" — they both come from the Greek root for the beautiful ordered universe. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians are very proud of their space history, especially Yuri Gagarin becoming the first human in space in 1961. Many families watch special TV shows about cosmonauts on Cosmonautics Day each April, celebrating how space exploration brings people together.

**Practice Challenge:**
For kids: Draw a picture of the sun, moon, and a rocket, then label them with the Russian words while saying each one out loud. For adults: Look up at the night sky tonight and practice saying "Я вижу звезду" (I see a star) and "Я вижу луну" (I see the moon) to a friend or family member.
