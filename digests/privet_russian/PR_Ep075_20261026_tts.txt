Today we step inside a cozy Russian home to unlock the everyday words that let you name the rooms and objects around you.

I'm Olya, and this is Привет, Русский! — episode seventy-five.

Our word of the day is окно.

Ok-NO.

It means window.

Imagine your window as the eye of the house. It lets light and views in just like an eye.

Я смотрю в окно.

Ya smot-RYU v ok-NO.

I am looking out the window.

Repeat after me. Ok-NO.

Now that you know окно, let's learn the word for door.

Дверь.

Dvyerr’.

Закрой дверь, пожалуйста.

Close the door, please.

It sounds a bit like dver, like a heavy wooden door you push to enter.

Repeat after me. Dvyerr’.

Next comes стол.

Stol.

It means table.

Книга лежит на столе.

The book is lying on the table.

Stol sounds like stool, but it names the flat surface where you eat or work.

Repeat after me. Stol.

Стул comes next.

Stool.

It means chair.

Сиди на стуле.

Sit on the chair.

It rhymes with stool, which makes sense for the thing you sit on.

Repeat after me. Stool.

Кровать means bed.

Kro-VAT’.

Я сплю в кровати.

I sleep in the bed.

Picture a cozy crib or cot where you rest at night.

Repeat after me. Kro-VAT’.

Лампа is our word for lamp.

LAM-pa.

Лампа светит в комнате.

The lamp shines in the room.

It sounds exactly like the English lamp, so it is easy to remember.

Repeat after me. LAM-pa.

Пол means floor.

Pol.

Кот лежит на полу.

The cat is lying on the floor.

Pol sounds like pole, yet it names the ground under your feet at home.

Repeat after me. Pol.

Потолок is the word for ceiling.

Po-to-LOK.

Потолок белый.

The ceiling is white.

Think of the top of the room that locks the walls together.

Repeat after me. Po-to-LOK.

Finally we have ванная.

Va-NNA-ya.

It means bathroom.

Я моюсь в ванной.

I wash in the bathroom.

Va-nna-ya sounds like a van full of water for bathing.

Repeat after me. Va-NNA-ya.

In Russian we use the preposition в to mean in when we talk about being inside a room.

It works like the English in, yet the noun often ends with an e or и sound.

Я в комнате.

I am in the room.

Кот в ванной.

The cat is in the bathroom.

We are in the house becomes мы в доме.

Now you can say where someone or something is.

In many Russian apartments you see heavy wooden doors and thick curtains over the windows to keep the long winters out.

Russians often say дом — это крепость, which means home is a fortress.

It shows how important a warm, safe home feels after cold months outside.

Before we practice, here is something really cool about one of today's words.

Окно started long ago as oko, the old Slavic word for eye.

People saw the window as the eye of the house that lets you look out.

The word kept that spirit through the centuries and became окно.

It looks nothing like the English window, yet both grew from the same idea of an opening to see through.

Picture your window as a giant friendly eye on the wall of your home.
