Now let us look at the verb чувствовать.

In Russian the subject changes the ending of the verb.

Я чувствую means I feel.

Ты чувствуешь means you feel.

Он or она чувствует means he or she feels.

English keeps the verb almost the same.

Russian changes the ending each time so the listener knows exactly who is feeling something.

Now you can say exactly who feels well or who feels pain.

Something really cool about one of today's words is how сердце traveled through time.

It started in ancient Proto-Slavic as sьrdьce and moved straight into modern Russian with almost no change.

It is a true cousin of the English word heart.

Both grew from the same old root that meant the centre of the body and emotions.

The Russian word looks nothing like heart on the page, yet they share that ancient idea of centre.

Picture your heart as the centre of your chest and both words grew from that same root.
