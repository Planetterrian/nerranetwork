# Привет, Русский! — Episode Plan

### Word of the Day
**хлеб**
Transliteration: khlyeb  
English meaning: bread  
Example sentence: Я ем хлеб. (Ya yem khlyeb.)  
English translation: I eat bread.  
Memory hook: It sounds a little like “loaf” if you stretch your imagination, and Russians often say “хлеб” the way English speakers say “bread and butter” for something basic and essential.  
Repeat after me: khlyeb… khlyeb.
- Russian (Cyrillic): молоко
  Transliteration: ma-la-KO  
  English: milk  
  Example sentence: Я пью молоко.  
  Example translation: I drink milk.  
  Memory hook: It starts with the same “mol” sound as “molecule,” so picture tiny milk molecules dancing in your glass.

- Russian (Cyrillic): сыр
  Transliteration: syr  
  English: cheese  
  Example sentence: Сыр вкусный.  
  Example translation: The cheese is delicious.  
  Memory hook: Say “seer” like a fortune-teller and you’ll remember the word for cheese.

- Russian (Cyrillic): фрукт
  Transliteration: frukt  
  English: fruit  
  Example sentence: Я люблю фрукт.  
  Example translation: I love fruit.  
  Memory hook: It looks and sounds almost exactly like the English word “fruit.”

- Russian (Cyrillic): овощ
  Transliteration: O-voshch  
  English: vegetable  
  Example sentence: Овощ полезный.  
  Example translation: The vegetable is healthy.  
  Memory hook: Think “oh, fresh!” when you see a bright vegetable on your plate.

- Russian (Cyrillic): яблоко
  Transliteration: YAB-la-ka  
  English: apple  
  Example sentence: Яблоко красное.  
  Example translation: The apple is red.  
  Memory hook: It starts with “yab” like “yummy bite.”

- Russian (Cyrillic): вода
  Transliteration: va-DA  
  English: water  
  Example sentence: Вода холодная.  
  Example translation: The water is cold.  
  Memory hook: It sounds like “va-DA” — imagine waving your hand and saying “da” for water.

- Russian (Cyrillic): мясо
  Transliteration: MYA-sa  
  English: meat  
  Example sentence: Мясо вкусное.  
  Example translation: The meat is delicious.  
  Memory hook: Say “MYA-sa” like “me and sa” — picture meat on your plate.

**Grammar Spotlight:**
Adjectives must match the gender of the noun they describe. Russian nouns are masculine, feminine, or neuter.  
- Хлеб вкусный (masculine)
- Вода вкусная (feminine)
- Яблоко вкусное (neuter)
English does not change adjective endings, but Russian adds -ый, -ая, or -ое so the words “agree.”

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: чай**
Did you know that the Russian word чай and the English word “tea” are actually cousins that both traveled from the same faraway place? This word started in China as “chá,” sailed along ancient trade routes through Central Asia, and arrived in Russian in the 1600s while English took a slightly different path through Dutch and Portuguese traders. But be careful — the Russian word “магазин” sounds like “magazine” but actually means “shop,” so don’t get them mixed up when you’re hungry! Next time you sip tea, remember it’s cousins with чай — they both come from the Chinese “chá.” That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, bread is treated with great respect — people often bring a loaf of bread and salt as a traditional welcome gift for guests. Families share one big loaf at the table, and children learn early that throwing away bread is considered unlucky.

**Practice Challenge:**
For kids: Draw your favorite food and label it with the Russian word (хлеб, сыр, яблоко). Say the word out loud three times while you color.  
For adults: Next time you make a sandwich or pour a glass of milk, name each item in Russian: “Это хлеб. Это сыр. Это молоко.”
