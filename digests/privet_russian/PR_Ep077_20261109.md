### Word of the Day
**школа**
Transliteration: SHKO-la
English meaning: school
Example sentence: Я хожу в школу каждый день.
Example translation: I go to school every day.
Memory hook: It sounds almost exactly like the English word “school” — a perfect first word to remember!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
Today we look at how Russian verbs change depending on who is doing the action — this is called conjugation. For the verb “читать” (to read), it becomes: я читаю (I read), ты читаешь (you read), он/она читает (he/she reads). It is similar to English “I read / you read / he reads,” but Russian adds clear endings so you always know who is doing it. The pattern helps you build real sentences quickly.

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: школа**
This word started in ancient Greek as “scholē,” meaning free time for learning, then travelled through Latin “schola” into Old Russian and finally modern Russian. English “school” followed almost the exact same path from the same Greek root, so the two words are linguistic cousins that arrived centuries apart. A fun false-friend moment: the Russian “школа” looks and sounds like “school,” yet in some contexts Russians also use the borrowed word “школа” for any training course, just like we say “driving school.” Here is a memory trick — picture a Greek scholar sitting in a Russian classroom; the word travelled with him all the way to you!

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, the first day of school on September 1 is called “День знаний” (Knowledge Day). Children bring flowers for their teachers and often wear special outfits, making the start of the school year feel like a small celebration.
