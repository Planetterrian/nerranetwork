The word of the day is дом.

Дом.

It sounds like dohm and rhymes with home.

This word means house or home.

Think of it as the dome that shelters your whole family, your own little kingdom.

Мой дом большой.

My house is big.

Repeat after me.

Дом.

Now that you know дом, let's add more words about the home.

The first one is комната.

Комната.

It sounds like kom-na-ta and means room.

Remember большая from a few lessons ago? It means big, and you can say моя большая комната.

Next comes кухня.

Кухня.

It sounds like kookh-nya and means kitchen.

Imagine cook-nya, where the cooking magic happens.

Мама готовит на кухне.

Mom cooks in the kitchen.

Let's keep going with окно.

Окно.

It sounds like ok-no and means window.

It comes from oko, or eye, because a window is like the eye of the house.

Я смотрю в окно.

I look out the window.

Now say дверь.

Дверь.

It sounds like dvyer and means door.

Dvyer sounds like dear, the door you open for dear friends.

Открой дверь, пожалуйста.

Open the door, please.

We are building a full picture now.

Next is стол.

Стол.

It sounds like stol and means table.

Мы едим за столом.

We eat at the table.

Then comes стул.

Стул.

It sounds like stool and means chair.

Stool is almost the same in English, so it is easy to remember.

Сидите на стуле.

Sit on the chair.

Two more words finish our home picture.

Кровать.

Кровать.

It sounds like kro-vat and means bed.

Picture crawling into your cozy crib-vat at night.

Я сплю в кровати.

I sleep in the bed.

Finally, лампа.

Лампа.

It sounds like lam-pa and means lamp.

Лампа светит ярко.

The lamp shines brightly.

In Russian we use the word в to say something is in a place.

В комнате means in the room.

На кухне means in the kitchen, and the ending changes for this word.

English stays the same, but Russian adds a special ending to show the place.

Now you can say where things are, like в комнате or на кухне.

That small change makes your sentences sound natural right away.

In many Russian homes the kitchen is the true heart of family life.

People gather there for tea and long talks, more than in the living room.

Guests often step straight into the kitchen.

This shows that warmth matters more than fancy furniture.

Do you remember what комната means?

It means room.

Something really cool about one of today's words is the story of окно.

It began long ago as oko, the old word for eye, because light enters a house just like light enters an eye.

English window came from a different idea about wind and eye.

The everyday Russian word for eye is now глаз, so oko feels like a poetic memory from the past.

Picture the window as the house's watchful eye that stays open to the world.
