### Word of the Day
**кит**
Transliteration: keet  
English meaning: whale  
Example sentence: Большой кит плавает в море. (Bol'shoy keet plavayet v more.) – The big whale swims in the sea.  
Memorable hook: Imagine a giant “keet” like a friendly submarine that sings!  
Repeat after me: кит… кит… (pause) Большой кит!
- **животное** (zhee-VOT-no-ye) – animal
  Example: Это животное очень умное. (Eto zhee-VOT-no-ye ochen' um-no-ye.) – This animal is very smart.  
  Memory hook: “Zhee-VOT-no-ye” sounds like “jee-vot” – think “jee” for “gee, what a cute animal!”
- **кит** (keet) – whale (Word of the Day)
- **обезьяна** (o-be-ZYA-na) – monkey/ape
  Example: Обезьяна любит бананы. (O-be-ZYA-na lyu-bit ba-na-ny.) – The monkey loves bananas.  
  Memory hook: “O-be-ZYA-na” has “zya” like “zee-ya” – a monkey swinging “zee-ya” from tree to tree.
- **акула** (a-KU-la) – shark
  Example: Акула плавает быстро. (A-KU-la plavayet bys-tro.) – The shark swims fast.  
  Memory hook: “A-KU-la” sounds like “a cool-a” – a cool shark!
- **птица** (PTI-tsa) – bird
  Example: Птица поёт песню. (PTI-tsa po-yot pes-nyu.) – The bird sings a song.  
  Memory hook: “PTI-tsa” like “p-tee-tsa” – a “pretty” bird.
- **собака** (so-BA-ka) – dog
  Example: Моя собака дружит со мной. (Mo-ya so-BA-ka dru-zhit so mnoy.) – My dog is friends with me.  
  Memory hook: “So-BA-ka” sounds like “so back-a” – your dog comes “so back” to you.
- **кошка** (KOSH-ka) – cat
  Example: Кошка спит на диване. (KOSH-ka spit na di-va-ne.) – The cat sleeps on the sofa.  
  Memory hook: “KOSH-ka” like “kosh” – a cozy “kosh” cat.
- **медведь** (med-VYED') – bear
  Example: Медведь ест мёд. (Med-VYED' yest myod.) – The bear eats honey.  
  Memory hook: “Med-VYED'” sounds like “med-ved” – a “med” (honey) loving bear.
- **рыба** (RY-ba) – fish
  Example: Рыба плавает в воде. (RY-ba plavayet v vo-de.) – The fish swims in the water.  
  Memory hook: “RY-ba” like “ree-ba” – a “ree” (reeel) fish.
- **друг** (druk) – friend (animal friend)
  Example: Мой друг – это собака. (Moy druk – eto so-BA-ka.) – My friend is a dog.  
  Memory hook: “Druk” sounds like “druh-k” – your animal “druh” (dude) friend.

**Grammar Spotlight:**
Russian nouns have genders (masculine, feminine, neuter) that affect endings. “Кит” is masculine, so we say “большой кит.” “Кошка” is feminine, so we say “большая кошка.” Unlike English, you must match the adjective ending to the noun’s gender. Examples: большой кит (masculine), большая кошка (feminine), большое животное (neuter).

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: кит**
Did you know that the Russian word кит and the English word “cetacean” (the scientific group for whales) both come from the same ancient root? This word started in ancient Greek as “kētos” meaning sea monster, travelled through Latin, and arrived in Russian as кит while English kept it in scientific terms. But be careful — the Russian word “кит” sounds a bit like “kit” but means a giant whale, not a small tool kit! Next time you see “cetacean” in English, remember it’s cousins with Russian кит — they both come from the Greek sea-monster root. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russian fairy tales, the giant fish-whale “Kit” often carries entire villages on its back, showing how Russians have long imagined whales as magical helpers from the sea.
