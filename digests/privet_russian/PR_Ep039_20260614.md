# Привет, Русский! — Episode Plan

### Word of the Day
> **Хлеб**
**Transliteration:** Khleb (pronounced like “khleb” with a soft “kh” at the start)
**English meaning:** Bread
**Example sentence:** Я ем хлеб. (Ya yem khleb.) — I eat bread.
Imagine a warm loaf of bread fresh from the oven — “khleb” sounds a little like “club” but means the food that Russians call the staff of life.
**Repeat after me:** Khleb… khleb… now say it faster: khleb!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
Russian nouns have gender — they are masculine, feminine, or neuter. Food words show this clearly: хлеб is masculine (ends in a consonant), молоко is neuter (ends in -о), and вода is feminine (ends in -а). In English we don’t change words this way, but in Russian you must match adjectives to the noun’s gender. Example: вкусный хлеб (masculine), вкусное молоко (neuter), вкусная вода (feminine). The ending of the adjective changes to match!

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: чай**
Did you know that the Russian word чай and the English word “chai” are actually the same word? They both come from the Chinese word for tea, “chá.” This word started in China, travelled along the ancient Silk Road through Central Asia, was borrowed into Russian in the 17th century, and later reached English as “chai” through trade. But be careful — the Russian word “магазин” sounds like “magazine” but actually means “shop”! Next time you see “chai” on a menu, remember it’s cousins with чай — they both come from Chinese “chá.” That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, tea is more than a drink — it is a daily ritual of hospitality. Families gather around the samovar (a special metal urn) to drink strong tea with jam, lemon, or sweets. Offering tea to guests is a warm way to say “you are welcome here.”
