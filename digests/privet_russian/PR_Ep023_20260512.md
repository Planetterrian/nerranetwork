# Привет, Русский! — Episode Plan
- Russian (Cyrillic): космос
  Transliteration: KOS-mos
  English: space
  Example sentence: Я смотрю на космос.
  Example translation: I look at space.
  Memory hook: It sounds exactly like the English word "cosmos" — the whole universe!

- Russian (Cyrillic): звезда
  Transliteration: zvez-DA
  English: star
  Example sentence: Звезда яркая.
  Example translation: The star is bright.
  Memory hook: Imagine a "zesty" star twinkling in the sky.

- Russian (Cyrillic): планета
  Transliteration: pla-NYE-ta
  English: planet
  Example sentence: Земля — это планета.
  Example translation: Earth is a planet.
  Memory hook: It looks and sounds like the English "planet" — easy to remember!

- Russian (Cyrillic): ракета
  Transliteration: ra-KYE-ta
  English: rocket
  Example sentence: Ракета летит вверх.
  Example translation: The rocket flies up.
  Memory hook: Picture a rocket racing like a "racket" ball shooting into the sky.

- Russian (Cyrillic): луна
  Transliteration: loo-NA
  English: moon
  Example sentence: Луна круглая.
  Example translation: The moon is round.
  Memory hook: Say "luna" like you're howling at the moon — "loo-NA!"

- Russian (Cyrillic): земля
  Transliteration: zem-LYA
  English: earth
  Example sentence: Мы живём на Земле.
  Example translation: We live on Earth.
  Memory hook: It rhymes with "zem" as in "zem" the ground under your feet.

- Russian (Cyrillic): телескоп
  Transliteration: te-le-SKOP
  English: telescope
  Example sentence: Я смотрю в телескоп.
  Example translation: I look through the telescope.
  Memory hook: "Tele" like telephone + "scope" like microscope — a far-seeing tool!

- Russian (Cyrillic): космонавт
  Transliteration: kos-mo-NAVT
  English: cosmonaut
  Example sentence: Космонавт в космосе.
  Example translation: The cosmonaut is in space.
  Memory hook: "Cosmo" for space + "naut" like astronaut — a space sailor!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
Russian nouns have genders (masculine, feminine, or neuter), but we don't need to memorize rules yet — just notice patterns. For example, many words ending in -a like планета and луна are feminine. In simple sentences we often skip "is" or "are." Compare to English: we say "The moon is round," but in Russian you can just say "Луна круглая" and it means the same thing. Try it: "Звезда яркая" (The star is bright).

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: космос**
Did you know that the Russian word космос and the English word "cosmos" are actually the same word? They both come from the ancient Greek "kosmos," which meant not just outer space but the entire ordered universe. This word started in ancient Greece, travelled through Latin and other European languages, and arrived in Russian and English with almost no change. But be careful — the Russian word "магазин" sounds like "magazine" but actually means "shop"! Next time you see the English word "cosmos," remember it's cousins with космос — they both come from the Greek idea of a beautiful, ordered world. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians have a special pride in space exploration because Yuri Gagarin became the first human in space in 1961. Many Russian cities have streets named after cosmonauts, and children often grow up dreaming of becoming one too. This love of the stars shows up in books, movies, and even everyday greetings about the sky.

**Practice Challenge:**
For kids: Draw your own rocket ship and label it with the new words — say each one out loud as you add it! For adults: Look up at the night sky tonight and practice saying three words in a row, like "Я вижу звезду и луну в космосе" (I see a star and the moon in space).
