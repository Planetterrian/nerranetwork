Now let's look at one simple grammar pattern that ties these words together.

We can say the weather with Сегодня followed by the weather word.

Segodnya means today.

For example, Сегодня тепло.

It is warm today.

Or Сегодня холодно.

It is cold today.

And Сегодня идёт дождь.

It is raining today.

In English we often add is and change the verb, but in Russian the weather word itself carries the meaning and stays simple. No extra verb is needed for temperature words.

Want to know a secret about one of today's words?

The Russian word солнце and the English word solar are actually cousins from the same ancient root. They both come from a very old word that simply meant sun.

This word traveled through Latin as sol, giving us solar and sun, while in Slavic languages it became solntse.

Along the way it picked up a playful false friend. In some old texts sol could hint at salt because the sun was seen as preserving life like salt does.

Be careful though. The real Russian word for salt is соль.

Next time you see the word solar or feel the sun on your face, remember it is cousins with солнце. They both come from the same ancient word for the bright star above us. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

Russians love talking about the weather because it changes dramatically across their huge country. From snowy Siberia to sunny Black Sea beaches.

A popular saying is Нет плохой погоды, есть неподходящая одежда.

There is no bad weather, only unsuitable clothing.

It teaches that you can enjoy any weather if you dress right.
