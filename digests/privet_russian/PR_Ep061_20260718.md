**Grammar Spotlight:**
The verb “to buy” (купить) changes depending on who is doing the action. In Russian we say я куплю (I will buy), ты купишь (you will buy), он/она купит (he/she will buy). Unlike English, the ending of the verb tells us who is acting — no need for extra words like “I” or “you” every time. Compare: English keeps “I buy,” but Russian changes куплю to show “I.”

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: магазин**
Did you know that the Russian word магазин and the English word “magazine” are actually the same word? They both come from the Arabic “makhazin,” meaning storehouses or shops. This word started in Arabic, travelled through Italian and French as “magasin,” and finally arrived in Russian in the 18th century. But be careful — the Russian word “магазин” sounds like “magazine” but actually means “shop”! Next time you see a magazine on a shelf, remember it’s cousins with магазин — they both come from the same Arabic root for storing goods. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, many people still love outdoor markets called “rynok” where they can buy fresh fruits, vegetables, and homemade goods directly from farmers. These markets are lively places full of friendly bargaining and tasting samples, showing how shopping is also a social event.
