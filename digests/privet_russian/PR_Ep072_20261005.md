### Word of the Day
**купить**
**Transliteration:** ku-PIT'
**English meaning:** to buy
**Example sentence:** Я хочу купить книгу.
**Example translation:** I want to buy a book.
Think of “cup it” — you cup the money in your hand to buy something!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
In Russian, the verb “to buy” changes depending on who is doing the action. This is called conjugation.  
- Я покупаю (ya po-ku-PA-yu) — I buy
- Ты покупаешь (ty po-ku-PA-yesh) — you buy
- Он/она покупает (on/o-na po-ku-PA-yet) — he/she buys
Unlike English, where “buy” stays mostly the same, Russian adds endings so the listener knows exactly who is shopping!

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: магазин**
This word started in Arabic as “makhzan,” meaning a storehouse or warehouse, then travelled through Turkish and French before landing in Russian in the 18th century as a place to buy goods. It sounds exactly like the English “magazine,” but while English kept the meaning of a printed publication, Russian kept the older sense of a storage place for things you can purchase. Be careful — it’s a classic false friend! Here’s a memory trick: picture a giant magazine that magically turns into shelves full of toys and snacks — that’s your Russian магазин.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, many people still shop at small neighbourhood shops called “продукты” instead of only big supermarkets. These little stores often stay open late and feel like part of the community, where the seller might know your favourite snacks.
