### Word of the Day
Поезд  
Transliteration: poyezd  
English meaning: train  
Example sentence: Я еду на поезде. (Ya yedu na poyezde.) — I am going by train.  
Memorable hook: It sounds a bit like “poised” but means the big vehicle that runs on tracks and takes you far away!

**Grammar Spotlight:**
In Russian, many travel words use the preposition “на” (na) to mean “by” or “on” when talking about transport. It works like this: на поезде (by train), на самолёте (by airplane). Unlike English “by,” Russian changes the ending of the word after “на.” Example: Я еду на поезде. (I go by train.) / Мы летим на самолёте. (We fly by airplane.) It is similar to English “on the train” but always uses “на” for vehicles.  

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: билет**
Did you know that the Russian word билет and the English word “ticket” are actually cousins that both come from the same old French root? This word started in Latin as “titulus” meaning a little label or note, travelled through French as “billet,” and arrived in Russian in the 1700s when Peter the Great was modernizing travel. But be careful — the Russian word “билет” can also mean an exam paper in school, so context tells you if it’s for a train or a test! Next time you see a ticket, remember it’s cousins with билет — they both come from that little Latin label. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.  

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russia, long train journeys are a beloved tradition. People often bring tea in special glasses with metal holders and chat with strangers in the compartment, turning a simple trip into a warm social experience.
