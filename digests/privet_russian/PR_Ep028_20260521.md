# Привет, Русский! — Episode Plan
- Russian (Cyrillic): солнце
  Transliteration: SOHLN-tseh
  English: sun
  Example sentence: Сегодня светит солнце.
  Example translation: Today the sun is shining.
  Memory hook: Imagine the sun as a big, round "soul" warming everyone up!
- Russian (Cyrillic): дождь
  Transliteration: DOZHD
  English: rain
  Example sentence: Идёт дождь.
  Example translation: It is raining.
  Memory hook: Think of "drizzle" but shorter and bouncier — dozh-d!
- Russian (Cyrillic): жара
  Transliteration: zha-RAH
  English: heat
  Example sentence: Летом бывает сильная жара.
  Example translation: In summer there is strong heat.
  Memory hook: It sounds like "jaunt" but hotter — ja-RAH means the air feels like fire!
- Russian (Cyrillic): ветер
  Transliteration: VYEH-tyer

  Example sentence: Дует сильный ветер.
  Example translation: A strong wind is blowing.
  Memory hook: "Vet" like a veterinarian who helps animals on a windy day.
- Russian (Cyrillic): облако
  Transliteration: OB-la-kah
  English: cloud
  Example sentence: В небе белое облако.

  Memory hook: "Ob-la" like "oh boy, a fluffy cloud!"

  Transliteration: tyep-LOH

  Example translation: It is warm outside.
  Memory hook: "Tep" like "tepid" but cozier and friendlier.

**Grammar Spotlight:**
We are learning how to say "it is" with weather words using the simple structure "На улице [weather word]". In Russian you often drop the verb "is" and just say the weather word after "outside". Examples: На улице жарко (It is hot outside), На улице холодно (It is cold outside), На улице тепло (It is warm outside). Unlike English, Russian weather phrases usually end in -о or -а and feel like describing the whole air around you.

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: температура**
Did you know that the Russian word температура and the English word temperature are actually the same word? They both come from the Latin "temperatura," meaning a proper mixture or balance of heat and cold. This word started in ancient Latin, travelled through scientific writing in Europe, and entered Russian in the 1700s when Peter the Great invited foreign experts to modernize the country. But be careful — the Russian word "жара" sounds a bit like "jar" but actually means intense heat, not a container! Next time you check the temperature on your phone, remember it's cousins with температура — they both come from the same Latin root about balancing hot and cold. That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.

**Cultural Corner:**
Russians love talking about the weather because their country has such extreme seasons — long snowy winters and short but very hot summers. In many cities people greet each other with phrases like "Какая сегодня погода?" to start friendly conversations, and weather often decides whether families go to the dacha or stay inside with tea and books.
