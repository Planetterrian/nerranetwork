Now let's look at one small grammar idea.

In Russian adjectives change a little to match the noun.

Небо is neuter so we say голубое небо.

Dozhd' is masculine so we say сильный дождь.

The ending shows the match and makes the sentence sound right.

English keeps adjectives the same for all nouns.

Russian adds endings like -ое or -ый to show agreement.

This small change helps your sentences sound natural.

Russians love talking about the weather.

Winters are long and cold while summers can be hot and sunny.

People often start a chat by asking как погода.

Kak pogoda. How is the weather?

This question serves as a friendly greeting.

It opens conversations in daily life.

Kids can use colors and shapes while adults can speak aloud.

Want to know a secret about one of today's words?

Did you know that the Russian word небо and the English word nebula are actually the same word?

They both come from the ancient Latin nebula meaning cloud or mist.

This word travelled through scientific writing into English as nebula for starry clouds.

It stayed in Russian as the everyday word for the sky above us.

But be careful. The Russian word небо also means heaven.

Next time you look up at the sky remember небо is cousins with nebula.

They both come from the same misty Latin root.

That connection is your secret shortcut to remembering it forever.
