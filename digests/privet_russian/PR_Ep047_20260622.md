**Grammar Spotlight:**
Russian nouns have genders (masculine, feminine, neuter) that affect endings. Most animal words ending in -а are feminine (собака, кошка), while words like слон or лев are masculine. Compare to English, where we rarely mark gender on nouns — in Russian it’s like giving every animal a secret “he” or “she” label that changes how you say “my” or “big.”

**Word Origins — Deep Dive: медведь**
Did you know the Russian word медведь and the English idea of “honey-eater” are basically the same concept? This word started in ancient Slavic languages as a polite way to avoid saying the real name of the scary forest animal — it literally means “the one who knows honey.” It travelled through old Slavic roots and stayed in Russian while English kept the fiercer-sounding “bear.” But be careful — the Russian word “медведь” sounds nothing like “bear,” yet both languages hide the animal’s true name out of respect! Next time you see honey, remember it’s cousins with медведь — they both come from the same sweet secret.

**Cultural Corner:**
In Russian fairy tales the bear is often called “Медведь” and acts like a wise but clumsy forest uncle. Children grow up hearing stories of bears helping or tricking other animals, which makes the word медведь feel like an old friend rather than just a zoo animal.
