### The Positive Papers
1. **Assam records zero rhino poaching as global one-horned rhino population rises**
   Assam reported no rhino poaching incidents throughout 2025 according to the latest IFR report. The state’s conservation measures appear to have held steady after years of targeted enforcement and community involvement. Rangers and local communities maintained patrols and monitoring that removed snares and deterred poachers. The same period saw the global one-horned rhino population continue its gradual increase. The result shows a single year of zero losses is possible when enforcement and habitat protection stay consistent. The open question is whether neighboring regions can replicate the same combination of patrols, community reporting, and funding before poaching pressure returns. Long-term success still depends on sustained budgets and cross-border coordination that have slipped in the past. The IFR Report labels the outcome a major milestone for the state. Separate coverage confirms the zero-poaching record held across the full calendar year. The global population trend provides an additional positive signal alongside the local enforcement gains. 2. **As Russia attacks Ukraine’s grid, solar offers a path to resilience**
   Distributed solar installations have kept power flowing to hospitals, schools, and homes even when centralized grid infrastructure was struck. Ukrainian communities and businesses have added panels and batteries at a pace that outstrips pre-war plans. The modular nature of rooftop and ground-mount systems means damage to one site does not collapse service for an entire region. Early data show these smaller arrays reduce outage duration compared with reliance on the main transmission network alone. The honest caveat is that winter output drops and supply-chain delays for inverters remain real constraints. The open question is how quickly neighboring countries can adopt similar decentralized approaches before the next winter heating season. Communities and businesses installed the systems faster than planners had projected before the conflict began. Hospitals and schools continued operations during strikes because individual arrays stayed online. The modular design limits the reach of any single attack. Data collected so far indicate shorter average outage times when distributed generation is present. Source: [grist.org](https://grist.org/international/as-russia-attacks-ukraines-grid-solar-offers-a-path-to-resilience/)

### Think Positive
Simon Sinek points out that people sustain effort longer when they first clarify the purpose behind the work. That “why” turns routine tasks into steps toward something larger than the immediate inconvenience. When the daily action feels connected to a bigger outcome, motivation is more likely to reappear even on low-energy days. The practice is simple: before starting one concrete task today, write the single reason it matters to you or to someone else. Then begin anyway. The same principle applies when volunteers sign up for monitoring work that protects a species over many seasons. A clear purpose helps the first phone call or form submission turn into repeated shifts rather than a one-time effort.
---
### The Lever
Contact your regional wildlife authority this week and ask to join an existing species-monitoring volunteer roster. The step usually takes one phone call or online form plus a short orientation, often one to two hours total. Reported outcomes from similar programs include earlier detection of snares and faster response times in protected zones, though exact per-volunteer impact varies by region. If even one percent of listeners in range of a monitored area signed up, the added eyes and ears could extend coverage on dozens of patrol routes. A common barrier is uncertainty about time commitment; most programs let volunteers choose single-day shifts rather than ongoing schedules. Ask specifically for the next available one-day monitoring slot when you call. The same call can clarify whether the roster focuses on rhino habitat or other species in your area. Volunteers who complete the orientation often receive basic training on reporting protocols and safety. The action connects directly to the enforcement model that produced Assam’s zero-poaching year.
