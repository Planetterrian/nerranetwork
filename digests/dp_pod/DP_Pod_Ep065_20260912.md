### The Positive Papers

1. **Smart pen detects pressure and movement to identify children's writing difficulties sooner**
   Researchers at Politecnico di Milano and the University of Insubria in Varese and Como developed the THInkPen to record pressure and movement data during standard writing tasks. The study was published in PLOS Digital Health and involved more than 700 children from primary and lower secondary school. Participants used the THInkPen while completing two BVSCO-3 tasks drawn from the Battery for the Clinical Assessment of Writing and Orthographic Skills. That battery remains the most widely used test in Italy for assessing writing difficulties, dysgraphia, and dysorthography. The pen captures real-time metrics that standard observation misses, allowing earlier identification of dysgraphia or dysorthography. Early results suggest it could shorten the time to diagnosis, though larger trials are still needed to confirm accuracy across regions. For families this means a low-cost tool that could reach schools without specialized equipment. The open question is whether health systems will integrate the data stream into routine pediatric checks or keep it limited to research settings. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-smart-pen-pressure-movement-children.html)

2. **First Permit to Clean Up Old Mine Waste Issued for WA State Under 2024 ‘Good Samaritan Act’**
   The US Environmental Protection Agency approved the first permit under the 2024 Good Samaritan Act to remediate abandoned mine workings in Washington state. The permit was issued to Trout Unlimited, a fishing and sportsmen-focused nonprofit that engages in riverine conservation across the West. The Good Samaritan Act passed in 2024 with broad bipartisan support and supplies liability protections that let volunteers act without fear of endless legal exposure. Trout Unlimited can now legally remove waste that has polluted waterways for decades. The mechanism relies on those liability protections to encourage groups to begin cleanup. Scale remains limited to permitted sites and volunteer capacity, so results will depend on how many additional groups apply. Regular people gain cleaner local rivers for fishing and recreation once work begins. The tension is whether the bipartisan law will attract enough participants to move beyond pilot projects. Source: [goodnewsnetwork.org](https://www.goodnewsnetwork.org/first-permit-to-clean-up-old-mine-waste-issued-for-wa-state-under-2024-good-samaritan-act/)

3. **Assam drives global rhino recovery as population reaches 4,075**
   Assam's conservation programs have lifted the greater one-horned rhino population to 4,075 through sustained anti-poaching patrols and habitat management. The numbers come from the most recent state census and reflect steady growth over multiple years. Local teams combine technology, community involvement, and enforcement to protect the animals inside and around Kaziranga National Park. The population increase demonstrates that targeted regional action can reverse species decline when maintained over time. Gains can stall if funding or political support drops, and the population remains concentrated in a few protected areas. Listeners see that consistent local effort produces measurable rebounds rather than waiting for global agreements. The unresolved question is how quickly similar models can expand to other rhino-range states. Source: [hubnetwork.in](https://hubnetwork.in/assam-drives-global-rhino-recovery-as-population-reaches-4075/)

### Think Positive
Carol Dweck's growth mindset research shows that viewing abilities as developable rather than fixed leads people to persist longer on difficult tasks. When effort is treated as the path to mastery, setbacks become data instead of verdicts. This matters for the builders in today's stories, where years of monitoring and small adjustments produced measurable recoveries in both wildlife populations and diagnostic tools. Teams that kept refining their methods after early setbacks reached the concrete numbers now reported. One concrete mental rep is to name one skill you want to improve, then schedule the next ten-minute practice block before the day ends.

### The Lever

The call takes under ten minutes and costs only your phone minutes. One volunteer day typically removes several dozen kilograms of waste from a single stretch of waterway, with the exact amount depending on site conditions. If even one percent of listeners followed through, the cumulative effect would add hundreds of extra hands to permitted projects in the first season. The main barrier is not knowing the schedule; the office can email the next date and any gear requirements once you express interest. The action ends with your name on the list and a confirmed date in your calendar. Volunteers work directly on the permitted sites opened by the new Good Samaritan rule, turning one phone call into physical removal of mine waste from local rivers.
