### The Positive Papers
1. **India's clean-energy push hits a milestone: Coal power sees no growth for first time in 50 years**
 India's electricity generation from coal remained flat for the first time in half a century while overall power demand rose. The milestone reflects faster deployment of solar, wind, and other renewables that now cover the incremental load. The shift reduces the country's annual emissions growth trajectory without requiring immediate plant retirements. Grid operators still face the practical challenge of balancing variable renewable output with existing coal plants that provide baseload stability. Early data suggest the trend can continue if storage and transmission keep pace, yet the exact pace depends on state-level project execution. The open question is whether this marks a permanent plateau or a temporary pause driven by one year's project pipeline. The data point arrives from reporting dated September 17, 2026. Renewables are absorbing new demand that would otherwise have required additional coal capacity. This pattern holds even as total electricity consumption continues its upward climb. Builders in the renewable sector can point to measurable displacement of new fossil capacity without waiting for full plant retirements. 2. **New mesothelioma drug turns cancer’s own defenses against it**
 An experimental drug disables the PRX3 antioxidant protein that mesothelioma tumors rely on to survive oxidative stress. In an early clinical trial the compound controlled disease progression in 67 percent of patients and produced encouraging survival signals. The approach is counterintuitive because it attacks a defense mechanism rather than the cancer cells directly. The trial remains small and early-stage, so larger studies are needed to confirm durability and safety across broader populations. If the mechanism holds, similar strategies could apply to other oxidative-stress-dependent cancers. The open question is how quickly this moves from trial to approved therapy and which patients benefit most. The compound was tested in patients with an aggressive asbestos-linked cancer that has few effective treatments. Disease control at the 67 percent mark came with survival results described as encouraging by the reporting team. The strategy exploits the intense oxidative stress already present inside these tumors. Translation to routine care will require confirmation that the effect persists beyond the initial cohort. ### Think Positive
Martin Seligman’s work on learned optimism shows that people can train themselves to treat setbacks as temporary and specific rather than permanent and pervasive. This shift in explanatory style reduces helplessness and improves persistence on difficult tasks. When builders face slow regulatory approvals or uncertain trial results, the same habit keeps effort moving forward instead of stalling. One concrete mental rep today is to write one sentence reframing a current obstacle as “this is one variable that can be tested,” then take the smallest next action on it. The practice draws directly from decades of research on how explanatory style influences resilience. It pairs naturally with the incremental milestones seen in both energy deployment and early-stage medical trials. A single reframing step lowers the emotional cost of continuing when outcomes remain uncertain. Over repeated days the habit compounds into greater willingness to ship imperfect work.

### The Lever

At honest scale, if even one in a thousand listeners performs the search and one in ten of those finds a match, dozens of additional screened patients could reach trials each month.
