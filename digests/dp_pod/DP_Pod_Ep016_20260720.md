### The Positive Papers
1. **Utilities Aren’t Ready for the Balcony Solar Revolution: IEEE Spectrum Energy**
   Utah legalized plug-in photovoltaic panels last March, triggering rapid adoption as Colorado, Virginia, New York, New Jersey, and California passed similar bills through one or both legislative houses by July 2026. Germany reached more than 2 gigawatts of balcony solar after 2022 natural-gas price spikes, with panels sold at Aldi and IKEA following microinverter adaptations from larger arrays. Craig Morris of Bundesverband Steckersolar secured firefighter approval that the systems pose no special danger and won legal protections so landlords must show a legitimate reason, such as balcony repairs, before blocking renter installations. UL Solutions developed the UL 3700 framework by the end of 2025 under Ken Boyce to test insulation thickness, bidirectional-current GFCIs, blackout shutdown, and extra overcurrent protection that household wiring never anticipated. Nadav Enbar at the Electric Power Research Institute notes the standard remains a high bar because bidirectional GFCI disconnects are not yet commercially available, leaving current panels uncertified or citing mismatched standards. David Eisenhauer of SoCal Edison emphasizes customer notification helps utilities track connections, yet German data already shows unregistered units outnumber registered ones two-to-one because some owners skip paperwork or hide installations from landlords. Source: [spectrum.ieee.org](https://spectrum.ieee.org/plug-in-solar-power)

2. **Scientists identify the rare meteorite that killed the dinosaurs 66 million years ago: ScienceDaily Top Science**
   The dinosaur-ending asteroid 66 million years ago has been traced to an exceptionally rare CO chondrite originating from a distant solar-system region. Its distinctive chemistry indicates the lethal effects came mainly from planet-cooling dust and debris rather than sulfur released by the impactor itself. ### Think Positive
Martin Seligman’s framework of learned optimism shows that people who treat setbacks as temporary and specific maintain forward momentum instead of sliding into helplessness. When regulatory gaps or certification delays appear in new technologies such as plug-in solar, viewing the obstacle as solvable rather than permanent keeps builders iterating on safety standards. The same stance turns the two-to-one unregistered ratio in Germany from a discouraging statistic into a clear engineering target. A listener can practice this today by writing one current frustration in a single sentence, then listing the smallest next action that moves the situation even slightly.

### The Lever
Notify your local utility before installing any plug-in solar panel so operators can log the connection and confirm certified equipment. The step takes roughly fifteen minutes online or by phone and costs nothing. In Germany the same notification habit would cut the current two-to-one unregistered ratio, improving grid visibility for everyone. If even one in ten new U.S. adopters followed through, utilities would gain data on thousands of small systems instead of flying blind. The main barrier is the extra paperwork some owners skip; treating the call as part of the purchase checkout removes that friction. Source: [spectrum.ieee.org](https://spectrum.ieee.org/plug-in-solar-power)
