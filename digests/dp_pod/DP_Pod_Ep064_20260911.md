### The Positive Papers
1. **Scientists discover the human heart can regrow muscle after a heart attack**
   Researchers have shown for the first time that human heart muscle cells can regrow after a heart attack. The finding overturns the idea that damaged areas of the heart are completely incapable of regeneration. Scientists now hope to identify ways to dramatically boost this natural repair process. The work points toward potential new treatments for heart failure. Early observations remain confined to laboratory settings with small sample sizes. No scalable clinical intervention has emerged yet from the data. A regular person sees an opening for future therapies that could restore actual heart function rather than only slowing decline. The open question the hosts can argue about is how large an effect size the natural regeneration process can reach once amplified. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260909231754.htm)

2. **Scientists capture two DNA strands zipping together for the first time**
   Scientists have directly observed how two negatively charged DNA molecules can pair up despite normally repelling each other. Positively charged metal ions appear to bridge the gap. Matching DNA helices align groove for groove like a molecular zipper. The discovery confirms a theory proposed two decades ago. The observation stays at the single-molecule level. No immediate drug applications have been developed from the images. A regular person gains a clearer view of cellular processes that can malfunction in cancer. The open question is how rapidly this structural detail can be turned into targeted therapies. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260909231717.htm)

3. **More than 70% of new solar is being built in states that voted for Trump**
   More than 70 percent of new solar capacity under construction sits in states that supported the current administration in the last presidential election. The distribution shows clean-energy projects advancing regardless of statewide political leanings. Deployment numbers come from project tracking data compiled through mid-2026. Grid interconnection queues in those states now contain the majority of planned utility-scale arrays. Local utilities and developers continue signing contracts at the same pace seen in prior years. A regular person notices that household and community solar choices remain available across a wide range of regions. The open question is whether this geographic spread will accelerate or slow once transmission upgrades catch up with the new generation. Source: [grist.org](https://grist.org/energy/more-than-70-of-new-solar-is-being-built-in-states-that-voted-for-trump/)

### Think Positive
Angela Duckworth describes grit as sustained passion and perseverance toward long-term goals even when progress feels invisible. The heart-regrowth and DNA-pairing stories show that scientific persistence often spans decades before a visible result appears. Listeners can borrow that stance by treating one small daily action as evidence that their own effort compounds. The concrete mental rep is to name the single next physical step on a project you have been postponing, then complete it before the day ends.
---
### The Lever
Open your city or regional government website today, locate the sustainability or climate-action page, and submit one concrete suggestion for joining an existing inter-city emissions-reduction network before the end of the week. The step takes roughly fifteen minutes and costs nothing beyond an internet connection. If even one in twenty listeners follows through, the volume of citizen input could nudge additional municipalities to join networks already moving megawatts of clean capacity. A common barrier is uncertainty about what counts as a useful suggestion; the fix is to reference the 70-percent solar figure from today’s Positive Papers and ask how your city can match that pace.
Source the impact numbers where the articles provide them: https://grist.org/energy/more-than-70-of-new-solar-is-being-built-in-states-that-voted-for-trump/
