# The DP Pod: The Do Positive Podcast

> **Most media profits from making people feel powerless, yet two friends—an airline pilot and a physical chemist—started from the opposite conviction: individuals are not powerless. They asked whether two people could run an entire podcast network by building an AI pipeline instead of hiring a studio. The answer became the Nerra Network. It began as standalone scripts held together by cron jobs, then refactored into one unified pipeline with a single runner, per-show config files, and a shared engine so adding a show dropped from writing two thousand lines to writing a config and prompts. Today that pipeline runs fourteen daily shows guarded by more than three and a half thousand automated tests. The economics only closed because language-model costs fell roughly in half and voices moved to custom-trained models at about four dollars per million characters instead of one hundred fifty. Prompts became the real product once the team learned that a single example phrase would repeat verbatim across episodes, forcing a rule to de-seed by shape rather than quotable text. Every episode now runs through a review agent that audits twice a week and logs predictions for later scoring. The network went multilingual early, translating and re-voicing English episodes into French, Russian, Spanish, and Chinese for roughly eighteen cents each, with separate feeds and a Russian-dubbed YouTube channel. Full videos and Shorts auto-produce with timed captions and heuristic-chosen hooks, all while respecting YouTube’s quota limits through careful rotation across shows such as First Principles Daily and SpaceX Daily. Reliability came from replacing GitHub schedulers with an external dispatcher that fires to the minute and triggers automatic recovery pull requests on failure. The fourteenth show is this one—the first two-voice dialogue and the first where the builders step in front of the microphone. It exists for builders, creators, and contributors who try to make a difference and for people who want to start. The goals are ten minutes a day of consequential, verified progress, one concrete action a week with honest numbers in The Lever, and proof it works through the Do Positive Dispatch. Listeners can find the show page at nerranetwork.com, take the pledge, and join the club. Open questions remain: what does it mean that two people can now do what once required a media company, where human judgment stays load-bearing, what breaks first at fifty shows, and whether full disclosure plus no-fabrication rules can earn lasting trust. The origins trace to 2025 when the two friends tested whether an AI pipeline could replace a full studio team. The refactor eliminated duplicated code across shows and turned every new program into a configuration file plus prompts. More than three and a half thousand automated tests now run before each publish to protect the fourteen-show slate. Cost curves mattered: newer language models halved per-episode expense while custom voices dropped from one hundred fifty dollars per million characters to roughly four. The review ledger records predictions from twice-weekly audits so the system can measure its own accuracy over time. Multilingual output added French, Russian, Spanish, and Chinese feeds at about eighteen cents per episode, plus a dedicated Russian YouTube channel. Video production respects daily API limits by rotating uploads, including First Principles Daily and SpaceX Daily, while generating per-word captions and hook-timed Shorts. An external dispatcher replaced GitHub’s schedulers to guarantee minute-level timing and automatic recovery pull requests on any mid-publish failure. This debut episode marks the first time the builders appear on microphone in dialogue format, closing the loop from behind-the-scenes operators to on-air hosts.**

### Think Positive
Viktor Frankl observed that between stimulus and response lies a space where we can choose our response, and that choice is the first freedom. In a world of constant headlines that reward helplessness, that space is where agency begins. Choosing a response does not erase difficulty, but it shifts attention from what happened to what one will do next. The hosts see this principle at work in the network itself: every technical setback became a prompt to decide the next measurable fix rather than a reason to stop. A concrete mental rep for today is to name one small action you have been postponing, pause for five seconds to notice the story you are telling yourself about it, then take the action anyway.
