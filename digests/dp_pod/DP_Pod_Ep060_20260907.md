### The Positive Papers
**A bold river experiment shows how removing invasive fish helped native species rebound in the Colorado River**
   Researchers removed invasive fish species from a section of the Colorado River in a controlled experiment. Native fish populations increased measurably afterward. The mechanism works by reducing competition and predation pressure that had suppressed the original species. The result matters because river systems supply water and habitat for millions of people and countless other species. The honest caveat is that the study covers one river segment and does not yet prove the approach scales to every watershed. An open question is whether similar removals would produce comparable rebounds in rivers with different invasive mixes or flow regimes. The work was covered by Diario AS on September 6 2026. Follow-up monitoring will determine how long the rebound persists without repeated intervention. Source: [en.as.com](https://en.as.com/latest_news/a-bold-river-experiment-shows-how-removing-invasive-fish-helped-native-species-rebound-in-the-colorado-river-f202609-n/)

**Six unusual structures identified at Earth's core-mantle boundary with the help of deep learning**
   Scientists applied deep learning to a large set of seismic waves from earthquakes to map the core-mantle boundary 2,900 km below the surface. The analysis found six continuous bands of irregularities instead of the sparse patches previously detected. These bands appear as distinct zones where seismic waves behave differently. The method works because machine learning can spot patterns across thousands of recordings that human analysts miss. The honest caveat is that the structures remain inaccessible for direct sampling and their exact composition is still unknown. An open question is how these bands affect heat flow and magnetic field generation over geologic time. The study was published in the journal JGR Solid Earth. Direct drilling to the boundary remains impossible so seismic data stays the only available window. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-unusual-earth-core-mantle-boundary.html)

**Extreme pressure turns blue pigment into record-long single-atom copper chains**
   Researchers subjected a blue pigment to extreme pressure and produced one of the longest single-atom copper chains observed to date. The chains could function as molecular-scale wires once electronics reach silicon’s physical limits. The pressure forces copper atoms into linear arrangements that remain stable after the pressure is released. The honest caveat is that the chains have only been made in laboratory quantities and integrating them into working devices remains unproven. An open question is whether the process can be scaled or adapted to other metals for practical electronics. The work appears in recent coverage from Phys.org. Traditional silicon chips approach fundamental physical limits as device sizes continue to shrink. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-09-extreme-pressure-blue-pigment-atom.html)

### Think Positive
Simon Sinek points out that people sustain effort longer when they first clarify their deeper reason for acting. That “why” turns ordinary tasks into meaningful steps rather than chores. When the reason is clear, setbacks become data instead of reasons to quit. The same principle applies to any new habit: the motivation to continue grows from the purpose, not the other way around. Today’s mental rep is to write one sentence stating why a single action you plan to take this week matters to you, then begin the action without waiting for extra motivation.
