### Tesla First Principles

Taking a step back from today's headlines, the detailed manufacturing process for Neuralink's brain implant wires challenges the assumption that high-channel-count neural interfaces must remain low-volume, hand-assembled devices.

The approach uses a fluoropolymer that melts and reflows under controlled heat and asymmetric pressure to create thousands of hermetic feedthroughs without fracturing the surrounding structure. This single step replaces multiple traditional materials and drilling operations that previously limited electrode density. The resulting assembly supports wireless power and data transfer while protecting the delicate polyimide ribbons and metallic traces inside.

If the same vertical-integration logic Tesla applies to vehicle and battery production extends to medical hardware, the bottleneck on scaling channel counts disappears. Production could shift from specialized clean-room craftsmanship toward automated lines capable of supporting tens of thousands of electrodes per implant. That shift would change both the economics of brain-computer interfaces and the timeline for broader clinical use.
