Owning a semiconductor fabrication plant moves capital from variable supplier contracts
into fixed facility costs and long-term depreciation.
Tesla and SpaceX chose to build and run TerraFab instead of depending on external foundries
for every wafer.
This step secures access to process nodes while contract manufacturers juggle capacity
demands for many AI clients.
The choice converts per wafer fees into upfront construction and equipment spending that stays
on the books for years.
Utilization rates determine the economics.
A dedicated line can maintain higher average loads than a shared fab balancing multiple
orders. Yet the dedicated line also demands ongoing equipment refreshes every 2-3 years. Intel
handles packaging while core front-end processes remain inside the Tesla Space X joint entity.
The setup cuts allocation risks during shortages, but raises exposure to yield and ramp execution.
Yield gains directly lower the cost per inference chip for Optimus and CyberCab.
The decision shapes gross margin on autonomy hardware, meeting internal cost targets reduces
the bill of materials per computer, lagging yields increase per unit expense from the same
capital commitment.
