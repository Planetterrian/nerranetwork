Owning the physical tooling for a component changes the balance of power in any supplier dispute.
When a manufacturer controls the dies, molds, and fixtures required to make a part,
it can shift production or bring work in-house without starting from scratch.
Tesla's lawsuit over the cyber truck supplier illustrates this leverage in practice because
the company is not arguing price or quality, but simply demanding return of assets it already
paid to create. The result is faster legal resolution than a typical contract fight and
lower risk of prolonged line stoppage. This approach carries its own costs because designing and
building custom tooling requires upfront capital and engineering time that many automakers preferred
outsource. Tesla accepts that burden because it aligns with a broader pattern of controlling the
