### Tesla First Principles

Fleet data turns every Tesla into a rolling sensor array that records exactly what happened in the moments before any incident. When regulators or media question whether autonomy software caused a crash, the same logs that enable continuous improvement also serve as the definitive record of driver intervention. This dual use means Tesla can resolve disputes faster than competitors who lack equivalent telemetry, yet it also raises the bar for what counts as proof in public debate.

The recent Texas crash illustrates the point. Vehicle data showed the driver pressed the accelerator to 100 percent, overriding the system and sustaining that input through impact. Without that granular trace, speculation would have continued for weeks. The episode shows how the same architecture that collects training data for FSD also protects the company when external narratives form quickly.

Over time this capability changes the economics of liability. Insurers and courts gain access to objective sequences rather than reconstructions, which should compress settlement cycles and reduce uncertainty. The open question is whether the public and regulators will consistently accept the data as authoritative when the outcome is unfavorable to Tesla.
