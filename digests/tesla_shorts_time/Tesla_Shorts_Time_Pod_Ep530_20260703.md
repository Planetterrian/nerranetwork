### Tesla First Principles

Subsidies in markets like South Korea alter the effective transaction price more directly than most product changes. When approval arrives, Tesla can raise the posted price without losing buyer interest because the net cost to the customer stays similar or improves. The adjustment reveals how much of the original price already incorporated expected incentive levels rather than pure production costs.

This dynamic differs from pure technology-driven price reductions, which reflect falling component costs or manufacturing scale. In South Korea the move followed subsidy confirmation and coincided with Tesla claiming 30 percent of imported vehicle sales. The sequence shows pricing power tied to policy timing instead of internal efficiencies alone.

For the business, the pattern affects gross margin visibility and demand forecasting. Companies must decide whether to lock in higher list prices once incentives appear or keep headroom for future policy shifts. Watching how Tesla manages this balance in additional markets will clarify whether subsidy-linked pricing becomes a recurring tool or a temporary response.
