### Tesla First Principles

Cold-gas thrusters on a passenger car change the mass and energy budget in ways that are straightforward to calculate but hard to reconcile with everyday driving. The Roadster demonstration used ten nozzles and approximately 250 kilograms of compressed gas to produce the flips and jumps shown in Texas. That propellant load must be carried at all times, directly subtracting from the vehicle’s 300-plus-mile range claim and adding structural mass that the fixed seats and exotic materials are already trying to offset. Replenishment after use requires an onboard compressor or refueling step that adds complexity to the vehicle’s support systems. The 250-kilogram figure appears only for the demonstration configuration and may differ across the three variants.

The suction system under the car is presented as an alternative to conventional wings or splitters, capable of pulling up to 2 G to keep the vehicle inverted on the ramp. Generating that force through fans rather than aerodynamic surfaces avoids drag penalties at high speed yet requires continuous electrical power and moving parts that must survive road debris and weather. The 2 G figure is impressive in a controlled demo, but scaling it to variable road surfaces and regulatory crash standards introduces variables the current prototype has not yet addressed in public testing. Power draw from the fans also competes with the propulsion and battery systems already tasked with delivering the sub-one-second acceleration times. The system’s ability to operate repeatedly without external support remains unproven outside the Texas event.

Sharing components across the Cybercab, Semi, Optimus, and Roadster programs spreads the cost of developing those thrusters and suction hardware, yet each platform carries different duty cycles and safety cases. A thruster pack sized for a one-seat Rocket variant may prove oversized or undersized once adapted to a two-seat Performance model or a commercial Semi. The engineering choice to amortize development this way reduces per-unit cost only if the shared hardware meets the strictest requirements of every program without excessive redesign. Production timing for 2028 on the Roadster overlaps with expected volume ramps for the other three programs, tightening the window for any necessary variant-specific changes. The approach also concentrates risk: a single component failure or regulatory delay can affect multiple revenue streams simultaneously.
