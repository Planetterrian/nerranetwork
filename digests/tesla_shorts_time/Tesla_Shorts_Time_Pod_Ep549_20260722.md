### Tesla First Principles

Camera-only autonomy places the entire burden of hazard detection on software that must interpret photon counts under every lighting and weather condition. When regulators demand proof that the system recognizes its own blindness, the question shifts from sensor count to algorithmic self-awareness. Tesla's approach bets that neural networks trained on fleet data can outperform fixed hardware rules, yet the August 12 filing will test whether that bet has produced measurable safeguards.

The internal document titled "Radar Saves Us" now sits at the center of the inquiry because it may reveal whether engineers once quantified the exact failure modes that later appeared in the nine reported crashes. If the code can flag degraded visibility and either alert the driver or disengage within milliseconds, an over-the-air fix remains possible. Should the data fall short, the company faces a choice between reintroducing radar or accepting limits on where FSD can operate.

That outcome carries direct cost implications. A software resolution preserves the capital already spent on vision training and avoids retrofitting millions of vehicles. A hardware reversal would reset part of the 2021 decision and add expense at a moment when cash flow is already under watch from investors. The next data point is whether the August submission closes the case or extends it.
