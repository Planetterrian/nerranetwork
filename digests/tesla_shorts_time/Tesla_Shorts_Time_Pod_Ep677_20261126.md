### Tesla First Principles
SpaceX received FCC approval to deploy up to one million Starmind satellites for orbital AI data centers, with the first satellite required by November 25, 2033, and 100,000 by 2035. The satellites will fly between 500 and 1,002 km, use optical inter-satellite links for primary communication, and fall back to Ka-band radio when those links fail. A completed debris-mitigation plan must be filed before any units operate above 600 km. The same approval sets hard milestones at 500,000 satellites by 2038 and full completion by 2040, or the authorization lapses. SpaceX has already contracted SINGULUS for solar-cell production equipment tied to the constellation. The FCC denied petitions from Amazon Leo, Viasat, and the American Astronomical Society. The lower orbit range chosen by the FCC reduces some latency concerns compared with the original 2,000 km upper limit in the application.

These dates and quantities sit alongside Tesla’s own need for massive inference compute to train and run both FSD and Optimus at scale. Placing the compute in orbit changes the power and cooling equations that constrain ground-based clusters, yet it still requires reliable downlink capacity and regulatory clearance for the full constellation. The 1-million-satellite ceiling and the 2033–2040 timeline therefore become concrete variables in any model that forecasts how quickly Tesla can expand its AI training throughput beyond terrestrial limits. Optical links between satellites enable the cluster-style computing approach SpaceX has described, while the Ka-band fallback provides a safety net for command and telemetry. Manufacturing contracts already signed indicate the company is moving from regulatory filing to hardware production on a timeline that aligns with the first-launch target Musk mentioned at the Baron Investment Conference.

For Tesla, any orbital capacity that becomes available would sit outside the power and land constraints that currently shape Cortex and Dojo expansion plans. The constellation is designed to meet growing demand for artificial intelligence computing by processing large amounts of data in orbit for use on Earth.
