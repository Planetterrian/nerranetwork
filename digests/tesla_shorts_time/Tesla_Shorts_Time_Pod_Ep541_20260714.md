### Tesla First Principles

Battery supply chains have long been viewed as a single bottleneck that any manufacturer must either own or endure. Tesla’s decision to have Samsung SDI build a dedicated US line for energy storage cells shows the company treating stationary batteries as a separate production track rather than a side stream of vehicle cells. This separation lets each product chase its own cost curve and chemistry without forcing trade-offs in energy density or cycle life that vehicle packs demand.

The practical result is faster scaling of Megapack output while vehicle programs continue to absorb 4680 and LFP cells at their own pace. It also reduces the risk that a sudden surge in car demand crowds out grid projects that now contribute meaningful revenue and margin. Investors watching only vehicle margins miss how this dual-track approach changes the capital allocation picture.

Over time the split could matter more than vertical integration itself. A dedicated storage line lets Tesla negotiate raw-material contracts sized to predictable utility demand instead of the lumpier vehicle schedule. That predictability feeds back into lower financing costs for the factories that build the factories.
