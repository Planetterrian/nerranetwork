### Tesla First Principles

Video compression was built for playback, not for selective machine learning. When every training step needs only a handful of frames scattered across petabytes of fleet footage, the conventional pipeline pays a steep tax by fully decoding long reference chains that the model never uses.

Tesla's approach moves the parsing step inside the GPU itself. The card builds lightweight lookup tables of byte offsets and timestamps, then isolates the shortest viable reference chain for each requested frame. Overlapping segments from nearby frames are deduplicated before any decode work begins, and intermediate reference frames are discarded from memory the moment they are no longer needed.

The practical result is lower memory bandwidth demand and higher training throughput on the same hardware. At the scale of distributed clusters holding 10-to-30 petabytes, that efficiency difference compounds into measurable reductions in both capex per model iteration and the time required to close the next loop on FSD or Optimus.
