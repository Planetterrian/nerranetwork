Battery cell chemistry and pack architecture decisions determine far more than range figures on a
spec sheet. Tesla's shift toward 4680 cells with structural integration changes how energy density,
thermal paths, and manufacturing tolerances interact on the same assembly line.
Conventional pack designs separate the cell array from the vehicle chassis,
allowing independent optimization of each subsystem but adding mass and connection points.
Structural packs collapse those layers, forcing every tolerance decision to satisfy both
electrical and mechanical requirements simultaneously. When a single stamping operation forms both
the battery enclosure and the vehicle floor, the capital equipment must deliver surface finish and
dimensional accuracy that used to be handled by two separate suppliers.
Yield losses at that station now affect both battery capacity and body rigidity,
creating a tighter feedback loop between cell manufacturing and final vehicle quality.
