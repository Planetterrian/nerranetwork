Regulatory approval for supervised autonomy now hinges less on technical validation and more on political consensus among member states.
The German Ministry's confirmation that the TCMV vote requires a qualified majority shows the process has moved past RDW technical review into a vote-counting exercise.
Tesla's data package is already with regulators. Yet the outcome still depends on how many governments weigh consumer safety arguments against domestic industry concerns.
This structure differs from the US approach where individual state DMVs or NHTSA can grant narrower permissions without needing cross-border alignment.
In Europe, the Single Implementing Act once passed applies uniformly which lowers the per-country compliance cost if the vote succeeds.
The risk is that one or two larger states can block progress even when technical objections have been addressed.
