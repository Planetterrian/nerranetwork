Battery longevity testing rarely receives the same attention as peak energy density or fast charge weights.
The CATL cells that powered the world's first large-scale storage project still operate
after 14 years with zero replacements.
That result shifts the economics of stationary storage because replacement cycles move from
a recurring cost into a distant future expense.
Vehicle batteries face different duty cycles than grid units, yet the same chemistry lessons
apply when Tesla designs packs for both Cybertruck and Megapack lines.
If a cell chemistry can retain capacity after 14 years of daily cycling, the capital
cost per kilowatt hour delivered over the pack's life drops sharply.
Manufacturers can therefore price longer warranties or larger energy buffers without eroding
margins.
The practical implication reaches beyond Tesla's own products.
Utilities evaluating 15-year asset lives now have field data that supports financing models
once considered optimistic.
When replacement intervals stretch that far, the levelized cost of storage falls enough
to accelerate coal plant retirements in regions where solar-plus storage already competes
on price.
