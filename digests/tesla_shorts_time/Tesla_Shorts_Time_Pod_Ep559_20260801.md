### Tesla First Principles

Subscription access to supervised FSD changes the timing of revenue recognition in ways that traditional vehicle sales never did. Instead of booking most of the margin at delivery, Tesla can now spread payments across months or years while the same hardware continues to generate incremental service revenue. That shift lowers the capital intensity of each new customer because the car is already built and the software is already written. The model also creates a feedback loop where usage data directly informs further software development without requiring additional hardware sales.

The $99 monthly plan also alters the risk profile for buyers who are unsure about the technology. A driver can test the system without committing to a large upfront purchase, which widens the pool of potential users beyond early adopters. At the same time, the recurring stream gives Tesla visibility into future cash flows that can be modeled against development and regulatory costs. Retention becomes the central variable because owners can cancel at any time if the supervised experience falls short of expectations.

If adoption scales, the model creates a margin layer that is less exposed to raw-material price swings or factory utilization rates. The open question is whether enough owners will keep the subscription active once the novelty wears off and the remaining limitations of supervised operation become familiar. That retention rate will determine whether the program becomes a durable profit center or a temporary bridge to full unsupervised capability. Over time the structure could shift Tesla's overall earnings mix toward services even before unsupervised deployment arrives.
