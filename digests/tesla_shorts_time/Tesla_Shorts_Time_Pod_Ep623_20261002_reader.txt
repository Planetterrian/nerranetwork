Memory capacity and memory bandwidth trade off differently once a system moves from training to inference at the edge.

Cutting total memory on the AI five and AI six chips lowers die size and therefore cost per unit.

Yet the bandwidth available to the neural network remains the binding constraint for real time control loops in a humanoid.

That distinction explains why the performance impact can stay small even after halving or trimming the memory footprint.

The same arithmetic appears in other high volume robotics programs.

When the limiting factor is how many weights can be moved per inference cycle rather than how many weights can be stored designers accept smaller on chip SRAM in exchange for cheaper fabrication and higher production volume.

Tesla’s decision follows that pattern exactly.

For Optimus the cost reduction matters more than marginal accuracy gains because the robot must reach price points that compete with human labor across thousands of tasks.

Lower memory specifications accelerate that cost curve without changing the fundamental bandwidth limited performance envelope.
