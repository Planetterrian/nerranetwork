### Tesla First Principles

Battery pack architecture decisions made today determine both the operating economics and the service model for vehicles that will run hundreds of thousands of miles with minimal downtime. Tesla’s Cybercab uses four individually sealed modules rather than a single structural block, a choice that accepts a modest reduction in cell volume in exchange for the ability to replace one module without discarding the entire pack. The design also reserves internal space specifically for a future shift to LFP cells, which cost less, generate less heat, and tolerate more charge cycles than the nickel-based 4680 cells currently installed. Robert Sumpf described the reserved volume as protection for moving to a lower energy density chemistry as production scales. Lars Moravy added that part of the extra room simply reflects lower battery requirements than first estimated. The result is a pack that can accept more LFP cells later while still meeting the original range target.

That modular approach directly addresses the cost structure of a robotaxi fleet. Replacing an entire pack before a vehicle reaches half a million miles would erase much of the per-mile savings that high utilization is supposed to deliver. By contrast, swapping a single module keeps the vehicle in service and spreads the capital cost of the battery across more revenue miles. The same logic applies to any high-duty-cycle application, whether the vehicle is owned by Tesla or by a fleet customer. Eric Earley noted that full pack replacement before the half-million-mile mark becomes a killer on cost when the car rarely sits parked. The four-module layout therefore turns a potential service liability into a manageable maintenance item.

The trade-off is visible in the numbers already disclosed. Tesla targeted a pack under 50 kWh for roughly 300 miles of range. Adding extra LFP cells to compensate for lower energy density still keeps total pack cost lower than a denser chemistry that cannot be serviced in the field. Over the life of the vehicle, the savings on replacement cycles and the reduction in downtime matter more than the marginal range lost on any single trip. The architecture therefore embeds a bet that utilization, not peak energy density, will be the dominant variable in robotaxi unit economics. Serviceability built into the design from the start aligns capital recovery with the actual duty cycle the vehicle will see.
