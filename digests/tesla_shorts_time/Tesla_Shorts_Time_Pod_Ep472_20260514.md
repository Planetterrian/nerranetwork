### Tesla First Principles

Taking a step back from today's headlines, the decision to wind down Model S and Model X production at Fremont while ramping Optimus output challenges the assumption that flagship sedans must remain in the lineup to preserve brand prestige.

Factory floor data shows that reallocating space and skilled labor to humanoid robots can deliver higher long-term margins once volume scales, because each robot can be sold repeatedly rather than once per customer. The trade-off is measured in lost revenue from low-volume halo cars versus the potential to sell thousands of robots into logistics and manufacturing roles where labor costs are rising.

Tesla's vertical integration in both battery production and software control loops gives it a structural edge here, since the same cell chemistry and inference hardware developed for vehicles can be repurposed for the robot without starting from scratch. That reuse shortens the timeline from prototype to commercial deployment and reduces the capital intensity that has slowed other robotics efforts. The practical result is a clearer path to a second growth curve that does not depend on selling another million passenger vehicles each year.
