Re-purposing automotive packs and drive units for underground freight changes the cost equation
for both the vehicle maker and the tunneling company.
The components now handle freight without a new powertrain design, cutting the marginal
expense of each additional truck.
That reuse also reduces the number of unique parts the supply chain must support, which matters
when production volumes for the base vehicle remain the priority.
Over repeated cycles, the shared hardware can lower the per-unit cost of every liner truck
that rolls out of the same supplier base.
For Tesla, the arrangement creates a small but steady internal demand signal for cells
and motors that would otherwise sit in finished vehicle inventory.
Over time, those steady, low variability orders can help smooth factory utilization, even
if external vehicle sales fluctuate.
The current modification is an incremental step rather than a complete re-architecture.
