When one car's visual map pose and raw GNSS measurements travel to a server and return corrected by relative vectors from nearby vehicles, the cost of accuracy spreads across the entire local fleet instead of remaining locked inside each machine.

That shift matters because urban GNSS errors are not random noise but repeatable geometry created by the same buildings and atmospheric conditions every day.

A network that measures those errors collectively reduces the per-car compute and sensor burden required to stay inside a lane at highway speed.

A vehicle entering an intersection can inherit a sharper pose from cars already crossing it, even without direct camera overlap.
