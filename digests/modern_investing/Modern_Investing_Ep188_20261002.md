### Investor Education: Asset Allocation
Imagine you allocated your entire TFSA last month into a single Canadian bank stock after seeing strong earnings growth. Your order filled at the prevailing price, yet the position later declined six percent while a balanced ETF holding the same name plus bonds and international equities fell only two percent. The mechanism at work is correlation: when one asset class moves sharply, an undiversified portfolio amplifies the swing because every dollar shares the same risk factor. Professionals reduce this exposure by mapping each holding to its expected volatility and covariance with other assets, then solving for the mix that sits on the efficient frontier. A concrete Canadian example is holding XEQT for full equity exposure, VGRO for a sixty-percent equity and forty-percent fixed-income blend, or XBAL for a more conservative fifty-fifty split; each rebalances automatically so investors avoid the common error of letting winners run unchecked. The pro tip most retail accounts miss is checking the actual correlation matrix rather than assuming bonds always offset stocks; post-twenty-twenty-two data shows that protection weakened when inflation surprised higher. The misconception to avoid is treating age-based rules like one hundred minus your age in stocks as precise; instead, rebalance once a year or when any sleeve drifts more than five percentage points from target.
