### Investor Education: Asset Allocation
Imagine you allocated your full TFSA contribution last week into a single sector ETF after a strong earnings season. Your order filled at the prevailing price, but the real mechanics involve how diversification across asset classes actually reduces portfolio volatility without lowering expected returns. Modern Portfolio Theory shows that combining equities, bonds, and alternatives moves the portfolio toward the efficient frontier where risk drops for the same return level. Strategic allocation sets long-term weights such as sixty percent equities and forty percent bonds, while tactical shifts respond to valuation signals like the current TSX trading near five percent below highs. Canadian examples include all-equity ETFs such as XEQT for younger investors or balanced options like VGRO and XBAL that automatically rebalance. Bonds still provide ballast post-twenty twenty-two because their correlation with stocks can turn negative during equity drawdowns. Age-based rules like one hundred minus age in equities oversimplify because they ignore personal risk tolerance and time horizon. Rebalancing on a threshold basis, such as when an asset class drifts more than five percent from target, outperforms calendar rebalancing by capturing mean reversion. The common misconception is that adding more stocks always increases risk. The fix is to run a simple correlation check on your current holdings using free portfolio tools before the next contribution.
