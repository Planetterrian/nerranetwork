### Strategy Spotlight
A covered-call ETF strategy lets investors collect premium income on holdings they already own by selling out-of-the-money calls each month. Today’s flat equity tape and elevated volatility make the approach attractive because premiums remain rich while downside risk stays contained. Implementation is straightforward on Canadian platforms: buy an ETF such as ZWC or QYLD inside a TFSA, then sell the front-month call at the 30-delta strike using the brokerage’s options chain. The tactic has historically added 4–8% annualized yield in sideways or mildly bullish regimes but caps upside during strong rallies and can underperform in sharp drops. Position sizing matters—limit covered-call exposure to 15–20% of the overall portfolio so the income sleeve does not dominate total return. Track the strategy’s net performance against a plain-vanilla equity ETF each quarter to confirm the income premium justifies the opportunity cost. ### Investor Education: Risk Management — Position Sizing and Stop Losses
Imagine you bought 200 shares of a healthcare name at $480 last week after an analyst upgrade; your order filled cleanly at the open. The position now represents 18% of your TFSA because you sized it by dollars rather than by risk, and a 6% adverse move would wipe out an entire month of expected gains. The mechanism is simple: maximum loss equals position size multiplied by distance to stop; if you risk only 1% of total capital per trade, a 6% stop requires the position to be no larger than 16.7% of the account. Professionals always calculate the dollar risk first, then back into share count using the planned stop level. The biggest mistake with position sizing is letting a “high-conviction” idea exceed the 1–2% risk budget. Instead, always pre-define the exit price and the maximum portfolio impact before the order is placed.
