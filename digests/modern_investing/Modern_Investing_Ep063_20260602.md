### Strategy Spotlight
Custom indexing lets investors build a fully personalized index portfolio without paying management fees, a structure Questrade just launched for Canadian accounts. The approach combines the low-cost, rules-based nature of indexing with the ability to exclude or overweight specific holdings to match tax, ESG, or sector preferences inside a TFSA or RRSP. In today’s environment of concentrated AI leadership and Canadian economic uncertainty, this tool gives retail investors a way to maintain broad market exposure while avoiding names that conflict with personal risk rules. Implementation starts by linking your Questrade account to the Custom Indexing interface, selecting a base index such as the S&P/TSX 60, then applying up to 50 custom exclusions or tilts before the next rebalance. The strategy has historically worked best in sideways or mildly trending markets where fee drag compounds over multi-year holding periods; the main risk is over-customization that inadvertently increases tracking error beyond the investor’s tolerance. ### Investor Education: Order Flow and Slippage Mechanics
Imagine you bought HPE shares yesterday morning after the earnings release when the stock was already up 20% pre-market. Your market order filled at the quoted price, but the actual execution landed three cents worse than the last traded price shown on your screen. The mechanism is straightforward: when retail flow hits a thinly traded name during a catalyst spike, market makers widen the spread and absorb the imbalance, so the effective fill price moves against you by the amount of that widening—often 5–15 cents on a fast-moving stock. What most retail investors don’t realize is that the displayed bid-ask quote is only the top of the book; the next levels can be much thinner, and a single large order can walk the book several cents in seconds. The professionals always check Level 2 depth and average daily volume before sizing a momentum trade, then use limit orders or iceberg orders to avoid telegraphing size. The biggest mistake with order flow is assuming the quoted price is the price you will receive. Instead, always size positions so that even a 0.3% slippage hit still leaves the trade inside your risk budget.
