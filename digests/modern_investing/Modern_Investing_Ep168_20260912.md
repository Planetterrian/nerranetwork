### Strategy Spotlight
Not every sell signal requires an immediate exit, especially when fundamentals remain intact. Today's market shows how countertrend signals can flag short-term overbought conditions without marking a true top. Check the underlying earnings trajectory and competitive position first; if the stock trades well below your estimated intrinsic value, the signal often serves as a pause on new buying rather than a sell order. In early-stage breakouts the same caution applies—volume and sector rotation data can override a lone momentum warning. Implement this by running a quick fundamentals screen in your brokerage platform before acting on any RSI or MACD crossover. The approach works best in high-conviction names where you already hold a margin of safety, but it carries the risk of holding through deeper pullbacks if the broader rotation accelerates. Investors can apply the same logic by reviewing the most recent quarterly revenue growth and free-cash-flow trends before deciding whether to trim or hold. This nuance prevents unnecessary turnover in taxable accounts and preserves long-term compounding in registered plans such as TFSAs. ### Investor Education: Passive Flows and Price Discovery
Imagine you added a broad-market ETF to your TFSA last month after seeing steady inflows into large-cap names. Your purchase executed at the prevailing ask, yet the real mechanism at work is that index funds must buy the exact constituents in market-cap weights regardless of valuation, creating mechanical demand that can lift prices even when earnings growth slows. Over the past decade this loop has concentrated gains in the largest holdings, but professionals still set marginal prices through active trading that remains many times larger than net passive inflows. The pro tip is to watch the ratio of ETF creation/redemption activity to total trading volume; when that ratio spikes above 15% on a single name it often signals the mechanical bid is dominating. Most retail investors assume index buying is always supportive, yet the concrete risk appears when active managers collectively step away and the self-reinforcing loop reverses. Track the percentage of S&P 500 volume still executed by discretionary accounts rather than assuming passive dominance will continue indefinitely. Canadian investors can monitor this dynamic through the TSX's own ETF creation data published monthly by the exchange, which reveals whether mechanical flows are overwhelming fundamental price discovery in domestic names. When the ratio of passive to active volume exceeds historical norms, rebalancing toward equal-weight or factor-based ETFs can reduce exposure to the largest constituents that benefit most from the mechanical bid.
