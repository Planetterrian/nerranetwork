### Strategy Spotlight
Support and resistance levels drawn from horizontal price history provide a more objective framework than trend lines because they rely on repeated tests at the same price rather than subjective slope. Today's market shows the S&P 500 with only thirty-two point five percent of stocks beating the index, a setup where clear horizontal levels help separate noise from genuine rotation signals. Investors can implement this by pulling a two-year daily chart in a platform like TradingView or the free OptionsPlay charting tool, marking every prior high and low that saw at least two touches, then waiting for a close beyond the level on volume above the twenty-day average before acting. This approach worked well during the twenty twenty-two bear market when many growth names respected the same horizontal ceilings on multiple retests before breaking lower. The risk is that low-volume breaks can produce false signals, which is why pairing the level with a volume filter reduces whipsaws. ### Investor Education: TFSA Habits That Shift in Retirement
Imagine you have been maxing your TFSA every year by treating it as a pure growth vehicle and never withdrawing even small amounts to rebalance. The mechanism that changes at retirement is the contribution room itself: once you stop earning eligible income the annual room stops growing, yet many investors continue aggressive equity allocations that were optimal during accumulation. The pro tip is to run a simple five-minute projection in your brokerage's retirement calculator using your current TFSA balance, expected withdrawal rate, and a conservative seven percent equity return assumption to see whether the same allocation still fits a decumulation glide path. The misconception to avoid is assuming the habits that maximized room during working years will automatically serve you once the contribution tap turns off. Withdrawal sequencing also matters because early retirement years often coincide with higher spending needs before government benefits begin. Rebalancing within the TFSA can preserve room for future contributions if small withdrawals are made strategically before age seventy-one. The shift from accumulation to decumulation changes the optimal risk level because sequence-of-returns risk becomes the dominant concern rather than long-term compounding alone.
