### Strategy Spotlight
Yield-curve steepness analysis helps investors decide whether to favor short-duration bonds or extend into longer maturities when central-bank decisions loom. The 2-year Treasury yield stood at 4.7494 percent while the 30-year reached 5.3200 percent, producing a 57-basis-point spread that indicates the market anticipates limited near-term easing. An investor opens a brokerage bond screener, selects investment-grade corporate issues maturing between 2028 and 2030, and compares each yield to the 2-year benchmark to locate names offering at least 80 basis points of additional carry over the short end. The same screener can be filtered for option-adjusted spreads so the investor sees how much extra compensation the market demands for credit risk at each maturity point. This technique draws directly from the forward curve embedded in the CME FedWatch Tool probabilities, where a 76.5 percent chance of a 25-basis-point hike by the December 9 meeting already prices modest further tightening. The concrete implementation step is to set a price alert on the 10-year/2-year spread at the 60-basis-point level and review any intermediate corporate holdings only if the spread widens before the next FOMC statement. The approach relies on the inverse relationship between yields and prices, so a steepening curve that lifts longer yields faster than shorter ones reduces the present value of distant cash flows on intermediate bonds. When the curve instead flattens because inflation data surprises higher, even carefully selected carry trades can lose ground as the entire curve shifts upward together. ### Investor Education: How Central-Bank Decisions Transmit to Corporate Bond Spreads
You hold a 5-year investment-grade corporate bond bought at a 120-basis-point spread over the 5-year Treasury. The Fed leaves rates unchanged but the chair’s statement hints at a higher neutral rate; within 90 minutes the Treasury yield rises 8 basis points while your corporate bond’s spread widens to 135 basis points, cutting the price by roughly 0.7 percent even though the policy rate itself did not move. The mechanism is that dealers widen credit spreads when they anticipate slower future rate cuts, because the present value of the issuer’s cash flows falls and the risk of refinancing at higher rates increases. A professional habit is to check the option-adjusted spread on your bond every time the FedWatch Tool probability of a December hike moves more than 10 percentage points; if the spread has already widened 15 basis points or more, you reduce the position size before the statement rather than after. The common misconception is that an unchanged policy rate means unchanged bond prices; the fix is to treat the forward curve and credit-spread reaction as the actual trade signal, not the headline rate decision itself. When the 10-year yield sits at 5.0134 percent and the 2-year at 4.7494 percent, any statement language that shifts the expected path of the federal funds rate changes the discount rate applied to every corporate cash flow beyond the next meeting. Investors who monitor only the spot rate miss the repricing that occurs in the 90-minute window after the press conference when the entire curve and the credit-spread matrix adjust simultaneously.
