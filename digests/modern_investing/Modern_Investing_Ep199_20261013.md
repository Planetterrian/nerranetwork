### Investor Education: ETF Series Structures
Imagine you bought units of a new Canadian ETF series the day after its launch and noticed the management expense ratio listed separately from the mutual-fund version. The mechanism is that ETF series units trade on the exchange with their own ticker while sharing the same underlying portfolio and manager, but they carry a distinct fee schedule and settlement process. In practice this means the bid-ask spread on the ETF series can widen to several cents in the first weeks of trading even when the mutual-fund version shows no price movement. The pro habit is to compare the ETF series MER against the mutual-fund trailer fee before purchase and confirm the series has at least $50 million in assets to keep spreads tight. The common misconception is assuming every ETF series is automatically cheaper than its mutual-fund counterpart; the fix is to read the prospectus fee table for the specific series before placing the order. ETF series structures also allow wider use of derivatives in alternative mutual fund categories, as seen with the Mackenzie Global Equity & Gold Overlay ETF launch that pairs global equities with gold futures. This structure lets investors access rules-based factor strategies through the new Modus brand funds without needing separate account types. Liquidity improves once assets under management exceed typical early-stage thresholds, reducing the chance of wide spreads during volatile sessions. Canadian investors using TFSA accounts benefit from the tax-deferred treatment of distributions, but must still track adjusted cost base for any return-of-capital components that appear in the fee table.
