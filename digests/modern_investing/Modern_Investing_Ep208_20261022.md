### Strategy Spotlight
Capex surges like the one reported by Meta can serve as a leading indicator for sector allocation shifts. Investors track year-over-year changes in capital expenditure across big-tech names to gauge whether AI infrastructure spending is accelerating or peaking. One practical implementation is to screen for companies whose capex-to-revenue ratio has risen at least 30 percent over the prior four quarters using a brokerage screener or free platform such as Finviz. Historically this approach has worked best during the early-to-mid phase of a technology cycle when hardware and data-center demand outpaces software margins. The main risk is that elevated spending can pressure near-term free cash flow and trigger multiple compression if revenue growth disappoints. Pair the screen with a same-day volume check above the 20-day average before sizing any position. Meta spent $33.9 billion on capex this past quarter, up from $18.8 billion in the same quarter last year, illustrating the scale of continued investment in AI-related infrastructure. Canadian investors can apply the same screen to TSX-listed names with U.S. exposure to capture parallel spending trends without duplicating U.S.-only holdings. The Meta figure represents a near-doubling in a single year and underscores how quickly AI buildout costs are scaling for hyperscalers. When screening, focus on the ratio of capex to revenue rather than the absolute dollar amount, because a smaller company increasing its ratio aggressively can signal the same infrastructure wave. The same discipline applies when scanning suppliers to these hyperscalers, where order visibility often appears one or two quarters after the capex announcement. Investors who wait for the revenue confirmation rather than jumping at the capex headline alone tend to avoid the multiple-compression phase that follows when spending outpaces results.
