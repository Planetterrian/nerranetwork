Motley Fool Canada walks through a planning tactic most people still leave until the last weeks of the year: move the annual TFSA contribution as soon as the new room opens, instead of waiting for a December cash scramble.

The twenty twenty-six contribution room is the figure that article uses, and the mechanic is a simple transfer from a non-registered account or a cash balance on any brokerage platform.

January first is when that new room becomes available, so the transfer can sit in the account before year-end bills and bonuses start competing for the same dollars.

The approach has worked best in years when contribution-room changes or tax-year deadlines create last-minute rushes, which is exactly when people miss the window.

The main risk is opportunity cost if that cash earns higher interest outside the TFSA, but that drag is usually small relative to the planning certainty you gain.

You are not chasing a bigger immediate tax saving with this move. You are removing deadline risk and the chance that December cash-flow pressure leaves the room unfilled.

Implementation is the boring part on purpose: open the TFSA contribution ticket, pull the cash across, and leave the investing decisions for after the room is locked.

Open your brokerage statement, find the contribution-room line, and set a calendar reminder for the first business day of twenty twenty-seven to move the full annual amount in immediately.

That reminder is the whole tactic. The contribution is the plan; December heroics are the failure mode.
