### Strategy Spotlight
Tax-efficient charitable giving lets investors donate appreciated securities directly to avoid realizing capital gains. The Globe and Mail article explains that clients do not have to pay the taxes they would otherwise incur on the capital gains realized when selling the securities. An investor holding shares that have risen 40% can transfer them in-kind to a registered charity and deduct the full current market value without triggering the inclusion rate on gains. The step is to instruct your brokerage to transfer the shares to the charity’s account rather than selling them yourself, then claim the donation receipt at the higher value on your tax return. This approach works best inside non-registered accounts where gains would otherwise be taxable; it fails when the position is underwater or when the charity cannot accept in-kind transfers. The mechanism rewards planning around the annual donation limit and the capital-gains inclusion rate that applies on sale. Investors can combine this with TFSA contribution room calculations to maximize after-tax impact across multiple accounts. The technique requires confirming the charity’s ability to receive electronic transfers before initiating the move. When executed correctly it preserves the full market value for the charity while eliminating the immediate tax liability that would arise from a cash sale.
