### Investor Education: Tax-Loss Harvesting Mechanics
Imagine you bought an ETF tracking Canadian banks last spring at a higher price and it now sits ten percent below your cost base inside your TFSA. The superficial loss rule means you cannot simply sell and repurchase the identical ETF within thirty days without losing the ability to claim the capital loss for tax purposes. Instead, you sell the original holding, immediately buy a similar but not identical ETF such as one focused on broad Canadian equities, and hold that substitute for the full thirty-day window before switching back if desired. Professionals always check the T5008 slip issued by their broker in February to confirm the adjusted cost base and reported proceeds match their records. The capital gains inclusion rate change means losses now offset a higher portion of gains, making the harvest more valuable for accounts that realize gains elsewhere. The concrete misconception to avoid is treating any sale within thirty days as harmless; the fix is mapping the exact thirty-day window on your calendar the moment you execute the harvest.
