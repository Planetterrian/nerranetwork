### Segment 1 — The Open
On May 20, 2026, Space Exploration Technologies Corp. filed its public S-1 registration statement with the SEC, converting months of draft-stage speculation into a concrete timeline that points to pricing around June 11 and first trading as early as June 12 under the ticker SPCX. By the end of this episode listeners will know exactly what the filing confirms about valuation targets, underwriters, and Starlink’s contribution to revenue, and they will leave with a practical three-horizon framework for deciding whether and how to participate once the stock begins trading.

### Segment 2 — The Background
SpaceX filed a confidential draft registration around April 1, 2026, then converted it to a public S-1 on May 20 under Registration No. 333-, making the prospectus available on EDGAR. Reporting from Reuters and CNBC indicates the company is aiming for a listing on Nasdaq as early as June 12, with the roadshow expected to begin around June 4 and pricing targeted for June 11. The valuation range discussed in recent coverage has settled around $1.75–2 trillion, with Bloomberg noting on May 29 that the target had been adjusted to “at least $1.8 trillion.” The deal is structured to include Starlink connectivity operations, launch and space services, and xAI, with Goldman Sachs serving as lead left bookrunner alongside Morgan Stanley, Bank of America, Citigroup, and J.P. Morgan. Starlink generated $11.4 billion in revenue in 2025, representing 61 percent of the company’s $18.7 billion total and the only segment showing operating profit, while the overall business posted a net loss in Q1 2026. A potential retail allocation near 30 percent of the float has been mentioned in secondary commentary but remains unconfirmed in primary filings or major reporting. The 2026 IPO calendar features several large technology and AI-related offerings, creating a market backdrop of elevated investor interest in growth names even as interest-rate and risk sentiment remain mixed.

### Segment 3 — The Core Analysis
The S-1 shows that Elon Musk holds approximately 42 percent of equity but 85 percent of voting power, preserving control through a dual-class structure that will matter for governance questions after listing. Starlink is positioned as the cash-generative core, with more than 10 million subscribers and continued growth into Q1 2026, while launch operations and xAI continue to consume capital. Underwriters typically allocate the majority of shares to institutional clients in the initial offering, so most retail investors will obtain shares only after trading begins unless their broker offers a directed-share program. Reading the prospectus requires focus on revenue concentration, the path from operating income to free cash flow, use of proceeds, and any government-contract exposure that could affect margins. Canadian investors must also weigh TFSA, RRSP, or FHSA eligibility against the fact that the shares will trade in U.S. dollars, introducing currency risk that can be hedged or left unhedged depending on account type. Valuation discipline starts with comparing the targeted multiple to other high-growth technology names already public, recognizing that the $1.8 trillion figure implies aggressive assumptions about subscriber growth and launch economics. Platforms such as Interactive Brokers, Questrade, Wealthsimple, and Robinhood will likely offer post-listing access, but conditional offer-to-buy windows before pricing remain broker-specific and are not guaranteed. The filing does not yet detail exact retail allocation mechanics, so investors should monitor subsequent amendments and broker announcements rather than assume early access.

### Segment 4 — The Framework (How to Think About It / How to Act)
A disciplined short-term approach treats the opening days as the highest-risk window: first-day pops are common but frequently retrace, and wide bid-ask spreads plus potential volatility around the open make chasing the initial print expensive for anyone without a pre-set rule. Investors can decide in advance whether they will participate at all on day one or wait for volume to normalize, thereby removing emotion from the decision. In the medium term the 180-day lock-up expiry creates a predictable supply event when early investors and insiders become free to sell; waiting for that window or dollar-cost-averaging afterward has historically reduced entry prices for comparable names. The first two earnings reports as a public company will supply the initial evidence on whether Starlink margins and subscriber metrics translate cleanly into GAAP results. Over the long term the thesis rests on recurring satellite broadband revenue, Starship-driven launch cost reductions, and the broader total addressable market cited in the filing, balanced against key-person risk, ongoing capital intensity, and competition in both connectivity and launch markets. Canadians should consider whether a taxable account or a registered plan better accommodates the currency exposure and single-stock volatility limits they are willing to accept. Position sizing should reflect the difference between a diversified ETF holding and a concentrated growth name, with clear criteria for what subscriber or margin data would confirm or break the investment case over multiple years. The same horizon framework can be reused for any future large-cap IPO: define the entry rule for each period before the stock begins trading, then execute only according to those pre-written rules.

### Segment 5 — The Risks & The Other Side
Historical data on large IPOs shows that many names experience first-day gains followed by underperformance over the subsequent one to three years once lock-up supply arrives and fundamentals are tested in public reporting. The current research notes that some analysts view the valuation as aggressive relative to a sum-of-parts breakdown, while others highlight the optionality embedded in Starship economics and future businesses. Execution risk remains material because launch cadence, satellite deployment, and regulatory approvals for spectrum and orbital slots must continue at scale. Musk’s concentrated voting control reduces the ability of public shareholders to influence strategy, which can be viewed as either a strength or a governance concern depending on execution outcomes. Competition in satellite broadband from established and new entrants, plus the capital demands of both the launch and AI segments, could pressure margins even if Starlink grows. The base rate that IPOs tend to lag broad indices in the first few years after listing is well documented across multiple market cycles and should inform any position-size decision.

### Segment 6 — The Takeaways
Pre-define separate entry and sizing rules for the first trading week, the lock-up expiry window, and the multi-year hold period rather than reacting to price action. Focus first on the quality and concentration of revenue and the path to sustainable free cash flow in the S-1 before considering valuation multiples. Treat any single-name growth stock as a satellite position whose size is capped by personal volatility tolerance and overall portfolio construction rules. Watch for amendments to the S-1 and broker announcements on retail access in the coming weeks, then compare those details against the three-horizon framework before taking any action.
