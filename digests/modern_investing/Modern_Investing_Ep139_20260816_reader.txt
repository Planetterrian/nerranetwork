Now, here is something most retail investors get wrong, and it cost me real friction before I mapped it properly.

Imagine you just decided to add a Canadian bank stock to your TFSA after an earnings beat. Your order fills at the posted price. The actual cost of that fill depends on which brokerage you used and how its fee structure and market access interact with the ticket.

Wealthsimple Trade is commission-free on Canadian and U.S. stocks with a clean mobile app, but it limits advanced order types and has no margin. That is the right box for a simple buy-and-hold TFSA investor who rarely needs U.S. options or real-time Level two data.

Questrade charges low commissions, gives free ETF purchases, and offers solid margin rates. That fits the active Canadian investor who wants occasional U.S. exposure without paying up per trade.

Interactive Brokers has the lowest margin rates and the broadest international access, but the platform complexity will overwhelm anyone who only needs basic TFSA execution. Bank-owned desks like TD Direct or RBC Direct plug into your chequing account and then charge you for the privilege with higher commissions and weaker mobile tools.

The pro tip most people skip is mechanical. Check whether the brokerage passes through exchange fees on U.S. trades, and check whether it supports automatic dividend reinvestment inside registered accounts. Those two lines on the fee schedule move more money over a decade than the advertised commission.

The misconception to kill is that the free platform is always cheapest. Wider spreads and missing order types force you to a second venue for a single ticket, and that second ticket is where the free trade becomes the expensive one.
