### Investor Education: Mechanics of Stop-Loss Placement in Volatile Markets
You entered Celestica at the open after its 8 percent rebound on AI news and placed a mental 10 percent stop because the stock had already dropped 12 percent the prior session. The mechanism that matters is the difference between a hard stop that triggers on the exchange book versus a mental stop that waits for your next login: during the 37 percent monthly drawdown the bid-ask spread widened to 40 cents while volume spiked above the 20-day average, so a hard stop at $152 would have filled near the low while a mental stop left you exposed to the next gap. Celestica nosedived close to 12 percent in a single day on AI chip jitters before bouncing back very sharply up more than 8 percent in Wednesday’s session after impressive third-quarter numbers and a hiked sales outlook. The pro habit is to set the stop at the most recent swing low on the daily chart plus one average true range, then enter it as a stop-limit order with a 2 percent buffer so slippage does not turn a 10 percent risk into 13 percent. The misconception to avoid is treating the stop as a prediction that the thesis is broken rather than a pre-committed exit that removes emotion once the position is live.
