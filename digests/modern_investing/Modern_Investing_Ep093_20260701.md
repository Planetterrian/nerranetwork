### Strategy Spotlight
Sector rotation into international equities works by systematically overweighting regions showing relative strength rather than chasing single-country mega-caps. Today's data shows U.S. big-tech delivered solid but not leading returns while emerging-market and non-U.S. tech names outperformed, creating a measurable spread. Canadian investors can implement this by allocating a sleeve of their TFSA to a broad international ETF such as XEF or VIU and rebalancing quarterly against the NASDAQ Composite. The approach has historically delivered excess returns during periods when U.S. valuations stretched and global earnings revisions turned positive. Risks include currency translation losses and delayed reaction when U.S. names re-accelerate. The key metric to watch is the rolling 3-month relative performance of the MSCI ACWI ex-USA versus the NASDAQ. ### Investor Education: Bid-Ask Spread Dynamics in Low-Volume Names
Imagine you bought a mid-cap Canadian name in your TFSA yesterday morning on a positive headline; your market order filled at $18.45. Between the moment you clicked and the fill, the bid-ask spread on that name widened from 3 cents to 14 cents because two market makers stepped away during the first 20 minutes of trading. The mechanism is simple: quoted size at the inside bid dropped from 1,200 shares to 300 while ask size stayed thin, so your order walked the book and paid the wider spread. What most retail investors don't realize is that the effective spread can be measured in real time on most platforms by comparing the mid-price to the actual execution price. The professionals always check Level 2 depth and average daily volume before entering anything under 500,000 shares ADV. The biggest mistake with spread risk is assuming the posted quote will still be there when your order arrives. Instead, always use a limit order set no more than one tick inside the current ask on names below 1 million shares ADV.
