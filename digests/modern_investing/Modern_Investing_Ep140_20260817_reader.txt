Here is something most retail investors get wrong, and it cost me a clean rebalance before I learned to ignore the pretty pie chart.

Imagine you opened your TFSA on July first with a sixty-forty equity-bond target after a strong June reset. By September thirtieth the equity sleeve is sitting at sixty-eight percent because one holding ran up twenty-five percent, and the brokerage screen still draws the original sixty-forty slices as if nothing happened.

That is drift. Time-weighted returns compound the overweight automatically, so the real allocation walks away from target without a single new deposit or withdrawal.

Professionals pull the full transaction history and calculate both time-weighted return, which judges the quality of the holdings, and money-weighted return, which judges your personal timing, before they touch a rebalance ticket.

The move most people skip is exporting the realized-gain report first. That one CSV tells you exactly how much room remains in the zero percent capital-gains bracket for the rest of a low-income year, which is the number that should decide whether you trim inside the TFSA now or wait.

The misconception is treating the brokerage target-allocation pie as gospel. It ignores dollar drift and tax-lot detail, and those two things are the actual trade.

Your homework: open the brokerage app, pull the last ninety days of realized-gain transactions for the TFSA, and write down the dollar amount still available in that zero percent long-term capital-gains bracket before year-end.
