### Strategy Spotlight
Stop-funding-losing-trades is a portfolio allocation rule that redirects new capital only to positions showing positive momentum rather than averaging down on underperformers. The rule draws a direct parallel to business investment decisions: when two family members pitch ideas at Thanksgiving and one turns $10,000 into $20,000 while the other struggles, rational capital goes to the winner. Today's sector-rotation data makes the rule timely because financials, energy, and health care already show confirmed leadership on both daily and weekly timeframes while solar and infrastructure lag. Implement it by running a weekly scan that ranks holdings by 20-day average volume and relative strength, then size new buys only in the top tier. The approach worked best in 2022-2023 when growth names rotated sharply and capital preservation mattered more than recovery bets. Risk arises when a laggard suddenly receives a volume-confirmed catalyst that the screen misses. Investors using platforms like Wealthsimple or Questrade can set up simple relative-strength alerts to automate the ranking step. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/OptionsPlay/status/2093337810312851541)

### Investor Education: Reading Volume Confirmation Before Catalyst Entries
Imagine you spot the new China AI Tigers LLM ETF $TGRZ on a launch headline and place an order at the open. Your fill arrives, yet the position drifts lower because the first-hour volume never cleared the 20-day average. The mechanism is simple: without that volume spike, institutional desks have not yet committed size, so retail flow alone cannot sustain the move and spreads stay wide for the first 30-60 minutes. On a thin-volume launch day the ETF may print only a few thousand shares in the opening hour, leaving the bid-ask spread at 20-30 cents instead of the tighter 5-cent level that appears once average daily volume is established. Professionals always cross-check the same-day volume print against the 20-day line before treating any news-driven pop as confirmed. The concrete misconception to avoid is assuming every headline equals immediate follow-through; the one-sentence fix is to require volume above the 20-day average on the entry day itself.
