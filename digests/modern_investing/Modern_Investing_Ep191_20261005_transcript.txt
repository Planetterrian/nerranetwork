Imagine you bought a technology ETF last week when AI infrastructure headlines lifted the sector.
Your order filled at the quoted price, but the real story lies in how the chart behaved between
your click and that fill. Support and resistance levels on the daily chart act as zones where
buying or selling pressure historically clusters, often near the 50 day or 200 day moving average.
Volume confirmation matters because a breakout above resistance on above average volume
has shown higher follow through rates and back tests than the same move on light volume.
RSI above 70 signals overbought conditions while below 30 signals oversold,
yet these readings work best as timing tools rather than standalone buy or sell triggers.
MACD crossovers can highlight momentum shifts, but they lag in strong trends and produce
whip saws in sideways markets.
The honest limitation is that technical analysis improves entry and exit timing more reliably
than it predicts which names will outperform fundamentally.
The 50-day moving average often serves as dynamic support during uptrends, while the 200-day
moving average filters longer term direction and helps avoid false breakouts.
The misconception to avoid is treating every chart pattern as predictive when only those
backed by volume and multiple time frame confirmation have shown statistical edge in repeated studies.
