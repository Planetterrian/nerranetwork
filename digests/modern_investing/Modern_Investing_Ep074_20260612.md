### Investor Education: How IPO Lock-Up Expirations Actually Move Prices
Imagine you bought shares of a newly public company on its first trading day at the offering price. Your order filled cleanly, but the real supply pressure arrives 180 days later when insiders can sell. The mechanism works through staggered release schedules: early investors and employees face sequential lock-ups that create predictable volume spikes, often pushing the stock 8-15% lower in the week before each tranche expires. Most retail investors assume the post-IPO pop is permanent and get caught holding through these engineered supply events. Professionals always check the S-1 filing for the exact lock-up language and set alerts 10 days before each scheduled release. The biggest mistake is treating the IPO price as a floor. Instead, always map the full lock-up calendar before sizing any position.
