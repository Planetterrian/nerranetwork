### Investor Education: How to Do a Quarterly Portfolio Review
Imagine you bought an energy ETF last quarter when oil supply headlines first emerged. Your target allocation was 15% but drift from price moves has pushed it to 19%. The mechanism here is simple: rebalancing requires calculating current weights using end-of-quarter market values, then comparing against your written policy ranges. Most retail investors skip the tax layer and realize gains unnecessarily; professionals always check realized versus unrealized gains first to decide whether loss harvesting in non-registered accounts can offset the rebalance. A practical pro tip is to export your brokerage CSV, sum dividend income separately from price appreciation, and subtract total fees paid over the quarter before deciding on new positions. The biggest mistake with quarterly reviews is treating them as performance scorecards instead of allocation and tax-planning exercises. Instead, always run the decision checklist in this order: asset allocation drift, position sizing limits, tax-loss opportunities, then new capital deployment.
