### Strategy Spotlight
Custom benchmarking lets TFSA investors measure performance against a personalized ETF mix that reflects the exact dates and dollar amounts of their contributions rather than a single static index. The Globe and Mail article describes a retired Albertan who weights several ETFs by contribution timing to separate genuine outperformance from simple market beta. To implement it, pull your TFSA contribution history from your brokerage, assign each deposit to the ETF allocation you would have held at that moment, then calculate the blended return for comparison against your actual holdings. This approach works best when contribution dates cluster around market lows or highs because it removes the timing distortion that a plain TSX or S&P 500 benchmark introduces. It fails when investors cherry-pick favorable ETF weights after the fact or ignore rebalancing costs inside the account. The technique is especially useful for Canadian investors who add to their TFSA irregularly and want an honest read on whether active picks are adding value. By rebuilding the benchmark from actual cash-flow dates, investors can see whether a holding-company name like POW.TO truly beat what a contribution-weighted mix would have delivered over the same periods. The method also highlights how irregular additions during volatile stretches can make a static index look either too generous or too harsh depending on when money arrived in the account. ### Investor Education: The Mechanics of Custom Benchmarking in Tax-Advantaged Accounts
If you held POW.TO in your TFSA and compared its return only to the TSX Composite, the gap might look large, yet weighting the benchmark to your actual contribution dates and amounts can shrink or reverse that gap. The mechanism works by treating each contribution as a separate "bucket" that earns the return of the chosen ETF mix from the day the money arrived, then summing the buckets to create a personalized hurdle rate. A pro habit is to export the contribution log once per quarter and run the weighted calculation in a simple spreadsheet before reviewing any individual holdings. The common misconception is that a single broad index already gives a fair performance picture; the fix is to rebuild the benchmark from the contribution timeline so the comparison reflects the cash flows you actually experienced. When contributions arrive in clusters near market peaks or troughs, the custom benchmark reveals whether outperformance came from security selection or simply from the timing of new capital entering the account. Investors who skip this step often credit or blame their picks for results that were largely driven by when they happened to add fresh TFSA room. Running the calculation quarterly also surfaces any drift between the intended ETF mix and the actual holdings, prompting rebalancing decisions that keep the performance test honest.
