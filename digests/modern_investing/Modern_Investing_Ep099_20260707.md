### Strategy Spotlight
A volume-confirmation filter requires that any momentum or catalyst trade must print volume at least 50% above its 20-day average on the entry day before capital is committed. Today’s mixed global session shows why this matters: AI stocks rebounded on Wall Street but Korean shares slumped on lighter volume, illustrating how headline-driven moves without participation often fade. Implementation is straightforward on any brokerage platform—pull the 20-day average volume line on a daily chart and compare it to the current bar before entering. This approach worked best during the 2023-2024 semiconductor rotation when volume spikes preceded sustained sector outperformance; the risk is missing fast gaps that later prove durable. The same filter would have kept the portfolio out of several low-volume defense-related bounces reported this week. ### Investor Education: Technical Analysis Mechanics
Imagine you bought an energy ETF last week after an Iraq output headline pushed crude higher; your order filled at the ask during the first 30 minutes when volume was still thin. The mechanism that actually moved price was the 50-day moving average acting as dynamic resistance while RSI(14) climbed from 48 to 62 on expanding volume—yet the 200-day average remained 4% above, capping upside until a close above both averages occurs. What most retail investors miss is that a golden cross (50-day crossing above 200-day) only carries statistical edge when accompanied by volume at least 30% above average; without it, the pattern often fails within two weeks. Professionals always check the MACD histogram slope before the cross itself, because a flattening histogram signals fading momentum even if the lines are about to cross. The biggest mistake with technical analysis is treating every support or resistance level as equally valid. Instead, always weight levels by the volume traded at that price in the prior 20 sessions.
