### Investor Education: How Lock-Up Expirations Create Selling Pressure
You bought shares of a newly public AI company on the first trading day after its debut and watched the price hold steady for two weeks before sliding. Lock-up agreements prevent insiders from selling for 180 days after the IPO, so the first wave of sales begins on the expiration date listed in the prospectus. Anthropic’s expirations start December 15, which means roughly 180 days of restricted supply ends then and additional tranches follow over subsequent months. Professionals set calendar alerts 30 days before each scheduled release and reduce position size in advance rather than waiting for volume to spike. The common misconception is that an IPO debut price already reflects all future selling; in reality the lock-up schedule creates a predictable supply overhang that only appears once the restriction lifts. The article notes that Anthropic could see a flood of selling over the next few months as multiple expiration windows open sequentially. Investors who ignore the calendar often find themselves competing against large blocks that hit the tape on the exact date the lock-up lifts. Setting a position-size reduction rule 30 days ahead converts that known supply event into a planned exit rather than an unexpected drop. The same pattern has repeated across prior high-profile AI and tech listings where the first post-lock-up session produced above-average volume and below-average returns for holders who stayed fully sized.
