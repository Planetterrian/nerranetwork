Imagine borrowing at 6% to purchase shares in a non-registered account that later rose 8% before you moved the position into a TFSA.
The interest on that loan stays deductible on your T1 return because the Canada revenue agency follows the original purpose of the borrowing rather than the final account location.
Interest deductibility hinges on the income-producing intent at the time the debt was created, not on the account type at any later date.
Most investors assume the TFSA transfer itself cancels prior deductions, yet the rule tracks whether the securities continue to generate taxable income outside the TFSA.
Professionals keep the original loan documents and the exact transfer date clearly recorded to support the claim if questions arise.
