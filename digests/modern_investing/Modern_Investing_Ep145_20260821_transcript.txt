When the treasury buys $X billion in a specific maturity, it removes supply from that segment.
Prices move up and yields move down in a mechanical way that does not require a fed rate cut.
Most retail investors miss that the effect is localized to the purchased maturities and can reverse quickly once the program size is fully priced in.
Professionals always check the treasury's announced purchase sizes and the exact maturities before assuming a broad rate rally.
