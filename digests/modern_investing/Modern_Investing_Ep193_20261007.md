Publicly traded REITs such as SmartCentres issue debentures like the new three hundred million dollar Series AE at four point seven nine five percent to fund property operations. These vehicles trade on the TSX and distribute income monthly while the trust handles property management and leasing. Mortgage investment corporations offer another route by pooling loans secured against real estate and paying out interest income that flows through to unitholders. Rental property ownership requires direct capital and effort but allows leverage and potential capital gains treatment on sale. Tax treatment differs sharply: REIT distributions often count as return of capital reducing adjusted cost base while direct rental income faces passive income rules inside a corporation. The pro tip is to compare the after tax yield net of fees across structures before committing capital because a seven percent REIT yield can drop below a leveraged rental property once financing costs and vacancy allowances are included.
