Your order filled at the prevailing price, but the real mechanics involve understanding how Buffett evolved from buying cheap assets to owning businesses with durable competitive advantages. The mechanism works through economic moats that protect returns over decades, measured by consistent free-cash-flow margins above fifteen percent. Professionals always check the insurance-float advantage at Berkshire before comparing it to a retail portfolio because that low-cost capital source is not replicable. The misconception that Buffett simply buys undervalued stocks misses the shift toward quality after partnering with Charlie Munger. The fix is to screen first for moat strength using return-on-equity trends, then apply a valuation test only on names that pass the quality filter. Buffett's early Graham-style purchases targeted liquidation values while later holdings such as See's Candies and Coca-Cola emphasized pricing power and brand durability. The Berkshire structure itself supplies an edge through deferred taxes on unrealized gains that retail accounts cannot replicate. Modern investors can still apply the core test by calculating owner earnings rather than reported net income and requiring a margin of safety of at least twenty-five percent below estimated intrinsic value.
