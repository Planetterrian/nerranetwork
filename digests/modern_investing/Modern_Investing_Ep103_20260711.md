### Investor Education: Ex-Dividend Date Mechanics
Imagine you bought BCE yesterday expecting to collect the upcoming quarterly dividend. Your order filled at the closing price, but the stock opened today roughly 1.5% lower because buyers after the ex-dividend date no longer receive that payment. The mechanism works through the exchange’s automatic adjustment: the previous day’s close becomes today’s opening print minus the dividend amount, with the actual gap sometimes widened or narrowed by normal trading. Most retail investors don’t realize the adjustment happens before the market opens and can be masked by broader moves. Professionals always check the ex-dividend date on the company’s investor page and compare it against their settlement cycle before placing the order. The biggest mistake with dividend capture is ignoring the price drop and transaction costs. Instead, always calculate the net yield after the ex-date adjustment and only proceed if the annualized return still exceeds your opportunity cost.
