A round-number stop is not protection; it is a price that can always be called too tight after the fact.

The fix is to write an observable falsification trigger on the same note as the entry price and the catalyst, before any capital is committed.

First sentence of that note is the thesis in one line: volume growth on the system will sustain distribution coverage above one point two times.

Second sentence is the observable that kills it, tied to a dated release rather than to the tape.

Third, the stop is calculated backward from that coverage number using the current payout ratio and the expected free-cash-flow range, so the exit is a translation of the original idea.

Because the trigger sits on a known calendar date, you cannot later claim the stop was too tight or too loose; the data either confirmed the sentence or refuted it.

That is also what blocks the common error of widening the stop after entry, because editing the invalidation means rewriting the thesis, not massaging a percentage.
