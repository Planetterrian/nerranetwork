### Strategy Spotlight
Leveraged ETFs let investors magnify exposure to specific themes like AI without buying individual stocks. Today’s chip-stock sell-off creates a timely test of whether these products deliver amplified upside or simply larger drawdowns when sentiment shifts. Implementation starts with screening for 2x or 3x ETFs tied to semiconductor or AI indexes on platforms such as Questrade or Interactive Brokers, then sizing the position at 5-10% of the speculative sleeve. These vehicles performed best during the 2023-2025 AI rally when volume confirmation accompanied earnings beats, but they also produced outsized losses during the 2022 rate-hike period. The key risk is daily reset mechanics that cause compounding drag in sideways or volatile markets. Always compare the ETF’s 20-day average volume against its AUM before entry to avoid liquidity traps. ### Investor Education: How to Read an Earnings Report
Imagine you bought a consumer stock last week after seeing a headline “earnings beat estimates.” Your order filled at the open, but the stock dropped 4% by lunch. The mechanism behind that gap is the difference between headline EPS and the full picture in the 10-Q. Revenue growth tells you if the top line is expanding, while EPS can be inflated by one-time tax items or share buybacks that won’t repeat. Forward guidance from management is the real signal—analysts update models within hours of the call, so a lowered revenue outlook often triggers immediate selling. Check the balance sheet for rising long-term debt or falling cash; free cash flow that diverges sharply from reported earnings is a red flag. Professionals always read the management commentary section for phrases like “we expect headwinds to persist” before deciding whether the beat is sustainable. The biggest mistake with earnings is reacting only to the EPS headline. Instead, always compare actual revenue and guidance against the prior quarter and consensus estimates before adjusting position size.
