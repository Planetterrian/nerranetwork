### Strategy Spotlight
Core-satellite construction lets investors anchor most of a TFSA or RRSP in low-cost broad-market ETFs while using a smaller sleeve for targeted opportunities. Today’s CHIPS Act quantum funding and European growth outlook make the satellite sleeve especially relevant because they create discrete, time-bound catalysts that broad indexes will not fully capture. Build the core with XEQT or VTI/VXUS at 70-80% of the portfolio, then allocate 20-30% to single-name or sector satellites such as quantum or European cyclicals. Rebalance the satellite sleeve quarterly or when any position doubles in weight; sell the satellite once the catalyst has been fully priced in or the thesis breaks. The approach has historically delivered excess returns during policy-driven rotations while keeping overall costs and tax drag low in registered accounts. The main risk is over-concentration in the satellite if multiple catalysts arrive at once. ### Investor Education: Core-Satellite ETF Construction
Imagine you added a 15% satellite position in a quantum-computing name last week after the CHIPS Act announcement; your order filled at the opening print, but the actual execution reflected a 0.8% premium to the previous close because the stock’s average daily volume is under 2 million shares. The mechanism is simple: the core (XEQT or VTI) trades with sub-penny spreads and massive depth, while the satellite can gap 1-2% on modest news flow, so the 15% sleeve can swing the entire portfolio by 20-30 basis points on any given day. Professionals always check the 20-day average dollar volume and the bid-ask spread in the first 15 minutes before sizing the satellite; most retail investors skip this step and end up paying the spread twice—once on entry and again on the quarterly rebalance. The biggest mistake with core-satellite is letting a satellite position grow past 25% of the total account without trimming. Instead, set a hard 20% cap and rebalance back to target every 90 days or on a 50% gain, whichever comes first.
