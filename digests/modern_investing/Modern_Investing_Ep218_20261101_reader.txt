Dividend-income screening is how you hunt for payouts backed by regulated or contracted cash flow that can compound inside a Tax-Free Savings Account, or TFSA, without immediate tax drag.

You screen first for a yield above three percent, then you check the payout against the latest distributable cash flow figure, and you only keep names that use less than seventy percent of expected cash flow so a bad quarter does not force a cut.

Last, you compare the multiple with the five-year average and you want the stock at or below that average, so the yield has room to compound without valuation compression eating the total return.

Think of the yield as the rent a tenant promises you. The payout ratio is whether their paycheck covers that rent after groceries, and you want at least thirty percent left so a broken fridge does not bounce the cheque.

A fifty thousand dollar TFSA sitting in one hundred percent equities, with Bitcoin up forty percent year-to-date and the broad-market fund going nowhere, is the fork a lot of you are staring at.

Adding a three percent slice means committing one thousand five hundred dollars, yet that sleeve can swing ten percent in a single week, far more than the equity side.

Canadian-listed crypto exchange-traded funds such as BTCX and ETHX trade on the TSX with management fees around one percent and settle in Canadian dollars, so you never touch a wallet or a private key.

The Ontario Securities Commission, or OSC, oversees those products and they come with custody at qualified banks, unlike offshore exchanges that have faced insolvency, and OSC rules require qualified third-party custodians.

Gains inside a TFSA stay tax-free, but any transfer to self-custody triggers a deemed disposition at fair market value, which is why most Canadian investors stay with the listed products and skip private-key risk.

Size the sleeve so that even a fifty percent drawdown leaves the overall portfolio inside your stated risk tolerance, keep the range at one to five percent so a bad week in crypto does not force a rebalance across the whole account, and rebalance only once a year to avoid churn outside registered accounts.

Bitcoin and Ethereum funds do not carry the same risk; Bitcoin has shown lower correlation to traditional equities during risk-off periods.
