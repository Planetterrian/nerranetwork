The actual cost of that fill depends on which brokerage you used and how its fee structure and market access interact with the ticket.
Wealth's simple trade is commission-free on Canadian and US stocks with a clean mobile app, but it limits advanced order types and has no margin.
That is the right box for a simple buy and hold TFA investor who rarely needs US options or real-time level 2 data.

That fits the active Canadian investor who wants occasional US exposure without paying up per trade.
Interactive brokers has the lowest margin rates and the broadest international access, but the platform complexity will overwhelm anyone
who only needs basic TFA execution. Bank-owned desks like TD Direct or RBC Direct plug into your checking account and then charge you for the privilege with higher commissions and weaker mobile tools.
The pro-tip most people skip is mechanical.
Check whether the brokerage passes through exchange fees on US trades and check whether it supports automatic dividend reinvestment inside registered accounts.
Those two lines on the fee schedule move more money over a decade than the advertised commission.
The misconception to kill is that the free platform is always cheapest.
Wider spreads and missing order types force you to a second venue for a single ticket, and that second ticket is where the free trade becomes the expensive one.
