### Investor Education: Passive Investing and Potential Valuation Loops
Imagine you added a broad S&P 500 ETF to your TFSA last month on the belief that market-cap weighting automatically captures the strongest companies. Your order filled at the prevailing NAV, but the actual mechanism is that inflows mechanically increase demand for the largest constituents regardless of their current earnings trajectory. In practice the bid-ask spread on that ETF can widen from two cents to fifteen cents in the first thirty minutes of heavy retail flows, and the underlying stocks themselves experience price pressure that is unrelated to fundamentals. Professionals always check the ETF’s premium or discount to NAV and the concurrent options skew before adding size. The biggest mistake with passive flows is assuming price discovery remains unaffected at the margin. Instead, always layer a simple active screen—such as free-cash-flow yield versus the index median—on top of any core ETF allocation to avoid becoming the marginal buyer at peak multiples.
