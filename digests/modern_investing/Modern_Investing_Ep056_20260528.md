### Strategy Spotlight
TIPS ETFs are seeing inflows as investors seek inflation protection, yet the underlying mechanism often fails to deliver when real yields shift or supply chains adjust faster than CPI. The strategy involves buying Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities through ETFs to hedge rising consumer prices, but the article shows that when inflation hits a three-year high the funds can still post negative real returns if breakeven rates are mispriced. Implementation requires checking the ETF’s real yield spread versus nominal Treasuries on your brokerage platform before allocating, then sizing the position to no more than 5-8% of fixed-income sleeve. This approach worked best in 2021-2022 when inflation surprised to the upside and TIPS outperformed nominal bonds by several percentage points, but it carries the risk of underperformance in disinflationary periods or when the Fed signals faster rate cuts. Always compare the ETF’s 30-day SEC yield to the 5-year breakeven inflation rate before purchase. ### Investor Education: Dividend Investing: Building Passive Income
Imagine you bought a Canadian dividend ETF last quarter expecting steady cash flow only to watch the payout ratio climb above 80% after an earnings miss. The mechanism here is that companies must generate sufficient free cash flow to cover dividends without eroding the balance sheet; when payout exceeds 70-75% for non-REITs the risk of a cut rises sharply even if the headline yield looks attractive. The dividend tax credit in non-registered accounts reduces the effective tax rate on eligible Canadian dividends to roughly 10-20% depending on your bracket, while TFSA and RRSP accounts eliminate that advantage entirely so growth-oriented holdings may be better there. A pro tip most retail investors miss is screening for dividend aristocrats with 10+ years of increases and payout ratios below 60% using your broker’s fundamental screener before adding to a TFSA. The biggest mistake with dividend strategies is chasing the highest yield without checking sustainability. Instead, always calculate the payout ratio and free-cash-flow coverage first.
