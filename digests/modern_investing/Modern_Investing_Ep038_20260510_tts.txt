Now that we have covered the mechanics of upgrades, let us build the foundational options knowledge every T F S A investor needs.

Imagine you own one hundred shares of a Canadian bank trading at eighty five dollars in your T F S A and want extra income without selling the position.

A covered call lets you sell the right for someone else to buy those shares at a higher strike, say ninety dollars, collecting a premium upfront.

That premium is your income, but you cap upside if the stock surges past the strike.

Calls give the buyer the right to purchase at the strike price, while puts give the right to sell, creating protection or bearish bets.

The key Greeks are delta, which shows how much the option price moves with the stock, and theta, which measures daily time decay working in the seller's favor.

Before trading, pull up the options chain on your broker platform and compare open interest and bid ask spreads across expirations.

The biggest mistake beginners make is selling naked options without collateral, exposing them to unlimited losses.

Instead, always start with covered calls on stocks you already own and would happily hold at the strike price.
