### Investor Education: Listener Guide: Investing Your First $10,000
Open a TFSA at a no-commission broker such as Wealthsimple or Questrade before funding any account. Confirm your emergency fund covers at least three months of expenses so the $10,000 is truly investable capital. Choose one broad ETF such as a total-market Canadian or global equity fund for the initial purchase to keep fees under 0.25% annually. Set up an automatic $200 monthly contribution from your paycheck the same day you make the first buy. The psychological step of clicking “buy” is hardest the first time; start with a dollar amount small enough that a 5% drop will not cause panic selling. Expect the long-term average return near 7% annually, but prepare for individual years ranging from -20% to +30%. Rebalance once a year rather than reacting to every headline. Begin by listing your current TFSA contribution room on the CRA website so you know exactly how much new capital can be added this calendar year without penalties. Next, compare the bid-ask spreads on the ETF you selected during regular market hours to ensure you are not paying unnecessary costs on the first purchase. After the initial buy, set calendar reminders for the automatic contribution date each month so the process becomes routine rather than a monthly decision. Track the first three months of performance against a simple benchmark such as the TSX Composite or a global equity ETF to establish a personal baseline before adding more complex holdings. This sequence keeps the focus on process rather than prediction and reduces the chance that early volatility will derail the plan.
