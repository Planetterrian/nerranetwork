### Investor Education: Crypto as a Portfolio Allocation: A Risk-Managed Approach
You hold a $50,000 TFSA that is 100 percent equities and notice Bitcoin has rallied 40 percent year-to-date while your broad-market ETF sits flat. Adding a 3 percent slice means committing $1,500, yet the position can swing 10 percent in a single week, far more than the equity sleeve. Canadian-listed crypto ETFs such as BTCX and ETHX trade on the TSX with management fees around 1 percent and settle in Canadian dollars, removing the need to manage wallets or private keys. The regulatory edge in Canada is that these ETFs are overseen by the OSC and come with custody arrangements at qualified banks, unlike offshore exchanges that have faced insolvency events. Gains inside a TFSA remain tax-free, but any transfer to self-custody triggers a deemed disposition at fair market value. Size the allocation so that even a 50 percent drawdown leaves the overall portfolio within your stated risk tolerance, and rebalance only once a year to avoid frequent taxable events outside registered accounts. The 1-5 percent range keeps the volatility contribution small enough that a single bad week in crypto does not force rebalancing across the entire TFSA. Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs carry different risk profiles, with Bitcoin showing lower correlation to traditional equities during risk-off periods. Self-custody adds private-key risk that the ETF structure avoids, which is why most Canadian investors stay with the listed products. The OSC custody rules require qualified third-party custodians, giving an extra layer of protection compared with holding tokens directly on an exchange.
