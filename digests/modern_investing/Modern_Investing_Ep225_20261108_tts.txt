Valuation screening after earnings beats is the filter that answers that question: you take reported earnings, any buyback announcement, and the forward multiple, then you ask whether the name is cheap relative to its own trajectory rather than whether the headline looked loud.

The working rule is simple enough to run after dinner. Screen the post-earnings price-to-earnings ratio, compare it with the five-year average, and require free-cash-flow yield above four percent before you add a share.

Think of it like reading the unit price on two cereal boxes after a coupon — the coupon is the beat and the buyback, and the unit price is that forward multiple versus the five-year average, which is the only number that tells you whether the discount is real.
