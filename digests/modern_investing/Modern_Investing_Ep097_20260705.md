### Investor Education: How Trade Execution Actually Works on Volatile Days
Imagine you bought an ETF tracking Canadian consumer stocks last week when Aritzia guidance looked constructive. Your order filled at the ask, but the bid-ask spread on that ETF widened from 3 cents to 14 cents within the first 25 minutes of trading as retail volume spiked. What most retail investors don't realize is that market makers widen spreads precisely when order flow becomes one-sided, so your effective cost can jump 0.4% even on a "commission-free" platform. The professionals always check Level 2 data or the time-and-sales tape for the prior 15 minutes before hitting market orders on anything below average daily volume. The biggest mistake with execution is assuming the quoted price is the price you'll get on fast moves. Instead, always use limit orders set 1-2 cents inside the current spread on ETFs or liquid stocks when volatility exceeds the 20-day average.
