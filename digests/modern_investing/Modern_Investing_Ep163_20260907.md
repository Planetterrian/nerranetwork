### Investor Education: Crypto in a Traditional Portfolio: How Much and Why
Imagine you added a 3% Bitcoin allocation to your TFSA last month when ETF flows turned positive. Your order filled at the prevailing spot price, but the actual mechanics involve the ETF's premium or discount to NAV plus the overnight custody fee embedded in the management expense ratio. Canadian vehicles such as BTCX and ETHX let you hold the exposure inside a TFSA without triggering foreign-exchange conversion costs or self-custody key risks. The efficient-frontier math shows that a 1-5% slice improves Sharpe ratio because Bitcoin's 0.2-0.3 correlation with equities breaks down mainly during liquidity shocks. Professionals always check the 30-day rolling correlation before sizing up; most retail investors skip this step and treat the allocation as a permanent diversifier. The common misconception is that any allocation above zero is either "too risky" or "the future"—the fix is to treat crypto as a satellite sleeve whose size is rebalanced annually against the rest of the portfolio. When the 30-day correlation spikes above 0.6, the diversification benefit shrinks and the sleeve should be trimmed back to the original target weight. Canadian investors gain an extra edge by using the tax-advantaged account wrapper because realized gains inside the TFSA never trigger capital-gains inclusion or superficial-loss tracking. The 1-5% range keeps the position small enough that a 50% drawdown in crypto still leaves overall portfolio volatility within most investors' stated risk tolerance. Rebalancing once per year forces a deliberate review rather than letting the allocation drift with price moves.
