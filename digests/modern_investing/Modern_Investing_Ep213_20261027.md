### Investor Education: Currency Impact on Canadian Dividend Holdings
A Canadian investor holding Enbridge in a TFSA sees the quarterly dividend in CAD but the underlying cash flows include U.S. pipeline revenue. When the Canadian dollar strengthens from 71.30 cents to 72 cents U.S. the translated U.S. earnings shrink and the effective yield on the position drops even though the nominal dividend stays unchanged. The mechanism appears in the first 30 minutes of any session when cross-border flows widen bid-ask spreads on dual-listed names by three to five cents. Professional desks hedge the FX exposure with rolling three-month forward contracts sized to 40 percent of the U.S. revenue weight. Retail investors can replicate the check by pulling the most recent 10-K segment note noting the U.S. EBITDA share and setting a price alert on USD/CAD at the 200-day moving average. The common misconception is that a stronger loonie only affects exporters. The fix is to treat FX translation as a second risk factor on any holding whose cash flows cross the border.
