### Investor Education: Options Basics for Intermediate Investors
Imagine you bought CRM last week after the AI fan-engagement partnership news and your market order filled at the ask. The bid-ask spread on the nearest weekly call was 8 cents wide in the first 15 minutes of trading, costing you an extra 0.4% before any price move. Calls give the right to buy at a set strike; puts give the right to sell. Intrinsic value is the difference between strike and current price, while time value reflects days until expiration and volatility. A covered call on shares you already own collects premium income but caps upside; a cash-secured put lets you buy at a discount if the stock drops. Delta measures how much the option price moves with the stock; theta shows daily time decay. The biggest mistake is selling naked calls without defined risk. Instead, always size positions so maximum loss stays under 2% of portfolio and check open interest before entering.
