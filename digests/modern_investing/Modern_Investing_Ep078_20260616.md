### Strategy Spotlight
A covered-call ETF strategy lets investors collect premium income on holdings they already own by selling out-of-the-money calls each month. Today’s subdued Wall Street futures and focus on the Fed make income generation more attractive than pure directional bets, especially when volatility compresses. Investors can implement this on platforms like Questrade or Interactive Brokers by selecting a broad equity ETF such as QQQ or XIU, then selling monthly calls at the 30-delta strike; the premium is collected regardless of modest upside. This approach has historically performed best in range-bound or mildly bullish markets where the underlying drifts sideways, delivering 8-12% annualized yield on top of any capital appreciation. The main risk is opportunity cost if the underlying rallies sharply above the strike, capping gains. Always size the position so the covered calls represent no more than 20-30% of total equity exposure to preserve upside participation. ### Investor Education: Contrarian: The Canadian Home Bias Problem
Imagine you allocated 40% of your TFSA to Canadian equities last year when the TSX lagged global markets by 12 percentage points. Your order filled cleanly, but the real cost showed up in missed compounding from U.S. tech and healthcare names that drove the NASDAQ higher. The mechanism is simple: Canada represents roughly 3% of global market capitalization yet many DIY investors hold 30-50% in domestic stocks because of the dividend tax credit and familiarity. What most retail investors don’t realize is that their job, home, and pension already embed massive Canada exposure, so the portfolio ends up triple-exposed to financials and energy, which together make up half the TSX. The professionals always check geographic revenue breakdown before adding another Canadian name. The biggest mistake with home bias is assuming familiarity reduces risk. Instead, always measure your true economic exposure across borders and cap Canadian equities at 15-25% unless you have a specific tactical reason.
