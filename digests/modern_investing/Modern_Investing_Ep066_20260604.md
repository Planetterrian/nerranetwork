### Investor Education: Asset Allocation: The Only Free Lunch in Investing
Imagine you bought XEQT last month expecting broad equity exposure only to watch a single sector rotation cut your monthly return by 1.8% while bonds held steady. The mechanism at work is Modern Portfolio Theory's core math: combining assets with low correlation reduces portfolio standard deviation without lowering expected return, moving you closer to the efficient frontier where risk-adjusted returns peak. Professionals always check the 36-month rolling correlation matrix between equities, bonds, and alternatives before adding a new ETF, something retail investors skip when chasing recent winners. Canadian examples show VGRO delivering smoother drawdowns than pure equity during the 2022 rate-hike cycle because its 20% fixed-income sleeve offset equity losses. The biggest mistake with asset allocation is treating age-based rules like "100 minus your age in stocks" as fixed rather than starting points. Instead, always run a quick risk-tolerance questionnaire on your brokerage site and set rebalancing bands at 5% drift from targets using calendar or threshold methods.
