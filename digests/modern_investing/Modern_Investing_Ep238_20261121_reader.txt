Contribution room in a TFSA does not grow when a holding falls from ten thousand dollars to six thousand, and Motley Fool Canada walks through that arithmetic.

The four-thousand-dollar mark-to-market hit does not give room back. The Canada Revenue Agency tallies withdrawals, and it ignores losses whether you have sold or not.

Pulling out the remaining six thousand creates six thousand of new room the following calendar year, and the original ten thousand never reappears.

Selling the position and leaving the cash inside the account also fails to generate new room — the tax agency is not running a sympathy desk for your losers.

Keep a running spreadsheet of every contribution and withdrawal date across every TFSA provider before you replace a sold position in the same year, because the Canada Revenue Agency does not automatically refresh statements with unused room.

The misconception is that selling a loser frees the full original contribution; the fix is to treat only actual withdrawals as future room and to reallocate inside the account without cashing out when you can.
