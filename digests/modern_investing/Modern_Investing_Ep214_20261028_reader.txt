Do you sit through the press conference, or do you cut when the option-adjusted spread has already widened fifteen basis points? Here is how I think about it.

Dealers widen credit spreads when they anticipate slower future cuts, because the present value of the issuer’s cash flows falls and the risk of refinancing at higher rates increases.

The professional habit is to check the option-adjusted spread on that bond every time the FedWatch probability of a December hike moves more than ten percentage points; if the spread has already widened fifteen basis points or more, you reduce size before the statement rather than after.

The common misconception is that an unchanged policy rate means unchanged bond prices.

The fix is to treat the forward curve and the credit-spread reaction as the actual trade signal, not the headline decision.

Any statement language that shifts the expected path of the federal funds rate changes the discount rate on every corporate cash flow beyond the next meeting, and investors who watch only the spot rate miss the repricing in that ninety-minute window when the curve and the credit-spread matrix adjust together.
