### Investor Education: TFSA Interest Deductibility Mechanics
Imagine you borrowed at 6% to buy shares in a non-registered account, then transferred those shares into your TFSA after the position rose 8%. The interest on that loan remains deductible on your T1 even after the transfer because the CRA traces the original purpose of the borrowing, not the final account location. The mechanism works because interest deductibility is tied to the income-producing intent at the time the debt was incurred, not to the account type at any later date. Most retail investors assume moving assets into a TFSA automatically voids prior deductions, but the rule hinges on whether the securities continue to generate taxable income outside the TFSA. Professionals always keep the original loan documentation and the transfer date clearly dated in their records to defend the claim. The concrete misconception to avoid is treating the TFSA transfer as an automatic interest-deductibility reset.
