### Investor Education: Sector Rotation Mechanics
Imagine you added a technology ETF to your TFSA last month after strong earnings from chip makers. Your order filled at the prevailing price, yet the ETF later lagged broader indices because capital rotated into financials on rising yields. The mechanism at work is relative performance: sectors do not move in lockstep because economic indicators such as the yield curve and PMI readings shift capital between defensive and cyclical groups. Most retail investors assume their chosen sector will keep leading once it starts outperforming, but the data shows leadership changes every six to eighteen months. The pro tip is to track two simple leading indicators each month: the shape of the two-year versus ten-year Treasury curve and the latest ISM manufacturing reading. When the curve steepens and PMI rises above fifty, tilt toward industrials and materials; when both flatten, shift toward utilities and consumer staples. The concrete misconception to avoid is treating any single sector as a permanent holding without checking these cycle signals at least quarterly. Investors who ignore the rotation signal often end up holding the previous cycle's leaders while the new leaders compound at a faster rate. Checking the two indicators takes less than five minutes once a month and can be automated with a simple spreadsheet that pulls the Treasury curve data from public sources. The same discipline applies inside an RRSP or FHSA where contribution room is limited and every percentage point of outperformance matters over a multi-year horizon.
