### Strategy Spotlight
PayPal’s depressed valuation combined with $6 billion in annual free cash flow creates a classic buyback-compounding setup that retail investors can replicate using simple screens. At roughly 6.5 times free cash flow and a $40 billion market cap, the company can retire a meaningful percentage of shares each year while the multiple stays compressed. The strategy works by holding through the period of depressed sentiment, letting the shrinking share count do the heavy lifting, then waiting for a rerating once the market stops treating the business as structurally broken. Implementation is straightforward: screen for companies with stable or growing free cash flow, buyback authorization above 10% of market cap, and forward P/E below 12. Historical parallels include mature payers in the early 2010s that delivered multi-bagger returns once sentiment normalized. ### Investor Education: Contrarian: The Canadian Home Bias Problem
Imagine you just rebalanced your TFSA and notice 42% of the equity sleeve sits in Canadian names because “I understand the banks and energy names better.” That allocation feels prudent until you compare it to Canada’s actual 3% weight in global market capitalization. The mechanism driving the mismatch is simple: your job, home, and pension already embed heavy Canada exposure, so adding another 30-50% in TSX stocks doubles down on the same two sectors that make up half the index. Professionals routinely check global market-cap weights first, then apply a modest home-bias tilt only for the dividend tax credit and currency hedging benefits. The biggest misconception is that familiarity reduces risk; instead, it concentrates sector and currency risk that a simple 20% Canadian equity cap would materially lower.
