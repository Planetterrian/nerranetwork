### Investor Education: Technical Analysis What the Charts Actually Tell You
Imagine you bought a technology ETF last week when AI infrastructure headlines lifted the sector. Your order filled at the quoted price but the real story lies in how the chart behaved between your click and that fill. Support and resistance levels on the daily chart act as zones where buying or selling pressure historically clusters, often near the fifty day or two hundred day moving average. Volume confirmation matters because a breakout above resistance on above average volume has shown higher follow through rates in backtests than the same move on light volume. RSI above seventy signals overbought conditions while below thirty signals oversold, yet these readings work best as timing tools rather than standalone buy or sell triggers. MACD crossovers can highlight momentum shifts but they lag in strong trends and produce whipsaws in sideways markets. The honest limitation is that technical analysis improves entry and exit timing more reliably than it predicts which names will outperform fundamentally. The fifty day moving average often serves as dynamic support during uptrends while the two hundred day moving average filters longer term direction and helps avoid false breakouts. The misconception to avoid is treating every chart pattern as predictive when only those backed by volume and multiple time frame confirmation have shown statistical edge in repeated studies.
