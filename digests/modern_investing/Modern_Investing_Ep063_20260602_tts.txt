Imagine you bought HPE shares yesterday morning after the earnings release when the stock was already up twenty percent pre market. Your market order filled at the quoted price but the actual execution landed three cents worse than the last traded price shown on your screen.

The mechanism is straightforward when retail flow hits a thinly traded name during a catalyst spike market makers widen the spread and absorb the imbalance so the effective fill price moves against you by the amount of that widening often five to fifteen cents on a fast moving stock.

What most retail investors do not realize is that the displayed bid ask quote is only the top of the book the next levels can be much thinner and a single large order can walk the book several cents in seconds.

The professionals always check Level two depth and average daily volume before sizing a momentum trade then use limit orders or iceberg orders to avoid telegraphing size. The biggest mistake with order flow is assuming the quoted price is the price you will receive.

Instead always size positions so that even a zero point three percent slippage hit still leaves the trade inside your risk budget.
