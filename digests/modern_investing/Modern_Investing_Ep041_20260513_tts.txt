Imagine you already own a T F S A and want to add real-estate exposure without buying a second property.

You could purchase units of a publicly traded REIT such as RioCan or Canadian Apartment REIT at market open.

The order fills instantly but the distribution yield in the four to six percent range for many names arrives monthly and is taxed as ordinary income inside an R R S P.

The mechanism that surprises most retail investors is the tax treatment difference.

REIT distributions are not eligible for the dividend tax credit.

Inside a T F S A or R R S P they grow tax-deferred.

Direct rental properties allow depreciation claims and potential principal-residence exemption on sale.

A pro tip is to check the REIT’s AFFO payout ratio before buying.

Anything above ninety percent leaves little cushion for maintenance capex.

Mortgage investment corporations offer another route with higher yields but illiquidity and credit risk that retail platforms rarely highlight.

The biggest mistake with real-estate allocation is treating your home and a REIT as interchangeable.

Instead always size the REIT or MIC position so that total real-estate exposure including your principal residence equity stays below thirty to forty percent of net worth.
