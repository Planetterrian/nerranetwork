### Strategy Spotlight
Selling cash-secured puts lets investors collect premium while waiting for a limit-order fill on a stock they already want to own. Today's market volatility makes the approach relevant because wide bid-ask spreads and headline-driven dips create premium income on names that would otherwise leave cash idle. Implementation requires choosing liquid underlyings, setting the strike at or below your target entry price, and ensuring the premium exceeds the expected slippage from waiting. The tactic has historically performed best in range-bound or mildly bullish regimes where implied volatility stays elevated but the underlying does not gap lower through the strike. Risk is still full downside ownership if assigned, so position size must stay inside normal allocation rules. The method works only on stocks an investor would be happy to own outright, turning idle cash into income while the limit order sits unfilled. ### Investor Education: Naming Your Invalidation Before You Buy
Imagine you bought a TSX mining name this morning on a financing announcement and your order filled at the open. The real mechanics between click and fill involve the market digesting the La Mancha strategic stake and any immediate volume surge, which can move price before your confirmation arrives. Professionals always write the exact observable trigger that kills the thesis—such as “if the next quarterly shows no new drill results by the end of Q4”—before the capital is committed. That written line becomes the stop-loss reference and prevents hindsight rewriting after a loss. Most retail investors instead say “if it drops” or “if the market turns,” which offers no testable condition and turns every exit into an emotional decision. The concrete fix is to state the invalidation in one sentence using a specific data release, guidance number, or technical level before entering any position. This approach converts losses into measurable feedback on the original thesis rather than vague regret.
