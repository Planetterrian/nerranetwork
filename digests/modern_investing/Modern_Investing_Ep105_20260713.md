### Investor Education: The Dot-Com Bubble: Lessons for AI Investors
Imagine you bought a high-multiple semiconductor name last month after an AI infrastructure headline. Your order filled at the open, but the stock then dropped 8% on profit-taking even though fundamentals had not changed. The mechanism at work is identical to 1999-2000: investors priced in years of future growth in a single quarter, so any hint of slower capex produced violent repricing. The bid-ask spread on many AI-related names widened from 5 cents to 25 cents in the first hour of trading on July 13 as volume spiked. What most retail investors miss is that the companies that survived the dot-com bust (Amazon, eBay) already had real revenue and defensible cash-flow models, while Pets.com and Webvan did not. AI stocks today sit in a middle ground—many report genuine revenue, yet valuations still embed the same “this time is different” assumption. The biggest mistake is treating every AI revenue beat as permanent; instead, always demand three consecutive quarters of accelerating free-cash-flow growth before adding to a position.
