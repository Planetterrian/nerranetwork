### Investor Education: Options Basics for Income and Protection
Imagine you own 100 shares of a Canadian bank trading at $65 that just raised its dividend; you want extra yield without selling. Selling a covered call at the $68 strike for a $1.20 premium gives you 1.85% income over 30 days on top of the dividend, but caps upside if the stock rallies sharply. The mechanism works because the premium reflects both intrinsic value (zero here) and time value driven by volatility and days to expiration. Professionals always check open interest and bid-ask width before entering; a wide spread on low-volume strikes can erase half the theoretical yield through slippage. The biggest mistake retail investors make is treating covered calls as “free money” without realizing they are short a call that can be assigned on news. Instead, always size the position so the premium covers at least 60% of your expected downside over the holding period.
