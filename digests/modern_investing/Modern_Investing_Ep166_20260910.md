### Investor Education: Reading Options Flow for Retail Edge
Imagine you notice nine separate call purchases on Micron totaling $30 million in premium. Your order would fill at the posted ask, but the real signal sits in the strike distribution and expiration clustering through next June. The mechanism works because institutions often pay up for leverage on conviction views, and the premium paid reveals how far they expect the move to travel. What most retail investors miss is that balanced flow at the same strike on AMD signals indecision rather than a directional bet. The pro tip is to filter for trades above $500,000 in premium and ignore anything under $100,000 that could be retail noise. The concrete misconception to avoid is treating every large options print as a sure directional signal without confirming it against sector rotation and volume. When the same strike shows both calls and puts in roughly equal dollar amounts, the market is effectively saying it needs more information before committing. That balanced AMD print at $520 for January therefore functions as a warning flag rather than a green light. Retail traders who skip the volume filter often end up entering names already rotating out of favor, which is exactly what the current cold-streak regime is designed to prevent.
