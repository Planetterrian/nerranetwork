### Investor Education: Technical Analysis Fundamentals for Modern Investors
Imagine you bought an ETF tracking the TSX last week after it bounced from the 50-day moving average. Your order filled at the ask, but the real mechanics involved the bid-ask spread widening from 3 cents to 12 cents in the first 20 minutes of the session because volume was only 60% of the 20-day average. Support and resistance are simply price levels where buying or selling pressure historically clustered; the 200-day moving average often acts as dynamic support in uptrends while the 50-day serves as a shorter-term trend filter. RSI measures momentum on a 0-100 scale with readings below 30 flagging oversold conditions that have preceded rebounds in roughly 65% of cases over the past decade on large-cap indices. MACD tracks the convergence or divergence of two exponential moving averages and generates crossovers that work best as confirmation rather than standalone signals. Volume analysis adds conviction: a breakout above resistance on volume 150% above average has shown higher follow-through rates than low-volume moves. The biggest mistake with technical analysis is treating it as a directional predictor instead of an entry and exit timing tool. Instead, always combine at least two indicators with a fundamental catalyst before committing capital.
