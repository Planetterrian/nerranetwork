$10,000 in cash and three account doors, TFSA, RRSP, and FHSA, and walking through the
wrong door first can leave thousands in lost tax savings over a decade.
Motley Fool Canada lays out the mechanics.
The TFSA grows tax-free with no tax on withdrawal.
The RRSP delivers an immediate tax refund, but taxes withdrawals as income.
And the FHSA combines both benefits only for first time home buyers.
The correct order depends on current marginal tax rate, expected future tax rate, and whether
home ownership is a near-term goal.
Contribution room in a TFSA carries forward indefinitely, RRSP room resets each year based
on earned income, and FHSA room is caped at a lifetime limit of $40,000.
A higher current tax rate favors the RRSP for the immediate deduction, whereas a lower
expected future rate favors the TFSA for tax-free growth.
Both. The practical sequence begins with the FHSA if a home purchase is planned within
15 years, then the TFSA for flexibility, then the RRSP for the tax refund. Modeling a $10,000
contribution at a 40% marginal rate shows an immediate $4,000 refund in the RRSP versus
zero in the TFSA, yet the TFSA preserves full access without clawback on future income
tested benefits. The TFSA offers the most flexibility for any future need while the RRSP provides
the largest upfront tax saving when income is high. Dividend payers such as Scotiabank fit inside
any of these accounts, but the tax credit on eligible Canadian dividends provides an extra advantage
only in non-registered accounts. FHSA contributions can be transferred tax-free to an RRSP
if home purchase plans change, so the choice hinges on whether you value immediate tax relief,
long-term tax-free growth, or the specific home buying incentive. Run the after-tax outcome with
