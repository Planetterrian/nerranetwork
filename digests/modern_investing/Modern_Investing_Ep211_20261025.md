### Strategy Spotlight
Screening for earnings beats uses consensus estimates to identify companies that consistently exceed Wall Street forecasts. Today's data shows 62 of 76 S&P 500 reporters beat EPS expectations while 60 grew EPS year over year, highlighting the value of tracking beat rates rather than headline surprises alone. Investors can implement this by pulling the latest Seeking Alpha or Yahoo Finance earnings preview tables, filtering for names with at least three prior beats and revenue growth above 5 percent, then cross-checking the upcoming report date. The approach has delivered edge when volume confirms the move post-release but fails when sector rotation overrides the individual beat, as seen in prior quarters when growth stocks rotated out despite positive results. Apply the screen inside a brokerage watchlist or free tool like Finviz by setting EPS surprise filters and sorting by upcoming announcement dates. This technique narrows candidates before any position sizing decision. The same data set also shows 48 of the 76 companies beat revenue expectations, giving a second filter that can be layered on top of the EPS screen to further reduce false positives. Cross-referencing the two metrics together produces a tighter list of names that have demonstrated both earnings power and top-line momentum in the current quarter. ### Investor Education: Excess Returns Valuation
A Canadian investor scanning bank stocks might notice CIBC trading near $159.59 with a 15 times earnings multiple and wonder whether that price fairly reflects the bank's ability to generate profits above its cost of capital. The excess returns model starts from book value of roughly CA$72.14 per share, applies an average return on equity of 16.76 percent, subtracts the cost of equity near CA$5.08 per share, and capitalizes the resulting CA$6.65 excess return per share to arrive at an intrinsic value estimate above the current quote. Professional desks run this calculation daily through Bloomberg or FactSet terminals; retail investors can approximate it in a spreadsheet by pulling analyst consensus ROE and stable EPS figures from Yahoo Finance or Simply Wall St, then comparing the implied value to market price. One pro habit is to rerun the model after each quarterly update rather than treating the single number as static. The common misconception is assuming a low P/E alone signals undervaluation; the fix is to verify whether returns on equity actually exceed the cost of capital before treating the discount as an opportunity. The model also incorporates a stable book value estimate of CA$69.96 per share, which anchors the terminal value and prevents the output from drifting when near-term earnings fluctuate. Investors who update the inputs quarterly can track whether the excess return margin is widening or narrowing, providing an early signal that the market price may be moving away from fair value.
