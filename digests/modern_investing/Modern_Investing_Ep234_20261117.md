### Investor Education: Naming Your Invalidation Before You Buy
You bought MP Materials after its November 5 earnings beat, expecting the 41 percent year-over-year rise in neodymium-praseodymium production to drive further gains, yet the stock could still fall if fourth-quarter sold tons drop below the guided 1,000 metric tons. The mechanism at work is that an invalidation is a specific, observable threshold—here a quarterly sales figure—that proves the original thesis incorrect, rather than a vague price drop. MP Materials ended the quarter with $1.34 billion in cash and equivalents while guiding capital expenditures for the full year between $500 million and $600 million. Professional traders write the exact condition down before entry so the stop-loss can be derived directly from it and so post-trade review focuses on whether the data point actually arrived. One concrete habit that separates informed investors is checking the company’s own guidance table in the earnings release for the precise metric and quarter that would falsify the case. The common misconception is treating any decline as proof the thesis failed; the fix is stating the measurable event that must occur inside the holding window and exiting only if it does.
