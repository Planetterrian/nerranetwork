The Bank of Canada’s Business Outlook Survey is the technique I want you to actually run, not just nod at.

It interviews roughly one hundred firms chosen to match GDP composition, it ships with the Canadian Survey of Consumer Expectations, and the decision it supports is a sector tilt — not a single-name gamble — before the next rate announcement.

You pull the latest PDF off the Bank of Canada site, write down the net percentage of firms expecting stronger sales or tighter labour markets, then in your brokerage screener you line those readings up against TSX sector exchange-traded fund, or ETF, holdings and favour the groups that historically moved when those nets improved, which in prior cycles meant industrials and consumer names.

Think of it like tasting every pot in a cafeteria whose menu was built to match the city’s appetite — you are not asking your favourite cook how lunch will go; you are sampling the same mix of kitchens the economy actually runs on.

What would you do if the net expecting stronger sales jumped and industrials looked cheap on the TSX screen — overweight the sector inside a TFSA, or wait for the rate decision itself? I treat the survey as a confirmation layer, not a standalone trigger, because the same approach failed when geopolitical shocks overrode the domestic signal and because survey answers lag sudden external events.

Combine the response mix across regions and firm sizes with those ETF screens so you are allocating to revenue exposure that already lines up with improving sentiment, which is how you stop chasing overnight headlines.
