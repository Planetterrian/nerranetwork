### Investor Education: Currency Exposure Inside Registered Accounts
Imagine you bought a U.S. equity ETF last week inside your TFSA when the yen intervention headlines hit. Your order filled at the posted NAV, but what actually happened is the brokerage converted your CAD at the prevailing spot rate plus a 1.5% markup before the trade even executed. The mechanism is straightforward: most Canadian platforms apply the interbank rate plus a spread that widens from 0.3% in calm markets to 1.8% during volatility spikes like today’s 4% yen move. Professionals always check the exact FX rate timestamp on the confirmation screen and compare it against the mid-market rate on a second platform before approving the trade. The concrete misconception to avoid is assuming your TFSA’s U.S. holdings are automatically insulated from currency moves because they sit in a tax-free wrapper. The one-sentence fix is to run a 30-second mid-market rate check on every USD purchase and set a price alert on USD/CAD at the level where hedging becomes cheaper than the expected volatility. When the yen intervention drove USD/JPY from above 163 down to 157, the CAD/USD cross moved in tandem because Canadian commodity exports and U.S. trade flows create tight linkages between the two pairs. A 4% swing in the yen therefore translated into roughly 1.2–1.8% movement in USD/CAD within the same trading session. Inside a TFSA the tax-free status does not eliminate the conversion cost; the spread is paid at the moment of purchase and again on any future sale back to CAD. Investors who ignore the timestamp on their trade confirmation often discover the platform used a rate 80–120 pips worse than the mid-market print that appeared on financial websites at the exact same minute. Setting a simple alert on USD/CAD at the 20-day moving average lets you decide in advance whether to convert early, hedge with a forward, or wait for the spread to compress. The same discipline applies to RRSP and FHSA accounts where larger USD positions are common; the dollar cost of an unhedged conversion on a $50,000 purchase can exceed $750 once the spread widens during geopolitical headlines.
