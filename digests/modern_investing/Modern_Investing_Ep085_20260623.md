### Investor Education: Reading the Bid-Ask Spread in Real Time
Imagine you bought an ETF tracking Japanese equities last week when the Nikkei 225 was already sliding; your order filled at the ask, but the quoted spread had just widened from 4 cents to 18 cents in the first 20 minutes of trading. The mechanism at work is liquidity withdrawal by market makers when overnight news (in this case rate worries and regional equity weakness) increases perceived risk, so the displayed bid drops faster than the ask can be hit. What most retail investors don’t realize is that the effective spread you actually pay can be two to three times the displayed quote once you factor in the depth at each level—checking Level 2 data before market open reveals whether the inside market is one-sided. The professionals always compare the current spread to the 20-day average before entering; if it is more than 50% wider, they either wait or use a limit order at the midpoint. The biggest mistake with spread dynamics is assuming the posted quote is the cost you will pay. Instead, always pull up the time-and-sales tape for the prior 15 minutes and size your order to no more than 20% of the visible depth at the bid.
