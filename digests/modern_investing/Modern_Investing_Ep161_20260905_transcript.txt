Imagine you bought an ETF tracking Canadian banks last spring and it now sits 12% below your cost base after rate-sensitive rotation.
Your order to sell would trigger a capital loss. Yet the superficial loss rule means you cannot repurchase the identical ETF for 30 days without the CRA denying the loss.
The mechanism works by requiring a 30-day window on either side of the sale during which you must stay out of the exact security or an identical one.
But you can immediately buy a similar but not identical ETFs such as switching from XIU to XIC or a global financials ETF to maintain market exposure while still claiming the loss.
Professionals always verify the T50008 slip issued by their broker the following February to confirm the loss was properly reported before carrying it forward indefinitely against future gains.
The capital gains inclusion rate change that took effect in 2024 makes harvesting more valuable because only half of gains are taxable.
So sheltering losses early compounds the benefit across multiple years.
The common misconception is that you must wait until December to harvest. The fix is to scan positions quarterly and execute any harvest as soon as the 30-day clock can be satisfied without disrupting your target allocation.
Losses realize this way remain available to offset gains in any future year and the T50008 reporting requirement means investors should retain brokerage statements that show both the sale and the replacement purchase to defend the claim if audited.
