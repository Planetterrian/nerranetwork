### Strategy Spotlight
Canadian investors have long favoured income-focused ETFs because the tax-free growth inside a TFSA turns modest yields into compounding machines without annual tax drag. Today’s Globe and Mail piece on “true yield strong and free” highlights how covered-call and high-dividend ETFs deliver 6-9% distributions that would otherwise trigger immediate taxation outside registered accounts. Implementation starts with screening for ETFs holding Canadian equities to avoid the 15% U.S. dividend withholding that still applies inside TFSAs. The strategy has worked best in sideways or modestly rising markets where option premiums remain elevated; the main risk is NAV erosion when underlying holdings decline sharply. Track contribution room monthly through your brokerage and set alerts for any distribution classified as return of capital, which reduces adjusted cost base. Use the ETF screener on Questrade or Wealthsimple to filter for management fees below 0.75% and trailing twelve-month yields above 6%. ### Investor Education: Maximizing Your TFSA as an Investment Vehicle
Imagine you bought shares of a U.S. growth stock inside your TFSA last month at $48.50; the order filled cleanly, yet you later discover the 15% withholding tax on any future dividends quietly reduces the compounding power you thought was fully tax-free. The mechanism works because TFSAs shield Canadian-source income and capital gains completely, but U.S. dividends still face that withholding unless held through a Canadian-listed ETF wrapper that avoids direct U.S. exposure. Most retail investors don’t realize contribution room resets only on January 1 and that over-contributions trigger a 1% per month penalty until withdrawn. Professionals always verify the exact unused room on the CRA’s My Account portal before any large purchase rather than relying on brokerage estimates. The biggest mistake with TFSAs is treating them like high-interest savings vehicles instead of long-term compounding accounts. Instead, always allocate the highest-growth assets you can tolerate to the TFSA first, then use non-registered accounts for income-heavy holdings that generate taxable distributions.
