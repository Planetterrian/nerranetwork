### Strategy Spotlight
Normal-course issuer bids let companies repurchase their own shares on the open market under TSX rules, effectively returning capital to shareholders while supporting the stock price. Colliers International Group’s newly approved NCIB for subordinate voting shares illustrates how management can signal undervaluation and reduce share count without a special dividend. Canadian investors can screen for TSX-listed firms announcing NCIBs through their brokerage news feeds or SEDAR+ filings, then compare the buyback yield to the company’s free-cash-flow generation. This approach has historically worked best in sideways or mildly declining markets where organic growth is modest but cash flow remains strong. Risks include management overpaying for shares or using buybacks to mask deteriorating fundamentals. Always verify the maximum number of shares authorized and the daily volume limits before adding exposure. ### Investor Education: Real Estate Investing in Canada Beyond Your Home
Imagine you already own a TFSA and want to add real-estate exposure without buying a second property. You could purchase units of a publicly traded REIT such as RioCan or Canadian Apartment REIT at market open; the order fills instantly but the distribution yield (currently 4–6% range for many names) arrives monthly and is taxed as ordinary income inside an RRSP. The mechanism that surprises most retail investors is the tax treatment difference: REIT distributions are not eligible for the dividend tax credit, yet inside a TFSA or RRSP they grow tax-deferred, while direct rental properties allow depreciation claims and potential principal-residence exemption on sale. A pro tip most advisors give is to check the REIT’s AFFO payout ratio before buying — anything above 90% leaves little cushion for maintenance capex. Mortgage investment corporations (MICs) offer another route with higher yields but illiquidity and credit risk that retail platforms rarely highlight. The biggest mistake with real-estate allocation is treating your home and a REIT as interchangeable; instead, always size the REIT or MIC position so that total real-estate exposure (including your principal residence equity) stays below 30–40% of net worth.
