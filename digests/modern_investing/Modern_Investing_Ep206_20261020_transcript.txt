The 50-day and 200-day moving averages are dynamic support and resistance that a lot of algorithms and desks actually watch,
so a break can invite more selling from systematic flows rather than from a
person changing their mind. The 50-day line is just the average closing price of
the prior 50 sessions. The 200-day line is the longer trend and price above both
is treated as an uptrend until a sustained break below the 50-day starts to look
like momentum loss, especially if volume is rising. RSI on the 14-day setting a
zero to 100-speed gauge would have needed a reading below 30 to even argue
you oversold, and even then a bounce attempt wanted volume above the 20-day average before
it counted as participation. A working habit is to mark the prior days low as first support
and put the alert there instead of guessing which way the next candle wants to go. Mac
deconflash when it's two exponential averages cross, and patterns like double bottoms or flags
can look like timing edges at known support, but those signals fail more often than not unless
volume confirms and you have a hard invalidation under the pattern low.
