### Strategy Spotlight
Covered-call ETFs such as the Hamilton Enhanced Canadian Covered Call ETF generate monthly income by selling out-of-the-money calls against a basket of Canadian equities. In the current environment of moderating GIC renewal rates, the 9.8 percent indicated yield offers a concrete alternative for income-focused accounts that can tolerate the capped upside and potential drawdowns during equity rallies. Investors can implement the approach directly through a TFSA or RRSP by purchasing HDIV on the TSX and holding for the monthly distribution cycle, or by replicating the mechanics themselves on individual holdings using their broker's options chain. The strategy has historically performed best in range-bound or modestly rising markets where volatility supports premium collection, but it underperforms sharply in strong trending bull markets when the calls are exercised away. Risk management requires monitoring the underlying equity exposure and being prepared to accept assignment or roll the calls if the market moves sharply higher. The ETF pays distributions monthly, which aligns with retiree cash-flow needs that previously relied on five-year GICs now renewing at lower rates. Canadian investors can compare the 9.8 percent yield directly against current GIC quotes and decide whether the equity risk premium justifies the move into covered-call structures. ### Investor Education: Quarterly Portfolio Review Mechanics
Imagine you hold Enbridge in your TFSA and want to verify whether the recent bought-deal issuance and acquisition spending have shifted your overall allocation targets. The review begins by exporting your account holdings into a spreadsheet and calculating each position's percentage of total portfolio value using current market prices rather than cost basis. Enbridge trades near $66 per share after raising roughly $3 billion in a bought-deal to fund US pipeline and utility acquisitions that now total more than US$3 billion on top of the earlier US$14 billion natural gas utility purchases. Next compare those weights against your stated targets, noting any drift above or below your tolerance bands, then calculate time-weighted returns by isolating each cash flow date and applying the appropriate growth factor. Review realized versus unrealized gains to identify tax-loss harvesting candidates before year-end, and separately track the trailing twelve-month dividend income trend to confirm whether the income stream is stable or eroding. Enbridge has raised its dividend in each of the past 31 years while advancing more than $40 billion in secured capital projects tied to North American energy demand growth from AI data centers and LNG exports. Subtract all fees paid in the quarter from the gross return to isolate the true net performance, then run a simple decision checklist: rebalance only if drift exceeds your band, harvest losses only if the tax benefit exceeds transaction costs, and add new capital only to names that still meet your original thesis criteria. The key misconception to avoid is treating the review as a performance scorecard instead of a rules-based rebalancing and tax-planning exercise.
