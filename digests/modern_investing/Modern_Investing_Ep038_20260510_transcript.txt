let us build the foundational options knowledge every TFSA investor needs. Imagine you own 100 shares
of a Canadian bank trading at $85 in your TFSA and want extra income without selling the position.
A covered call lets you sell the right for someone else to buy those shares at a higher strike,
say $90. Collecting a premium upfront, that premium is your income, but you cap upside if the stock
surges past the strike. Calls give the buyer the right to purchase at the strike price while puts
give the right to sell, creating protection or bearish bets. The key Greeks are Delta,
which shows how much the option price moves with the stock and theta, which measures daily time decay
working in the seller's favor. Before trading, pull up the options chain on your broker platform
and compare open interest and bid-ask spreads across explorations. The biggest mistake beginners make
is selling naked options without collateral, exposing them to unlimited losses. Instead,
always start with covered calls on stocks you already owned and would happily hold at the strike price.
