### Investor Education: Tax-Loss Harvesting in Canada
Imagine you bought an ETF tracking Canadian banks last fall and it is now down 12% in your TFSA. You sell to lock in the loss for tax purposes, but the superficial loss rule means you cannot repurchase the identical ETF for 30 days or the loss is denied. The mechanism works like this: the 30-day window applies to the same security or one the CRA considers identical, so switching to a similar but not identical broad-market ETF (for example, moving from XIU to XIC) lets you keep market exposure while claiming the capital loss. A pro tip most retail investors miss is that losses can be carried forward indefinitely and offset future gains, and the new 50% capital gains inclusion rate makes harvesting even more valuable because every dollar of loss now shelters more taxable income. The T5008 slip your broker issues will report the proceeds, so track your adjusted cost base carefully in a spreadsheet to avoid double-counting. The biggest mistake with tax-loss harvesting is selling and immediately buying back the exact same ticker. Instead, always map to a close substitute and set a calendar reminder for the 31st day to decide whether to switch back.
