### Strategy Spotlight
Tax-efficient growth allocation inside a TFSA lets investors compound large capital gains without triggering inclusion-rate taxes on withdrawal. Article 11 shows Aritzia (TSX: ATZ) up 519 percent over three years as a candidate whose future doubling target would otherwise face tax drag outside the TFSA. Canadians born in 1991 or earlier already hold $109,000 in accumulated TFSA contribution room, giving room for concentrated positions in high-growth names. The TFSA is the easiest registered account to use because all investment income earned inside it stays tax-free and withdrawals carry no tax liability. Open your TFSA on Wealthsimple or Questrade, screen for names with five-year revenue growth above 15 percent and market caps under $10 billion, then size the position so it fits your existing contribution room. Aritzia exceeded its five-year growth targets a year earlier than planned and continues to aim to double again in the coming five years, making the account structure especially valuable for shielding those potential gains. The approach works when the stock delivers multi-bagger returns; it fails if the name enters a prolonged drawdown and you cannot harvest the loss against other gains. Use the platform's contribution-room tracker before allocating to avoid over-contribution penalties. RRSP contributions still deliver an immediate tax deduction that can push peak earners into a lower bracket, making both accounts complementary rather than competitive for most retirement plans. The RRSP can reduce taxable income in the contribution year and allow tax-free growth while capital remains inside, yet withdrawals count as income that may trigger OAS clawbacks once they exceed certain thresholds. Canadians should therefore target largest growth investments inside the TFSA and use the RRSP for the tax-deduction benefit during high-earning years. ### Investor Education: Technical Analysis Mechanics
You hold a basket of semiconductor names inside your TFSA that have already dropped 31 percent over three months and you place a market order to exit at the open. The mechanism that actually determines your fill is the bid-ask spread and order-book depth at that moment, not the last traded price you saw on the chart. The portfolio in question sits at $475.88 after a $216.87 loss, representing a 31.30 percent decline over the three-month period for a minimum-wage earner who cannot claim capital losses inside the TFSA. When volume spikes on news the spread can widen from a few cents to 15 or 20 cents within the first 30 minutes, turning a planned exit into a larger realized loss than the 50-day moving average alone suggested. The pro habit is to set a limit order at the midpoint of the current spread and refresh it every 60 seconds rather than accepting the market price. The common misconception is that a clean chart pattern guarantees an orderly exit; the fix is to treat every technical level as a probability zone that only becomes reliable when confirmed by volume above the 20-session average on the day you act. Because the holdings sit in a TFSA, any realized loss stays trapped inside the account and cannot offset gains elsewhere, making the timing of the exit decision even more consequential for preserving remaining capital. Support and resistance levels on daily charts provide zones rather than exact prices, while moving averages such as the 50-day and 200-day lines help identify trend direction but require volume confirmation to carry statistical weight. RSI and MACD serve as momentum indicators that can flag overbought or oversold conditions, yet they lose reliability during low-volume periods or when broader sector rotation overrides individual stock signals. Chart patterns with documented edge include breakouts accompanied by above-average volume, whereas unconfirmed head-and-shoulders or cup-and-handle formations often fail without that volume filter. The honest limitation remains that technical analysis improves timing more than selection and works until it does not, so pairing it with fundamental catalysts and position sizing rules produces better outcomes than relying on patterns alone.
