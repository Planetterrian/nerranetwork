### Investor Education: How Index Funds Changed Investing Forever
Imagine you bought a broad-market ETF last week when the NASDAQ dipped 1.5%. Your order filled at the closing NAV, but what actually happened between your click and that price was a silent auction among authorized participants who arbitrage any premium or discount to the underlying basket in real time. The mechanism keeps tracking error under 0.05% for most S&P 500 ETFs because creation/redemption units of 50,000 shares force market makers to keep the ETF price within a few basis points of fair value. What most retail investors don’t realize is that this same mechanism is why active mutual funds with 2%+ MERs have delivered negative alpha versus the index in 85% of rolling 10-year periods after fees. The professionals always check the ETF’s premium/discount history on the exchange website before allocating, because even a 0.3% persistent premium compounds into meaningful drag over a decade. The biggest mistake with index investing is assuming “set and forget” means ignoring costs entirely. Instead, always compare the ETF’s expense ratio and bid-ask spread against the mutual fund alternative in the same asset class before committing capital.
