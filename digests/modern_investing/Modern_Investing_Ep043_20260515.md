### Strategy Spotlight
The barbell portfolio pairs a concentrated set of high-conviction growth holdings with a sleeve of defensive, income-generating names to perform across market regimes. Today’s environment of elevated oil prices and selective AI infrastructure spending makes the approach relevant because it limits drawdowns when momentum fades while still capturing upside in data-center and networking names. Implementation starts by allocating roughly 60% to eight core positions—typically four growth and four defensive—then rebalancing quarterly using a simple moving-average filter on each sleeve. Historical data shows the structure delivered lower volatility than pure equity benchmarks during 2022’s rate-hike cycle while still participating in the subsequent recovery. The main risk is over-concentration in the growth leg if sector rotation stalls, so position sizing should respect a 5% single-name cap inside the TFSA. Platforms such as Wealthsimple or Questrade make the rebalancing step straightforward with low-cost limit orders. ### Investor Education: How Insurance Float Powers Compounding at Scale
Imagine you bought a Berkshire Hathaway position last quarter after reading about its insurance operations. Your shares were acquired at the prevailing market price, yet the real engine behind the long-term return wasn’t the equity portfolio alone—it was the float generated by premiums collected upfront and invested for years before claims were paid. In 2025 the float exceeded $170 billion, effectively providing Berkshire with interest-free leverage that amplifies every percentage point of investment return. Most retail investors overlook this because they focus only on the stock holdings rather than the liability side of the balance sheet. The pro tip is to scan the footnotes of any insurance-heavy company’s 10-K for the “float” line item and compare its growth rate to the investment yield; when float expands faster than claims, the compounding advantage widens. The biggest misconception is treating float as free money without duration risk. Instead, always check the duration of the insurance liabilities before assuming the structure will work at smaller scales.
