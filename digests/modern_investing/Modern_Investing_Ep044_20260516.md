### Strategy Spotlight
Brokerage divergence on the 2026 Fed path creates a clear options-strategy window for income-focused accounts. When forecasts range from zero cuts (BofA, JPMorgan) to three cuts (Citigroup), the market is pricing in higher-for-longer terminal rates than many expect. This setup favors selling premium on rate-sensitive names rather than directional bets. Use your broker's options chain to identify names with elevated implied volatility but stable fundamentals, then sell 30-45 day calls or puts against core holdings. The approach has historically delivered best results in sideways-to-higher rate environments where realized volatility stays below implied levels. Risk is a surprise policy pivot that compresses volatility faster than expected. Platforms such as Interactive Brokers or Questrade let you screen for the highest-premium names while keeping position size under 5% of the account. ### Investor Education: Profile: Cathie Wood and the Innovation Investing Debate
Imagine you bought ARKK last week after a 5% bounce on AI-news rotation. Your fill came at the ask because the ETF's average daily volume had thinned to roughly 4 million shares, widening the spread from 3 cents to 12 cents in the first hour. That spread cost you an immediate 0.4% drag before any price movement. Cathie Wood's ARK Invest model bets on convergence across five platforms—AI, robotics, genomics, energy storage, and blockchain—yet the 2020 150%+ gain was followed by a 70% drawdown as mean reversion hit concentrated bets. The key mechanic most retail investors miss is that thematic ETFs rebalance daily based on price momentum, automatically selling winners and buying laggards, which amplifies volatility. Professionals always check the holdings overlap with broad indexes before adding; ARKK's top ten often exceed 50% weight, turning a "future" bet into a concentrated momentum vehicle. The biggest mistake with innovation-themed products is treating them as diversified growth exposure. Instead, always size them as satellite positions no larger than 5-8% of total equity and rebalance annually against a broad benchmark.
