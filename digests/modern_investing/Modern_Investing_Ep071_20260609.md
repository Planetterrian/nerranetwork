### Investor Education: Real Estate Investing in Canada Beyond Your Home
Imagine you allocated $50,000 from your TFSA into a rental property in a mid-sized Canadian city last year expecting steady cash flow. The mortgage payment and maintenance quickly consumed most of the rental income while capital-gains tax treatment on any eventual sale still applies at the new inclusion rate. Publicly traded REITs such as RioCan or Canadian Apartment REIT offer the same real-estate exposure with daily liquidity and no property-management burden, though their distributions are taxed as ordinary income inside non-registered accounts. Mortgage investment corporations provide higher yields through private lending but carry credit risk that is harder to diversify than a broad REIT ETF. The BRRRR strategy can be adapted for Canadian markets by using a HELOC on an existing property, yet it triggers superficial-loss rules if you repurchase the same asset within 30 days. Professionals always compare after-tax internal rates of return across REITs, MICs, and direct ownership before committing capital. The biggest mistake with real-estate allocation is assuming direct ownership always outperforms liquid alternatives. Instead, always model the full tax and effort cost of each vehicle before choosing.
