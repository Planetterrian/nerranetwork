### Investor Education: Core-Satellite ETF Portfolio Strategy
Imagine you bought XIC last week when the TSX rallied on mining gains and your order filled at the market price. The mechanism that actually determines your cost is the ETF's creation-redemption process which keeps the price within one or two basis points of net asset value even during volatile sessions. Most retail investors do not realize that holding the core in a registered account like a TFSA avoids annual capital gains distributions that can erode returns in non-registered accounts. The pro tip is to set satellite positions at fixed dollar amounts rather than percentages so rebalancing happens only when the core drifts more than five percent from target. The common misconception is that frequent satellite trading improves returns when the real edge comes from keeping the core allocation stable and letting the satellites run until they hit a predefined exit rule such as a twenty percent gain or a sector rotation signal. The creation-redemption arbitrage keeps tracking error low enough that even a five percent satellite allocation can be added or removed without moving the overall portfolio beta outside its intended range. Registered-account placement of the core also shields the investor from the annual tax drag that appears when the same ETF sits in a taxable brokerage account during high-distribution years. Fixed-dollar satellite sizing removes the need for constant percentage calculations and instead triggers a review only when the core weight moves beyond the five percent drift threshold. Exit rules tied to absolute gains or sector signals prevent the common error of holding a satellite too long after its original catalyst has faded.
