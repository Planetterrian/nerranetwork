at the strike. If the stock stays below it, you keep the shares and the premium. Think of it as
renting out a parking spot on land you still own. A pro habit is to read open interest and the
bid asks spread on the chain before you send the order. Because a widespread on a low volume strike
can erase most of the income edge. Covered calls are not free money. They reduce but do not
eliminate losses if the stock falls hard. And they clip you in a strong rally. Size so the premium
offsets no more than 2-3% of potential downside before you would sell the shares anyway. Inside
a TFSA, or a registered retirement savings plan, or RRSP, the premium does not trigger
an immediate tax bill, which is why this is a Canadian account mechanic rather than a taxable
account afterthought. Delta on that 170 call typically sits around 0.25, so the position
behaves like 75 shares of long stock, plus 25 shares of short stock for risk purposes.
