Now, here's something that most retail investors get wrong about dividends, and it can quietly erode returns if ignored.

Imagine you bought B C E yesterday expecting to collect the upcoming quarterly dividend.

Your order filled at the closing price, but the stock opened today roughly one point five percent lower because buyers after the ex-dividend date no longer receive that payment.

The mechanism works through the exchange’s automatic adjustment where the previous day’s close becomes today’s opening print minus the dividend amount.

The actual gap can sometimes widen or narrow due to normal trading.

Most retail investors don’t realize the adjustment happens before the market opens and can be masked by broader moves.

Professionals always check the ex-dividend date on the company’s investor page and compare it against their settlement cycle before placing the order.

The biggest mistake with dividend capture is ignoring the price drop and transaction costs.

Instead, always calculate the net yield after the ex-date adjustment and only proceed if the annualized return still exceeds your opportunity cost.
