### Strategy Spotlight
When oil prices surge, the first-order effect is higher input costs for retailers and manufacturers, but the second-order effect is which companies can actually pass those costs through without losing volume. The practical approach is to screen for firms with demonstrated pricing power—measured by gross-margin stability over the prior two quarters and low customer concentration. Run the screen in your brokerage’s fundamental scanner using the filters “gross margin variance < 150 bps over 4 quarters” and “revenue from top customer < 15 %.” This setup has historically worked best in the first 90 days after a sustained oil spike above $80, when energy costs are still rising but consumer staples and select discretionary names have not yet been fully repriced. The risk is a rapid demand destruction scenario that compresses volumes faster than prices can adjust. ### Investor Education: Is Passive Investing Creating a Bubble?
Imagine you bought a broad-market ETF last week when oil headlines pushed energy names higher; your order filled at the prevailing ask, but the ETF’s underlying index weights had already shifted another 0.4 % toward the largest constituents by the time the trade settled. That 0.4 % drift is the mechanical outcome of market-cap weighting: every new passive dollar automatically buys more of the stocks that have already risen, regardless of valuation. Over the past decade this loop has added roughly 1.8 percentage points of annual return to the S&P 500’s largest names simply from inflows, according to flow-adjusted attribution studies. What most retail investors do not realize is that active managers still execute the marginal trade that sets closing prices, so the bubble risk only materializes if passive flows overwhelm that price-discovery capacity. The concrete misconception to avoid is assuming “if the index keeps rising, my ETF will keep working.” Instead, always check the percentage of total trading volume still coming from active desks before adding fresh passive exposure after a 20 % run-up in mega-caps.
