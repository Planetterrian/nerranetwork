### Strategy Spotlight
Diversification across developed Asian markets can protect against single-country policy risk when one market like Hong Kong’s Hang Seng remains far below prior peaks due to property-sector liabilities exceeding $300 billion and regulatory crackdowns on major tech names. Today’s conditions make this relevant because Japan’s Nikkei, Taiwan’s market, and Korea’s KOSPI have all hit or approached records on AI and semiconductor strength while the Hang Seng has not recovered. Implementation starts by allocating a sleeve of a TFSA or RRSP to a broad Asia ex-China ETF or individual holdings in Japanese and Taiwanese semiconductor supply-chain names, then rebalancing quarterly using the 200-day moving average as a guide. This approach worked best during the 2015-2016 China slowdown and the 2022 global rate-hike cycle when markets with clearer policy predictability outperformed. The main risk is currency fluctuation and slower overall GDP growth in the region, so position size should stay under 10% of the equity sleeve. ### Investor Education: How Stagflation Alters Fed Reaction Functions
Imagine you added a broad equity ETF last month expecting the new Fed chair to cut rates on any growth slowdown, only to watch the 10-year yield spike instead because inflation and weak growth arrived together. The mechanism works through the Fed’s dual mandate: when consumer spending edges up only marginally while the saving rate hits a four-year low and war-driven price pressures persist, the central bank faces a credibility test because traditional rate cuts would fuel inflation further. Professionals always check the personal consumption expenditures deflator and the saving rate before assuming policy easing, because those two data points together determine whether the Fed can ease without breaking its inflation target. The biggest mistake with stagflation setups is assuming the Fed will prioritize growth over price stability the way it did in 2020. Instead, always model two scenarios—one with 50-basis-point cuts and one with hikes—before sizing any duration or growth-stock exposure.
