### Investor Education: Real Estate Investing in Canada Beyond Your Home
Imagine you already own a rental condo in Toronto and last month’s rent increase notice pushed your cash-on-cash yield below 4%. The actual mechanics between that notice and your net return involve mortgage interest deductibility, capital-gains inclusion rates, and REIT distribution tax treatment that most DIY investors miscalculate. Publicly traded REITs such as RioCan or Canadian Apartment REIT deliver monthly distributions taxed as ordinary income inside a non-registered account yet flow through tax-deferred in an RRSP, while a direct rental property forces you to track adjusted cost base and claim CCA only on the building portion. Mortgage investment corporations add another layer: they pool first- and second-mortgage debt and pay out interest income that is fully taxable, yet they avoid the maintenance calls of physical ownership. The BRRRR strategy adapted for Canada requires a 20% down payment on the refinance leg and must clear the stress-test rate at the time of renewal, which can erase the intended leverage if rates have risen. Professionals always compare after-tax internal rates of return across the three vehicles before allocating fresh TFSA room. The common misconception is that direct rental property always beats REITs because of control; the one-sentence fix is to run the after-tax cash-flow and total-return numbers for each option on the same spreadsheet before committing capital. Syndicated mortgages introduce an additional liquidity layer where investor capital is locked for fixed terms, often three to five years, with returns driven by the underlying mortgage coupon rather than property appreciation. Tax treatment differs again because interest income flows through as ordinary income without the capital-gains deferral available inside a TFSA. Comparing these options side-by-side reveals that REITs often deliver the lowest ongoing effort while direct ownership maximizes control at the cost of higher operational overhead and tax complexity.
