### Strategy Spotlight
When oil prices exit the danger zone and roll over, history shows equities—especially cyclical sectors—tend to accelerate because lower energy costs act as a broad tax cut on consumers and businesses. The current swift pullback in crude creates exactly that setup, with Evercore ISI noting the move supplies a tailwind for the broader economy. Investors can implement this by screening for sectors with depressed multiples that also show same-day volume above their 20-day average, then rotating a small TFSA allocation into a broad financials or industrials ETF. The pattern has worked best in post-recession recoveries when oil declines coincide with stable or falling rates; the risk is a sudden geopolitical spike that reverses the oil drop before equities price it in. Pair any rotation with a same-day volume check to avoid false signals. ### Investor Education: Volume Confirmation Before Momentum Entries
Imagine you bought a bank ETF last week after the Globe and Mail noted the sector’s best quarter since 2009 and the price ticked higher on light volume. Your order filled, yet the next session the index reversed because the move lacked participation from larger players. The mechanism is straightforward: volume below the 20-day average signals the advance rests on thin buying that can evaporate when broader sentiment shifts, turning a 2% gain into a 4% loss within two sessions. Professionals always check whether the day’s volume exceeds that average before treating any breakout as confirmed; most retail investors skip this step and chase the headline instead. The biggest mistake with momentum entries is entering on price action alone. Instead, always require volume above the 20-day average on the same day the catalyst appears.
