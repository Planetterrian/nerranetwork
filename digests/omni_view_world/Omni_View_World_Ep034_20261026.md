**Sudan paramilitary drone strikes cause mass power cuts in eastern states: The Guardian**
In Sudan, the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces launched a coordinated drone attack on five power stations, a government official told The Guardian. The drones hit two stations in Al-Jazira, two in Gedaref and one in White Nile, all states the official said are held by the Sudanese army, and cut power across large parts of those three states. The official spoke on condition of anonymity. The army and the Rapid Support Forces, former allies, have been at war since April 2023. The United Nations has warned that repeated attacks on power stations threaten health care and other services for millions of people already facing what it calls the world's largest hunger crisis. In Al-Fao, in Gedaref, resident Babiker Youssef said the power had been out since early morning and that his diabetic son's insulin has to be stored in a fridge. A United Nations tally says drone strikes killed more than 300 people and injured hundreds more in the first three weeks of October. Aid workers estimate the war has killed more than 200,000 people in a country of an estimated 50 million; The Guardian reports that most hospitals have been forced to close and that at least 8 million children are out of school. In Al-Junaid, in Al-Jazira, farmer Abdelhafez Daafallah, 54, said a station the militia had destroyed was repaired and power had started to improve three months ago, then was hit again, blocking irrigation pumps he hoped would let him farm. In Gedaref, grocer Ibrahim Mohammed, 37, said he faced losing refrigerated chicken or selling it at any price, and The Guardian reports the currency lost more than 25 percent of its value in recent weeks.

**Algeria to send troops to Niger in first foreign deployment in over 50 years: France 24**
In Algeria, both houses of parliament on Sunday approved sending troops to neighbouring Niger, the first foreign deployment of Algerian forces since troops went to support Egypt in the 1973 Arab-Israeli war, France 24 and Al Jazeera reported. The upper house approved the proposal with all 148 members in attendance voting yes, a legislator told AFP; the lower house said it voted by an absolute majority. The deployment is to protect energy facilities, and Senator Belkacem Boulghiti told the Associated Press, as carried by Al Jazeera, that troops would protect Algeria's economic interests, particularly Sonatrach oil facilities in Niger. Niger faces persistent attacks by armed groups, including near the 960-kilometre desert border, and the neighbours have agreed to deepen cooperation against jihadist violence, cross-border crime and trafficking. The vote followed Wednesday's visit by Niger's defence minister, Salifou Modi, who met President Abdelmadjid Tebboune and army chief Said Chengriha; Algerian reports said a military cooperation protocol was signed. On October 3, Tebboune said in a televised address, "We will protect Niger no matter what," and said agreements in oil, mining and other sectors were at stake. Al Jazeera reported that nearly two months ago Algeria sent four Russian-made Sukhoi Su-30 jets, a transport aircraft and an aerial refuelling aircraft to Niamey after what Algerian authorities called an attempt to destabilise Niger's military-led government, and France 24 reported that Algiers had gifted four military helicopters. A 2020 constitutional amendment requires two-thirds backing in parliament for troops abroad; Al Jazeera cited the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute as putting Algeria's 2025 military spending above 25 billion dollars, the most in Africa. Researcher Abdellah Akir told AFP the decision breaks a non-intervention doctrine, is tied to securing the Trans-Saharan Gas Pipeline from Nigeria through Niger toward Europe, and signals Morocco and the United Arab Emirates, countries with which Algiers no longer has diplomatic ties.

**Saudi Arabia confirms January start for Asian Cup 2027 despite attacks: Al Jazeera**
In Saudi Arabia, the 2027 Asian Cup will start on January 7 in Riyadh, where the hosts face Palestine in Group A, under a schedule the Asian Football Confederation announced Monday, Al Jazeera reported. The final is set for February 5 at Riyadh's 72,000-seat King Fahd Sports City Stadium, which also hosts the opening match, and 51 matches will be played over 29 days at eight venues, five in Riyadh, two in Jeddah and one in Al-Khobar. The local organising committee said it is sticking to the original schedule, although AFC chief Salman bin Ibrahim Al Khalifa said this month that a postponement until 2028 was possible if necessary. Japan Football Association president Tsuneyasu Miyamoto said that, based on the news, the situation was still unsafe and that he did not think it would be easy to hold the cup there, adding that Saudi officials told him safety of players, fans and everyone attending was the top priority. Earlier Monday, Dakar Rally organisers said the 2027 race would be moved out of Saudi Arabia because of recent developments in the region. The Saudi-led coalition intercepted a barrage of projectiles from Yemen on Sunday, and Saudi Civil Defence said shrapnel from two intercepted ballistic missiles, suspected to have been fired by Yemen's Houthis, fell in Jeddah, Taif, Rabigh and Wadi Al-Mughammas in Mecca province. Several major events have been cancelled or postponed amid escalating Houthi attacks; international flights from King Khalid International Airport were set to resume from Terminal 2 on Sunday, with Terminal 1 for domestic flights, after suspensions that followed strikes including one on October 10 that killed 13 people and wounded hundreds. Several of the 24 participating federations have raised security concerns; defending champion Qatar opens against Thailand on January 11, the same day Japan plays Indonesia, and Saudi Arabia is hosting the Asian Cup for the first time, seven years before the 2034 World Cup.
Ranks: players, fans and residents in Riyadh, Jeddah and Al-Khobar, plus 24 national teams · a confirmed January tournament amid missile and drone attacks · January 7, 2027, unless organisers later change course Source: [aljazeera.com](https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/10/26/saudi-arabia-confirms-january-start-for-asian-cup-2027-despite-attacks?traffic_source=rss)

**Mississippi's maternity deserts deliver a political crisis: The Globe and Mail**
In the United States, a new report from the non-profit March of Dimes finds that nearly half the counties in Mississippi are maternity deserts, meaning there is no obstetrician, no family physician who delivers babies, no nurse midwife, and no hospital or clinic, The Globe and Mail reported. Michael Warren, the organisation's chief medical and health officer, said this is the result of deliberate policy decisions and resource allocations. He said 17 percent of the state's counties are one clinician away from having no one to provide obstetric care, so the state risks still more maternity deserts. The Globe reports that more infants die in Mississippi than in any other state, and that too many mothers die of pregnancy-related causes as well. Women travel long distances to a hospital, which the paper says can make prenatal appointments less likely and the trip for delivery harder. In Cleveland, Mississippi, Pam Chatman, 57, gives away baby supplies at her store, Boss Lady4Kids, through a partnership with 4 Good Community, and she has delivered fruit and vegetables to expectant mothers because people in the area struggle with obesity, diabetes and high blood pressure, which she treats as risks in pregnancy. She created Boss Lady Economic Planning and Development Workplace Transportation to offer free rides from the rural Delta to FedEx work in Memphis, and during the pandemic she helped deliver vaccines. The Mississippi Delta, in the state's northwestern corner along the Mississippi River, is known as the birthplace of the blues and the civil rights movement, and the paper describes neighbours filling gaps where services are scarce.

### Both Sides: Should the 2027 Asian Cup stay on its January dates in Saudi Arabia?
The Asian Football Confederation and Saudi organisers have published a January and February schedule for the 2027 Asian Cup, while attacks on the kingdom and a rival event's exit have put that choice under challenge. The case for going ahead is that the local organising committee is sticking to the original dates, that the AFC has now announced a full match calendar, and that Saudi officials told Japan Football Association president Tsuneyasu Miyamoto their top priority is the safety of players, fans and everyone attending. AFC chief Salman bin Ibrahim Al Khalifa has already said a postponement until 2028 is possible if necessary, which leaves a formal off-ramp rather than an open-ended commitment. The case against keeping the dates is Miyamoto's: based on what he had learned from the news, the situation was still unsafe, and he did not think it would be easy to hold the cup there. Dakar Rally organisers, earlier Monday, moved the 2027 race out of Saudi Arabia because of recent developments in the region. Saudi Civil Defence said shrapnel from two intercepted ballistic missiles, suspected to have been fired by Yemen's Houthis, fell on Sunday in Jeddah, Taif, Rabigh and Wadi Al-Mughammas, and the kingdom has faced waves of Houthi missile and drone attacks, including an October 10 strike that killed 13 people and wounded hundreds and led to a suspension of flights at Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport. Several of the 24 federations have raised security concerns. The next dated step on the published schedule is the January 7 opening in Riyadh, unless the AFC uses the postponement option Al Khalifa described.
