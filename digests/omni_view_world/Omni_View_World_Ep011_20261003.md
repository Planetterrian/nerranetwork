### The Ten
**Iran gearing up for 'major round of fighting' with US, source says: The National**

Iran, in the Middle East, is preparing for what a source in Tehran called a major round of fighting with the United States. The National carried that account on Saturday, as another American carrier moves toward the region and blasts hit Houthi-held Sanaa.

The source said the American military option has not been abandoned and that this is clear to the Iranians. They are preparing for a major round and have the capabilities to fight it, the source said. The two sides met in New York last month to find common ground before full negotiations to end seven months of conflict. No breakthrough emerged after several meetings. President Donald Trump and Iranian officials have since signalled that they are preparing for the war to resume. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has said Iran would fight even if it meant an apocalyptic war.

A United States official told The National on Thursday that a third aircraft-carrier strike group, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, is en route to Central Command’s area of operations, which spans the Middle East. Axios reported that Trump’s top national security officials held a secret meeting at Camp David to discuss next steps in the Iran war and Washington’s approach to the Iran-backed Houthi offensive in Yemen. Vice President JD Vance chaired the meeting, citing people familiar with the discussions.

Yemeni rebels have been attacking Taiz in the south-west after taking the western coast. Multiple Yemeni and Iranian sources said Iranian operatives helped them seize that coast. The offensive has put significant pressure on government forces and is preventing them from launching counter-attacks to regain areas overlooking the Bab al-Mandeb strait. The rebels have not yet disrupted shipping through the waterway. The Tehran source said this caution does not mean later action is impossible, and that an aerial blockade on Iran does not mean Tehran will stand by and watch. Iran’s security, the source said, cannot be separated from the security of the region.

France 24 reported loud explosions in Sanaa on Saturday. One correspondent heard at least five blasts in different areas. Another saw two warplanes firing missiles on the city. Al-Masirah, a Houthi-affiliated channel, reported Saudi aggression against the capital. Earlier on Saturday the channel reported missile strikes on the telecommunications network in Saada, a Houthi stronghold. The Houthis claim Saudi Arabia bombed Sanaa on Thursday night for the first time since fighting resumed in July, after Iran sent a plane carrying a Houthi delegation without Saudi authorisation and Riyadh struck the airport. Sources on both sides reported eighty combatants dead on Friday in fighting centred on Taiz. The Houthis have announced a naval blockade on Saudi ships in the Red Sea and have struck Saudi oil infrastructure and Riyadh. On Saturday an AFP journalist saw flames and smoke at an Aramco facility south of Riyadh. The company is majority-owned by the Saudi state. The circumstances remained unclear. In recent weeks Aramco has come under Houthi attack.

That leaves Iranians and Americans without a negotiated end, a third carrier moving, and Tehran’s foreign minister on record for a fight he has called apocalyptic. It leaves people in Sanaa, Saada and Taiz under reported fire, and Saudis with an oil-site blaze whose cause is unclear plus a rebel announcement of a Red Sea blockade.

### Both Sides: Should border-wall work in Big Bend wait on the law?
A federal court has paused border-barrier construction across a long stretch of the Texas border until agencies follow laws the judge says still apply. Judge Kathleen Cardone, of the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Texas, issued a preliminary injunction on Friday. It says federal agencies shall not build a border barrier or related infrastructure, and shall not do construction or ground-disturbing work, in the Big Bend Sector until they comply with all applicable laws.

The case for building is the priority President Trump has set and the money already voted. He has made reducing illegal crossings of the southern border a main policy priority. Republicans control the Senate and the House. His 2025 measure, described in the report as the Big Beautiful Bill, earmarked 46.5 billion dollars for fencing and other obstacles along the 1,954-mile southern border. Planned works include hundreds of miles of 30-foot wall, surveillance systems and river barriers, on private land as well as federal land. In August, bulldozers were seen clearing land in Big Bend National Park.

The case for the pause is the one Cardone accepted for now, brought by a coalition of environmental groups and local landowners. She concluded they are likely to succeed on at least one claim, that they are likely to suffer serious, irreparable harm without an injunction, and that the balance of the equities and the public interest weigh in their favour. The order covers the whole Big Bend sector, about 500 miles, around a quarter of the US-Mexico border, including Big Bend National Park, which the National Park Service says has 118 miles of border with Mexico. The court did not decide the full legal case for a wall there. It found the challengers likely to win on at least one claim.

The injunction is in force, and barrier work in that sector stays barred until the agencies comply with all applicable laws. Cardone left the final merits undecided. Source: [newsweek.com](https://www.newsweek.com/trump-suffers-legal-setback-as-texas-border-wall-blocked-in-national-park-12519989)

### Progress Watch
Greece and Bulgaria have sealed a cultural exchange negotiated over fifty years: under the deal, forty-eight relics taken from churches and monasteries in northern Greece by Bulgarian troops in the First World War — icons, crosses and ecclesiastical vessels kept at Bulgaria’s National History Museum — are to return to Athens, and remains thought to belong to Tsar Samuel were flown to Sofia on a military aircraft with a fighter escort and a twenty-one-gun salute. Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis told a crowd in Thessaloniki on Saturday that monuments and historical heirlooms cannot be treated as spoils of war.
