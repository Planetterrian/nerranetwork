**Zelensky criticises 'strange' Trump-Putin friendship in BBC interview:** BBC
In Ukraine, President Volodymyr Zelensky told the BBC at his central Kyiv office that delays in American anti-ballistic missiles are costing lives and that Washington is not pushing Russia hard enough in peace talks. He called it strange that President Donald Trump posted a photo of himself embracing Vladimir Putin, and said an aggressor and a killer cannot be a friend, likening that to saying one was friends with Adolf Hitler during the Second World War. Since a bruising Oval Office encounter with Trump in 2025, the BBC reported, Zelensky has been careful in how he handles the United States. Trump's two envoys have been trying to negotiate an end to the loss of life; the BBC reported they often seem more in tune with Moscow's position than with Kyiv's. The latest round, mediated in Abu Dhabi earlier this month, made no obvious progress. Zelensky's description was that the more they know, the more problems, and he said talks are expected to resume in December. On Thursday Trump posted on Truth Social that, at his own summit with Putin in China last week, he had urged Russia to pull back all military aggression against territories throughout Europe and that he believed a peace deal with Ukraine was a strong possibility. The BBC reported that, from its public statements, Russia appears to have regressed to its initial hardline negotiating positions on everything. Zelensky said it was not clear whether European negotiators would be at the table, as the American envoys had announced, and the BBC reported the Kremlin appears to be resisting. The BBC said Ukraine urgently needs the talks to move and to bring five exhausting years of Russia's full-scale invasion to an end, and that Russian strikes are growing more frequent and more deadly, with the number of civilians being killed at an all-time high. Zelensky called for real helpful allies who can push Russia, and said that trying to be very neutral with the aggressor means supporting the victims and also the aggressor. The BBC said the United States argues it is neutral in the search for a deal that can hold. Questioned again on Thursday about calling Putin a friend, Trump told reporters he wants to stop the killing, that he could say Putin is not a friend, and that he wants to make a deal; he added that walking in and saying he does not like Putin would repeat the problem of two leaders who really hate each other, which he called the biggest problem for a settlement. In the meantime, the BBC reported, air alerts day and night are a draining feature of everyday life: Russian ballistic missiles target power stations and bridges, jet-powered drones are launched daily at central Kyiv and other big cities, and Ukraine launches hundreds of drones at targets in Russia in response. Zelensky said the military can now destroy about 70 percent of Russia's fastest drones, a statistic he said was improving, but that there is a very, very big deficit of interceptors for ballistic missiles. He said the Americans were delaying deliveries of Patriot systems, that it is a pity they made a focus on the Middle East and help them more, and that the deficit of these missiles is people's lives.

**‘I cannot go to work’: Iranians contend with pollution as well as US war:** Al Jazeera
In Iran, the threat of American bombs is not the only danger residents of the capital must contend with, Al Jazeera reported: checking the air has become part of the daily routine. Tehran resident Ghoral, 40, told Al Jazeera that headaches, shortness of breath and blurred vision he once had only in the heavily polluted cold seasons are now year-round. He said the nausea hits even before the Meteorological Organization announces that the air is polluted, and that on heavily polluted days he tries to leave the house as little as possible, which means he sometimes cannot go to work, cutting his income, while the cost of a doctor and of medicine for his breathing problems rises. Hossein, 36, who both lives and works in the city centre, told Al Jazeera that for someone like him pollution is no longer just a part of life; it has become life itself. He said there is little anyone can do except escape the city as often as possible, particularly in winter, and that at the very least he tries to spend weekends in nearby cities where the air is better. Weather apps, Al Jazeera reported, are used less for rain or thunderstorms than for readings of fine particulate matter, PM2.5, the tiny airborne particles that can be deadly when breathed in over a prolonged period. The second half of the year has traditionally been a time of temperature inversion, when a layer of warm air traps cooler air and pollutants close to the ground and keeps them from dispersing. Pollution has become year-round, Al Jazeera reported, but it is particularly bad in winter, when that trapping is common, in cities and industrial areas. Mashhad, Isfahan, Shiraz, Tabriz and Ahvaz are also grappling with it. For more than half of the autumn and winter months, warnings are regular, advising people with heart and respiratory conditions to take precautions, and advising children, older people and pregnant women to stay indoors and avoid outdoor activities. The Meteorological Organization uses a yellow weather warning for days when pollution is particularly high; on those days schools typically cancel outdoor physical education. When levels approach an orange weather warning, generally an air quality index above 140 on a scale of 0 to 500, additional restrictions may follow: some employees switch to remote work, an odd-even licence-plate scheme limits cars, schools close, and some university classes move online. Dr Hadi Yazdani, who works at a medical centre in Isfahan, told Al Jazeera that one of the first signs of rising pollution is more hospital visits by patients with chronic asthma and other respiratory conditions. Figures from the Ministry of Health, as Al Jazeera reported them, say hospital visits for those conditions and for heart conditions rise by as much as 50 percent in winter, and that eye and skin problems also tend to become more common. Yazdani said children and older people are particularly vulnerable, that they may develop new illnesses or see existing ones worsen, and that because pollution reduces the circulation of fresh air, respiratory infections such as influenza and COVID also become more prevalent.

**Russia Moved Fuel by Train to a Secret Forest Depot—Ukraine Found and Destroyed the Supply Hub:** UNITED24 Media
In Ukraine, forces have destroyed a concealed Russian fuel and lubricants depot, along with supporting infrastructure, in the occupied Luhansk region, UNITED24 Media reported, targeting a logistics network that supplied Russian military units operating in Ukraine. The strikes targeted facilities in Vilkhove and Dovzhansk as part of Operation Hell-3, according to statements published on November 27 by Ukraine's 1st Azov Corps of the National Guard and the 9th Army Corps. Those statements said the operation was carried out jointly by the SxLüd unit and the Signum Unmanned Systems Battalion of Ukraine's 59th Assault Brigade. Russian troops belonging to the Center military grouping had established the fuel storage inside a forest, the statements said, using underground tanks to conceal its location and reduce its vulnerability to Ukrainian strikes. The Russian military also used camouflage, electronic warfare systems, and mobile firing teams intended to defend the hub against aerial attacks. Despite those precautions, Ukrainian units said they located and destroyed the concealed depot and the infrastructure supporting its operations. The facility served as a distribution point: fuel was transported to the depot by rail, then transferred into vehicles and delivered to individual Russian units. UNITED24 also reported earlier accounts that Russia's worsening fuel crisis was beginning to disrupt military operations in Ukraine, with Russian troops reporting reduced gasoline and diesel deliveries, strict refueling limits, and growing difficulty supplying frontline positions. In some cases, that reporting said, Russian troops are forced to abandon military equipment because it runs out of fuel. Recently, UNITED24 reported, Russian troops abandoned a working T-72 tank on the battlefield during a retreat after it simply ran out of fuel. The November 27 statements are Ukrainian military accounts of a strike already carried out; they are not a Russian confirmation.
