In the Pacific, Australia’s Bureau of Meteorology says average weekly sea temperatures in the central ocean are the highest it has on record.

A daily mark in the central-eastern Pacific in September already passed the very strong El Niño of twenty fifteen, the BBC reports, and the expected peak is still December.

Forecasters in Australia and the United States now use a relative index so human-caused ocean warming does not hide the natural spike.

On that index, the bureau’s weekly series, which starts in two thousand eight, is already above December twenty fifteen.

Zeke Hausfather, a climate scientist at Berkeley Earth, says this year’s strength is spilling past the usual monitoring box.

Set beside the big events of nineteen seventy-two, nineteen eighty-two, nineteen ninety-seven and twenty fifteen, the warm water reaches farther across the Pacific and into the Gulf of Mexico.

The pattern turns up about every two to seven years and usually runs for about a year.

Brazil’s Senate is where the case on both sides of the six-day workweek now sits.
