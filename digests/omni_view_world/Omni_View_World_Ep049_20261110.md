**What is Hasina’s game plan as she seeks a passport to return to Bangladesh?: Al Jazeera**
Bangladesh's former prime minister Sheikh Hasina is seeking a new passport to return next month from exile in India. The 79-year-old was sentenced to death in absentia last year for crimes against humanity over the 2024 student crackdown, a charge she denies. The United Nations has put the death toll from that uprising at more than 1,400. She fled to Delhi in August 2024 after a mass uprising ended nearly 20 years of her rule, and a British lawyer wrote on Monday to the Bangladesh high commission there asking for a passport and a national identity card. Exiled Awami League figures told the Guardian she is confident of a December return, and a party leader in Kolkata said she will seek a travel pass if a passport is refused. Prosecutor Md Aminul Islam said there is no legal scope to issue documents to someone he called a convicted fugitive, and that she would face execution of the verdict if she returned because the appeal deadline has passed. Retired US diplomat Jon Danilowicz told Al Jazeera there is zero chance she returns in December and called the move a bluff, while Faiz Sobhan of the Bangladesh Enterprise Institute said it is meant to lift a banned party's morale. Zillur Rahman of the Centre for Governance Studies said she wants to keep Awami League activists engaged and will not hand the party to anyone else. A return would likely mean immediate arrest under Prime Minister Tarique Rahman, who took office in February after what the Guardian described as the country's first democratic election in decades.

**U.S.-mediated talks on ending the war between Russia and Ukraine begin in Abu Dhabi: NBC News**
In the United Arab Emirates, three-way talks between Russia, Ukraine and the United States opened Tuesday in Abu Dhabi, the first such meeting since February in a war that followed Moscow's all-out invasion nearly five years ago. The White House said the two-day session will focus on security guarantees. Jared Kushner wrote that he and Steve Witkoff were working on a negotiated solution for a sovereign, secure Ukraine inside a more stable Europe. Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 162 drones and a ballistic missile overnight, while Russia's Defence Ministry said its forces shot down 155 Ukrainian drones over eight Russian regions and Crimea. Vladimir Medinsky, leading the Russian delegation, said the team had grown and that he hoped the meeting would advance peace for the people of Russia and Ukraine. Rustem Umerov, leading Kyiv's delegation, said the day began with the US envoys and representatives of the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the European Commission. President Vladimir Putin wants Ukraine to withdraw from Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson, abandon a NATO bid, accept limits on its military and drop restrictions on the Russian language and the Moscow-affiliated Orthodox Church, and he rejects a general ceasefire as a pause that would let Ukrainian forces rearm. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who was in Denmark, rejects ceding land Russia has not taken, rejects limits on Ukraine's forces or its Western arrangements, and says a ceasefire must come first and that only a direct meeting with Putin can settle territory. NBC News said earlier rounds this year brought no significant breakthrough, and that analysts and humanitarian organizations put the dead at hundreds of thousands of soldiers and thousands of civilians.

### Both Sides: Ceasefire first, or a full Ukraine deal only?
What is being decided in Abu Dhabi is whether any halt in the Russia-Ukraine war can come before a comprehensive settlement, and on whose terms. The White House said the two-day talks focus on security guarantees. Russia sees NATO as a threat. Ukraine wants arrangements that would prevent another invasion.

President Vladimir Putin's case, as NBC News reported it, is that only a full deal will do. He wants Ukrainian forces withdrawn from Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson, the four regions Moscow annexed in September 2022 but never fully captured. He also wants Ukraine to drop its NATO bid, accept strict limits on its military, and end restrictions on the Russian language and the Moscow-affiliated Orthodox Church. His stated reason for refusing a general ceasefire is that a pause would let Ukrainian troops rest and replenish weapons. He has warned that Russia will achieve its goals by force if Kyiv refuses those conditions.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's case is the reverse order. He rejects giving up territory Russia has not taken on the battlefield, and he rejects limits on Ukraine's military or on its arrangements with Western partners, which Kyiv calls essential guarantees against another invasion. Ukraine's stated reason for an immediate ceasefire is that it is the necessary first step toward any wider settlement, and that territorial questions can be settled only in a direct meeting between Putin and Zelenskyy.

The talks are already underway, with Vladimir Medinsky leading Russia's delegation and Rustem Umerov leading Ukraine's, and with Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff for the United States. No date for a leaders' meeting is set in today's reporting.
