### The Ten
**Why is Trump backing Iraq’s anti-smuggling campaign?: Al Jazeera**
Iraq’s federal government spent this week pulling oil routes, border gates and armed groups toward Baghdad, and the United States president put his name on that campaign before a dialogue with the Kurdish region that is already on the calendar.

The public American turn came on Friday. President Donald Trump wrote on Truth Social: “We support Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi’s efforts to assert state authority throughout Iraq, stop the smuggling of Iraqi oil, end corruption, and bring all weapons under the exclusive control of the state.” A second sentence in the same post said, “We support Prime Minister al-Zaidi’s efforts to build a united, prosperous, and strong Iraq that makes its own political decisions.” Al Jazeera reported that the post was Washington’s first comment on the tensions between Baghdad and Erbil. It arrived days before talks between the two, due on December 1 under the sponsorship of the head of Iraq’s Supreme Judicial Council.

The week’s operational sequence, as Al Jazeera lays it out, is short and dated. On Tuesday, al-Zaidi moved to place the Kurdish region’s border crossings and several oilfields under federal control. On Wednesday, Iraq’s top court ruled that Baghdad can deploy federal forces along all of the country’s international borders, including those of the Kurdish region. On Thursday, Iraq closed two crossings in that region, one with Syria and one with Iran. France 24 described the prime minister as taking bold steps to curb rampant oil smuggling, moves the United States has praised, and steps that risk putting him at odds with Iraq’s Kurds and with neighbouring Iran. The French account does not add a separate casualty figure, a named field, or a quoted Kurdish reply. The American post, on Al Jazeera’s reading, is the first Washington comment after those measures, not a new operational order of its own.

Salim Zakhour, a researcher specialising in constitutional law and political systems, told Al Jazeera the timing is significant because Trump’s post comes after Baghdad’s measures and before the planned dialogue. “It is a clear message, first to the federal government, as a show of support for it,” he said. “But the more important message is for the authorities and Kurdish leaders in the Kurdistan region. It tells them that all the measures taken by the federal government have strong American backing.” That is an analyst’s account of who the post is aimed at. Trump’s own sentences name four aims in one breath: state authority throughout Iraq, a stop to smuggling of Iraqi oil, an end to corruption, and weapons under the exclusive control of the state.

Baghdad’s stated reason is the treasury. The Iraqi government says it wants to clamp down on oil smuggling that is harming its economy. Government spokesperson Haider al-Aboudi told the Iraqi News Agency that Iraq has lost an estimated $100 billion between 2008 and 2025 from oil smuggled through the Kurdish region. That number is the government’s estimate, given by its spokesperson to the state news agency, and carried by Al Jazeera. It covers seventeen years and a route the government says ran through the semi-autonomous region, not a single seizure announced this week.

The campaign did not open on the Kurdish frontier. Al Jazeera reports that it began in Jurf al-Sakhar, also known as Jurf al-Nasr, a town in Babil province south of Baghdad. The armed group Kataib Hezbollah has held the town since 2014, when Iran-backed groups took it from ISIL, also called ISIS. Its mostly Sunni residents were displaced. Al Jazeera describes Kataib Hezbollah as Iran’s most powerful armed ally in Iraq. The United States designates the group a terrorist organisation. The group denies the allegations.

Senior Iraqi security, army, police and oil officials told the Reuters news agency that Kataib Hezbollah has since run an oil smuggling network there, siphoning off state-owned crude worth hundreds of millions of dollars since 2021. Those are allegations from officials, relayed by Reuters and reported by Al Jazeera. They are not a court finding in these articles. Nine senior security and oil officials have been dismissed and referred to the courts. Referral is the step the reports record. They do not record a verdict against those nine.

Al-Zaidi gave the group until November 20 to leave the town. A day before the deadline, the two sides reached a compromise, reportedly following direct intervention by Esmail Qaani, the head of Iran’s Quds Force. Under the deal, government forces entered Jurf al-Sakhar and set up a joint operations centre. Kataib Hezbollah units are due to withdraw gradually as army, federal police, energy police and Popular Mobilisation Forces units deploy in phases. The articles describe an agreed sequence and an entry already made. They describe the armed units’ exit as due, and gradual, as those other units deploy.

Kataib Hezbollah is not the only group resisting Baghdad’s push to bring weapons under state control. Harakat al-Nujaba and Kataib Sayyid al-Shuhada have also refused to disarm. After the armed groups missed a September 30 deadline to hand over their weapons to the state, the government pushed the deadline to June 2027. Two clocks now sit side by side. One is local: a phased entry in Babil after a compromise reached the day before November 20, with Iran’s Quds Force chief reported as the intervener. The other is national: a weapons date the government itself moved from the end of September to June 2027 after groups did not meet the first date. Trump’s Friday sentence folds both kinds of aim into one endorsement — oil smuggling stopped, and all weapons under exclusive state control — without setting either clock.

Baghdad then extended the campaign from that town to the Kurdish region. The extension, in the reporting, is concrete: border crossings and several oilfields placed under a federal claim on Tuesday; a top-court ruling on Wednesday that federal forces may deploy on every international border, including the Kurdish region’s; two closures on Thursday, one gate with Syria and one with Iran. The people the week changes something for are not one bloc. Kurdish authorities face federal claims on crossings and fields they had been running inside a semi-autonomous region, with a dialogue dated for Monday under the head of the Supreme Judicial Council. Traders and officials who used the two closed gates face a shut frontier on two sides of the region at once. Armed groups that have refused to disarm face a stated federal aim, now echoed by the American president, that weapons sit only with the state, against a deadline already slipped to June 2027. Residents of Jurf al-Sakhar live with a joint operations centre in a town held since 2014, after officials told Reuters that state crude was siphoned there; the group that held the town denies that account. Mostly Sunni residents of the town were displaced when Iran-backed groups took it from ISIL. Iran appears twice in these reports: as the neighbour France 24 says Baghdad risks clashing with, and as the state whose Quds Force chief is reported to have stepped in so a compromise could be reached before the November 20 deadline.

What the Friday post changes, on the evidence these articles actually give, is the diplomatic frame for December 1, not a completed transfer of fields and not a finished disarmament. Zakhour’s point is that Kurdish leaders are being shown American backing for measures already taken, before they sit down. Trump’s words set the four aims. He did not, in the text Al Jazeera quotes, describe the two closed crossings or the $100 billion estimate. The operational steps named in the reporting are al-Zaidi’s: Tuesday’s move on crossings and oilfields, Wednesday’s court ruling as the legal step Baghdad can cite, Thursday’s closures, the Jurf al-Sakhar entry, the referral of nine officials to the courts, and the June 2027 weapons date.

The next dated step these articles name for the Kurdish dispute is December 1. The next dated step they name for weapons is June 2027. Between those dates, the Babil compromise still has Kataib Hezbollah units withdrawing only as army, federal police, energy police and Popular Mobilisation Forces units deploy in phases. The government’s loss figure remains an estimate spoken by Haider al-Aboudi to the Iraqi News Agency, attached to oil the government says was smuggled through the region from 2008 to 2025. The hundreds of millions of dollars since 2021 remain an allegation from senior Iraqi officials to Reuters, denied by the group the United States designates a terrorist organisation. France 24’s risk line — at odds with Iraq’s Kurds and with Iran — sits next to an American endorsement that arrived after the closures and before the talks.

### Both Sides: Federal control of Kurdish oil routes and weapons
Iraq’s government is deciding how far Baghdad, rather than the Kurdish region or armed groups, will run international borders, oilfields and weapons. Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi has already moved crossings and several oilfields toward federal control, closed two regional crossings, and set a state monopoly on arms as policy. President Donald Trump has endorsed that package in a Truth Social post. Talks with Erbil are due on December 1 under the head of the Supreme Judicial Council. A separate weapons deadline now sits at June 2027, after groups missed September 30.

The case for exclusive federal control, as al-Zaidi’s government and Trump state it, starts with the treasury and the state’s claim to force. The government says oil smuggling is harming the economy. Spokesperson Haider al-Aboudi told the Iraqi News Agency the loss through the Kurdish region is an estimated $100 billion from 2008 to 2025. Senior Iraqi security, army, police and oil officials told Reuters that a network in Jurf al-Sakhar, also called Jurf al-Nasr, siphoned state-owned crude worth hundreds of millions of dollars since 2021. Trump’s post ties that crackdown to a second aim: all weapons under the exclusive control of the state, corruption ended, and an Iraq that “makes its own political decisions.” Iraq’s top court has ruled that Baghdad may deploy federal forces on all international borders, including the Kurdish region’s. Salim Zakhour told Al Jazeera the American post is a show of support for the federal government and a message to Kurdish leaders that the measures already taken have strong American backing. A supporter of the push can point to a court ruling, a dated loss estimate from the government spokesperson, allegations from senior officials, referrals to the courts, and an American endorsement before Monday’s talks.

The case against treating those steps as settled starts with denial, refusal, and the risk of a wider rift. Kataib Hezbollah, which the United States designates a terrorist organisation and which Al Jazeera calls Iran’s most powerful armed ally in Iraq, denies the smuggling allegations. The group has held Jurf al-Sakhar since 2014, when Iran-backed groups took it from ISIL, and its mostly Sunni residents were displaced. The government already moved its weapons deadline from September 30 to June 2027 after groups missed the first date. A compromise in Jurf al-Sakhar, reportedly after intervention by Esmail Qaani, head of Iran’s Quds Force, has government forces inside the town with a joint operations centre, and Kataib Hezbollah units leaving only gradually as army, federal police, energy police and Popular Mobilisation Forces units deploy in phases. France 24 reports that the smuggling crackdown, which the United States has praised, risks putting al-Zaidi at odds with Iraq’s Kurds and with neighbouring Iran. On Zakhour’s reading, Kurdish leaders are being shown that Washington backs measures taken before they sit down on December 1. The $100 billion figure is the federal spokesperson’s estimate. The hundreds of millions are allegations officials gave Reuters, which the accused group denies. Thursday’s two closures — one crossing with Syria, one with Iran — are federal acts taken before the dialogue, not a joint decision announced with Erbil.

What happens next is dated, not decided. The weapons handover date the government now cites is June 2027. The phased withdrawal in Babil is still described as due, not done. Trump’s post names the aims.

The UN health agency estimated 9.8 million measles cases and about 72,000 deaths worldwide in 2025, down 74 percent from 37.8 million cases in 2000, with deaths falling roughly 91 percent from about 769,000. The same account says millions of children still missed vaccinations and that outbreaks persisted in dozens of countries. The measured drop is the result the agency published.
