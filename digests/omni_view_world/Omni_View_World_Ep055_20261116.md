**Yemeni forces retreat to defend Aden from Houthis: BBC World**
In Yemen, Saudi-backed pro-government forces say they are moving troops off the Bab al-Mandab Strait to defend the interim capital, Aden, after weeks of intensified fighting with the Iran-backed Houthi movement. The Southern Armed Forces said on Sunday they were withdrawing from Dhubab Airport and the Bab al-Mandab area to strengthen front lines, and said this step was driven by the priority of securing Aden and neighbouring governorates. Houthi-run media say the movement has taken control of the evacuated territory; Saudi Arabia has not commented. The pullback ends a weeks-long offensive near the shipping route the Houthis seized in a surprise offensive in September. Pro-government forces said they had secured the strait in early October, while the Houthis said they had captured Perim Island and the port city of Mokha a month before that claim. The Houthis said they posed no threat to international shipping through the waterway but would keep targeting vessels from Saudi Arabia, and the seizure contributed to a surge in global oil prices because Saudi Arabia had been relying on the Red Sea for oil exports since the US and Israel's war with Iran effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz. A UN Human Rights spokesperson, Ravina Shamdasani, urged all parties to protect civilians and civilian infrastructure, and noted that since fighting escalated in August more than 60 percent of documented civilian casualties were women and children. The Houthis have repeatedly attacked Aden, including an assault on 15 October on the presidential palace that killed five and injured 16; Saudi Arabia responded with hundreds of air strikes, and the Houthis launched hundreds of drones and missiles at Saudi cities and infrastructure. The UN has estimated that nearly 330,000 people have been displaced as the fighting escalated, and hundreds of people are reported to have been killed. Yemen's civil war began in 2014, when the Houthis ousted the government from Sanaa; a Saudi-led coalition intervened in 2015, and a four-year informal ceasefire began to unravel in July, when the Houthis announced a maritime embargo against Saudi Arabia.

**USS Roosevelt arrives in Middle East: Is the US-Iran war about to resume?: Al Jazeera**
In the Middle East, a US official told Al Jazeera that the aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived on Monday, the third American carrier in the region, joining the USS George Washington and the USS George H.W. Bush in the US Central Command area. The Pentagon and Central Command have not formally confirmed the arrival. Another official told Al Jazeera the ship reached the Middle East on 15 November after leaving San Diego in September. The United States and Iran agreed a two-week ceasefire in April and a broader memorandum of understanding in June that collapsed in early July. Mediators from Pakistan and Qatar have since tried to prevent a return to the full-scale attacks that hit the wider Gulf in March. Diplomatic efforts to permanently stop the US-Israel war on Iran and reopen the Strait of Hormuz remain stalled. Last week President Donald Trump said further military strikes against Iran were "quite unlikely" and that the military component of the war was "effectively over" after talks with Qatar's prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani, while adding that the US military remains "locked and loaded" if conditions change. The Roosevelt, commissioned in 1986, carries more than 5,000 personnel and more than 60 aircraft, including F-18s, the US Navy says. A US official told Al Jazeera last month that the Makin Island Amphibious Ready Group, carrying more than 2,000 Marines, had also headed for the region; the Wall Street Journal reported those naval deployments could involve an additional 9,000 to 10,000 US soldiers in total, which the US military has not confirmed.

**Record US LNG Exports May Bring Relief to War-Strained Markets: Energy Connects**
In the United States, estimated pipeline deliveries to all US liquefied natural gas export plants climbed to about 21.8 billion cubic feet on Saturday. BloombergNEF data, reported by Bloomberg, put that flow at an all-time high and more than 12 percent above a year earlier. Pipeline gas to the Golden Pass project reached a record on Saturday, and flows to Sabine Pass were the highest since April. Bloomberg reported that the added supply could ease gas markets in Europe and Asia as the Northern Hemisphere enters winter. The war in the Middle East has forced countries to look elsewhere for cargoes and pushed prices to the highest in years. Prices in Asia, home to the largest importers, have tripled compared with pre-war levels, while European gas futures have climbed nearly threefold. US LNG output is poised to double by the end of the decade, and the International Energy Agency says an expected shift into oversupply has been pushed back at least two years by disruptions to output from the Persian Gulf.

**Two mass shootings in South Africa leave 17 dead: BBC World**
In South Africa, police are investigating the murders of 17 people in two shootings on Sunday night that they say are unrelated. Eight men were fatally shot in a minibus outside Pietermaritzburg in KwaZulu-Natal; police spokesperson Robert Netshiunda said officers found a white seven-seater riddled with bullet holes, and that the motive and the suspects' identities had not been established. Hours later, police found six men and three women shot dead inside a four-room shack in the Jeffsville informal settlement in Atteridgeville, west of Pretoria, between 22:00 and 23:00, with bodies scattered across the rooms and spent cartridges outside. Brigadier Brenda Muridili said the Gauteng provincial commissioner had mobilised crime intelligence and a tracking team. Community members initially mistook the gunshots for thunder. The country is struggling to contain violent crime, including gang killings, and has one of the world's highest murder rates. In September, 18 people were killed when eight gunmen stormed a bar near Johannesburg on 25 September, with 14 others injured, and the next day 10 people were killed outside an entertainment venue near Cape Town, with 11 injured. Much of the violence has been linked to turf wars between gangs involved in drug trafficking and illegal mining. In February President Cyril Ramaphosa called organised crime "the most immediate threat to our democracy, our society and our economic development," and earlier this year the military was deployed with police in five provinces with the highest murder rates, including Gauteng. In January, five men were shot dead outside a shop in that Pretoria area in daylight.

**EU clears UniCredit takeover of Germany's Commerzbank: Yahoo Finance UK**
In Europe, the European Commission on Monday approved Italian bank UniCredit's takeover of Germany's Commerzbank. EU competition chief Teresa Ribera said Europe needs stronger banks that can compete, grow and support businesses and families, and that cross-border mergers can help banks operate more efficiently and better withstand economic shocks. The Commission approved the acquisition after finding the deal would not raise competition concerns. Ribera said the decision shows it is possible to support a stronger European banking sector while safeguarding competition and choice. The European Central Bank had already approved UniCredit's hostile takeover. UniCredit has pursued Commerzbank for two years and built a stake of nearly 50 percent, alarming Germany, where the bank is a key financing source for small and midsize industrial companies. Berlin and the German lender resisted fiercely. UniCredit plans to use the acquisition to cement its status as a European heavyweight, a goal the European Union shares, and the EU this year announced plans to overhaul merger rules to forge what it calls European champions.
