German public broadcasters and a newspaper, in an account carried by the BBC, say anonymous military and intelligence sources described a satellite called Sputnik-S, fitted with a nuclear warhead and able to disrupt or destroy other satellites. France 24 adds that those accounts cite the BND as well as the military, and speak of a launch in the coming weeks.

It is not clear which country wrote the intelligence that allies shared this month. Chancellor Friedrich Merz neither confirmed nor denied the reports. He called them not new and still shocking, and said they show how far Russia is prepared to escalate, while spokesman Stefan Kornelius said Berlin is taking them very seriously and also warned against panic over hypothetical dangers.

Dmitry Peskov said he had not seen the reports and that stories built on unknown sources are hardly worth taking seriously. The Russian embassy in Berlin called the claims unfounded and a propaganda campaign. Russia has signed the nineteen sixty-seven Outer Space Treaty, which bars weapons of mass destruction from orbit.
