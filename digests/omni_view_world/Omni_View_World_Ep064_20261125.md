**Iran rations electricity: How deeply are US sanctions, blockade biting?: Al Jazeera**
Iran is imposing rolling power cuts from Sunday, expected to last about two hours a day, on an electricity system that supplies 92 million people. President Masoud Pezeshkian asked millions of Iranians to conserve power in a televised interview with state broadcaster IRIB, conducted with the presidential-office chandeliers switched off and daylight let in. Tehran controls part of the world's largest natural gas field and has the world's third-largest proven oil reserves, yet government spokeswoman Fatemeh Mohajerani warned the gas shortfall could reach 430 million cubic metres a day this winter, compared with about 300 million in previous years. National Iranian Gas Company managing director Saeed Tavakoli said four processing plants damaged in the first phase of the conflict initially cut capacity by 230 million cubic metres, of which about 100 million had been restored, and the company warned that 11 of 31 provinces could face supply difficulties, particularly Mazandaran, Golestan and North Khorasan. Mohammad Allahdad, head of the national electricity company Tavanir, told state television that fuel conditions forced distribution restrictions from Sunday. An Al Jazeera analysis put usual generation at about 86 percent natural gas, roughly 7 percent oil-fired, about 5 percent hydropower and about 2 percent nuclear, mainly from Bushehr, Iran's only operational reactor. Energy Minister Abbas Aliabadi said in August that war-related attacks had cost plants 4,200 megawatts, with repairs under way. Pezeshkian told ISNA that the attack on gas infrastructure had aggravated winter difficulties, and told investors on November 21, in remarks carried by IRNA, that Iran faces a "silent economic war" that is "tougher than a war with missiles," after February 28 strikes by the United States and Israel, later hits on energy sites, sanctions and months of a US naval blockade, with the war at an impasse.

**How the War Turned Sudan Into a Country Without a Newspaper: Time Magazine**
Sudan has gone more than three years without a single newspaper printed, the first such gap in over a century, while the Sudanese Armed Forces and the Rapid Support Forces fight an information war and journalists are targeted. Thirty-four journalists have been killed in three and a half years, sixteen of them believed targeted for their work; hundreds have been displaced with their families and lost jobs, and dozens have been threatened, assaulted and detained. Most newspaper offices, television studios and radio stations have been looted, damaged or destroyed. Journalists have still tried to report costs Time put at more than 400,000 people estimated killed and 12 million believed displaced in a nation of 53 million. The war has split control, with the armed forces holding around 60 percent of the territory in the east and centre and the Rapid Support Forces about 40 percent, concentrated in Darfur and adjoining southern and western areas, and both sides tightly control what can be reported. The New York Times reported that the armed forces used banned chemical weapons, and a UN fact-finding report accused the Rapid Support Forces of sexual violence and slavery and described violence in El Fasher as bearing the "defining characteristics of genocide." On October 10, 2023, an Rapid Support Forces vehicle ran over and killed Halima Idris Salim, a Sudan Bukra correspondent, as she reported on a hospital in Omdurman. In December 2024 the armed forces arrested editor Yahya Hamad Fadlallah and his son in Khartoum; the Sudanese Journalists Syndicate and the Darfur Bar Association say he was falsely accused of collaborating with the Rapid Support Forces, tortured and denied diabetes treatment, then released on January 10, 2025, and died in hospital three days later. In May a Port Sudan court sentenced AlBalad TV journalist Rishan Oshi to a year in prison and a $16,553 fine for defamation after a Facebook post on government corruption. Syndicate chairman Abdelmonium Abouedries said, "The war came with a knockout punch to the press," and, "Truth became the war's first victim."
Ranks: a country of 53 million, plus killed, displaced and working journalists · information blackout inside a war already estimated to have killed more than 400,000 · already in force, with no printed paper for more than three years Source: [time.com](https://time.com/article/2026/11/25/sudan-war-rsf-elfasher-journalists-killed-arrested/)

**Huawei found guilty of stealing US trade secrets but acquitted of racketeering: BBC World**
In the United States, a Brooklyn jury found Chinese telecommunications company Huawei guilty of conspiracy to steal trade secrets and of bank fraud, and not guilty of racketeering, after a two-month trial. Jurors convicted Huawei on 10 of 12 counts, including conspiracy to violate US sanctions, and declined the racketeering count, which required a finding that the firm acted like a criminal enterprise. Federal prosecutors first announced the charges in 2019, after the late-2018 arrest in Canada of senior executive Meng Wanzhou, also known as Cathy Meng, who was held for three years and then released to China through a deal with US prosecutors. Assistant US attorney Meredith Arfa told jurors Huawei spent 20 years doing whatever it took to expand, including lying to banks about business in Iran and stealing technology from rivals. US Attorney General Todd Blanche said, "For decades, Huawei and three of its subsidiaries conducted their business in the United States on the basis of lies and theft." Huawei said the racketeering acquittal "confirms that these allegations lack factual basis," and that it would take all necessary legal actions. Defence attorney Brian Heberlig said the company's reach — operations in 170 countries and more than 200,000 staff — came from hard work, called the racketeering count a significant overreach the jury rejected, and said the defence had mixed emotions and had sought acquittals across the board. The Chinese government called the case US suppression of Chinese companies and said it supports Chinese firms protecting their interests. Sentencing is planned for September 2027; Heberlig said Huawei will seek the lowest sentence and he fully expects an appeal.

**Cyberattack exposes unclassified information from CIA system, US media reports: France 24**
In the United States, a cybersecurity breach at the C.I.A. and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence exposed some unclassified information, US media reported on Tuesday. The New York Times said the intrusions targeted an open-source intelligence analysis system that sifts public, unclassified material, which can still give analysts insight on its own or combined with classified spying. The Times, citing US officials and people familiar with the issues, said classified material was not jeopardised. A person familiar with the incident told the Associated Press, on condition of anonymity, that the issue that resulted in the breach has been resolved. The Times quoted an ODNI official as saying there had been no disruptions to critical capabilities, and said the C.I.A. declined comment. The report follows reported cyberattacks on the Agriculture Department and the F.B.I. Asked about the reported F.B.I. breach on Saturday, President Donald Trump told Fox News, "I will say that it's not good. I don't like that," and said he had only recently learned of it. Last week an internal National Security Agency network used to communicate classified information was offline for a time, a nationwide ground stop snarled air travel after controllers struggled to reach pilots, and Apple, JPMorgan Chase and Microsoft had systems go down this month. White House cybersecurity chief Sean Cairncross was fired last week. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said on Sunday that the outages largely stem from a rush to patch and upgrade systems ahead of hackers using artificial intelligence tools at unprecedented scale.
Ranks: US intelligence agencies and users of travel, banking and everyday digital systems · unclassified material exposed, classified systems and travel already disrupted in the same wave · breach reported resolved; the wider outage pattern is current Source: [france24.com](https://www.france24.com/en/americas/20261125-cyberattack-exposes-unclassified-information-from-cia-system-us-media-reports)

**Fact check: Deepfakes and 'US abandons Taiwan' claims spread ahead of vote: DW World**
Taiwan holds local elections on November 28 that could shift power between the sovereignty-leaning Democratic Progressive Party and the Beijing-friendly Kuomintang, with consequences for ties among Taipei, Beijing and Washington. A month before the vote, AI-generated content featuring Democratic Progressive Party Taipei mayoral candidate Puma Shen spiked, and claims that the United States is abandoning Taiwan increased over the same period. DW Fact Check found identical headlines claiming US troops were withdrawing from Taiwan, repeated as articles and videos on the Chinese platforms NetEase and Sohu. The V-Dem Institute at the University of Gothenburg ranked Taiwan the country most severely targeted by foreign disinformation for eleven consecutive years, from 2014 to 2024. Taiwan's Ministry of the Interior has said foreign actors are increasingly interfering through election betting, invitations of Taiwanese citizens to China, donations and banquets, and temple and religious activities. An October 29 Facebook audio clip claims Shen admitted taking kickbacks on US arms purchases; multiple detection platforms flagged it as likely AI-generated, and the posting account was created that same day. A separate image claiming Shen was taking drugs carried an "AI content" label and was flagged by detector tools; Taiwanese outlet Watchout found that account had run at least five Facebook ads whose advertisers, Peng Zhiwei and Guo Yang, are based in China. Taiwan has functioned with de facto independence since 1949 despite Beijing's claim to the island as a province and repeated vows to annex it. Experts told DW that fake quotes and misleading clips are being used to weaken trust in US security commitments.
Ranks: Taiwan's voters, and the Taipei-Beijing-Washington relationship · false candidate and alliance claims in the final days before local elections · vote on November 28 Source: [dw.com](https://www.dw.com/en/fact-check-deepfakes-and-us-abandons-taiwan-claims-spread-ahead-of-vote/a-80004834?maca=en-rss-en-world-4025-rdf)

### Both Sides: Should Israel strike Iran before a government is formed?
What is being decided is whether Israel strikes Iran before a coalition exists, ahead of an initial December 6 deadline to form a government. Interim Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu argues Israel needs a government free of what he calls the "taint" of Arab-party participation so it can answer what he says is an imminent military threat from Iran. On Tuesday, Defence Minister Israel Katz called for preemptive strikes before Iran could fully develop a chemical-weapons programme he said was already under way. Al Jazeera reported that US media said Netanyahu sent Mossad chief Roman Gofman to Washington in mid-November to urge joint strikes, citing intelligence that Tehran was developing chemical weapons, and that Gofman, Shin Bet chief David Zini, National Security Council chair Shmuel Ben Ezra and ambassador Yechiel Leiter back new strikes. In a November 19 Fox News interview, President Donald Trump said there was indication Iran was "looking at gases, looking at bio," because nuclear work had been shut down, while stopping short of committing to military action before the end of the year.

Opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot has told party members he has seen no imminent threat requiring action before a government is sworn in. Avigdor Liberman, who sits on the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Defence Committee, told Israeli media, "If we end up seeing a strike right now, then we'll know that it's completely motivated by politics and not security concerns." Lieutenant General Eyal Zamir and researchers within Mossad reportedly see no case for an immediate attack, and defence officials have warned Netanyahu that new attacks could trigger a wider regional conflict.

Eisenkot's bloc has 48 seats, 13 short of a majority in the 120-seat Knesset. He could look to Arab parties' 19 seats from outside government, which Netanyahu's supporters reject. Likud has 46 recommendations after United Torah Judaism endorsed neither candidate. Eisenkot has ruled out serving under Netanyahu and wants a unity government that shares leadership. Talks between his Yashar party and Likud ended without a deal. The next dated step is December 6.
