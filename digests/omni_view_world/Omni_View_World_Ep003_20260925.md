**Poverty in Argentina rises to 32.3%, ending two years of decline: MercoPress**
In South America, Argentina’s National Institute of Statistics and Census reported on Thursday that poverty reached 32.3 percent of people in the first half of 2026, 4.1 points above the 28.2 percent of the second half of 2025, ending a fall that had run since mid-2024. Extreme poverty rose from 6.3 percent to 7.5 percent. In the 31 urban agglomerations surveyed, 9,729,000 people were below the poverty line and 2,249,000 below the extreme poverty line; estimates published by Argentine media put the national figures near 15.5 million and 3.6 million. Among children under 14, 44.5 percent live in households below the poverty line.

**Full results from Russia’s parliamentary election confirm Kremlin’s dominance: The Globe and Mail**
In Europe, Russia’s Central Election Commission on Friday published full results of the country’s first wartime parliamentary election, giving the Kremlin party United Russia 349 of 450 State Duma seats, a record and more than three-quarters of the chamber. United Russia took 58 percent of the party-list vote and 209 single-seat races. Nearly 60 percent of 111 million eligible voters turned out in three days of balloting that ended Sunday. The vote included four Ukrainian regions Moscow seized and illegally annexed in 2022, and Crimea, illegally annexed in 2014; Kyiv denounced that voting as illegal. President Vladimir Putin said on Wednesday that the results showed public support for Kremlin policy, while the liberal Yabloko party was removed from the ballot last month by Russia’s Supreme Court.

**France to protect key Saudi oil terminal as Houthis step up missile attacks: South China Morning Post**
In the Middle East, President Emmanuel Macron said on Thursday that France will send soldiers, radar systems and defence systems to protect the Saudi oil port of Yanbu on the Red Sea, and that French forces will not join combat. Saudi Arabia said it intercepted missiles fired toward Yanbu and Taif, and the Iran-backed Houthi militia took responsibility for new attacks on the kingdom. The Guardian reported that Saudi Arabia said it intercepted six ballistic missiles, and that Senate Republicans blocked a House-passed war-powers resolution on the US war with Iran by 49 votes to 50. Macron tied surging French fuel prices to that war and to disruption of the Strait of Hormuz.

**Ukraine: Deadly Russian drone strike hits central Kyiv: France 24**
In Europe, a Russian drone strike hit central Kyiv before 8am on 25 September, France 24 reported, killing a 14-year-old. Rescue workers cleared rubble from damaged buildings. Residents said there was little time to react to the air-raid sirens. The attacks also struck logistics facilities and postal infrastructure elsewhere in the city.

**Ethiopia: Tigray rebels claim "full-blown war": France 24**
In East Africa, the Tigray People’s Liberation Front said it is in a full-blown war with Ethiopia’s federal government after seizing key airports and launching attacks into Amhara and Afar. The escalation comes days after the TPLF joined six other armed groups in a new alliance. France 24 said the fighting raises fears of a return to the 2020-2022 war, which killed at least 600,000 people. Ethiopia’s federal army said 272 rebels were killed and 260 wounded in the north.

### Both Sides: Should the United States join X’s challenge to the EU fine?
The European Union’s General Court must decide whether Washington may join Elon Musk and X in seeking to overturn a European Commission fine. In December 2025 the Commission fined X and Musk jointly and severally 120 million euros, or 136.5 million dollars, for violations of the Digital Services Act. TradingView, carrying a Seeking Alpha account, reports that this was the first non-compliance decision under that law, after a two-year investigation into X’s blue-check verification, its advertising repository, and researchers’ access to public data.

The case for letting the United States in is made by the Justice Department, which has asked the General Court to take part in separate challenges by X and Musk. Assistant Attorney General Brett Shumate said the Commission “inappropriately attempted to expand its regulatory authority to reach American companies not present or operating within its jurisdiction.” On that argument, the fine reaches a US company and its owner outside the bloc’s proper authority, so Washington has a direct stake in the review.

The case for leaving the fine in place rests on the Commission’s account of its own decision. It says the penalty followed a finished investigation into specific duties under the Digital Services Act and was imposed on X and Musk together for those violations. The obligations attach to how the service is offered to people in the European Union, and the decision is the first formal finding that X did not comply.

Both sides are before a court that has not lifted the fine. The US request does not itself overturn it. The General Court will decide whether Washington may participate in the proceedings.
