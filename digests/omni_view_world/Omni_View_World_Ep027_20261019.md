### The Ten
**Cuts force World Food Programme to end support for half a million Palestinians in Gaza: The Guardian**
In the Palestinian territory of Gaza, the UN World Food Programme is ending food support for more than half a million people each month after sweeping aid cuts, the agency’s director for Palestine told The Guardian. The director said this winter is likely to deepen hunger for Palestinians there. The Guardian reports that two-thirds of the population face crisis levels of food insecurity. It reports that 200,000 people are in danger of famine. More than a year after a ceasefire brokered by President Donald Trump came into force, the paper reports that promised reconstruction has not begun. It reports that there are few jobs. It reports that the vast majority in Gaza still rely on aid to eat. The cut is monthly support, not a one-time parcel: the director’s account, as The Guardian presents it, is that the agency can no longer carry that caseload each month. The same account places the loss against a ceasefire that has already lasted more than a year, with reconstruction still unstarted and work still scarce. Hunger, on the director’s telling, is set to worsen as winter arrives, on top of the crisis-level insecurity The Guardian assigns to two-thirds of the population and the famine danger it assigns to 200,000 people. The people losing the monthly ration are inside that larger group still depending on aid for food.

**China’s economy grows faster than forecast after AI boom boosts exports: The Guardian**
In China, the National Bureau of Statistics reported Monday that gross domestic product grew 4.5 percent year on year between July and September. The Guardian reports that the figures were better than economists had expected. It reports that the pace was up from the second quarter’s growth rate of 4.3 percent. Roaring exports lifted growth in the quarter, on the paper’s account. Factory output grew much faster than consumer spending. The headline account ties the export rise to an artificial-intelligence boom. It says industrial output was buoyed by electronics demand. The comparison the bureau’s release supports is quarter against quarter: 4.5 percent after 4.3 percent, with the gain coming on the production and export side rather than from households spending faster. The Guardian’s reading is that electronics demand, linked in the headline to that artificial-intelligence boom, is what buoyed industrial output. Consumer spending, in the same account, lagged factory output. The official figure is the National Bureau of Statistics measure for the third quarter of this year, published Monday, and The Guardian presents it as ahead of the forecast economists had made.
Ranks: China’s workforce, households and trading partners · a faster official growth reading than the prior quarter, led by exports and factory output rather than household spending · already in the reported quarter, with the figure public today Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/19/china-economy-grows-faster-than-forecast-after-ai-boom-boosted-exports)

**French interior minister admits police 'lost control' of protests: France 24**
In France, Interior Minister Laurent Nunez admitted Monday that the police “lost control” in some cities during violent protests that erupted across the country at the weekend. He said there had been a “lack of anticipation” in preparations. He said he had tendered his resignation to President Emmanuel Macron “because I felt responsible for everything that happened.” Macron did not accept the resignation. France 24 reports that protests sparked widespread looting and violence, leaving shopfronts demolished and thousands of euros’ worth of stock stolen. On Sunday, Macron held an emergency meeting with senior government officials. He warned that “those who looted will have to pay for the damage.” The Guardian, in its Europe live coverage, reports that Macron criticised “unbridled violence” after 2,400 arrests across France. The same live report says the yellow-vest movement has called a further demonstration for Saturday, October 24, and another a week after that, with a group member telling Agence France-Presse the aim is to keep momentum until a major demonstration planned for November 5. It also reports that Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni will hold a crisis meeting with top security officials on Monday after two days of ransacking and looting in Milan, which the report says was allegedly perpetrated by gangs of hooded youths. Nunez’s offer to leave, and Macron’s refusal of it, sit beside that presidential warning that looters must pay, and beside calls for more street action later this month and on November 5. The unrest the French accounts describe has, on The Guardian’s reporting, already crossed into Italian cities, where Meloni has scheduled security talks for Monday.

**Texans head to the polls, putting Trump’s redistricting map to the test: Democracy Docket**
In the United States, early voting in Texas kicks off Monday and runs through October 30, under newly drawn congressional maps as well as other races on the ballot. President Donald Trump announced last year that the Republican Party was “entitled” to five more congressional seats in Texas, and Republican lawmakers passed that map, Democracy Docket reports. Voters in Tarrant County, which the outlet calls a major battleground area, face the possibility of longer lines after Republican local officials slashed Election Day voting locations by 30 percent. Already this year, thousands of Texans were affected by a statewide voter-registration problem that left local officials scrambling to process a significant backlog shortly before the election. In Texas, mail ballots must be returned by 7 p.m. on Election Day, or by 5 p.m. the following day if the envelope is postmarked by Election Day. This is also the first test for Secretary of State Robert Howden, who took over in July after the previous officeholder was replaced without explanation. Democracy Docket reports that Attorney General Ken Paxton, a Republican candidate for a US Senate seat, could exploit the powers of his office to investigate his own election if he loses, and that Texas legal experts it cites say they have confidence the courts would protect the outcome against bad-faith efforts to overturn it. The outlet says it remains to be seen how Howden will respond to potential pressure from Paxton if Paxton loses and challenges the results. Democracy Docket also names, as candidates it describes as election deniers, state Senator Mayes Middleton, the Republican candidate for Texas attorney general; state Representative Steve Toth, a Republican candidate for a US House seat; and Alex Mealer, another Republican candidate for a US House seat. Governor Greg Abbott, a Republican, is on the ballot against state Representative Gina Hinojosa, a Democrat, and Paxton’s Democratic opponent for the Senate seat is state Representative James Talarico. Democracy Docket discloses that the Elias Law Group represents the Talarico campaign and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee, and that the group’s founder, Marc Elias, founded Democracy Docket. The map Trump sought, the cut in Tarrant County polling places, the registration backlog, and the mail-ballot clock are all in force for a vote whose early period opened Monday.
Ranks: Texas voters using the new congressional map, including Tarrant County voters with 30 percent fewer Election Day sites · where and how ballots can be cast, and which party’s map is tested at the polls · early voting from today through October 30 Source: [democracydocket.com](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/texans-head-to-the-polls-putting-trumps-redistricting-map-to-the-test/)

**Trump is open to a trilateral summit with Putin and Xi Jinping without Zelensky’s participation: Voice of Emirates**
Russia has proposed a meeting of President Vladimir Putin, President Donald Trump and China’s President Xi Jinping to explore ways to end the war in Ukraine, Voice of Emirates reports, citing The Wall Street Journal. The reported Russian initiative would hold that meeting on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum in the Chinese city of Shenzhen between November 8 and 19. Individuals familiar with the discussions, as cited by the newspaper, say the proposal entirely excludes Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelensky. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov confirmed that discussions and consultations are underway. He said there is, as of now, no concrete plan and no clear agenda. He said all three parties agree only in theoretical terms that such a meeting could take place if the appropriate conditions are met. Additional leaks, which Voice of Emirates attributes to media outlets citing a former Russian intelligence officer, say Putin raised the summit proposal in a direct phone call with Trump on October 9. The same attributed account says that call produced a preliminary agreement for the United States to purchase Russian diesel fuel, and an agreement to facilitate the marketing and sale of that fuel in global markets. Voice of Emirates presents the exclusion of Zelensky as raising questions about Ukrainian sovereignty and about a comprehensive political solution. What is on the record from the officials named is narrower: a Russian proposal, an American president who has not approved it, a Kremlin statement that there is no agenda, and a window of November 8 to 19 if a meeting were held beside the Shenzhen forum.
