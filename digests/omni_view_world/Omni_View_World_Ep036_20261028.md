**Rivals in Israel Election Strike Tone of Triumph, but Outcome Is Far From Clear: The New York Times**
In Israel, voters turned out Tuesday in the largest numbers in a generation, and exit polls left Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's alliance roughly even with the opposition bloc led by Gadi Eisenkot. The New York Times reported that the election did not immediately decide who would lead next, and that counting was expected to last days, followed by a potentially lengthy effort to assemble a parliamentary majority. If those polls hold, neither bloc can form a government without two Arab parties projected to take 17 or 18 seats in the 120-seat parliament, which the paper called their strongest showing ever. Netanyahu effectively ruled those parties out of a coalition, and Eisenkot and his partners have balked at including them too. France 24 reported that Arab-led parties appear to have made a historic showing, and that it is not clear whether other opponents of Netanyahu's government would join them for a majority. Eisenkot is a former military chief whose son was killed in the Gaza war. Past midnight, he told supporters, "Tonight we got the answer: The public wants change." Hours later, Netanyahu said it was clear Likud was the largest party, mocked months of polls that had shown his party trailing Eisenkot's, invoked his relationship with President Trump and his case for removing what he calls the threat of Iran, and declared, "We must form a strong right-wing government." The Times said Netanyahu has led the most far-right government in Israeli history, and that Eisenkot has called him unfit to lead. Analysts are already weighing whether the two could join in a unity government; the paper said that path faces major obstacles, because Eisenkot's voters despise Netanyahu and the two would have to agree on who serves as prime minister. After official totals, President Isaac Herzog is to invite the leader he thinks can build a coalition, usually at least 61 lawmakers; Eisenkot has floated a minority government. The Jerusalem Post reported that with more than 4 million votes counted, Netanyahu was pulling ahead of Eisenkot, and that exit polls projected nine seats for Yair Golan's Democrats, potentially more than doubling that party's representation. The same paper reported that Winter and Hendel were both projected to fall short of the 3.25 percent threshold needed for any Knesset seats. The Guardian reported that far-right national security minister Itamar Ben-Gvir's Otzma Yehudit was expected to remain on six seats, the same as in 2022, and that his watch party met the exit polls in stunned silence. Hundreds of thousands of ballots from military bases, hospitals, prisons and embassies were still outside the exit polls; last time those votes favored Netanyahu's coalition, and Israel's exit polls have a spotty record in tight races.

**Mind-Boggling True Cost of Trump’s War on Iran Revealed: The Daily Beast**
In the United States, the Pentagon told Congress that taxpayers had spent at least $47.1 billion on the Iran war through October 16, The Daily Beast reported, nine months into a conflict President Donald Trump had said would last weeks. Congressional officials shared that bill with The New York Times; it is $3.5 billion above the accounting six weeks earlier, and officials told the Times the estimate is almost certainly low. Munitions are $29 billion of the total. Replacements and repairs are $6.1 billion. The Air Force has spent $7.6 billion operating several squadrons and hundreds of sorties. The Army's tab is $2.1 billion for bases in the region, and the Navy's is $1.5 billion, with about 20 ships in the Middle Eastern theater at a time. The Pentagon also listed $92 million for moving supplies, $14 million for cyber operations, $24 million for medical costs, and $645 million for elite commandos. CENTCOM says 50,000 troops are involved. The Daily Beast reported that many Army bases are understood to have come under intense Iranian airstrikes, and that The New York Times found repair costs for those bases were not in this bill. A Congressional Research Service report this month said 80 aircraft had been lost or damaged; the Pentagon said the war has cost 18 American lives and left about 860 people injured. Heavy use of munitions is slowing Patriot shipments to Ukraine, and President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine had waited nearly a month for Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to sign off on missiles for Patriots and NASAMS. In June the White House and the Pentagon asked Congress for another $70 billion. Major strikes are paused while Trump decides whether to resume them after next week's midterm elections. Yahoo reported that he told a rally in Lubbock, Texas, the war would end "whenever I want it to," and that he will soon decide whether to "make a deal" with Iran or "finish it off." The same report said The New York Times had reported that U.S. and Israeli militaries drew up plans for a short but intensive operation against Iran after the midterms, and that the United States evacuated diplomats from Saudi Arabia earlier this month.

**Africa’s ‘unprecedented’ Ebola outbreak could reach UK, says top health official: The Guardian**
In Africa, the current Ebola outbreak has reached 10,000 cases and almost 5,000 deaths, The Guardian reported. Dr Jean Kaseya, director general of the Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention, called that spread unprecedented. He warned the illness could reach the United Kingdom and other countries. He said that risk holds unless more funding is secured for preparedness and possible treatment. He compared the outbreak to Covid, which first took hold in China before crossing borders, claiming millions of lives and ravaging health systems and economies.

**Two high-speed trains collide in Spain, halting traffic on Madrid-Andalusia line: France 24**
In Spain, at least ten people have died in a crash between two high-speed trains Wednesday morning in Toledo province, France 24 reported. Esther Padilla, speaking for the Castilla-La Mancha government, told state broadcaster TVE that both trains were heading from Madrid to Seville and that one ran into the tail of the other while it was stopped, knocking two cars off the track. She told local media there are at least 10 dead and that the number may increase. Rail operator Adif said the collision was between Mora and Orgaz, on the line between Madrid and Seville, and suspended traffic on the Madrid-Andalusia high-speed line, including service between Madrid, Ciudad Real and Andalusia. A regional government spokesperson said a Renfe train and an Iryo train crashed just after 9 a.m., with 397 passengers on the Renfe train and 147 on the Iryo train, and that the cause and the number of injuries were still being determined. The Guardian reported that Spain's state broadcaster said a high-speed train ran into the back of another service and knocked two carriages off the track. Footage on regional Castilla-La Mancha Television showed a derailed train, at least one carriage still on an elevated section, and twisted metal and wreckage on the embankment below. France 24 said El País cited witnesses describing numerous injuries, with two rail cars leaving the tracks and falling down an embankment. Government sources told El País the crash could be worse than the January disaster near Adamuz, on the same line and between trains of the same operators, that killed 46 people. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez wrote on X that he was shocked, said he remained in contact with the transport minister and the president of Castilla-La Mancha, and offered solidarity to the victims and their families. The opposition People's Party cancelled its campaign events; leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo said the priority is to attend to the victims and support their families. Spain's main parties, preparing for next month's general election, suspended planned activities, and the prime minister's published agenda was cleared for the day. Manuel Galán, mayor of Urda, near Toledo, said emergency services asked him to prepare a sports hall for the injured.

**Kim Jong Un tells commanders to prepare for war as border tensions rise: UPI**
In North Korea, Kim Jong Un told army company leaders on Tuesday to get their units fully ready to fight a war, the official Korean Central News Agency reported, as UPI relayed from Seoul. He said the primary revolutionary task of those leaders is to round off combat preparations of their units, and he called for a radical improvement in strenuous, intensive training. He praised company leaders as the foundation of the military's strength. Defense Minister Kim Song Gi, military chief of staff Ri Yong Gil, and Pak Jong Chon, vice chairman of the Workers' Party Central Military Commission, joined him. Kim had chaired the company leaders' meeting in Pyongyang on October 22 and 23, the first army-wide gathering since the party's ninth congress in February. He said the gathering aimed to build the strongest army and accelerate what he called a Korean-style military revolution by training the strongest companies, and he called for units able to carry out any combat mission on their own. The remarks followed September 21 land-mine blasts inside the Demilitarized Zone that wounded three South Korean soldiers, two of them seriously, and fueled a dispute over the inter-Korean boundary. A joint investigation by the South Korean military and the U.N. Command concluded that North Korean antipersonnel mines planted south of the Military Demarcation Line caused the explosions and that their placement violated the Korean War armistice. Pyongyang has denied responsibility and, in a letter to the U.N. Security Council earlier this week, called the incident fabricated by Seoul. South Korea on Friday asked for military talks to clarify the line's location, restore communication channels and prevent accidental clashes. Seoul says North Korean troops doing border fortification have repeatedly crossed the line, prompting warning broadcasts and warning shots, and that many original boundary markers have deteriorated or disappeared. On Wednesday, Unification Minister Chung Dong-young urged the North to accept the talks. In remarks read by a senior ministry official at a peace forum in Goyang, and reported by Yonhap, he said confrontation only escalates tensions and does no good for either side.
