**Super El Niño sets another record as it strengthens to unprecedented levels: BBC World**
In the Pacific, a pattern that reaches the Americas, Asia and beyond, El Niño has strengthened to levels some scientists call unprecedented, weeks before an expected December peak. Australia’s Bureau of Meteorology said average weekly sea-surface temperatures in the central Pacific are the highest on record, after a September daily record in the central-eastern Pacific beat the very strong El Niño of 2015, and temperatures above 30C were recorded for the first time in parts of the tropical Pacific. To strip out human-caused ocean warming, Australian and U.S. forecasters use a relative index; on that measure the bureau’s weekly data, which goes back to 2008, now exceeds December 2015. Zeke Hausfather of Berkeley Earth told the BBC this event is unprecedented in the historical record and that its strength is spilling beyond the usual monitoring region, with warmth more widespread than in 1972-73, 1982-83, 1997-98 and 2015-16, including into the Gulf of Mexico. The pattern occurs about every two to seven years and tends to last about a year, so the record heat is already in place for communities whose weather tracks it, before the peak.

**Ex-president named leader of new DR Congo rebel coalition: BBC World**
In Central Africa, former DR Congo president Joseph Kabila was named leader of the Front for the Refoundation of the Nation, which says it will coordinate resistance forces to end what it calls tyranny, by all means including force. Adviser Claude Ibalanky announced the leadership on Monday at Kabila’s residence in Goma, the largest city in the east, held by the M23 since last year; video released by the group showed Kabila signing, and M23 leader Sultani Makenga will be one of four deputies, the other three also from the AFC-M23 alliance that seized much of mineral-rich eastern Congo. Kabila, 55, ruled from 2001 until January 2019, had denied links to M23, and was sentenced to death in absentia for treason a year ago. The Congolese government has not commented. The naming is the clearest sign yet, the BBC reported, of his direct role in an armed movement challenging President Félix Tshisekedi.

**Deportation plans use quiet northeast Ohio airports: The Guardian**
In North America, planes using Youngstown-Warren and Akron-Canton airports in Ohio are a main route in the Trump administration’s Great Lakes deportation campaign, The Guardian reported. Youngstown-Warren, which handles no commercial flights, saw 398 ICE-contracted shuffle and direct deportation flights last year. ICE Flights Monitor, an advocacy group, has recorded about 300 flights so far this year from that airport and about 300 from Akron-Canton, and only seven other U.S. airports have logged more deportation shuffle flights than the two combined. Aircraft there can handle about 300 passengers at a time, and the paper reported that every day a network of contracted planes carries hundreds of immigrants among Wisconsin, Michigan, Ohio and Massachusetts.

### Both Sides: Should Brazil end the six-day workweek?
Brazil’s Senate can decide whether to end the six-days-on, one-day-off schedule. A constitutional amendment would replace it with five days of work and two of rest. Senate president Davi Alcolumbre has said debates on Tuesday and Wednesday can be followed by a first-round vote, and the amendment is the only item on the Senate floor this week. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva’s government wants both rounds finished before the campaign ends on 25 October. Senate government leader Teresa Leitão is expected by Wednesday to seek a special calendar shortening the gap between rounds. The rules require five discussion sessions before the first vote, and three were held last week. After a first-round approval, five business days and three more sessions must pass before a second vote.

The case for moving now is the government’s, and its best stated reason is the change itself. O Globo describes the amendment as President Lula’s electoral banner, and the government is pushing a decision before 25 October so the legal week would become five days on and two off.

The case against is flexibility, and it is Flávio Bolsonaro’s. He wants workers to negotiate hours with employers, the model also advanced by campaign coordinator Senator Rogério Marinho, who prefers that the Liberal Party abstain. Party Senate leader Carlos Portinho called the amendment an out-of-control electoral vehicle he said was supposedly driven by Alcolumbre and Lula. Senator Wellington Fagundes said coordination prefers abstention, yet most of the bench wants senators to take a position. Senator Tereza Cristina said other opposition parties should free their members too. They plan to settle that at a lunch on Wednesday. None of these accounts states how the vote will fall.
