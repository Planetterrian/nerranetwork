**Houthis warn passengers against using Saudi airports after deadly Riyadh attack: The Guardian**
In Saudi Arabia, in the Middle East, the Houthis’ first public message since Saturday’s airport strike told travelers and airlines to stay away. Military spokesman Yahya Saree said Mecca and Medina were excepted, but that other Saudi airports and airspace “are exposed to targeting and constitute a theatre of operations,” urged the public to take the warning seriously, and claimed Saudi Arabia bears responsibility for any failed interceptions. UN Secretary General Antonio Guterres said he was “deeply shocked” by the attack, which killed 12 people and wounded over 300, according to aviation authorities, and said attacks on civilians and civilian infrastructure violate international law and must cease immediately. EU chief Ursula von der Leyen called the strike a “dangerous escalation” by Iran’s proxies and said the EU stands in solidarity with Saudi Arabia; German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul called it a terrorist act against Saudi Arabia and civil aviation; UK Defence Minister Wes Streeting called the attacks contemptible. The Houthi foreign ministry said that unless Saudi Arabia stops its aggression and interference in Yemen, worse is yet to come, while the Houthi-run health ministry says 91 Yemenis have been killed and 168 injured in five weeks of Saudi-led attacks, 60 percent of them women and children; President Donald Trump is weighing whether the United States will join Saudi strikes, as Houthis hold Bab al-Mandab islands and Red Sea positions and the Strait of Hormuz remains shut.

**In Kenya, 1.5 million people may be affected by El Niño rains: UA.NEWS**
In Kenya, in East Africa, about 1.5 million people may be affected by forecast above-normal rainfall from October to December, and up to 500,000 residents may be forced to leave their homes. Authorities named Nairobi, Kisumu, Busia and Mandera the four counties at highest risk, and the National Disaster Operations Centre said heavy rain could endanger 18 counties. Director David Samoei said preparations began after the Kenya Meteorological Department warned of above-normal rain, including rescue centres and safe shelters. Officials are working the Nyando River in Kisumu, the Nzoia River and Budalangi in Busia, and the road between Mandera and Wajir.

### Both Sides: Should Italy widen its deficit for defense and energy?
Italy’s government is presenting extra borrowing as defense and energy policy, and bond analysts disagree on the risk. On October 2, Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni’s center-right government approved an extra 28 billion euros in borrowing over two years, split evenly between defense and energy, each about 0.3 percent of GDP a year in 2027 and 2028. The 2027 deficit target is now 3.4 percent of GDP and the 2028 target 3.2 percent, up from April projections of 2.8 percent and 2.5 percent. The budget encompassing those allocations is due next week.

The case for the spending is the government’s decision itself: it approved the money, after scaling the plans back, for defense and energy rather than as an untitled increase. PIMCO said domestic ownership of Italian debt and stronger fundamentals offer support for the bonds, a reason not to treat Rome as the next fiscal break.

The case for caution is Goldman Sachs’. Senior European economist Filippo Taddei said wider deficits and higher yields could push Italy’s debt ratio to Europe’s highest by 2028, and that fiscal risk premia could rise before a general election due no later than December 22, 2027. On Friday, France’s benchmark 10-year yield was 4.85 percent and Italy’s 10-year was 4.55 percent, about 108 basis points over German Bunds, after French yields had surged to multiyear highs.
