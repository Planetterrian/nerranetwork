**Iran says Hormuz to remain closed until US meets conditions: Al Jazeera**
In Iran, in the Middle East, parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Sunday the Strait of Hormuz will not open until seven conditions based on the Islamabad memorandum are met. **Iraq transports its oil past Hormuz for first time in decades as war with Iran chokes exports: The National**
In Iraq, in the Middle East, the state oil tanker company moved its own crude beyond the Strait of Hormuz for the first time in decades as the war with Iran chokes the main export route. Director general Ali Qais, quoted by state media, said the Iraqi Oil Tankers Company shipped two million barrels on a very large crude carrier, shifting some sales from free-on-board loading at Basra to delivery outside the strait. Oil provides at least 90 percent of Iraq's revenue, and most exports — about 3.3 million barrels a day before the war — left Basra and crossed the strait. Oil Minister Bassim Khudair said last month that Iraq has been able to move three million barrels a day since the beginning of September. The National reports the strait has been effectively closed by Iran since the conflict broke out in February, with shipping hit by missile and drone attacks, insurance surges and naval confrontations.

### Both Sides: Should Ukraine get full EU membership or associated status without a vote?
The question is whether Ukraine should receive full European Union membership or the associated status Chancellor Friedrich Merz has offered, and the case on both sides is being set out in Kyiv today. Merz has said he is skeptical that accession is possible in the coming years. His proposal would give Ukraine access to the single market and observer status in the Council, the European Commission and the European Parliament, with no voting rights. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has rejected it and said Ukraine needs full and equal membership.

Merz's best reason is that full entry will not come soon, so an associated status would open the market and the institutions without a vote. He has refused to supply Germany's Taurus cruise missiles, despite calling for that transfer when he was opposition leader. His government has instead given financial and technical help for Ukraine's own long-range weapons.

Zelenskyy's best reason is that Ukraine needs an equal place in the decisions that defend it, not a seat without a vote. He called Merz the leader of a country that consistently protects Ukrainian lives from Russian strikes. He said the two sides are preparing decisions that would give Ukraine and all of Europe greater means to defend themselves, and that Russia must be stopped together.

Both sides accept that Merz is in Kyiv on Sunday and that Germany is Ukraine's largest donor. The next dated step is the agreements the two leaders say they are preparing today. On Friday Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said Russia's leadership shows no real interest in ending the war and intends to continue it.
