In Peshawar, in northwestern Pakistan, the roof of the private Al-Khair school came down on Thursday. Authorities and a hospital official put the dead at thirty-four children and a teacher, with more than fifty other people hurt.

Lady Reading Hospital spokesman Mohammad Asim said those children were between six and twelve, the oldest fourteen, and that some of the wounded were critical. Emergency services spokesperson Bilal Faizi said two classroom roofs fell while second- and third-grade children were inside with their teachers, and that everyone trapped had been pulled out.

Faizi told The Associated Press that nearly one hundred rescuers joined, and that the speed of the evacuation saved at least forty lives. Injured teacher Mahwish Zaman said the school had about twelve hundred students, nursery through fifth grade, and that the collapse felt as if the sky had fallen.

Peshawar police chief Saeed Ahmed said the owner, Mohammad Khan, had been arrested and that the building was structurally weak. The Globe and Mail notes that collapses like this are common in Pakistan, where building rules are often poorly enforced.
