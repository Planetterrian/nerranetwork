### The Ten
**Qatar LNG carrier struck by ‘unidentified objects’ in Gulf: Al Jazeera**
In Qatar, the Qatar Gas Transport Company said its liquefied natural gas carrier Al Mayeda was hit by “unidentified objects” while sailing in the Gulf on Friday evening. The company called it the latest attack on commercial shipping since the US-Israel war on Iran began in February, and it issued the statement on Sunday. The statement, published by the Qatar Stock Exchange, said none of the crew was injured and no environmental damage was reported. It said the vessel is in a safe condition and operational, and that the impact is being assessed. The company supplies Qatari ports and terminals with towage and marine support services. More than 120 commercial ships have been hit since the war began, according to data from the International Maritime Organization and the UK Maritime Trade Operations. Iran, which closed the Strait of Hormuz early in the war, has repeatedly warned that vessels trying to pass without its permission would face “destruction”. Attacks have increased since the United States reimposed a blockade on Iran’s southern ports in July; with at least 29 ships hit, October was the worst month, ahead of the 20 attacks recorded in March.

**Shops looted as German cost of living protests turn violent: BBC World**
In Germany, rioters looted shops in central Frankfurt late on Saturday as clashes over the cost of living also broke out in Berlin, Cologne and Hamburg. Police said they used water cannons to disperse protesters targeting emergency workers with fireworks, stones and bottles. Frankfurt police said “violent riots” hit the city centre, that Molotov cocktails were thrown at emergency services, and that windows of a gun shop and other shops were smashed, with trainers stolen and two weapons seized after a suspect was arrested. Thousands had joined largely peaceful demonstrations earlier, organised by Gen Z regelt against underfunding in schools, the high cost of living, public spending cuts and military service. The BBC reported that more than 320,000 people also demonstrated in France on Saturday, a third consecutive weekend of largely peaceful protests over living costs and poor public services, especially education. Authorities in Frankfurt, Munich and Berlin reported as many as 18,000 protesters, roughly half the organisers’ website estimate, while German media said tens of thousands marched. Spokesperson Lina Friedrich said the demonstrations were “to fight for peace and freedom, rather than for war and coercion”, and that the group wants better health and education support, opposes compulsory military service, and favours a fairer distribution of the burden. Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt told Bild that overnight police had prevented “violence like that seen in Paris” and called for “harsh punishments” for rioters and looters. Hesse interior minister Roman Poseck said “around 100 clearly identified left-wing extremists” infiltrated Frankfurt; Berlin police said 27 officers were injured and up to 56 people were temporarily detained, and Hamburg broadcaster NDR reported 120 arrests.

**Bulgarians vote in presidential run-off amid Black Sea tensions: Al Jazeera**
In Bulgaria, voting is under way in a presidential run-off marked by regional security threats and domestic economic concerns. President Iliana Iotova, 61, led former caretaker prime minister Andrey Gyurov, 50, in the first round but fell short of the 50 percent needed to win. Election officials said the first round drew a record low turnout, with only 23 percent of eligible voters casting a ballot. Exit polls will be published after voting ends at 8pm, 18:00 GMT, on Sunday, with a result expected on Monday. The vote comes after a series of drone attacks on merchant ships off Bulgaria’s Black Sea coast in the past month. About two dozen seafarers are feared dead, and on Wednesday a Greek container ship sank after being hit by sea drones. Prime Minister Rumen Radev, who backs Iotova, said the strikes’ outcome “benefits the Russian Federation”; both leading candidates have condemned the attacks and called for a diplomatic end to the war in Ukraine. A win for Iotova would make her Bulgaria’s first elected female president; she became acting president in January after Radev resigned to enter and win a parliamentary race. Gyurov, backed by pro-Western reformist parties and by figures including former prime minister Boiko Borisov, signed a security cooperation agreement with Kyiv while premier and says he wants Bulgaria to be a reliable NATO ally.

**Pakistan, Saudi Arabia push for early signing of GCC free trade agreement: Profit by Pakistan Today**
In Pakistan, Commerce Minister Jam Kamal Khan and Saudi Arabia’s new ambassador, Dr Rajeh Albugamy, discussed early signing and implementation of a proposed Pakistan-Gulf Cooperation Council free trade agreement. The minister said the proposed agreement could expand market access and create opportunities for Pakistani exporters. He tied the talks to Saudi Vision 2030 and to Pakistan’s industrial capacity, and he pointed to cooperation on production, product quality, technology and private-sector partnerships. Pakistan and the GCC concluded negotiations in September 2023, but the agreement has yet to be formally signed and implemented. Deputy Prime Minister Ishaq Dar said in October that the agreement was nearing completion, with approval potentially pending from one GCC member state. The ambassador reaffirmed Saudi commitment to economic ties and highlighted agriculture and regional connectivity. Both sides agreed to keep coordinating on trade, investment and agricultural cooperation. The proposed deal would cover Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait and Oman. The meeting, described in a statement issued on Saturday, did not itself sign the agreement.

**Tommy Robinson conviction leaves far right with leadership void: The Guardian World**
In Britain, a jury at Southwark crown court found far-right activist Tommy Robinson, whose real name is Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, guilty of harassment causing fear of violence to two journalists. Deliberations took more than 11 hours. The prosecution said he used “bullyboy, intimidatory tactics” against Daily Mail reporters Andrew Young and Jacob Dirnhuber after they reported on his alleged role influencing the 2024 Southend riots. Robinson admitted “paying” for the personal number of Daily Mail editor Ted Verity and left a message threatening to make the paper’s journalists and their families “famous”. He potentially faces up to 10 years in prison and will learn his sentence on 16 December. Anti-migrant activist Daniel Thomas, known as Danny Tommo, whom the Guardian described as the obvious heir apparent, is in custody ahead of a trial over the alleged slashing of a dinghy in the Channel. Joe Mulhall, director of research at Hope Not Hate, said there is “definitely a leadership void in the anti-migrant space”. Restore Britain leader Rupert Lowe, the MP for Great Yarmouth, wrote that the system had treated Robinson unfairly. Robinson thanked Elon Musk for bankrolling his legal expenses and told supporters one day at Southwark court cost £20,000.

### Both Sides: How Germany should answer the cost-of-living protests
Saturday’s marches have left Germany’s government and the organisers arguing over what the state should do next: punish the night’s violence, or move on the spending, schools and military-service demands that brought people out in daylight.

Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt’s case for punishment starts with the harm already done. He told Bild that overnight police had prevented “violence like that seen in Paris” and called for “harsh punishments” for rioters and looters. Shops were looted in Frankfurt, emergency workers were targeted, Berlin police reported 27 officers injured, and Hamburg broadcaster NDR reported 120 arrests. Hesse interior minister Roman Poseck said “around 100 clearly identified left-wing extremists” infiltrated Frankfurt and that France must not be held up as a model. Cologne police chief Johannes Hermanns said extremists who had “nothing but violence in mind” had entered a march by young people who wanted to demonstrate peacefully.

Gen Z regelt spokesperson Lina Friedrich’s case starts with what the daytime crowds asked for. She said the demonstrations were “to fight for peace and freedom, rather than for war and coercion”, and that what matters most is better support for health and education, opposition to compulsory military service, and a fairer distribution of the burden. Thousands joined largely peaceful demonstrations before the clashes. The same day, the BBC reported, more than 320,000 people demonstrated in France over the cost of living and public services, especially education.
