**Microsoft outages extend into 2nd day, company not aware of malicious activity: CBC**
A failure in Microsoft services tied to Exchange Online and email is in a second day. France 24’s tech correspondent Peter O’Brien said the scale and duration make it one of the biggest outages ever. Offices and public agencies that run mail and meetings on those tools are the ones living through the second day.

**Oil supply to exceed global demand for first time since Iran war: Belga**
The International Energy Agency said on Friday that more oil will be available worldwide than is needed to meet demand in the fourth quarter. It is the first time supply has exceeded demand since the war in Iran began in February. Belga reported the agency’s line that the market is showing tentative signs of rebalancing. Gulf exports are now at 74 percent of pre-war levels. Belga reported that Saudi Arabia and Iran have increased production, and that more tankers are passing the Strait of Hormuz even though attacks are still taking place there. The agency said Gulf exports have recovered much faster than global oil demand. Brent crude is trading at 96 dollars a barrel, down from more than 117 dollars at the end of October. Belga tied the drop to higher Middle East exports and hopes of a diplomatic solution. Rigzone, on the same monthly report, said the agency raised its estimate of global oil supplies in 2026 by 920,000 barrels a day. This year’s average deficit is now 685,000 barrels a day, against 1.7 million a day in last month’s projection, and the annual shortfall is 60 percent lower than that earlier report. This quarter shows an overhang of roughly 2 million barrels a day where a deficit of similar size had been projected a month ago. Saudi production reached 10 million barrels a day in October. Iraq’s output hit a seven-year high of 4.6 million a day. Gulf shipments excluding Oman reached 18.4 million barrels a day last month. Observed inventories rose in October for the first time since June, mostly as barrels at sea, while onshore stocks remained at their lowest in a decade. Global demand is forecast to fall by 2.68 million barrels a day this year, to an average of 102.3 million. IEA members will speed the release of remaining emergency stockpiles, discharging a further 71 million barrels through the end of February. Benchmark crude is down about 18 percent this month. The agency said the outlook for gasoil and diesel has marginally improved as high prices reined in consumption, and it expects the surplus emerging this quarter to widen next year.

**The U.S. and Iran are at war. But ICE is still working with Tehran on deportations: Los Angeles Times**
In the United States, newly unsealed documents in a lawsuit show the administration has been communicating with Iran about deporting people back while the two countries are at war. The Los Angeles Times reported that at an October 15 hearing, Judge Ana C. Reyes voiced strong opposition to the Trump administration’s coordination with the Iranian government. She did not grant an injunction to halt information sharing. She will decide in the coming months whether the case may proceed. The government told the court it is “executing lawful orders of removal.” The Department of Homeland Security said ICE “actively coordinates” with countries to secure travel documents and is committed to ensuring that people are informed of their right to communicate with their consular representatives. The suit, filed in July by the Iranian American Legal Defense Fund against Secretary of State Marco Rubio, accuses immigration officials of sharing confidential information about Iranian asylum seekers with their home country and of a coordinated effort to identify Iranians and pressure them to return. Evidence includes a June incident at a California detention facility in which Iranian officials from Pakistan’s embassy in Washington interrogated an Iranian man days after he was detained. Mohammadjamal Azizi, an Iranian refugee in Tujunga who with his wife claimed asylum as Christian converts, said his wife received a message from ICE last month telling her to contact Iranian government officials in Washington. She had been held close to two years and feared deportation. Yasmine Chubin, executive director of an Iranian advocacy group, said attorneys knew of at least a dozen detainees approached about removal in recent weeks. All were asylum seekers, a majority Christian converts who entered through the southern border and had been held 18 months or longer. Chubin said one attempted suicide after hearing he might be returned. Pastor Kevin Kang of Tujunga United Methodist Church, who has housed Azizi and other formerly detained refugees, said applicants are pressed to put detailed information in asylum papers and fear that information reaching the government they fled. The Times noted that the Los Angeles area is home to the largest population of people of Iranian descent outside Iran.

### Both Sides: A former defence site for detained asylum seekers
The question is whether a former Ministry of Defence site near the Oxfordshire village of Piddington should house more than 1,200 detained asylum seekers. Housing and communities secretary Angela Rayner approved full planning permission, a decision that emerged on Thursday. The Home Office welcomed that step because it wants to end the use of asylum hotels. A spokesperson said the government is working on a fairer distribution of asylum accommodation across the country, with over one billion pounds saved in costs since the election. The same statement said former military sites are designed to be self-contained, with security and cameras around the clock, to limit the effect on the local community. Defence minister Luke Pollard told LBC there is a strong sense of feeling around Bicester and especially in Piddington, that the Home Office continues to engage, and that a few more stages remain so the plan is not yet fully locked in and concerns can be mitigated. Parish council chair Tim McNally told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme that villagers will take many actions and that ministers should absolutely expect a legal challenge, which would have to be lodged within the next six weeks. The village, about 350 residents, has already made a symbolic attempt to declare independence from the rest of the country after a local referendum. McNally said today is a day of reflection and that campaigners will apply the Hillsborough law, still being considered by parliament; once passed, he said, it would create a legally enforceable duty of candour compelling public officials to act transparently in investigations and inquiries. Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch called the plans a betrayal of the women of Piddington and emblematic of what she called Labour and Angela Rayner’s disregard for the law and for women and girls’ safety. She said the more than 1,000 male migrants Labour is about to place in Oxfordshire are in Britain illegally and should be deported as soon as possible. The Home Office said a final decision to proceed has not yet been taken.
