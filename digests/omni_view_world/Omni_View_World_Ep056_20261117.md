**Ukraine critically damages Kerch bridge linking occupied Crimea with Russia: The Guardian**
Ukraine says it critically damaged the Kerch bridge, the road and rail link between Russia and Russian-occupied Crimea, in an overnight missile attack that President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called an unprecedented operation and a response to Russian strikes on Ukrainian infrastructure. The BBC reported that Ukraine says the attack took weeks to plan, while the Kremlin vows to rebuild. Zelenskyy wrote that Ukrainian forces had gradually destroyed air defences, radars and electronic-warfare equipment on the way to the bridge, that Palianytsia drones hit the road section, and that Neptune and other missiles hit the rail section; the Guardian said footage showed a Flamingo cruise missile seconds before impact, after a Monday strike on a central arch. Photos described by the Guardian suggest a 227-metre road arch span has fallen into the Kerch Strait, which joins the Sea of Azov and the Black Sea, and that the adjacent railway arch is also damaged. Ukrainian commentators told the Guardian the steel arch cannot easily be replaced, and that traffic can now leave the peninsula only by a land corridor through occupied southern Ukraine, exposed to Ukrainian drones. The Kremlin-appointed governor of Crimea, Sergey Aksyonov, said the bridge is no longer usable, that traffic is suspended, and that residents and visitors should not travel toward it. Russian officials quoted by Reuters insisted the damage was only minor and would be quick to repair; Deputy Prime Minister Marat Khusnullin wrote on Telegram that designers and builders are already gathered and that logistics are being sorted with regional leaders. After an emergency government meeting, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Khusnullin had briefed President Vladimir Putin, and told Izvestia the bridge would be restored and would be even better than before. The bridge was built eight years ago after the 2014 annexation, which Ukraine calls illegal; Ukraine struck it with a truck bomb in 2022 and a sea drone in 2023. The Guardian said debris appears to have blocked the main navigable channel, 120 metres wide and 8.3 metres deep, used by ships from the Sea of Azov and the ports of Berdiansk and Mariupol, and that the Rybar channel, which has close links to the defence ministry, warned that fitting a new arch would be formidable because Ukraine can now strike repair crews. Russian forces struck two major bridges across the Dnipro in Kyiv late on Monday, plus other critical infrastructure, in drone and missile attacks.

**Bahrain thwarts drone attack; Iran claims targeting US radar: Al Jazeera**
Bahrain said on Tuesday that its air defences intercepted a drone attack after Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it had struck a United States radar at the Sheikh Isa base, also referred to as the Isa airbase, in Bahrain, home of the US Navy's Fifth Fleet. Blasts were heard over Manama after a phone alert warned of a potential threat and told people to go to the nearest safe place; the Interior Ministry later sent an all-clear, said precautionary measures remain, and urged caution. The Globe and Mail reported no casualties or damage from Tuesday's interception. The IRGC, in a statement carried by Iran's state broadcaster IRIB, said the attack was part of efforts to counter hostile US activity in the region. Al Jazeera's Resul Serdar, reporting from Tehran, said that since Friday a pattern has emerged of Iran trying to restore deterrence, and that Tehran is tying this strike to what it calls continued violations of Iranian airspace rather than to one specific US attack. On Sunday the IRGC said it launched six drones at a US air-defence radar at Bahrain's Riffa airbase, which the Globe and Mail rendered as Al-Rifaa, after what it called hostile US actions; Bahrain said it had also intercepted several Iranian attacks that day. On Friday the IRGC claimed a hit on a US early-warning radar in Bahrain; the US military denied the facility was hit and said it intercepted two Iranian drones with no damage or casualties. These strikes come in a war that began with US and Israeli attacks on Iran on February 28 and that reports describe as stalled. President Donald Trump has said he might hold off attacking Iran if the United States does not face what he called provocations from Tehran or the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen, while keeping a US naval blockade over Tehran's disruption of shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, which carried about a fifth of global energy supplies before the war. On Tuesday Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi was in Oman, which has mediated; Omani Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi and he discussed safeguarding maritime corridors, the flow of shipping, and reviving talks to prevent escalation, the Omani foreign ministry said. Last week Iran attacked ship navigation systems on the Omani island of Didamar, in the strait. Tuesday's attack followed Bahrain's sentencing of 64 people, mostly Shiite clerics, to life in prison on charges of colluding with Iran to undermine the state, including Sheikh Isa Qassim, described by the Bahrain Institute for Rights and Democracy as the spiritual leader of Bahrain's Shiite community, who has lived in Iran since 2018. Iran's foreign ministry condemned the ruling and called the accusations baseless. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio condemned Iranian strikes in Bahrain in a call on Monday with Bahrain's foreign minister.

**Trump arrives in Philippines for ASEAN Summit, will meet Quad leaders: The Tribune**
President Donald Trump landed in Manila on Tuesday on a 48-hour Asia trip, arriving as the 49th ASEAN summit was already wrapping up, the new step in a visit whose later meeting with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin had already been expected. The New York Times reported he is due to meet President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. and that, after less than 24 hours in Manila, he heads to the APEC summit in Shenzhen; The Tribune reported a state visit and lunch with Marcos on Wednesday, then departure for Shenzhen, with meetings on Thursday with President Xi and President Putin expected to cover Iran and nuclear non-proliferation. Soon after arrival he met leaders of the Quad — the United States, India, Japan and Australia — the first in-person Quad leaders' summit in two years, according to the White House itinerary cited by The Tribune; Prime Minister Narendra Modi also took part in the 23rd ASEAN-India Summit in Manila. The Times reported that Southeast Asian leaders, tariffed even when they are allies, have come to see Washington as an unreliable partner and will look for any sign that the Republican midterm defeat has changed Mr Trump's approach, and that Quad partners are dealing with high energy prices after the Iran war, which the Times said he launched in late February without discussing it with them. In Shenzhen, the heart of China's robotics and artificial-intelligence industries, he needs to extend a soon-expiring trade truce that limited tariffs on Chinese goods in return for a Chinese commitment not to cut off rare earths and magnets. It would be his third meeting with Mr Xi this year, and a three-way meeting with Mr Xi and Mr Putin would be the first of its kind. Before leaving Washington he told reporters they would talk about denuclearization, which he called a tough subject because it is very hard for any of them to say they will get rid of their nuclear weapons; Chinese officials have made clear they are not interested in nuclear talks until their arsenal is roughly the size of the American one. He also said they would talk about Iran a little and that the situation was in very good shape; analysts told the Times that progress on Iran is unlikely, with Russia content to see energy prices elevated since the war began and China eager to see them fall. Thousands of demonstrators gathered in downtown Manila against his visit and against Pax Silica, a US-led effort to unite partners around artificial-intelligence supply chains independent of China; the Times reported that most were students who said their government was more focused on its relationship with the United States than on helping Filipinos. Russian state news agencies, citing the Kremlin spokesman, said Mr Putin could meet Kim Jong-un if the North Korean leader also travels to China for APEC; it was not clear whether Mr Kim would attend. The Tribune reported that during the Philippines stop Mr Trump is also scheduled to sign a trade agreement with Thailand, and that the Philippines' ASEAN chairmanship has stressed maritime security and a code of conduct with China for the South China Sea.

### Both Sides: Should Britain adopt Alan Milburn's youth plan?
Britain is arguing over a review by Alan Milburn on young people outside work, training or education. The Guardian reported that the full report has not formally been published, though it has circulated in Westminster on an embargoed basis since yesterday, so the parties already know its contents. No publication hour beyond that pending release is set out in today's account.

Milburn's case, as the Guardian led it, is that his plan could cut the welfare bill before the next election, while overall savings will take years. The reason offered for moving now is the scale of the problem already on the books. In the opposition responses the Guardian published, one argument said nearly a million young people are out of work or training, more than there are job vacancies in the entire country, and that a target to halve the number of Neets by 2036 needs growth, including what that response called scrapping Labour's jobs tax and joining the EU single market.

The Conservative case against, from Helen Whately, the shadow work and pensions secretary, starts from the young people already on welfare. She said Conservatives agree with Milburn on the problem and want the review to succeed, and will back him where he takes up their proposals, including a shift from cash sickness benefits to support for milder conditions and a cut in national insurance for young people. She said he is right to criticise tax and regulation burdens Labour has put on business, but that the review is too cautious about reversing that damage, too often answers with a large and expensive state, fails to bring down the welfare bill, proposes billions more in spending, and will not change anything for young people stuck on welfare now because nothing will happen for at least three years.

The Green party put the opposite objection. It said its alternative is proper work and training for proper pay, so young people can reach their potential without the threat of poverty and homelessness. Around that line, the published Green response rejected threatening vulnerable people with benefits they need, and unsuitable short-term work, and treated the review as more of a model it says Andy Burnham had promised to end.

What happens next is formal publication of the review. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/politics/live/2026/nov/17/alan-milburn-youth-unempolyment-neets-economy-welfare-brexit-latest-news-updates)
