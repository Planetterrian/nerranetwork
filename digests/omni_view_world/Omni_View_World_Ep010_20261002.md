**Houthis push for Taiz as fighting intensifies around strategic Yemeni city: The National**
In Yemen, in the Middle East, fighting has intensified as the Houthis try to break into Taiz, and Armed Forces spokesman Col Majed Al Nazili said government warplanes carried out 15 air strikes on Houthi positions on the outskirts. He said those strikes killed dozens of Houthi fighters and reinforcements and destroyed the group’s equipment and vehicles; the rebels clashed with government forces in residential neighbourhoods east of the city, about 100 kilometres from Mokha, which the Houthis seized last month. The UN said renewed fighting has displaced more than 133,000 people, mostly from Taiz and Lahj, and envoy Hans Grundberg urged the Houthis to halt an offensive in Yemen’s third-largest city. The Saudi-led coalition said the Houthis hit a power station in Madinah that supplies the Prophet’s Mosque, putting one transformer out while the network stayed up; the Houthis denied that attack. On Friday the coalition said it intercepted and destroyed a ballistic missile fired toward Khamis Mushait in southern Saudi Arabia.

**Egypt and Ethiopia expel diplomats in tit-for-tat diplomatic row: The National**
In Ethiopia and Egypt, in Africa, Ethiopia on Thursday ordered Egyptian diplomat Ahmed Moharam Ahmed Soliman to leave within 48 hours, and Egypt expelled an unnamed Ethiopian embassy counsellor on the same deadline. A day earlier Ethiopia ordered its embassy in Eritrea closed and declared 10 Eritrean diplomats persona non grata, after which Asmara severed diplomatic ties following overnight blasts in Addis Ababa. Addis Ababa accuses Eritrea, Sudan and Egypt of supporting a Tigray People’s Liberation Front alliance that has fought federal troops since last week; the three countries deny it. Egypt says the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam on the Blue Nile, which supplies more than 80 percent of the water reaching Egypt, threatens a country that depends on the Nile for nearly all its freshwater, and years of talks with Sudan have not produced a binding deal on running the dam.

**Kin of Manipur’s 49 people missing since 2023 ethnic conflict to get ₹10 lakh each: The Hindu**
In Manipur, in India, in Asia, the state Cabinet approved a one-time payment of ₹10 lakh for the kin of each of 49 people missing since ethnic conflict began on 3 May 2023. Government spokesperson Sapam Ranjan Singh called it a special humanitarian measure after Thursday’s Cabinet meeting, chaired by Chief Minister Yunman Khemchand Singh. Home Minister Konthoujam Govindas told the Assembly in September that the conflicts, first between Kuki-Zo and Meitei communities and then between Kukis and Nagas, claimed 306 lives and displaced more than 60,000 people. The law presumes a missing person dead only after seven years, so these families lack the death certificates the central assistance scheme requires.
