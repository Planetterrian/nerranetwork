### The Ten
**Children aged 5, 8 and 11 killed in US school shooting before suspect shot by bystander: BBC**
In the United States, Bend police said a 16-year-old boy killed three children and a teacher at Silver Rail Elementary School on Thursday afternoon, then was shot dead by a community member who pursued him into the building and engaged him. Bend Police Chief Mike Krantz said the attacker, a Bend resident who has not been publicly identified, forced entry by shooting at locks on the doors. Police said it is unclear whether he had any link to the school. The children who were killed were five, eight and eleven. A seven-year-old and a 54-year-old teacher were taken to hospital in critical condition. The first alert reached police at 12:59 local time, 19:59 GMT, from a caller who said an unknown person was firing a long gun into a car. Photos released from the scene show a red truck with bullet holes. Minutes later, county dispatch took 911 calls that the attacker had entered the school. Officers arrived three minutes after the bystander shot him, confirmed he was dead, and searched the building. Oregon Governor Tina Kotek praised the bystander who, she said, heroically intervened, and told that person the actions carry a heavy emotional burden and that her thoughts were with them. Steven Cook, the school district’s top administrative official, said the community was stunned and heartbroken, and that no words could adequately express the grief. About 450 children attend Silver Rail, which has more than 40 adult staff, and Bend schools are closed on Friday so the community can have time to heal. The Guardian reported that children were taken by bus to a nearby church used as a reunification centre, and that some parents brought stuffed animals or other comfort items from home. Teacher Kathy Sheil Smith told CNN affiliate KPTV she tried to keep children calm after spotting the gunman approach, that some were praying and some were consoling others, and that she was sure every teacher thought that day might be the day they would die. Bulletin reporter Michael Kohn told the BBC the school was in lockdown, that students reached parents outside on Apple watches, and that parents were stunned in what he called a neighbourhood school in a mountain town. Local media reported the school received classroom doors in 2022 that can be locked with the flip of a switch. The BBC, citing the K-12 School Shooting Database, said there were 235 shooting incidents at American schools last year and at least 140 so far this year. A person under 18 cannot buy a firearm in Oregon. The state allows a juvenile to possess one with a parent’s permission, and only for certain uses such as hunting. Officials would not say what type of weapon was used or how many rounds were fired. Multiple law enforcement sources told CBS News, the BBC reported, that the attacker used a shotgun.

**Poland scrambles fighter jets as Russia strikes western Ukraine: Al Jazeera**
Poland’s armed forces said on Friday they scrambled fighter jets as Russian attacks overnight hit western Ukraine close to the Polish border, and that no violation of Polish airspace was recorded. In the Volyn region, on that border, numerous strikes cut electricity to 370,000 people, and Governor Roman Romaniuk said two people were injured. In the eastern city of Kharkiv, a drone attack killed three people and injured five, the regional governor said. In central Ukraine, police said strikes on an apartment block in Uman injured a similar number. Private power company DTEK said it had implemented emergency power cuts across Kyiv because of ongoing strikes. Russia said it shot down 644 Ukrainian drones overnight, 94 of them headed for Moscow, and officials said air defences had blocked a Ukrainian drone attack against a heating plant in the Vladimir region. Al Jazeera’s Yulia Shapovalova, reporting from Moscow, said the capital’s four airports and St Petersburg’s Pulkovo Airport had suspended operations. A Russian-installed governor said Ukrainian attacks killed two people in the Russian-controlled Luhansk region of eastern Ukraine. Al Jazeera reported that concern along NATO’s eastern flank has grown since the summer, after Romania, Moldova, Poland and the Baltic states reported drone intrusions into their airspace and waters, and that the activity appears to be becoming more bellicose. In recent weeks, drones have struck at least seven cargo ships off the Black Sea coasts of Romania and Bulgaria, leaving 25 people dead or missing. Drones hit two ships off Romania’s coast this week. A hit on a commercial vessel that sank off Bulgaria’s coast on Tuesday prompted Sofia to summon Russia’s ambassador. Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev said on Friday that while the evidence pointed to Russia, his government does not have proof. Shortly afterwards, Defence Minister Dimitar Stoyanov said Bulgaria has formally asked NATO to deploy combat aircraft and antidrone systems. He said letters had gone to the NATO secretary general and the supreme allied commander, and that Sofia is awaiting their decision. NATO did not immediately respond to a request for comment, Reuters reported, as carried by Al Jazeera. Stoyanov said he plans to meet his Romanian and Turkish counterparts soon to discuss a common approach in the Black Sea. Bulgaria, which has proposed a moratorium on attacks on civilian ships in the Black Sea, also expects to join a NATO antidrone initiative by the end of October. Romania’s president has said his country has asked for additional equipment. Al Jazeera reported that Ukraine has had significant success attacking Russia’s energy infrastructure and oil refineries, and that President Donald Trump has blamed Ukraine for pushing up global oil prices.
Ranks: hundreds of thousands in western and eastern Ukraine, plus NATO states on the Black Sea · power lost for 370,000, deaths in Kharkiv and at sea, and a formal Bulgarian request for NATO aircraft · overnight and through this month Source: [aljazeera.com](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/16/poland-scrambles-fighter-jets-as-russia-strikes-western-ukraine?traffic_source=rss)

**Flash floods destroy villages in central Nepal: The Guardian**
In Nepal, flash floods swept through the central Gorkha district after a pre-dawn landslide on 11 October blocked the Budhi Gandaki River and triggered severe downstream flooding. Two villages in the worst-affected Chumanubri rural municipality were reportedly destroyed, with homes, roads and bridges washed away, and part of a hydropower station was damaged, The Guardian reported. After the river rose from 2 metres to nearly 8.5 metres within hours, the national disaster risk reduction and management authority reported that 204 homes were damaged and more than 650 people had been affected. More than 100 foreign trekkers were stranded in the popular hiking region. The Guardian reported that the floods come just two months after catastrophic flooding and landslides devastated parts of the country on the border with Tibet, leaving thousands dead or missing.

**Locals in India’s Odisha allege forgery behind land taken for Adani mine: Al Jazeera**
In India, residents of Balda and neighbouring Bannur, in the tribal-dominated Koraput district of Odisha, allege that forged signatures and thumbprints, including of dead and fictitious people, were used to obtain forest land for a bauxite mine. Al Jazeera reported the project, valued at 24 million dollars, is being developed by Kalinga Alumina Limited, a subsidiary of Adani Enterprises, a conglomerate owned by Gautam Adani, whom the report described as India’s second-richest businessman and close to Prime Minister Narendra Modi. The project will consume a large chunk of tabletop Balda Hill, home to forests, grasslands and caves considered sacred by at least 10 tribal villages around it. Purported signatures and thumb impressions of nearly 600 people were allegedly collected at village council meetings in Balda and Bannur on 9 October last year. Bannur is 5 kilometres from Balda. Shopkeeper Raghunath Khara told Al Jazeera the thumb impression beside his name was not his, that he always signs in English, and that he never attended the Balda meeting. He said he was protesting with his wife, sister and other villagers about 100 metres from the council office. India’s constitution recognises Indigenous rights over traditional lands, and forest laws and tribal-autonomy laws require a village-council resolution, valid only if 50 percent of adults consent in writing, before such land is diverted. Government documents claim all 580 adults — 360 in Balda and 227 in Bannur — agreed to give up the land. Elected village heads in both villages denied that any signatures were collected and called the two meetings invalid. Photographs taken by villagers show a combined attendance of about 100 people, nearly 40 in Balda and 68 in Bannur, far below that quorum. Villagers also allege that documents carry signatures of fictitious persons and of people who died years ago, and that some signatures from an earlier, unrelated council meeting were pasted onto the mine document. Many who did attend said they opposed the mine. Farmer Madhav Kuntili, of Bannur, said they were told that if mining was allowed the village would get roads, jobs, drainage, water connections and a school. Al Jazeera noted that Koraput is one of India’s least developed districts and that similar promises often sway people. Al Jazeera said it emailed the Adani group and its communications chief on 18 August, followed up on 25 September, and also sought comment from district magistrate Manoj Satyawan Mahajan and from Koraput police superintendent Rohit Varma, and received no response.
Ranks: hundreds of Indigenous residents of two Odisha villages · forest land and a sacred hill slated for a 24-million-dollar mine · the project is already being developed Source: [aljazeera.com](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/16/tribals-in-indias-odisha-allege-forgery-behind-lands-taken-for-adani-mine?traffic_source=rss)

### Both Sides: Should Ukraine keep striking Russian oil refineries?
European Union leaders and President Donald Trump are on opposite sides of whether Ukraine should keep striking Russian oil refineries.

Trump’s objection, reported by Reuters and by CBC on Friday, is that the strikes are a disgrace. The Guardian’s U.S. politics live file carried a further description from him: he called President Volodymyr Zelenskyy more difficult to deal with than Putin. Al Jazeera reported he has blamed Ukraine for pushing up global oil prices. That is the harm his criticism is tied to in today’s coverage: a higher oil price, laid at Kyiv’s campaign against Russian refineries. The remark landed as European leaders met to debate funding for Ukraine, with CBC describing Zelenskyy’s relations with the Trump administration as ebbing again.

The other case is that Ukraine still has to be able to protect itself, and that Moscow should face more pressure, not less. Ursula von der Leyen said the European Union intends to increase pressure on Russia and promised a 22nd round of sanctions. Emmanuel Macron said it was crucial that Ukraine receive all the means necessary to protect itself. CBC reported the summit rallied around Zelenskyy while leaders debated funding. The Guardian framed those statements against growing doubts about long-term American commitment to Ukraine.

The next step officials named is the sanctions round von der Leyen promised, beside the funding debate already under way at the summit. Neither side, in the articles, set a date for a decision on the refinery strikes themselves.

### Progress Watch
Doctors treating Pope Leo XIV said his lung had recovered well after Tuesday’s surgery, that other post-operative tests were in the norm, and that recovery was proceeding as expected, The Globe and Mail reported. The 71-year-old American pope had the lower lobe of his right lung removed on Tuesday after doctors identified a suspected cancerous nodule. The surgery confirmed adenocarcinoma, the most common type of lung cancer. On Thursday he resumed working from his hospital room, met his chief of staff, and continued respiratory physiotherapy. The Vatican on Friday stopped morning updates that had only confirmed he was awake and had eaten, because that recovery had progressed so normally. Doctors are still awaiting tests on the cancer’s precise molecular and genetic makeup, whether it has spread beyond the removed lobe, and whether further treatment is necessary. The Vatican also released photos of get-well notes from patients in Gemelli’s pediatric oncology ward. Seven-year-old Angelo wrote that he had been on that floor for 36 days and that he and his mother pray for the pope every night. A university student, Giorgia, wrote in English that she and the pope were in some ways going through the same thing, and asked him to pray for her too.
