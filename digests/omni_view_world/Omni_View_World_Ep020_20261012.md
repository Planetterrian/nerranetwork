**EU to limit Chinese hybrid vehicle imports to 350,000 a year: The Guardian**
In the European Union, in Europe, the European Commission confirmed it will limit hybrid electric vehicles imported from China to 350,000 a year from December, a quota it expects will halve China’s market share from 6 percent to 3 percent. About 800,000 battery and plug-in hybrids are likely to be sold this year, and Chinese customs data show hybrid exports to the EU tripled to 6.7 billion euros in the first half, from 2.2 billion a year earlier. Cars above the quota would be held in customs rather than tariffed; vehicles assembled in the EU, including in Hungary and later Spain, are exempt, while EU companies building in China are not. Commission officials said China fully supports the quota and has agreed to drop some tariffs on auto parts and to cut duties by half on EU footwear, olive oil and ice cream. The curb comes as the EU runs a goods deficit with China of about 1 billion pounds a day.

**Commission selects 46 new Strategic Projects to bolster the EU’s supply of critical raw materials: The European Sting**
In the European Union, in Europe, the European Commission said it selected 46 new Strategic Projects in 16 member states, from 102 applications, to diversify critical raw materials and depend less on any single supplier. Under the Critical Raw Materials Act, 2030 benchmarks call for EU capacity to meet at least 10 percent of annual consumption in extraction, 40 percent in processing and 25 percent in recycling. The list has eight extraction projects, 11 processing, 19 recycling and eight integrated projects, covering 15 of 17 strategic raw materials, including lithium, nickel, cobalt and rare earths. With 47 EU projects and 13 third-country projects chosen last year, the Commission said the set should move the bloc toward those targets.

### Both Sides: A short ceasefire or only a full peace deal?
Ukraine’s president and the Kremlin disagree on whether the war should pause briefly or only under a full settlement.

Volodymyr Zelenskyy proposed a 60-day total ceasefire so Ukraine can hold a presidential election. His stated need is a window long enough for a vote, not a one-day lull. In Warsaw he said that if Russia does not agree to an energy ceasefire this winter, Ukraine will respond. He has accused Russia of violating the energy ceasefire Donald Trump announced. Kyiv has also said Russia broke temporary truces in the past, which is the reason a defined ceasefire, rather than an informal pause, is the offer.

Dmitry Peskov gives Moscow’s case. He said President Vladimir Putin and other officials have repeatedly said Russia does not want a ceasefire, because it wants long-term peace and is prepared to do everything possible to achieve it. The Kremlin rejected the 60-day idea on the ground that it wants an overall peace deal instead. The limited step Peskov described is an order not to launch strikes on election day only, and only if Ukraine holds the vote. On Trump’s energy ceasefire he would not be drawn, saying contacts continue and Moscow will speak when it has something to say.

Next is those contacts, not a signed truce. The same day, the EU agreed listings against almost 1,600 individuals and entities in Russia’s military-industrial complex, and NATO’s commander said he does not judge an attack on alliance territory imminent or inevitable.
