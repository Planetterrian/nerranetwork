**Almost 1,000 arrested as clashes follow another day of mass protests across France: BBC**
France saw clashes late Saturday after another day of protests over the cost of living and poor school conditions. The interior ministry estimated 410,000 people marched; the unions put the number at about 800,000. Police fired tear gas and water cannon in cities including Paris after largely peaceful demonstrations, barricades were set alight in Marseille, and people ripped boards from a shop in Bordeaux. Some 70,000 officers were deployed after President Emmanuel Macron decreed earlier this week that only demonstrations organised by recognised trade unions would be permitted. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said, in remarks reported by AFP, that several thousand radical activists gathered at the rear of the Paris march, including around 500 people identified as rioters. The Interior Ministry said 142 people were arrested in the capital, less than half the number on the same day last week, after last weekend’s unrest became some of the worst disorder in modern French history. Nuñez said two people were seriously injured, one in Toulouse and one in Clermont-Ferrand; Franceinfo reported that a 20-year-old protester in Toulouse had several fingers torn off when a nearby explosive went off. Authorities in Bouches-du-Rhône said seven businesses had been looted or damaged. A Paris protester who gave her name as Zelie told the BBC people were frustrated and had been suffering since Macron came to office.

**Serbia votes in election pitting longtime leader against student movement: Al Jazeera**
Serbia began voting Sunday in snap parliamentary elections called by Aleksandar Vucic in September. The vote follows almost two years of student-led demonstrations after 16 people died when a train-station canopy collapsed in Novi Sad in 2024, a disaster widely blamed on government corruption. Vucic, prime minister and then president for more than a decade, resigned the presidency in September to run again for prime minister. Al Jazeera describes a country of six million; France 24 puts the electorate at 6.4 million, choosing among 18 parties for a 250-seat parliament. Vucic has promised higher pensions and public-sector salaries, investment in state health care and subsidies for young couples, and has kept ties with China and with Russia, Serbia’s main gas supplier, while leaving the door open to the European Union. The Student List, led by cardiologist Ilija Srdanovic, fields 250 largely unfamiliar candidates and says its priority is rooting out corruption and restoring the rule of law. Vucic and his Serbian Progressive Party deny repression, corruption and spying, and he claims the students are funded by foreign agents in Croatia and Montenegro, a charge the students deny. About 150,000 student-movement supporters rallied in Belgrade on Thursday. Polls close at 8 p.m. local time, with first indications expected about two hours later; France 24 says the state election commission is not expected to release the official tally before Monday. Vucic has said his side would concede if it lost, and has also warned that protests alleging fraud if his party wins would be quashed.

**British Columbia Conservatives win a majority and David Eby resigns: The Globe and Mail**
Canada’s British Columbia has elected a Conservative majority for the first time in nearly a century. With 95 percent of polls reporting, The Globe and Mail said 64 Conservatives, 27 New Democrats and two Greens were elected. David Eby resigned as NDP leader, accepting responsibility for an election he called two years early, and said the result ends nine years of NDP rule, four of them under him. He told supporters the party would serve as official opposition and cited recruiting family doctors, helping to lower rents and raising the minimum wage. Interim Conservative leader Lorne Doerkson, in his victory speech, pledged to boost the economy, cut red tape, freeze all provincial taxes, triple natural gas production over nine years and hold an international investment summit in Vancouver, with health care fully and publicly funded. He said repealing the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act would be among his top priorities; Indigenous leaders have opposed any changes to that law. He also told First Nations the future would be built as partners, and told the crowd he believed in private property rights and parks open to all British Columbians. Green leader Emily Lowan was elected in Saanich North and the Islands, and Jeremy Valeriote was re-elected in West Vancouver-Sea to Sky. The province faces a continuing trade war with the United States, Doerkson’s backdrop as he takes office.

**Bulgaria holds presidential elections amid divisions over Ukraine war: DW**
Bulgaria is voting Sunday for a president, a largely ceremonial post that can still appoint interim governments, command the armed forces and veto legislation. Incumbent Iliana Iotova, who took office this year after Rumen Radev left the presidency and became prime minister, favors dialogue with Moscow to bring peace to Ukraine and opposes military aid to Kyiv. Her main challenger, former caretaker prime minister Andrey Gyurov, is backed by the pro-Western opposition, criticizes Radev’s foreign policy and supports Bulgaria’s Euro-Atlantic ties as a European Union and NATO member. Kostadin Kostadinov of the pro-Russian Vazrazhdane party is also running; DW says 27 candidate pairs are on the ballot and nearly half are openly pro-Kremlin. The war is a central theme after four merchant ships were allegedly hit by drones off the Bulgarian coast this month, and after Starlink antennas were set on fire near Sofia. Radev has said the attacks seemed to advantage Russia and summoned the Russian ambassador; his government has declined to blame the incidents directly on Moscow. Rising energy prices, inflation and corruption are also issues in the EU’s poorest member state. Polls are open from 7 a.m. to 8 p.m., official results can take up to 48 hours, and a runoff is scheduled for November 1 if no candidate wins an absolute majority.
Ranks: Bulgarian voters and Ukraine-war policy in an EU and NATO state · who holds the presidency and its veto · Sunday’s vote, with a possible November 1 runoff Source: [dw.com](https://www.dw.com/en/bulgaria-holds-presidential-elections-amid-divisions-over-ukraine-war/a-79753496?maca=en-rss-en-world-4025-rdf)

**Storm Boelo: warnings in place in Scotland, Northern Ireland and north of England as high winds batter UK: The Guardian**
The United Kingdom took a second named storm in two days as Storm Boelo followed Friday’s Storm Austen. The Met Office said winds in the amber zone, in force from 9 p.m. Saturday, reached 60 to 70 mph, with gusts above 80 mph on exposed coasts, including 82 mph on Tiree in the Inner Hebrides at about 4 a.m. Sunday. Chief forecaster Jason Kelly said the strongest winds and greatest disruption risk were in the north, and warned of danger to life from flying debris and crashing waves; people were told to stay indoors and secure loose items. Gusts of 50 to 60 mph were possible elsewhere, with heavy rain adding a hazard in the north and risks to travel and power. Liverpool’s River of Light and London’s Diwali on the Square were cancelled, Edinburgh Castle is closed Sunday, and Fountains Abbey in North Yorkshire closed for the day. Flooding blocked the railway between Preston and Blackpool North, trees came down near Dumfries, and hundreds of flights were delayed or cancelled at Dublin, Heathrow, Manchester, Edinburgh and Glasgow. By 7 a.m. Sunday the Environment Agency had 66 flood alerts and 10 flood warnings in England; Cumbria police reported flooding and fallen trees. Hundreds of homes in south-west England were still without power after Austen, which brought 76 mph winds at St Mary’s airport in the Isles of Scilly.

### Both Sides: Who is responsible for an AI-assisted strike on a school?
Vox reports that on the first day of the United States war against Iran, a strike on an elementary school killed 123 children. The strikes were ultimately approved by humans and facilitated by the Maven Smart System, Palantir software that aggregates battlefield data. Operators fed Maven intelligence that classified the school as an Iranian military facility; the site had been converted into an elementary school in 2017, and that intelligence did not reflect the change. Some reporters have called it one of the worst massacres by the US government since the Vietnam War. The open question is who bears responsibility when humans and a machine are both in the chain.

Some US Central Command personnel, according to Pentagon officials who spoke to Bloomberg, expected Maven to flag stale information or inconsistencies in the target intelligence. Their case is that the system should have caught the bad classification before the school was marked a priority target for day one.

A Palantir spokesperson told Bloomberg the company is not responsible for the underlying data or for identifying intelligence deficiencies, and denied that the evidence showed its software was at fault. Stanford law lecturer Allen Weiner, a former State Department lawyer, says one legal theory puts war-crimes responsibility on manufacturers because of the code, while another says that does not hold, especially if the military has said the tool may not make substantial autonomous judgments. No dated next step is set out in the article; the dispute as reported is between those Pentagon officials’ expectation and Palantir’s denial. Source: [vox.com](https://www.vox.com/future-perfect/507570/ai-weapons-war-crime-responsibility)
