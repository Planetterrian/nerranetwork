**Macron secures diesel deals on Gulf visit as fuel prices stoke unrest in France: France 24**
In France, President Emmanuel Macron has secured the delivery of an additional 200,000 tonnes of diesel a month from the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain, the French presidency said on Tuesday. The Elysee Palace said he obtained commitments for around 100,000 tonnes a month from the Emirates, while an additional 100,000 tonnes a month will be secured during a stop in Bahrain later in the day. With deliveries from Saudi Arabia added in, France has commitments for 295,000 tonnes a month, representing just over 10 percent of its consumption, the presidency said. Macron is on a tour of the UAE, Bahrain and Qatar, key energy suppliers, to secure oil and diesel and bring prices down in France after a weekend of violent protests sparked by soaring living costs. France 24 reported that those three Gulf states have been thrown into turmoil since February by the US-Iran war. Crude oil prices have ballooned during the eight-month conflict as Iran blockades the Strait of Hormuz, the main exit route for the Gulf’s energy exports. The G7 countries, chaired this year by France, agreed in early October to draw up to 100 million barrels of oil from their strategic reserves to ease pressure. Kuwait has also given what the French presidency called very clear commitments to deliver diesel and kerosene to the French overseas territories of Mayotte and Réunion in the Indian Ocean. Those shipments will be sent via Oman, bypassing the Strait of Hormuz, the presidency said. Talks are underway with countries in the region to bring down maritime insurance prices so companies can ship greater quantities through the strait at lower cost, the palace said. The presidency said France is strengthening its energy security with these long-term commitments from its partners. France 24 issued the report on Tuesday morning, timed at 11:44 and modified at 11:45.

**Billionaire Mukesh Ambani launches India’s biggest-ever IPO: The Guardian**
In India, Mukesh Ambani, described by The Guardian as the country’s second-richest man, is launching what could be India’s biggest-ever initial public offering, despite a stock-market downturn. Mumbai-based Jio Platforms, a telecoms company backed by Meta Platforms and Google, is hoping to raise up to 302 billion rupees, or 2.4 billion pounds, and is targeting a 10.3 trillion rupee valuation when it floats from Wednesday. The Guardian’s headline figure for that valuation is 81 billion pounds. Set up a decade ago as a subsidiary of Ambani’s conglomerate Reliance Industries, and led by Akash Ambani, Mukesh’s oldest son, Jio offers low-cost broadband to millions of Indians. Jio secured 212 anchor investors on Monday before the sale. The Guardian named BlackRock, Singapore’s sovereign wealth fund, the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority, Fidelity Investments, Schroders, JP Morgan Asset Management, Franklin Templeton, Invesco and Prudential among them. On Wednesday, shares will be offered to retail investors, and the IPO is set to close on Friday. Reliance Industries said in a stock-exchange filing last week that Jio had set a price range of 1,065 rupees to 1,119 rupees a share for its maiden share sale, offering up to 270 million new shares. The family-owned conglomerate has been encouraging its more than 400,000 employees to buy shares, offering them a discount of 54 rupees a share. At the top of the price range, Jio would be worth 10.3 trillion rupees, which The Guardian said would make it India’s fourth-largest company, behind parent Reliance Industries, Bharti Airtel and HDFC Bank. That would be more than twice the 4.9 trillion rupee valuation in 2020, when Meta and Google invested. The Guardian said the top valuation is far below initial expectations of more than 110 billion pounds when the telecoms group posted a draft prospectus in the summer. It comes amid a broader slowdown in India, with the blue-chip Nifty 50 index down more than 12 percent this year. Jio launched its telecoms services in September 2016. According to Reliance Industries, it gained 16 million subscribers in the first month and topped 100 million within 170 days, and is credited with making mobile internet more affordable by triggering a price war. The company intends to expand further into artificial intelligence, cloud computing and digital infrastructure. Isha Ambani, a director on Jio’s board, told investors last week that the idea was spawned by the poor connectivity she and her brother Akash faced when they returned to India on a break from studying in the United States in about 2009-10. She said she struggled to submit a college assignment online because the internet was not working, and that what followed was a conversation about why India should be left behind. She said Akash firmly believed the answer was creating a digital technology platform company.

**Millions unable to use Revolut app or cards as company suffers ‘service disruption’: The Guardian**
In Britain, Revolut customers have been unable to access their money after the banking app went down, affecting card payments, cash withdrawals and transfers, The Guardian reported. The UK banking company said its app was down because of a service disruption affecting several services, but insisted that customers’ funds and data were safe. Revolut said its services were partially unavailable, with problems affecting sign-up, sign-in, card payments, transfers, ATM withdrawals and cryptocurrency services. Users reported error alerts when they tried to check their accounts. When opening the app, they received a “something went wrong” message, instructing them to try again later, and leaving them unable to access their funds. In a statement, Revolut said it was experiencing a service disruption affecting multiple services, confirmed that all customers’ money and data are safe, and said it had been working to fix the issue since it was identified this morning. It apologised to anyone affected and for any inconvenience caused. Revolut is not the only company experiencing problems. The Guardian reported the outage could be linked to issues with Google’s cloud services. The two companies have a strategic partnership under which Revolut uses Google Cloud’s scalable infrastructure to operate its services. Several fintech companies have been affected after Google reported that multiple Google Cloud services are experiencing elevated errors and intermittent network failures in the europe-west2 region, adding that it did not have a workaround.

**Alstom secures €800 mln train order for Hanoi urban rail projects: Theinvestor**
In Vietnam, French rail manufacturer Alstom has secured a first-tranche order worth approximately 800 million euros for the supply of Metropolis trains for urban rail projects in Hanoi. In its announcement on Monday, Alstom said the trains are part of the agreement announced on October 6 with Vietnamese conglomerate Vingroup. The trains will be designed and manufactured at Alstom’s Valenciennes Petite-Forêt site in France. The order will be recorded in the third quarter of Alstom’s 2026/2027 fiscal year. Under the deal, Alstom will design and manufacture up to 83 Metropolis trains at that site. Vingroup, listed in Ho Chi Minh City as VIC, through its subsidiary Vinhomes, listed as VHM, will assemble up to 117 additional trains in Vietnam using components supplied by Alstom. The deal also includes a technology licence to support the local assembly of those additional trains. Alstom said the five-car trains will feature new-generation designs aimed at improving operational efficiency and passenger safety. Under Hanoi’s master plan with a 100-year vision, the capital aims to develop a 979-kilometre urban rail network comprising 18 lines by 2045. The network is expected to become the backbone of public transport in the capital. Authorities also see urban rail as a way to ease traffic congestion, reduce pollution and improve residents’ quality of life. Alstom has operated in Vietnam for more than 30 years and has supplied railway signalling, telecommunications and metro systems in the country. It has provided signalling and telecommunications modernisation for the first and second phases of the Hanoi-Vinh regional railway line, in Nghe An province, and has supplied signalling and telecommunications systems for Ninh Binh station. For Hanoi Metro Line No. 3, Alstom supplied and integrated the metro system, including 10 four-car Metropolis trains designed and manufactured in France, plus the Urbalis 400 signalling system, power-supply equipment and depot systems. Theinvestor timed the announcement at 4:31 pm GMT+7 on Tuesday.

**South Korea to pull more embassy staff from Ukraine after POW row: Al Jazeera**
In Ukraine, South Korea’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs says it will withdraw additional embassy personnel following what it called Kyiv’s lack of a constructive response in a dispute about North Korean prisoners of war. Ministry spokesperson Park Doo-soon said on Tuesday that Seoul had told Kyiv of its position via multiple channels but had not received a sufficient response from Ukraine. Earlier this month, South Korea recalled its ambassador from Kyiv after President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told the United Nations General Assembly that Ukraine had transferred two captured North Korean soldiers to the South. The disclosure angered Seoul, which said the operation should have remained confidential to protect the soldiers’ families in North Korea. Park told a regular press briefing that the further measure was taken because Seoul judged it necessary to facilitate an amicable resolution and restore trust between the two countries. He did not say how many staff members would leave. A chargé d’affaires would stay on in Kyiv to maintain diplomatic communication, along with all consular staff to protect South Korean nationals, Park added. Persistent threats of air strikes across Ukraine had also made it difficult to ensure staff safety, he said. Seoul previously summoned the Ukrainian ambassador and threatened unspecified further measures. Ukraine has insisted that diplomacy must prevail over emotions in relations between the countries, and also said Kyiv considered the situation resolved. South Korea’s Foreign Minister Cho Hyun said earlier this month that Ukraine had sent a message that included words of apology over Zelenskyy’s disclosure, but that it fell short of the level Seoul had in mind. Under South Korea’s constitution, all North Koreans are considered citizens of the Republic of Korea, as the country is officially known, and those who reach its territory and seek to defect are generally allowed to stay.

**Albertans reject referendum on independence from Canada: Al Jazeera**
In Canada, around 76 percent of voters in Alberta rejected a proposal to hold a referendum on independence, according to unofficial results Al Jazeera reported, while more than 300,000 backed the proposal and separatists say their grievances remain. The Guardian, citing unofficial results from Elections Alberta with more than 1.5 million votes counted, said 76 percent supported remaining in Canada and close to 24 percent backed beginning a process toward separation. Prime Minister Mark Carney, who grew up in Edmonton, said Albertans made a clear choice, chose unity, and chose Canada. Premier Danielle Smith posted that Albertans had voted for a stronger Alberta in a united Canada. Separatist leader Keith Wilson said at least a quarter of Albertans feel Alberta needs to be treated better. The ballot asked whether to remain in Canada or begin a legal process that could lead to a second vote, and was one of 10 questions; Elections Alberta said the remaining results could take days to count. Delays at roughly 100 of about 1,400 polling stations pushed results back by more than two hours. Elections Alberta data indicated roughly 43 percent of eligible voters, or 1.3 million Albertans, had already cast ballots before Monday through advance voting and mail-in ballots.
