**Ukraine drone attack kills five in Russian-occupied Zaporizhia: Al Jazeera**
In Ukraine, a drone attack killed at least five people and wounded three in Vesioloye, in Russian-occupied Zaporizhia, Moscow-installed governor Yevgeny Balitsky wrote on Telegram on Thursday. He said the strike hit a parking lot. Russian forces hit Kyiv with ballistic missiles late on Wednesday. Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the missiles struck a shopping centre and two other buildings on the east side of the Dnipro, and warnings to remain in shelters were still in place. Al Jazeera’s Audrey MacAlpine, reporting from Kyiv, said residents heard loud impact sounds and that those impacts injured at least seven people. She said people in frequently hit districts go to shelters even before sirens, and that Russia has heightened attacks on energy facilities as winter arrives. Russia’s Defence Ministry said, in an account carried by Interfax, that its forces carried out a massive overnight strike on military-industrial facilities in Kyiv and struck a dry cargo vessel southeast of Odesa, which the ministry alleged was carrying military equipment and dual-use goods. The General Staff of the Armed Forces of Ukraine said Ukrainian forces had earlier hit a major Russian military base east of Moscow and damaged five aircraft used to launch missiles and bombs. Russian authorities reported repelling drone attacks in the Tuymazinsky district of Bashkortostan and in Nevinnomyssk, in Stavropol. Al Jazeera’s Yulia Shapovalova, reporting from Moscow, said Russia is pressing Kyiv’s military infrastructure, straining air defences and supply chains, and hitting power plants ahead of cold weather to wear down morale and inflict economic damage. MacAlpine said Ukraine’s energy capacity is below 40 percent of pre-war levels, with combined heating and power plants in Kyiv and the region repeatedly offline. Russia began its full-scale invasion in February 2022 and has claimed annexation of Zaporizhia, which it partially controls, since September 2022. Fighting has continued despite a renewed U.S.-backed push to end the war. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, answering Donald Trump’s description of Vladimir Putin as “my friend,” told CNN, “For us, he is a killer.”

**Trump, NATO chief caution Russia as attacks on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure intensify: CNBC**
In the United States, President Donald Trump and NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte urged Russia, in a joint statement on Thursday, not to take any actions that would further expand the war in Ukraine. They met on Wednesday at Mar-a-Lago in Palm Beach, Florida, called the meeting very impactful, and said Russia’s nearly five-year war and the Iran war were among the topics. Their statement said they discussed the importance of Russia avoiding actions that intentionally or unintentionally expand the war, and of obtaining a peaceful resolution as rapidly as possible. The Florida meeting followed Trump’s talks with Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping in Asia, which raised concern among European lawmakers about Washington’s commitment to transatlantic security. At that meeting Trump described Putin as his friend. Rutte, a consensus builder during nearly 14 years as Dutch prime minister, has often used flattery with the U.S. president; Trump has called him a great leader and NATO’s biggest asset, while some have questioned whether that approach has delivered tangible benefits. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz on Wednesday accused the Kremlin of escalating threats toward European security, citing the Baltic region, Poland and the Black Sea, and said Russia knows what it wants and wants more than Ukraine. In Kryvyi Rih, Russian attacks disrupted power supplies and stopped municipal electric public transport, according to Oleksandr Vilkul, head of the city’s defense council. Ukrainian officials said on Wednesday that strikes on the power sector damaged infrastructure and caused outages in five regions as cold weather set in. A spokesperson for Russia’s Foreign Ministry did not immediately respond to a CNBC request for comment on Thursday morning, and the Kremlin has repeatedly denied seeking military confrontation with NATO countries.

**Musk's plan to send a million SpaceX data satellites into space approved: BBC**
In the United States, the Federal Communications Commission has approved a SpaceX plan to launch a million satellites to process artificial-intelligence data in orbit, the first licence of its kind. The project, Starmind, would use sunlight to power the satellites and send results back to Earth. The commission said the project would support America’s booming super intelligence economy. Chairman Brendan Carr said it opens a bold new frontier for the computing infrastructure needed to power America’s leadership and growth, and that it can unite strengths in space and advanced technology, reinforce economic leadership, and expand prosperity at home. Elon Musk wrote on X: “Much appreciated. This will be a great asset to America and the world.” SpaceX says orbital data centres are the most cost- and energy-efficient way to meet demand for computing power beyond terrestrial capabilities. Opposition to data centres on Earth has grown, including in the United States, where experts say they were a factor in large Republican losses in the midterm elections. Amazon criticised the plan as a lofty ambition rather than a real plan. The American Astronomical Society said it was disheartened and dismayed, and that the constellation would significantly undermine decades of federal investment in astronomical facilities and the public’s ability to study the night sky, because the satellites would be larger, brighter, more numerous, and visible as extremely bright objects for longer. Under the licence, deployment must begin no later than November 2033, with 10 percent deployed by November 2035 and 50 percent by November 2038. SpaceX has said it wants to launch its first Starmind satellite next year. The satellites would operate in low-Earth orbit between 500 and 2,000 kilometres, communicate with Starlink’s more than 10,000 satellites, and relay data to Earth. Professor Sir Martin Sweeting, founder and executive chairman of Surrey Satellite Technology, told BBC Radio 4’s Today programme that the idea seemed technically feasible, but that whether it makes sense at scale still needs analysis, and that laser links from the ground are not yet perfected for the data volumes expected. It is unclear whether a million satellites will fly, or whether SpaceX sought room for more than it will use.

**Ships in Baltic and Mediterranean targeted by Ukraine and Russia: DW**
Ukraine has struck Russian ships far from its own coast, including the cargo vessel Sparta II off Finland and the corvette Merkury and a dry cargo vessel, allegedly carrying weapons, off Libya. The strikes came shortly after several suspected Russian missiles crashed in Poland near an international military exercise. Intelligence agencies have long warned that Russia was preparing some form of provocation along NATO’s eastern flank. DW reports that Moscow has already pressed countries that support Ukraine with military provocation, hybrid operations and sabotage, while Ukraine has targeted Russian military and economic sites beyond its borders. Since the summer, attacks by Russia and Ukraine have increased in the Black Sea, hitting Russian and Ukrainian cargo ships and vessels from other countries, particularly Turkey. Ukraine has also used aerial and maritime drones against military airfields, oil refineries including one in Omsk, and vessels in the eastern Black Sea. On the Baltic, Russia has only a narrow coast flanked by Finland and Estonia, and uses it to supply Kaliningrad and to export oil with its shadow fleet. Even before Sparta II, Ukraine had hit oil terminals near St. Petersburg, at Ust-Luga and Primorsk. The corvette, part of the Black Sea Fleet, could not return through Turkey’s blockade of the Bosporus and the Dardanelles, and had been escorting cargo ships in the Mediterranean; DW described that corvette as sunk. The attacks, DW writes, send a political message that Russian ships can no longer feel safe anywhere. Elisabeth Braw of the Atlantic Council told DW that Sparta II was not a random shadow vessel simply moving oil above the price cap, that it was suspected of carrying military equipment, which Moscow denies, and that Ukraine is showing it knows where such ships are and can hit them in whatever waters they are in. Kyiv’s stated goal is to disrupt Russian logistics and supply routes. Military experts and politicians also see a signal of strength from President Volodymyr Zelenskyy ahead of possible peace talks. Ukrainian special forces are also said to have struck Russian military facilities in Syria before the fall of the Assad regime in December 2024, and are reported to have supported Tuareg fighters in Mali against the Malian army and Russian mercenaries.

**Seven injured in explosion at oil facility in Thailand’s Bangkok: Al Jazeera**
In Thailand, an explosion and fire at Bangchak Corporation’s Phra Khanong refinery in eastern Bangkok injured at least seven people, four of them seriously, Bangkok’s Erawan Emergency Medical Centre said. An earlier video report said no casualties had been reported, and that the blast could be felt for several kilometres, with authorities already urging nearby residents to evacuate. The company said it shut down all production units, contained the incident, and brought the situation under control. The Bangna District Office, on Facebook, urged people nearby to evacuate immediately and said the cause was under investigation. Videos showed flames high above the site and thick black smoke across the skyline. Tony Cheng, reporting for Al Jazeera from Chiang Mai, said the explosion was heard at about 3 p.m. local time, in an industrial area near warehouses and Bangkok’s main port, while highways and major routes remained open. He said the site serves Bangkok and a large part of central Thailand, and that most oil imports for this part of the country go there, so the damage is likely considerable even if its full extent is not yet known. A citywide phone alert said the fire was under control and urged people to avoid the area. Bangchak describes the site as a modern complex refinery with a maximum capacity of 120,000 barrels per day. Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul said he was monitoring the situation, that it appeared under control, and that the fire did not appear to be in the main production area, without further detail. An explosion at the refinery in July 2012, then owned by Bangchak Petroleum, triggered a large fire and no reported injuries, and raised questions about a large facility in what is now a residential area.

### Progress Watch
After fears of a lead plume, the U.S. Army detonated nearly five tonnes of degraded lead azide at an ammunition plant in Iowa and called the controlled blast a huge success. Smoke rose shortly after 9 a.m. local time on Wednesday, and the Army said the risk was as close to zero as is absolutely humanly possible. Plans to detonate the compound, stored about 20 years and made more sensitive by recrystallisation, had been paused several times after public outcry and legal challenges. No level of lead exposure is considered safe, and children and pregnant women are particularly vulnerable. Environmental Protection Agency Administrator Lee Zeldin said air monitors were detecting zero airborne lead. He wrote that the agency would stay radically transparent as laboratory results arrive over the next 24 to 48 hours, that the goal was to finish all sampling by Thursday, and that crews would remain for any remediation if needed. More than 1,000 post-detonation samples had already come from homes, parks, farms and drinking-water taps, with churches, schools and playgrounds due next. The Army said FEMA had issued an all-clear, and that surface-sampling results would be released once complete. President Donald Trump said reports showed mitigation efforts far exceeded expectations. Middletown Mayor Rodney Johnson, watching from eight miles away and by camera at a home one mile from the site, said he felt relieved and neither felt nor saw anything. Sandy Mabeus, also eight miles out, closed windows, brought a cat inside, watched three live streams, and neither felt nor heard the blast. Farmer David Vandenberg, about 20 miles away, heard a rumble near nine o’clock after the Army Corps of Engineers sampled his yard and pond on Tuesday, and said he was happy the blast looked relatively contained. Illinois Governor JB Pritzker said his state was seeing little to no impact and kept agencies on standby.
