**Russian attack on bus kills at least 30 people near Ukraine frontline: The Guardian**
In Ukraine, in eastern Europe, a Russian strike on a bus in Kramatorsk killed at least 30 people on Thursday, the Donetsk regional prosecutor's office said, and wounded 18. The strike followed Wednesday's attack on a five-storey block in Pryluky, in the Chernihiv region, where at least 22 people were killed, including five children aged between one and eight, after the building collapsed. Zelenskyy urged Western allies to supply anti-ballistic weapons, aircraft ammunition and interceptors, and said talks with partners would take place today and tomorrow; Kramatorsk and Sloviansk are the last two big cities in northern Donetsk under full Ukrainian control.

### Both Sides: Should Britain keep a working mission in East Jerusalem?
Britain and Israel are settling, in talks already held, whether a British diplomatic post in East Jerusalem continues after Israel moved against the Consulate General.

The UK government's case, in the Foreign Secretary's statement, is that Israel has no legal right to close a consulate that has been there for nearly 200 years. London's strongest reason is its long-standing view that East Jerusalem is occupied territory, and that an independent UK Mission, Jerusalem — the name the building takes from today — can keep delivering services and working with Palestinians in support of a two-state solution. Britain said it stands by measures taken on 8 September with other countries for peace and security for Israelis and Palestinians, and that it will not take reciprocal action while discussions remain underway and credible.

Israel's stated position, in the Guardian's account of the row, is that the British consulate has ceased to function, with 20 diplomats due to leave. That is the concrete claim on the Israeli side in today's reporting: the post, as a working consulate, is already finished.

Both accounts agree that British staff are leaving and that the two governments have discussed the building. London says the withdrawal of a number of staff and families is temporary, made because without an understanding it lacked security assurances, and that the building itself will not close. What comes next is the UK's stated plan to build that presence back up in the coming months, if the talks stay credible.
