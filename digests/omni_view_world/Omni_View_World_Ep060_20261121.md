> **Two Russian missiles and a drone fell in Poland, testing NATO's line on collective defence.**

**What You Need to Know:** Two Russian missiles and a drone fell on Polish territory during Russia's overnight attack on Ukraine, and Warsaw is treating the hits as a deliberate test of the alliance. Poland's defence minister says the weapons came down in the southeast on November 20, with no immediate injuries, while the prime minister calls it an intentional provocation. This edition also draws on the Omni View Omni View North America desk.

### The Ten
**Russian missiles hit Poland: What happened, and could NATO respond?: Al Jazeera**

In Poland, a NATO member on Ukraine's western border, the government says Russian weapons fell on its territory during the night of November 20, while Russia was bombarding Ukraine and European troops were drilling in the southeast. Poland's Defence Minister, Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz, told a news conference in Warsaw that two missiles and a jet-powered drone came down in the country's southeast. He placed the missiles at Nowy Lipowiec and Rudnik nad Sanem, and the drone at Molodycz. Al Jazeera reports that security cameras captured the moment a suspected Russian missile struck Polish territory during that attack on Ukraine. The government says Poland may have been hit by two missiles and a jet-powered drone. No immediate injuries or damage were reported.

The geography is what turns debris into a political fact. One missile is believed to have crashed about 35 kilometres, or 22 miles, from where European troops were exercising. A Kh-101 cruise missile went down about 50 kilometres, or 31 miles, from the Ukrainian border. What officials believe was a jet-powered drone hit about 30 kilometres, or 20 miles, from that border. The incidents came as the United States pushes to revive talks on ending the war in Ukraine, and they have raised the question of whether the strike was deliberate. Al Jazeera's account notes that Warsaw accuses Moscow of intentional provocation, while the bar for triggering NATO's collective-defence clause remains high.

That same night, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia fired more than 400 drones and at least 110 missiles, including 27 ballistic missiles, some with cluster warheads. He said at least eight civilians were killed and more than two dozen were wounded. Al Jazeera describes the barrage as one of Russia's heaviest bombardments of Ukraine. The Polish impacts are therefore not a separate skirmish in the reporting. They are objects that left the attack on Ukraine and came down inside a treaty ally, close to a border and close to foreign troops who were already in the field.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk said it appeared "we are dealing with an intentional provocation" by Russia. He said the incident was likely timed to coincide with the European military exercises in Poland. Those exercises are described as unprecedented. The two-week drill began on November 9 in southeastern Poland. It is preparing forces for a possible post-war mission in Ukraine, to help stabilise the country and rebuild its military. Eleven countries are holding the exercises. One impact is believed to have landed about 35 kilometres from that training ground, so the drill and the debris share the same southeastern corner of the country.

Scott Lucas, professor of international politics at the Clinton Institute, University College Dublin, told Al Jazeera the incident "fits into a pattern going back for months" of Russia "testing how far they can go." He said that a few months ago the pattern was drones flown into airspace, and that a couple of times fighter jets were flown across the border into the Baltic states. "You just test how NATO responds," he said. This time, he said, Moscow had "gone a bit further." "You lob a couple of missiles across and a drone" near where 11 countries are holding exercises. His reading is a sequence, not a finding of intent by a court: drones, then jets, then missiles and a drone beside a multinational drill.

The Polish Air Force had already launched preventive air patrols on September 17, aimed at securing the eastern part of Polish airspace after repeated incursions in the previous weeks. That date sits two months before the November 20 impacts and three weeks before the November 9 start of the southeastern exercise. The week of the hits therefore has a longer runway in the same reporting: patrols in September, a two-week drill from November 9 aimed at a possible post-war mission, an American push to revive talks, and then weapons on the ground on November 20.

Harry Nedelcu, partner and senior director for geopolitics at Rasmussen Global, told Al Jazeera the testing has not been limited to Poland. "During the war, there's been repeated instances of Russia testing NATO allies – Poland, Romania and also non-NATO Moldova as well, which is experiencing – especially over the last few months – increasing numbers of drones and missiles flying over its territory and airspace," he said. He tied the incursions to a core Russian war aim: preventing Ukraine from being part of NATO. He said the full-scale invasion has lasted almost five years, and that Moscow's goal is to undermine the alliance. "What Russia is doing with these missiles, drones, that sometimes find their way into NATO territory, tests NATO just under the radar of Article 5 to see how far can they push things. How far can they take hybrid warfare?" he said. Article 5 is the collective-defence clause. Al Jazeera's framing is that the bar for triggering it remains high even as Warsaw accuses Moscow of intentional provocation.

What changes, and for whom, follows from those distances and from that framing. People in three southeastern Polish localities — Nowy Lipowiec, Rudnik nad Sanem, and Molodycz — now have a recorded impact, even with no immediate injuries or damage reported. The troops of 11 countries, already in a drill that began on November 9 and pointed at a possible post-war mission in Ukraine, were within about 35 kilometres of one impact. Ukrainians absorbed, on Zelenskyy's numbers, a night of more than 400 drones and at least 110 missiles, among them 27 ballistic missiles, some with cluster warheads, with at least eight civilians killed and more than two dozen wounded. NATO governments are left with a Polish prime minister calling the episode an intentional provocation, a defence minister naming two missiles and a drone, and analysts describing a months-long test kept, in Nedelcu's words, just under Article 5. The United States, in the same reporting, is pushing to revive talks on ending the war. A strike that Polish leaders call deliberate, landing while those talks are being pushed and while European forces rehearse a mission inside Ukraine, is the decision point the day actually contains: not a declared alliance war, and not a confirmed Russian admission in this account, but a named allied government's account of weapons on its soil and an open question, which Al Jazeera puts in the headline, of whether NATO responds.

The distinction the reporting keeps is between a claim and a finding. Kosiniak-Kamysz stated the impacts as fact at a Warsaw news conference: two missiles, one jet-powered drone, three named places, the night of November 20. Tusk's sentence is a judgement of purpose: an intentional provocation, likely timed to the exercises. Lucas and Nedelcu supply the pattern they say the night fits — months of drones and jets, tests of Poland, Romania, and Moldova, a war aim of keeping Ukraine out of NATO. Cameras, Al Jazeera says, captured a suspected Russian missile striking Polish territory. Officials believe one object was a Kh-101 and another a jet-powered drone. Those labels are official belief and ministerial statement, not a published forensic report in the articles. No immediate injuries or damage were reported, which is why the consequence is political and military rather than a casualty count inside Poland. The people it changes something for, and soon, are the residents of those three places, the Ukrainian civilians under that night's barrage, the soldiers of 11 countries whose drill sat about 35 kilometres from one impact, and the governments that must decide whether a high bar for Article 5 still holds after a prime minister has called the night deliberate.

### Both Sides: Should the EU cut Russian travel after Denmark's allegation?
Denmark is asking European Union governments to answer a plot it says Russian intelligence planned and started, and that Danish security services say they stopped. The person named by Danish media is the rapper Kyivstoner, whose real name is Albert Vasiliev, a Ukrainian-American. Denmark's security and intelligence agency, PET, said it stopped individuals who were intending to kill him at a concert in September. Three individuals were stopped in a car near the venue on 25 September. Firearms and knives were found in the vehicle. Two people have been charged with attempted murder and with assisting a foreign intelligence service, and both pleaded not guilty. PET said the individuals were associated with a banned Danish gang, Loyal to Familia, which the agency describes as known for extensive, serious and violent criminal activity. PET worked with police on the case. Denmark's foreign minister said Russia's defence attaché will now be expelled and must leave within seven days.

The case for tighter travel rules is Denmark's, joined by the European Commission president. Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen called the alleged attempt Russian intimidation meant to make Denmark waver in its support for Ukraine. In a message to EU governments, confirmed by two diplomats and seen by Reuters, Denmark urged "concrete actions" demonstrating European strength against Moscow, including further restrictions on Russian nationals' access to the Schengen zone, a halt to tourist visas for Russian citizens, and a Schengen-wide policy of not recognising Russian non-biometric passports. Rasmussen said Copenhagen was already very reluctant to issue visas, but Russians can still obtain a Schengen visa in another country and travel to Denmark. He wants such visas to apply only in the issuing country. Denmark would present the proposals to EU ambassadors in Brussels on Monday. France 24 reports that Danish authorities have ordered the expulsion of a Russian diplomat and announced tighter restrictions on visas for Russian tourists. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said on Saturday she strongly condemns an assassination attempt against a US-Ukrainian citizen in Denmark, orchestrated, she said, by Russian intelligence services. "This was an attack on EU soil. And it is unacceptable," she said, adding that the EU stands in full solidarity with Denmark and with all member states targeted by Russian hybrid attacks. She said Russia's hybrid threats are becoming less and less hybrid, and more and more real, and that the EU will soon propose a new European Security Strategy to reinforce preparedness and deterrence.

The case against is the Russian embassy's in Copenhagen, and Moscow's stated intention to answer the expulsion. The embassy said on Friday the accusations were not backed with proof. It called the allegations unsubstantiated and said Copenhagen failed to provide evidence of Moscow's involvement. It described a "Danish model" in which a "Russian trace" is discovered and "exploited for a propaganda spectacle," and accused Denmark of provocations meant to frighten the Danish population and justify a prolonged confrontation with Russia. Russian state media cite the foreign ministry in Moscow as saying it will respond to the expulsion of its official "accordingly." Separately, Russia's FSB security service accused Vasiliev and the Ukrainian authorities in July of organising a "terrorist attack" on military facilities and personnel in Russia. The rapper denied that allegation on social media. The two people charged pleaded not guilty. Those pleas are not findings. What happens next is dated: Denmark takes the visa and passport proposals to EU ambassadors on Monday, the defence attaché has seven days to leave, and von der Leyen says a new European Security Strategy will be proposed soon.

### Progress Watch
Ukrainian forces have liberated 14 settlements and reclaimed nearly 200 square kilometres, about 77 square miles, in the Kharkiv region in a counteroffensive codenamed Bear Trap, according to BBC News Ukraine on November 20, as reported by UNITED24 Media. The operation disrupted a Russian offensive, codenamed Bear Corner, that began in late June 2026 and aimed at encircling Ukrainian troops near Prykolotne and capturing about 500 square kilometres. BBC News Ukraine said Russian troops had established positions near Vilkhuvatka and threatened Ukrainian supply routes, and that Ukrainian commanders deployed additional forces to the sector in September. The counteroffensive began on September 20. Rather than hitting Russian positions head-on, Ukrainian troops used armoured assaults to draw reinforcements toward Vilkhuvatka, then struck the concentrated forces with 18 Fire Point fixed-wing attack drones, advanced from two flanks, and by October 10 had reportedly completed the encirclement. The 475th Separate Assault Regiment CODE 9.2 was a key assault unit, with the multidomain centre Phalanx coordinating planning. CODE 9.2 released footage of M1 Abrams and T-80 tanks, Bradley infantry fighting vehicles, attack drones, and assault teams near Vilkhuvatka. Its figures of about 911 Russian personnel losses and nearly 400 pieces of equipment damaged or destroyed have not been independently verified. Ukrainian forces also used drones to mine Russian logistics routes up to 30 kilometres deep, on a mesh network that worked without Starlink, and used StarGate heavy-lift drones for seven evacuations of wounded soldiers over several weeks. Lieutenant Colonel Oleksandr Nastenko, callsign Flint, commander of CODE 9.2, told BBC News Ukraine the operation remains ongoing, with troops still clearing settlements near the Russian border. The Ukrainian military says the counteroffensive has stabilised the sector, despite Russian Chief of the General Staff Valery Gerasimov reporting to Vladimir Putin on October 16 that Moscow's encirclement operation had been completed. In October, Ukraine's 3rd Army Corps completed Operation Vivaldi in the Donetsk region, reporting the recapture of 16 settlements and 277 square kilometres.
