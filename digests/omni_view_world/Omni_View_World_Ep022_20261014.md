**Ukrainian soldier to go on trial over sabotage of Nord Stream gas pipelines: The Guardian**
In Germany, a Ukrainian former officer went on trial in Hamburg on Wednesday over the 26 September 2022 blasts that ruptured the Nord Stream gas pipelines under the Baltic Sea. The Guardian names him as special forces officer Serhii Kuznetsov; German privacy rules in The Globe and Mail's account identify him as Serhii K. Prosecutors accuse him of helping plan a clandestine attack on civilian infrastructure, and say the alleged conduct would amount to a war crime. The Guardian says the indictment alleges at least four devices of up to 27 kilograms, at about 80 metres near Bornholm, and six accomplices including divers, an explosives expert and a captain, using a rented yacht and timers. He denies any role, saluted as he entered court, and Ukraine's government and President Volodymyr Zelensky have denied ordering the attack. The Globe and Mail reports prosecutors allege he worked with Ukrainian military members for unidentified state entities, an awkward point because Germany is Ukraine's largest European supplier of military aid. Russia, which denies blowing up its own lines, said on Monday it expected a just verdict naming who carried out the blasts and who ordered them. Germany once took half its natural-gas imports through Nord Stream 1; a twin line, Nord Stream 2, was built, but Berlin cancelled approval to open it just before Russia's February 2022 invasion. A second suspect, Volodymyr Zhuravlyov, also identified as Vladimir Z., was arrested in Croatia and is fighting extradition; the Hamburg trial is expected to run into February.

**Saudi Arabia overturns death sentence for man over Facebook comment: BBC**
In Saudi Arabia, the Supreme Court has set aside the death sentence of Anojan Sivarasa, a 27-year-old Sri Lankan migrant worker, over a Facebook comment, Colombo's Deputy Foreign Minister Arun Hemachandra said. A Saudi appellate court had sentenced him to death in September after convicting him of blasphemy against Islam and the Prophet Muhammad. Hemachandra said on Wednesday that the top court set that sentence aside, and he thanked the authorities and legal teams of both countries. The comment came in an online argument in late June after footage spread of a young Muslim man kicking a dog near Hindu pilgrims; Sivarasa replied in Tamil with an insult to the Prophet, then deleted it and posted an apology video. A screenshot reached his employer and Saudi officials, and he was arrested in July. The Al-Ahsa Criminal Court had first sentenced him to five years and a fine of three million Saudi riyals. Sri Lankan lawmaker Faizer Mustafa told the BBC the death sentence was overturned and that lawyers are reviewing the latest ruling; it is not clear whether further proceedings or detention will follow. His family said they have had no direct contact with him since 28 September. Joey Shea of Human Rights Watch said on Tuesday that Saudi authorities should stop handing extraordinary sentences for speech, especially to migrant workers.

**Dangote refinery extends IPO: Plant has reshaped Nigeria's fuel market: France 24**
In Nigeria, the Dangote refinery has extended its share sale to 27 October after a two-week extension, France 24 reported, describing a huge popular response. The same report says the Middle East war and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz have pushed global crude prices up and produced supply shortages. African and European buyers have turned in large numbers to the plant, France 24 said. Nigeria, which the report calls the leading African producer of crude, is trying to shift from that role toward a regional hub for refining and petrochemicals. The extension means the offer, already past its first close, now runs through 27 October. Chinwe Ossundu reported the story for France 24.

**Pope diagnosed with lung cancer but had successful surgery, Vatican says: BBC World**
In Italy, the Vatican said Pope Leo XIV, 71, has lung adenocarcinoma after surgeons removed the lower lobe of his right lung on Tuesday at Rome's Gemelli Hospital. He had told St Peter's Square on Sunday that doctors found a nodule, caught early and well contained, and advised removal — the step that became a roughly 90-minute operation the Vatican called successful and free of complications. He woke normally, spent a peaceful night, and is expected to remain about a week; audiences are cleared until 20 October. Cardinal Kikuchi Tarcisio Isao said the pope, whom he saw on Monday, wanted surgery quickly so a planned early-November trip to Latin America, including Peru, would not slip. A Vatican source told AFP that further tests are checking whether the cancer has spread. Secretary of State Cardinal Parolin called the news a bolt from the blue and pointed to the pope's serenity. France 24 noted he was elected only in May 2025, and that the diagnosis shocked Catholics who had seen him as relatively young and energetic.

### Both Sides: Should the Saudi-led coalition keep striking Houthi fuel sites?
Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthis are trading strikes on fuel sites and cities, and neither side has announced a stop. The Saudi cabinet, chaired by Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, vowed that the Houthis would bear the consequences of attacks on the kingdom, including a missile strike on Riyadh's airport on Saturday that the cabinet said killed 13 people. Coalition spokesman Maj Gen Turki al-Maliki said the coalition is giving air support to Yemeni pro-government forces on the Red Sea coast and other fronts. The case for hitting the Yemen Petroleum Company's al-Sabaha depot near Sanaa, and facilities at Ras Isa, is that those sites sit with an Iran-backed movement firing missiles and drones at Saudi airports, cities and Aramco. The coalition issued a rare civilian evacuation warning before what it called a special operation at al-Sabaha.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Sarea said the group then targeted Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran, an Aramco refinery in Riyadh and King Khalid International Airport, and that strikes on Saudi airports and oil facilities would continue in the coming days. The Houthi-run health ministry said two civilians were killed and two injured at Ras Isa. The Houthi-run Yemen Petroleum Company said al-Sabaha does not contain any stock of petroleum products. Their case against the bombing is that it hits civilians and fuel infrastructure in territory they hold, and that they are answering deadly coalition strikes.

The Saudi energy ministry said a later Houthi hit caused a limited fire and no injuries in an administrative building at Aramco's Dhahran headquarters. International Monetary Fund director Kristalina Georgieva said the escalation could have potentially negative spillovers for everybody, everywhere. The Houthis say the attacks continue in the coming days; the Saudi cabinet says the group will bear the consequences.
