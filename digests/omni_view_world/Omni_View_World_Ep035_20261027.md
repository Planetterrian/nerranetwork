**Israeli election puts Netanyahu’s political survival on the line: The Globe and Mail**
In Israel, voters cast ballots Tuesday in the first national election since the Hamas-led attacks of October 7, 2023, a vote many see as a referendum on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and one widely described as the most consequential in the country's history. The Israeli Central Election Committee said 7.3 million Israelis are eligible, about 330,000 of them for the first time, and the Central Elections Committee said turnout was 16.7 percent by 10 a.m., three hours after polls opened at 7 a.m., higher than in recent general elections. Polls close at 10 p.m., and The New York Times reported an immediate verdict on the next prime minister is unlikely, because opinion polls suggest Mr. Netanyahu's allies and rivals are roughly tied and assembling a coalition could take weeks or months. Mr. Netanyahu, 77, seeks re-election at the head of a coalition of nationalists and ultra-Orthodox parties that the Times called the most far-right government in Israeli history; a fractious opposition is loosely led by Gadi Eisenkot, a former military chief of staff whose son was killed in the Gaza war. The Times reported that Mr. Netanyahu's coalition offers a more religious Israel with a tighter grip on the West Bank and Gaza, while opponents cast the vote as a last chance to preserve democracy and have pledged a reckoning over October 7 and a crackdown on Israeli extremist violence against Palestinians in the occupied West Bank. President Trump has endorsed Mr. Netanyahu, the Times reported, saying anyone else would be the "wrong leader"; The Globe and Mail reported that Mr. Netanyahu said Sunday Mr. Trump was prepared to act again to remove what he called the Iranian threat only if Mr. Netanyahu remained in power, while Mr. Eisenkot accused him of a campaign of fear-mongering, lies and threats and said he was confident Mr. Trump would work with any leader the people chose. Mr. Netanyahu has resisted an independent commission on the October 7 attacks and has vowed to continue overhauling the judiciary, a move critics say could undermine liberal democracy, the Times reported. Palestinians in the West Bank and Gaza cannot vote, and the Times reported that Palestinians in the West Bank are under an Israeli military lockdown as Israelis vote. The Israel Democracy Institute's Viterbi Family Center found 13 percent of Israeli voters still undecided, and The Globe and Mail reported that if neither side forms a government in the 120-seat parliament, Mr. Netanyahu could remain caretaker until another election next year.

**Finnish and Norwegian leaders hold rare ‘frank and substantive’ call with Putin on Ukraine: The Guardian**
In Europe, Finland's president, Alexander Stubb, and Norway's prime minister, Jonas Gahr Støre, spoke by phone with Russia's president, Vladimir Putin, about the war in Ukraine. Stubb's office said the call was a follow-up to US-Ukrainian talks that Støre hosted in Oslo last week, and that the aim was to convey messages directly to Mr. Putin on future negotiations to help end the war. The office said the call was related to meetings expected in Abu Dhabi in early November among Russia, Ukraine and the United States. The statement said Finland and Norway border Russia, are firm supporters of Ukraine, and did not speak on behalf of Europe as a whole. It said the two countries highlighted a shared interest in stability in the Baltic Sea region and the High North. After the call, Mr. Stubb called the discussions "frank and substantive" and said ending the war requires direct discussions with Russia. Mr. Støre said the war carries enormous human and material costs that will affect generations, and that they stressed the importance of de-escalation. Agence France-Presse reported that Mr. Stubb and Mr. Støre were then briefing Ukraine's president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, on the talks.
Ranks: people living through the Ukraine war, plus Finland and Norway as neighbours · a direct channel into talks, not a ceasefire · Abu Dhabi meetings expected in early November Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/world/live/2026/oct/27/russia-ukraine-oleshky-serbia-france-europe-latest-news-updates)

**Moscow gas summit draws Iran and Qatar leaders for Putin talks: Minute Mirror**
In Russia, President Vladimir Putin opened a diplomatic week in Moscow that includes the eighth summit of the Gas Exporting Countries Forum and separate talks with the leaders of Iran and Qatar. The Kremlin said both meetings sit alongside the forum and that no agreements have emerged yet. Iran's president addresses the summit on Tuesday; Tehran said his talks with Mr. Putin will centre on economic ties and regional and global issues, after Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov's visit to Tehran on October 19. Iran's acting oil minister, Hamid Bovard, listed Tehran's priorities as talks on developing gas fields, swapping gas, shielding energy facilities in wartime and aligning export policies, which Minute Mirror said are negotiating priorities, not finished deals. Aides said Mr. Putin will also hold bilateral meetings with the leaders of Mozambique and Libya, with Turkmenistan and South Africa as guests, and will speak on supply stability against a projection that global gas demand will grow 28 percent by 2055, with a "Moscow Declaration" expected. On October 28 and 29, Moscow hosts the third Russia-Africa Summit, with 48 countries taking part, 22 at the highest level, and 24 bilateral meetings for Mr. Putin, including Egypt's Abdel Fattah el-Sisi, Ethiopia's Abiy Ahmed and Congo's Denis Sassou Nguesso. Voice of Emirates reported that trade between Russia and Africa reached 27 billion dollars in 2025, and that 33 intergovernmental agreements are set to be signed.

**AI-driven disinformation running rampant on social media before US midterms, experts warn: The Guardian**
In the United States, experts told The Guardian that social media feeds are awash with AI-driven disinformation ahead of November's midterm elections, and that many posts are likely slipping through the cracks. The Guardian reported that companies including Meta have scaled back efforts to protect voters from election-meddling lies, after layoffs in trust and safety teams that followed a backlash over speech restrictions. Former Facebook engineer Frances Haugen said an information operation that was impossible at this quality in 2016 can now be run "super, super cheap," and that small tweaks can get posts past moderation. In Texas, a false claim that Democratic Senate candidate James Talarico was in a mental health facility in Ohio spread while he was home with the flu; his campaign released security-camera video of him at home, and he said he had never been to Ohio. The Digital Democracy Institute of the Americas tracked the lie as it mutated in conservative Spanish-language media into a false claim that he had attempted suicide. DDIA's Roberta Braga said platforms enforce terms of service more in English than in Spanish, and that a shift toward AI moderation and community notes means more is likely slipping through. Meta and X did not respond to requests for comment; YouTube said all users are subject to the same guidelines. Mr. Talarico's Republican opponent, Ken Paxton, earlier this year launched an advert using AI-generated footage of Mr. Talarico supposedly rewriting the Bible.

**RCMP probe potential fraud, money laundering in Alberta health care procurement controversy: The Globe and Mail**
In Canada, the RCMP is investigating fraud, breach of trust and money laundering connected to Alberta's health-care procurement controversy, according to a letter filed in court. The letter, dated October 15 and signed by Ontario Crown prosecutor Tom Andreopoulos, is the first public account of the potential offences police are examining. It says his office is providing advisory support and would prosecute any charges from the investigation into procurement at Alberta Health and Alberta Health Services. The Mounties confirmed in early 2025 that they had opened an investigation and earlier this year executed searches. Former Alberta Health Services chief executive Athana Mentzelopoulos sued the government and the health authority in February 2025 for wrongful dismissal, alleging pressure from government officials to drop an internal investigation into contracting and ties between private businesses and senior officials. The government says it fired her because she failed to execute its vision for the health system. None of the allegations have been tested in court. Justice Michael J. Lema ruled last month that most disputed records were privileged or confidential and ordered her to return or destroy them; Mr. Andreopoulos wrote that the Crown will ask the Alberta Court of King's Bench how to handle privilege review of seized materials.
Ranks: patients and staff in Alberta's health system, and officials under investigation · a criminal probe is now described in court, with no charges tested · the privilege fight continues in Alberta court Source: [theglobeandmail.com](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-rcmp-probe-potential-fraud-money-laundering-alberta-health-controversy/)

### Both Sides: Should Britain reopen free movement with the EU?
What is being decided is whether the United Kingdom will treat a return to free movement as an option in a long-term relationship with the European Union. Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds said British officials have begun discussions with the EU about that long-term relationship, which could include free movement, while the near-term focus is a November summit meant to ease some trading restrictions and reach a youth mobility deal. He said Prime Minister Andy Burnham wants as little uncertainty as possible and will set out options and trade-offs after the summit. Mr. Reynolds said no assumptions have been made and no decision would be made without voters having a say. "The British people are the boss on this," he said. Mr. Burnham, in a Sunday Times interview, called for an honest discussion and said staying out of free movement would no longer be a red line. He said rejoining the single market and accepting free movement could be done without another referendum if Labour put it in its manifesto, while fully rejoining the EU would need another in-out poll.

Nigel Farage, leader of Reform UK, said discussing freedom of movement would be "very, very poor indeed." His reason is mandate: he said that five months ago Mr. Burnham told leave-voting Makerfield he would not reopen these arguments, and that two and a half years ago Labour won a large majority saying it would not re-enter the single market or the customs union or open a debate on free movement of people. Labour MP Melanie Onn, whose seat is in the north, said the scars of the post-referendum debate have not healed and that this is an unnecessary conversation now. The next dated step Mr. Reynolds named is the November summit; he said Mr. Burnham will set out the longer-term options after it.
