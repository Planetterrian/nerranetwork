**Italy: At least 300 schools evacuated after email bomb alerts: France 24**
Italy evacuated at least 300 schools on Wednesday after they received the same bomb-alert message, Education Minister Giuseppe Valditara told La7, adding that the number could rise. A spokesperson for Rome police said Italian police forces are investigating. One school in Rome told parents that, following a nationwide bomb alert, all students had been taken to a safe area and remained under the supervision of teaching staff. A government source with knowledge of the matter said the email was signed "Mikhail Andropov, leader of the Vilayat of Misoginistan." The Guardian, citing Rai and Sky TG24, said that name was used in several hoax bomb alarms against European flights last week. France 24 said the alert came amid rising concern in Europe about so-called hybrid warfare threats that have targeted supermarkets, blood banks, air traffic and television broadcasters. Valditara's count is the figure he gave on air, with his own warning that it may still climb.

**El Salvador's Bukele makes a political gamble with a Haiti gang offensive ahead of an election: WSLS**
El Salvador has sent 2,500 soldiers to Haiti since mid-October, after four Salvadoran aviation technicians working with a U.N.-backed mission went missing in a gang attack, the Associated Press reported. President Nayib Bukele has filled his social media with videos of helicopter bombardment, cheering civilians and armored cars, and a message in Haitian Creole, English and Spanish: "We will help you take back your country ... Finally, Haiti will be free." On Tuesday he posted that Salvadoran forces had bombed a gang leader's house after he had asked for the coordinates on X, writing, "We kept our promise," and, "Most of them ran away without even trying to fight. What cowards!" A gang leader said the four technicians are dead, and the president's stated aim has shifted from rescuing them to eradicating Haiti's gangs. The deployment has come with little transparency or oversight, the AP reported, and the videos echo the cinematic clips his government uses at home around a crackdown that has fueled accusations of mass human rights abuses. Communications strategist Roberto Dubon called it an electoral campaign that portrays Bukele as a superhero and uses Haiti as a testing ground. Security consultant Luis Enrique Amaya said Bukele has to build a narrative of success in Haiti ahead of February elections, after a legislature controlled by his party moved the vote from 2029 to 2027 and eliminated presidential term limits. Polling puts approval for the president well over 90 percent. Dubon and Amaya said the offensive could isolate allies such as the Trump administration and dent support at home.

**Carney weighs end to Western Arctic offshore drilling ban ahead of 2028 expiry: The Hub**
Canada's prime minister, Mark Carney, is weighing whether to end a decade-old ban on offshore oil and gas exploration in the Western Arctic before it expires in 2028. The fall budget sets aside $11 million over four years for governance and oversight of Arctic offshore petroleum, with $1 million for oversight in 2027-28, rising to $3 million the year after. Finance Canada spokesperson Marie-France Faucher told the Globe and Mail the funds would be used in part for "consideration of potentially lifting the current moratorium on Arctic offshore oil and gas exploration in the Western Arctic." Carney told reporters in Montreal that the money is capacity funding for Inuvialuit partners under a 2023 co-management agreement and "absolutely not, in any way, shape or form, a decision about lifting the moratorium." He said any change would have to be made with northern partners, the Inuvialuit in particular. Northern and Arctic Affairs Minister Rebecca Chartrand said the funding "does not authorize exploration or drilling." N.W.T. Deputy Premier Caroline Wawzonek said lifting the ban would not necessarily mean immediate development, but would signal that Ottawa is prepared to honour northern self-governance over resources. Nunavut Premier John Main said the 2016 ban was imposed with little consultation and has driven away investors. Critics counter that a spill in icy waters could not be cleaned up and that the business case for Arctic drilling is doubtful. Then-prime minister Justin Trudeau announced the moratorium in December 2016 with then-U.S. president Barack Obama; Ottawa prohibited offshore oil and gas activity outright in 2019.
Ranks: northern communities and Arctic waters · a possible end to a drilling ban, which Carney says is not decided · before a 2028 expiry Source: [thehub.ca](https://thehub.ca/2026/11/11/carney-weighs-end-to-western-arctic-offshore-drilling-ban-ahead-of-2028-expiry/)

**Pope Leo to end Argentina tour with large outdoor mass before 'emotional' Peru homecoming: France 24**

Martin Cassinelli of the Atlantic Council said a reflection on that past could be at odds with her government, and Jose Luis Guadalupe Pérez of Peru's Pacific University said Leo's knowledge of the country means he is bound to step on some toes. A 14-meter statue of Leo stands in a seaside park in Callao, and the wider tour runs through November 17.

### Both Sides: Must new electricity be clean?
Negotiators from nearly 200 countries at COP31 in Antalya are seeking a plan to raise electricity's share of global energy use, and host Turkey wants that goal at the centre of a final deal. The Turkish proposal targets a rise to 35 percent of total global energy consumption by 2035, from a little over a fifth now, in theory cutting greenhouse gases from cars, factories and buildings. It does not include language requiring new power to be generated by clean sources, and governments have yet to clarify a broader plan to move away from coal, oil and gas.

Turkey's case is that a voluntary electrification push can still shrink emissions from transport, buildings and industry, and that putting a number on electricity's share gives the talks something concrete while wars, inflation and an oil-price shock compete for attention. Critics, including those cited by The Globe and Mail, say a target that does not require clean generation may do little to curb warming. The European Union has stressed that electrification must be "clean," which Channels Television said could spark debate over oil, gas and coal in the Turkish proposal. U.N. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres urged governments to end what he called a fossil-fuel addiction, said fossil-fuel interests "keep making a killing, while killing our planet," and urged governments to tax oil companies' windfall profits. He said fossil fuels must go the way of the dinosaurs, adding, "The dinosaurs did not have a lobby, but fossil fuels do."

Wopke Hoekstra, the EU's top climate official, said expectations are "not necessarily high" for this conference, and that leaders' absences were "not completely atypical." The talks run until November 20. Since a 2023 agreement to transition away from fossil fuels, later conferences have struggled to advance that goal.
