**At least 34 dead after Europe-bound migrant boat sinks off Senegal: BBC World**
Senegal’s interior ministry said at least 34 people died after a small wooden boat capsized between Dakar and Saint-Louis while heading toward Spain’s Canary Islands, the Senegalese Press Agency reported, citing an official source. The ministry said one person was rescued. Saliou Diouf, founder of the Senegalese migrant aid group Boza Fii, told AFP that all the bodies recovered so far were Senegalese, with search operations continuing. President Bassirou Diomaye Faye expressed condolences, urged nationals not to risk the crossings, and pledged on X to keep fighting the networks that organize the journeys and to give young people reasons to hope at home. Spanish group Walking Borders recorded 635 deaths on the Atlantic route to the Canary Islands between January and May 2026, the highest toll among the routes it monitors, and estimated about 21 deaths for every 100 people who reached the islands in that period. Senegalese authorities said they intercepted another boat on Friday carrying 142 migrants, including 23 women, two babies and two other children, nationals of Senegal, Gambia, Guinea-Bissau, Guinea and Nigeria. On El Hierro, local authorities said more than 3,300 people had reached the island in about 30 boats since the beginning of November, almost a third of the population, and a state of emergency was declared on Saturday. Fernando Clavijo, president of the Canary Islands regional government, called the situation absolutely unsustainable, Reuters reported. Nearly one in four young people in Senegal was unemployed in the second quarter of 2026, the country’s statistics agency said, and the interior ministry said on Sunday it would keep combating smuggling networks, including with neighboring countries.

**French police officers face trial over beating of black man they accused of not wearing Covid face mask: The Guardian**
In France, three police officers go on trial Monday over the beating of music producer Michel Zecler, now 47, outside his Paris studio on 21 November 2020, when they accused him of failing to wear a mandatory face mask and suspected him of cannabis use. Officers Aurelien B., now 29, Pierre P., 37, and Philippe T. face charges of intentional violence committed in a group and of forgery in an official report, and they face up to 15 years in jail. Their lawyers declined to comment before the trial. The investigation found he suffered about 30 punches and truncheon blows over about five minutes; the medical report noted craniofacial trauma, a wound to the lip and scalp, and a complete rupture of a biceps tendon, leaving him unable to do his job for 45 days. Zecler told AFP he expects the trial to hold the officers accountable, that he was insulted and will say so at the hearing, and that without the studio cameras their report could have sent him to prison. Campaigners and researchers describe police violence as systemic; authorities and law enforcement unions say it is limited to isolated cases. A report last year by the rights ombudswoman, after a survey of more than 5,000 people, said young men perceived as Arab, black or from North Africa are four times more likely to be stopped than the rest of the population and 12 times more likely to face more severe measures, including being frisked. In recent student-led protests a 14-year-old lost most of his teeth when an officer appeared to fire a grenade at close range; in 2024 a judge gave suspended jail sentences over irreversible rectal injuries to Théo Luhaka in a 2017 stop-and-search, and another officer is to be tried over the June 2023 point-blank shooting of 17-year-old Nahel Merzouk.

**The American and Ukrainian Chaplains Traveling to the War’s Frontlines: Christianity Today**

### Both Sides: Is French police violence systemic or isolated?
What is in dispute is how France should account for police violence, as three officers face a criminal trial over the 21 November 2020 beating of music producer Michel Zecler. No new statute is being voted today. The trial itself is the next formal step, and it opens Monday. The officers are accused. They have not been convicted.

Campaigners and rights groups say the violence is systemic, and that ethnic minorities receive harsher treatment than white people. Their strongest reason is the pattern they cite around this case and beyond it. France’s human rights ombudswoman said police used racist insults, according to four witnesses. Zecler says he was insulted with a racist slur and will repeat that at the hearing. A report last year by the ombudswoman, after a survey of more than 5,000 people, said young men perceived as Arab, black or from North Africa are four times more likely to be stopped than the rest of the population and 12 times more likely to face more severe measures, including frisks. Campaigners also point to other filmed cases, including the June 2023 shooting of 17-year-old Nahel Merzouk and the 2017 injuries to Théo Luhaka, for which a judge in 2024 gave three officers suspended jail sentences.

Authorities and law enforcement unions say brutality is limited to isolated cases. Their strongest reason is that a criminal trial is the exception: most alleged brutality is handled internally, and guilty verdicts are rare. Their lawyers declined to comment before the trial. If a court convicts them, the charges carry up to 15 years in jail. No article gives a verdict date.
