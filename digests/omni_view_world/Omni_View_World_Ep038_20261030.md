**Saudi Arabia reports interception of Houthi drone targeting Mecca: Al Jazeera**
In Saudi Arabia, the Saudi-led coalition in Yemen said it intercepted a drone the Houthis launched toward the Mecca region. Coalition spokesman Turki al-Malki said in a social media post that the drone was intercepted and destroyed on Friday morning. It was also reported that a missile aimed at western Saudi Arabia was brought down earlier. Al-Malki said on Friday that the coalition’s air defences intercepted and destroyed a Houthi cruise missile that targeted western Saudi Arabia. On Thursday, the coalition said it destroyed a ballistic missile fired by the Houthis toward the kingdom’s eastern region. It later said two ballistic missiles launched toward the Saudi capital, Riyadh, were also intercepted and destroyed. A Houthi spokesperson denied targeting the Muslim holy city or the surrounding region, and said the group’s cross-border attacks remain strictly focused on Saudi energy and military infrastructure. The spokesman wrote that operations target Saudi oil facilities and military bases, and that no one believes what he called fabrications and lies propagated by the Al Saud regime. The coalition has previously accused the Houthis of launching drones toward Islam’s two holiest cities, Mecca and Medina. Al Jazeera reported that hostilities between the Riyadh-backed Yemeni government and the Iran-backed Houthis continue to escalate, that attacks have continued with no sign of slowing, and that humanitarian agencies warn of worsening conditions for civilians.

**The Philippine archipelago on the front line of China’s territorial ambitions: The Globe and Mail**
In the Philippines, US Secretary of War Pete Hegseth met Secretary of National Defense Gilberto Teodoro Jr. on October 28, the US Mission to ASEAN said. The two discussed progress on security cooperation, alliance modernization and posture initiatives. They agreed to launch an alliance command and control modernization sprint to enhance real-time bilateral coordination and strengthen alliance resilience. They also welcomed progress operationalizing the bilateral Enhanced Defense Cooperation Agreement sites, including investments in critical infrastructure and storage facilities across the Philippines. Secretary Hegseth announced an increase in the Department of War’s security cooperation funds to accelerate modernization of the Armed Forces of the Philippines and enhance interoperability along the First Island Chain. The Globe and Mail reported that Teodoro has separately said Manila needs to increase access by air and sea to Batanes, the northernmost province, and that there will be a multidomain deployment of military assets to the islands. Teodoro told the paper that in any Taiwan contingency, Philippine waters and Batanes may be affected, and that Manila includes that in its defence calculations. Batanes covers about 200 square kilometres, about a third the size of the Greater Toronto Area, and sits in the middle of the Luzon Strait, which connects the Pacific Ocean to the South China Sea. The islands do not have a large permanent garrison, owing to their environmentally protected status and a propensity for being struck by super typhoons. In June, 20 Tao people rowed nearly 200 kilometres from Taiwan’s Orchid Island to Batanes in a narrow 12-metre wooden boat painted white and red, a crossing the paper said had not been made in 300 years. Proto Galarion, a cultural worker in Mahatao on the main island of Batan, where the boat is now displayed, said the trip was meant to respect the historical relationship and that Batanes is part of the Philippines. Some in China have used those historic links to suggest Batanes’s three populated islands and smaller islets and atolls should be under Chinese control. The Globe and Mail said discussion of Batanes has so far been limited to Chinese academia and state media, with Beijing’s government remaining silent. Teodoro said the recent noise is concerning because it echoes Beijing’s approach in the South China Sea, which began with historical claims before territory was seized and Filipino fishermen were pushed out of traditional waters. In 2016 an international tribunal ruled much of China’s claims unlawful, but Beijing has refused to budge, and clashes between Chinese and Philippine coast guard and fishing vessels are becoming more common around Scarborough Shoal and the Spratly Islands. The Philippines is a US treaty ally, with Washington required to come to Manila’s aid if it is attacked.

**Russian newspaper Novaya Gazeta to close, citing pressure from ‘special services’: The Guardian**
In Russia, the independent newspaper Novaya Gazeta announced on Friday that it is closing, citing pressure from authorities. Editor-in-chief Sergei Sokolov said in a video statement published on YouTube that Novaya Gazeta is ceasing to exist and that the Russian authorities, the Russian special services, have forced the editorial team to end its work. He said Novaya Gazeta has been killed, and that on November 9 the newsroom will turn off the lights in the home where it has lived for 30 years, shut down the servers and leave. The paper cited authorities who recently searched the home of its senior editor and earlier this year detained one of its journalists. This month Sokolov was taken in for questioning and Russian authorities searched his home over a criminal case involving colleague Oleg Roldugin, who was arrested in April. Roldugin was detained and charged with unlawful access to computerised information, without any details being given, and pleaded not guilty, according to the state RIA news agency. He is awaiting trial under house arrest that has been extended until November 10, according to Novaya Gazeta. Moscow classes the paper a foreign agent, a label used to stifle dissent. Russia has introduced near-Soviet levels of censorship since sending troops to Ukraine, pushing the vast majority of independent journalists into exile. Novaya Gazeta cut down production inside Russia after the offensive began in February 2022, and authorities revoked its media licence the same year. The paper’s then editor-in-chief, Dmitry Muratov, jointly won the Nobel peace prize in 2021 for his efforts to safeguard freedom of expression at the helm of the paper. France 24 also reported the closure, describing Novaya Gazeta as one of the last vestiges of independent journalism in Russia and citing the search of the senior editor’s home and the detention of a journalist earlier this year.

**Rescue operations on after Indian helicopter crashes in Himalayan state: BBC World**
In India, a search for survivors is under way after a helicopter carrying six people, including five pilgrims, crashed in the northern Himalayan state of Uttarakhand, officials say, BBC World reported. Uttarakhand’s Chief Minister Pushkar Singh Dhami said the helicopter had crashed near a village close to Joshimath in Chamoli district. Rescue teams have been dispatched to the mountainous area, but the condition of those on board remains unclear. The aircraft reportedly took off from the Hindu pilgrimage site of Badrinath on Friday afternoon before losing contact. The cause of the crash is not yet known. The incident happened amid reports of heavy rain and poor visibility, and India’s weather department had issued an orange alert for the area, warning of thunderstorms, hail and strong winds. Dhami said a joint rescue operation was under way involving the district administration, the disaster management department and the State Disaster Response Force. He said he is continuously monitoring the entire incident, and that he had ordered that anyone seriously injured be airlifted for treatment. Uttarakhand borders China and Nepal, is home to some of Hinduism’s holiest shrines, and attracts millions of pilgrims every year. Badrinath is one of four sacred sites on the Char Dham pilgrimage circuit, alongside Kedarnath, Gangotri and Yamunotri, and many devotees travel through steep Himalayan valleys to temples open for only part of the year because of harsh winter conditions. Helicopter services have become increasingly popular among pilgrims seeking to avoid difficult mountain journeys, but their safety has come under scrutiny following a series of accidents. In June 2025, seven people were killed when a helicopter carrying pilgrims crashed near Kedarnath, and another seven died in a crash on the same pilgrimage route in October 2022. Several other helicopter accidents were reported during the 2025 pilgrimage season, prompting aviation authorities to tighten safety checks and restrict some operations.

**UK house prices grow at slowest pace in two years as mortgage rates rise: The Guardian**
In Britain, house prices grew by an annual rate of just 0.5 percent in October, the slowest pace in more than two years, as higher mortgage rates put off potential buyers, The Guardian reported. The average price for a property in the UK stood at £273,566 in October, according to the lender Nationwide. That average was flat on the previous month. Annual growth was down from 0.8 percent in the year to September. Nationwide is the source of the October reading, the monthly comparison and the September comparison. For buyers, the lender pointed to higher mortgage rates as the reason the yearly pace cooled. For owners, the average price Nationwide published did not move from the prior month.

### Both Sides: Was a Houthi drone aimed at Mecca?
The question is whether a drone the Saudi-led coalition says it destroyed on Friday was aimed at the Mecca region. Coalition spokesman Turki al-Malki said the aircraft was intercepted and destroyed Friday morning after the Houthis launched it toward that region. The coalition’s case is that the intercept fits a pattern it has already described: it has previously accused the Houthis of sending drones toward Mecca and Medina, and it reported further intercepts around the same days, including a cruise missile aimed at western Saudi Arabia, a ballistic missile toward the eastern region on Thursday, and two ballistic missiles toward Riyadh. The Houthi spokesperson’s case is a denial that the group targeted the holy city or the surrounding region. He said cross-border attacks remain strictly focused on Saudi energy and military infrastructure, wrote that operations target oil facilities and military bases, and called the Mecca allegation a fabrication and a lie propagated by the Al Saud regime. Al Jazeera’s account does not include independent tracking that would settle the flight path. What the reporting does date is the sequence: the eastern-region missile and the two Riyadh missiles on Thursday, the Mecca-region drone and the western cruise missile on Friday, with humanitarian agencies warning that conditions for civilians are worsening as the exchanges continue and attacks show no sign of slowing.
