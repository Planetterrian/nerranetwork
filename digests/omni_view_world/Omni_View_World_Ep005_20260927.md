> **Two thousand civilians in Oleshky have run out of food under Russian occupation.**

**What You Need to Know:** About two thousand civilians still in Russian-occupied Oleshky, in southern Ukraine, have run out of food; Ukraine's foreign minister says Russia is starving them, and the Kremlin says Ukrainian forces are blocking supplies. Twenty-seven people were killed in two mass shootings in South Africa, Bangkok declared a flood disaster, and Maharashtra declared drought across most of its talukas. This edition draws on all 5 Omni View regional desks.

### The Ten
**‘Hell on earth’: last residents eat weeds to survive in Russian-occupied Oleshky:** The Guardian
In Ukraine, in Europe, food for the roughly 2,000 civilians still in Russian-occupied Oleshky and nearby villages has run out, The Guardian reports. A woman waiting to be evacuated by the occupying forces wrote that she had nothing to eat and that her weight had fallen from 110 kilograms to 67. Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha told the UN General Assembly of a humanitarian catastrophe, accused Russia of starving residents, and called Russia the only obstacle to evacuation. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov acknowledged a critical situation but accused Ukrainian forces of blocking humanitarian supplies. Supplies that still arrived until May have stopped as Russian forces expanded minefields and drone cover, and Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian supply lines have left frontline troops taking food civilians had saved.

**Two mass shootings in South Africa leave 27 dead:** BBC
In South Africa, in Africa, at least 27 people were killed in two mass shootings, the BBC reports. The attacks came hours apart. They landed as the country struggles to stop high levels of gang-related armed violence, and days after a house-party shooting near Durban killed 11 people, including a pregnant woman.

**Bangkok floods force thousands into shelters as disaster declared:** The Guardian
In Thailand, in Asia, the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration declared a disaster across the capital after floods sent nearly 4,900 people to shelters and affected about 52,000 households, The Guardian reports. More than 330 millimetres of rain fell in some areas between Thursday and Sunday. Office worker Kosin Nilset, 44, said his house along the Premprachakorn canal was under water for about 30 hours and that the level was higher than in the 2011 floods, which killed more than 500 people. Governor Chadchart Sittipunt warned residents to avoid unnecessary travel and said about 1,000 people were at one school shelter in Kheha Romklao. Authorities were trying to drain still-high canals, with more rain forecast for Sunday and a taper from Monday.

**UK police make arrests, evacuate homes in ‘major incident’ near air base used by US forces:** France 24
In Britain, in Europe, police evacuated homes on Sunday near RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire, an air base used by United States forces, and arrested several men on suspicion of explosives offences. France 24 reports the base has been used by the United States to launch operations against Iran. Police called it a major incident and said they believed the situation was contained. Army bomb disposal experts were examining a number of vehicles.

**Maharashtra declares drought in 265 talukas:** The Hindu
In India, in Asia, the Maharashtra government on Saturday declared drought in 265 of the state's 358 talukas after a prolonged rainfall deficit, The Hindu reports. The state recorded an 18.8 percent deficit, with 755.9 millimetres from June to September 18. The worst-hit districts include Yavatmal with 16 talukas, Jalgaon with 15, and Ahilyanagar, Nagpur, Amravati and Nanded with 14 each. The order activates 12 immediate reliefs, among them a land-revenue waiver, crop-loan restructuring, a stay on farm-loan recovery and drinking-water tankers, after a dry spell of at least 21 days. Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis had on September 22 named a cabinet subcommittee under Revenue Minister Chandrashekhar Bawankule to oversee relief, and Deputy Chief Minister Sunetra Ajit Pawar said crop-loss assistance would come in a later resolution.

### Both Sides: Removing Cape Town's baboons
Cape Town's council has said it will imminently round up two troops of chacma baboons that keep clashing with residents, sterilise the animals, and move them for good into a newly built sanctuary. The council's case starts with daily harm on a peninsula where the animals have nowhere low and open left to go. Justin O'Riain, a behavioural ecologist at the University of Cape Town, says houses are hard to sell when they are covered in baboon faeces and paw prints and the animals come every day. Troops rip off roof sheeting, get into unlocked cars and raid pantries; some now stay out late to break into homes in Simon's Town and take food from shops. Joselyn Mormile, who leads the Cape Baboon Partnership, says baboons seek low-lying land for water and better plants, but development has taken most of those places, leaving the animals between Table Mountain National Park and the ocean. A 2025 census found the population up by a third since 2000, from 360 baboons to 493, in 12 managed troops, after the city stopped culling in the late 1990s under public pressure. Animal rights activists answer that the animals should keep roaming freely, that coexistence is the workable path, and that less destructive options have not been properly considered. Both sides accept that two troops are breaking into homes and shops. The council says the roundup is imminent.
