**Mexico's Pacific coast braces for Hurricane Polo: BBC World**
In Mexico, on the Pacific edge of the Americas, residents are bracing for Hurricane Polo, which is forecast to make landfall later on Monday or early on Tuesday. The category three storm, with maximum sustained winds near 115 mph (185 km/h), is expected to move over Baja California Sur, including the resort of Los Cabos. The U.S. National Hurricane Center warned of heavy rain that could cause life-threatening flooding and mudslides, especially on steep terrain, and of storm surge with large, destructive waves. The state government ordered schools closed on Monday; Governor Víctor Manuel Castro Cosío said temporary shelters had been opened; and federal authorities deployed thousands of navy and army personnel with specialised machinery. In Cabo San Lucas, authorities restricted beach access, and meteorologists said extremely warm water linked to El Niño was fuelling the slow-moving storm.

**Eight dead in Thailand floods since mid-September: CNA**
In Thailand, in Asia, the government said Monday that eight people have died in flooding since September 16 — five in Sa Kaeo, two in Chanthaburi and one in Saraburi — and none of them in Bangkok. The Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation said that, not including the capital, more than 500,000 households and 1.7 million people across 27 provinces have been affected, with a further 52,000 households hit in Bangkok. The capital declared a city-wide disaster on Saturday after almost 48 hours of heavy rain; schools are closed and people have been urged to work from home. Government spokesman Ekkapob Pianpises said some areas still face high water, though drainage has lowered levels enough that more people could travel, and the department said levels had stabilised.

**Gone in 90 seconds: an ‘invisibilized’ ICE is arresting more people than ever: The Guardian**
In the United States, in North America, immigration officers have moved from highly visible raids to quieter arrests that still reach into daily life. After officers killed two U.S. citizens during an operation in Minneapolis, President Donald Trump said in February that he had learned a softer touch might be wiser, and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said at his March confirmation hearing that his goal was to be off the lead story every day within six months. Last week an ICE agent shot Wilber Rafael Garcés Pérez in Austin; the Venezuelan asylum seeker, who had a deportation order after missing a letter to appear in court, left hospital with a bullet still near his spine and was taken to detention.

**The EU is preparing "road maps" for accession for Ukraine and three other countries: Українські Національні Новини**
In Europe, the European Commission is drawing up proposals for step-by-step membership paths for Ukraine, Montenegro, Moldova and Albania, in an account Українські Національні Новини carried from Politico. Two senior Commission officials working on the drafts said targeted road maps are being considered for those four, treated as the front of the queue even though progress is slow and no formal entry date is set. Politico writes that the maps would be the first time the four are offered guarantees of admission if reforms continue, plus indicative deadlines for negotiations and approximate dates for reform clusters, and that current members would take on obligations because they must approve each step. A Commission spokesperson said the maps will list priorities, timelines and outcomes so the most advanced candidates can finish reforms; officials said Kosovo, North Macedonia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Georgia, Turkey and Serbia will not be offered maps at this stage. Commission President Ursula von der Leyen promised targeted support for candidates that have made the most progress in her State of the Union address in early September.

### Both Sides: Should Canada keep matching U.S. tariffs?
Canadian tariffs on several hundred American products took effect on September 8, after talks on a new trade agreement were suspended on August 21. The choice in front of both capitals is whether to climb down or keep answering tariff for tariff.

The case for the American position, as U.S. ambassador to the United Nations Mike Waltz put it on Fox News, is that the deal was almost done and Prime Minister Mark Carney walked away because it would hurt his image. Waltz said Carney, elected on an anti-Trump platform, derailed the agreement for political gain. President Donald Trump later wrote on Truth Social that on January 1, 2027, tariffs on cars, trucks, auto parts and steel will rise to 50 percent, and that building in the United States would mean a zero tariff.

The case for Canada, as Carney states it, is that he had cause to break off the talks on August 21. He accuses the United States of trying to impose an unfair agreement meant to turn Canada into a subsidiary and destroy its industry, especially autos. He says the White House tried to add conditions on the day set for signing, and that the talks ran under threat of a 50 percent tariff, which Trump suspended on August 19 and imposed once negotiations collapsed. Since August 22 that extra 50 percent has covered several hundred Canadian products, with an estimated annual impact of 20 billion dollars, or 5 percent of Canadian exports to the United States. Carney answered with tariffs of 15 to 50 percent on more than 700 U.S. products, also covering 20 billion dollars, and called it an eye for an eye and a dollar for a dollar.

Both sides accept that talks stopped on August 21 and that each has now tariffed about 20 billion dollars of the other’s goods. The dated next step Trump has posted is the January 1, 2027 rise on autos and steel; the escalation, as reported, continues.
