### The Ten
**Peshawar school roof collapse kills at least 34 children: The Globe and Mail**

The roof of a private school collapsed on Thursday in Peshawar, in northwestern Pakistan, killing at least 34 children and a teacher and injuring more than 50 other people, authorities and a hospital official said. Lady Reading Hospital spokesman Mohammad Asim said the children killed or injured at Al-Khair school were between six and 12, the oldest 14, and that some of the injured were in critical condition. Emergency services spokesperson Bilal Faizi said the roofs of two classrooms fell while second- and third-grade children were inside with their teachers, and that everyone trapped under the rubble had been pulled out. He told The Associated Press that nearly 100 rescuers joined the operation and that he credited the rapid evacuation with saving at least 40 lives. Injured teacher Mahwish Zaman said the school had an estimated 1,200 students from nursery through fifth grade, and that when the roof fell it felt as if the sky had fallen. Peshawar police chief Saeed Ahmed said the owner, Mohammad Khan, had been arrested and that the building was structurally weak; police said Khan blamed this week’s rains, and that the investigation was still under way. Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Governor Faisal Karim Kundi sought a report on the cause, and the provincial government said a committee had been formed. The Globe and Mail noted that building collapses are common in Pakistan, where construction standards are often poorly enforced.

**Container ship sinks in the Strait of Hormuz after a strike: The National**

A Singapore-registered container ship, the GFS Pride, caught fire and sank in the Strait of Hormuz after an unidentified projectile struck it. Singapore’s maritime authority said all 21 crew were evacuated and that no injuries were reported. The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency reported the attack on Tuesday; the sinking was confirmed on Wednesday. A sister ship, the GFS Giselle, was damaged in the same wave of attacks but reached anchorage, and Singapore condemned the strikes as a breach of international law. Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said the ship carried more than 2,500 containers, including alleged contraband, a claim that could not be substantiated, and said the ship hit a naval mine on an unauthorised route. Maritime security firm Marisks said there was no independent evidence that a mine sank the vessel, and described Iran’s account as consistent with a psychological operations campaign. The National reported that Reuters quoted two senior Iranian officials saying Tehran was changing tactics against shipping to keep oil prices high and to influence the November 3 US midterm elections. One warned such vessels would be safe nowhere, even outside the strait; another said a complete closure of the waterway would come soon.

**Gulf oil transfers spill over to India’s west coast: The National**

Gulf producers are pushing ship-to-ship oil transfers as far as India’s west coast as congestion and attacks strain the workaround around Hormuz, The National reported. Shipping analytics firm Vortexa said transfers in India’s Gulf of Kutch reached 82 cargoes in September, about 680,000 barrels a day, with refined products about 65 percent of the volume and crude the rest. Vortexa analyst Varaleka Pant said Fujairah and Sohar zones were handling record volumes, with signs of delay, so the work spilled to India. Rystad Energy said crude transfers onto very large crude carriers have held at about 5 million to 7 million barrels a day since September, 60 to 70 percent of Hormuz flows, and that the full process now takes more than a week, against five to six days earlier in the conflict. Bimco, using Signal Ocean data that counts transfers differently from Vortexa, reported 12 operations in the area in September. South Korea, India, China and Japan took almost 70 percent of the Kutch volume, Vortexa said. Pant said the transfer itself is relatively low risk, and that the real risk is moving the barrels from west of Hormuz to the transfer zone.
Ranks: refiners in South Korea, India, China and Japan taking most of that volume · hundreds of thousands of barrels a day rerouted, and voyages now past a week · already, and still growing Source: [thenationalnews.com](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/energy/2026/10/15/oil-transfers-off-indias-west-coast-surge-as-gulf-shipping-congestion-worsens/)

**Eight crew missing after a drone strike off Romania: BBC**

Eight of 12 crew are missing after a drone attack left the San Marino-flagged tanker Wine Trader ablaze 13 nautical miles east of Sulina, off Romania, naval authorities said. Romanian President Nicușor Dan confirmed that drones struck the ship, which was sailing from Tunisia to Romania and reported explosions at about 07:00 local time. Romanian media cited crews on nearby ships as saying two drones hit it. Four crew are confirmed alive; two Black Hawk helicopters and two rescue boats are searching in very high waves. On Tuesday, two merchant ships were struck off Bulgaria, one sank, and 11 crew were missing; Bulgaria said it suspected Russia and summoned the country’s envoy. The leaders of Romania and Bulgaria are due to discuss whether to invoke Article 4 of the NATO treaty, and Dan is to meet NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte in Brussels with Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev. Ukraine and its European allies say merchant ships carrying grain and other civilian cargo are being attacked. Moscow says it is targeting weapons and dual-use cargo, and has rejected US and European calls for a naval ceasefire in the Black Sea, blaming Ukraine for attacks on Russian merchant ships. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said on Tuesday that the Black Sea was becoming another front of Russia’s war in Ukraine, and that this was unacceptable.

### Both Sides: Should Albania’s student unrest be met by a resignation, or only by an election?
Albania’s prime minister, Edi Rama, is refusing to leave office after a third day of student-led clashes in Tirana. Protesters want his resignation and more support for students, citing poor university conditions and high tuition fees. Police have used tear gas and water cannon. A Guardian account of Wednesday’s march said tens of thousands took to the streets, a government building and two banks were set alight, and authorities then said 23 officers and six other people were being treated, with 35 people detained. A later Guardian report said police counted 51 officers injured on Wednesday and 65 people arrested, while Tirana’s public hospital said seven protesters sought treatment.

Rama’s case for staying is that governments change at the ballot box. He told Reuters that Albania had elections last year, not a revolution, and that leaving would betray voters who gave him a mandate to finish European Union membership talks. He said he is ready to discuss the rising cost of education, and that attacking institutions, destroying property and threatening public safety have nothing to do with students’ legitimate demands. The European Union’s delegation in Tirana condemned the violent escalation and the destruction of property, and called on protesters to refrain from violence.

The case for treating the streets as more than a policing problem is the students’ own. Organisers say the protest is about university conditions, costs, a lack of financial support and jobs after graduation. One student, Lora, who declined to give a last name, told the Guardian, “This is a revolution. We are tired of peaceful protests. We will not stop until Rama resigns.” President Bajram Begaj condemned the violence and said the constitution and the law must remain the boundaries for everyone. The EU delegation also said it had taken note of allegations of excessive police violence against protesters and journalists, and called for a prompt investigation and for all sides to de-escalate. Rama won a fourth term last year. No date for a further concession, beyond his offer to discuss education costs, is set out in today’s accounts.
