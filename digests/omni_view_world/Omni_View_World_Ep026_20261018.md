**EPA quietly backtracks on claims US has phased out toxic Pfas compounds:** The Guardian
In the United States, the Environmental Protection Agency has quietly rewritten a website claim that PFOA and PFOS had been phased out. The page had said manufacture and import of PFOA had been phased out in the United States; it now says quantities of PFOA may be produced, imported and used by companies. The EPA has found that virtually no level of either compound in drinking water is safe, and the International Agency for Research on Cancer classifies PFOA as carcinogenic to humans and PFOS as possibly carcinogenic. The agency is still allowing Houston-based Inhance to make up to 200 million plastic containers a year, containers from which PFOA has been found to leach at high levels into the contents. Kyla Bennett, a former EPA scientist now with Public Employees for Environmental Responsibility, said those containers present an ongoing serious threat to public health. Inhance denied that the chemical leaches in measurable amounts, called any PFOA an impurity, and said it was never one of the eight producers that agreed in 2015 to a phase-out, which was not a ban. Peer said the wording change followed its complaint under the Information Quality Act; an epidemiological panel in the West Virginia DuPont case had found PFOA and PFOS likely cause cancer, heart disease, liver disease and kidney disease. The EPA did not immediately comment.

**Some conservative states aren’t using Medicaid hardship exemption – for political reasons:** The Guardian
In the United States, new federal Medicaid work rules take effect on 1 January, and some states are declining a hardship exemption that could keep people covered. Adults on Affordable Care Act expansion coverage will have to work, attend school, volunteer or do another qualifying activity at least 80 hours a month. Federal exemptions cover hospitalisation, travel for medical care, disasters and high unemployment. New York City has taken the high-unemployment exemption for the Bronx, where about 200,000 residents would otherwise face the rules. Research by KFF finds Missouri and Iowa are not taking it up, even though they have counties that would qualify. Jennifer Tolbert of KFF said officials who favour work rules may want them applied evenly, and that claiming the exemption also means sending data to the federal Medicaid agency and coding eligibility systems by county. Sarah H. Gordon of Boston University said states skipping the exemption tend to be more conservative and to tie insurance to work, and that more people will lose coverage where states do less to limit losses. Joel Ferber of Legal Services of Eastern Missouri called the state’s choice disappointing and said officials cite extra bureaucracy, while he argued participants would rather file a hardship form than lose care. The Guardian reported that the new requirements will leave millions of Americans without coverage, citing Arkansas’s 2018 work rules as a prior case.
Ranks: expansion enrollees in states that skip the exemption, including about 200,000 in the Bronx who are shielded · loss of Medicaid if the 80-hour rule is not met · 1 January Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/18/medicaid-hardship-exemptions-conservative-states)

**Oil facilities hit in deadly overnight attack on St Petersburg:** BBC World
In Russia, at least one person was killed and at least five injured in an overnight attack on St Petersburg that hit two oil facilities. Governor Alexander Beglov said fires were being put out at the St Petersburg Oil Terminal and the Ruchyi oil depot. A logistics centre run by Ozon and a data centre used by VKontakte were also hit; VK said services were available as usual, and Ozon said staff were evacuated, nobody there was injured, and it was not taking seller shipments at that site. France 24 reported the strike as a large-scale Ukrainian drone attack that set an oil refinery and a depot on fire; Kyiv had not commented. Beglov said 12 apartment buildings and two homes were damaged and Pulkovo Airport was closed for a time, and that mobile internet might be disrupted. Russia’s defence ministry said it shot down more than 1,000 Ukrainian drones over Russian territory overnight into Sunday. President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Saturday that Russia had hit Ukraine’s energy sector 76 times since Donald Trump announced an energy ceasefire; Ukraine paused strikes on Russian oil sites for three days, then resumed them. The city’s oil terminal was last hit in July, the BBC reported.

**Several dead or injured in major fire at Canada military base:** BBC World
In Canada, several people were killed or injured in a large fire at Canadian Forces Base Wainwright in Alberta, the Canadian Armed Forces said. The fire broke out around 16:00 local time on Saturday, 22:00 GMT, at one of the country’s busiest training bases. The military said the full impact was still being assessed and that casualty details would not be made public until that work was done. Global News reported at least seven killed and dozens injured when a gas line or gas well was hit during live-fire training, citing unnamed military sources. Defence Minister David McGuinty called the situation very active and delicate and said he could not recall an incident like it in recent memory. Prime Minister Mark Carney said his thoughts were with the women and men affected. The Canadian Broadcasting Corporation said Royal Alexandra Hospital in Edmonton declared a code orange; emergency doctor Warren Thirsk called it a one-in-15-to-25-year disaster and said the patient load would otherwise overwhelm Edmonton’s facilities. The Municipal District of Wainwright said the fire spread rapidly in gusts up to 100 km/h and tore through the base fences. Mayor Bruce Pugh called it a heavy day and said he believed it would be the area’s largest mass-casualty event; Saskatchewan and British Columbia offered help. About 690 military personnel and 300 civilian workers are on site on a typical day, the CBC said.
Ranks: people at the base and in nearby Wainwright, hundreds on a normal day · several dead or injured, with a hospital disaster protocol in Edmonton · Saturday into Sunday, assessment still under way Source: [bbc.co.uk](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cr86z0w4lxdxo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)

**Who is Mehrdad Akhlaghi, Iran’s new tech-focused defence minister?:** Al Jazeera
In Iran, parliament on Sunday approved Mehrdad Akhlaghi Ketabchi, 68, as defence minister, 237 votes to 15, while the war with the United States is expected to escalate. Lawmakers have met online since the US-Israel war began on 28 February. Former defence minister Aziz Nasirzadeh was killed on the opening day along with Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei and other senior figures; Majid Ebn-e Reza, who filled the post temporarily, becomes deputy. Akhlaghi is to rebuild military-industrial infrastructure heavily targeted by the United States and Israel. President Masoud Pezeshkian said Iran will not start aggression against any country but will defend its territory to the death, and said Akhlaghi has more than 42 years in the defence ministry and related bodies. State media said his expertise is ballistic missiles, solid-fuel propulsion, aerospace and military production; he previously led the Shahid Bagheri Industrial Group, the Aerospace Industries Organization and the Defence Industries Organization, and he and those entities are under US sanctions, with Akhlaghi also under United Nations sanctions. Tasnim, linked to the Revolutionary Guard, associated him with the Sejjil and Zolfaghar missile families and with weapons including Almas missiles and Yasin guided bombs. Defence ministry spokesman Brigadier General Reza Talaei-Nik said the nomination reflected a need for more and better missiles and drones, and that offensive arms are being actively improved. The United States has said it destroyed most of Iran’s military industry; Tehran says considerable capability remains and has been upgraded from the war.

### Both Sides: Should Israel prosecute the Taybeh attackers now?
A delegation of diplomats from Belgium, France, Poland and the Catholic Church was confronted in Taybeh, near Ramallah, while assessing settler attacks on Palestinians bringing in the olive harvest. Belgium, France, Poland and the European Union want those responsible brought to justice and the violence stopped. Belgium’s foreign minister, Maxime Prevot, said a consulate car window was smashed, condemned the assault, and said Israel must prosecute those responsible and protect diplomats, journalists and Palestinians, who he said face this violence every day. France’s foreign minister, Jean-Noël Barrot, called the attack appalling and told Israeli authorities to halt what he called criminal violence and guarantee civilian safety. Poland asked for a firm response. The EU urged Israel to follow international and human rights law, stop settlement expansion, and deliver full accountability. The Israel Defense Forces condemned what they called a severe act of violence by Israeli rioters, said a preliminary inquiry found no soldiers present, and said they were investigating. Foreign Minister Gideon Sa’ar condemned violence by what he called Israeli rioters and said his ministry had contacted the ambassadors, while adding that the visit was not coordinated with the army in advance and that coordination could have helped protect the visitors. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s office urged caution before drawing conclusions, said social-media images do not establish the full sequence or who was responsible, and suggested the reports were part of a coordinated campaign to smear Israel. Belgium says it will keep pressing for EU sanctions on those behind the violence. Source: [bbc.co.uk](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/c962ydpmjm3zo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)
