> **Daylight Saving Time was introduced to conserve coal and oil, yet modern evidence shows it saves almost no energy while raising heart attacks and crashes.**
---
### Segment 1 — The Cold Open
On the morning of March 13, 2016, emergency rooms in Michigan recorded a 24 percent jump in heart-attack admissions compared with the previous Monday. The only change in the preceding twenty-four hours had been the shift to daylight saving time. The policy had been sold, first in 1916 and again in 1974, as a way to stretch scarce fuel supplies by moving an hour of daylight into the evening; instead, the same one-hour shift now reliably produced measurable increases in myocardial infarctions, fatal traffic accidents, and workplace injuries.

### Segment 2 — The Good Intention
The first nationwide adoption occurred in Germany and Austria-Hungary on 30 April 1916. The Imperial German government faced an Allied blockade that had cut off overseas coal and food; every ton of fuel burned for lighting was a ton unavailable for the war economy. Officials calculated that shifting clocks forward one hour would reduce evening demand for artificial light and thereby free coal for the steel mills and railroads. The same logic reappeared in the United States during the 1973 oil embargo. President Nixon asked Congress for emergency authority to extend daylight saving time year-round, arguing that an extra hour of evening daylight would cut national petroleum consumption by roughly 1 percent. Lawmakers, facing gas lines and rationing plans, passed the Emergency Daylight Saving Time Energy Conservation Act in December 1973 with broad bipartisan support.

### Segment 3 — The Implementation
Germany’s 1916 decree took effect in a single night; clocks advanced at 11 p.m. on 30 April and the change was presented as a patriotic duty. The United States followed in March 1918 under the Standard Time Act, though the measure was repealed after the armistice. During the 1973 crisis the new federal rule began on 6 January 1974 and was scheduled to last until the end of the embargo. Proponents cited British data from both world wars showing reduced gas-lighting loads. Skeptics in Congress, including Senator Bob Dole, warned that children would be walking to school in darkness and that the energy savings might prove illusory once heating and cooling patterns were considered.

### Segment 4 — The Unintended Consequences
Large-scale studies later quantified the health effects. A 2008 Swedish registry study of more than 1.5 million heart-attack cases found a 5 percent increase in incidence during the first week after the spring transition. Similar spikes appeared in Michigan and Texas data sets. The causal pathway runs through sleep deprivation: the body’s circadian clock advances more slowly than the calendar, producing an average loss of forty to sixty minutes of sleep on the night of the change. Sleep loss raises blood pressure and inflammatory markers, elevating cardiac risk for people already vulnerable. Traffic data show a parallel pattern; the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration recorded a 6 to 7 percent rise in fatal crashes on the Monday after spring-forward in states that observe the shift. Energy savings proved negligible. A 2008 Department of Energy report on the 2007 extension of daylight saving time found a 0.03 percent reduction in electricity use—statistically indistinguishable from zero once air-conditioning loads in the evening were counted. In warm climates the extra hour of evening light increased cooling demand more than it reduced lighting demand.

### Segment 5 — The Aftermath
By the 2010s a growing number of jurisdictions began reversing course. Russia abandoned the practice in 2014 after three years of year-round daylight saving time produced widespread complaints of winter darkness in the morning. The European Parliament voted in 2019 to end seasonal clock changes by 2021, though implementation stalled among member states. In the United States, Arizona (except the Navajo Nation) and Hawaii have never observed daylight saving time; Florida and several other states have passed laws to remain on daylight time permanently if Congress permits. No large jurisdiction has reported new problems from staying on standard time year-round.

### Segment 6 — The Lesson
Complex physiological and behavioral systems often absorb a simple schedule change by shifting risk rather than eliminating cost. When an intervention is justified solely by an assumed first-order resource saving, any second-order effects on sleep, commuting, or temperature control can erase the intended benefit. Because the original wartime justification no longer applies, the policy now persists largely through institutional inertia. The practical question for any proposed clock change is therefore whether the measurable health and safety externalities have been priced into the decision at all.
