> **Platforms on Turkish poles hosted 13,000 storks yearly but concentrated nests raised rural outage rates in storms.**

---

### Segment 1 — The Cold Open
In 2009, ADM Elektrik began mounting special life platforms on distribution poles in Söke, Aydın, to give white storks safe nesting sites away from live conductors. The measure spread to other distribution companies and, by the tenth year of the program, was credited with hosting roughly 13,000 storks each season across Aydın, Denizli and Muğla. Instead of removing the contact hazard, the platforms gathered many large nests onto fewer poles, raising both the chance of electrocution during wet weather and the frequency of line faults when heavy nests shifted in storms.

### Segment 2 — The Good Intention
Engin Öztürk, environment and urbanization general director in Edirne province, described the problem officials saw in villages along Saros Bay: storks returning each spring built nests directly on cross-arms and insulators, where their wings or nesting material touched energized parts. In November 2020 the Provincial Directorate of Environment and Urbanization therefore began fitting portable nesting platforms on poles together with teams from the local electricity distribution company. The stated goals were to stop birds from being caught in electrical current, to cut the number of malfunctions on rural lines, and to let repair crews work more efficiently when faults did occur. At the time the region was known to host about 50,000 migratory birds, so keeping nests off live equipment looked like a straightforward way to protect both wildlife and supply reliability. Similar reasoning had already prompted ADM Elektrik to launch its “A New Home for Storks” project in 2009; the company placed platforms on poles in western Turkey and later reported that the Ministry of Energy and Natural Resources had asked other distributors to copy the approach. Officials treated the platforms as an accommodation that worked with the birds’ known habit of returning to the same site each year rather than an attempt to remove poles from the landscape.

### Segment 3 — The Implementation
By 2020 the Edirne teams had installed the first portable nests on poles in protected breeding areas. In the Thrace region, TREDAŞ (the distribution company owned by IC Holding) began its own platform program around the same period and, by the mid-2020s, had placed 1,196 platforms along its lines while also adding conductor insulation. ADM Elektrik and its affiliate GDZ Elektrik continued annual pre-migration maintenance on platforms in Aydın, Denizli and Muğla, cleaning debris and adding insulation near nests. The companies reported early operational gains: fewer emergency call-outs for nests that had previously shorted lines, and positive coverage in national media that framed the work as an example of utility–nature cooperation. Partnerships formed with Trakya University and Ege University for monitoring, and the Ministry of Energy circulated the ADM model to other operators.

### Segment 4 — The Unintended Consequences
Once many storks used the new platforms, nest density on individual poles rose sharply because each platform offered a stable, elevated site that several pairs sometimes adopted or that successive seasons enlarged. TEPAV’s October 2026 policy note on storks, power lines and fire risk observed that these concentrated nests increased the mass and wind load on single structures, so that during storms a nest could shift, drop wet material onto conductors below, or cause flashovers that earlier scattered nests had been less likely to produce. The same note recorded that bird-related contact remained a leading cause of faults on Turkish high-voltage lines, with one technical study counting 2,645 such events between 2019 and 2024, most attributed to migrating white storks. Because platforms kept birds on the network rather than moving them elsewhere, the electrocution risk simply moved from the cross-arm to the edge of the platform or to neighboring equipment when fledglings fluttered. Rural customers served by lighter distribution lines experienced more frequent and longer outages when a single large nest faulted a pole that now carried several nests instead of one. TREDAŞ data later showed that insulation and platforms together reduced deaths at monitored eagle nests, yet the underlying density effect on stork nests persisted where platforms were the main intervention. Second-order effects appeared when heavier nests required more frequent mechanical checks, raising maintenance costs, and when storm-related faults triggered automatic reclosers that sometimes started small fires in dry vegetation beneath the lines. The original decision to treat each pole as an independent nesting site had not accounted for the way attractive platforms would cluster activity that had once been dispersed across many unsuitable locations.

### Segment 5 — The Aftermath
By late 2026 TEPAV recommended that insulation, markers, safe perches and platforms be used together rather than platforms alone, and that national risk maps incorporate migration routes. TREDAŞ expanded its program to include GPS tracking of birds and additional conductor covering, reporting no electrocutions at 46 monitored imperial eagle nests in 2025 while still operating 1,196 platforms. ADM Elektrik and GDZ Elektrik continued annual platform maintenance and insulation work, now framed as part of a broader effort that also addressed climate-driven shifts in stork routes. No large-scale removal of platforms has been reported; instead, operators treat the structures as permanent habitat features that must be managed with other engineering controls.

### Segment 6 — The Lesson
When a single pole is altered to reward nesting, the measure concentrates activity that was previously spread across many poles, so fault probability per pole rises even if total bird deaths fall. Decision-makers who change only the most visible contact point must therefore model how that change redistributes the entire population of nests across the remaining network. In today’s grid-modernization projects, does the chosen retrofit alter where animals or people gather, or does it merely move the original hazard to a new location?
