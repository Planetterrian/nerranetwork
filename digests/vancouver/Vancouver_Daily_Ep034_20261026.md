# Vancouver Daily News
> **A thirty-two-thousand-dollar basic exemption is the first bill Lorne Doerkson wants for B.C. taxpayers.**

**What You Need to Know:** Premier-designate Lorne Doerkson says his first bill will raise the basic personal exemption to $32,000, so earnings up to that amount are not taxed. A day after the Conservative win, he also left a fall sitting open, named a transition chair, and set a day-one review of a deficit he put at nearly $14 billion. Elsewhere, more than 1,200 workers are off the job at Highland Valley Copper, and about 150 North Vancouver grads spent hours stuck on a party boat in Burrard Inlet.

### Top Stories
**B.C. premier-designate Lorne Doerkson hints at a fall legislative session: Vancouver Sun**
A day after becoming premier-designate, Lorne Doerkson told reporters at the JW Marriott Parq Vancouver that he has not ruled out a fall legislative session, and that briefings with the public service begin in the coming days. He said that on day one the government will launch an independent review of the province’s finances before a budget, citing a deficit of nearly $14 billion and eight credit downgrades in three years, and that within the first 100 days it will stand up an expert commission on keeping emergency rooms open and cutting wait times. The Vancouver Sun reported that his first big bill will raise the basic personal exemption to $32,000, and that the change has to be made by January 1 if British Columbians are to see it in the 2027 tax year; Daily Hive reported the cut as about $1,000 a year for nearly every working British Columbian, and quoted him saying, “We said we will not raise your taxes, and we will not.” Daily Hive also reported NDP forecasts of a $13.8 billion deficit for 2026/2027, $12.7 billion for 2027/2028 and $12 billion for 2028/2029, with total provincial debt expected at $180.9 billion by the end of this fiscal year and almost $236 billion before the end of the decade, against about $89 billion when David Eby became premier in late 2022. He appointed Kim Henderson, former deputy minister to Christy Clark and former head of the public service, to chair the transition team; Daily Hive reported she will do it as a volunteer on unpaid leave from Trans Mountain, and that he has spoken with Lieutenant Governor Wendy Cocchia, with a swearing-in later next month. He said he will remain party leader and called the interim-leader rule an administrative detail; Daily Hive reported the party constitution does not let an interim leader run for the permanent job without a two-thirds vote, and that the board pledged during the campaign to pursue that change. He said he still intends to repeal the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act, wants that as soon as possible and pointed to the spring 2027 session when asked about timing, while saying he will take a meeting with First Nations any day a partnership can grow. He said he wants a call with Prime Minister Mark Carney and Alberta Premier Danielle Smith on the proposed Pacific Link pipeline, called the U.S. trade conflict the province’s biggest economic threat, and said he wants to talk to Carney about expanding $10-a-day daycare, including spaces in private facilities. He reaffirmed a switch back to a 10-lane bridge to replace the George Massey Tunnel, said early site work should continue for now, and said the immersed-tunnel project cannot proceed in its current form; Daily Hive reported the updated tunnel cost at $8.5 billion, up from $4.15 billion, with no environmental assessment certificate and the bidding split into five contracts. He promised a Respect Local Democracy Act to ban provincial and municipal campaigns within 90 days of each other, said he has no plan yet for the Vancouver Whitecaps but wants a conversation, and said he will hold a business summit in Vancouver next summer. As of Sunday morning the Sun reported the Conservatives had won 59 seats and led in five more, the NDP had won 23 and led in four, and the Greens had won two, with 99.75 percent of ballot boxes reporting and a final count on November 6; Daily Hive later reported the Conservatives leading in 63 of 93 ridings, the NDP ahead in 28 and the Greens in two, with the final count scheduled for November 6 to 10. ### City & Province
**Conservatives sweep Surrey as record 15 Punjab-origin candidates win in British Columbia: The New Indian Express**
The New Indian Express reported that a record 15 Indian-origin candidates from Punjab were elected to the B.C. legislature, passing the 2024 high of 14, with 11 Conservatives and four New Democrats. It cited the 2021 census figure that South Asians account for 9.6 percent of British Columbia’s population, and said seven of the 15 were re-elected, including Deputy Premier Niki Sharma in Vancouver-Hastings and Speaker Raj Chauhan in Burnaby-New Westminster. In Surrey, Mandeep Dhaliwal held Surrey North, Garry Purewal took Surrey-Fleetwood from Jagrup Brar, Harj Dhaliwal took Surrey-Newton from Jessie Sunner, Garry Thind took Surrey-Guildford from Garry Begg a week after also winning a Surrey school-trustee seat, Zeeshan Wahla took Surrey City Centre from Amna Shah, and Jessy Sahota won Surrey-Serpentine River. Other Conservative winners named were Monica Mohan in Delta North, Pavneet Singh in Kelowna-Lake Country-Coldstream, Harman Bhangu in Langley-Abbotsford, Jody Toor in Langley-Willowbrook, Steve Kooner in Richmond-Queensborough and Deep Sandhu in Vancouver-Fraserview; other NDP winners named were Ravi Parmar in Langford-Highlands and Rohini Arora in Burnaby East. The paper said the wins followed a Conservative sweep of Surrey, that final counts in early November could still change the tally, and that once confirmed the 15 would be about one-sixth of the legislature. ### The Closer Look
**BC NDP party faithful dismayed at impacts of Eby’s snap election: Island Social Trends**
Island Social Trends reported from Victoria that the snap election has put the B.C. NDP back in Official Opposition after nine years leading the province, and that Sunday found party loyalists still sorting a campaign many had not expected. The next election had been scheduled for October 2028, with two years left in the 43rd Parliament. The provincial campaign also overlapped municipal campaigns already underway toward an October 17 civic vote, with the provincial election set for October 24.

Bowinn Ma, re-elected in North Vancouver-Lonsdale, defended the call on election night and repeated David Eby’s point from the trail: votes to pass legislation had to be cobbled together from MLAs who were in hospital, including for maternity or cancer. Adrian Dix, re-elected in Vancouver-Kingsway for a seventh time, told television the other way. “Obviously it was a mistake to call the election,” Dix said. He said the call overwhelmed the campaign, did not let the party’s issues break through, and hurt people’s assessment of the premier, and that the premier took responsibility that night.

Dix, who lost as NDP leader in 2013, said the burden on a leader who loses is enormous, for the candidates, the people and the issues. He recalled that the NDP won only two seats in 2001, nearly won in 2005, and then won in 2017, 2020 and 2024. “We’ll be back, you bet we’ll be back,” he said. He said the party’s job now is a strong Opposition that holds the Conservatives to account, and he described that victory as one “without substance, a clear platform or agenda.” He called the night immensely disappointing and named losses by incumbent Sunita Dhir, hopeful Richard Zussman, and Surrey candidates Amna Shah and Jesse Sunner, whom he called future stars, plus Jagrup Brar and Gary Begg. He said health care and education delivered over nine years are part of what was at stake.

Former Victoria-Swan Lake MLA Rob Fleming, who held the education file under John Horgan from 2005 to 2024, spoke Saturday night at the South Island after-party in downtown Victoria. “It was David Eby’s decision and it was his decision alone to call this snap election,” Fleming told Island Social Trends. He called it a spectacularly poor political calculus, seen as cynical and unwanted beside the municipal elections. He said Kerry-Lynne Findlay resigned on a Sunday and Eby called the election on a Tuesday anyway, after expecting to run against her. Fleming said good candidates and former ministers who lost would probably not send Eby a best-wishes card. He emceed for four who were re-elected: Diana Gibson, Nina Krieger, Grace Lore and Darlene Rotchford. He also said a Green vote produced Conservatives on the south Island, and that he believes Saanich South was lost for Richard Zussman because of a heavy Green vote. **Hughes scores in return to Vancouver as Canucks lose to Wild: Sportsnet.ca**
The Minnesota Wild beat the Vancouver Canucks 4-2 on Sunday at Rogers Arena, the Canucks’ tenth straight loss, leaving them at 2-10-1 and a game from the franchise record of 11 straight set last season from December 30 to January 19. Quinn Hughes, in his first return since the December trade, scored his 500th NHL point and added an assist; fans booed him through warmups and whenever he touched the puck, with chants that included “Quinn Hughes sucks” and “Quitter,” while a first-period tribute drew a mix of cheers and boos. Maxim Shabanov, Bobby Brink and Kirill Kaprizov also scored for Minnesota; Max Sasson and Brock Boeser scored for Vancouver in the third. Sportsnet’s game story said Calvin Pickard stopped 16 of 18 shots and Leevi Merilainen made 30 saves, the Canucks outshot the Wild 10-5 in the first, and Vancouver gave up two power-play goals. Head coach Manny Malhotra said the team is searching for answers and that the fade after a strong first period is concerning. Sportsnet said the Canucks visit the Anaheim Ducks on Tuesday and the Wild visit the Utah Mammoth the same day.
