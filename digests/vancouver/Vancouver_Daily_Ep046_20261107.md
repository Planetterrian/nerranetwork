**Two men charged in 2025 Surrey extortion shootings: Vancouver Sun**

### The Closer Look
**U.S. Treasury sanctions Canadian firms connected to Ryan Wedding and Iran: Global BC**
The United States Treasury Department has sanctioned eight Canadian businesses for allegedly helping to evade sanctions on Iran's government. Bob Mackin reports that two of the money-services businesses are in North Vancouver. Global News says the Treasury acted with Fintrac, Canada's anti-money laundering watchdog, against money services businesses in British Columbia and Ontario.

Named companies based in Ontario and British Columbia include the Million Exchange, NIA Nations Currency, Farah Exchange, Darvish Exchange and several numbered companies. The department alleges several of the entities also worked with Ryan Wedding, the alleged drug kingpin and former Canadian Olympic snowboarder. It alleges he used several of these services to launder millions of dollars in drug money through digital assets before his arrest in January.

"Canada cannot be a safe haven for illicit financial facilitators that provide sanctions-evasion services to the Iranian regime and its proxies," U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said in a news release. "Treasury will pursue and disrupt the Iranian regime's financial channels anywhere in the world." Global News describes the sanctions as the latest U.S. move to clamp down on transactions evading sanctions as the United States continues its war with Iran.

Wedding was arrested in Mexico in January and taken to the United States to face drug trafficking, murder and attempted murder charges. He remains in federal custody awaiting two trials in March and October of next year. He represented Canada in a snowboarding slalom at the 2002 Winter Olympics in Salt Lake City, was later convicted in the United States of conspiracy to distribute cocaine, and was sentenced to prison in 2010. U.S. authorities allege that after his release he resumed drug trafficking. Eight people were also arrested in Canada a year ago as part of the U.S. investigation.

A Mexican judge was arrested this week on allegations that he accepted a $2-million bribe in exchange for granting bail to a man accused of acting as second-in-command to Wedding. The U.S. Department of Justice said Luis Benitez Alcantara is accused of unlawfully securing the release of Andrew Clark, a Canadian man alleged to have played a key role in a violent international drug trafficking ring. Wedding is alleged to have ordered the murders of several people, including a witness who could testify against him in a 2024 narcotics case. U.S. authorities have said he had been on the run for years.

For the North Vancouver businesses, the sanction is the change in force now: U.S. measures cover Canadian currency-exchange firms the Treasury accuses of serving the Iranian regime and, in several cases, Wedding. The next dates the coverage names are his trials in March and October of next year. **Whitecaps' Halbouni says he has been diagnosed with Hodgkin lymphoma: TSN**
Vancouver Whitecaps centre back Belal Halbouni said Friday he has been diagnosed with Hodgkin lymphoma. The 26-year-old from London, Ont., began treatment in his hometown last week. Sporting director Axel Schuster said Halbouni and his family have the full support of the club, that Halbouni will step away to focus on treatment and recovery, and that the club will be there and looks forward to welcoming him back.
