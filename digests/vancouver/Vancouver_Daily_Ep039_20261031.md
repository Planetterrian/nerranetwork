### The Closer Look
**Feds list Port of Vancouver's $5.9 billion Roberts Bank Terminal 2 as project of national interest: Vancouver Sun**
The federal government has named Roberts Bank Terminal 2, the planned container expansion at the Port of Vancouver in Delta, a project of national interest. Transport Minister Steven MacKinnon announced the listing Friday, October 30, at the Delta port, with Delta MP Jill McKnight, B.C. premier-designate Lorne Doerkson, Tsawwassen First Nation Chief Laura Cassidy, and Vancouver Fraser Port Authority president and CEO Peter Xotta beside him. The Surrey Now-Leader reports the step is meant to streamline approvals for the $5.9-billion project and to accelerate commercial operations by up to four years, to 2035 rather than 2039. Business in Vancouver reports the expansion is expected to add 2.4 million containers of capacity. The Vancouver Sun reports this is the second project fast-tracked by Prime Minister Mark Carney's major projects office.

MacKinnon called the Port of Vancouver Canada's largest by a long margin. He said it connects Canadian businesses to more than 170 markets and handles about one-third of Canada's trade outside the United States. He tied the expansion to a federal goal of doubling exports to non-U.S. markets by 2035, and said an investment in the port is an investment in farmers, manufacturers, producers, exporters, small businesses, and communities across the country. Carney said in a statement that the listing better connects the economy with the world and diversifies trade, and that Canada will not wait as the world competes for capital. The Surrey Now-Leader reports the designation lets the project bypass remaining federal permits and authorizations. The Major Projects Office will keep coordinating consultation with Indigenous communities, and a conditions document will be issued by the minister responsible under the act, currently Internal Trade Minister Dominic Leblanc. Senior government officials told a technical briefing that document can be ready within a couple of months. Environmental protections are among the conditions, including effects on southern resident killer whales, described as an endangered species, and on chinook salmon. The port authority has proposed avoidance, mitigation, and offsetting, including measures for underwater noise, and has committed to 102 hectares of fish-habitat offsetting.

Chief Laura Cassidy said Tsawwassen First Nation's support is not unconditional. She said the nation still has concerns about the long-term health of the Fraser River estuary and the Salish Sea, and that years of consultation produced accommodations for Tsawwassen harvesters, environmental measures, and commitments to ongoing oversight. She said the nation sought assurances the Building Canada Act would not be used to cut corners or weaken protections, and that Canada committed to upholding conditions the nation has long fought for and to managing impacts in the spirit of their treaty. Cassidy said a high priority is a breach in the Roberts Bank Causeway to improve habitat access for young salmon, and that major projects should aim for net environmental gain rather than only minimizing harm.
