### The Closer Look
**'Small number of people' bitten by rabid skunk in downtown Vancouver: Daily Hive Vancouver**
Vancouver Coastal Health says a skunk from downtown Vancouver has tested positive for rabies, and a small number of people bitten by that skunk have already been assessed and given rabies vaccinations and immunoglobulin. The Vancouver Sun reports that a man was bitten in the West End. Castanet reports the health authority confirmed the case in a Wednesday news release and described rabies as a viral infection that affects the brain and nervous system.

Vancouver Coastal Health says the virus sits in the saliva of an infected animal and can pass through a bite or, less commonly, a scratch. The authority says it is not transmitted through a skunk's spray, urine, or feces. People are being asked to come forward if, between September 22 and October 17, they were bitten or scratched, or if they handled, picked up, or otherwise had direct contact with a sick or abnormally behaving skunk in downtown Vancouver's West End and Stanley Park area. Castanet says anyone who lives outside the Vancouver Coastal Health region and may have had that contact should call their own local health authority.

For people inside the region, Vancouver Coastal Health says to call public health immediately at 604-675-3900, open Monday to Friday from 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., so a health professional can assess the contact. After those hours, the authority says to go to an urgent care centre or an emergency department. If someone believes they were bitten, Vancouver Coastal Health says to wash the wound with soap and warm water for at least 15 minutes and seek immediate medical care. Pet owners who think an animal met the skunk are told to call a veterinarian promptly and say the pet may have been exposed to a rabid skunk.

The same notice asks anyone who sees a wild animal, including a skunk, acting abnormally to call the B.C. Report All Poachers and Polluters line at 1-877-952-7277. Abnormal, in the authority's wording, can mean disoriented, sick, injured, stumbling, active at unusual times of day, showing no fear of people, or aggressive. The practical change is for anyone who had direct contact in that window, and for anyone walking a pet through the West End or Stanley Park: the assessment line is open on weekdays, and after hours the instruction is urgent care or an emergency department, not a wait until morning. ### Both Sides: Campaign mail that looks official
Elections B.C. is now holding a complaint about how clearly a party must identify itself on a health-care letter sent during the provincial campaign. The letter is from Dr. Habib Ur-Rehman, recently arrived in B.C. from the U.K. In it, he wrote that one reason he came was that "we saw a government that listened to doctors and was willing to act." Later he wrote that "the B.C. Conservatives would take us the other way" and that "they've admitted healthcare and education are going to have to take the brunt of their cuts."

Brennan Day, the B.C. Conservative candidate for Courtenay-Comox, told Global News he has filed a complaint with Elections B.C. and that the mailout is "clearly designed to mislead people." His strongest reason is identification. He said it first looks like official Elections B.C. mail, then reads as a letter from a doctor, and that the only indication it is paid for by the NDP is a tiny authorized-by statement he called barely visible. He said numerous recipients complained that the NDP campaign did not go out of its way to make clear it was the sender.

B.C. NDP leader David Eby defended the mailer. He said he thought it was quite explicit who it was coming from, and that people understand it was promoting the B.C. NDP because this doctor believes the party is best for health care. In B.C., messages like the letter must state who authorized them. Where that line must sit, and how prominent it must be, is not immediately clear. Global News tried to reach Ur-Rehman about how much he knew of how the message would be sent, and was unable to reach him. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12112558/bc-conservative-candidate-files-complaint-ndp-letter-mailout-campaign/)
