### Top Stories
**Global defence bank headquarters office sought by Vancouver could grow to 500,000 sq. ft.:** Daily Hive Vancouver
Ottawa opened a formal competition on November 13 for the Canadian city that will host the Defence, Security and Resilience Bank, and only Canadian municipalities may apply, with proposals due December 3. The bank is expected to start with about 50 employees and could grow to about 700 within 10 years, in offices that could expand from about 7,500 square feet to about 500,000, far below earlier estimates of as many as 3,500 staff. Canada was unanimously backed as host country in April; Canada and partners are due to sign the Articles of Agreement in Luxembourg on November 30, with operations aimed at 2027 and a European hub also in Luxembourg. Finance Minister Francois-Philippe Champagne said hosting would put a Canadian municipality at the centre of an institution supporting collective security, defence industries and more resilient supply chains. Vancouver’s private-sector bid, which the province and the city backed in February through the Pacific Security Bank Bid Committee, has identified Telus Garden — about 160,000 square feet Amazon vacated in July, plus an 88,000-square-foot sublease — and about 90,000 square feet at The Exchange. Premier-designate Lorne Doerkson called Vancouver a natural choice and said his government stands ready to champion it with industry and federal partners. Doug Guzman, chief executive of Canada’s Defence Investment Agency, will shortlist bids for the prime minister and the finance minister; no date is set for naming the city. **What could happen if the B.C. legislature sits this fall — and if it doesn't:** Vancouver Sun
Premier-designate Lorne Doerkson has not said whether he will recall the legislature for a shortened fall session after he is sworn in next week, with the cabinet to be unveiled on November 20. He said Thursday he is still waiting on information late next week and has asked staff, the opposition and his own MLAs to keep those weeks free for work. Former Conservative leader John Rustad said a fall sitting should pass the promised tax cuts, including a rollback of the provincial sales-tax expansion onto professional services that Premier David Eby moved from October 1 to January 1, 2027, and a rise in the basic personal exemption from $13,216 to $32,000, a cut of up to $1,000 a year that economists have criticized as cutting revenue by up to $4 billion against a $13.8 billion deficit. Rustad said a spring vote is possible, but reversing the sales-tax change after firms prepare for January would be harder, and he also worried new ministers would have little time to settle. Former premier Christy Clark said the tax cuts might be the only bill finished besides a throne speech, that many incoming ministers already know the files as critics, and that an early session could be fairly uneventful while the NDP regroups. Restoring letter grades for Grades 4 to 9, reviewing the latest NDP budget, and auditing capital spending and Crown corporations would not require a sitting. ### The Closer Look
**Vancouver woman faces forfeiture of goods police allege were stolen and offered for sale online:** Vancouver Sun
B.C.’s director of civil forfeiture has sued in B.C. Supreme Court to take 124 items of clothing, jewelry and accessories that police seized from a Vancouver condo, alleging they were obtained through unlawful activity. The claim, filed this week, names Dana Rosen and alleges she knowingly acquired, stored and trafficked property obtained by crime, and that she failed to take reasonable steps to avoid receiving or selling it.

The director’s case rests on a privately employed investigator who, between December 2025 and May 7, 2026, reported 150 active online listings tied to Rosen’s profile and identified about 1,500 items, totalling about $158,800, as already sold. He told police many of the goods had come from stores in the Vancouver region. He bought two listed items, later confirmed them stolen, and contacted police. Vancouver police undercover officers in the anti-fencing and theft identity unit then bought listed items, including at least one the investigator identified as stolen. Some goods were still in original packaging, some still carried anti-theft tags, and some had serial numbers tampered with or removed, the lawsuit says. It does not say who hired the investigator.

In May, police obtained a warrant and searched a third-floor condo in a three-storey walkup above retail stores on Macdonald Street near West Broadway, and a storage locker tied to the unit. The investigator also identified seized items as stolen, the claim says. The director alleges Rosen bought goods from sellers she knew to be prolific property offenders, used sale proceeds to buy more stolen goods, and did not have sufficient legitimate income to have acquired the merchandise lawfully. The claim says that conduct is illegal under the Criminal Code as possession and trafficking of stolen property over $5,000 and laundering proceeds of crime, and as a failure to declare taxable income under the Income Tax Act. It says Rosen was arrested for possession and trafficking of property obtained by crime. It does not say whether she was charged. The allegations are unproven in court.
