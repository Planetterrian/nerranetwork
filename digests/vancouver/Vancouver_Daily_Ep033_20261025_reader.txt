Yesterday the polls closed at 8 p.m., and Lorne Doerkson, the Conservative interim leader, is now premier-elect.

The Philippine Canadian Inquirer said he was leading in 64 of 93 districts, the NDP in 27 and the Greens in two, and a majority needed 47.

Island Social Trends, citing Elections BC, put the Conservative vote at 48.05 per cent, the NDP at 37.38 and the Greens at 10.97, with preliminary turnout at 48.06 per cent.

Eby had called the snap vote on September 22 from a one-seat majority, nearly two years early, and he conceded in less than two hours.

He told supporters in Vancouver it was something he did with a very heavy heart, and that it was time for new leadership in the party.

He was projected to hold Vancouver-Point Grey, said he had phoned Doerkson to congratulate him, and he stays premier only while the new government takes over.

That result puts Doerkson on course to be the first Conservative premier since 1933.

The Vancouver Sun counted 1,770,371 votes, 47.97 per cent of registered voters, against more than two million people and 58.74 per cent in 2024.

Green leader Emily Lowan, who is 26, won Saanich North and the Islands, and Daily Hive quoted her calling the Greens the only party equipped to stand against the Conservatives and their existence.

Elections BC’s final count runs November 6 to 10, and Island Social Trends said about 70,000 mail and other ballots could shift close races, not who formed government.

Vancouver itself did not flip the way the province did.

The Vancouver Sun said the NDP still took eight of 12 city ridings, and lost three it held in 2024: Vancouver-Langara, Vancouver-Fraserview and Vancouver-Yaletown.

Vancouver-Quilchena, the only city seat the Conservatives won in 2024, stayed Conservative.

Adrian Dix, back in Vancouver-Renfrew for a seventh time, told the Sun the snap call was obviously a mistake, and that he gives Eby great credit for owning it.

With all polls in, Niki Sharma beat Phyllis Tang 9,706 votes to 4,794.

Terry Yung lost Vancouver-Yaletown to Conservative Margareta Dovgal by 844 votes, and Dovgal said she wants to advocate on public safety, which she called abysmal.

In Vancouver-Langara, Sunita Dhir lost to Jaime Stein, who took 50.4 per cent.

Amanda Burrows won the NDP seat after Joan Phillip, the previous MLA, died last month at 74.

The Conservatives dropped candidate Jordan Zheng McIntosh on October 15 after it was alleged he posted remarks about the Holocaust and Hitler.

Surrey is a different map from Vancouver.

Surrey Now-Leader and the Vancouver Sun both recorded a Conservative sweep of all 10 Surrey ridings, after the party had won six of those 10 in 2024.

The Sun named Jagrup Brar, Amna Shah, Jessie Sunner and Garry Begg among the NDP MLAs who lost, and Surrey Now-Leader said Conservative Purewal took Surrey-Fleetwood from Brar.

The closest race was Surrey-City Centre, where Zeeshan Wahla finished with 5,696 votes, 47.5 per cent, to Shah’s 5,305, 44.2 per cent, all 72 boxes counted.

The lead changed hands all night, and at one point they were a single vote apart.

Shah had beaten Wahla in 2024 only after an Elections BC recount.

Oliver Wildfong took 583 votes there and Raman Sharma 418.

In Surrey-Newton, Harj Dhaliwal had 7,316 to Jessie Sunner’s 5,461.

Sunner, the minister of post-secondary education and future skills, had won that seat in 2024 with 7,924 votes.

In Surrey North, Mandeep Singh Dhaliwal had 7,378 to Sahib Thind’s 4,201, all 65 boxes in.

In Surrey-Panorama, Bryan Tepper had 8,557 to Jinny Sims’s 4,886, all 60 boxes in.

Megan Knight, the former White Rock mayor, won Surrey South with 12,550 votes, 61.4 per cent, to Parik Tahlan’s 4,533 and CentreBC leader Elenore Sturko’s 2,074, after all 82 boxes.

Knight told Peace Arch News she thought Eby didn’t listen to the people, and that a Conservative government is likely to look at housing edicts White Rock has resisted.

Sturko congratulated Knight and said she hopes the new government serves British Columbia well.

Trevor Halford won Surrey-White Rock again, 14,332 votes, 58.2 per cent, to Darryl Walker’s 7,123.

Halford told supporters some 20,000 B.C. workers had lost their jobs in recent weeks, and that tonight David Eby lost his job.

Garry Thind won Surrey-Guildford, 7,693 to Garry Begg’s 6,230, all 76 boxes counted.

A week earlier he placed second for Surrey school trustee with 43,900 votes, because the September 18 withdrawal deadline had passed and his name stayed on the October 17 civic ballot.

He told Surrey Now-Leader he had not been sworn in as trustee, and that staff were looking for a legal way to skip a byelection.

The paper cited the Eligibility to Hold Public Office Act: an MLA is disqualified from local office and is deemed to have resigned that office on the date of the writ.

Thind said people would be happy if taxpayers did not pay for another vote.

The Sun said that same wave also swept Delta and Richmond.

Cariboo-Chilcotin sent Doerkson back for a third term.

Surrey Now-Leader said he had 76.9 per cent as of 10:30 p.m., with 75 of 89 boxes counted, Kathryn Askew of the NDP about 17.1 per cent and Douglas Gook of the Greens about 6.

In Victoria he thanked volunteers and said a mill running in Quesnel keeps a trucker working in Surrey, and a mine in the North keeps a port busy in Delta.

He called B.C. one economy and said serving as premier would be the honour of his lifetime.

The paper said it is the first time in over a century an MLA from the Cariboo has been premier.

South Vancouver Island did not move as one bloc.

Two seats flipped Conservative: Mike Hicks unseated Dana Lajeunesse in Juan de Fuca-Malahat, and Cathie Ounsted won Saanich South, previously held by Lana Popham.
