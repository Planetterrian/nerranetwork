### The Closer Look
**B.C. moves to seize cars, cash, Rolexes and drug lab properties in the Fraser Valley: Vancouver Sun**
B.C.’s director of civil forfeiture has filed in B.C. Supreme Court against 10 owners and their companies, seeking forfeiture of five Fraser Valley properties police allege were used to make and sell drugs through a drug superlab. The lawsuit also seeks a 2024 Jeep Cherokee, a 2017 Dodge Ram, a 2019 Audi A7, and jewelry including two Rolex watches, two Cartier bracelets and a Van Cleef and Arpels bracelet. Police seized cash they say came from illegal activities in 10 listed amounts from $89 to $15,550, about $38,000 in total. None of the allegations has been proven in court.

The searches of properties and vehicles were on Oct. 2, 2025, a day after undercover RCMP officers watched two men transport containers of chemicals to a clandestine lab on South Sumas Road in Chilliwack, according to the lawsuit. Those warrants followed two police operations in 2025 and 2026 aimed at labs producing fentanyl, methamphetamine, MDMA and other street drugs. At the South Sumas property, police found chemicals, equipment and recipes, including 39 kilograms of fentanyl and 3,766 MDMA pills. The Vancouver Sun reports that police seized more fentanyl at that Chilliwack lab than in the previous Canadian record bust in Falkland. Cellphones showed extensive communications between Justin Fauth and Carlos Martinez about drug production and trafficking, the lawsuit says. That investigation led police to Nigel Kiyoshi Yakashiro; the filing says he had no source of lawful income and was highly surveillance conscious.

Fauth and Martinez face criminal drug charges. They are among 10 individuals and two companies, plus the bankruptcy trustee for the estate of an 11th person, named as owners of property the province intends to keep as the means or proceeds of crime. Also named are Yakashiro, Ravinder (Robbie) Singh Ghag, Dharminder (Happy) Singh Ghag, Swarn Singh Ghag, Courtney MacKenzie, Roopwant Badwal, Alexis Coughlan, Carley Kristine Tower, the estate of Satnam (Sonny) Singh Ghag, SGASS Holding Co. and Ghag & Ghag Co. The lawsuit alleges the defendants manufactured, possessed and trafficked drugs and disposed of hazardous wastes, and that Fauth, Martinez and Yakashiro were members of a group whose main purposes included drug production and trafficking for financial benefit.

Three properties were connected to the Ghag family, including South Sumas Road, and two were leased to Fauth and Martinez, who the lawsuit says conducted secret meetings and ran a lab there and used a third Ghag property for chemical disposal. The filing alleges the Ghags knew or were wilfully blind to that use. Martinez lived with Badwal at a fourth property on Sheffield Way in Chilliwack, which Badwal owned and where police allegedly found fentanyl, trafficking equipment and about $13,000 in cash. Yakashiro and Tower owned a fifth property, in Abbotsford, where the lawsuit says cash and jewelry seized on July 9 were proceeds of crime and Yakashiro’s Jeep Cherokee was used for trafficking. The lawsuit also says Fauth and MacKenzie rented a house in Lindell Beach where police found fentanyl and MDMA, trafficking equipment and records, fake ID, about $16,000 in cash and the two Rolexes. **Rossi scores twice, helps Canucks rally for shootout win against Wild: NHL.com**
The Vancouver Canucks rallied for a 4-3 shootout win over the Minnesota Wild on Saturday at Rogers Arena. Marco Rossi, who came to Vancouver in the Dec. 12, 2025 trade for Quinn Hughes, scored twice, including the tying goal with 1:23 left, his first goal in 21 games after last scoring Oct. 1 against Edmonton. Zeev Buium, also part of that trade, scored his first of the season 30 seconds into the third and played a career-high 27:41. Brock Boeser had three assists and extended a career-high point streak to 10 games, with four goals and 11 assists in that stretch. Jake DeBrusk scored the only shootout goal. Leevi Merilainen made 22 saves and stopped Maxim Shabanov, Matt Boldy and Kirill Kaprizov in the shootout; Jesper Wallstedt made 21 saves. The Canucks are 6-18-1, have won three of their past four after going 1-16-1 in the previous 18, and snapped an eight-game home losing skid for their second home win of the season. The Wild are 17-3-5; a five-game win streak ended, but they have points in 14 straight. Boldy, Jared Spurgeon and Nick Foligno scored for Minnesota, and Kaprizov’s two assists extended his point streak to 14 games, tying the franchise record. Hughes was sent back to Minnesota to be reevaluated after a hit from Flames forward Adam Klapka on Thursday. Elias Pettersson is day to day with an upper-body injury and is expected to miss the next two games. The Canucks list a Rogers Arena game against the Chicago Blackhawks at 6:00 p.m. PCT.
