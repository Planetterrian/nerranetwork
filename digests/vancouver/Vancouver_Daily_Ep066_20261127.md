> **Vancouver finished 6,008 net new homes, six percent over its yearly target.**

**What You Need to Know:** Vancouver finished 6,008 net new homes in the year ending September 30, 2026, nearly six percent above the provincially legislated target, while the new government prepares to scrap that target system. Premier Lorne Doerkson has ordered ministers to cut two existing regulations for every new one and to return the rulebook to 2017 levels. Surrey Mayor Doug McCallum is running for Metro Vancouver chair on a promise to take utilities out of local politicians’ direct control.

### Top Stories
**City of Vancouver beats annual housing completion target, as new Conservative provincial government prepares to scrap BC NDP rules: Daily Hive Vancouver**
Vancouver recorded 6,008 net new homes between October 1, 2025 and September 30, 2026, about 24 percent more than the previous reporting year and nearly six percent above the Housing Supply Act target of 5,684. The city counted 6,809 completed units and subtracted 801 demolitions. Since October 2023 it has recorded 14,989 net new homes, about 92 percent of the three-year target of 16,281, leaving it 1,292 short, while staff say the pipeline can still meet the five-year mark of 28,900 by September 2028. Rental housing was 4,653 of this year’s net homes, about 77 percent, against provincial guidance of 72 percent; 824 of the rental homes met the income-based affordability benchmark, about 18 percent against guidance of 38 percent. Premier Lorne Doerkson’s government has indicated it will abolish the Bill 43 municipal targets. New legislation is not expected until the spring 2027 sitting, and staff say this could be the city’s final report under the current act. Source: [dailyhive.com](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/vancouver-annual-housing-completion-targets-november-2026)

**B.C. premier seeks red tape rollback to 2017 levels, before NDP took power: Daily Hive Vancouver**
Premier Lorne Doerkson has put Carson Binda, Minister of State for Red Tape Reduction and MLA for Vancouver-Quilchena, at the centre of a drive to remove two existing regulatory requirements for every new one. In a November 24, 2026 mandate letter, Doerkson directs Binda to work with other ministers to bring provincial regulatory requirements back to 2017 levels by the end of the current term. Environment Minister Áʼa:líya Warbus is directed to bring forward legislation to repeal CleanBC and associated regulations, end zero-emission vehicle sales quotas, and set firm deadlines for each stage of environmental assessments. Housing ministers Cathie Ounsted and Zeeshan Wahla are tasked with speeding development approvals, reviewing the B.C. Building Code to lower construction costs, overhauling the Step Code, and restoring municipal control over residential development. **Buyer fails to close on $18 million West Vancouver mansion, owes seller millions: B.C. Court of Appeal: Vancouver Sun**
The B.C. Court of Appeal has ordered Stephen Brown to pay Rakesh Dhunna $4,262,369.40 after Brown signed a 2021 deal to buy a West Vancouver house for $18 million and never completed the sale. When Dhunna put the house back on the market, the best price he could get was just under $14 million. Brown did not file a response within the 21-day deadline, and a default judgment was granted less than two months after the lawsuit was filed. A 2023 hearing he did not attend assessed the damages. In 2025 he asked to have the judgment set aside. This week Justice Geoffrey Gomery, writing for a unanimous three-judge panel, said Brown’s failure to respond “was wilful and deliberate” and “not at all inadvertent,” and let the default judgment stand. Gomery noted Brown, a CEO who described himself as a sophisticated businessman, had lawsuit experience in Canada and the United States and had hired a lawyer in a separate 2021 proceeding. ### The Closer Look
**Trina Hunt’s family alleges husband killed her, seeks return of assets in B.C. lawsuit: Global BC**
A lawsuit filed November 25 in B.C. Supreme Court in New Westminster alleges that Iain Hunt killed his wife, Trina Hunt, and should not profit from her death. The plaintiffs are Trina’s mother, her brother and her sister-in-law. The documents allege that on or about January 14 or 15, 2021, at or near Port Moody, Hunt intentionally caused Trina’s death by means the plaintiffs say are unknown to them but known to him, in circumstances amounting to murder. Global News reports that no one has been charged with murder and that the cause of death remains unknown.

On February 4, 2025, Iain Hunt was charged with one count of indignity to human remains. Police and court documents allege he disposed of Trina’s body in the woods at or near Port Moody and Hope on January 16, 2021, two days before he reported her missing. He has made dozens of appearances on that charge, the trial has been pushed back repeatedly, and he remains out of custody. Global News learned the criminal trial will not take place until October 2027.

The civil claim says Hunt inherited and acquired Trina’s estate and assets through her will or other applicable law. It argues he should not benefit from an illegal or dishonourable act and that the assets should go to her mother, Dianna Mae Ibbott. None of the allegations have been proven in court. Global News contacted Hunt’s lawyers and did not receive a response. He has 30 days to file a response.

The filing says the couple owned a Port Moody home purchased in 2006 for $632,230 and allegedly sold in May 2023 for $1.925 million. Trina’s mother is claiming a 50 percent stake in that property or the proceeds. The claim says Hunt then bought a home in Harrison Mills using proceeds from that sale. Ibbott is claiming a 50 percent interest in that property or its proceeds and is seeking a certificate of pending litigation against it. The plaintiffs also want an accounting and tracing of other assets they say he inherited or acquired from Trina, and an order preventing him from disposing of assets while the lawsuit is before the court.

The family further alleges Hunt intentionally misled Trina’s mother, brother and sister-in-law, and law enforcement, by claiming he knew nothing about her disappearance and by reporting her missing when, they allege, he knew she was already dead and knew where her body was. They allege he initially took part in a reward for information. They say they believed him and intensified the search, thinking she might still be alive, and that this caused continuing mental anguish, distress and anxiety amounting to intentional infliction of mental suffering. They are asking, in part, for a declaration that Hunt killed Trina, a declaration that he is disentitled from inheriting, the return of assets, punitive, exemplary and aggravated damages, and damages for that mental suffering. The next dated step named in the criminal case is the October 2027 trial. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12167520/trina-hunts-family-alleges-husband-killed-her-seeks-return-assets-bc-lawsuit/)
