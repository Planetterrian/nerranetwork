### Both Sides: Water conservation before a strong El Niño
Advocates want the next British Columbia government to strengthen water conservation because a forecast super El Niño could worsen drought. The World Meteorological Organization said Thursday that sea-surface temperatures in the central and eastern tropical Pacific are forecast to reach record levels, peaking in December. Secretary-General Celeste Saulo said El Niño is firmly established and strengthening rapidly, with a near-100 per cent likelihood it persists through February 2027, potentially one of the strongest since at least 1950.

Coree Tull, chair of the B.C. Watershed Coalition, says that pattern should bring a milder winter, more rain, less mountain snowpack and a smaller spring melt. She says many watersheds are carrying a long-term deficit into 2027, so another thin snow year could compound drought already here. She names possible next-summer impacts as more water restrictions, higher costs for communities and farmers, critically low flows for fish and other wildlife, and increased wildfire fuels. River Forecast Centre data cited with those comments show high-level drought in B.C. every year since 2015, including more widespread severe drought from 2022 to 2024.

This week's Drought and Water Scarcity Bulletin says recent precipitation has improved conditions across the province. It also says Okanagan streamflow remained concerningly low, and that communities on southeast Vancouver Island and in the Thompson-Okanagan still face concerns about drinking water. Tull and the bulletin both treat the drought as multi-year, and both treat those regions as still under stress. The dated marker in the forecast is the WMO window through February 2027.
