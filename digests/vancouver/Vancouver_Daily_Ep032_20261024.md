### Top Stories
**Voters head to the polls for B.C. provincial election: CBC British Columbia**
Voters are casting ballots today for 93 MLAs after a snap election called last month. NanaimoNewsNOW reports polls open at 8 a.m. and close at 8 p.m. in the vote NDP Leader David Eby called two years early. He initially said he needed a new mandate for challenges posed by U.S. President Donald Trump. Eby said Friday the election was needed to end frustrations of a “stalled out” legislature where the NDP held a one-seat majority, and that he regretted not making that clearer. The five-week campaign, NanaimoNewsNOW reports, turned on health care, housing, the province’s record deficit and the cost of living. Business in Vancouver reports Eby said, as he wrapped the final day of campaigning, that Saturday’s election will be determined by the large group of people still undecided and sitting at home. The Canadian Press reports Eby called Conservative Leader Lorne Doerkson a “Trojan horse” for Conservative extremists as the campaign wrapped. ### City & Province
**Why the BC NDP and Conservatives are making so many election promises to Surrey: Daily Hive Vancouver**
Daily Hive reports that on Thursday, October 22, the BC NDP and the Conservative Party of British Columbia each issued announcements aimed at Surrey ridings. Surrey-named ridings, including Surrey-White Rock, are 10 of 93 provincial districts. Conservative Leader Lorne Doerkson has promised a third Surrey hospital as a women’s and children’s hospital, with an estimated $25 million in a first term for a site, a concept and detailed planning; the platform does not promise the hospital would open in that term. Surrey mayor-elect Doug McCallum and his Safe Surrey Coalition have promised City-owned land on King George Boulevard in Newton for a provincial hospital project. NDP Leader David Eby has promised to work with Surrey on another healthcare facility if the city provides land, and has not identified it as a full-service hospital. The NDP has also promised three more urgent and primary care centres in Surrey, further work at Surrey Memorial Hospital, and a purpose-built SFU medical school building in Surrey City Centre expected to cost $520 million, with construction expected to begin in late 2026 and finish in 2030. Eby has promised legislation for a “Surrey Charter,” similar to the Vancouver Charter. McCallum appeared at a Conservative rally in Surrey on Thursday and endorsed Doerkson’s party. ### Both Sides: SkyTrain to Newton
Voters are being asked to weigh a promised SkyTrain extension along King George Boulevard from Surrey City Centre to Newton Town Centre. Daily Hive reports both the Conservative Party of British Columbia and the BC NDP announced support this week.

The Conservative case, as Lorne Doerkson’s party states it, is a commitment to work with the federal government to fund and build the Expo Line extension to Newton Town Centre. Surrey mayor-elect Doug McCallum has favoured that wording. He says the Conservatives made an explicit commitment to build the line, and he endorsed Doerkson’s party at a Surrey rally on Thursday.

The NDP case, as David Eby’s party states it, is that it also supports building the extension, matching a promise McCallum made in the civic campaign. The week before, the NDP promised $250 million toward TransLink’s proposed $700-million King George Boulevard bus rapid transit line from Surrey City Centre to Semiahmoo Town Centre. Its later announcement says that money could be redirected to help advance a Newton SkyTrain extension instead. McCallum says the NDP has only expressed a willingness to discuss the project.

Polls are open today, Saturday, October 24, from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Daily Hive reports the next government will still have to set budgets, construction timelines and, in some cases, federal agreements before either version of the line is delivered.
