### Top Stories
**96,238 patients waiting for surgery, SecondStreet data show: Vancouver Sun**
Canadian think tank SecondStreet.org released data showing 96,238 patients are waiting for surgery in British Columbia, up 8.4 per cent since 2022. The Vancouver Sun's report frames the province as in "crisis" and says the number of patients waiting for surgery, and the number dying on wait-lists, are both increasing. Source: [vancouversun.com](https://vancouversun.com/news/bc-crisis-number-patients-waiting-surgery-increases-data-show)

**Canadian citizen questioned for hours at Point Roberts: CBC British Columbia**
Paige Adamson, a Canadian citizen, was on her way home from a birthday dinner in Tsawwassen on September 15 when she was stopped at the Point Roberts border crossing and questioned about voting for hours. CBC reports that lawyers are raising concerns about vulnerabilities Canadians face while living in the United States, especially over being registered to vote. ### Both Sides: Whether local candidates should take up the provincial argument
Premier David Eby, asked Wednesday about election timing by CBC Early Edition host Stephen Quinn, said concurrent votes "will give an opportunity for local government leaders to pick a side here as well," Les Leyne reports in the Times Colonist. A provincial election day is set for October 24; Tom Fletcher reports Eby called it on September 22 outside Government House in Victoria.

Eby's case, in that Quinn interview, is that local leaders now face the same choice he wants provincial voters to face. He asked whether they are "on the side for their communities, building out the infrastructure" and "doing that work with the federal government," or "advocates and supporters of the Conservative approach of doubling down on the old way, when we were too closely tied to the U.S., fighting unnecessary culture wars, dividing people and cutting quality programs." He said, "I think that choice is as relevant for local leaders as it is for the provincial government."

The case for keeping municipal campaigns on municipal ground comes from the Union of B.C. Municipalities. Delegates passed an emergency resolution urging Eby to respect the integrity of the local votes and not override municipal issues currently up for debate by calling a provincial election. Leyne reports various mayors were offended by Tuesday's call, particularly because Eby had been asked at the convention to wait his turn. He appeared there, said he would consider it, said he understood their anxiety, and said no decision had been made. Four days later, he called the election.

Both the premier's ask and the municipal resolution sit on the same calendar: local campaigns are underway, and provincial election day is October 24, 2026.
