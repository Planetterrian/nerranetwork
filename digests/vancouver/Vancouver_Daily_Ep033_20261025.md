# Vancouver Daily News
> **Sixty-four seats give the Conservatives a majority, and David Eby is resigning as NDP leader.**

**What You Need to Know:** Sixty-four seats give the Conservatives a majority, and David Eby is resigning as NDP leader after Saturday’s snap election. He conceded less than two hours after polls closed, keeps his own Vancouver seat on the projection, and stays premier only until the new government takes over. Elections BC’s final count runs November 6 to 10.

### Top Stories
**David Eby steps down as BC NDP leader after snap election defeat: Philippine Canadian Inquirer**
Conservative interim leader Lorne Doerkson is premier-elect after Saturday’s vote, with the Conservatives leading in 64 of 93 districts to the NDP’s 27 and the Greens’ two. Island Social Trends, citing Elections BC, put the Conservative share at 48.05 per cent, the NDP at 37.38 per cent and the Greens at 10.97 per cent, with preliminary turnout at 48.06 per cent. Eby, who called the election on September 22 from a one-seat majority, nearly two years early, conceded less than two hours after polls closed at 8 p.m. and told supporters in Vancouver, “This is obviously something I do with a very heavy heart. But it is time for new leadership within our party.” He was projected to hold Vancouver-Point Grey, said he had called Doerkson to congratulate him, and remains premier during the transition. The Philippine Canadian Inquirer reported the result puts Doerkson on course to be the first Conservative premier since 1933; a majority needed 47 seats. The Vancouver Sun counted 1,770,371 votes, 47.97 per cent of registered voters, against more than two million and 58.74 per cent in 2024. Green leader Emily Lowan, 26, won Saanich North and the Islands, and Daily Hive quoted her saying the Greens are “the only party who is equipped to stand against the Conservatives and their existence.” Elections BC’s final count is November 6 to 10; Island Social Trends said about 70,000 mail and other ballots could move close races but not who formed government. **B.C. Election Results: NDP loses ground in Vancouver, but still holds most of city: Vancouver Sun**
The NDP won eight of 12 Vancouver ridings and lost three it held in 2024: Vancouver-Langara, Vancouver-Fraserview and Vancouver-Yaletown. Vancouver-Quilchena, the only city riding the Conservatives won in 2024, stayed Conservative. Adrian Dix, re-elected in Vancouver-Renfrew for a seventh time, told the Sun that calling the snap election was “Obviously” a mistake, and that he gives Eby “great credit” for taking responsibility. With all polls reporting, Niki Sharma beat Phyllis Tang 9,706 to 4,794. Terry Yung lost Vancouver-Yaletown to Conservative Margareta Dovgal by 844 votes; Dovgal told the Sun her aim was to be a public advocate on safety, which she called abysmal. In Vancouver-Langara, Sunita Dhir lost to Jaime Stein, who took 50.4 per cent. Amanda Burrows won the NDP seat after Joan Phillip, the previous NDP MLA, died last month at 74. The Conservatives dropped candidate Jordan Zheng McIntosh on October 15 after it was alleged he posted remarks about the Holocaust and Hitler. ### City & Province
**Cariboo-Chilcotin MLA Lorne Doerkson wins re-election for third time in a row: Surrey Now-Leader**
Voters in Cariboo-Chilcotin returned Lorne Doerkson for a third term, with 76.9 per cent of the vote as of 10:30 p.m. and 75 of 89 ballot boxes counted. Kathryn Askew of the NDP had about 17.1 per cent and Douglas Gook of the Greens about 6 per cent. Speaking in Victoria, Doerkson thanked volunteers and said a mill running in Quesnel keeps a trucker working in Surrey, and a mine opening in the North keeps a port busy in Delta. He called the province one economy and said serving as premier would be the honour of his lifetime. Surrey Now-Leader reported it is the first time in over a century that an MLA from the Cariboo has been premier. ### The Closer Look
**ELECTION NIGHT LIVE: B.C. Conservatives capture all 10 seats as blue wave engulfs Surrey: Surrey Now-Leader**
The Conservatives took all 10 Surrey ridings on Saturday, a clean sweep the Vancouver Sun also recorded, in a city where the party had won six of 10 in 2024. The Sun named longtime NDP MLAs who lost: Jagrup Brar in Surrey-Fleetwood, Amna Shah in Surrey-City Centre, Jessie Sunner in Surrey-Newton and Garry Begg in Surrey-Guildford. Surrey Now-Leader reported Conservative Purewal won Surrey-Fleetwood, ousting Brar.

The closest count was Surrey-City Centre. With all 72 ballot boxes in, Zeeshan Wahla had 5,696 votes, 47.5 per cent, to incumbent Amna Shah’s 5,305, 44.2 per cent. The lead changed hands through the night and, at one point, the two were a single vote apart. Shah had beaten Wahla in 2024 only after an Elections BC recount. Oliver Wildfong took 583 votes and Raman Sharma 418.

In Surrey-Newton, Harj Dhaliwal finished with 7,316 votes to Jessie Sunner’s 5,461, ending years of NDP control. Sunner, Minister of Post-Secondary Education and Future Skills, had won the seat in 2024 with 7,924 votes. In Surrey North, incumbent Mandeep Singh Dhaliwal had 7,378 to Sahib Thind’s 4,201 with all 65 boxes counted. In Surrey-Panorama, Bryan Tepper had 8,557 to Jinny Sims’s 4,886 with all 60 boxes in.

Surrey South went to Megan Knight, the former White Rock mayor, with 12,550 votes, 61.4 per cent, to Parik Tahlan’s 4,533 and CentreBC leader Elenore Sturko’s 2,074, after all 82 boxes. Knight told Peace Arch News she thought Eby “didn’t listen to the people,” and that a Conservative government is likely to look into housing edicts resisted in White Rock. Sturko congratulated Knight and said she hoped the new government serves British Columbia well. In Surrey-White Rock, Trevor Halford won again with 14,332 votes, 58.2 per cent, to Darryl Walker’s 7,123. Halford told supporters that some 20,000 B.C. workers had lost their jobs in recent weeks and said, “Tonight, David Eby lost his job.”

Garry Thind won Surrey-Guildford with 7,693 votes to Garry Begg’s 6,230, all 76 boxes counted. A week earlier he had placed second for Surrey school trustee with 43,900 votes, after the September 18 withdrawal deadline had passed and his name stayed on the October 17 civic ballot. He told Surrey Now-Leader he had not yet been sworn in as trustee and that staff were looking for a legal way to avoid a byelection. The Eligibility to Hold Public Office Act, as the paper cited it, disqualifies an MLA from local office and deems the person to have resigned that office on the date of the writ. Thind said people would be happy if taxpayers did not pay for another vote. The Sun said the same Conservative wave also swept Delta and Richmond.
