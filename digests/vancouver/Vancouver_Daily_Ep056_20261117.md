### The Closer Look
**Surrey divided on byelection possibility for vacant school board slot: Vancouver Sun**
Garry Thind's Surrey school board seat becomes vacant when he is sworn in as an MLA on Thursday, and Friday is the deadline to ask a court what happens next.

Thind, a realtor, was re-elected last month with the Surrey First Education slate, which has held the board for 12 years. He also won a provincial seat in an election held a week after the local vote. That win disqualifies him from the trustee seat once he is sworn in. Under the Local Government Act, the chief electoral officer, a candidate, or any four electors may apply to the court. A judge can order the position vacant, which could mean a byelection, or declare elected the candidate with the next-highest vote total. Any application must be filed by Friday. The Surrey school district says it is waiting to see whether an application is made and how the court rules. It says a byelection would cost about $840,000, funded entirely by the school district.

If no application is made, a byelection is automatically triggered. If a byelection is not held, Gary Tymoschuk would be reinstated. He was the only Surrey First Education member not re-elected, served 12 years on the board, and his return would keep the slate's majority. Earlier this month, chief election officer Gib van Ert wrote the Municipal Affairs Ministry asking the government to decree the seventh-place finisher elected instead of holding a byelection, according to the Surrey Now-Leader. Trustees now are re-elected Terry Allen, Bob Holmes, Laurie Larsen and Shawn Wilson, with newcomers Michelle Ceniza and Laura Lattanzio, the White Rock representative.

Thind declined to discuss a byelection. He said, "I got disqualified from that position, and so that's my only statement for school board," and that he is "super excited" about the provincial win. Ninth-place finisher Anne Whitmore, president of Surrey's district parent advisory council, who took a leave to run, said she is not applying to the court because of the cost. She and her team do not support giving the seat to Tymoschuk without a byelection. She said the slate ran Thind knowing he had run provincially before and was expected to run again, so a byelection cost was "completely foreseeable." His name stayed on the website, signs and newspaper ads, and social media posts included his name more than 30 times after he announced his MLA candidacy. Whitmore said the province could cover a byelection while pursuing legislative changes, and that "Surrey students should not bear the financial consequences of provincial political decisions."
