**B.C. among Canada's flu hot spots as outbreaks hit long-term care homes: Vancouver Sun**
B.C. has some of the highest influenza activity in Canada, and the virus is linked to multiple outbreaks at long-term care homes across the Lower Mainland. Federal data put B.C. flu activity in the high category, with 17.8 to 27.4 per cent of tests positive at public health laboratories, and only Nunavut ranked higher. Dr. Brian Conway, medical director of the Vancouver Infectious Disease Centre, told Postmedia the spread appears linked to a mutated H1N1 strain of influenza-A that transmits a little more easily, and that people 65 and older are still the majority of cases. Fraser Health lists 12 active influenza outbreaks at long-term care homes, declared between October 23 and November 10. Vancouver Coastal Health lists five flu outbreaks, four at care homes and one at a hospital in Sechelt. A B.C. Centre for Disease Control report published November 4 found 82 of 363 sentinel samples from August 30 to October 24 positive for influenza, a 23 per cent rate, and the centre called activity moderate for October 25 to 31, against low in the comparable week last year. Federal data show 1,699 influenza-associated hospitalizations since August 30, 77 ICU admissions and 31 deaths, with hospitalization data covering 83 per cent of Canadians and ICU and death data covering 22 per cent. Conway said the B.C. vaccine is expected to offer meaningful protection against severe illness from the circulating strain, and that peak protection takes about two weeks. Appointments can be booked at a B.C. pharmacy by calling 1-833-838-2323. **There appear to have been senior staff changes at Surrey City Hall: Vancouver Sun**
The City of Surrey website now lists senior-management changes after the election of Mayor Doug McCallum, and the city is refusing to confirm them or say why. Benjie Lee is listed as acting city solicitor, a post held by Philip Huynh before the election. Fire Chief Jason Cairney is also listed as acting general manager of public safety, a post held before the election by Brian Edwards, whom former mayor Brenda Locke appointed in 2025 after he had been in charge of the Surrey RCMP. At a council meeting this week, Huynh, Edwards and city manager Rob Costanzo were absent. Councillor Harry Bains, the only member remaining from Locke's council, said nobody from the mayor or the other councillors had spoken to him about it, and he asked how much severance is being paid, noting Locke's team waited nine months after October 2022 to change the city manager. The city's media relations department told Postmedia it is unable to comment on personnel matters because of privacy and confidentiality. McCallum and deputy mayor Councillor Mandeep Nagra did not respond to questions. An unsigned email to councillors, sent as from Engineering, Planning, Corporate Services and Legal Staff, called the first week absolutely disgraceful and the handling of staff employment borderline criminal. Postmedia emails to Edwards and Huynh bounced back, and an auto-reply said mayor's assistant Harp Dhillon is no longer available. The city's 2025 salary filing lists Huynh at a base of $228,227 plus $52,475 in benefits, Edwards at $99,849 plus $7,844 for a partial year that began at the end of August 2025, Costanzo at a base of $426,821 plus $122,277 in benefits, and Dhillon at a 2025 base of $102,655 plus $14,540. **Unfinished 24-storey Surrey City Centre condo tower listed for $30 million: Daily Hive**
An unfinished condominium tower at 13862 101 Avenue in Surrey City Centre, built to six storeys of a planned 24, is offered at $30 million in a court-ordered sale. Goodman Commercial is marketing the Centra site for court-appointed receiver Alvarez & Marsal Canada Inc. The 1.2-acre property, about a 13-minute walk from King George Station, was approved for 167 strata homes and 194 parking stalls, and Goodman says more than $23 million has already been spent on hard construction costs, with the asking price about $206 per square foot of approved buildable area. B.C. Assessment's July 2025 roll put the value at $26.71 million. The Supreme Court of British Columbia appointed the receiver in March 2026 over Centra Limited Partnership and other entities linked to the Everest Group. A December 2025 petition from Domain Mortgage Corp., for Capstone lenders, said $27.8 million was advanced in March 2022 at 14 per cent a year and that about $44.2 million was owing, with about $15.2 million in builders' liens on the site. Construction had stopped since late summer 2024. By the March 2026 receivership date, Alvarez & Marsal later reported about $45.7 million owing on the Capstone financing, $8.9 million to Desjardins and $11.5 million to general contractor Metro-Can, which terminated its contract in December 2025 for non-payment. Of 167 homes, 152 had been pre-sold. Those agreements are cancelled, and Goodman is offering the site for a new condominium sales program or for market rental. ### The Closer Look
**City of Vancouver staff urge rejection of 15-storey downtown hotel: Daily Hive**
City of Vancouver staff want the new council to reject a 15-storey hotel at 852-872 Seymour Street, a mid-block lot between Robson and Smithe across from the Orpheum. Council considers the referral report on November 17. In a rare move, staff recommend a later public hearing and a recommendation to refuse the Bene Group application, designed by Arno Matis Architecture. The plan would keep the historic two-storey front facade of the 1926 Great War Veterans' Association Hall, hold 151 guest rooms, and put a 2,500-square-foot restaurant and bar at street level. The building would be 163 feet tall and about 82,500 square feet, a floor-space ratio of 11 on a lot of roughly 7,500 square feet, with about 18 parking spaces on two underground levels reached by car elevators from the lane.

The previous council protected that facade with a heritage designation in 2023, and staff say the proposed preservation is sufficient. The hall later housed LGBTQ nightclubs, including Playpen Central and Hollywood North. When the neighbouring Marriott tower was approved in 2023, plans for this site contemplated about seven storeys. That 30-storey, 390-room Moxy and Elements project is under construction at 848 Seymour and is expected to open in 2028. Seven of its guest rooms would face only a lightwell on the new hotel's north line. Staff say those rooms would lack enough daylight, sunlight and an outward view, and the Marriott developer has raised significant concerns. To the south, the lot has 62 feet of Seymour frontage and is about 120 feet deep. The design would leave as little as a 36-foot gap to the 29-storey Vita tower at 565 Smithe, built in 2009, short of the typical 60-foot separation from homes that is meant to protect privacy, daylight and sky views. Staff say meeting that standard here would require a lower building. The design uses privacy glass and a climbing-vine wall on the south side. Staff acknowledge the benefit of more hotel rooms, but say this form would create undesirable conflicts with the adjacent hotel and residential sites.

Council can still approve the project. The report includes draft zoning and conditions for that path. A hearing is not legally required, but staff want one because of public feedback and the effect on the two neighbours. Their September 2026 tally says the city is on track for a target of 10,000 net new hotel rooms: seven rezonings under review with 1,480 rooms, 13 approved rezonings with 3,380, four projects with 1,060 in permit steps, six under construction with 1,010, and eight recently completed with 540, about 7,500 rooms in 38 projects. Destination Vancouver estimates 20,000 net new rooms are needed across Metro Vancouver, including 10,000 in the city. About 25,000 hotel rooms exist in the region now, 13,000 of them in Vancouver, fewer than in the early 2000s. Staff say challenging costs leave it uncertain how many approved projects will actually be built.
