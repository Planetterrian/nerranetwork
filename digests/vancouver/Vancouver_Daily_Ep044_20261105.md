### Top Stories
**Metro Vancouver home sales fall 10 per cent as Fraser Valley remains in buyer’s market: Real Estate Magazine Canada**
Greater Vancouver Realtors says 2,024 homes sold in October, 10.2 per cent fewer than a year earlier and 22.5 per cent below the 10-year average for the month. Andrew Lis, the board’s chief economist, said a Bank of Canada rate hike, the snap provincial election, and uncertainty about mortgage rates led buyers and sellers to step back. The composite benchmark fell to $1,068,600, down 5.2 per cent from October 2025 and 0.7 per cent from September. Detached homes are at $1,775,200, apartments at $673,400, and townhouses at $1,006,500. New listings fell 11.6 per cent to 4,805, while 15,688 active listings are still 24 per cent above the 10-year norm, and the sales-to-active ratio is 13.4 per cent. In Fraser Valley Real Estate Board territory, which includes Surrey, both Langleys, White Rock, and North Delta, sales rose 14 per cent from September to 1,051 but remained about 6 per cent under last October, and the benchmark is $860,000, down 6.4 per cent on the year. Board CEO Rock Lefebvre said much of the pandemic-era price growth has been erased, which he called the best affordability in five years, while warning that higher borrowing costs complicate that opening. Lis said markets were pricing a slight chance of another quarter-point hike at the Bank of Canada’s December update. ### The Closer Look
**The Aristocratic sign glows again in neon exhibit at the Post: Vancouver Sun**
The giant neon sign for the Aristocratic restaurant is lit again at The Post, the office building in Vancouver’s former downtown post office. Last week the Museum of Vancouver lent it, with other pieces from its neon collection, to an installation called Lit! Neons of Vancouver. The signs are lined along a wall across from Deville Coffee at the 366 Dunsmuir Street entrance, facing the street. People can walk up to them while the building is open in the day, and they remain visible from the sidewalk at night.

The Aristocratic sign stood at Broadway and Granville from 1960 to 1997. It mixed red and blue neon, used four type styles, and carried a plastic panel of Risty, the restaurant’s mascot, a man in a top hat and monocle. The Vancouver Sun notes that in 1953 Neon Products counted 19,000 neon signs in the city, one for every 18 residents. The lot was later sold, the restaurant was demolished, and a Chapters bookstore was built there with a much smaller replica inside. The museum saved the original, three metres high, 3.3 metres long, and 0.3 metres deep, too big to fit through its doors. Aside from a brief spell hung outside the museum, it stayed in storage.

The restaurant was once part of a chain of 12 that started in 1928. The Broadway and Granville branch opened in 1938 and was the last one standing. A 1951 photograph shows a different Aristocratic sign at that corner; the sign now at The Post is the 1960 version. Beside it are a big “R” from the former Ridge Theatre at Arbutus Street and West 16th Avenue, a red-and-green Palm Ice Cream sign, the red neon of the American Hotel on Main Street, and a white neon sign for The Cookie Jar, a 1950s chain. The Ridge “R” went up in 1950 and came down when the theatre closed in 2013. It is lit by red neon and clear flashbulbs. The painted metal looks weathered after decades outside; the museum is keeping that patina. The other Ridge letters are still in Building 14, the warehouse where the museum keeps oversized objects, which is also where the Aristocratic sign had been stored. A 2001 replica of the Welding Shop and Engineering sign, which stood at 1600 Main Street from 1930 to 1974, is the only animated piece on display: a welder in a mask, a red torch, a green rod, and blue sparks that flash on and off.

Neon expert John Atkin told the Sun the signs sit at eye level, with overlapping colour the way they once worked on the street, and that the wall now has a purpose for people sitting with coffee. Karen Zajac, walking past, called the Aristocratic sign’s return fantastic and said one of her first dates was at the restaurant. The display has been in the works since 2019. **Vancouver's holiday festival featuring over a million twinkling lights returns: Vancouver Is Awesome**
The Festival of Lights at VanDusen Botanical Garden, 5251 Oak Street, runs daily from November 27 to January 3, closed December 25, from 4 to 10 p.m., with final entry from 8:30 to 9 p.m. It is the 42nd year, with more than a million lights across 15 acres, including the Dancing Lights show at Livingstone Lake and three new shows alternating every 20 minutes. A sensory-friendly night on December 1 cuts capacity by half, lowers the audio, and turns flashing lights off. Early-bird tickets are 10 per cent off from November 5 to 26. Adults are $29, seniors and youth 13 to 18 are $19, garden members $18, children 5 to 12 are $12, and children four and under are free, before GST and service fees; groups of 25 or more save 10 per cent.
