### Top Stories
**Surrey Mayor Doug McCallum calls for immediate action on all campaign promises: Vancouver Sun**
Surrey council is being asked to vote yes to all of newly elected mayor Doug McCallum's campaign promises at its inaugural meeting on Monday. McCallum, serving a fifth term after terms from 1996 to 2005 and 2018 to 2022, was re-elected with 31.8 per cent of the vote. He and the Safe Surrey Coalition won all but one seat on the nine-member council on a 15-point agenda. Items named for the vote include withdrawing the city from Metro Vancouver while maintaining core services, equipping the Surrey Police Service with a helicopter and drone fleet within three months, freezing property taxes, using city-owned land in Newton for a new women and children's hospital, building pools in Clayton Heights, Newton and Cloverdale, providing free swim lessons for children, protecting farm land, and promoting a SkyTrain expansion down King George Boulevard to Newton. At his inauguration address on Nov. 3, McCallum said, "The people of Surrey did not elect this council to study things. They elected us to get things done, and that work starts at our first meeting." UBC political scientist Stewart Prest said staff are normally asked to report back before council decides, and called the strategy unusual. Prest said, "This is extremely Doug McCallum," and, "I am concerned he is biting off too much too quickly with this approach." Prest said a helicopter and drone purchase alongside a property-tax freeze would require significant cuts elsewhere, and that Surrey cannot simply pass a motion to leave Metro Vancouver, which handles the region's drinking water, wastewater treatment, solid waste management and regional parks. Prest said a pullout, even if successful, would leave a significant hole in regional governance and real costs. ### The Closer Look
**B.C. soldier killed in CFB Wainwright fire found 'purpose, family' in military, mother says at Vancouver funeral: Vancouver Sun**
Private Aidan Goulding was honoured Sunday morning at a military armoury in Vancouver. Family, friends and colleagues filled the room for the 23-year-old, one of four men killed in a fire at CFB Wainwright. His mother, Sonia Monk, said he could always make anyone laugh. She called him fearless, sometimes more fearless than she would have preferred, and said he loved life and wanted to experience all of it. She thanked people for the outpouring of support in recent weeks and called him her bright light. She said she feels as if she is driving with one headlight: the road is still there and she will try to keep moving forward, but it will never be quite as bright.

Goulding had been on live-fire exercises at CFB Wainwright last month, training for potential deployment overseas, when a grass fire overtook soldiers. Global News reported that Alberta saw uncommonly strong winds that day. The fire killed Goulding, Sergeant Morgan Wong, Private Shaun Farrell and Private Hayden Lykkemark. Another 39 soldiers were taken to hospital at the time. Funerals for Wong and Farrell have been held in recent weeks. An investigation is ongoing, including into the grass fire.

Goulding served with the 3rd Battalion, Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry, which was part of that training. Captain Jack O'Connell, his commander, called him an outgoing, confident, charismatic warrior. O'Connell said Goulding completed infantry training in July 2026 before being posted to the battalion, and that he often saw him on runs after work or going the extra mile. At Seaforth Armoury, Goulding's brother Wylder said the siblings got into all kinds of mischief when they were younger, from bike riding and tree climbing to skateboarding, often ending up hurt. Wylder called him his best friend, his worst enemy, his harshest critic and his fiercest competitor, and said Aidan went places, met people, had adventures and got into situations most people could not imagine. Monk said the family will continue with something always missing while the investigation goes on.
