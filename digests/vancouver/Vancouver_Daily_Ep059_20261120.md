> **One hundred twenty million dollars in Vancouver budget cuts now face a public audit.**

**What You Need to Know:** Vancouver's new council has ordered a public accounting of one hundred twenty million dollars in 2026 budget cuts, with a staff report due in the first quarter of 2027. Only Councillor Mike Klassen voted against the audit and a companion motion on city policy. The same meeting delayed a gas water-heater ban to 2028 and cancelled a sidewalk robot-delivery pilot.

### Top Stories
**Vancouver Council Passes ‘the People’s Audit’: The Tyee**
At its second meeting, the newly elected council authorized a People's Audit of the 2026 budget cuts and passed an omnibus motion titled Restoring Vancouver's Leadership as an Inclusive, Compassionate and Resilient City. Only Councillor Mike Klassen voted no. COPE Councillor Sean Orr's audit requires staff to report in public in the first quarter of 2027 on the effects of $120 million in cuts, including job eliminations, vacancies, layoffs, cancelled projects and reduced hours back to January 1, 2024. Council also directed staff to carry out a June report on ending traffic deaths and injuries, including recommendations for 30 or 40 km/h limits on major streets, after three pedestrians were killed and two injured since late October, among them an 87-year-old woman whose death Vancouver police announced in November. It put off a gas water-heater ban until January 1, 2028: the BC Coalition for Affordable Dependable Energy argued for consumer choice and affordability, and said a municipal ban clashed with provincial promises to undo such limits, while speakers from the Canadian National Institute for the Blind argued electrification protects health and the environment. Council cancelled a robot food-delivery pilot that Serve Robotics' Vignesh Ram defended as an evidence trial for which the city had already hired local staff; the Canadian National Institute for the Blind and Vision Zero Vancouver said the machines threaten people who need clear, predictable sidewalks. **British Columbia’s premier-designate Lorne Doerkson, cabinet to be sworn in: Global BC**
Lorne Doerkson is to be sworn in today at Government House as British Columbia's 38th premier, with his ministers, and to unveil a first cabinet — a day after Conservative MLAs took their oaths, and as the first Conservative government in almost a century takes office. Peter Milobar, the party's former finance critic, says Doerkson has an embarrassment of riches in skills on the elected team and a place for everyone to contribute. Caroline Elliott, who narrowly lost the leadership to former leader Kerry-Lynne Findlay, says she does not know what role she will have and will be glad to represent her constituents. UBC political science lecturer Stewart Prest expects a relatively smaller cabinet than under John Horgan and David Eby, and describes the choices as a multi-dimensional balancing act across regions and the population. Elections BC's official count is 64 Conservatives, 27 NDP members and two Greens, with judicial recounts in two ridings won by fewer than 50 votes, one by a Conservative and one by a New Democrat. **Canadian military will keep firing howitzers to trigger B.C. avalanches, pausing plan to end program: CBC**
Parks Canada and the Canadian Armed Forces say that after further consideration they no longer plan to end Operation Palaci. The long-running program uses military howitzers to set off avalanches in Rogers Pass, through the Selkirk Mountains in Glacier National Park. The plan to shut that control work down is now paused. **Court orders 'deep consultation' between B.C. gov't, First Nation over KSM Mine environmental approval: CBC**
A B.C. Supreme Court judge has ordered that a decision labelling the KSM Mine in northwestern B.C. "substantially started" be sent back to the provincial government. The province must hold what the court called deep consultation with a First Nation whose territory includes the area. The ruling returns the environmental-approval status to government rather than leaving the label in place. **Catton gets first NHL hat trick, Kraken ease past Canucks: NHL.com**
Berkly Catton's first NHL hat trick carried the Seattle Kraken past the Vancouver Canucks 5-1 on Thursday at Climate Pledge Arena. At 20 years and 310 days, Catton became the youngest player in Kraken history to record a hat trick, and he finished with eight goals in 19 games after six in 66 as a rookie. Jake DeBrusk scored Vancouver's only goal, on a power play, after Brock Boeser's tip. Leevi Merilainen made 38 saves, matching his career best, while Seattle put 24 shots on net in the first period to Vancouver's five, a Kraken franchise record for one period. The Canucks fall to 5-18-1 after two straight wins; the Kraken move to 10-8-1 after three straight losses and extended a point streak against Vancouver to 10 games. Brendan Gallagher left with a lower-body injury. Filip Hronek played 30 minutes and 11 seconds, the third time in five games he has topped 30 minutes, and coach Manny Malhotra said riding one defenceman that long is not how the team foresees playing a full season.
