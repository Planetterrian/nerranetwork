**B.C. NDP and Conservatives set out health-care promises: My Nelson Now**
NDP Leader David Eby, speaking Monday at the Victoria Curling Club, said a re-elected NDP would resume seven long-term care projects the February budget paused indefinitely, in Abbotsford, Campbell River, Chilliwack, Delta, Fort St. John, Kelowna and Squamish. The 2026 budget put the cost to complete them at close to $1.4 billion. The NDP said the money would come from a partnership agreement B.C. and Ottawa reached in July. Eby also pledged funding for 500 additional health-care workers for at-home care, and a free shingles vaccine for everyone over 50 and all immunocompromised adults. The vaccine typically takes two doses, at about $150 a shot. The same day in 100 Mile House, Interim Leader Lorne Doerkson said a Conservative government would set up a $50 million emergency fund to help prevent emergency-room and maternity closures and to aid recruitment to rural practices, and would launch an expert review panel within 100 days to identify practices from other universal, publicly funded systems that could be used in B.C. Final voting day is Oct. 24. Source: [mynelsonnow.com](https://www.mynelsonnow.com/80690/news/elections/2026-bc-election/b-c-ndp-plans-to-resume-seven-paused-long-term-care-projects-if-re-elected/)

### Both Sides: Narrowing Metro Vancouver to water and sewage
Voters are being asked, in a Conservative platform promise, whether Metro Vancouver Regional District should be cut back to drinking water and sewage. Lorne Doerkson’s B.C. Conservatives say that if elected on Oct. 24 they would drop the regional role in housing, economic development, air quality and climate policy, and leave bulk water and sewage treatment for local governments. Daily Hive reports those two utilities already account for most of the regional operating and capital budgets.

Carson Binda, the Conservative candidate for Vancouver-Quilchena, called the present setup “not a utility” but “an unaccountable bureaucratic mess with a blank cheque,” and said Conservatives would “take the chequebook back, focus Metro on water and sewage, and make sure taxpayers get value for every dollar.” The party says consultant and contractor costs now exceed in-house salaries and benefits, cites growing debt and the operating budget, and argues the province and municipalities should take the other four services because that work duplicates what they already do.

Lynne Block, the candidate for North Vancouver-Seymour, said “a smaller, professional board will run this utility like the essential service it is.” The board itself, in July 2026, asked the province to allow a 25 percent cut in directors. Directors now come from 21 municipalities, Electoral Area A and the Tsawwassen First Nation.

On the North Shore Wastewater Treatment Plant, Trevor Withers, the candidate for North Vancouver-Lonsdale, said people “want clean water and working sewers” and not “a regional bureaucracy that can’t build a plant on budget and never answers for it.” Caroline Elliott, the candidate for West Vancouver-Capilano, said Conservatives would audit “what went so disastrously wrong under the NDP’s watch.” Construction began in 2017 at about $700 million, then aimed at 2020. The cost is now about $3.9 billion, with completion in 2030, and households face upward pressure on user fees. The district restarted an independent review this summer, with a final report expected in early 2027. A May 2025 Deloitte review, done with the province, has already led to stronger internal auditing and changes to board pay. In early September 2026 the district parted ways with chief administrative officer Jerry Dobrovolny, in that job since 2019. Civic election day is Oct. 17.
