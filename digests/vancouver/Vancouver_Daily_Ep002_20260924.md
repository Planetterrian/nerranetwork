### City & Province
**Mount Jack wildfire north of Sooke still out of control: CBC**
The Mount Jack wildfire on southern Vancouver Island, north of Sooke, is still classified as out of control by the B.C. Wildfire Service, CBC reports. It was discovered on September 20 and has grown to at least 210 hectares. Part of the Galloping Goose Trail has been closed as a precaution. Source: [cbc.ca](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/mount-jack-wildfire-remains-out-of-control-9.7297493?cmp=rss)

**Faulder resident says life is ashes after wildfire: Global News**
Joel Patan says his life is ashes as he sifts the ruins of his home, incinerated last month along with most structures in the small community of Faulder, Global News reports. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12071400/bc-wildfire-recovery/)

### Sports
**Demko placed on injured reserve with hip injury: Yardbarker**
Thatcher Demko has been placed on injured reserve with a hip injury, and the expectation now is that he will not be ready to start the 2026-27 season, Yardbarker reports. The Canucks had already said he would not be a full participant at training camp, with Kevin Lankinen expected to start in his place. Demko played 23 games in 2024-25 and 20 last season before hip surgery. The priority is getting him healthy, not setting a return date. Sportsnet reported that Lankinen is ready to shoulder the load again, and he looked good in the team's 3-1 preseason loss to the Calgary Flames. ### Both Sides: An early provincial vote beside municipal campaigns
British Columbians vote in a provincial election on October 24, two years after the last vote kept the B.C. NDP in power, the Vancouver Sun reports. The contested question is what that early call does to municipal campaigns already underway.

Dan Fumano reports in the Vancouver Sun that some Vancouver candidates are leaning into the overlap. Their strongest reason, as he describes it, is opportunity: a provincial campaign beside a civic one is a chance to make political hay, and to tie a local race to the bigger ballot.

Other candidates, Fumano reports, rail against the call. Their strongest reason is that overlapping elections bring confusion and distraction. Alec Lazenby reports in the Vancouver Sun, in a piece listed by CityHallWatch, that the snap election throws a curveball into municipal campaigns. Vaughn Palmer's Vancouver Sun column, as listed by CityHallWatch, is headlined as Eby about to call a totally unnecessary B.C. election. Palmer also reports that Doerkson lost no time dealing with Premier David Eby's allegation of Conservatives' Trump leanings. Yesterday Eby cited an existential Trump threat in calling the vote.

Both sides are operating from the same calendar: a provincial ballot on October 24 while municipal campaigns continue. The next dated step those reports name is that October 24 vote.
