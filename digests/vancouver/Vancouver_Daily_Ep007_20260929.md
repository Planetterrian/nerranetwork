**Vancouver transit operator expands into real estate:** The Globe and Mail
TransLink is expanding to become a residential and commercial landlord. In its 2022 investment plan, the agency said it would turn real estate it owns into a revenue source that is not fares and not taxation. The Real Estate Development Program is meant to tighten transit connections and add housing as Metro Vancouver grows. In late July, with PCI Developments, TransLink broke ground on a 31-storey tower at the southwest corner of West Broadway and Arbutus Street in Kitsilano: 256 at-market rental units, 47 below-market rental units, retail space and a public plaza. TransLink chief executive Kevin Quinn and PCI president Tim Grant said the 50/50 partnership came about because the two owned neighbouring properties that would work better as one site. TransLink says it is developing excess land it owns instead of selling it, so it can shape how the buildings are built and how they meet the transit network. The site sits across the street from the future Arbutus Station, the last stop on the Millennium Line extension that is adding six stations along Broadway and linking Burnaby, Port Moody and Coquitlam toward that corridor. PCI's earlier transit hubs include Marine Gateway in Vancouver and the King George Hub in Surrey. ### Both Sides: How West Vancouver luxury homes should be sold
What is being decided, in a market report rather than a council vote, is how homes above $4 million in West Vancouver should be priced and shown this fall. Sotheby's International Realty Canada says the market is firmly in buyers' territory. Its best reason is the count: from Jan. 1 to Aug. 30, 57 properties sold for more than $4 million, a 25 percent decrease from 76 in the same period last year. Managing broker Kevin O'Toole says 195 residential properties were listed above $4 million as of Sept. 1, and that sellers need to be realistic with pricing expectations, conditions and timeframes to attract qualified buyers. The case for a wider net, including a public listing, is also Sotheby's. The firm says its review of land title and MLS records from January 2024 through August 2026 found that one in seven West Vancouver properties above $4 million sells without ever appearing on MLS, and it still says that in current conditions broader public marketing on MLS, with private outreach, is key to attracting qualified purchasers. Chief marketing officer Elaine Hung says that where supply exceeds demand, advisors are encouraging sellers to combine targeted outreach with exposure in premier media and on MLS. Daily Hive, using Zealty, says about 30 homes sold above $4 million in the last 90 days, and that only one of those was ultra-luxury, at $10 million or more. Both readings accept the same drop in sales and the same surplus of listings. The report puts the market in buyers' territory this fall and does not set a date when that would flip. Source: [dailyhive.com](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/west-vancouver-luxury-real-estate-sept-2026-sothebys)
