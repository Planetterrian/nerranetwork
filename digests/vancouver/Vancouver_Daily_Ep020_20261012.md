### Both Sides: Downtown office vacancy
The question in a Vancouver Sun report is whether downtown Vancouver's office market is recovering with other Canadian downtowns. The reading on offer is an assessment the paper publishes, not a council vote. The strongest case that recovery is underway, for downtowns generally, is that assessment's claim that every city in Canada has turned the corner on downtown vacancy and has started to move positively. Its best reason is the direction of the change: those downtowns, it says, are no longer stuck and have begun to improve. The strongest case that Vancouver is outside that recovery is the exception in the same assessment. It says Vancouver has not turned the corner, and it calls the difference highly unusual. That is the reason it offers for treating this core differently from the others. The fact that reading treats as common ground is the yardstick: downtown vacancy, compared city by city. On that yardstick, the assessment says Vancouver is the market that has not started to move positively. For anyone leasing, staffing or relying on foot traffic downtown, that is the reported gap between this core and the other Canadian downtowns in the Sun's account. Source: [vancouversun.com](https://vancouversun.com/news/downtown-vancouver-office-vacancy-recovery-slower-compared-to-other-canadian-cities)
