> **Eby promised Thursday to scrap the forty-five-dollar bus pass for low-income seniors.**

**What You Need to Know:** NDP Leader David Eby promised Thursday in Surrey to scrap the forty-five-dollar bus pass fee for low-income seniors and to let more people qualify. The same stop put two hundred and fifty million dollars toward bus rapid transit between Surrey and White Rock, while other parties offered their own promises and a leaked Crown-land note drew questions. Homicide investigators are still trying to name a man found dead in Surrey, and Vancouver police are looking into reports of AI images of female students at Sir Winston Churchill Secondary.

### Top Stories
**Eby promises a free senior bus pass on a Surrey campaign stop: Global News**
A day after the leaders' debate, NDP Leader David Eby was back in Surrey on Thursday and promised, if re-elected, to drop the forty-five-dollar bus pass fee for low-income seniors, to let more people qualify, and to put two hundred and fifty million dollars toward bus rapid transit between Surrey and White Rock, working with the federal government. The Vancouver Sun also reports that he said a re-elected NDP would keep building the Surrey-to-Langley SkyTrain. Most questions he faced concerned a leaked briefing note, first obtained by Northern Beat, which the Sun says shows the province developing legislation to transfer four hundred plots of Crown land to First Nations without compensating forestry companies. Eby said many of those plots sit in agreements going back years, including under Christy Clark's B.C. Liberal government, and that the proposal is meant to cut the fiscal cost of those obligations in a sector that has faced mill closures and job losses. Conservative Leader Lorne Doerkson had no public events Thursday; his party promised a new women and children's hospital in Surrey. Green Leader Emily Lowan said she would lift the minimum wage by more than inflation every year until it hits a living wage, widen sectoral bargaining, and reverse direct-to-consumer alcohol delivery, which she said would open the B.C. Liquor Distribution Branch to privatization. CentreBC Leader Elenore Sturko said she would add five hundred RCMP officers to the provincial business line, one hundred a year over five years, and said that line has stayed at two thousand six hundred and two; the Conservatives also said Jordan McIntosh will no longer be their Vancouver-Strathcona candidate after he was accused of posting remarks about Hitler and the Holocaust, and that if elected he will not sit in caucus. **IHIT seeks the name of a man found dead in Surrey: Vancouver Sun**
Around 1 p.m. Wednesday, Surrey police and firefighters went to the sixty-one-hundred block of 156th Street after calls about a body at the roadside. An adult man was pronounced dead at the scene. Foul play is suspected, and the Integrated Homicide Investigation Team has taken the file. Sergeant Freda Fong of IHIT said the case is in its early stages, a forensic examination of the scene is underway, and investigators need a timeline of events before the death. Anyone who was in that block between October 8 and October 14, or who has dashcam or other information, is asked to contact IHIT. **Vancouver police investigate reported AI images at Churchill Secondary: Global News**
Vancouver police and the Vancouver School Board are investigating reports of inappropriate online behaviour involving some Grade 12 students at Sir Winston Churchill Secondary. Global News reports talk among students last week that some male Grade 12 students had made AI content of female students; what that content shows is not established, and parents believe it is sexual. A parent identified only as Gene, who has a daughter in Grade 12, said some of the allegations are pretty disgusting and vile, and said a principal's email told families the school was working with police. The school board said it is taking the matter seriously, gathering information, supporting students who may have been affected, and will not share details about individual students. Police said they are in contact with the school and the board and that an investigation is underway. Criminal defence lawyer Michael Shapray told Global News the Criminal Code on child exploitation material was amended in the summer of 2026 so that artificially generated images with a person's likeness fall within that definition. Global News has learned at least one student received a short suspension; how many students are involved is not known. ### The Closer Look
**'Heavy distractions' stalling the Greater Vancouver housing market: Vancouver Sun**
A Royal LePage market survey released Thursday shows sales and prices down across Greater Vancouver, with further price drops expected in the final quarter of 2026. The aggregate price of a home fell four per cent year over year to one point one million dollars in the third quarter. The median price of a single-family detached home fell five point six per cent to two million dollars. The median condo price fell four point one per cent to seven hundred and ten thousand dollars. Transaction volumes are running twenty-five to thirty per cent below the ten-year average.

Randy Ryalls, managing broker at Royal LePage Sterling Realty, oversees one hundred and twenty realtors from Vancouver to Maple Ridge. He told the Vancouver Sun that 2021 felt like fifty buyers for every home listed, and that he now sees fifty listings for every buyer. More choice and less competition have not been enough to move many people off the sidelines. Ryalls called geopolitical tensions, a trade war, and the coming elections "heavy distractions" that have people putting major moves on hold. He said the development industry has virtually ground to a halt, buyers are no longer lining up for pre-construction sales, and the investor class has gone quiet, with some shifting capital to Alberta and Washington State. A mom-and-pop investor who might have bought one apartment building in Vancouver might afford two or three in Calgary, he said. He listed rent controls, restrictions, and the property transfer tax among the reasons B.C. looks less attractive, and said that does not help when the market is already slow.

Ryalls said many of Vancouver's unsold units are small and were built with short-term rentals or international students in mind. Those markets dried up when government policy changed. There is also uncertainty over mortgage rates. Inflation may cause the Bank of Canada to raise rates, and owners who locked in during 2021 and 2022 are looking at significantly higher rates when they renew. Royal LePage president and chief executive Phil Soper said he expects the bank to hold rates through 2026, with an increase more likely in early 2027. He said the prospect of higher borrowing costs usually draws some buyers in sooner, but low confidence is outweighing the urgency to lock in today's rates. The report notes Canada's busy political calendar. In B.C., municipal elections will be followed a week later by a provincial election. Soper said elections can give buyers and sellers a reason to pause while they judge what a change in government might mean, and that a home is a long-term commitment to a community. Ryalls said stalled development can leave too little supply later: if a developer cannot sell for the cost to build, housing does not get built. ### Sports
**Tkachuk's hat trick lifts the Panthers over the Canucks 3-2 in overtime: Langley Advance Times**
Brady Tkachuk scored three times as the Florida Panthers rallied for a 3-2 overtime win over the Vancouver Canucks on Thursday at Amerant Bank Arena in Sunrise. The Panthers are 4-2-2 and the Canucks 2-6-1; Canucks Army describes the loss as a sixth straight. Seth Jones had two assists, and Akira Schmid made 15 saves. Liam Öhgren and defenceman Elias Pettersson scored for Vancouver, and Leevi Merilainen made 36 saves. Öhgren's goal, his second of the season, came at 7:23 on Vancouver's first shot; Pettersson's third of the year came at 17:05 off a pass from Filip Chytil, who was making his season debut after a shoulder injury. Tkachuk scored at 11:11 of the third, tied it with 12.8 seconds left after Florida pulled Schmid with 2:30 to go, and finished the hat trick — his second in two games, and his fifth in the NHL — at 2:23 of overtime. Canucks Army notes Vancouver killed both Florida power plays and has now gone two games without allowing a power-play goal. Before the game, general manager Ryan Johnson traded centre Aatu Räty to the Toronto Maple Leafs for forwards Jacob Quillan and Brandon Buhr; Buhr, 24, is a Burnaby product, and Quillan has three points in 24 NHL games. Scratches were Guillaume Brisebois, Luke Schenn, and Arshdeep Bains. The Canucks end a six-game road trip Saturday at 1 p.m. against the Tampa Bay Lightning.
