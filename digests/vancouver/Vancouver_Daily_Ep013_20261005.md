# Vancouver Daily News
> **21,824 voters set Vancouver's advance-day record, up 196 per cent from 2022.**

**What You Need to Know:** Vancouver’s first advance-voting day, Saturday, drew 21,824 people and a city record, a 196 per cent increase from the first advance day in 2022. Fifty-nine stations were open, more advance days run this month, and election day is October 17. Elsewhere this morning: a Creston man missing 22 years has been found, a swordfish turned up on Haida Gwaii, and the provincial campaign added a top-end income-tax proposal.

### Top Stories
**Vancouver’s first advance day sets a turnout record: CityNews**
On October 3, Vancouver opened 59 polling stations for the first advanced voting day of the civic election. The City of Vancouver told CityNews by email that about 21,824 people voted, a 196 per cent increase from the first advance day in 2022, and said the city had set a new record for advance voting. Advance voting is also set for October 7, October 10, and October 13, from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. The city said more than 18,000 vote-by-mail packages had been requested, up more than 70 per cent from the 2022 Vancouver election. Election day is October 17. Source: [vancouver.citynews.ca](https://vancouver.citynews.ca/2026/10/04/first-advanced-voting-day/)

**Frank Davis found in the Kootenay River after 22 years: Global BC**
Frank Davis was 40 when he went missing from Creston. For 22 years his disappearance stayed an unanswered mystery for his family. He has been found in the Kootenay River. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12087479/missing-22-years-frank-davis-found-kootenay-river/)

**A swordfish is found on Haida Gwaii: CBC British Columbia**
A swordfish has been found on Haida Gwaii. A University of British Columbia researcher says the finding is unusual, but no longer unexpected, because waters are warming worldwide, including in Canada. ### Both Sides: A higher provincial tax on income over $190,405
What is on the table is a campaign promise, not a tax already in force. On Sunday in Comox, B.C. NDP Leader David Eby said that if the NDP is re-elected, British Columbians earning more than $190,405 a year would pay more provincial income tax, including a new bracket on income over $1 million. Canada’s National Observer reports the election is October 24, and that party documents date the changes to the 2027 tax year.

Eby’s case is that almost everyone would be untouched and the money would go to services. He said more than 96 per cent of residents who make $190,000 or less would see no tax increase, and that the highest earners would pay slightly more “to fund improvements in health care and to lower costs for British Columbians.” He also said it is right to ask people who earn a little more to pitch in when public services are needed. The party said B.C. would be the first large province with a bracket on individuals earning more than a million. The Vancouver Sun reports the proposal would lift the rate above $265,545 from 20.5 per cent to 22.5 per cent, the rate from $190,405 to $265,545 from 16.5 per cent to 18.5 per cent, and set 24.5 per cent on each dollar over $1 million. Eby said the taxes would raise an extra $1 billion a year. National Observer, citing party documents, projects $225 million in 2026/27, $925 million the next year, and $1 billion the year after, and puts the $190,405-to-$265,545 rate at 18.8 per cent.

The Conservative Party of B.C. answers that the plan would tax the doctors and specialists every community is fighting to recruit, while emergency rooms are still closing at night. Its statement said there is no problem the NDP do not think can be fixed by raising taxes, that the party has committed to no new taxes, and that a government led by Lorne Doerkson would make B.C. the place doctors choose to build a practice.

Both sides are responding to a proposal that, as announced, raises provincial tax only on income above $190,405 and only if the NDP is re-elected. Voters go to the polls October 24; NDP documents put any change in the 2027 tax year.
