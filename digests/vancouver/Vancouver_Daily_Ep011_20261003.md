### Top Stories
**Eby rules out a Metro Vancouver vehicle levy: CBC British Columbia**
NDP Leader David Eby has ruled out a vehicle levy for Metro Vancouver. TransLink has talked about that levy for the last 18 months as a way to build a more sustainable funding model while revenue from fuel taxes continues to decline. CBC reports that a nearly $14-billion deficit made the levy politically unviable from the NDP leader's perspective. **Surrey voters face two elections, one week apart: The Globe and Mail**
The Globe and Mail reports this is the first overlap of municipal and provincial elections since B.C.'s Election Act was passed in 1995. In Surrey, more than 380,000 eligible voters are asked to choose a mayor, eight councillors and six school trustees on Oct. 17, then an MLA on Oct. 24. The city has nearly 100 municipal candidates, and at least 20 provincial candidates are expected across 10 ridings. Premier David Eby called the snap election on Sept. 22, and experts warn turnout could suffer, especially in municipal races planned years earlier. Saanich resident Robert Randall, who ran unsuccessfully for Victoria council in 2008, says the overlap is confusing because affordability, homelessness and housing draw many of the same answers at both levels. The Globe reports two upstart right-wing parties say they will compete in a majority of the 93 ridings. ### Both Sides: A cap on staple grocery margins
What is on the table is a campaign promise, not a statute passed this week. At a Kelowna stop on Friday, B.C. NDP Leader David Eby committed to capping profits on some staple products and to steps he said would increase competition. The party billed it as taking the fight to big grocery companies.

Eby's case is that families are paying more while grocery companies' profits rise. He said corporate retailers are using inflation as a cover to raise prices on essential goods, and that "we've all seen our grocery bills go up and up while the profits of grocery giants soar." He singled out Save-On-Foods owner Jimmy Pattison and the Weston family, owners of Loblaws, saying their net worth rose by $4.4 billion in the last year while families struggle. He said not to worry about Save-On-Foods or Jimmy Pattison, who would land on his feet, and that families have to be protected. The price guard, which he compared to a fuel price guard promised the day before, would be a hard ceiling on retail profit margins for milk, cheese, butter and poultry, items that already have wholesale prices managed by marketing boards. The party said that would prohibit unfair markups. He also promised clear per-unit pricing to curb shrinkflation, and a ban on real estate deals and property controls that big chains use to keep smaller stores from opening nearby.

The counterweight in Times Colonist columnist Les Leyne's account is the last grocery promise. Leyne reports the NDP had promised a $1,000 grocery rebate to every family, then abandoned it four months after the election. Asked about that, Eby said his government eliminated the carbon tax, a $2-billion-a-year cut in the tax take. Leyne writes that later price hikes erased a portion of the savings at the pump, after the federal government eliminated the national carbon tax and left B.C. to follow. Both Eby and the column treat rising grocery bills as the problem this promise is aimed at. The commitment was made Friday in Kelowna.
