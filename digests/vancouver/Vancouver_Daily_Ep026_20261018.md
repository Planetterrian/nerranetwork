# Vancouver Daily News
> **Forty-nine percent of the vote made Melissa Granum Delta's new mayor Saturday night.**

**What You Need to Know:** Melissa Granum is Delta's new mayor after taking 49 percent of the vote Saturday night, ahead of Dylan Kruger and George Harvie. Across Metro Vancouver, unofficial counts also put new mayors in Richmond and Surrey and returned Mike Hurley in Burnaby. Highway 99 paving in Vancouver runs through Monday morning, with a mild, mainly sunny Sunday and no weather alerts.

### Top Stories
**Melissa Granum elected mayor of Delta:** Vancouver Sun
Melissa Granum won the Delta mayoral race with 49 percent of the vote, 14,988 ballots, in results the Vancouver Sun reported at 10 p.m. Dylan Kruger was second with 28 percent, 8,567 votes, and George Harvie third with 23 percent, 6,985. The Sun said the early figures were advance and mail-in votes, and that Granum's lead held as more polls reported. Granum told the Sun there had been "a groundswell of support for hope for the future, and transparent governance and change," and that the campaign had been "about everyday residents working together for change." Source: [vancouversun.com](https://vancouversun.com/news/bc-municipal-election-day-2026-updates-results-reaction)

**Doug McCallum unseats Brenda Locke in Surrey:** Vancouver Sun
Former mayor Doug McCallum unseated incumbent Brenda Locke, taking 31.9 percent of the vote to Locke's 23 percent with all 164 polls reporting, 42,741 votes to 30,886. Locke conceded just before 10 p.m., telling a solemn crowd, "Surrey voters have definitely spoken and this was not the result we counted on," then congratulated McCallum and, asked if she would run again, said, "Never say never." McCallum said, "We will address affordability, we will address your safety, and we will address your infrastructure," and said he planned to appoint Mandeep Nagra as deputy mayor. Mike Starchuk finished with 12.9 percent and Linda Annis with 12.39 percent. Safe Surrey won a council majority; the only councillor not on that slate is incumbent Harry Bains, from Locke's team, who received the most votes of any candidate. The rest of council is incumbents Doug Elford and Mandeep Nagra, former councillors Allison Patton and Laurie Guerra, and newcomers Meera Gill, Suhana Gill and Cheney Cloke. Elford called the result surprising and said residents could expect the slate to carry out its promises, including a four-year property-tax freeze, purchasing a police helicopter and seeking funding for another Surrey hospital, "as best as we can." A Leger poll for Postmedia News, conducted Sept. 23 to 30, had suggested Locke would receive a second term. Advance voting was 52,600 ballots, about 15 percent of registered voters, up 47 percent from 35,701 advance votes in 2022. McCallum previously served as mayor from 1996 to 2005 and from 2018 until his loss to Locke in 2022; Locke won that race by less than 1,000 votes. The Sun reported the two became rivals over policing, with McCallum wanting a municipal force and Locke wanting to keep the RCMP. Source: [vancouversun.com](https://vancouversun.com/news/municipal-election-surrey-results-2026)

### The Closer Look
**Mike Hurley wins third term as Burnaby mayor:** Freshet News
Mike Hurley has won a third term as mayor of Burnaby, and the Burnaby Citizens Association kept a majority on city council. Freshet News reported unofficial results from Saturday's vote. Hurley won a six-way mayoral race with 28,946 votes, 58.43 percent of 50,326 ballots cast. Burnaby First candidate Heymann Yip was second with 9,250 votes, 18.67 percent.

He thanked residents for electing him to another four years and said, "It's an amazing journey, an opportunity to get out there and help people, which is something that I live to do."

The council ballot had 42 candidates and several new parties. The BCA majority held. Three BCA incumbents did not run: Sav Dhaliwal, Pietro Calendino and Alison Gu. Three new BCA candidates were elected in their place: Cindy Lee, Ryan Erwin and Kiran Singh. New Burnaby Party incumbent Richard Lee remains the only non-BCA member of council. Freshet News said it was unable to reach Lee for comment on election night.

Joe Keithley, first elected with the Burnaby Green Party and switched to the BCA in December, led the council count with 18,073 votes, 5.51 percent. Cindy Lee was next with 17,429, 5.32 percent, then Ryan Erwin with 16,476, 5.03 percent. Lee said she had not expected that finish and credited support from her own community and from running with the BCA team. She said many residents had read the candidates' profiles and recognized her while she was door-knocking, and that "in Burnaby, our residents are really responsible. That's why we have a great city."

Incumbent BCA councillors Maita Santiago and Daniel Tetrault each kept a seat for a second term, with 16,292 votes, 4.97 percent, and 15,288, 4.66 percent. Richard Lee also won a second term, with 13,257 votes, 4.04 percent. Kiran Singh took the last council seat, finishing 1,180 votes ahead of independent Allen Chen: Singh had 13,209 votes, 4.03 percent, and Chen 12,029, 3.67 percent.

Voter turnout in Burnaby rose to 29.23 percent of eligible voters, from 19.7 percent in 2022. Source: [freshetnews.ca](https://www.freshetnews.ca/mike-hurley-wins-third-term-as-burnaby-mayor/)
