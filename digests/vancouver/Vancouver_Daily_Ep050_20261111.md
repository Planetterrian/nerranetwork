**Court appoints receiver for Westbank project site in Downtown Vancouver: Daily Hive**
Justice Loo of the B.C. Supreme Court has appointed a receiver over companies tied to developer Westbank at 1668-1684 Alberni Street, at Bidwell, after they failed to repay more than $36 million to the Bank of Montreal. The order is limited until February 13, 2027: the companies keep running two occupied rental buildings, but rental income after expenses goes to the receiver now, and the receiver cannot run a sale before that date if the debt is still unpaid. The site holds a 15-storey building from 1980 and a 14-storey building from 1990, 23 strata units in all. Interest has been missed since at least September 2025, a January 2026 repayment demand went unmet, and interest was running at about $5,150 a day, with more than $300,000 accrued since BMO’s August 12, 2026 petition. Council approved a 40-storey, 128-unit luxury condo rezoning in May 2022 with a $44 million public-benefits package, including $37 million in cash meant in part for a new Vancouver Aquatic Centre; a development permit was withdrawn in September 2025, and the ruling refers to a purpose-built rental revision with no formal city application yet. Loo found no evidence of the land’s value or of wasted assets, and still held that prolonged non-payment, no repayment proposal, and BMO’s contract right justified the receiver; Westbank’s warning that a receivership could default other projects was not backed by evidence in court. Guarantors named in the case are 700 Bidwell Limited Partnership, 700 Bidwell Property Inc., and Westbank Holdings Ltd., beside borrower 700 Bidwell Holdings Inc. **Metro Vancouver and Fraser Valley home sales forecast to rebound in 2027: Daily Hive**
The B.C. Real Estate Association’s fourth-quarter forecast, out today, expects home sales in Metro Vancouver and the Fraser Valley to rise by roughly nine per cent in 2027 after another declining year. Province-wide, it puts 2026 at just over 67,000 sales, down 4.5 per cent from 70,186 in 2025, then 71,840 in 2027, up 7.2 per cent. Chief economist Brendon Ogmundson said a modest recovery should follow as the impact of new United States tariffs fades and fixed mortgage rates decline, while active listings sit near the highest level since 2015. Greater Vancouver Realtors’ average price is forecast at $1.223 million in 2026 and $1.25 million in 2027; the Fraser Valley board’s average is forecast to fall 4.8 per cent to $962,900 in 2026, then rise to $980,000 in 2027, still about $31,800 below 2025. On October 28 the Bank of Canada raised its policy rate by 0.25 percentage points to 2.5 per cent, the first increase since July 2023, citing inflation concerns from oil prices tied to the U.S.-led war in Iran, with its next decision set for December 9. **Chilliwack veteran, 95, recalls dangerous journey to Korean War: Surrey Now-Leader**
Leroy George, 95, of Chilliwack, told an Indigenous veterans ceremony Friday at the K'ómoks First Nation Gym in Courtenay that he married Ruthie on August 7, 1950, after promising he would not enlist, and joined within a week. On the train from Shilo, Manitoba, toward Fort Lewis, he gave up a card seat to soldier Basil Patrick McKeown minutes before the Canoe River crash, which killed 21 people, including 17 soldiers, McKeown, and George’s cousin Austin Emery George. Ruthie was told he was dead, then, after a roll call at Wainwright, Alberta, that he was alive. He served 11 months in Korea, was wounded in the right eye by a shell, and served 40 years in all. “War is hell,” he said. “It’s boring 90 per cent of the time, and 10 per cent of just hell.” Source: [surreynowleader.com](https://surreynowleader.com/2026/11/11/chilliwack-veteran-95-recalls-dangerous-journey-to-korean-war/)

**'Is this the best we can do?': B.C. mom of boy with muscular dystrophy calls for rare disease strategy: Vancouver Sun**
Kasha Mitton, a Kamloops nurse, is in Vancouver this week for the Till and McCulloch Meetings, asking for a national rare-disease strategy so children can reach drugs already approved but still stuck in provincial funding review. Her son Jude, now 14, was diagnosed at four with Duchenne muscular dystrophy; he can no longer walk, which she says cuts his chances of trials that want patients who are still ambulatory. He had been in a trial for vamorolone, the first Duchenne treatment approved in Canada and commercially available since April, which the family still cannot get in B.C. because funding approval is unfinished. They moved to a trial of givinostat, which University of B.C. professor Fabio Rossi says can slow the disease and is in priority review at Health Canada. The Canadian Organization for Rare Disorders says only 60 per cent of rare-disease treatments reach Canada, most up to six years later than in the United States. “We don’t have time to wait. Time is muscle,” Mitton said. Source: [vancouversun.com](https://vancouversun.com/news/bc-mom-of-boy-with-muscular-dystrophy-calls-for-rare-disease-strategy)

### The Closer Look
**Federal budget's $1.3 billion for Eastern Canada ferries sparks calls for more BC Ferries funding: Daily Hive**
The BC Ferry and Marine Workers’ Union wants Prime Minister Mark Carney to explain why last week’s 2026 federal budget assigns $1.3 billion to ferry services in Eastern Canada and no matching sum to BC Ferries. That money runs over eight years from the 2026/2027 fiscal year, renewing the Ferry Services Contribution Program, including new terminals at Wood Islands, Prince Edward Island, and Caribou, Nova Scotia, plus charters for temporary replacement vessels. Union president Eric McNeely said the prime minister wants to build a stronger, more connected Canada, then added, “Apparently, that doesn’t include the Pacific coast.”

The comparison sits on a 1977 agreement, updated in 2005/2006. Transport Canada expects B.C. to receive $37.8 million in 2025/2026 and $39.05 million in each of 2026/2027, 2027/2028, and 2028/2029, with an evaluation set for 2027/2028. The union says Ottawa covers about 43 per cent of operating and maintenance costs for Atlantic ferry services and about three per cent for BC Ferries. Marine Atlantic’s year-round North Sydney to Port aux Basques run, and the Confederation Bridge to Prince Edward Island, meet constitutional links between provinces. BC Ferries, owned by the province and no longer a Crown corporation after 2003, has said its federal infrastructure financing must be repaid, unlike eastern grants and operating funds.

Fares here are already in front of a regulator. In late 2024, BC Ferries leadership warned that operating, labour, and capital costs could equal a single-year increase of up to 30 per cent in 2028. That was a warning, not an approved fare. The commissioner is reviewing the submission for the four years from April 2028. Through March 2028, average annual increases stay capped at 3.2 per cent, after a $500 million provincial contribution in 2023 that avoided a 10.4 per cent hike each year from 2024 to 2028. The network carried about 23.3 million passengers and 10 million vehicles in the year ending March 2026. The union estimates about $8 billion in cargo a year and says higher fares would hit businesses that depend on the ships, and Vancouver Island’s roughly one million residents. McNeely wants premier-elect Lorne Doerkson to press Ottawa for a larger share. On October 26, 2026, the board replaced CEO Nicolas Jimenez with interim leader Brian Anderson and opened a Revitalization Review, without a public reason. Four Summit-class ships, the fleet’s largest capital project at well over a billion dollars, are due from 2029 to 2031 on the major routes from Metro Vancouver to Vancouver Island and the Sunshine Coast, built at a mainland China yard owned by the Chinese government. Source: [dailyhive.com](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/canada-federal-budget-eastern-canada-bc-ferries-funding-criticism)
