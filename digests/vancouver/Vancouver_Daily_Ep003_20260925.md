### Both Sides: Below-market homes in a Cambie rental approval
Vancouver's approval of 229 secured purpose-built rental homes near King Edward Station, with no below-market units, is what the court was asked to undo. The Cambie Corridor Neighbourhood Association sought to rescind the approval. The City of Vancouver and developer Sightline Properties were the respondents. Justice Morellato of the Supreme Court of British Columbia dismissed the association's challenge last week.

The association's case is that the approval should be overturned. Daily Hive reports the bid targeted a low-rise secured rental project after council proceeded with no below-market rental homes included.

The city's case, as the court stated it, is that the process was fair and council's decision was reasonable. Council unanimously approved the rezoning in July 2025. Sightline's reason for building rental, as set out in the ruling, is practical: it had planned 46 townhomes expected to sell for 2 million to 2.3 million dollars each, obtained permits in 2022, demolished the houses and started excavation, then had no pre-sales in a weakening market and shifted to rental after provincial changes that encouraged more density near SkyTrain.

Both sides are dealing with the same project: 520-590 West 29th Avenue and 4510-4550 Ash Street, about a 10-minute walk south of the Canada Line station, with two six-storey buildings and two townhouse blocks. The unit count went from 230 at rezoning to 229 when the development permit was issued in December 2025, and below-market homes were not part of the approval. The dismissal leaves that approval in place. Source: [dailyhive.com](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/520-590-west-29th-avenue-4510-4550-ash-street-vancouver-rental-housing-sightline-cambie-corridor-legal-challenge)
