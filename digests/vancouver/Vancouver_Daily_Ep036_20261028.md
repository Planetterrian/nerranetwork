### The Closer Look
**Dan Fumano: In poignant final council meeting, Vancouver Mayor Ken Sim and most of his party say goodbye and good luck: Vancouver Sun**
Ken Sim presided Tuesday over his last Vancouver city council meeting, the only public session between the October 17 election that defeated him and ABC and the November 2 swearing-in of the new council.

Partway through, he dropped the titles he had used for four years. Instead of Coun. Kirby-Yung, Coun. Dominato and Coun. Fry, he said Sarah, Lisa and Pete. Vancouver Sun columnist Dan Fumano wrote that the change did not read as disrespect, but as warmth toward colleagues his party had campaigned against, sometimes aggressively.

The term opened on a different note. After ABC's 2022 landslide, Sim's party gave some board roles to councillors from outside the team and talked about being post-partisan. By the halfway point, Sim had stripped non-ABC councillors of those extra roles and the pay that came with them. In 2023, 2024 and 2025, politicians elected with ABC resigned from caucus or were ejected after splits over abolishing the elected park board, halting the municipal ethics watchdog's work, and freezing supportive housing construction.

When the campaign began in September, Sim was party to two defamation lawsuits that are still going. He is the plaintiff suing former chief of staff Kareem Allam, who ran against him for mayor. He is a defendant in a suit filed by Coun. Sean Orr.

Before Tuesday's meeting, Sim embraced Orr, who was re-elected with three new councillors from COPE. Asked about that chat, Sim said, "You know, bygones be bygones," and that Orr had stepped up to serve even where their politics differ.

Outgoing councillors then addressed the room. ABC's Lisa Dominato, Sarah Kirby-Yung, Lenny Zhou and Peter Meiszner, all defeated for a second term, and Rebecca Bligh, elected with ABC in 2022 and a mayoral candidate this year against Sim, thanked staff and voters and wished their successors well. Brian Montague, who did not seek re-election, attended and did not speak.

Sim spoke last. He thanked his family, joked, and paraphrased Dickens to his colleagues: it had been the best of times, and sometimes not the best of times, and he was not going to say the worst of times. He said that on his deathbed he would look back on being mayor as the most meaningful thing he had been involved in. The new council is sworn in November 2. Fumano's column notes that Pete Fry, who unseated Sim, will be Vancouver's first Green mayor. ### Both Sides: Metro Vancouver's Friday budget vote
Friday, Metro Vancouver's board meets to work through a 1,190-page agenda and to approve the 2027 budget and five-year financial plan. The board is mayors and councillors from 21 municipalities, each with a weighted vote by the size of their city. The proposed budget calls for nearly $1.7 billion in operating spending, a 7.4 per cent increase from this year, and $2 billion in planned capital spending. The average household's bill for regional water, sewer, garbage and parks is projected to rise to $914 a year from $897.

Surrey mayor-elect Doug McCallum says Friday's October 30 meeting should be cancelled until the new mayors are sworn in. He says that on October 17, voters in 13 of the region's 21 municipalities chose a new mayor, including Vancouver, Surrey, Richmond, Coquitlam, Delta and both Langleys, and that an outgoing board should not set what every household pays. His strongest reason is time and accountability: the board has until next March to adopt its financial plan, so waiting for the November 27 regular meeting should not be a problem. If one item truly cannot wait four weeks, he says, deal with that item and leave the rest. He has asked board chair Mike Hurley and the directors to cancel.

New Westminster mayor-elect Daniel Fontaine says he fully supports that request. He says about two-thirds of the 41 directors were defeated, resigned or did not run again, and that ratifying a significant budget in the dying days of this board is incomprehensible. His case is that people who will not face the voters for these decisions should not lock in a plan the next board may have to manage for four years. He says Metro Vancouver is not under a statutory deadline to approve the budget Friday. He also plans to push an accountability charter, already backed by West Vancouver's mayor-elect Kirk LaPointe and White Rock's mayor-elect Chris Shields, including a full review of cost overruns at the North Shore wastewater treatment plant.

A Metro Vancouver spokesperson said Monday the 2027 budget will be considered and voted on this Friday. The reason given is practical: an October decision gives member jurisdictions time to prepare their own financial plans and to meet legislative requirements for when budgets should be established. The spokesperson said board meetings fall on the last Friday of every month regardless of the election cycle.
