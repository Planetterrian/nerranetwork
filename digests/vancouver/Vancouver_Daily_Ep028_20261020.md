**B.C. Toyota owner battles automaker over $13K bill after transmission fails: Global BC**
Dona Wilkie told Global BC her 2021 Toyota Highlander whined and shifted hard on an August trip to Calgary, and her dealership then said the vehicle, bought new, needed a new transmission. The dealership said she was 600 kilometres past the 100,000-kilometre powertrain warranty. She said an oil-change receipt showed 98,278 kilometres before she left and the noise began near 98,500 kilometres, and the replacement bill was $13,586.88. She said Toyota Canada denied a goodwill request because she had no Toyota-dealership service history and no transmission-fluid records, though she says she kept independent service records. George Iny, director of the Automobile Protection Association, said engine oil-change location is not connected to this failure, that the group has more than 60 complaints about the eight-speed transmission, mainly in the Highlander, and that Toyota Canada issued a February 2021 technical service bulletin for some 2021 Highlander, Camry and Avalon vehicles with UA80E or UA80F transmissions that may whine or grind. Toyota Canada told Consumer Matters the out-of-warranty request was already under review and that, given her situation, it would cover the repair. Wilkie said she had waited weeks and filed a small-claims notice before that call, and she now has a loaner and expects the transmission by the end of the week. ### The Closer Look
**99 B-Line, the busiest bus route in Metro Vancouver, could be renamed in 2027: Daily Hive**
Daily Hive reports that TransLink is discussing a new name for the bus that would replace the 99 B-Line when the Millennium Line's Broadway extension opens in late 2027. The busiest bus route in Metro Vancouver would then run only between the University of British Columbia and the new Arbutus Station. Its segment between Arbutus Street and Commercial-Broadway Station would be discontinued. That would be the route's second major shortening in a 30-year history. BC Transit launched the 99 B-Line in 1996 between UBC and Lougheed Mall, beyond Commercial-Broadway along Lougheed Highway in Burnaby. After the Millennium Line opened in 2002, TransLink shortened it to what is now Commercial-Broadway Station and kept the name. A TransLink spokesperson told Daily Hive that discussions are under way about a potential name change, that no final decision has been made, and that further details will be announced in the coming months. The B-Line brand meant faster, frequent, limited-stop service using 60-foot, three-door buses. The 98 B-Line ran from downtown Vancouver to Richmond on Granville Street from 2000 until the Canada Line opened in 2009. The 97 B-Line, from 2002, linked Lougheed Town Centre Station and Coquitlam Centre until the Evergreen extension in 2016. The 96 B-Line launched in 2013 on 104 Avenue and King George Boulevard between Guildford Town Centre and Newton, and the 95 B-Line ran Hastings Street between downtown and Simon Fraser University's Burnaby Mountain campus. In 2020 TransLink retired the B-Line name on every remaining route except the 99 and introduced RapidBus. The 96 became the R1 King George Boulevard RapidBus and the 95 the R5 Hastings Street RapidBus, joined that year by the R2 Marine Drive, R3 Lougheed Highway and R4 41st Avenue routes. The R6 Scott Road RapidBus followed in 2024. RapidBus added bus-priority lanes on some sections, stop signs with route information, real-time arrival displays and bright green branding. Dozens of articulated buses serve the 99, especially at peak. TransLink has projected the shortened route could reach capacity as soon as the extension opens to Arbutus, because the subway is expected to draw new riders and existing riders from other buses even with a transfer at Arbutus for the remaining trip to UBC. Arbutus Station could become one of the SkyTrain network's busiest stations when the extension opens. TransLink says the 99 now has peak capacity of about 1,800 passengers per hour per direction. With the Broadway extension, Millennium Line capacity is expected to rise to 7,100 passengers per hour per direction, with trains every three minutes at peak. In 2025 the 99 had nearly 10.7 million boardings, averaging 34,700 on a weekday, 20,700 on a Saturday and 15,100 on a Sunday or holiday. When the line is extended from Arbutus to UBC, potentially in the 2030s, the express bus would be fully discontinued. TransLink is also proposing Bus Rapid Transit, with more bus-priority measures and passenger amenities than RapidBus or the B-Line. ### Both Sides: A third hospital for Surrey
What is on the table is a promised third hospital for Surrey, raised in the provincial campaign before general voting day on Saturday, October 24. Surrey is already due a second hospital: the Cloverdale hospital and BC Cancer Centre, a $2.9-billion project with 168 beds that began construction in 2023 and is expected to open in 2030.

The Conservative case, from leader Lorne Doerkson, is that a dedicated women's and children's hospital would spare families an hour or more travelling to BC Children's Hospital and BC Women's Hospital in Vancouver. Doerkson says David Eby is good at announcements and bad at finishing projects, and his party promises the Cloverdale project will be completed on time and on budget. Surrey mayor-elect Doug McCallum, whose Safe Surrey Coalition won a council supermajority, says he will offer the 7.2-acre city site at 6965 King George Boulevard in Newton, previously planned for a community centre, and at the first working meeting after inauguration will dedicate that land and vow to expedite municipal permits within 60 days. He says mothers are sent to other cities to give birth and children are driven across a bridge because Fraser Health has no pediatric intensive care.

The NDP case, from Premier David Eby, is that if the city offers land to expand care in a fast-growing community, the province would work with it, and that asking the wealthiest to pay more is how the government would fund that care. The party points to the Stephens Family School of Medicine at Simon Fraser University's Surrey campus, which welcomed its first class this past summer, and to a $520-million purpose-built medical school building with construction expected to begin in late 2026, plus continued work at Surrey Memorial Hospital. Earlier this year the government cancelled the second phase of the Burnaby Hospital redevelopment after cost estimates ran over budget, then, the day before the snap election call, announced $210 million for that hospital's emergency department and surgical unit, including 16 more emergency treatment spaces and four upgraded operating rooms.
