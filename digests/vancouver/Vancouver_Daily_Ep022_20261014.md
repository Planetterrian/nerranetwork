### Top Stories
**Vancouver advance voting closes near 104,000 after four record days: Daily Hive**
The City of Vancouver's preliminary figures put advance turnout at 103,810 across four days. Tuesday, October 13, drew 29,096 voters, a new single-day early-voting record, after 21,824 on October 3, 25,142 on October 7 and 27,748 on October 10. Daily Hive reports that is about 60 per cent above roughly 65,000 advance voters in the 2022 civic election, and about 61 per cent of the 171,494 ballots cast in that entire election. Advance locations nearly tripled, from 22 to 59, while advance days fell from five to four. The City said voters reported quick visits despite the higher turnout. Main voting day is Saturday, October 17, with 85 locations open from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m.; mail-in ballots must be at a designated drop-off by 8 p.m. that day to count. Voters choose a mayor, councillors, park board commissioners and school trustees, plus a capital-plan borrowing plebiscite and two non-binding public-safety questions on hard-drug enforcement in public and a secure treatment facility. Daily Hive says it is unclear whether the early surge will lift overall turnout. **Appeal court pauses Vancouver Aquatic Centre demolition until November 3: Daily Hive**
B.C. Court of Appeal Justice Dickson has granted an interim injunction halting further demolition of the closed Vancouver Aquatic Centre until November 3, the day after the new park board's inaugural meeting on November 2. Protecting Our Vancouver Aquatic Centre Society and TEAM for a Livable Vancouver announced the order. It does not cancel the redevelopment, require a 50-metre pool, or overturn a September Supreme Court ruling that the City and park board could use $103 million in 2022 voter-approved borrowing for a 25-metre concept. Society director and TEAM park board candidate Jonathan Girard said interim injunctions are among the hardest orders to win, and that the court "made sure the people's representatives still get to decide." The order covers interior demolition, stripping, site preparation, and dismantling of the exterior, interior structures and pool basins. The City and park board may apply to allow hazardous-materials removal that would preserve the building, with two days' notice to the society. A $3.5-million demolition contract went to Heatherbrae Builders in July; the construction contract is not yet awarded. The City had aimed to start major work in late 2026 or early 2027 and open in 2029. The centre opened in 1974, closed June 28, 2026, and had been set for demolition on October 1. The approved concept is a $175-million complex with a 25-metre, eight-lane lap pool, leisure pool, diving pool, hot tub and gym. TEAM, ABC Vancouver and the Vancouver Liberals have promised a 50-metre pool at this Sunset Beach site; the Green Party of Vancouver, Vote Vancouver and OneCity have promised a 50-metre pool elsewhere in the city. ### City & Province
**Locke pressed on what she knew before the police chief's firing: Vancouver Sun**
Days before Surrey's election, mayoral candidate Mike Starchuk is pressing incumbent mayor Brenda Locke on the May 29 closed police-board meeting that moved to terminate then-chief Norm Lipinski. Starchuk said councillor Rob Stutt, of Locke's Surrey Connect party, chaired that meeting and should say whether he told Locke its purpose beforehand. "Either he talked to the mayor about that meeting or he didn't," Starchuk said. Surrey Connect said Locke has no involvement in human-resources matters and that Surrey Police Service staff decisions are made by the police board, independent of the city and the mayor. At a CKNW debate last week, Locke said she knew the board had an important meeting that day and, asked if she knew what was in it, answered, "Not really." Starchuk said that is not a no. Surrey First's Linda Annis said that if elected she will carry out an anti-corruption review, citing resident questions about land purchases, stop-work orders and Lipinski's termination before the RCMP transition was complete. Lipinski was chief from 2020 until June, when the board ended his contract without cause; the board had been in place fewer than 10 weeks. His contract had been renewed in October 2025 for three years, with an option for two more. Board chair Harley Chappell was not at the termination meeting. Late last month Lipinski asked the B.C. Supreme Court to quash the firing as unlawful and motivated by extraneous political considerations. None of those allegations has been tested in court, and no response had been filed as of Tuesday. ### The Closer Look
**Surrey firefighters win Muscular Dystrophy Canada's fire department award: Fire & Safety Journal Americas**
Sixty Surrey firefighters raised $25,000 for Muscular Dystrophy Canada this year, and that work has now drawn a national award. Fire & Safety Journal Americas reports the campaign lifted Surrey Local 1271's lifetime contribution to the charity to $266,000 and earned the Surrey Fire Fighters Charitable Society the 2026 Dr. David Green Award for Fire Department of the Year. The award will be presented to the local at a ceremony in Ottawa in November.

The fundraising is an annual boot-toll drive. Typically over a long weekend, firefighters volunteering with Local 1271's charitable society spread across 20 locations in Surrey and collect at busy commercial sites. Local 1271 president Mike Vincent said the recognition mattered to members and their families. "It hits home with our members," Vincent said. "They bring out their families and their kids and they engage the community. It's not easy asking for money with the way inflation is these days, but once people see you're a firefighter, they're just glad to come by and drop off a donation."

John Watkins, a Local 1271 member who also serves with the charitable society, tied the award to the job itself. "We do this work in the community because we made a commitment to help others when we signed up for this job," Watkins said. "This award is really a testament to the commitment of our members."

The Dr. David Green Awards recognize people and organizations whose dedication has had a lasting impact on Canadians living with neuromuscular disorders. Green, a neurologist, researcher and advocate, co-founded Muscular Dystrophy Canada in 1954 with Arthur Minden and pushed for better care, research, awareness and inclusion. Chief executive Stacey Lintern said Surrey firefighters "lead by example" and have encouraged other British Columbia affiliates of the International Association of Fire Fighters to support the charity. "We're deeply grateful for their partnership in breaking down barriers for people living with neuromuscular disorders, helping expand access to support, strengthen community connections and advance research as we continue the search for cures," Lintern said. Sixth District vice president Mike Carter called the award "a tremendous honor" and a reflection of what those members can do in their communities, and of the union's partnership with the charity.

The same charitable society delivers food to approximately 75 schools, awards student bursaries and has provided around 400 refurbished laptops to local students. Affiliates across the union raised $1.1 million for Muscular Dystrophy Canada last year through boot tolls, rooftop campouts and other community drives.
