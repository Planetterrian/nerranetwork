### Top Stories
**As B.C. scraps time change, experts have a warning about the risks: Global BC**
For the first time in more than a century, B.C. will not turn the clocks back this fall. Vancouver Coastal Health warns the morning commute will be darker than usual and could discourage walking or biking to school or work. The health authority recommends bright colours, reflective gear and eye contact with drivers, and tells drivers to slow down for the conditions. Ralph Mistlberger, a psychology professor at Simon Fraser University, told Global News that humans depend on morning light to set the body’s internal clock, and that later morning light makes it easier to stay up and harder to wake while a fixed social schedule does not move. He co-authored a Canadian Medical Association Journal report on the dangers of year-round daylight time. Patrick Baylis of the University of British Columbia’s School of Economics said the change hits harder when work hours are less flexible and a start time sits near the old sunrise, including a required 8 a.m. start. After November 1, B.C. will be one hour ahead of Washington State. Alberta and Manitoba are also ending the clock change this year. Quebec’s government said Wednesday it plans to scrap the change. Saskatchewan has not changed its clocks since 1966, and Yukon ended the practice in 2020. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12125021/bc-time-change-experts-warn/)

Horgan left a $5.7-billion surplus when he stepped aside in 2022. The deficit is forecast to hit $14 billion this year. **‘Indestructible’ Downtown Eastside toilet suddenly out of service: Global BC**
The City of Vancouver’s Portland Loo at Main and Powell, which opened June 8, is closed for a lock repair less than five months later. Procurement and installation cost $446,145, and annual maintenance is about $95,000. The city said the repair is a lock part or a replacement lock system, that it does not expect a large cost, and that the bill may fall inside the maintenance budget or need extra funding. Six Portland Loos are in Vancouver. The Park Board manages five: two in CRAB Park, two in Columbia Park and one in Coopers’ Park. The city manages the Main and Powell unit. Community member Nat Canuel said a previous self-cleaning toilet there sat broken for years, and that this one, bought in time for the World Cup, lasted four months. People in the neighbourhood say someone had been living in the washroom for about a week before a closed-for-repairs sign appeared. The Park Board had billed the units as almost indestructible. The two in CRAB Park survived more than two years of an encampment. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12124727/indestructible-downtown-eastside-toilet-suddenly-out-of-service/)

**Accessibility in Action: The District's Plan: Education News Canada**

### The Closer Look
**Maple Green's Penny Hill honoured with Dyslexia Canada award: Education News Canada**
Penny Hill, learning support teacher at Maple Green Elementary in Surrey, received a Dyslexia Canada Educational Excellence Award at a school assembly on October 6. Alicia Smith, executive director of Dyslexia Canada, travelled from Ottawa to present it. Angie Calleberg, executive director of curriculum and assessment at the Ministry of Education and Child Care, shared a message of congratulations. The award recognizes educators who advance structured literacy instruction, universal screening and effective intervention, and who support colleagues through leadership and knowledge-sharing.

Hill’s path started in her first year of teaching, when some students responded to her instruction and others did not. In 2003 she attended a workshop in California and learned about dyslexia. “I remember crying,” she said. “I thought to myself, ‘I can help these kids.’” She began tutoring and brought that approach into class, teaching every student as if they were dyslexic, with a focus on what she calls metaphonological awareness. That conclusion became the thesis of her master’s degree in special education, completed under Dr. Linda Siegel, an authority on learning disabilities. Explicit, systematic, evidence-based instruction is “helpful for all, harmful for none and crucial for some,” Hill said.

Screening for early reading difficulties began at Maple Green when Hill joined. Staff screen students with Acadience and DIBELS. She shared the work with colleagues. Former principal Jennifer Pilchak encouraged lunch-and-learn sessions. Hill joked that the encouragement was free lunch. She has presented at conventions in Canada and abroad, guest lectured in university special education classes, led lunch-and-learns, and now mentors a cohort of learning support teachers. Principal Michael Speak said, “This award is so well deserved. Penny has spent her career making sure students with dyslexia are seen, understood and taught in ways that work for them. We are incredibly proud to have her at Maple Green.”

Hill said she has been knocking on the doors of people who control policy for decades. She said she is encouraged that many more people want to know about dyslexia and how to ameliorate its effects. The award names a Surrey teacher whose screening, classroom method and mentoring already shape how colleagues at Maple Green, and the learning support teachers she mentors, teach early reading.
