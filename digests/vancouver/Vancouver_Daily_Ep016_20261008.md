**B.C. asking rents fall for a second year: CBC British Columbia**
Average asking rent in British Columbia fell 2.9 percent in September, CBC British Columbia reports, citing a monthly report from Rentals.ca and Urbanation. That report said the figures mark two full years of annual declines, the longest downturn the Canadian rental market has experienced in recent history. ### Both Sides: A provincial inquiry into the North Shore sewage plant
The decision in front of the provincial government is whether to open its own inquiry into the North Shore Wastewater Treatment Plant. Daily Hive reports the construction budget has risen from 700 million dollars to 3.9 billion, and the construction timeline has been extended by a decade.

City of North Vancouver mayor Linda Buchanan and District of North Vancouver mayor Mike Little want the province to commission an independent inquiry into the plant, and a broader review of how Metro Vancouver manages multibillion-dollar capital projects. Their case starts with the households they represent: their municipalities are among those most affected by the cost increases and by the pressure those increases put on household bills. They are asking for scrutiny that is not limited to a review ordered by the regional government running the project.

Metro Vancouver’s case is that a review is already underway. The regional government has commissioned an independent review and is already examining the escalating costs and the oversight, with findings expected by January 2027. It argues that commissioning another review would create an unnecessary cost for taxpayers. In the same response to election criticism, board chair Mike Hurley, mayor of Burnaby, said claims that Metro Vancouver is out of control rely on outdated or untrue information. The district says less than eight percent of its operating budget goes to non-utility services, and that the Iona Island plant estimate has been cut from 9.9 billion dollars to 6 billion.

Both sides are working from the same overrun: 700 million dollars became 3.9 billion, and the build gained a decade. The dated next step on the books is the regional review’s findings in January 2027. A separate provincial inquiry would be a decision for the provincial government.
