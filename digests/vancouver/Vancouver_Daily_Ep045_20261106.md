**The life of Fry: A portrait of the artist and activist who became mayor of Vancouver: Vancouver Sun**
Pete Fry is Vancouver’s mayor-elect and the city’s first Green mayor, the Vancouver Sun reports, a week after an election-night party a few blocks from where a passerby stopped him on Burrard Street. The Consulate General of Ireland in Vancouver congratulated the city’s first Irish-born mayor since Robert Alexander Anderson, who was born in Armagh in 1856. ### The Closer Look
**It can cost thousands to get rid of an old RV in B.C., so people are dumping them: Vancouver Sun**
On October 31, a travel trailer appeared overnight in a cornfield off a quiet country road in Abbotsford. Neighbours later remarked that someone had taken care to back it neatly into the field before driving away. The Vancouver Sun reports that abandoned recreational vehicles are showing up in fields, forests, rest areas and parks because disposing of an old RV in British Columbia is difficult and costly.

Phil Pires, a junk-removal expert who owns Big Phil’s Rubbish Removal and specializes in RV recycling, says people think the units have scrap value, but they do not. The only metal is the frame, and the whole unit has to be dismantled to reach it: sawing through siding and layers of insulation, separating Fiberglas from wood and upholstery, and draining the grey and black water tanks. Landfills may take some pieces, while recyclers take others. Tanks that once held septic waste have to be handled carefully, as do old fridges and appliances that might contain ammonia. Pires says that work is expensive. Disposal of a small camper, including the kind that fits on the back of a truck, typically runs about one thousand dollars. Large trailers can cost several thousand dollars. If the RV is worth fixing and he can find a buyer, he charges for removal, accepts legal ownership and then sells it for refurbishing. In other cases, owners balk. Some pay a company about two hundred dollars to take a unit away. Pires says if that seems too good to be true, it usually is. He recalled a woman in a Maple Ridge community Facebook group offering several hundred dollars to have an old boat removed. He gave her a quote; she did not call back. Several days later, a neighbour saw the boat abandoned on the side of the highway in Deroche. Police investigated. Pires said he does not think she was charged.

Abbotsford police Sergeant Paul Walker says illegal dumping is not typically a police matter. Officers may get involved if the vehicle is stolen, connected to a crime or presents a public safety risk. RVs have a VIN, but tracing it is complicated because people sometimes do not update the registration when they sell. Walker says that in most cases these abandoned RVs and vehicles are uninsured and, quite often, have been off insurance for many years, and that proving who dumped one is challenging.

Abbotsford is home to a large number of RVs, old and new, and several large dealerships. Until recently there were three RV encampments in highway rest areas and truck parking lots. At one time close to eighty old vehicles were in the camps, some abandoned before being occupied by people looking for shelter. The province closed the camps as construction on Highway 1 progressed. In a statement, the City of Abbotsford said bylaw officers responded to four reports of abandoned vehicles in the last year, including two RVs, a trailer and a boat. When the provincial government closed the camps, the city responded to three calls related to occupied RVs on city land and worked to connect the occupants with support services. Abandoned RVs have also been an issue in the Chilliwack River Valley, where seven RVs were removed from the forest near the Chilliwack River in January during a cleanup of abandoned encampments. Patti MacAhonic, director of Electoral Area E, said crews seem to be getting the upper hand, although she has accepted the problem will never be solved. The best they can do, she said, is manage it. Source: [vancouversun.com](https://vancouversun.com/news/it-can-cost-thousands-to-get-rid-of-an-old-rv-in-bc-so-people-are-dumping-them)

**Jets carry momentum from ‘Miracle in Montreal’ to dominate Canucks: TSN**
The Winnipeg Jets beat the Vancouver Canucks 6-2 on Thursday night before 13,312 at Canada Life Centre, scoring twice in the first four minutes. Dawson Mercer scored his first goal as a Jet and added an assist; Mark Scheifele had a goal and an assist, and Mario Ferraro, Neal Pionk, Alex Iafallo and Cole Koepke also scored, with Stuart Skinner making 24 saves. Brock Boeser and Liam Ohgren scored for Vancouver, and Leevi Merilainen stopped 23 of 29 shots. Winnipeg is 6-8-3 after a nine-game losing streak and a 5-4 overtime win in Montreal on Tuesday, when the Jets erased a four-goal deficit in the final 2:39. Vancouver fell to 3-14-1 and has one win in its last fifteen games. Ohgren, 22, scored his fourth goal in five games and has six on the season, tied with Boeser and Jonathan Lekkerimaki. Head coach Manny Malhotra said it was a lack of effort and compete in certain departments, and that the Jets played a big, heavy game without the pushback to match it. The Canucks are at Edmonton on Saturday night; the Jets host Tampa Bay on Sunday.
