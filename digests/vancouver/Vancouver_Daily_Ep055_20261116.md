### The Closer Look
**Melting glaciers a golden opportunity for one of B.C.'s biggest rock hounds: Vancouver Sun**
Adam Kelliher, 36, spends much of his summers on a mining claim on the peak of Mount Breakenridge, near the northeast corner of Harrison Lake, taking semi-precious stones off ground a glacier used to cover. The Vanderhoof native owns Rubble, Rock and Gems at 250 Southwest Marine Drive in Vancouver, one of the biggest gem and fossil stores in Canada. He says that glacier has receded by at least three metres in the past year, opening virgin pockets of quartz and citrine that weathering and the freeze-thaw cycle have not damaged.

He majored in environmental science at Langara College, with philosophy electives, and told the Vancouver Sun he had to become an artist because neither of those got him a job. Fifteen years later he has left what he calls his starving-artist era. The climate tension is still there. “I was an environmental studies student. So I care about the climate. But at the same time, I’m a bit like, ‘Let’s gooo glacier!,’” he said. He added that he has not “lost my soul where I sold out solely for money.”

Quartz from the King Citrine Mine at Harrison is shot with green needles of actinolite. He says the rare natural yellow citrine often has an amethyst on top — a sceptre he says does not occur anywhere else in the world — and describes a hole a person can sit in, filled with thousands of crystals the size of fingers.

Chloé Monty, a Simon Fraser University master’s student in glaciology, led a study that found the Whistler, Horstman and Blackcomb glaciers have lost between 60 and 95 per cent of their area and are projected to be gone by the turn of the century. She said the Whistler Glacier will likely last a decade, Horstman will vanish in the next 10 to 50 years, and Blackcomb within the next 20 to 70 years. “It was shocking to see how much the glaciers have shrunk when compared to images from the 1970s,” Monty said. “They are shrinking almost in real time when glacier change should be nearly imperceptible on human time scales.”

University of Northern B.C. professor Brian Menounos tracked a faster loss of glacier ice in B.C. and Switzerland with a lidar study, tied to a climate-driven feedback loop. In B.C., soot from wildfires darkens the ice, raises the surface temperature and speeds the melt. Switzerland has lost nearly 20 per cent of its total ice volume in the last five years, with dust from Saharan storms doing similar work. Brent Ward, a professor in SFU’s department of earth sciences, said early spring heat waves now melt the snow quickly, so later summer warmth melts ice directly, worsened here by fire soot. He called that a cascading hazard. Only a few alpine glaciers remain from the Cordilleran ice sheet that covered this region 17,000 years ago and stretched 120 kilometres south of Seattle. Ward said the glacier on Kelliher’s claim looked unhealthy, and the lack of snow on its surface indicated rapid mass loss. In Switzerland, receding ice has uncovered hidden bunkers, munitions and bombs from the First World War. ### Sports
**Canucks defeat Golden Knights to end seven-game skid: NHL.com**
The Vancouver Canucks beat the Vegas Golden Knights 6-3 on Sunday at T-Mobile Arena and ended a seven-game losing streak. Filip Chytil and Linus Karlsson each scored twice. Forward Elias Pettersson scored and had an assist, and Brock Boeser had two assists. Leevi Merilainen made 34 saves on 37 shots in his 12th start in the Canucks’ last 15 games. Vancouver is 4-17-1, and 2-8-0 in its past 10. The team came in with one win in its last 18 games, had not won in regulation since October 3, and had lost eight straight to the Golden Knights. Coach Manny Malhotra said it was really good to hear the win song after a 60-minute effort. Vegas coach Ryan Craig, asked when things went wrong, said “Puck drop.” Jonathan Lekkerimäki set up Pettersson’s power-play one-timer for the first goal. Chytil’s first goal of the night was his first since October 16, 2025, against Dallas; his second was the first short-handed goal of his career, off a stretch pass from Jamie Oleksiak. Mark Stone’s power-play goal was his 417th point with Vegas, tying Jonathan Marchessault for the franchise record, and Drew O’Connor answered 24 seconds later. Karlsson’s empty-net goal closed it. Carter Hart made 21 saves. Vegas is 13-6-3, and a six-game point streak ended. Pettersson has 23 points in 22 games; Boeser has 20. Chytil said the team would enjoy the win until midnight, then focus on the Dallas Stars on Tuesday. ### Both Sides: City promises and municipal limits
What is in dispute is not one bylaw vote today. It is whether the promises new Metro Vancouver mayors made — a four-year property-tax freeze, a police helicopter, pools, SkyTrain expansion — are work these councils can do, or pledges that sit outside city money and city powers. The Vancouver Sun reports that two-thirds of the region’s 21 mayors are first-timers, including nine who defeated incumbents.

Surrey Mayor Doug McCallum’s case is that voters hired the council to act. On Monday, a new council made up almost entirely of his Safe Surrey Coalition adopted 16 priorities, including a property-tax freeze, SkyTrain expansion and buying a police helicopter, plus aerial drones for police, four new community swimming pools, and an end to some recreation fees and business licences, all while freezing property taxes for four years. “The people of Surrey did not elect this council to study things. They elected us to get things done,” McCallum said. “Every commitment we made during the election is now a priority of council.” Langley Township Mayor Jay Lundgren, at his November 2 swearing-in, said voters “asked for change,” and “It’s here,” “because something had gone wrong with the way this place was being run.” Vancouver Mayor Pete Fry, who upset Ken Sim, told mostly rookie councillors, “We’re going to do things a little differently,” and warned of turbulence.

Surrey Coun. Harry Bains, the only opposition councillor, questioned whether some of those priorities are appropriate or achievable. “I haven’t seen a request from the police board for a helicopter,” he said, and said city hall needs to separate itself from the operational management of the Surrey police board. Burnaby Mayor Mike Hurley, re-elected to a third term as an independent who must work with eight party-aligned councillors, said several mayoral candidates campaigned on health or education, which provincial or federal governments oversee. “I think we need to be very careful about not kidding ourselves that we can make big changes,” he said. “It’s important that local government learn to stay in their lane.” Former North Vancouver City mayor Darrell Mussatto, mayor from 2005 to 2018, said, “The reality is it’s much more difficult than you realize,” and that big-ticket promises are easy to make and harder to keep. New Westminster Mayor Daniel Fontaine, who ousted an incumbent, said cities collect just pennies of every tax dollar and are financially limited, though his council passed a 100-day plan to add garbage bins, repair sidewalks, ease traffic bottlenecks and eliminate restaurant patio fees.
