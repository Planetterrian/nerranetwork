### Top Stories
**Cyberattack may have leaked student and parent information, Burnaby school district says: The Globe and Mail**
The Burnaby School District says a ransomware attack on September 21 hit school websites and portals, internal network services and phone lines. In an online notice, the district says hackers copied and stole files that may have held lists of parent, guardian and student names and contact information, plus birthdates, grades and school names. The notice says the copied material may also include registration forms, kindergarten assessment notes and detailed lists of students on specialized learning plans. CBC reports the district now says the compromised material may include identifying documents and medical information, including medical diagnoses. The district says the RCMP and B.C.'s privacy commissioner have the matter, that system security is being evaluated, and that staff, students and families have been offered registration with TransUnion's fraud alert service. The notice asks people to be wary of suspicious communications and says the district will update the community as the investigation proceeds. ### City & Province
**Vancouver Aquatic Centre 25-metre rebuild a First Nation’s land claim issue: Park Board chair: Global News**
Park Board chair Tom Digby, who is running for re-election as a Vancouver Green Party commissioner and was chair when the board decided to demolish the aging Vancouver Aquatic Centre, told Global News the centre cannot be rebuilt as a 50-metre pool because of unsettled land claims. The Park Board has said a 50-metre pool would need a larger footprint to meet modern safety and competition rules. Digby said the province owns the land and will not allow that expansion, mainly because land claims with First Nations on the foreshore are unsettled. Musqueam Chief Wayne Sparrow confirmed a request to expand the footprint and said the province sent a letter to MST Development Corporation, the partnership of the Musqueam Indian Band, Squamish Nation and Tsleil-Waututh Nation, wanting a lease to the city. Sparrow said the province was proposing to lease the land for as little as one dollar, and asked what Musqueam's accommodation would be. He said there has been no meeting yet with the province and MST, and that one could still happen. Protecting Our Vancouver Aquatic Centre won an interim injunction pausing demolition until at least November 3, the day after the first scheduled Park Board meeting. Group director and TEAM park board candidate Jon Girard said that if the facility is renovated or rebuilt, it needs to stay a 50-metre pool. ### The Closer Look
**B.C. Election: Eby, Doerkson and Lowan spar over economic vision in televised leader's debate: Vancouver Sun**
NDP Leader David Eby, Conservative interim leader Lorne Doerkson and Green Leader Emily Lowan debated for almost two hours Wednesday night in downtown Vancouver, in the only televised leaders' debate before the October 24 provincial election. Moderator Shachi Kurl, president of the Angus Reid Institute, led them through the topics. The Vancouver Sun reports the exchange was long on policy. Voting by mail has to be delivered by the October 17 deadline, a related note in that coverage says.

Eby was challenged at the outset for calling a snap election two years after the last vote, with his party holding a majority. He said that with a one-seat majority, appointing the speaker leaves the house tied, so every vote could bring down the government. He said this was not a government able to deliver the big swings families need, and that the province needs representatives from every part of B.C. focused on priorities, not culture wars or repealing the human rights code. Doerkson said the election is about Eby's decade in the legislature, that nothing is better than years ago, and that he will lead with respect and dignity and expect that from his caucus. He told CityNews he does not want to introduce a platform he breaks promises on. Lowan, described by one report as the youngest and only woman on the stage, said she is a renter who has done minimum-wage work, and that the Greens have a plan on the cost of living and climate through sustainability and community-driven growth. If the Greens held the balance of power, she said, her top priority would be a better voting system, which she located in proportional representation.

On the books, Eby cited hospitals and schools for the projected deficit and said he would address wasteful spending. He said spending had been essential and that the budget could be balanced without burdening residents. His plan, as the Sun reports it, would raise taxes on individuals making over one hundred ninety thousand dollars a year to fund expanded social services, and give a temporary break on the ten-cent-per-litre fuel tax. He attacked the Conservative plan to raise the amount people can earn before paying income tax to thirty-two thousand dollars, which some economists say could cost the treasury up to four billion dollars, and said Conservatives cut when they need money. Doerkson criticized spending, including a previously proposed one-thousand-dollar rebate that was retracted, and said he would supercharge the economy with that tax change, a tripling of LNG production by 2035, fewer regulations, and a temporary removal of the ten percent PST on Canadian liquor. He argued for forestry and LNG. Lowan said money was going into resource extraction without a sustainable path, and she wants value-added local industry. She proposed a tax on ultra-wealthy households to fund social services. Eby said he had created thousands of daycare spaces and that more progress is needed. On health care, Lowan said he had not met commitments on access to family doctors. Eby stressed recruiting professionals and pointed to demographic challenges ahead.

On ICBC, Doerkson proposed replacing the no-fault system with a hybrid model. Eby pointed to losses he said followed when personal-injury lawyers were in the system. On Indigenous rights, Eby reaffirmed partnership with Indigenous communities. Doerkson pledged to repeal the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act, arguing it has bred division. Lowan defended the act and said the work is to realize it, not dismantle it.

Doerkson was asked about social-media reposts by campaign manager Katy Merrifield, including one calling Islam a literal rape culture and another calling New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani a Jihadi-loving mayor. He said she was reacting to events in the U.K. as a survivor of sexual assault, has apologized and deleted the reposts, and will stay. He has already removed one candidate over a post targeting pro-Palestinian protesters. Eby said her feed at points reads like the worst corners of the dark web, and that Doerkson was standing against a call from national Muslim organizations that people need to feel safe. Lowan said the exchange felt like the 2024 race, with a Conservative party divided by candidates' comments and what she called a managed decline under Eby. Asked if he could work with the Conservatives to avoid another election, Eby said he struggles to see common ground, that he is not perfect, and that he will act in the best interests of British Columbians.

CentreBC leader Elenore Sturko, in video remarks, said she is not asking voters to pretend CentreBC will form government, and asked who will hold the next government accountable. She called her party socially progressive, environmentally pragmatic and fiscally responsible. OneBC leader Dallas Brodie, also in video remarks, said her party will push a twenty-five percent income-tax cut for individuals and corporations, a two percent PST cut, and a fight against what she called land-back giveaways and the reconciliation industry.
