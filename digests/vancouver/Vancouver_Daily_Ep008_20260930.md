### Both Sides: The drug law and a Vancouver compassion club
A B.C. judge has dropped drug charges against a Vancouver compassion club and ruled the Controlled Drug and Substance Act unconstitutional because it prevents drug users from accessing a safe supply, the Vancouver Sun reports. CBC reports a B.C. Supreme Court judge tossed drug-trafficking convictions against two harm reduction activists who argued they were doing it to reduce the harms caused by toxic drugs.

The strongest case for the club is the one those activists made, as CBC reports it. They argued the point of the supply was to reduce the harms caused by toxic drugs. The Sun's account of the ruling carries that argument to a legal result: the judge found the Act unconstitutional because it blocks drug users from a safe supply.

The strongest case for leaving the statute in place, on the record these reports actually contain, is the court's own pause. The Vancouver Sun says the Supreme Court ruling is put on hold for six months to allow the federal government to change the Controlled Drug and Substance Act. That delay is attributed to the court in the Sun's account. It is the mechanism that keeps the statute available to amend before the judgment takes effect.

What both accounts accept is that operators of a Vancouver compassion club faced drug charges under that federal Act, and that a B.C. court has now ruled for them on safe supply. The next step the Sun names is that six-month window for the federal government to change the Act.
