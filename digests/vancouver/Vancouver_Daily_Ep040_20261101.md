### The Closer Look
**The 10 must-see concerts in Vancouver this November: Vancouver Sun**
November in Vancouver is packed with dated shows, and the Vancouver Sun's list treats the month as the last stretch before holiday songs take over the speakers. The paper notes that early fall is harvest and Halloween, that decorations go up almost as soon as the jack-o'-lantern candle goes out, and that Remembrance Day is the month's most solemn mark. Against that, it offers 10 shows, most of them with a night, a time, and a room.

The Jade Music Festival runs November 3 to 8 at various times and enters its fifth year larger than ever. Its stated goal is elevating Chinese-language music in Canada and around the world. The Sun points to Vancouver-based jazzy R&B artist Kristin Fung as one way in, among Canadian and international acts working cross-cultural traditions.

Crazy Diamonds, a Vancouver Pink Floyd tribute with two decades of live work, plays November 14 at 8 p.m. The Sun's case for a tribute is that seeing Pink Floyd itself is something no one will experience again, because the surviving members rarely perform live. Tyla plays November 15 at 8 p.m. at the Doug Mitchell Thunderbird Sports Centre at UBC. The Sun calls her the youngest South African artist in history to win a Grammy, and the "Queen of Popiano," a hybrid of pop and South African amapiano beats. It cites this year's album A*Pop and songs Chanel and That Girl, and says the live show is full of dance.

Art d'Ecco, the Vancouver Island singer presently appearing in the Crave comedy series I Kill The Bear, plays November 20 at 8 p.m. at the Rickshaw Theatre, 254 East Hastings. The new album is titled Scheele's Green, after a colour first mixed by artist Carl Wilhelm Scheele in 1775, and the Sun says the release finds him rocking hard with his backing band. On November 25 at 8 p.m., the Vogue Theatre at 918 Granville Street hosts an all-ages bill led by Gwar, with Midnight, Mac Sabbath, and Nailed Shut also on it. The Sun calls it a loud, sweaty night.

The Pointed Sticks play a farewell, billed Last Hurrah, on November 28 at 8 p.m. The Sun calls them a legendary Vancouver New Wave group that put out some of the best singles of that era, skirted global success, later reunited for new records and tours to Europe, Japan, and elsewhere, and is now bidding fans farewell one last time. The same date, November 28, from 6 p.m. to 2 a.m., DJ Tiësto headlines an all-ages EDM festival at Tradex, 1190 Cornell Street in Abbotsford, with Aaron Hibell, Benwal, Darude, Dash Berlin, Matty Ralph, and Modeā. On November 29 at 8 p.m., Edmonton-based tenor saxophonist Jacob Do leads a band at Frankie's Jazz Club, 755 Beatty Street, around his debut album First Chorus on MacEwan University's Bent River Records. The Sun says he was already an award-winning musician before he graduated, and names alto saxophonist Steve Kaldestad, pianist Tilden Webb, bassist Dan Howard, and drummer Joe Poole. One listing in the same piece, Bonobo, is dated March 16 at 8 p.m., not November, with a new album and a live show designed by stage artist Pierre Claude. Source: [vancouversun.com](https://vancouversun.com/entertainment/the-10-must-see-concerts-in-vancouver-this-november)

### Sports
**Matthews, Nylander lead Leafs to win over Canucks: Global BC**
The Vancouver Canucks lost 4-2 to the Toronto Maple Leafs on Saturday at home and are 3-12-1, with one win in their last 13 games, a stretch Pacific North Hockey recorded as 1-11-1. Toronto is 8-6-2 and finished a four-game road trip 2-2-0, snapping a two-game skid. William Nylander opened the scoring 83 seconds in, scooping a puck off Jamie Oleksiak's stick for a breakaway and lifting a backhand past Leevi Meriläinen. Brock Boeser tied it on the power play about nine minutes later, his fifth goal of the season, taking a stretch pass from Filip Hronek and beating Anthony Stolarz. Boeser told reporters he and Hronek had been looking for that pass for a few games, and he later said the league is too good to take a period off.

Auston Matthews scored his team-leading 11th at 3:47 of the second, beating Meriläinen short side after a pass with Darren Raddysh, for a 2-1 lead. He came into the night with 10 goals and now has 46 shots through 15 games. Editor In Leaf reported a point in every road game this season, seven goals and 15 points, and an eight-game road point streak, the longest by a Leaf since Doug Gilmour's 12-game stretch in 1993-94. Zack MacEwen, who played 62 games for Vancouver, scored the winner at 12:34 of the second. Daily Hive and Canadian Press described a point shot that came off Meriläinen's glove, hit Luke Schenn in the back, and rolled in. Teddy Blueger, 198 games as a Canuck, assisted on that goal and scored the empty-netter at 18:24 of the third after Stolarz tried to fire the puck up the ice. Nylander and Blueger each finished with a goal and an assist. Dakota Joshua, back for his first game of the season after a core injury and a veteran of 212 Canucks games, had five shots and seven hits. Chris Tanev, 537 games in Vancouver, played his first game since an upper-body injury on October 10 at Colorado. He told Sportsnet's Dan Murphy he was nervous coming back after three weeks off against a team that works hard and is desperate for a win.

Max Sasson scored his fourth of the season at 9:27 of the third, off the rebound of a Brendan Gallagher shot after Paul Cotter gained the zone, to make it 3-2. Sasson said the fourth line with Cotter and Gallagher is trying to be simple. Meriläinen made 24 saves in a fourth straight start and stopped first-overall pick Gavin McKenna on a first-period power play, a cross-ice pass from Jack Roslovic. Stolarz stopped 26 of 28 shots for his fifth win, with a 2.66 goals-against average and a .910 save percentage, and picked up his third assist of the season. Even-strength shot attempts were 22-12 Vancouver in the first and 25-12 Toronto in the second. The Leafs outshot the Canucks 13-6 in the middle frame. It was the third time this season Vancouver outshot an opponent overall. Six of the Canucks' last eight losses have been by two goals or fewer.

Elias Pettersson's six-game point streak ended, a run of two goals and five assists. He drew three penalties, blocked five shots, and threw three hits, but went 7-for-25 on faceoffs, 28 percent. He told Postmedia he was not too good with the puck, made too many errors, and was terrible in the faceoff dot. No NHLer has taken more faceoffs than he has this season: 337 through 16 games, at a 45 percent win rate, and he has no five-on-five goal since January. Head coach Manny Malhotra said Pettersson is frustrated on offence but is blocking shots and recovering pucks with more jam, and that the offence will come. On the loss, Malhotra said there is progress, but it is tough to see when there is no correlation to wins, and he pointed to breakouts through the neutral zone in the second. Boeser said the team is generating more shots and the defensive game is coming along. Sasson said it is tough in the room, and that the group has to stick with the process. Malhotra said every game Meriläinen has played this year he has given them an opportunity to win, and that the run of starts is because of that.

Centre Jacob Quillan, acquired October 15 in a trade from Toronto and recalled Friday from Abbotsford of the AHL, made his Canucks debut in place of Filip Chytil, who was placed on injured reserve after reaggravating a shoulder injury in Thursday's 3-2 loss at the San Jose Sharks. Malhotra said Quillan made poised plays on breakouts and had a few good forechecks, and that one game is not a full analysis. Jack Roslovic earned an assist on Matthews's goal and extended a six-game point streak to three goals and seven assists. Toronto has 28 wins in 86 games in Vancouver. Leafs coach Jim Hiller said the team has been too inconsistent, and that the second period was as good a forecheck as they have had all year. After the win, Sportsnet reported Nikita Kucherov had requested a trade from Tampa Bay amid a contract stalemate with general manager Julien BriseBois, who has said he has no intention to trade the 33-year-old. The two sides remain millions apart. Elliotte Friedman reported that Toronto and Montreal would be considered among Canadian landing spots. Darren Raddysh, a former Lightning teammate, said he wished both sides the best. Vancouver hosts the Anaheim Ducks on Tuesday. Toronto hosts the Utah Mammoth on Tuesday.
