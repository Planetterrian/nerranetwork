### Both Sides: Two election days, one week apart
What is being decided is the calendar in front of B.C. voters. CBC reports that candidates say overlapping elections are causing confusion. On October 17, voters elect mayors, councillors, school board trustees and other local officials. On October 24, a provincial vote elects 93 members of the legislature, which will determine B.C.’s next premier. Global News reports the same two dates and describes the dual elections as a headache for some B.C. voters.

The strongest case that the overlap is a problem comes from those candidates. Their reason, as CBC reports it, is that people are being asked to track two elections at once, for different offices, seven days apart. Global News puts that cost on voters who are already finding the pair of dates a headache, with local offices one week and the legislature the next.

Vaughn Palmer reports in the Vancouver Sun that the B.C. NDP leader sidestepped repeated questions about election timing. What CBC and Global both state, and what neither account disputes, is the calendar itself: local officials on October 17, then 93 legislature seats and the next premier on October 24. Those two dates are the next step.
