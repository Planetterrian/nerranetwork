**VIFF documentaries highlight Terry Fox and Rick Hansen: CityNews**
Two documentaries at the Vancouver International Film Festival look at British Columbians Terry Fox and Rick Hansen. Run Terry Run, directed by Sean Menard and running 108 minutes, follows Fox’s Marathon of Hope from April 12, 1980, in St. John’s, Newfoundland, aimed at raising $1 million for cancer research, through 143 days of running. It screens at the Vancouver Playhouse on Sunday, October 4, at 1:45 p.m., and at International Village on October 5 at 5:30 p.m. Man in Motion: The Rick Hansen Story, directed by Adrian Buitenhuis and running 96 minutes, follows Hansen’s 1985 journey and the 40,000-kilometre trek he completed. It shows at the Vancouver Playhouse on Saturday. Source: [vancouver.citynews.ca](https://vancouver.citynews.ca/2026/09/30/viff-documentaries-terry-fox-and-rick-hansen/)

### Both Sides: The Vancouver Aquatic Centre pool
CBC British Columbia reports that the Vancouver Aquatic Centre may get a 50-metre pool after all, and it links that possibility to the election. The rebuild had plans to build a 25-metre pool. Parties vying for votes are now promising to build a full-sized competition pool as part of the same Vancouver Aquatic Centre rebuild.

The case for the larger pool, as CBC reports it, is that campaign promise: parties seeking votes say they will build a full-sized competition pool, the pool CBC describes as 50 metres, inside the rebuild. That promise is a promise in a campaign, not a pool already approved in the item CBC published.

The case for the shorter pool is the plan already on the project. CBC reports that the rebuild was set to build a 25-metre pool, and it sets the new promises against that plan. The standing design, on CBC’s account, is the 25-metre tank, and the competition-length pool is what those parties are offering to change it to.

The fact both positions share, in that report, is the rebuild itself: the 25-metre plan and the full-sized promise are about the same Vancouver Aquatic Centre project. What is not yet a decision, on CBC’s wording, is the larger pool: the centre “may” get it, and the parties are “promising” it while they vie for votes. Source: [cbc.ca](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/vancouver-aquatic-centre-50-metre-pool-9.7362607?cmp=rss)
