**Police investigating after a man's body was found on Highway 1 in Burnaby: CityNews Vancouver**
Burnaby RCMP says it is investigating after a man's body was found lying in the eastbound lanes of Highway 1 just east of Kensington on Monday morning. Officers were called shortly before 6 a.m. Mounties said the man was obviously deceased and had injuries consistent with being hit by a vehicle. Police closed all eastbound lanes so evidence could be preserved; the lanes stayed closed for more than twelve hours and have since reopened. Mounties say it is possible the man was killed by a vehicle that then left the scene. BC Highway Patrol Sgt. Michael McLaughlin said officers are investigating all possibilities and asked anyone who saw anything unusual near Kensington between 5:30 a.m. and 6 a.m. to call, including dash-cam video that may not show a collision, at 604-526-9744. Source: [vancouver.citynews.ca](https://vancouver.citynews.ca/2026/10/06/highway-1-burnaby-closed-eastbound-incident/)

**BC Greens propose a wealth tax on fortunes over $50 million: Daily Hive Vancouver**
BC Greens leader Emily Lowan announced a proposed Fair Share Act ahead of the October 24 provincial election. The party says it would apply a one-time tax on net wealth above fifty million dollars, at five percent above that line, ten percent above one hundred million, and twenty percent above one billion, then an annual tax from the second year of two percent, three percent, and five percent at those same thresholds. Lowan says the money would support universal childcare, free public transit, and affordable housing, and would also pay down provincial debt. The party estimates twenty-two point two billion dollars in the first year and five point four billion dollars a year after that, and says the estimate accounts for administrative costs and changes in taxpayer behaviour. Lowan said families keep hearing that the money is not there, and that it sits with people who make more in their sleep than a worker makes in a year. Daily Hive reports that critics call the rates excessive, a deterrent to investment, and a risk that some households would owe large bills without cash on hand. Source: [dailyhive.com](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-greens-wealth-tax-fair-share-act)

### Both Sides: A local role on Pacific Link
Prime Minister Mark Carney has designated the proposed Pacific Link oil pipeline a project of national interest. It would carry an additional one million barrels of Alberta oil a day toward a possible marine export terminal at Roberts Bank in Tsawwassen, largely following the existing Trans Mountain corridor into the eastern Lower Mainland. Ottawa is aiming to finalize the conditions needed to proceed by September 2027, while proponents work on route planning, environmental studies, cost estimates, and public and Indigenous consultations. The civic election is Saturday, October 17.

Vancouver Mayor Ken Sim, of ABC Vancouver, says his party supports projects that expand access to overseas markets, attract investment, and cut reliance on any single trading partner. He calls the pipeline a federal matter, says it would not pass through Vancouver, and says the city will not hire lawyers to try to block it. Surrey Mayor Brenda Locke, of Surrey Connect, calls the designation an important step toward diversifying energy exports, creating jobs, and strengthening economic sovereignty, and she points to meaningful Indigenous ownership and a focus on Canadian workers and materials.

Delta Mayor George Harvie, running again with Achieving For Delta, says these projects are significant economic opportunities and that Delta's job is to have a seat at the table and to understand benefits and potential harm together, including Vancouver Fraser Port Authority's separate Roberts Bank Terminal 2. He points to combined pressure on truck and train traffic, roads, transit, public services, farmland, and the environment, and says that stance is not anti-development. Tsawwassen First Nation has said it wants to be consulted directly because of possible impacts on its lands. All three mayors have welcomed the prospect of investment and jobs. Source: [dailyhive.com](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/metro-vancouver-mayors-pacific-link-pipeline-support)
