# Vancouver Daily News
> **A 19-year-old woman is dead after an e-scooter hit a bus on Seymour Street.**

**What You Need to Know:** A 19-year-old woman is dead after an e-scooter and a transit bus collided on Seymour Street in Vancouver. Police say she and a man were riding the scooter northbound when they hit the side of a westbound bus head-on, and the Vancouver Sun reports neither rider was wearing a helmet. Wind warnings, Highway 10 in Surrey, and more storm systems are the other immediate problems for anyone moving around the South Coast this morning.

### Top Stories
**19-year-old woman killed in collision between e-scooter and transit bus in Vancouver: CBC British Columbia**
Police say a man and a woman were riding an e-scooter northbound on Seymour Street when they collided head-on with the side of a westbound transit bus. The woman, 19, was killed. The Vancouver Sun reports that neither the victim nor the man riding with her on the scooter was wearing a helmet. CBC's account is the collision itself: two people on one scooter, a transit bus travelling west, and a head-on strike into the side of that bus on Seymour. The Sun's added fact is the helmets. Neither outlet's copy in today's file names the bus route, the hour, or whether anyone else was hurt. **It's not over yet: B.C. Hydro warns of more storm systems on the way: Vancouver Sun**
After B.C. was hit by wet, windy weather on Friday, B.C. Hydro says to expect more challenging weather in the days ahead. The Vancouver Sun's headline puts it plainly: it is not over yet, and more storm systems are on the way. Global News reports that as storms continue, authorities are warning about high winds bringing more power outages and about the dangers of rising water levels, after what officials called minor flooding from the first of the season's storms. CBC reports that a round of blustery fall weather had already caused ferry cancellations and knocked out power to thousands, and that the South Coast appears set for another blast of wind. Fraser Valley Today reports more than 15,000 customers lacked power in Langley and Surrey on Saturday, and that Langley RCMP were responding to crashes caused by fallen trees. Farther north, CBC reports wind gusts up to 100 km/h in Prince George and the region on Friday night, with trees down on homes and, in one case, a traffic light gone from its location. The local picture this morning is the same system still moving through: outages already counted in the thousands in Langley and Surrey, ferries already cancelled in the earlier round, minor flooding already reported, and Hydro saying the next systems are still ahead. **Whitecaps standing strong for Belal Halbouni in his cancer fight: Vancouver Sun**
The new fact is the contract. Centre back Belal Halbouni revealed Friday that he has been diagnosed with Hodgkin lymphoma, a cancer that affects white blood cells and is considered aggressive, though B.C. Cancer says treatment works well for most people. He is 26. He began treatment last week in his hometown of London, Ontario. He has not played since last fall, after a knee injury at the end of the playoff series against Los Angeles FC that needed surgical repair. He had been a key performer late in the season and in the playoffs while other Whitecaps defenders were hurt. The club and the player had hoped he would be back by now. His deal had been up at the end of this season. The Whitecaps offered a contract that does not stop at the end of the 2027 sprint season next June. It runs through the end of the 2027-28 season. Chief executive Axel Schuster told Postmedia the club does not know when Halbouni will be able to play again, and that doing the right thing for the player did not seem to have been in doubt. Schuster said, in the team's account: "As a club, we have made the decision to support Belal throughout this difficult process by providing him with stability, security, and peace of mind, while also giving him a positive goal to work toward, his return to the training field and to playing in MLS." He added, "We will be here for him every step of the way, and we look forward to welcoming him back when the time is right." Halbouni said in a statement released by the team: "I want to express my gratitude to Vancouver Whitecaps FC. The way this club has stood by me goes far beyond football, and it has reminded me what it truly means to be part of a family. I'm proud to represent this badge, grateful to call Vancouver home, and thankful for the light at the end of the tunnel that they give me." ### City & Province
**$1.7M Vancouver home sells for half the price at tax auction: CBC British Columbia**
CBC reports a Vancouver home valued at $1.7 million sold for half that price at this year's City of Vancouver tax sale. The home was among more than 60 properties auctioned off. The sale was set off by owners who have not paid their taxes for three years in a row. Reserve prices, also called upset prices, were set at the amounts owed to the city. That is the mechanism in the copy: three missed years, a city auction, a floor equal to the tax debt, and at least one house whose assessed figure was $1.7 million changing hands at half that. **'Absolutely' possible: Vancouver, Victoria mayors back Ottawa's bid to end homelessness: CBC British Columbia**
The mayors of Vancouver and Victoria are backing Ottawa's bid to end homelessness, CBC reports. The federal Liberal budget, delivered Thursday, set aside $11.2 billion over the next four years in a bid to end homelessness in the next 10 years. The local stake is that two B.C. mayors, including Vancouver's, have lined up behind that federal timetable and that dollar figure. The money is federal. **Draisaitl nets 2 as Edmonton Oilers dump Vancouver Canucks 4-2: Surrey Now-Leader**
The Oilers beat the Canucks 4-2 on Saturday at Rogers Place. Leon Draisaitl scored twice and is at 16 goals. Mattias Ekholm and Jason Dickinson also scored. Connor McDavid added three assists. Devon Levi made 20 saves in his return from injury. Max Sasson and Brock Boeser scored for Vancouver. Leevi Merilainen stopped 26 of 29 shots. The Canucks are 3-15-1. They have lost five straight and 15 of their last 16. The Oilers are 12-3-3, and Global News reports they are 10-0-2 this season when they score first. They snapped a two-game losing skid. Edmonton opened the scoring 9:14 into the first, Ekholm one-timing a pass from McDavid from the right circle for his third goal of the year. Vancouver tied it with nine seconds left in the period, Sasson tipping a Jamie Oleksiak point shot, his fifth of the season, with Guillaume Brisebois also on the play. Shots after one were 12-4 for Edmonton. The second period was scoreless. Shots after 40 minutes were 21-10 for the Oilers. Dickinson made it 2-1 forty-nine seconds into the third, his second goal of the season, on a rebound off a Ty Emberson shot. Draisaitl made it 3-1 on the power play at the four-minute mark, another McDavid setup, his 15th of the season, with Evan Bouchard also assisting. Vancouver pulled Merilainen with four minutes left. Boeser redirected a Filip Hronek point shot with 2:35 left, his seventh, with Elias Pettersson also credited. With the goalie still on the bench, McDavid fed Draisaitl for an empty-netter with 45 seconds left. Global News reports McDavid's season-opening points and assists streak is now 18 games, matching Adam Oates for the second-longest assist streak in NHL history, behind only Wayne Gretzky's 23 games in 1990-91. McDavid leads the league with 10 goals, 37 assists and 47 points, and he is the sixth player in NHL history with a season-opening 18-game point streak. Coach Manny Malhotra said there were positives in the first two periods, that the attention to detail was there, and that the team had a chance to win going into the third. He said momentary lapses, missed assignments and a missed puck battle add up against a team as high-powered as Edmonton. Winger Jake DeBrusk called it a response to Thursday's loss in Winnipeg and said the record is 15 losses out of 16. Centre Max Sasson said the breakouts looked cleaner and that the team used the middle of the ice better for two periods. The Canucks host the Philadelphia Flyers on Monday at 7:30 p.m. at Rogers Arena. The Oilers host the New York Rangers on Monday. Surrey Now-Leader notes Vancouver is 116-129-19-15 all-time against Edmonton, and that defenceman Tom Willander missed a third straight game with a lower-body injury. Edmonton Elks quarterback Cody Fajardo, who earlier Saturday led his team to a Western Final win over the B.C. Lions, was at Rogers Place. The Edmonton Journal reports that football win came in front of 40,000 at Commonwealth Stadium and sent the Elks to the Grey Cup. On the hockey side, Ryan Nugent-Hopkins left after a puck hit the side of his face on a Ryan Shea dump-in midway through the first. He had played 6 minutes 38 seconds, after missing the first nine games and then putting up 11 points in eight. Oilers coach Mike Babcock said he does not think Nugent-Hopkins will miss time. Babcock said the first worry was a broken jaw, that medical staff assessed him, and that he seemed to be doing OK in the hallway. Babcock also said he would have liked a 6-0 win, and that a closer game was better because Vancouver pushed back and Edmonton had to stick to doing it right to get to a 3-1 lead.
