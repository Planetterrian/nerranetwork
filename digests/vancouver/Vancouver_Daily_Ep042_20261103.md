**Pete Fry sworn in as Vancouver's 42nd mayor promising 'collaboration over division': CBC British Columbia**
Pete Fry was sworn in Monday as Vancouver’s 42nd mayor, the city’s first Green mayor, after defeating incumbent Ken Sim of ABC Vancouver in the October 17 election. OneCity’s Lucy Maloney and ABC’s Mike Klassen are each their party’s only member; both said they looked forward to working with the new council. After the ceremony Fry held a press conference with all 10 councillors, from COPE, OneCity, ABC Vancouver and the Green Party. Park board commissioners and school board trustees were sworn in the same day. The crowd included former mayors Kennedy Stewart and Larry Campbell, and Fry’s mother, Liberal MP Hedy Fry. **B.C. seizes Vancouver Island couple's bank accounts, alleges insider trading: Vancouver Sun**
B.C.’s director of civil forfeiture has seized seven bank and investment accounts belonging to David and Leslie Finn of Nanoose Bay, alleging insider trading ahead of the sale of Sabina Gold and Silver Corp. A lawsuit in B.C. Supreme Court says the couple bought almost 500,000 Sabina shares for about $685,000 between October 2022 and February 10, 2023, the day B2Gold Corp. announced it was buying Sabina. In October 2022 they owned only a nominal number of shares, the lawsuit says. It alleges the purchases are “indicative of prohibited insider trading” and that the accounts are proceeds and instruments of unlawful activity under the Civil Forfeiture Act, citing the Criminal Code and the B.C. Securities Act. The Canadian Investment Regulatory Organization flagged more than 100 investors who bought $10,000 or more of Sabina shares in the 40 days before the announcement. The lawsuit says a TD account in Leslie Finn’s name, over which David Finn had power of attorney, was used to buy 150,000 shares worth $232,000 the day the sale was announced. None of the allegations has been proven in court. Source: [vancouversun.com](https://vancouversun.com/news/bc-seizes-vancouver-island-bank-accounts-alleges-insider-trading)

### The Closer Look
**B.C. ostrich farm files lawsuit against CFIA, government and RCMP: Surrey Now-Leader**
Universal Ostrich Farm and Landco Ventures filed a multi-million-dollar civil claim on October 30 against the Canadian Food Inspection Agency, the Attorney General of Canada and the RCMP. The claim comes almost a year after the flock at their Edgewood farm, in B.C.’s Interior, was killed. Farm representatives Karen Espersen and Katiey Pasitney, a mother and daughter, say 351 ostriches were killed on November 6 and 7, 2025. They describe a 35-year family legacy and say the suit seeks justice, accountability and policy change.

They say the claim arises from what they call aggressive and intrusive regulatory action that culminated in a 52-day occupation of the property, the forced slaughter of the flock, and the destruction of a commercial enterprise. They date an alleged unlawful search and occupation by the CFIA and the RCMP to on or about September 3, 2025. The pleadings also allege inadequate statutory compensation, animal cruelty, missing birds, unlawful seizure of ostrich eggs, property damage and trespassing. A CBC report on the same filing says the owners accuse the federal government of animal cruelty, under-compensation and preventing the launch of a lucrative commercial enterprise, and that the claim concerns 37 birds they say were missing after the 2025 cull.

The relief they seek includes an order that the defendants remove encroachments and remediate the property; a complete accounting and the return of, or compensation for, removed documents, materials, birds and eggs; and general, special, punitive and exemplary damages for trespass, nuisance, intrusion upon seclusion, harassment, negligence and Charter breaches, plus damages for permanent devaluation of the land. Espersen and Pasitney said they cannot bring the birds back, and that the lawsuit is about protecting rights and transparency as well as compensation.

American billionaire John Catsimatidis, who supported the farm’s effort last year to have the ostriches tested before they were killed, said he was disappointed the CFIA did not allow Secretary Kennedy, Dr. Oz or the U.S. Food and Drug Administration to test the birds as requested. He suggested that decision contributed to tensions between the two countries, and said he hopes the countries can work more closely again. None of the accusations has been proven in court. ### Both Sides: Pacific Link pipeline
Ottawa’s national-interest designation for the proposed Pacific Link oil pipeline is now the subject of eight separate Federal Court challenges from First Nations in B.C. The line, the first project designated under the Building Canada Act, would move an additional one million barrels a day from the Edmonton area to a new marine export terminal in Tsawwassen, largely along the Trans Mountain corridor. Global News puts the estimated cost between $35.2 billion and $42.7 billion. The final route still depends on design, review, consultation and approvals. Premier-designate Lorne Doerkson has pledged to work with Alberta and Ottawa to advance it, with major ongoing economic benefits for B.C. in return.

The federal case, as set out when Prime Minister Mark Carney and Alberta Premier Danielle Smith announced the designation last month, is that the line would reach more Asian customers, reduce dependence on the United States, and give Alberta, B.C. and Ottawa a long-term revenue source. Indigenous communities are to be offered at least a 10 percent ownership interest, backed by federal and Alberta loan guarantees. The Major Projects Office said it had consulted more than 130 Indigenous communities over three months; further consultation would set conditions, which Ottawa aims to finalize by September 2027 so construction can begin. National-interest status gives advance federal approval subject to those conditions, folding permits into one document.

The case against, from the nations filing, is that the designation was made without the consultation the law requires and without an impact assessment. St’át’imc Chiefs Council chair Justin Kane said the threat of a spill to Fraser River salmon, fishing rights and “our way of being” is heinous, and that the St’át’imc Nation as a whole was not consulted. The council, representing 11 communities, says the proposal means more than 500 river crossings, including two new Fraser crossings, and would increase tanker traffic in the Salish Sea. Tsartlip spokesman Adam Olsen called the process reckless and rushed, completed in less than three months for a project not expected to be finished until 2032, in fishing areas the nation is responsible for. Elder Art Adolph said governments and industry would push First Nations further into third-world conditions through harm to wild salmon. Grand Chief Stewart Phillip, president of the Union of B.C. Indian Chiefs, told corporations to beware when investing in B.C., because “we are not standing down.” Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12130609/bc-first-nations-file-challenges-pacific-link-pipeline-decision/) Source: [dailyhive.com](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-first-nations-lawsuit-pacific-link-oil-pipeline-national-interest) Source: [vancouversun.com](https://vancouversun.com/news/bc-first-nations-file-court-challenge-against-proposed-pacific-link-pipeline)
