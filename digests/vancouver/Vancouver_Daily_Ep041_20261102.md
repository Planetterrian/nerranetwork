### Top Stories
**Blaze kills one in Chilliwack home fire: CityNews**
A blaze broke out at a home on Young Road in Chilliwack on Friday afternoon and left one person dead, the Chilliwack Fire Department confirms. Crews were called to the 9800 block of the street around 2:15 p.m. When they arrived, they learned that one person might still be inside the home. In a news release Friday night, the department said firefighters worked to extinguish the fire and located an individual inside the home. The department said that individual was pronounced deceased at the scene. Fire crews said the house had extensive fire, smoke and water damage, which kept the other residents from returning Friday night. The department said the origin and cause of the fire remain under investigation. Source: [vancouver.citynews.ca](https://vancouver.citynews.ca/2026/11/01/chilliwack-fire-fatal/)

**Samaritans rescue truck from Kootenay Lake: CityNews**
Three good Samaritans jumped into Kootenay Lake in Nelson on Saturday to go after a truck that drove over the embankment and into the water. Nelson Police Department said it received 911 calls around 1:18 p.m. saying a truck had left the parking lot of Chahko Mika Mall and landed in the lake. CBC reported the vehicle was a pickup and that the driver was a woman. The three who dove in were able to rescue her from the sinking truck. Nelson’s police, fire department and BC Emergency Health Services attended and found the driver had already been pulled from the water. She was checked by paramedics and released without being taken to hospital. Drugs or alcohol have been ruled out as a possible cause. The incident remains under investigation. **Phones showed the wrong time after the West stayed on daylight time: Global BC**
After British Columbia, Alberta, Manitoba, northwestern Ontario and the Northwest Territories stayed on daylight time year-round rather than falling back an hour, some Canadians reported Sunday that their cellphones displayed the incorrect time. Saskatchewan already does not adjust its clocks. A statement from Telus says the issue is related to how some phones update their time-zone information, rather than the service provider. Telus says customers can check that “set time zone automatically” is selected, then restart the phone to help apply any available time-zone updates. A Rogers spokesperson said the company experienced no issues with its network and referred questions to an industry group that posted instructions for updating phones and tablets. Air Canada said Sunday its operations were normal, noting that all flights use the internationally recognized time standard and that time changes are managed using established industry data sources. Google responded to an inquiry by linking to a blog post with instructions for keeping devices and services on schedule, and said its teams are working to ensure the updates reach all Canadian users as quickly as possible. The post said people rely on devices for morning alarms and critical calendar invites. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12129795/western-canada-time-change/)

### The Closer Look
**It's a prime time for classical music in Vancouver: Vancouver Sun**
November is prime time for classical music in Vancouver, and the Vancouver Sun’s concert docket for the month is full of dated shows at named rooms. The month opens with the Vancouver Symphony Orchestra on November 6 and 7 at 7:30 p.m. at the Orpheum. Guest conductor Antony Hermus, until recently chief conductor of the Belgian National Orchestra, offers an all-Russian program: rarely heard Scriabin to start, and Prokofiev’s fairy-tale ballet music for Cinderella to end. The anchoring work is Shostakovich’s First Violin Concerto, with American violinist Kevin Zhu. The Sun notes that the orchestra prides itself on its way with Russian repertoire. That same weekend, mezzo-soprano Ema Nikolovska and guitarist Sean Shibe return with their Orlando Project on November 8 at 3 p.m. at the Telus Studio Theatre in the Chan Centre for the Performing Arts. The program runs from Dowland to Bob Dylan, framed as a musical reflection of Ariosto’s Orlando Furioso and Virginia Woolf’s 1928 novel Orlando: A Biography. The following Sunday, the Vancouver Recital Society presents harpsichordist Jean Rondeau playing Couperin and Rameau at the Barnet Recital Hall on November 15 at 3 p.m. The Sun says the room, in the University of British Columbia’s School of Music, is small, and that Rondeau has a dedicated following, so tickets need to be secured quickly. Chor Leoni’s Remembrance Day concert, The Soul Flies Away, is the choir’s 35th annual event of that kind, at 2 p.m. and 5 p.m. on November 11 at St. Andrew’s-Wesley church. Two days later, two concerts occupy the same hour. Early Music Vancouver presents The Magic of Monteverdi by the French ensemble Les Arts Florissants, co-directed by William Christie and Paul Agnew, on November 17 at 7:30 p.m. at the Chan Centre. The program draws on Monteverdi’s books of madrigals for mixed voices, including the proto-opera Il Combattimento di Tancredi e Clorinda. At the same time, downtown at the Vancouver Playhouse, Friends of Chamber Music hosts the Terra String Quartet, winner of the 2025 Naumburg Chamber Music Competition and a prizewinner at the 2025 Bordeaux and Wigmore Hall International String Quartet Competitions. Terra plays late Beethoven, arrangements of madrigals by Maddalena Casulana, and Benjamin Britten’s Third String Quartet, drawn from his final opera, Death in Venice. At month’s end, Thomas Dunford’s Jupiter Ensemble returns for the Vancouver Recital Society on November 29 at 3 p.m. at the Chan Centre, with Italian, German and French baroque music unified by the legend of the Amazons. Source: [vancouversun.com](https://vancouversun.com/entertainment/vancouver-classical-music-concerts-november-2026)
