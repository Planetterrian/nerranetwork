OK, let's pop the hood on activation steering for dialect bias.

The technique is often described as a simple add-this-vector, subtract-that-vector step.

In practice it requires locating the exact layers where the unwanted behavior lives and extracting a direction that isolates the target without hurting fluency.

Researchers first run causal tracing to identify layers that most strongly influence biased continuations.

They then compute a steering vector from paired African American English and Standard American English examples.

At inference they add a scaled version of that vector only to the identified layers.

The method cut dialect bias five to twenty times more than prompting while keeping Standard American English fluency.

Steering strength must still be tuned per model family, or the output starts to sound unnatural.

The same direction can affect unrelated syntactic features if the localization step is skipped.

Teams should begin with a small validation set of dialect pairs and sweep the steering coefficient before scaling.

The approach works across model sizes from fourteen billion to seventy billion parameters without retraining.

It does require the REAL-AAE corpus of seventeen thousand four hundred seventy-nine validated triplets to produce reliable directions.

So when should you reach for activation steering versus prompting? Start with a validation sweep on your target model family.
