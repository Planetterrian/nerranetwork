Test-Time Scaling for Scientific Equation Discovery frames LLM-driven equation discovery as an iterative search process.

Search width emerges as the dominant parameter under fixed compute budgets on LLM-SRBench.

Wider search improves performance while also raising wall-clock efficiency through parallelism.

OK, let's pop the hood on test-time compute allocation.

Everyone treats test-time scaling as simply spend more tokens and get better answers.

In practice the gains come from how you allocate that extra compute across parallel samples, sequential refinement, or tree search.

The core insight is that once you have a reliable verifier, widening the search usually beats deepening any single path because independent samples explore different regions of the output space.

On equation-discovery tasks, increasing width with budget produced the largest lifts while also improving wall-clock speed through parallelism.

Branching factors and controller choice mattered far less.

The quality ceiling still depends on verifier strength.

Weak verifiers make extra samples mostly noise.

Use width-first allocation when your verifier is cheap and accurate.

Fall back to sequential refinement only when verification itself is expensive or when you need to stay inside tight latency bounds.

The practical gotcha is that most production verifiers are still noisy, so teams often over-allocate before they notice diminishing returns.
