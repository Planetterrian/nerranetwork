Idle time on a five-device chain is the reason to pop the hood on pipelined speculation for mixed kits.

Multi-device mixture-of-experts inference looks like simple layer sharding until the slowest link leaves four or five gadgets waiting.

The practical fix is a small draft head on the first device that guesses the next few tokens, after which the entire chain validates them in one pass.

That first device then starts the next speculation while earlier guesses are still travelling.

A verified step averages about two point two tokens, so the wait becomes decode.

On a forty-eight-layer, five-hundred-twelve-expert model split across two PCs, an M four Pro MacBook, and two phones, this yields thirty-seven to thirty-nine tokens a second at sixty thousand context with every weight kept in VRAM or unified memory.

The first PC holds layers zero through nine plus the output layer and the draft head.

The MacBook holds layers ten through twenty-nine.

The second PC holds layers thirty through forty-seven.

Phones handle expert selection for layers ten through eighteen over USB-C, while the Mac keeps the hottest experts in its own memory.

That draft head has to beat the extra PCIe and USB-C latency as the window grows, and once acceptance drops under about fifty-five percent the quality edge is gone.

The layout serves sixty-nine point six thousand context natively before key-value cache pressure forces a second configuration that moves two layers back to the first PC and keeps eight-bit key-value on the Mac and phones.

Every change is exact by construction except the Mac's expert cache, which adds partial sums in a different order.

The KL check against a reference has not yet been run on today's changes.

The pattern pays off on mixed kits that already sit on your desk and need long-context decode.

When the whole model fits in twenty-four gigabytes and you would rather keep the graph simple, cache experts on one GPU instead.
