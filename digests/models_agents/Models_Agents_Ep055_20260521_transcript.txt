Okay, let's pop the hood on multi-agent verification loops. Everyone talks about multi-agent systems
as if simply adding more models will automatically improve reliability. In practice, verification works
through explicit hand-off protocols. One agent must cite every evidence fragment it used.
Another agent is tasked with attempting to disprove the proposed causal chain. This adds measurable
latency, often 200-400 milliseconds per verification round, yet it dramatically reduces confident but
unsupported conclusions on complex incidents. The key engineering choice is deciding how many
independent hypotheses to explore in parallel versus how deeply to verify each one.
Teams that oververify see diminishing returns once accuracy plateaus, around 85-90% on their
internal eVal sets. When the cost of a wrong route causes high, the architecture shines. When speed
matters more than perfect explanations, a single well-prompted agent with strong retrieval still wins.
