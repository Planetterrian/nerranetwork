OK, let's pop the hood on flow matching as it appears in the minFLUX implementation. The technique reparameterizes generation as learning a velocity field that transports noise to data along straight paths in probability space. The model therefore predicts the instantaneous direction of the probability flow rather than a score or noise estimate.

Straight paths let the ODE solver take larger steps at inference time and cut the number of function evaluations required. Training pays a cost because the velocity target must be estimated from paired noise data points, which raises gradient variance compared with the simpler noise prediction loss.

In the minFLUX loop the VAE first encodes images, the flow matching velocity is computed, and an MSE loss regresses against that velocity. Choose flow matching when your inference budget is dominated by solver steps. Stick with classic diffusion when training stability matters more than step count.
