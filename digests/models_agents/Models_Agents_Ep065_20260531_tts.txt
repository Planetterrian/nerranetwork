From there it’s natural to ask how we make the instructions we give agents more dependable in the first place. A new paper on SkillOpt treats markdown skill files as trainable parameters. A frontier model proposes bounded edits—add, delete, or replace—and only keeps changes that improve a held-out validation set.

When the edit budget stays between four and eight proposals, skills typically settle after one to four accepted edits. Removing the budget cap, though, caused performance to drop across the tasks they tested. The method gives teams a repeatable loop for turning ad-hoc instructions into validated components, another incremental step on the same reliability track.

That pattern feeds directly into the standardization of agent and tool protocols we’ve been following, because it turns sandboxing from a one-time setup into an ongoing discipline that scales with what the agent can actually do.

CoreWeave’s new unified agentic platform tries to close another part of the loop. It lets agents iterate on their own outputs in self-improvement cycles without waiting for full retraining runs.
