Sequences past one hundred twenty-eight thousand tokens are where we pop the hood on SeoulTech’s SLAKE, which, techxplore reports, keeps an approximate representation of discarded tokens instead of dropping them outright.

Two standard approaches each fail in a different way: discarding low-value tokens loses information, while simplifying attention calculations everywhere can degrade accuracy across the board.

Partially Taylor-Approximated Attention applies a Taylor expansion only to low-value tokens and full attention on important ones, and Value-Aware Budget Scoring ranks tokens by their expected contribution to the final output.

Because the approximation retains a rough picture of the full context, information loss stays lower than pure eviction while memory usage remains fixed.

The quality gain disappears once context length exceeds the point where even the retained high-value tokens no longer fit the budget, the regime where current large language models already strain compute and memory.

SLAKE applies the approximation selectively and scores tokens by downstream impact, preserving the most useful signals inside a fixed memory envelope; use it for stable long-context work on hardware with strict memory caps when a small accuracy trade-off is acceptable, and fall back to standard eviction or full attention when every retained token must be exact.
