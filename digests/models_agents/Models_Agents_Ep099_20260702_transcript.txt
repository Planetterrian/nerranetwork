output. Okay, let's pop the hood on self-conditioning in continuous diffusion language models.
Everyone talks about self-conditioning in flow-based language models as if it were a simple
quality boost. In practice, it creates a fixed point iteration inside the denoising loop,
where each step feeds the model's own clean estimate back as additional context.
The core insight is that this feedback quickly collapses toward a low-dimensional attractor.
When that direction encodes repetition, perplexity drops while diversity collapses.
The contraction is one-dimensional, so subtracting a single estimated vector from the feedback
at every step removes the attractor without retraining.
On the 105 million model, this cut repetition to human levels and transferred unchanged to 342 million
and 652 million scales. The practical trade-off is roughly 1.5 to 5 times more compute to reach
human clean text, but the method stays training-free and works across samplers. Use it when generation
diversity matters more than raw token throughput. Skip it for short, factual outputs where repetition is
unlikely anyway. The gacha most teams miss is that standard GenPPL metrics reward the very repetition
