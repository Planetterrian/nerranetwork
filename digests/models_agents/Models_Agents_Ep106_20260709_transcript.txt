Okay, let's pop the hood on activation steering
for dialect bias.
The technique is often described as a simple
add this vector, subtract that vector step.
In practice, it requires locating the exact layers
where the unwanted behavior lives
and extracting a direction that isolates the target
without hurting fluency.
Researchers first run causal tracing to identify layers
that most strongly influence biased continuations.
They then compute a steering vector
from paired African-American English
and standard American English examples.
At inference, they add a scaled version of that vector
only to the identified layers.
The method cut dialect bias five to 20 times more than prompting
while keeping standard American English fluency.
Steering strength must still be tuned per model family
or the output starts to sound unnatural.
The same direction can affect unrelated syntactic features
if the localization step is skipped.
Teams should begin with a small validation set of dialect pairs
and sweep the steering coefficient before scaling.
The approach works across model sizes
from 14 billion to 70 billion parameters without retraining.
It does require the ERAAE corpus
of 17,479 validated triplets
to produce reliable directions.
