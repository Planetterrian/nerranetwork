OK, let's pop the hood on pure quantization.

Everyone talks about quantization as a simple make it smaller knob.

In practice it is a set of deliberate trade-offs between bit-width, calibration data, and hardware mapping that determine whether a model stays usable.

Pure quantization methods skip imatrix calibration and instead apply uniform or near-uniform scaling across weights, which reduces preprocessing time and sometimes improves throughput on CPUs during offload.

The cost appears in slightly higher perplexity, typically zero point zero five to zero point one seven above imatrix equivalents on the same model size.

For MoE architectures the savings compound because only active experts are loaded.

Dropping from four point five bits per weight to four point two two bits per weight can free enough VRAM to keep an extra expert resident and raise cache hit rates.

The practical decision rule is straightforward.

Use pure quants when generation speed on partial offload matters more than squeezing every last point of benchmark quality, and fall back to imatrix when you have the calibration budget and need maximum fidelity on long-context tasks.
