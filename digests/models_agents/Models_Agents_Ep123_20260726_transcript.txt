In memory, HNSW delivers single-digit millisecond queries, but requires the entire graph to
fit in RAM. Teams quickly hit expensive instance sizes once collections exceed a few hundred million
vectors. Disk-based approaches, such as span and disk A&N, keep only a small hot set in memory,
and fetch compressed neighbor lists from SSD. This adds roughly 10 to 30 milliseconds per query,
while cutting RAM needs by five to ten times on the same data set. The quality gap appears mainly
at high recall targets above 95%, where disk methods need extra re-ranking passes.
When your working set stays under roughly 50 million vectors and sub-ten millisecond latency
matters more than cost, stay in RAM. Once collections grow larger or you need to serve many tenants on
the same hardware. Move to a disk-aware index and budget for the extra re-ranking step.
