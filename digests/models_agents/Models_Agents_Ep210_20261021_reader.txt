Shared prompts shrink per-task traces, and that is the failure we pop the hood on for implicit budgeting under task composition.

When several tasks share a single prompt, models allocate fewer tokens to each task than they would in isolation.

That happens even when the total context remains comfortably inside the window.

The model maintains an implicit per-task compute budget that shrinks under competition.

Tasks whose isolated solutions already require long traces suffer the largest accuracy drop.

The budgeting is visible in token counts per sub-task.

Errors rise suddenly once any single task's trace length exceeds a model-specific threshold.

Adding explicit per-task length instructions or forcing intermediate verification steps restores most of the lost performance, at the cost of extra tokens overall.

Measure per-task token usage on representative multi-task prompts before deployment.

If any task routinely exceeds roughly sixty percent of its isolated length, split the prompt or add verification rather than relying on the model to self-regulate.
