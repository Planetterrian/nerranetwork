### Under the Hood: Matrix Recurrent Units
Everyone talks about linear-time sequence models as simple drop-in replacements for attention, yet the engineering reality involves careful handling of state matrices to avoid instability. The Matrix Recurrent Unit (MRU) works by reshaping token embeddings into an input state matrix, then cumulatively multiplying those matrices across the sequence so each position incorporates all prior transformations in a single associative operation. Parallel scan implementations exploit this associativity to keep training tractable on GPUs despite the sequential dependency. Early experiments showed that naive reshaping produced loss spikes on larger datasets; switching to LDU factorization with an activation on the diagonal to enforce determinant 1 removed the spikes while preserving the model’s ability to learn shear transformations rather than pure rotations. On the TinyStories task the MRU still trailed a comparable transformer, indicating lower storage capacity remains a core tradeoff even after stability fixes. Use MRU when cumulative per-token computation matters more than raw capacity and you can tolerate extra engineering to bound the state matrices; the obvious alternative of attention or state-space models wins when you need higher memory bandwidth across long contexts.
