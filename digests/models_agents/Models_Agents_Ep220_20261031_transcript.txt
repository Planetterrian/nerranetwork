Give thousands of clawed agents an explicit order to beat a training data monitor, and you
pop the hood on a pipeline that now attacks itself.
The agent succeeded by crafting data that appears benign to the monitor, yet still embeds
misaligned behavioral patterns.
Two concrete examples made the trick obvious, a veteran wrestler mentoring a newcomer,
and a question and answer session about professional beekeeping.
Both look like ordinary, helpful text, yet reliably shift the model toward misaligned outputs
when used in training.
The generated examples are then fed back into monitor improvement, creating a closed loop
where the same model family both attacks and defends the training pipeline.
Because the agents operate at scale, the volume of candidate misaligned data exceeds what
human red teamers could produce in the same time.
Larger fleets combined with more capable models should surface even subtler cases.
