Google put Gemini 4 Neon in the middle of the Gemini 4 lineup between Carbon and Hydrogen, the company blog said, and retired Argon for knowledge work and science workflows.

Output runs fifteen dollars per million tokens, with four effort levels and a default sixty-four thousand token cap that can stretch to one million through background long decode.

Turned up to high effort, Neon ties Carbon on the Intelligence Index, scores fifty-eight point six on the AA Coding Agent Index, and hits ninety-five point two percent on GraphWalks long-context tasks.

Weights stay closed, it runs only on Google-hosted machines, and inside Antigravity it can orchestrate parallel Hydrogen sub-agents.

Access lands in the paid Gemini API, AI Studio, Android Studio, Gemini for business, and Pro and Ultra subscribers in the Gemini app under the Deeper analysis label.

Price changes on the rest of the Gemini 4 line take effect December first.

Decision models, an arXiv paper finds, return a typed answer plus a probability instead of generating free text, and that is where I want to pop the hood for bounded tasks.

Classification and judging no longer need a parser glued on the end, because the label arrives already typed.

In contact-center workloads those models match frontier large language models, or LLMs, on six classification problems, and they do it at the lowest per-request cost among the API options tested.

Abstention is the hole: most of them almost never pick the explicit no-answer option, and that is the widest gap against the strongest generative baselines.

On two judging tasks, accuracy falls sharply once parameter count drops, and they show higher position bias — they lean on answer order — than generative judges.

Eight real-world use cases point to a split: automatically accept high-confidence decision-model predictions, and send the uncertain remainder to generative LLMs or to a human.

That routing produced the best accuracy-cost trade-off in the study.

High-volume intent classification and compliance checking are the workloads where a decision model gets benchmarked first on the exact label set; judging, small parameter counts, and anything that needs a clean no-answer still go to a full generative call.
