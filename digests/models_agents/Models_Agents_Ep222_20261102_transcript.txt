67.6% average accuracy at 4 billion is why we pop the hood on EDU Jev's pointer heads
for single-pass categorical decisions.
Pointerheads let a model output a probability distribution over a fixed set of options in
one forward pass by scoring each option with a learned marker instead of generating tokens.
The core move is to replace the usual language modeling head with a small set of option-specific
linear probes that share the same encoder.
Because the output vocabulary is now exactly the allowed choices, calibration becomes a direct
softmax over those scores rather than an indirect mapping from freeform text.
That removes both the parsing step and the risk that the model produces an out-of-set answer.
The family supplies single-pass decision models from 0.8 billion to 4 billion for education
tasks.
The 4B variant hits 67.6% average accuracy across 12 education benchmarks, beating the strongest
commercial API by 9.4 points, and the best open 4B model by 18.7 points. It runs approximately
150 decisions per second on 1H100, roughly an order of magnitude faster than any text generation
baseline that must be post-processed. The quality gain disappears once the option set grows beyond
a few hundred entries, because the pointer head must still compete with the encoder's capacity.
At that scale, a lightweight re-ranker on top of a generative model becomes preferable.
Use pointer heads when the task is closed set classification with a stable label inventory
and latency matters.
Fall back to generation plus parsing when the label space is open or changes frequently.
Before we go, decision model re-rankers trained with item space on policy distillation are expected
to target additional retrieval benchmarks.
