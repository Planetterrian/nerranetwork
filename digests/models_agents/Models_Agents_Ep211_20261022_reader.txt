Preference losses pop the hood on a shared hidden-state shift that ranking never penalizes.

Those losses operate on relative differences between chosen and rejected pairs, so any shared directional shift that moves both representations equally produces almost no gradient signal.

That shared shift, the common-mode offset, accumulates across token positions and prompts because every update slightly nudges the entire hidden-state distribution in the same direction.

Once the offset grows large enough it projects through the unembedding matrix and creates a single high-probability token that dominates sampling, producing vocabulary collapse.

The offset is already visible in textbook DPO and SimPO runs that never reach collapse, which is why ranking metrics can keep improving while generation quality silently degrades.

Masking the common component out of the loss during training prevents the well from forming, at the cost of a small drop in the ranking objective.

Monitor the norm of the mean-centered hidden-state shift on a small validation set after each epoch; when it exceeds roughly zero point three to zero point four of the typical per-token activation norm, generation quality is likely to degrade even if pairwise ranking scores remain high.

Teams that already track output entropy or repetition rate can add this hidden-state statistic without extra inference passes, and masking belongs in the loss when that offset threshold is crossed, not when ranking score is the only objective in use.
