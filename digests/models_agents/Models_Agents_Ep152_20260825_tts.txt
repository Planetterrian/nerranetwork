OK, let’s pop the hood on tokenization.

Byte-pair encoding allocates vocabulary slots proportional to observed frequency in training data, so languages with less web text receive fewer dedicated tokens and must be spelled out with many more subword pieces.

Each extra token consumes KV-cache memory and attention compute, turning Ukrainian’s measured 68 to 121 percent overhead versus English into roughly 1.7 to 2.2 times higher per-query cost on the same model.

Inference-time compression such as LLMLingua-2 recovers nearly half the length on retrieval workloads without retraining.

Training a fresh byte-level BPE with a hard vocabulary cap narrows the gap from 2.22 times to 1.30 times.

When you control the tokenizer and can afford one-time training cost, invest in balanced vocabulary allocation.

Otherwise apply lightweight compression at query time and accept the residual overhead.

Romanization often increases rather than decreases token count on modern multilingual tokenizers.
