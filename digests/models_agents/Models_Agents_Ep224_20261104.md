### Model Updates
**MEMENTO: Learning Context Management for Long Context Processing Agents — arXiv NLP**
MEMENTO is an 8B Qwen3-based agent trained first on synthetic hindsight notes then with RL that rewards answer correctness, note quality, and calibration. It compacts history into a fresh 20K-token window and at test time also supports folding and backtracking. On book-level QA with inputs up to 2.5M tokens it lifts base Qwen3-8B accuracy from 36.9% to 67.8%, beating a GPT-5.5 agent (13.1%) at less than 1/50 the cost and nearly matching a 100× larger GLM-5.3 agent at 1/9 the cost. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00244)

**GAVAGAI: Referential Alignment for Infant-Scale Vision-Language Models via Balanced Optimal Transport — arXiv NLP**
GAVAGAI adds an explicit word-to-region assignment step solved with entropic optimal transport plus NOTHING-column and capacity constraints. On the SAYCam infant video corpus the alignment loss raises word-to-picture accuracy from 32.4% to 48.7% and localization IoU from 0.18 to 0.38 when added to a next-token captioning objective. The method is intended for captioning models under high non-reference rates rather than as a replacement for contrastive pre-training. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00319)

**Cross-Prompt Reuse of Entity-Replacement Responses — arXiv NLP**
The work measures how much hidden-state change from source questions transfers to a target question about the same entity when injected at 50% depth. On 0.5-1B Qwen and Llama models, other-relation hidden reuse removes 45-49% of no-change error; a later protocol that restores the BOS token reaches 58-68% on three cohorts. Larger models and chat formats show replication failures. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00352)

**Circuit Oracle: Automating Attribution Graph Analysis via Natural-Language Queries — arXiv NLP**
Circuit Oracle wraps an off-the-shelf LLM with a multi-agent harness that answers natural-language questions about a target model by reading its attribution graph through tool calls. On spurious-feature detection it reaches 78.2% accuracy, outperforming single-layer ranking baselines; on refusal suppression its single committed intervention is slightly below a diff-in-means baseline unless five attempts are allowed. The authors release pruned subgraphs and context-specific feature readings. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00420)

**Fidelity Collapses with Table Size, Not Turn Count: Delivery Architecture in a Deployed Tool-Using Agent — arXiv NLP**
In a production award-travel agent, feeding full record sets into the planner’s context (SYNTHESIS) scores 45.7% fully correct turns while keeping records in session state and delivering rows over a typed side channel (BYPASS) scores 92.3%. SYNTHESIS collapses once tables exceed roughly 74 rows; BYPASS stays flat across four models. Beyond 100 rows each correct SYNTHESIS answer costs roughly 200× more than BYPASS. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00442)

**From Lookups to Lessons: Turning Retrieved Knowledge into Parametric Memory — arXiv NLP**
ResMem trains a parametric memory on teacher targets generated by an LLM that reads retrieved evidence offline, learning an additive logit residual rather than mimicking a retriever. On five QA datasets it raises mean token F1 from 32.83% to 34.48% while breaking 21% fewer of the base LLM’s correct answers than a kNN-trained memory; storage for teacher construction drops 94.7% versus the evaluated kNN pipeline. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00450)

### Agent & Tool Developments
**Humanized but Still True? Auditing Facts and Detection in AI-Text Humanizers — arXiv NLP**
All 11 commercial humanizers on an October 2026 public leaderboard altered or dropped at least one checkable unit (numbers, dates, names, quoted spans) in 35-65% of documents containing them. An open 12B humanizer retained 97% of numbers and names on federal text but still reworded 67-78% of quoted spans. A zero-shot decision model, Jev, separated human originals from humanized rewrites at AUROC 0.864. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00359)

**CheXPoT: Post-Hoc Visual Verification and Correction of VLM-Generated Radiology Reports — arXiv NLP**
CheXPoT decomposes generated reports into RadGraph predicates, grounds them against a DenseNet-121 + Grad-CAM table, executes verification code, and rewrites the report after dropping unsupported predicates. On a 4,145-image held-out split it lifts micro-average precision from 19.2% to 46.8% and F1 from 23.0% to 49.4% for MedGemma-4B; similar absolute gains appear on LLaVA-Med and MAIRA-2. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00785)

**HALO: Hierarchical Evidence Selection and Reinforcement Learning for Allocating Budgeted Label Optimization from Longitudinal Clinical Records — arXiv NLP**
HALO jointly learns which evidence to keep from longitudinal records and which patients deserve higher-fidelity labels under a fixed budget. In a heart-failure case study it attained the highest MedAlign acquired-label F1 at six of eight target budgets; a downstream classifier trained on HALO labels reached the highest external MGB F1 at budgets 15-60. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00986)

### Practical & Community
**AStockArena: Beyond Returns, Can We Entrust Real Capital to the Decisions of Financial Agents? — arXiv NLP**
AStockArena audits 3,198 sequential trading decisions by 13 models on China’s A-share market with eight checks for execution traceability, evidence sufficiency, and financial meta-awareness. 72.99% of 1,070 trading points showed at least one audit gap; historical replay confirmed that portfolio outcomes are sensitive to the flagged trades. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00515)

**Dual-Axis Policy Entailment: A Benchmark for Policy-Change Reasoning — arXiv NLP**
DAPE requires models to judge both domain match and the direction of policy change (continue, broaden, narrow, reverse) on ordered pairs of Chinese government directives. Fourteen open-weight models and four frontier APIs all fall well below human agreement (best flagship κ ≈ 0.29 vs. 0.53); a LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen3-14B reaches the human ceiling (κ = 0.524). Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00536)

**Saidzoda-Tajik-E5-Large: Fine-Tuning a 560M Embedder for Tajik Retrieval — arXiv NLP**
The 560M model, trained on 235k synthetic Tajik question-passage pairs plus English and Russian negatives, raises top-10 recall on unseen law text from 80.2% to 93.6% and on tax documents from 82.6% to 94.5%. It matches a 3B model on style-matched law and tax questions but trails on the human-written Belebele set. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00592)

**Constructing Budgeted KGW Evasion through Token-Event Evidence Replacement — arXiv NLP**
The method uses LLM-proposed phrase rewrites and exact coverage search to remove watermark evidence under a 20% token-edit budget. 39 of 82 initially detected short passages yielded rewrites with terminal detector Z < 4 at a mean 16.0% edit cost; mean suppression came mainly from removing watermark-enriched source events. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00726)

### Under the Hood: Transformer Layer Stacking and Depth
Transformer layers are sequential transformation blocks; each applies self-attention to mix information across the sequence followed by a feed-forward network that refines every token’s representation, with residuals and normalization around both. Stacking more layers lets the network build progressively higher-level features and longer-range dependencies because early layers capture local patterns while later layers integrate them into abstract reasoning. Depth therefore increases both expressive capacity and the amount of sequential computation performed on hidden states. The original “Attention Is All You Need” paper demonstrated that this architecture alone, without recurrence or convolutions, matched or exceeded recurrent models on translation while being far more parallelizable. Later work on scaling to 1,000 layers showed that a modified residual connection (DeepNorm) plus theoretically derived initialization keeps updates stable where plain Post-LN or Pre-LN variants diverge. In practice the quality gain from added depth diminishes once models exceed roughly 70B parameters unless training data and optimization are scaled accordingly; the counter-intuitive result is that a 200-layer 3.2B model can outperform a 48-layer 12B model on multilingual translation when the deeper model is properly stabilized. When choosing depth versus width, teams should first verify that their optimizer and normalization scheme remain stable at the target depth; otherwise width scaling or recurrent reuse of the same block is often cheaper to stabilize. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762)
