A 421 million parameter non-autoregressive decision model called Laya was trained on a 100 percent human-annotated corpus.

The corpus contains over twenty-five thousand real-world examples.

Training used an RLCD policy-gradient approach.

The model pairs a bidirectional ModernBERT-large encoder with a scratch Transformer head.

It runs in a single 35 millisecond forward pass on low-end hardware.

The model is available on Hugging Face at the convaiinnovations laya repository.

A demo space and GitHub repo are included.

Training ran on a single RTX sixty zero Pro with 96 GB VRAM.

A proposed frontend-backend split lets a duplex speech-to-text frontend emit a delegation token.

ASR transcripts stream to a text-based backend L L M for tool calls.

Tool results return through a lightweight prefill-and-repeat mechanism.

Streaming TTS synthesis follows.

The system preserves regular duplex turn-taking and interruption handling.

It achieves 92 to 97 percent tool-call recall.

Accuracy rejecting irrelevant calls reaches 81.2 percent.

With a larger backend such as Qwen3-235B-A22B it outperforms G P T realtime-mini and Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct on EVA-Bench.

The architecture requires only minimal changes to the frontend model.

TrioRAG retrieves independently over a shared multi-vector index.

It uses the question, the anchor image, and a V L M enhanced query.

Results combine through late fusion.

The approach matches or exceeds graph-based systems on three benchmarks.

Total cost drops while per-query inference accelerates by 1.6 to 2.3 times.

It is paired with the new AutoQA automotive benchmark built from noisy web-sourced images.

The framework avoids maintaining a corpus-level graph that would need frequent rebuilds.

CliniCIRCA is a multi-stage L L M framework.

It produces calendar-anchored, imprecision-aware reconstructions of clinical events.

The source is unstructured discharge summaries without event-level timestamps.

On 52 MIMIC-III summaries it extracted fifteen thousand eight hundred ninety-one temporally tagged events.

After clinician correction the timelines compress source material 1.52 times into date-grouped records.

Instruction tuning on the resulting silver-standard data improves five open-weight models.

Gains appear on event extraction, temporal tagging, and summarization.

The framework was evaluated on a clinician-in-the-loop benchmark of verified gold-standard labels.

ProKDA uses an agentic background-knowledge pipeline.

A three-stage training sequence follows.

The stages are background learning, detection learning, and boundary alignment.

The method avoids interference between explanation generation and label prediction.

Prior explain-then-detect approaches were limited by that interference.

It reaches state-of-the-art detection on three public hateful-meme benchmarks.

Accurate, evidence-supported explanations are produced.

The staged approach was tested on three public benchmarks with consistent gains over joint-training baselines.

SynUC is a constraint-guided synthesis method.

It represents user-side conflicts in a constraint space.

The SPEAKING framework generates traceable implicit conflict samples.

Applied to WildChat it produced UC-Data, a two thousand four hundred eighty-seven-example training set.

A Qwen3.5-4B model trained on UC-Data outperforms larger general-purpose L L M's.

Claude Opus 4.8 is among those outperformed.

The evaluation uses the new UC-Bench conflict-detection benchmark.

The method explicitly models implicit incompatibilities between historical and current utterances.

Reflective Recovery extracts initial segments of failed trajectories.

Those segments are concatenated with prompts.

Models are trained to recognize and correct mistakes.

On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B the method raised accuracy from 30.0 percent to 37.5 percent on AIME 2025.

Accuracy rose from 37.6 percent to 47.8 percent on Minerva.

Emergent self-correction behaviors appear.

The technique was evaluated across extensive benchmarks.

It breaks the scaling collapse seen in pure imitation learning.

Late fusion keeps each retrieval signal independent until the final ranking step.

Early joint embedding is avoided.

The three signals in TrioRAG each query the same multi-vector index.

A weak image match can still surface if the V L M enhanced text query scores strongly.

This independence removes the need to maintain a single corpus-level graph.

The graph would otherwise need rebuilding whenever any document changes.

On the reported benchmarks the approach delivers 1.6 to 2.3 times faster per-query inference.

No cross-signal attention occurs until after the top-k candidates are already fetched.

The tradeoff appears when one modality dominates.

If the anchor image is the only reliable cue, the text-only signals add noise.

Late fusion must down-weight that noise at ranking time.

Teams facing rapidly changing document collections therefore prefer late fusion when update latency matters more than the marginal gain from joint early fusion training.

When the corpus is static and every modality is equally informative, early graph construction can still justify its rebuild cost.

The design choice trades a small amount of potential accuracy for substantially lower maintenance overhead in production settings.
