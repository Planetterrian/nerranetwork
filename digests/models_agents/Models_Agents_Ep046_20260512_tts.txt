OK, let's pop the hood on the frozen-tower approach to multimodal embeddings.

The real engineering win comes from keeping the original text towers frozen and training only thin adapters on top.

This preserves the exact text embedding space you already tuned while adding new modalities at roughly zero point three five percent of total parameter cost.

The adapters project non-text features into the frozen language model input space, so inference latency grows only by the cost of the new encoders plus a small projection step.

Quality stays within a few points of fully trained multimodal models on retrieval benchmarks because the heavy semantic work still happens in the original text backbone.

The main tradeoff is that cross-modal alignment cannot exceed what the frozen text space already encodes.

If your downstream task needs tighter image-text coupling, you eventually pay for joint fine-tuning.
