Okay, let's pop the hood on the Stam optimizer. Most people treat optimizer choice as a simple
hyperparameter swap. In practice, Adam family methods carry fixed momentum decay that can drag
stale gradient information forward for thousands of steps. Stam measures the residual between the
current gradient and running momentum at each update. When the gap spikes, it lowers beta one on
the fly, so the optimizer avoids pushing in an outdated direction. This adds a small amount of
per step arithmetic, but removes the need for the second full momentum buffer that Adam W keeps.
Optimizer state drops from roughly two times model size down to one times in the light variant.
The quality gain shows up clearest on long non-stationary runs where data distribution shifts.
Above roughly 70 billion parameters, the relative benefit shrinks because gradient noise averages
out anyway. Most teams should default to Stam light when GPU memory is the bottleneck and fall back
to Adam W, only of heavy regularization pipelines are already tuned around its exact momentum behavior.
