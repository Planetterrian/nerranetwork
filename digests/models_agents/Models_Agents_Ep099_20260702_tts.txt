OK, let's pop the hood on self-conditioning in continuous diffusion language models.

Everyone talks about self-conditioning in flow-based language models as if it were a simple quality boost.

In practice it creates a fixed-point iteration inside the denoising loop where each step feeds the model’s own clean estimate back as additional context.

The core insight is that this feedback quickly collapses toward a low-dimensional attractor.

When that direction encodes repetition, perplexity drops while diversity collapses.

The contraction is one-dimensional, so subtracting a single estimated vector from the feedback at every step removes the attractor without retraining.

On the one hundred five million model this cut repetition to human levels and transferred unchanged to three hundred forty-two million and six hundred fifty-two million scales.

The practical tradeoff is roughly one point five to five times more compute to reach human-clean text, but the method stays training-free and works across samplers.

Use it when generation diversity matters more than raw token throughput.

Skip it for short, factual outputs where repetition is unlikely anyway.

The gotcha most teams miss is that standard Gen-PPL metrics reward the very repetition the attractor produces, so always measure against a human reference corpus instead.
