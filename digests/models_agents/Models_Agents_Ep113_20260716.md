**Ring-Zero: Scaling Zero RL to a Trillion Parameters for Emergent Reasoning: arXiv NLP**
A 1T-parameter model trained with zero RL (verifiable rewards, no human annotations) reached competitive results on seven math benchmarks after a two-phase training run. The pipeline added clipped importance sampling and mixed-precision controls to keep training stable at this scale. Emergent behaviors observed include self-verification and parallel reasoning traces. Teams scaling RL beyond 100B parameters should examine the reported training-inference ratio correction. The work directly tests the open question of whether reasoning gains from test-time compute continue to scale linearly once models exceed several hundred billion parameters. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12395)

**Evaluating Health Misinformation in Low-Resource Languages: Integrating Small Language Models with a Culturally-Sensitive Responsible NLP Framework (Bangla as a Case Study): arXiv NLP**
Phi-4 was the strongest small model for Bangla health-misinformation claim extraction in the reported experiments. The authors paired it with a responsible-NLP framework that adds cultural-sensitivity and harm-potential checks. The work targets low-resource settings where large models remain impractical. Anyone building detection pipelines for non-English health content can start with the Phi-4 baseline and the released framework. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12336)

### Practical & Community
**Policy-Conditioned Constrained Decoding for Column-Level Access Control in Text-to-SQL: arXiv NLP**
PCC-SQL applies a per-token logits mask derived from column-use policies so that single-query violations are deterministically eliminated during decoding. It achieves 0 % leakage and up to 88.7 % coverage on Spider-CU while staying within +10 % tokens of direct prompting. The approach works across three open-source models and three benchmarks. Teams that need deterministic policy enforcement in Text-to-SQL pipelines can adopt the grammar-aligned masking technique immediately. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12341)

**QUBO-Optimized Evidence Selection for Retrieval-Augmented Question Answering with Unconventional Solvers: arXiv NLP**
Evidence selection is cast as a QUBO problem that balances relevance, requirement coverage, redundancy, and compactness. The selector matches LLM-based set selectors on HotpotQA exact-match and token-F1 while remaining solver-compatible. This separates combinatorial selection from semantic generation, letting teams reserve LLMs for answer synthesis. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.12334)

**PalmClaw: A Native On-Device Agent Framework for Mobile Phones: arXiv NLP**
PalmClaw runs the full agent loop, memory, skills, and tools directly on-device and exposes mobile capabilities as explicit device tools. It records an 11.5 % relative task-success gain and 94.9 % reduction in completion time versus the strongest baseline. The design keeps every action inside clear execution boundaries. Mobile developers looking for a native alternative to GUI-automation agents can start from the open-source release. Source: [arxiv.org](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13027)

### Under the Hood: Cost-Aware Speculative Decoding for MoE
Everyone talks about speculative decoding as a simple “draft-and-verify” speed-up. In practice the technique collides with the non-uniform memory cost of Mixture-of-Experts inference. When high-probability draft tokens route to disjoint experts, the verification step triggers scattered expert loads that erase most of the latency win. EcoSpec therefore adds a lightweight expert predictor and a dynamic buffer that prefers draft paths whose experts are already resident in the current verification set. The added predictor costs a few milliseconds yet reclaims up to 1.62× end-to-end speedup on 671B-scale MoE models by cutting expert-weight traffic. The key engineering decision is to keep the target model’s verification rule unchanged so the method works as a drop-in draft selector. Use it when your MoE workload is memory-bandwidth bound and you already have a fast expert predictor; skip it if expert activation is already dense or your draft model is tiny enough that scattering is rare.
