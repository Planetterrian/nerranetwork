OK, let's pop the hood on vector search indexes.

In-memory HNSW delivers single-digit millisecond queries but requires the entire graph to fit in RAM.

Teams quickly hit expensive instance sizes once collections exceed a few hundred million vectors.

Disk-based approaches such as SPANN and DiskANN keep only a small hot set in memory and fetch compressed neighbor lists from SSD.

This adds roughly ten to thirty milliseconds per query while cutting RAM needs by five to ten times on the same dataset.

The quality gap appears mainly at high recall targets above ninety five percent, where disk methods need extra re-ranking passes.

When your working set stays under roughly fifty million vectors and sub-ten millisecond latency matters more than cost, stay in RAM.

Once collections grow larger or you need to serve many tenants on the same hardware, move to a disk-aware index and budget for the extra re-ranking step.

The gotcha that bites most teams is underestimating how quickly re-ranking cost grows once recall targets push past zero point nine five on billion-scale collections.
