One extra learned parameter per head is the cost when we pop the hood on First Passage Attention.

The mechanism replaces monotonic accumulation of decay along the key-query path with a path-extrema operation.

That operation lowers a key's logit by the deepest dip of a learned scalar field below that key's level.

When the field and the levels follow a shared learned walk, the mechanism naturally forms a stack.

A constant-dimension head can then realise any subset of keys left alive by subsequent dips.

In synthetic experiments a single-layer FPA head converges to the target alive-key sets on every seed.

Comparable softmax and Forgetting-Transformer baselines fail on all seeds.

At one hundred ten million parameters the model matches Forgetting-Transformer perplexity.

It still achieves perfect passkey retrieval at eight times the training length even when its key-value cache is compressed to roughly twenty-five percent.

The added scalar field introduces that one extra learned parameter per head and a small constant-time max-tracking operation per step.

Use FPA when long-context retrieval stability matters more than the marginal training cost of the extra field.

The main gotcha is that the learned walk must be shared between field and levels, otherwise the stack property disappears and performance falls back to softmax levels.
