Now let's pop the hood on synthetic data homogenization.

Everyone talks about model “sameness” as an inevitable side effect of scale.

In practice it emerges from the structure of the synthetic data flywheel itself.

When successive models train on outputs that already share overlapping token distributions and hedging patterns, the next generation inherits a narrower set of stylistic attractors.

This compounds across turns because each new sample reinforces the same high-probability continuations rather than introducing fresh variance.

The effect is measurable in comparative evals once you push past generic prompts into niche domains, where outputs converge on cadence and blind spots even before full collapse occurs.

Teams that fine-tune exclusively on human-curated data see the homogenization slow, but the cost is steep and the data volume is limited.

The practical decision is whether your workload tolerates reduced texture in exchange for cheaper generation or whether you need to maintain a parallel human seed set specifically to counteract the flywheel.
