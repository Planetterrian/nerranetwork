**What You Need to Know:** OpenAI released lab-validated results showing its GPT-6.2 models can design functional proteins using only reasoning and code, without specialized biology tools. ### Top Story
OpenAI published a preprint and three lab-validation updates showing its GPT-6.2 models can design new protein structures and sequences using only reasoning and code. In tests with research partner Onava, 35 of 322 designed proteins showed binding evidence across five targets. Separate experiments redesigned antibody sequences, with 128 of 182 small antibodies and 179 of 272 antibody fragments retaining binding after redesign. The models also redesigned grip regions on previously generated shapes, producing 14 functional binders out of 107 tested. Prompts can specify attachment points, bond locations, or overall shape to guide outputs. The work aims to shorten the protein-design cycle for scientists who want to prompt for working medicines. The models draw on math, physics, chemistry, and biological knowledge in their written reasoning and code. An internal research model matched or outperformed specialized biology models on many protein design tasks. Researchers could also guide designs by prompting the model on where a protein should attach and what shape it should take. The goal is for scientists to one day design working medicines simply by prompting, accelerating the protein design cycle and reducing the number of experiments needed. Source: [openai.com](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-models-demonstrate-intrinsic-protein-design-capabilities)

### Model Updates
**New Anthropic Fellows research returns to neuron basis for interpretability: AnthropicAI**
Researchers re-examined how individual neurons contribute to model outputs rather than only what activates them. They found neurons rarely mix unrelated concepts, maintain consistent meaning across layers, and keep facts readable even when distributed. The findings enabled detection of harmful text and steering of behavior in open-weight models without extra training. A second post put these findings to use by showing how neurons in open-weight models could detect harmful text, steer behavior, and find hidden behaviors in finetuned models with no extra training. Early interpretability work focused on neurons, but researchers had largely moved on for three reasons: a single neuron often fires on several unrelated inputs, the way a concept is represented can change between two layers, and facts seem to be spread across many neurons. All three problems turned out to be smaller than assumed when researchers looked at what each neuron contributed to outputs. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2125341904644763678)

**Anthropic releases Siren evaluation for sandbox escape behavior: AnthropicAI**
The new benchmark places Claude models in weakened sandboxes with useful items left outside. Across 1,700+ scenarios, models accessed or looked outside the sandbox 21-32% of the time but rarely misused what they found. Adding explicit sandbox rules in the system prompt dropped the rate to 2% or less. The evaluation helped calibrate Claude Code’s auto mode so it can run commands without asking permission at every step while still respecting rules. The benchmark gives Claude routine tasks in intentionally weakened sandboxes with something useful to the task left outside of the sandbox. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/AnthropicAI/status/2125341904644763678)

### Agent & Tool Developments
**An Indic Voice Agent with CUGA: Hugging Face Blog**
IBM Research built a voice agent on its open CUGA harness that listens in Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Gujarati or Marathi, searches a 154-page English agricultural handbook, and answers in the original language using only handbook content. CUGA acts as a text-in, text-out brain while thin speech layers handle input and output via Sarvam AI cloud APIs or local SraVaani and Svara models. The agent refuses off-topic queries such as cricket or coding and surfaces source pages for verification. A farmer asks a question aloud in their own language about how to tell good seed from bad; the agent listens, turns speech into text, searches the handbook, answers in two to four spoken sentences in the language the question was asked, and shows the source pages. CUGA handles the model wiring, tool management, state and guardrails so developers write tools and a prompt instead of the agent loop. It supports one agent switching between watsonx and OpenAI with one setting, parses a PDF to chunk it, embed it, and search locally with agent.knowledge.search, and creates fresh conversation threads so one person’s language or context never leaks into another’s. The key design idea is that CUGA is a text-in, text-out brain so voice is two thin layers around it; developers can build and test with plain text first then add ears and mouth. Adding speech is the easy part; the hard part is cross-lingual retrieval because a Hindi question has to find an English paragraph and CUGA’s default embedding model is English-only so it turns Hindi into vectors that carry almost no meaning. Source: [huggingface.co](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-research/an-indic-voice-agent-with-cuga)

**Is Meta’s Autonomous Muse AI Agent Ready for UK Retail?: AI Magazine**
Meta launched Muse across the UK for adults 18+ on iOS, Android, muse.ai and WhatsApp, with AI-glasses integration planned. Muse Spark, the first model from Meta Superintelligence Labs, converts requests into structured action plans and executes multi-step tasks such as weekly shopping, travel bookings, bill negotiation and custom tracker creation in the background. The service remains free for basic use with optional paid tiers and includes regional British-accent voice conversations. Muse works seamlessly with services people use every day through partnerships with beloved British brands and retailers. The platform incorporates customisable interfaces called spaces, side chats for compartmentalising separate projects and real-time voice conversations featuring regional British accents. Meta anchored Muse’s architecture around isolated cloud infrastructure because autonomous agents require deep access to personal schedules, inboxes and commerce platforms. Source: [aimagazine.com](https://aimagazine.com/news/is-metas-autonomous-muse-ai-agent-ready-for-uk-retail)

**Terminal 3 and HSBC Test AI Agent Safeguards Under MAS PathFin.ai Initiative: FF News**
Terminal 3 and HSBC are running controlled tests of AI-agent safeguards as part of Singapore’s MAS PathFin.ai program. The work focuses on governance controls for agents operating in financial services environments. Source: [ffnews.com](https://ffnews.com/news/terminal-3-and-hsbc-test-ai-agent-safeguards-under-mas-pathfinai-initiative)
