OK, let's pop the hood on diffusion decoding for L L M's.

The technique replaces sequential token-by-token sampling with iterative denoising of a full latent sequence.

The model starts with random noise and applies a learned denoiser for a fixed number of steps, refining every position simultaneously.

Expensive operations become matrix multiplies instead of memory-bound KV cache reads, which is why Zyphra saw seven point seven times speedups on the eight B mixture-of-experts model.

Training adds a second loss term and requires careful scheduling of noise levels across diffusion steps.

Quality matches autoregressive baselines only when the schedule is tuned per model size.

Below roughly seven B parameters the gap widens quickly.

When you need maximum tokens per second on short-to-medium outputs and can accept a small quality regression during early training, diffusion decoding is worth trying.

For long coherent generation or when you must stay inside existing autoregressive fine-tunes, the classic next-token approach remains simpler.

The common gotcha is assuming any diffusion schedule works.

Without per-model calibration the output often collapses into repetitive patterns after step four.
