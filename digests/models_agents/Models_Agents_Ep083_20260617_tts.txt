OK, let's pop the hood on speculative decoding because everyone talks about it as simply running a small draft model then verifying.

In practice the technique is a balanced pipeline whose gains depend on acceptance-rate statistics and KV-cache reuse patterns.

The draft model proposes several tokens in one forward pass while the target model verifies them in parallel and accepts the longest correct prefix.

Higher acceptance rates come from training the drafter on the target’s output distribution yet each extra draft token still costs compute that must be amortized.

Real speedups of two to three times appear most reliably on repetitive or structured text where acceptance lengths stay above two point five tokens on average.

On highly creative generation the acceptance rate often drops below one point eight and the overhead can erase gains.

Measure acceptance length on your target workload first because if it stays under two point zero tokens you are better off with standard decoding or continuous batching.
