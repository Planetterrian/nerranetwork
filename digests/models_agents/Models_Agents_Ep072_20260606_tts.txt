OK, let's pop the hood on KV cache quantization.

Everyone talks about it as a simple turn-it-on-and-save-VRAM toggle.

In practice it is a precision-versus-throughput negotiation that depends on model architecture, context length, and downstream task.

Attention keys and values are not equally sensitive to quantization.

Value vectors often tolerate lower precision better than keys because they are summed rather than used for similarity lookups.

Lower-bit KV formats therefore introduce small per-token errors that accumulate across long contexts.

That is why some teams see reasoning degradation only after thirty two k to sixty four k tokens even when perplexity looks acceptable.

New methods such as KVarN attempt to close this gap by learning per-channel scales that preserve the distribution of important value dimensions.

They deliver q five level KLD at roughly four bit storage in early Lah-mah dot cpp tests.

Aggressive KV quantization can still hurt multi-step agent trajectories more than single-turn chat because small embedding drift compounds when the model must maintain consistent state across tool calls.

When you are running agents with fifty k plus context or long-horizon planning, keep at least five to six bits on the value cache unless you have measured task-specific tolerance.

For shorter chat workloads the memory win is usually worth it.
