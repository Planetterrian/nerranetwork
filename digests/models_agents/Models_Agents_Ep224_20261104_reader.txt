Transformer layers are sequential transformation blocks; each applies self-attention to mix information across the sequence, then a feed-forward network that refines every token's representation, with residual connections and normalization around both.

Stacking more layers lets the network build progressively higher-level features and longer-range dependencies, because early layers capture local patterns while later layers integrate them into abstract reasoning, so depth increases both expressive capacity and the amount of sequential computation performed on hidden states.

The original Attention Is All You Need paper showed that this architecture alone, without recurrence or convolutions, matched or exceeded recurrent models on translation while being far more parallelizable.

Later work on scaling to one thousand layers showed that a modified residual connection called DeepNorm, plus theoretically derived initialization, keeps updates stable where plain post layer-norm or pre layer-norm variants diverge.

In practice the quality gain from added depth diminishes once models exceed roughly seventy billion parameters unless training data and optimization are scaled accordingly.

When choosing depth versus width, teams should first verify that their optimizer and normalization scheme remain stable at the target depth; otherwise width scaling or recurrent reuse of the same block is often cheaper to stabilize.
