with full training code and data. Okay, let's pop the hood on diffusion decoding for LLMs.
The technique replaces sequential token by token sampling with iterative denoising of a full
latent sequence. The model starts with random noise and applies a learned denoiser for a fixed number
of steps, refining every position simultaneously. Expensive operations become matrix multiplies
instead of memory bound KV cache reads, which is why Zifra saw 7.7 times speed ups on the 8B
mixture of experts model. Training adds a second loss term and requires careful scheduling of noise
levels across diffusion steps. Quality matches autoregressive baselines only when the schedule is
tuned per model size. Below roughly 7B parameters, the gap widens quickly.
When you need maximum tokens per second on short to medium outputs and can accept a small quality
regression during early training, diffusion decoding is worth trying. For long coherent generation
or when you must stay inside existing autoregressive fine tunes, the classic next token approach remains
simpler. The common gotcha is assuming any diffusion schedule works. Without per model calibration,
the output often collapses into repetitive patterns after step 4. Tomorrow, keep an eye on Zifra's
