### Top 12 Space & Astronomy Stories

1. **ESA selects Plasma Observatory as next medium-class mission** — European Space Agency
   The mission will fly seven coordinated spacecraft. They will measure how solar energy flows through Earth’s magnetic shield at multiple scales of distance and time. The selection gives the project formal approval to proceed into detailed development. Source: [esa.int](https://www.esa.int/articles/about-us/directorates/science/seven-spacecraft-observatory-to-study-plasma-universe)

2. **Israel proposes new space law on launches and asteroid mining** — The Jerusalem Post
   The draft law would require permits for satellite launches, satellite sales, and asteroid mining activities. It aims to regulate commercial and national space operations under a single licensing framework. Source: [jpost.com](https://www.jpost.com/science/space/article-915623)

3. **Aldebaran is an impostor in the Hyades star cluster** — Space.com
   Aldebaran appears to the eye as the brightest member of the Hyades but lies at a different distance. Its proper motion and parallax measurements show it is unrelated to the cluster’s stars. The discovery illustrates how line-of-sight alignments can mislead visual impressions of stellar groups. Source: [space.com](https://www.space.com/stargazing/theres-an-impostor-hiding-in-the-hyades-star-cluster-can-you-spot-it)

4. **New method extracts oxygen from lunar soil** — Phys.org
   Researchers combined semiconductor manufacturing techniques with lunar regolith chemistry. The process releases oxygen while producing usable metals as by-products. The approach could reduce the mass of oxygen that future lunar bases must carry from Earth. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-oxygen-lunar-soil-bases-moon.html)

5. **JWST detects pulsed infrared emission from magnetar 4U 0142+61** — AAS Nova
   The James Webb Space Telescope recorded infrared pulses from the magnetar. The pulses align in time with the object’s known X-ray pulses. The overlap indicates the infrared emission originates inside the magnetosphere rather than from a surrounding disk. Source: [doi.org](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-4357/ae9745#new_tab)

6. **Study examines physics of cannibal coronal mass ejections** — AAS Nova
   Successive eruptions from the same solar active region can merge into a single, more energetic event. The research models how the leading ejection clears a path that allows the following one to travel faster and farther. Understanding these mergers improves forecasts of geomagnetic storms at Earth. Source: [aasnova.org](https://aasnova.org/2026/11/20/launching-an-investigation-into-cannibal-coronal-mass-ejections/)

7. **Astronomers track shifting pulsation modes in δ Scuti star KIC 4737720** — Phys.org
   The star shows multiple simultaneous pulsation modes that change over time. High-precision brightness measurements reveal how these modes interact inside the star. The data provide a direct probe of the star’s internal density and temperature structure. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-shifting-pulsation-modes-scuti-star.html)

8. **Venus-like conditions tested for preserving ancient water traces** — Phys.org
   Laboratory experiments exposed minerals to high temperature and pressure similar to Venus’s surface. Certain minerals retained chemical signatures of past water even after prolonged exposure. The results suggest that some Venus surface rocks could still hold evidence of earlier habitable conditions. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-venus-conditions-reveal-minerals-ancient.html)

9. **Low-viscosity lavas offer clues to early Mars history** — Phys.org
   Perseverance images of the Séítah formation show textures consistent with very fluid lava flows. The flows date to roughly four billion years ago. Their low viscosity implies higher eruption temperatures or different composition than typical Earth basalts. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-viscosity-lavas-reveal-key-mars.html)

10. **ALMA images giant gas ring around a dying star** — Phys.org
    The star is shedding its outer layers and forming a large expanding ring of gas. ALMA observations also show an emerging hourglass shape in the inner regions. The structures record the final stages of stellar evolution before the star becomes a planetary nebula. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-star-gasps-alma-reveals-giant.html)
