### Cosmic Spotlight
The James Webb Space Telescope captured powerful outflows of gas streaming from young galaxies in the early universe. These winds appear strong enough to strip away the raw material needed for new stars, offering a direct look at how some galaxies stopped growing shortly after the Big Bang. Spectra reveal the outflows' speed and composition, showing they originate from intense star formation or central black hole activity. What remains unclear is whether these winds permanently quench galaxies or merely pause their growth until fresh gas arrives from the surrounding cosmic web.

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Radar Satellites Track Seasonal Change from Orbit
If you stacked every Sentinel-1 radar image taken over Buenos Aires in a single year, the stack would be taller than Mount Everest yet still fit inside a single laptop. The satellite fires microwave pulses toward the ground and records the faint echoes that bounce back, measuring surface roughness and moisture at centimetre wavelengths even through clouds or darkness. Over Buenos Aires the same fields shift from blue to green to red in the composite because March crops scatter the signal differently than January bare soil or May regrowth. A single pixel can register changes as small as a few millimetres in vegetation height across hundreds of square kilometres. The open question is how to separate the radar signature of a new irrigation project from the signature of an unexpected rainstorm when both produce identical bright returns.
