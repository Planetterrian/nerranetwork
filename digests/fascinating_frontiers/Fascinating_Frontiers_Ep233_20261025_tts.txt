Researchers applied a new model to an M six point five class solar flare observed on June twenty-second, twenty fifteen, by the Interface Region Imaging Spectrograph satellite.

The model quantifies how flare ribbon substructure evolves and links those changes to fragmentation in the reconnecting current sheet in the corona, Phys dot org reports.

Flare ribbons form when high-energy particles slam back into the chromosphere along magnetic field lines.

The team found that energy deposition occurs in irregular bursts rather than a smooth process, with ribbons breaking into fragments of varying sizes.

Lead author Marcel Corchado Albelo noted the work provides the first experimental demonstration of a physical relationship between observed ribbon shapes and the structure of the energy conversion region.

Co-author Maria Kazachenko stated that simulations had long predicted ribbon boundary behavior but lacked observational confirmation until this quantitative test.

A single solar flare can release energy equivalent to the simultaneous detonation of millions of hydrogen bombs, yet the conversion of stored magnetic energy stays hidden behind the Sun's bright surface.

Ride a magnetic field line as plasma twists and shears it until the line can no longer hold the stress, then it snaps, reconnects into a simpler shape, and dumps heat and accelerated particles.

Those particles stream downward along newly formed field lines, and the fine structure in the ribbons they light records breakup happening far above in the corona.

The quantitative model ties the spatial complexity of those ribbon boundaries directly to the breakup of the reconnecting current sheet.

That turns the ribbons into a remote diagnostic of coronal physics that cannot be watched on the spot.

One persistent mystery is why the fragmentation occurs at the particular scales in the data.

The model reproduces the pattern, yet it leaves open which instability or turbulence process sets the fragment sizes that control how and where the Sun's magnetic energy is released.
