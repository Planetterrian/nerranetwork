### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Tiny Distant Worlds Hold Onto Atmospheres
If Pluto were scaled to the size of a grapefruit, this newly observed body would be no larger than a lemon, yet it still supports a detectable gaseous envelope. Gravity on such a small object is weak, so any gas molecules should quickly reach escape velocity and drift away into the vacuum. Instead, the atmosphere appears replenished by slow sublimation from surface ices that have remained cold enough over billions of years to trap volatiles. Temperatures hover near 40 kelvin, where even trace amounts of nitrogen or methane can linger in equilibrium between solid and gas phases. The exact balance between escape and replenishment remains unclear, leaving astronomers wondering which internal heat source or seasonal cycle keeps the layer intact when similar objects show none at all.
