### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **ESA Opens New UK Space Gateway** — ESA Activities
   King Charles III visited the UK’s largest space cluster on 10 July to launch the new initiative designed to shape the future of the space and defence economy. The gateway strengthens ties between the UK and ESA across civil space programmes and defence applications. It builds directly on existing UK facilities that already support multiple ESA missions in satellite technology and launch services. Officials expect the centre to coordinate future joint projects, including shared infrastructure for workforce development and technology transfer. Formal agreements on operational scope and funding contributions are the next milestones.

2. **ESA Plans New Centre in Warsaw** — TVP World
   The European Space Agency will establish a dedicated facility in the Polish capital focused on research, technology development, and industry partnerships. This marks an expansion of ESA’s physical presence into Central Europe beyond its current member-state sites. Local authorities are preparing sites and support structures to host the new operations. Construction timelines and the initial programme focus areas, such as satellite systems and data services, remain under discussion between ESA and Polish partners.

3. **India Outlines Next Decade of Human Spaceflight** — The New Indian Express
   Astronaut Shubhanshu Shukla described plans that include an Indian space station and crewed lunar missions as part of the next decade’s roadmap. The programme builds on current robotic lunar efforts and the ongoing Gaganyaan human spaceflight project. Engineers are developing life-support systems and docking technologies required for long-duration flights and station assembly. International partnerships are being explored to accelerate capability development in propulsion and habitat modules. The first crewed flight remains targeted within the coming years.

4. **China’s Tianwen-2 Reaches Quasi-Moon** — r/space
   The probe has arrived at Earth’s quasi-satellite 469219 Kamoʻoalewa following its rendezvous. Mission controllers are now assessing the asteroid’s composition and rotation state using onboard remote-sensing instruments. The spacecraft carries hardware for both remote sensing and potential sample collection. Data returned so far confirm the target’s stable orbit relative to Earth. Further manoeuvres will refine the observation campaign before any contact or sampling attempt.

5. **Reflect Orbital Wins First Mirror Launch Approval** — Space.com
   The company received regulatory clearance to fly its initial light-directing satellite later this year. The test craft will demonstrate controlled reflection of sunlight toward specific ground locations. Plans call for thousands of additional mirrors over the next decade if the technology proves viable. Engineers must still address orbital debris and brightness concerns raised during the review process. The first launch window opens in the coming months.

6. **Helio Completes Antenna Vibration Tests** — Yahoo Finance
   The deployable antenna system passed critical vibration trials under a NASA Phase II SBIR contract. The hardware is designed for small satellites operating in demanding launch environments. Successful testing moves the project closer to orbital demonstration. Engineers recorded performance data across multiple frequency ranges during the campaign. Follow-on work will focus on thermal and deployment verification before flight.

7. **China Advances Methalox Long March 10C** — SpaceNews
   The Long March 10C variant has been designated the primary commercial launch vehicle following the first successful booster recovery. The rocket uses liquid methane and liquid oxygen engines to enable rapid reuse. Additional funding has been allocated to several commercial rocket developers working alongside the state programme. Recovery operations will be refined on upcoming flights. The vehicle is expected to support both government and private payloads in the commercial market.

8. **M29 Open Cluster Rises in Cygnus** — Astronomy
   The young star cluster sits just under 8° south of Gamma Cygni and is already well placed after sunset. Observers can locate it with binoculars or small telescopes under modest light pollution. The cluster contains several bright blue stars that stand out against the surrounding Milky Way field. Its distance and age make it a useful target for studying recent star formation. Conditions remain favourable through the coming week.

9. **NASA CubeSat Readies for Technology Tests** — ASDNews
   The small satellite will demonstrate new components in low Earth orbit. Mission planners selected the CubeSat platform to reduce cost and shorten development time. Instruments aboard will collect performance data on propulsion, communications, and sensors. Launch is scheduled aboard a rideshare opportunity later this year. Results will inform designs for future operational missions.

10. **JWST Watches Hot Jupiter’s Contrasting Hemispheres** — Universe Today
    The telescope captured simultaneous views of the planet’s dayside and nightside. Strong winds appear to strip water molecules apart on the hotter face while the cooler side remains comparatively calm. The observations highlight gaps in current atmospheric models that do not fully account for such sharp gradients. Data were collected during a single transit event using multiple instruments. Follow-up programmes will target similar worlds to test the findings. Remember, the show covered JWST exoplanet work in Ep127; these new hemisphere contrasts directly address open questions on biosignature detection prospects by exposing model shortfalls in extreme weather regimes.

11. **Voyager 1 Remains Within One Light-Day** — r/space
    After nearly 49 years the spacecraft has reached only 166 AU, roughly 24.8 billion kilometres from Earth. The distance underscores how vast interstellar space remains even at record speeds. Both Voyager probes continue to return measurements from the heliopause region. Power levels are declining but instruments are still operational. Scientists expect data collection to continue into the 2030s.

12. **Underground Detector Seeks Supernova Background** — Universe Today
    A kilometre-deep instrument in Japan recorded a possible first signal from the cumulative neutrino flux of all past supernovae. Confirmation would provide a new census of stellar deaths across cosmic time. The detector’s sensitivity allows it to register events far below the threshold of individual supernova observations. Analysis is ongoing to distinguish the signal from background noise. Additional data runs are planned through the coming year.

13. **Moon’s Water Mostly Locked Deep Inside** — Universe Today
    Most lunar water is thought to exist in chemically bound form within the mantle rather than as ice at the poles. Polar crater deposits represent only a small fraction of the total inventory. Sample analysis and remote sensing both point to an interior source that has not fully outgassed. Future landers may drill or use seismic methods to measure the deeper reservoir. Understanding this distribution affects resource estimates for sustained lunar presence.

14. **Gravitational Waves Expose Black Hole Formation Channels** — Phys.org Space
    LIGO, Virgo and KAGRA have now catalogued hundreds of mergers, revealing distinct populations. Some events match predictions for isolated binary evolution while others suggest dynamical formation in dense clusters. The growing sample allows statisticians to separate the two channels for the first time. Continued observations will tighten constraints on progenitor masses and merger rates. Next observing runs aim to double the current event count.

15. **Euclid Finds Oldest Known Quasars** — Phys.org Space
    The space telescope identified quasars at greater distances than any previously recorded. Their existence at such early cosmic times challenges models of how supermassive black holes grew so quickly. Spectra confirm the objects are powered by accretion onto massive central engines. The discoveries add new data points to the timeline of reionisation. Additional candidates from the survey are under review.

### Cosmic Spotlight
Astronomers using JWST observed a tidally locked hot Jupiter showing two dramatically different faces at once. The dayside appears battered by fierce winds that break apart water molecules, while the nightside remains calmer. The data expose shortcomings in current atmospheric models that fail to predict such sharp contrasts. Follow-up observations of similar planets will test whether this pattern is common or exceptional. These results extend the JWST exoplanet programme by supplying direct evidence of violent chemistry that earlier models overlooked.

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Tidally Locked Worlds Create Extreme Weather
If Earth were locked so one side always faced the Sun, the dayside would roast at temperatures hot enough to melt lead while the nightside plunged below freezing. On a hot Jupiter orbiting its star in just a few days, that contrast becomes extreme enough to drive planet-wide winds faster than any hurricane on Earth. The permanent day side can exceed 2,000 °C while the night side stays hundreds of degrees cooler, forcing a powerful circulation cell that carries heat across the terminator. JWST has now measured these winds indirectly by watching how different molecules appear or vanish between the two hemispheres during a single transit. The mechanism is simple in principle yet produces chemistry that current models still cannot fully reproduce, especially the apparent destruction of water on the hot side. The open question is whether these same winds also mix heavier elements upward from deeper layers or whether the atmosphere remains stratified despite the violent flow. No one yet knows how the vertical mixing scales with wind speed across the full range of observed hot Jupiters.
