### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **Webb Reveals Dusty Core of Centaurus A** — NASA
   The James Webb Space Telescope will mark four years of science operations on July 12. Its infrared vision pierced thick dust lanes to expose the starry center of galaxy Centaurus A in greater detail than previous observations. The image highlights structures shaped by a past cosmic collision. Scientists will continue using the telescope to map similar dust-obscured regions across nearby galaxies. Next data releases are expected to include additional early-universe targets observed during the same cycle.

2. **Neutrino Whispers Detected from Ancient Supernovae** — Space.com
   Researchers report the first possible signals of neutrinos produced by supernova explosions over billions of years. These ghost particles travel vast distances and interact so weakly that only rare underground detectors can register them. The signals appear as a diffuse background rather than individual events. Confirmation would link current observations directly to the cumulative history of stellar deaths. Follow-up analyses of detector data are already underway.

3. **Prestellar Cores Show Internal Drift Before Collapse** — Phys.org
   Radio telescopes captured gas motions inside cold, dense prestellar cores where stars like the Sun form. Gravity holds the cores together while internal motions reveal how material drifts before final collapse. The observations help clarify why some cores form single stars and others produce multiples. Specific velocity patterns measured across several cores now constrain formation models. Additional mapping campaigns with the same instruments are scheduled for later this year.

4. **New Horizons Awakens After 321-Day Hibernation** — r/space
   Flight controllers at Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory confirmed New Horizons resumed operations on June 23 after nearly a year in sleep mode. The spacecraft sits roughly 5.9 billion miles from Earth, where signals take eight hours and fifty-two minutes to travel. Instruments will soon downlink heliospheric plasma data and perform health checks. The Venetia Burney Student Dust Counter continues collecting Kuiper Belt particle counts. Software upgrades for distant-Sun operations are being tested through the end of the year.

5. **China Recovers Long March 10B First Stage at Sea** — SpaceNews
   The maiden flight of China’s Long March 10B lifted off at 0415 UTC on July 10 from Hainan. Its kerolox first stage landed successfully on a downrange barge, marking the country’s first orbital booster recovery. The methalox second stage placed an unidentified payload into orbit. This achievement places China alongside only one other nation with demonstrated orbital-stage reuse capability. Additional recovery attempts are planned for upcoming flights.

6. **Falcon Heavy Assigned to Launch Roman Space Telescope** — Teslarati
   NASA selected SpaceX’s Falcon Heavy to carry the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope to orbit this August. The telescope will survey wide areas of the sky for exoplanets and dark energy signatures. The mission represents one of NASA’s next flagship astrophysics projects after Webb. Ground teams are completing final integration and testing at the launch site. Launch vehicle certification reviews remain the next major milestone.

7. **Anil Menon Joins Expedition 74 Crew on Soyuz** — NASA
   NASA astronaut Anil Menon will launch aboard Soyuz MS-29 on July 14 with cosmonauts Pyotr Dubrov and Anna Kikina. The eight-month mission will join the Expedition 74 crew aboard the International Space Station. Menon’s assignment continues NASA’s participation in long-duration station research. Training for the crew concluded earlier this month at the Gagarin Cosmonaut Training Center. Live coverage of the launch is scheduled through NASA channels.

8. **Sentinel-2 Captures Great Bear Lake in Detail** — European Space Agency
   The Copernicus Sentinel-2 mission returned a striking color image of Canada’s Great Bear Lake. The satellite data highlight water color variations and surrounding terrain features. Such observations support ongoing studies of northern lake systems and seasonal ice cover. The image forms part of ESA’s regular Earth-from-space series. Additional passes over the same region are planned throughout the summer.

9. **Wally Funk, Early Spaceflight Pioneer, Dies at 87** — Phys.org
   Aviation pioneer Wally Funk, who became the oldest woman to fly in space, has passed away. She flew aboard a Blue Origin suborbital flight in 2021 at age 82. Funk’s career included work as one of the Mercury 13 women tested for astronaut fitness in the 1960s. Her flight demonstrated expanded opportunities for private citizens in space. Memorial statements from the commercial spaceflight community are expected in coming days.

10. **Upper Scorpius Dominates Evening Sky This Week** — Sky & Telescope
    The bright pattern of Upper Scorpius appears prominently in the southern sky just after nightfall. Telescopic observers can target star clusters and nebulae within the constellation. In the west, Regulus is departing from Venus. The Big Dipper remains visible to the lower left of Polaris for northern viewers. Weekly sky updates will continue through July 19.

11. **NISAR Data Maps Venezuela Earthquake Displacement** — NASA
    Radar measurements from the NISAR satellite recorded significant ground shifts in La Guaira and surrounding areas after June 2026 earthquakes. The data quantify surface movement across multiple fault segments. Such observations help refine models of regional seismic hazard. Processing of additional radar scenes is ongoing. The mission continues routine monitoring of active tectonic zones worldwide.

12. **Interior Department Seeks Offshore Launch Concepts** — SpaceNews
    An office within the U.S. Department of the Interior issued a request for information on offshore orbital launch options. The effort aims to reduce pressure on existing coastal spaceports. Concepts under consideration include mobile platforms and fixed marine structures. Responses will inform future regulatory and infrastructure planning. The comment period remains open for industry input.

13. **Esrange Spaceport Expansion Advances in Sweden** — Travel And Tour World
    Sweden is coordinating with Italy, France, and Germany plus European aerospace firms on upgrades at Spaceport Esrange. The project targets growth in commercial launches and new technology demonstrations. Expanded facilities will support both suborbital and orbital missions. Timeline milestones include infrastructure completion within the next several years. Additional partner announcements are anticipated.

14. **Solar Eclipse Videos Explore Other Worlds** — NASA
    New NASA animations compare solar eclipses across the solar system, including events on Jupiter and Saturn. The sequences illustrate how different moon sizes and orbital distances produce varied eclipse types. One video addresses the eventual end of total solar eclipses visible from Earth. The resources are intended for educational use. Additional planetary eclipse visualizations are in development.

15. **Alaska Sounding Rockets Study Sun-Earth Links** — NASA
    Researchers are launching sounding rockets from Alaska to examine solar flares, auroras, and related phenomena. The state’s position partly inside the Arctic Circle provides unique access to high-latitude space weather effects. Data will feed into models of the Sun-Earth system. Multiple flights are scheduled through the remainder of the season. Results will complement measurements from orbiting observatories.

### Cosmic Spotlight
New Horizons has returned to active duty after 321 days of hibernation, its instruments now streaming fresh measurements from the outer heliosphere. The spacecraft’s plasma sensors and dust counter continue recording conditions far beyond the planets, where solar wind interactions weaken and interstellar material begins to dominate. Mission teams will next downlink ultraviolet spectrograph observations of hydrogen gas distribution. With power levels declining and signal travel times stretching past nine hours, every downlink becomes a carefully planned window into a region visited by only two other spacecraft. What patterns will the dust detector reveal once the full dataset reaches Earth?

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Neutrinos Carry Echoes of Dead Stars
A single supernova can release roughly 10^58 neutrinos in a burst lasting seconds, yet these particles slip through entire planets without leaving a trace. Imagine riding alongside one of them as it exits the collapsing core: in less than a second it crosses the star’s outer layers, then spends thousands of years crossing the galaxy at nearly the speed of light while ordinary light and matter lag behind. Detectors buried kilometers underground register perhaps one interaction per day from the steady rain of neutrinos that have been arriving from ancient explosions across cosmic time. That background flux carries information about the total number of core-collapse events over billions of years, a census no telescope using light can match. The remaining puzzle is whether the energy spectrum of these ghost particles will finally reveal how often the most massive stars exploded in the early universe, or whether some other, still-unseen process is also feeding the signal.
