1. **ULA Launches 29 Amazon Leo Satellites on Atlas 5** — Spaceflight Now

2. **MAVEN Detects New Plasma Behavior at Mars** — Universe Today
   NASA’s MAVEN spacecraft observed unexpected plasma squeezing in the Martian atmosphere. The measurements show regions where the solar wind interacts differently than previously modeled. This finding refines understanding of how Mars lost much of its early atmosphere. Researchers will compare the data against future orbiter passes. Follow-up papers are expected once the full dataset is processed.

### Cosmic Spotlight
The MAVEN findings on plasma squeezing at Mars add a new layer to the long-running story of atmospheric loss on the Red Planet. The orbiter’s measurements show localized compression that earlier models did not predict, tightening constraints on how solar wind strips ions over time. This directly informs the open question of whether Mars ever held a thicker atmosphere capable of supporting surface liquid water for extended periods. Watch for the next MAVEN data release and any coordinated observations with the European Space Agency’s Trace Gas Orbiter.

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Red Dwarf Stars Erode Planetary Atmospheres
If the Sun were replaced by a typical red dwarf, Earth would orbit inside the star’s habitable zone yet face radiation levels hundreds of times higher than we receive today. Charged particles and intense ultraviolet flares slam into the upper atmosphere, heating it until gas molecules reach escape velocity. A single powerful flare can strip away thousands of tonnes of atmosphere in hours, and many red dwarfs produce such events several times per week. Over a billion years this relentless bombardment can reduce an Earth-like world to a thin remnant or bare rock. The exact balance between a planet’s magnetic field strength, its initial atmosphere mass, and the star’s activity history remains unknown, leaving astronomers unsure which worlds might retain enough air for life to persist.
