7. **Long Cloud Stretches Across Mars — NASA Breaking**
   An unusually long water-ice cloud known as the Arsia Mons Elongated Cloud forms near the volcano Arsia Mons only during spring and summer mornings. The cloud can extend more than 1500 kilometers as winds carry ice crystals downstream. It was first recorded by NASA’s Viking Orbiter 2 in 1977 and has been imaged in detail by ESA’s Mars Express spacecraft. The thin Martian atmosphere lacks enough dust at high altitudes for normal cloud formation, so researchers are studying alternative mechanisms. Continued observations aim to improve understanding of cloud processes on both Mars and Earth. Source: [science.nasa.gov](https://science.nasa.gov/image-article/apod-2026-november-2-a-long-cloud-on-mars/)

9. **JWST Spots Mysterious Single-Line Object in Deep Field — r/space**
   JWST detected a strong emission line at 1.60 micrometers in the Hubble Ultra Deep Field using a new slitless spectroscopy technique. The line appeared in four dither positions and two position angles across observations separated by nearly three months. No source is visible in any prior Hubble or JWST imaging at that location. The observed-frame equivalent width exceeds 7620 angstroms, ruling out several common emission lines at lower redshifts. Possible explanations include an active galactic nucleus or a transient event, but none fully match the data. ### Cosmic Spotlight
The Arsia Mons Elongated Cloud forms only in morning hours during Martian spring and summer yet stretches more than 1500 kilometers. Wind carries freshly formed water-ice crystals away from the volcano, creating a tail that persists long enough for Mars Express to photograph it in detail. Because the upper atmosphere holds so little dust, standard nucleation around particles cannot explain the crystals, forcing researchers to consider other triggers such as local temperature drops or wave dynamics. The same processes that shape this cloud may also operate in Earth’s thinner upper layers, offering a natural laboratory for testing cloud physics without the complicating effects of abundant aerosols. Continued seasonal monitoring could reveal whether the cloud’s length or timing is shifting as Mars experiences longer-term climate cycles.

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Thin Atmospheres Still Make Clouds
Mars holds an atmosphere roughly one percent as dense as Earth’s, yet it routinely produces water-ice clouds that stretch across thousands of kilometers. Picture a single teaspoon of Martian air at the altitude where these clouds form; it contains so few molecules that the same volume on Earth would barely register on a barometer. The Arsia Mons Elongated Cloud demonstrates how little material is needed: water vapor rises from the surface near the volcano, cools rapidly in the thin air, and condenses into ice crystals that winds then stretch into a tail longer than the distance from New York to Chicago. Mars Express has tracked the feature forming only in morning hours during spring and summer, when local temperatures drop just enough for ice to nucleate even without abundant dust grains to serve as seeds. The process repeats year after year, showing that cloud formation depends more on precise temperature thresholds than on the total amount of gas present. One open question is whether the same wave-driven cooling that appears to initiate the Arsia Mons cloud also operates at other longitudes, or whether the volcano’s topography creates a unique trigger that has no counterpart elsewhere on the planet.
