### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **Mega-Constellations Risk Runaway Orbital Chaos** — Universe Today
   A new preprint by Hugh G. Lewis at the University of Birmingham warns that current satellite megaconstellation plans could trigger cascading collisions in low Earth orbit. The analysis models how repeated launches and limited disposal options amplify collision probabilities over decades. Lewis stresses that without stricter end-of-life rules, including mandatory heliocentric disposal and published state vectors, the debris environment could become unmanageable. The paper examines specific growth rates in the latest commercial filings and compares them against existing mitigation guidelines. Follow-up work will test updated disposal scenarios against projected constellation sizes.

2. **Hubble Confirms Ancient Dwarf Galaxy Merger** — NASA Image of the Day
   Hubble observations of globular clusters have traced the chemical and orbital signatures of a dwarf galaxy named LKH that merged with the Milky Way roughly 12 billion years ago. The clusters retain distinct abundance patterns that match the accreted system's stars rather than the main galactic population. This evidence strengthens the picture of the Milky Way growing through repeated mergers in its early history. The same dataset is now being cross-checked with Gaia proper motions to map the remnant debris across the halo.

3. **NASA Funds State Workforce Hubs for Aerospace Jobs** — NASA Science
   Seven institutions will share approximately $10.5 million to build state-level pathways into skilled technical roles that do not require a bachelor's degree. The new NASA Aerospace Skilled Technical Workforce Hubs initiative targets shortages in manufacturing, testing, and operations support. Awards focus on partnerships between community colleges, industry, and state agencies to accelerate training pipelines. Early metrics will track enrollment growth and placement rates into NASA-related contractors.

4. **Russian Activity Prompts Antarctic Flight Turnaround** — Space.com
   A U.S. Air Force aircraft heading to Antarctica reversed course on August 18 after receiving a space-debris hazard alert tied to unspecified Russian operations over the South Pacific. The incident highlights how even routine polar flights now intersect with growing orbital traffic. Officials have not released the exact object or altitude involved. Similar alerts are expected to increase as launch rates rise.

5. **Radio Telescope Proposed for Lunar Far Side** — Universe Today
   A new arXiv preprint led by David DeBoer of the University of Oxford outlines a phased array concept that would operate in the radio-quiet zone behind the Moon. The design targets frequencies blocked or contaminated by Earth's ionosphere and human transmitters, including the still-unexplained 1977 "Wow!" signal band. The team argues construction must begin before additional lunar infrastructure adds its own interference. Next steps include site surveys from upcoming orbiters and prototype antenna tests on Earth analogs.

6. **Moog Expands METEORITE Bus for Maneuverable Missions** — SpaceNews
   Moog Inc. has announced expanded production capacity for its METEORITE satellite platform aimed at high-agility Earth-observation and defense payloads. The bus supports rapid orbit changes and precise pointing needed for responsive imaging. New configurations will accommodate larger propellant loads while maintaining the core attitude-control architecture. First flight units are slated for upcoming commercial and government manifests.

7. **JWST Data Show Ancient Galaxies Even More Massive** — Universe Today
   Revised stellar-mass estimates for early galaxies observed by JWST indicate they contain larger populations of low-mass stars than initial models assumed. The adjustment raises total masses and tightens the tension with standard formation timelines. Multi-wavelength follow-up is planned to refine the initial-mass-function assumptions used in the re-analysis. The findings come from reprocessing of the same deep-field datasets released earlier this year.

8. **Deep Partial Lunar Eclipse Set for August 27–28** — Sky & Telescope
   Observers across the Americas and Europe will see the Moon pass through the outer portion of Earth's umbral shadow on the night of August 27–28. The event completes a rare August sequence that also included a total solar eclipse and the Perseid peak. Maximum eclipse occurs when roughly 94 percent of the Moon's diameter lies inside the umbra. Timing predictions and visibility maps are already posted for public use.

9. **Fastest Known Milky Way Star Tests Black Hole Spin** — Phys.org Space
   The star S301, detected with ESO's VLTI, reaches 25,000 km/s while passing closer to Sagittarius A* than any previously tracked object. Its orbit is tight enough for the black hole's frame-dragging effect to measurably alter the path. Continued VLTI monitoring over the next several years will tighten constraints on the spin parameter. The measurement offers an independent check on earlier Event Horizon Telescope results.

10. **VR Study Explores Astronaut Mental Health** — Phys.org Space
    Texas A&M researchers used virtual-reality environments plus added smell and touch cues to simulate long-duration isolation experienced by sailors. The work aims to identify sensory interventions that could mitigate psychological strain on future Mars missions. Early results show measurable drops in reported stress when multiple senses are engaged. Follow-on trials will test similar setups with astronaut analogs.

11. **Young Mega-Earth Challenges Formation Models** — NewsBytes
    Astronomers have identified a roughly 23-Earth-mass planet still embedded in its natal disk, far exceeding typical core-accretion predictions for its age. The object's density and orbital distance suggest it may have formed through a different channel, possibly involving disk instabilities. Additional radial-velocity and transit data are being collected to refine its mass and radius. The discovery adds to a small but growing sample of massive young worlds.

12. **Perseverance Images Martian Solar Eclipse** — Space.com
    The rover captured a transit of Phobos across the Sun from the surface of Mars, producing a brief, deep partial eclipse. The event was timed to coincide with the spacecraft's science operations window. Images show the moon's irregular silhouette against the solar disk and will help refine Phobos' orbital parameters. Similar observations are scheduled during future conjunction seasons.

13. **Supply-Chain Bottlenecks Threaten Space Growth** — SpaceNews
    A new analysis warns that U.S. industrial capacity is lagging behind the combined demand from commercial constellations, national-security programs, and civil missions. Key pressure points include specialty electronics, propulsion components, and qualified launch-site personnel. The report recommends targeted investments in domestic suppliers and workforce training. Without action, schedule slips and cost overruns are projected to worsen.

14. **NASA's New Frontier in Space Exploration** — aldianews.com
    The article profiles emerging leadership and partnership models NASA is adopting to expand exploration beyond traditional centers. It highlights how smaller institutions and private firms are being integrated into mission planning. Specific examples include technology demonstrations slated for upcoming Artemis flights. The piece notes that these shifts aim to broaden the industrial base supporting human spaceflight.

15. **John Flamsteed Born August 19, 1646** — Astronomy
    The first Astronomer Royal began systematic observations that laid groundwork for accurate stellar catalogs used in navigation. Despite leaving school early, Flamsteed taught himself the mathematics needed to compute planetary positions. His work directly addressed the longitude problem that plagued 17th-century sailors. Modern historians continue to study his notebooks for insights into early telescope calibration techniques.

### Cosmic Spotlight
The fastest known Milky Way star, S301, is providing a new probe of Sagittarius A*'s rotation. Its extreme speed and proximity mean the black hole's frame-dragging effect should measurably shift the star's path over the next few years. ESO's VLTI has already delivered the initial orbit; continued observations will test whether the spin value matches independent Event Horizon Telescope estimates. The measurement could resolve lingering uncertainty about how quickly the black hole itself is turning.

### Cosmic Deep Dive: Shielding a Radio Telescope on the Moon
Picture a single large radio dish placed on the Moon's far side: every terrestrial transmitter, every satellite downlink, and even Earth's own ionosphere would sit behind 3,500 kilometres of solid rock, creating a silence deeper than any spot on our planet. Radio waves from cosmic sources would arrive unimpeded while human-generated noise, which already swamps the "Wow!" signal's frequency band today, would be blocked. The proposed array would scan at wavelengths blocked from Earth, listening for faint signals that might reveal the universe's first stars or unknown natural phenomena. Yet the same far side is now targeted for future lunar bases, and each new lander or relay satellite risks injecting its own radio leakage into that protected zone. Scientists still do not know how much interference a single unshielded transmitter would create at these frequencies, nor whether international rules can be written and enforced before construction begins.
