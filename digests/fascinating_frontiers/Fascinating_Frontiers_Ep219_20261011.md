### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Transmission Spectroscopy Reveals Exoplanet Atmospheres
A planet the size of Earth passing in front of a star fifty light years away blocks only one hundredth of one percent of the starlight, yet that sliver carries the chemical signature of an entire atmosphere. During transit the starlight filters through the planet's upper layers where different molecules absorb specific wavelengths and leave dark lines in the spectrum. Instruments spread that light across ultraviolet to infrared bands and measure the depth of each absorption feature to identify helium hydrogen or even complex organics. The method works because the planet's atmosphere acts like a temporary filter that changes with altitude and temperature. One surprising number is that helium escape rates of several hundred tons per second have already been recorded on worlds forty nine light years away. We can see the gas leaving but nobody knows whether the flow will strip the entire atmosphere or leave a denser layer behind.
