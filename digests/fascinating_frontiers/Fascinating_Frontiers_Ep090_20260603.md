### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **Artemis Hardware Ships from Utah Town to Kennedy** — The Salt Lake Tribune
   Crucial components for the next Artemis mission have departed a small Utah manufacturing site bound for Kennedy Space Center. The parts were fabricated locally and represent key elements in the lunar program’s supply chain. Their departure marks another step in preparing hardware for upcoming crewed flights. Observers will watch how these components integrate into the larger vehicle stack at the Florida launch site.

2. **Balcony Image Reveals 548 Galaxies in Leo Triplet** — Space.com
   An astrophotographer assembled the view from more than 60 hours of exposure data collected on a residential balcony. The image captures the gravitationally bound Leo Triplet system in unprecedented detail. Processing combined multiple nights of light to highlight faint structures across the galaxy group. The result demonstrates how dedicated amateur equipment can resolve large-scale cosmic features from urban locations.

3. **Europe Strengthens Plutonium-238 Production for Deep Space** — European Space Agency
   New facilities are advancing the supply chain for plutonium-238, the radioisotope that powers spacecraft on long-duration missions beyond the inner solar system. The effort focuses on reliable fuel production for future probes that cannot rely on solar arrays. European agencies aim to reduce dependence on external sources for these power systems. Next milestones include qualification testing of the new production methods.

4. **Models Explore Crew Dynamics for Future Lunar Bases** — Asgardia - The Space Nation
   Researchers are simulating social and operational interactions among crews living on the Moon for extended periods. The work targets practical challenges such as task allocation, conflict resolution, and habitat layout. Findings will inform design choices for sustained lunar outposts. Follow-on studies will test the models against planned Artemis surface operations.

5. **Blue Origin Assesses Launch Pad After New Glenn Test Explosion** — Universe Today
   An explosion during a static-fire test created a large fireball and damaged infrastructure at the company’s Florida site. Blue Origin has released an official statement confirming the incident and beginning damage evaluation. The event occurred while preparing the first New Glenn vehicle for flight. Recovery timelines will depend on the extent of pad repairs required.

6. **Winds on Hot Jupiters Hint at Planetary Magnetic Fields** — NOIRLab
   Observations of atmospheric flows on several exoplanets show patterns that align with strong internal magnetic activity. The data come from high-resolution spectroscopy that tracks cloud movements and temperature variations. These measurements provide the clearest indirect signs yet of magnetic fields on worlds outside our solar system. Additional targets will be observed to test how common the phenomenon may be.

7. **White Dwarf and Red Dwarf Pair Source Long-Period Radio Bursts** — Space.com
   Twenty years of searching have traced enigmatic radio signals to the interaction between a white dwarf and a red dwarf companion. Magnetic reconnection occurs as the white dwarf accretes material from its neighbor, producing the distinctive bursts. The system offers a nearby laboratory for studying similar processes in more distant objects. Further monitoring will map how the emission varies with orbital phase.

8. **NASA Opens Robotic Arm Experiments to Researchers** — NASA
   The Fly Foundational Robots mission will carry a seven-degree-of-freedom robotic arm to low Earth orbit. Selected U.S. investigators can propose experiments that use the arm once it reaches space. The opportunity targets principal investigators, postdoctoral researchers, and qualified graduate students. Proposals will be evaluated for scientific merit and technical readiness.

9. **SpaceX Schedules Morning Falcon 9 Launch from Florida** — Daytona Beach News-Journal
   A Falcon 9 is set to lift off from Cape Canaveral with a batch of Starlink satellites. Viewing opportunities exist along the Volusia County coastline depending on trajectory and weather. The mission continues the company’s high-cadence launch schedule. Updates on exact timing will be released closer to the window.

10. **Meteorite Confirms Existence of Ancient Protoplanet** — Phys.org
    Chemical and mineral analysis of a rare meteorite shows it originated inside a differentiated body roughly the size of Mars or the Moon. The parent world formed within the first few million years of the solar system before a collision destroyed it. Isotopic signatures match no known asteroid and point to an early planetary embryo. Follow-up studies will search for additional fragments that could refine the body’s size and composition.

11. **China Debuts Partially Reusable Long March 12B Rocket** — Space.com
    The Long March 12B lifted off for the first time without prior airspace notices, demonstrating a design similar to Falcon 9. The vehicle reached orbit successfully on its inaugural flight. Engineers will examine booster recovery performance from this test. Future missions will determine whether the rocket achieves routine reuse.

12. **Statistical Clues Point to Recoiling Supermassive Black Holes** — Universe Today
    Galaxy merger simulations predict that merged black holes can receive velocity kicks sufficient to eject them from galactic centers. A new analysis of large survey data finds spatial offsets consistent with this recoiling population. The method uses statistical distributions rather than individual identifications. Larger samples from upcoming surveys will test the fraction of galaxies that host such displaced black holes.

13. **Study Questions Lunar Origin for Asteroid Kamo’oalewa** — Phys.org
    China’s Tianwen-2 mission is en route to the near-Earth asteroid previously thought to be lunar ejecta. New dynamical modeling suggests the object’s spectrum and orbit are more consistent with main-belt material. The spacecraft is expected to return samples in late 2027. Those samples will provide the decisive test of the asteroid’s true provenance.

14. **SETI Institute Distributes $1 Million in Research Grants** — EurekAlert!
    The STRIDE program has awarded funding to projects spanning astrobiology, exoplanet characterization, and public outreach. Recipients include teams studying biosignature detection methods and community engagement strategies. The grants aim to accelerate both scientific and educational outcomes. Annual calls will continue to support new proposals in these areas.

15. **ngVLA Prototype Antenna Captures First Light on Crab Nebula** — Universe Today
    The prototype antenna for the next-generation Very Large Array successfully tracked and imaged Taurus A. The observation validated the new dish design and receiver systems under real observing conditions. Data from the test will guide final specifications for the full array. Construction of additional antennas is expected to follow in coming years.

### Cosmic Spotlight
A meteorite fragment has supplied the first unambiguous proof that a differentiated protoplanet once existed in the inner solar system. Its unique isotopic ratios and mineral textures could only form inside a body large enough to melt and separate into core and mantle layers. The parent world was destroyed billions of years ago, yet these surviving pieces preserve a record of that lost planet’s geology. What other fragments of early planetary embryos might still be orbiting undetected among the asteroids?

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Meteorites Preserve the Memory of Lost Worlds
If the solar system’s first planets were the size of basketballs scattered across a city block, most of them were smashed into gravel within a few million years. When two such embryos collided, the impact energy flash-melted rock and launched fragments onto new orbits that eventually reached Earth as meteorites. One surviving piece still carries distinct nickel and chromium ratios that match only the core-mantle boundary of a body hundreds of kilometers across. We can measure those ratios in the lab today, yet we still cannot explain why some embryos grew large enough to differentiate while others remained rubble piles.
