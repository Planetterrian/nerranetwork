### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **NASA Selects Rovers and Landers for Moon Base** — Spaceflight Now
   NASA has awarded initial contracts to Astrolab and Lunar Outpost for roughly one-ton rovers that astronauts will drive on the lunar surface. The vehicles will support travel, supply transport and remote operations across a base planned to cover hundreds of square miles. Separate lander and drone providers are also moving into final design phases over the next 18 months. The effort forms the first concrete hardware purchases for sustained human presence beyond low Earth orbit. Next milestone is completion of the Phase II design reviews before hardware fabrication begins.

2. **TESS Produces First All-Sky Map of Exoplanets** — Universe Today
   NASA’s Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite has compiled an all-sky catalog showing thousands of distant worlds around nearby stars. The map reveals how planets are distributed across different stellar types and galactic environments. Data processing compared transit signals against background star fields to confirm candidates. The release gives astronomers a ready target list for atmospheric follow-up with larger telescopes. Researchers will next cross-match the catalog with ground-based radial-velocity surveys.

3. **ESA’s Smile Spacecraft Enters Science Orbit** — Reddit Astronomy
   The European Space Agency’s Smile mission has completed its launch phase and settled into a highly elliptical orbit. Instruments will study the interaction between the solar wind and Earth’s magnetosphere from a vantage that covers both regions repeatedly. The orbit allows continuous monitoring of the dayside magnetopause and the magnetotail. Mission controllers confirmed all systems are operating after the initial orbit-raising maneuvers. Data collection for the primary science phase begins once commissioning finishes.

4. **Russian Cosmonauts Complete Five-Hour ISS Spacewalk** — Space.com
   Cosmonauts Sergey Kud-Sverchkov and Sergei Mikaev performed a five-hour spacewalk outside the International Space Station. The crew installed new equipment and conducted maintenance on the station’s exterior. Live coverage was provided by Roscosmos throughout the excursion. The spacewalk marks another step in ongoing station upkeep ahead of future crew rotations. Both cosmonauts returned safely to the airlock after completing all planned tasks.

5. **JWST Spots Most Chemically Primitive Galaxy Yet** — Universe Today
   An international team used the James Webb Space Telescope and gravitational lensing to characterize galaxy LAP1-B, formed roughly 13 billion years ago. The galaxy shows extremely low metallicity, offering a direct view of conditions shortly after the first stars ignited. Lensing by a foreground cluster magnified the faint target enough for detailed spectroscopy. Lead researcher Associate Professor Kimihiko Nakajima of Kanazawa University coordinated the observations. Follow-up programs will search for similar ultra-faint systems in other lensed fields.

6. **NASA Hosts Astrophysics Missions Innovation Workshop** — NASA Science
   The two-day Innovation for Astrophysics Missions Workshop opened in Washington, DC, to explore new technologies for future space telescopes. Participants include engineers and scientists working on detector advances, lightweight optics and propulsion concepts. Sessions focus on moving high-risk technologies from laboratory demonstrations toward flight readiness. Outcomes will feed into upcoming mission concept studies. A summary report is expected after the workshop concludes.

7. **NASA Images Ongoing Eruption of Mount Dukono** — NASA Science
   Satellite observations captured ash plumes, volcanic gases and ejected bombs from Indonesia’s Mount Dukono. The volcano on Halmahera Island has maintained near-continuous activity, sending material to varying altitudes. Imagery helps aviation authorities issue timely warnings for regional flight corridors. Repeated passes allow tracking of plume dispersal patterns over time. Scientists continue monitoring the site for changes in eruption intensity.

8. **European Nations Blend Sovereign and Commercial Space Assets** — SpaceNews
   Countries across Europe are combining national systems, bilateral projects, federated networks and commercial services for military space needs. Officials noted that no single approach meets all requirements for independence and resilience. The strategy includes dual-use commercial satellites alongside dedicated government assets. Discussions took place at a recent Amsterdam conference on space security. Implementation timelines vary by nation and capability area.

9. **GM Technology Selected for Lunar Rover Power System** — General Motors
   General Motors will supply electric propulsion components for Lunar Outpost’s Pegasus rover under NASA’s Artemis program. The hardware adapts automotive battery and motor technology for the lunar environment. Pegasus is one of two rovers chosen for early Artemis surface operations. Integration testing will occur after the rover design reaches its next maturity gate. The partnership marks another transfer of terrestrial automotive experience to lunar hardware.

10. **Astronomy Magazine Highlights La Superba** — Astronomy Magazine
    Observers can locate the carbon star Y Canum Venaticorum, known as La Superba, high overhead in Canes Venatici after dark. The star’s deep red color stands out against surrounding field stars. Its brightness makes it an easy target for binoculars or small telescopes on clear nights. The feature appears in the weekly Sky This Week column for May 27. No special equipment beyond dark-adapted eyes is required.

11. **Mainland China Welcomes Taiwan Participation in Space Projects** — China Daily
    A Chinese government spokesman invited Taiwan compatriots to join joint space exploration efforts. The statement emphasized shared heritage and mutual benefits from collaborative missions. No specific projects or timelines were detailed in the announcement. The overture comes amid broader regional discussions on technology cooperation. Future statements are expected to outline possible participation mechanisms.

12. **Orbital Data Confirms New Lunar Material Detection** — Orbital Today
    NASA scientists identified previously unrecognized lunar surface material during recent orbital observations. The find adds to the catalog of known regolith compositions at unexplored sites. Analysis compared spectral signatures against existing sample libraries. Further mapping passes are planned to determine the extent of the deposit. The discovery will inform landing site choices for upcoming landers.

### Cosmic Spotlight
The detection of LAP1-B offers the clearest view yet of a galaxy from the universe’s first billion years. Gravitational lensing stretched its light enough for JWST to measure chemical abundances directly. The galaxy’s extremely low metallicity shows that star formation had barely begun when the light left. Scientists now wonder how such faint systems assembled so quickly after the Big Bang and whether many more remain hidden behind other clusters.
---
### Cosmic Deep Dive: Gravitational Lensing as a Natural Telescope
If you could stretch a single distant galaxy’s light across an entire foreground cluster, the magnification would reach factors of dozens or even hundreds. That is exactly what happens when a massive galaxy cluster lies between us and a faraway object: its gravity warps spacetime and bends light rays like a giant lens. Light from LAP1-B traveled 13 billion years and was bent into multiple bright arcs that JWST could resolve. The same geometry also stretches the apparent size of the background source, turning an otherwise undetectable smudge into a target for spectroscopy. One lingering puzzle is why some of the most magnified systems still appear chemically simpler than models predict; no one yet knows whether that reflects true early-universe conditions or selection effects in which objects get strongly lensed.
