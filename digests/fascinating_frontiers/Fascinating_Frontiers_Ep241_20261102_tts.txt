Arsia Mons on Mars produces a water-ice cloud that forms only during spring and summer mornings.

The European Space Agency, or E S A, Mars Express spacecraft has imaged it in detail.

The thin Martian atmosphere lacks enough dust at high altitudes for normal cloud formation, so researchers are studying alternative mechanisms.

Freshly formed water-ice crystals stream away from the volcano and leave a tail that lasts long enough to photograph.
