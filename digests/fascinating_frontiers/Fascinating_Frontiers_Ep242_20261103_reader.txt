The star sits roughly one hundred light-years away, and Universe Space Tech reports the innermost world completes an orbit in nine point one one days.

The second planet takes thirteen point six seven days, and the third takes twenty point five two days.

The European Space Agency's CHEOPS telescope supplied the data that revealed the pattern after TESS had spotted initial transits in two thousand twenty.

Researchers used the resonance to predict and then verify three additional outer planets whose orbits matched earlier anomalies in the star's brightness.

Only about one percent of planetary systems retain this original resonant state after billions of years.

Each world is smaller than Neptune and carries a thick gaseous envelope resembling those of Uranus or Neptune.

The system's brightness makes it the most prominent known example containing four or more planets, positioning it as a prime target for atmospheric study with the James Webb Space Telescope, or JWST.

Astronomers want to determine whether these planets are rocky worlds with exceptionally thick atmospheres or whether solid surfaces with liquid-water oceans exist beneath the clouds.

University of Washington researchers built a virtual Earth model that runs geochemical exchanges among core, mantle, crust, surface, and atmosphere for five billion years while excluding any biological influence.

The Times of India reports the simulation reproduces nineteen observed planetary properties, including mantle heat flow.

Lead author Samantha Gilbert-Janizek completed the work as a doctoral student and is now a postdoctoral fellow at UC Riverside.

She added surface and atmosphere modeling to an existing framework developed in senior author Rory Barnes's lab.

The model can now be applied to any rocky exoplanet to test whether an observed atmosphere could arise without life.

If the model fails to match the data, the mismatch strengthens the case for biological activity.

The work was published in The Planetary Science Journal on September eighteenth.

SpaceX opened a public page that lists Starfall's commercial specifications for the first time, Teslarati reports.

Each mission can carry up to one thousand kilograms, supply one kilowatt of power, and maintain continuous Starlink connectivity for real-time data return.

The service targets biomedical customers who can grow stem-cell organoids and replacement tissue, pharmaceutical companies working on protein and small-molecule drug crystals, and materials-science users growing semiconductors in vacuum and microgravity.

Three commercial flights have already been booked and will launch on Starship.

The capsule measures about ten point two feet wide and two point five feet tall and can return two thousand two hundred pounds of cargo.

President Gwynne Shotwell described the capsule as providing routine, relatively low-cost access to microgravity environments for scientific research, including improving understanding of diseases like cancer and diabetes.

The first flight occurred in June and the second on the Bandwagon-5 rideshare in October.

The Southern Taurids peak around November fifth and the Northern Taurids around November twelfth, each offering a chance of bright fireballs though producing only a handful of meteors per hour under ideal conditions.

Mars and Jupiter appear one point two degrees apart in the predawn sky on November fifteenth and sixteenth, with Jupiter the brighter point and reddish Mars nearby.

Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth on November seventeenth, meaning a signal at the speed of light takes twenty-four hours to reach the spacecraft and another twenty-four hours for a reply.

The Leonids peak on November seventeenth and eighteenth after moonset, potentially producing up to fifteen meteors per hour under ideal conditions.

A supermoon rises on November twenty-fourth, the first of three consecutive supermoons, and may appear larger near the horizon due to the Moon illusion.

The eight-U ERMIS-3 CubeSat established an optical link with a ground station only weeks after commissioning, Phys.org reports.

The successful link marks a step in validating optical communications on small satellites despite the complexity of the operation.

JWST observations of interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS are supplying fresh data on the conditions under which the object formed around another star, Space.com reports.

The findings provide another clue toward understanding the origins of 3I/ATLAS and the planet-forming conditions around other stars.

HiRISE images show that water-ice frost in Martian polar craters changes location and thickness with the seasons, behaving differently even in neighboring craters because of local slopes and rim heights, Phys.org reports.

Mars shares many landforms and weather patterns with Earth, including changing seasons, yet its north polar ice cap is composed only of water while the southern cap is carbon dioxide.

Fine-grained material preserved in asteroid Ryugu and similar meteorites reveals that water altered minerals extensively yet left some primitive chemistry intact.

The pattern suggests localized rather than uniform aqueous processing inside the parent bodies.

These rocks are considered chemically primitive because they preserve the chemical composition of the early solar system remarkably well while also reacting extensively with water after formation.

In brief, Hubble has mapped an extended structure spanning ninety-three billion miles in a nearby star system whose nature remains under study, SciTechDaily reports.

JWST data point to young massive star clusters at high redshift as possible birthplaces of intermediate-mass black hole seeds, according to Universe Today.

Six metronomes placed on a single table can swing at different speeds yet stay locked so that every sixth swing of the slowest exactly matches the fastest one's count.

That coordination mirrors an unbroken chain of simple whole-number orbital ratios.

The pattern is the surviving signature of the gas-and-dust disk in which the planets grew.

While the disk was still present, the planets migrated inward together, their mutual gravitational tugs keeping the resonances tight.

Once the gas vanished, the chain froze in place.

Any later giant impact or passing star would have broken the links, so the continued existence of the resonance tells us the system has experienced almost no violence since the disk dispersed billions of years ago.

Most systems lose this delicate balance when giant planets form, neighboring stars tug on orbits, or collisions scramble the arrangement.

Here the planets apparently avoided every one of those disruptions, leaving their original spacing frozen in place.

Because the periods are known to high precision, future transit observations can be timed exactly, allowing telescopes to catch every planet passing in front of the star without wasted pointings.

Small deviations from perfect commensurability reveal the planets' masses through their gravitational influence on one another.

Those masses, combined with radius measurements from the transit depths, yield bulk densities that already hint at substantial atmospheres rather than bare rock.

The resonance therefore functions as a natural scale that converts timing data into physical properties without requiring additional assumptions.

Astronomers can now use the known periods to refine mass estimates and test whether any of the worlds retain substantial rocky cores beneath their thick envelopes.

Targeted JWST spectroscopy is lined up to measure atmospheric composition and search for signs of the kind of chemical disequilibrium the whole-Earth simulation is designed to flag.

The precise timing provided by the resonance makes it an especially efficient target for follow-up observations that could distinguish between formation scenarios.

Models show that resonant chains are common at formation but rare today.

What protected this particular chain for billions of years while thousands of others were disrupted?

The answer may lie in the absence of a gas giant that could have scattered the inner planets, or in the quiet stellar environment around HD110067.

Either way, the system stands as a benchmark: any theory of planet formation must now explain not only how resonances arise, but how a few of them endure across cosmic time.
