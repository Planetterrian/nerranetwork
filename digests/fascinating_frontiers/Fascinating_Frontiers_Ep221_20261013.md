2. **Primordial Helium Matches Big Bang Predictions** — Phys.org Space
   Astronomers distinguish primordial helium formed in the first minutes after the Big Bang from helium later produced in stars. The early universe held roughly three parts hydrogen to one part helium by mass, with all heavier elements created later through stellar fusion or supernovae. These heavier elements remain so scarce that astronomers still classify them collectively as metals. New observations of the primordial fraction continue to align with standard Big Bang nucleosynthesis calculations. The measurements come from spectral lines in the oldest gas clouds that preserve the original abundance ratios. Distinguishing the two helium sources requires careful accounting of stellar contributions over cosmic time. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-primordial-helium-big.html)

3. **Dying Sun May Disrupt Outer Planets Sooner** — Phys.org Space
   New models show the Sun's mass loss could destabilize some outer planets on a timescale of roughly one billion years rather than a quintillion. The change shortens earlier estimates by many orders of magnitude for the most distant worlds. Researchers published the revised timeline in The Astrophysical Journal Letters after recalculating orbital evolution during the Sun's red-giant phase. The simulations track how gradual mass shedding weakens gravitational binding for planets at large distances. Small perturbations that once took immense periods to grow now produce instability on far shorter cosmic timescales. The work focuses specifically on the outer solar system where effects accumulate most noticeably. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-dying-sun-solar-sooner-thought.html)

4. **Moon Dust Could Charge Astronauts Like Capacitors** — Space.com
   NASA is seeking ideas to prevent static electricity from building up on spacesuits and equipment during lunar surface operations. The Moon's dry, dusty environment allows charge to accumulate on astronauts, who could then discharge into spacecraft or tools. The agency highlighted the risk in a call for mitigation concepts ahead of planned Artemis landings. Dry regolith readily transfers charge in the absence of atmospheric moisture. Astronauts moving across the surface could act as walking capacitors capable of delivering unexpected shocks to hardware. The request for proposals targets both suit materials and operational procedures to reduce the hazard. Source: [space.com](https://www.space.com/astronomy/moon/the-moon-could-turn-astronauts-into-walking-high-voltage-capacitors-that-accidentally-zap-spacecraft)

The revised solar-mass-loss models show outer planets could face orbital instability far earlier than prior quintillion-year estimates. The key change comes from updated calculations of how the Sun's gradual shedding of mass alters gravitational binding over time. Researchers ran the new simulations specifically for the most distant members of the system, where small perturbations grow over long periods. ### Cosmic Deep Dive: How the Early Universe Locked In Its Hydrogen-Helium Ratio
Picture a volume the size of a large city block at one second after the Big Bang; that single block already contained enough energy to form every atom now in the observable universe. In the next two minutes the temperature fell from billions of degrees to roughly one billion, allowing protons and neutrons to fuse into the first nuclei before the universe cooled too far for further reactions. Only about one helium nucleus formed for every twelve hydrogen nuclei, fixing the 75-to-25 percent mix we still measure today. That ratio depends on the exact expansion rate and the number of neutrino species present in those first moments. We can still detect the imprint through the relative strengths of spectral lines in the oldest gas clouds, yet no one has explained why the measured value sits so precisely on the theoretical curve rather than deviating by even a few percent. The mismatch, if it appears in future data, would force a revision of the standard model itself. Observations continue to test whether additional physics operated during those first minutes.
