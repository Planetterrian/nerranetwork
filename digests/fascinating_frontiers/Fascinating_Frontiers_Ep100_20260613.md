### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Phosphorus Ratios Trace Early Solar-System Mixing
If every phosphorus atom on Earth arrived in a single teaspoon of material, that teaspoon would still outweigh an entire mountain range. In the swirling disk of gas and dust that became our solar system, Jupiter’s growing gravity acted like a giant traffic controller, flinging icy and rocky bodies inward and outward on chaotic paths. Iron meteorites that formed closer to the Sun carry one phosphorus-to-nitrogen signature, while younger chondrites record a distinctly different ratio after Jupiter’s migration stirred the inventory. The difference is measurable down to parts per million, revealing exactly when and where the ingredients for oceans and biology were delivered. Yet the precise timing of Jupiter’s first inward-then-outward journey remains an open question that no current sample set can fully pin down.
