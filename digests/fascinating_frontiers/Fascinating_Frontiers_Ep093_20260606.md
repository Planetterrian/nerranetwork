12. **Astronomers Detect Winds from Milky Way’s Central Black Hole** — Space.com
    After fifty years of searching, astronomers identified powerful winds flowing outward from Sagittarius A*. The winds appear as a cone-shaped cavity in surrounding gas and dust. The discovery confirms theoretical predictions that active black holes drive galactic-scale outflows. Observations used multiple telescopes to map the empty region pointing back to the black hole. Further mapping will reveal how these winds affect star formation in the galactic center. Source: [space.com](https://www.space.com/astronomy/black-holes/scientists-find-wind-blowing-from-our-milky-ways-black-hole-after-half-century-search-there-it-is)

The new view helps test models of how the first galaxies assembled. Additional JWST fields will extend the map to even earlier times. 14. **Team Captures Rotation of Protoplanetary Disk Around AB Aurigae** — Universe Today

The observations help refine models of how planets form within rotating disks. Follow-up studies will track changes in disk structure over time. ### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Supermassive Black Holes Drive Galactic Winds
If the Sun were scaled to the size of a basketball in Toronto, Sagittarius A* would still be smaller than a single atom yet contain four million times the Sun’s mass. Material spiraling inward releases enormous energy that heats and accelerates surrounding gas into powerful outflows. These winds can reach thousands of kilometers per second and clear cavities hundreds of light-years across. The process links the tiny region near the event horizon to structures visible across the entire galaxy. We can measure the winds’ speed and extent, yet the exact fraction of energy converted into outflow versus radiation remains unknown.
