### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **NASA Previews Moon Base Strategy Update** — Moneycontrol.com
   NASA plans to share details on its evolving approach to a sustained lunar presence and the missions that will follow. The update will outline how the agency intends to move from initial landings toward longer-term surface operations. Officials are expected to discuss timelines for habitat development and resource utilization. This briefing comes as NASA continues to refine its architecture for returning astronauts to the Moon and eventually sending crews to Mars. Watch for the specific milestones and technology priorities that emerge from the announcement.

2. **Chandrayaan-3 Receives Global Honour for Moon Landing** — India Today
   India’s Chandrayaan-3 mission has been recognized with a top international space award for its successful lunar south pole landing. The achievement marked the first time a spacecraft touched down near the Moon’s south pole. The mission demonstrated precise navigation and landing capabilities under challenging conditions. Organizers highlighted the engineering and scientific contributions that advanced lunar exploration for all nations. Follow-up coverage will likely detail how the award reflects growing international cooperation in lunar science.

3. **NASA Ames Marks Viking 50th Anniversary** — NASA
   A science symposium at NASA Ames on July 20 will celebrate the 50th anniversary of the Viking missions to Mars. The event will spotlight contributions from Ames scientists across multiple disciplines. Participants will hear directly from Viking team members about the original mission’s findings. Discussions will also cover how Viking’s legacy shapes current and future Mars exploration efforts. The symposium will be livestreamed for public viewing.

4. **Ground Equipment Issue Scrubs Starship V3 Test** — SpaceNews
   SpaceX called off the first launch attempt of its new Block 3 Starship vehicle due to a ground equipment problem. The scrub occurred on May 21 during final countdown preparations. Teams are now assessing the issue before scheduling the next attempt. The vehicle represents a major redesign compared with the previous eleven flights. Observers note that early test flights of new versions often encounter such setbacks.

5. **NASA Images Mississippi Tornado Damage Path** — NASA
   Satellite views reveal the jagged scar left by a powerful tornado that struck southern Mississippi in early May. The track shows clear patterns of damaged vegetation across the landscape. Multiple twisters touched down in the region during the same severe weather outbreak. The imagery helps emergency responders and researchers map the storm’s footprint. Similar Earth-observing data continues to support rapid disaster assessment.

6. **Copernicus Sentinel-2 Captures Algerian Sahara** — European Space Agency
   New images from the Copernicus Sentinel-2 mission show the sandy and rocky terrain of central Algeria’s Sahara Desert. The satellite data highlights contrasting textures of dunes and rocky plateaus. Such observations contribute to ongoing studies of arid landforms and surface processes. The mission routinely provides high-resolution views of Earth’s changing landscapes. Researchers use these scenes to track desert expansion and resource management.

7. **Jupiter’s Galilean Moons Cross Planet’s Disk** — Astronomy Magazine
   Observers can watch Io and another Galilean moon transit Jupiter this evening. East Coast viewers have the best early-evening window shortly after sunset. The events offer a chance to see the moons as small dark silhouettes against the planet’s bright face. Jupiter remains well placed for telescopic viewing throughout the night. The configuration repeats regularly as the moons orbit the giant planet.

8. **Mercury Reappears in Morning Sky This Week** — Astronomy Magazine
   Mercury begins emerging from the Sun’s glare and becomes visible low in the predawn sky. Uranus remains in conjunction with the Sun and stays out of sight until June. The Summer Triangle asterism dominates the eastern sky after midnight. Observers can use the bright stars Vega, Deneb, and Altair as reliable guides. The coming week features steadily improving conditions for spotting the innermost planet.

9. **Venus and Jupiter Shine in July Evening Sky** — Astronomy Magazine
   Venus and Jupiter appear together in the western sky shortly after sunset during early July. Jupiter will soon drop lower and become harder to see as the month progresses. In the morning sky, Saturn rises earlier each week and pairs with Neptune in Pisces. A close conjunction between Mars and Uranus is also on the calendar before dawn. The month offers several planetary pairings for both naked-eye and binocular observers.

10. **Ophiuchus Holds Deep-Sky Targets** — Astronomy Magazine
    The large constellation Ophiuchus contains numerous globular clusters and planetary nebulae worth hunting with a telescope. Its brightest star, Rasalhague, lies about 50 light-years away and shines with roughly 25 times the Sun’s luminosity. The constellation’s position between Hercules and Scorpius makes it easy to locate on summer evenings. Observers can sweep the area for faint fuzzies that stand out against the Milky Way background. Many of these objects reward patient viewing under dark skies.

11. **Ara Offers Southern Deep-Sky Views** — Astronomy Magazine
    The constellation Ara sits directly beneath Scorpius and contains several attractive star clusters. Ancient Greek astronomers included it among the original 48 constellations described by Aratus. Its compact shape makes it straightforward to identify when facing south on clear nights. Binoculars reveal the clusters as small knots of light against the rich star fields of the Milky Way. Southern Hemisphere observers have the best vantage point for exploring this region.

12. **Auriga Features Bright Capella** — Astronomy Magazine
    Capella, the sixth-brightest star in the night sky, anchors the constellation Auriga high overhead during winter and spring evenings. The star shines with an intense yellow hue and lies roughly 40 light-years away. Beta Aurigae, known as Menkalinan, is another prominent member of the pattern. The constellation’s distinctive pentagon shape has been recognized for thousands of years. It serves as a useful signpost for locating other northern spring constellations.

13. **Southern Hemisphere July Sky Highlights Planets** — Astronomy Magazine
    Venus gleams at magnitude –4.1 in the northwestern evening sky after sunset. Jupiter appears nearby but will soon fade from view as it moves closer to the Sun. In the morning hours, Saturn climbs higher and pairs with Neptune in the same region of Pisces. A Mars-Uranus conjunction offers an additional early-morning target. The combination of bright planets and darker skies rewards southern observers throughout the month.

14. **Unidentified Light Turns Out to Be Aircraft** — Astronomy Magazine
    An observer at an Ohio astronomy club site watched a stationary bright light that later revealed itself as an airplane. The object drifted slowly before the sound of jet engines confirmed its identity. Such sightings are common near urban areas with busy flight paths. Learning to distinguish aircraft lights from celestial objects is a useful skill for new stargazers. The experience illustrates how context and patience help solve sky mysteries.

15. **Public Meteorite Testing Options Explained** — Astronomy Magazine
    Laboratories and universities can analyze suspected meteorites for members of the public. Collectors should first document the find location and any fusion crust or magnetic properties. Professional testing typically involves checking for nickel content and other diagnostic features. The process helps distinguish true meteorites from terrestrial rocks that merely look unusual. Guidance from local astronomy clubs can point interested individuals toward reputable testing facilities.

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Light Travel Time Creates Cosmic Time Machines
If the Sun were a basketball in downtown Toronto, the nearest star would sit another basketball in Vancouver, yet light from that star still takes more than four years to reach us. Imagine riding alongside a photon that left a distant galaxy when dinosaurs still walked Earth; by the time it arrives at our telescopes, the universe has aged billions of years while the photon has experienced none. Light from the Milky Way’s center, 26,000 light-years away, shows us the galaxy as it looked when early humans were first using stone tools. We can see entire galaxies whose light began its journey before our own solar system formed, yet we cannot know what those galaxies look like right now. The unanswered question that keeps astronomers awake is whether any signal or structure in that ancient light can ever reveal what those same galaxies are doing at this exact moment.
