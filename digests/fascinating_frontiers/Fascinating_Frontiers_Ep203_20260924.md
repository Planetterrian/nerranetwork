### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Black Hole Jets Heat Galaxy Gas
A single supermassive black hole can drive jets that extend tens of thousands of light-years, far beyond the scale of an entire galaxy disk. If you could ride one of those jets outward from the galactic center, you would first encounter gas heated to millions of degrees by shock waves, then see that material cool and form new structures along the jet path. In Messier 106 the jets have created two extra arms of glowing gas that sit at right angles to the normal spiral arms. Chandra X-ray observations show these arms glow in royal blue because the gas reaches temperatures hot enough to emit X-rays. The jets themselves move at a substantial fraction of the speed of light, carrying energy equivalent to the output of millions of ordinary stars. Yet astronomers still cannot explain exactly why some black holes launch steady, powerful jets while others remain quiet despite similar masses and accretion rates. We can map the jets and measure the heated gas, but the trigger that decides whether a jet forms remains unknown.
