Planetary scientists have revised their picture of the ice at Mars' North Pole. They combined measurements from the Omega spectrometer on Mars' express, the chrism instrument on Mars' reconnaissance orbiter, and the surface stereo imager that flew on the Phoenix lander.
The new calculation shows the top layer holds only 3% dust by mass.
Earlier models had assumed 25% dust by mass.
The difference comes from recognizing that the ice sits in distinct layers with thin
dust sheets between them.
Seasonal frost that forms in winter carries more dust while summer exposes cleaner, older
ice beneath.
