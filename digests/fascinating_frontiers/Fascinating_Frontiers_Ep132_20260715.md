### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **China's Tianwen-2 Reaches Asteroid Target After Billion-Kilometre Journey** — Universe Today
   The spacecraft closed to within twenty kilometres of its target and has begun detailed scientific observations. The mission will examine whether the asteroid is a captured piece of the Moon or a typical near-Earth object. Data from the encounter could resolve long-standing questions about the origins of certain Earth companions. Follow-up measurements will focus on surface composition and rotation characteristics. This development advances the Robotic Exploration program by delivering the first close-range data on a potential lunar fragment after years of cruise.

2. **Mars Express Images Metallic-Looking Dunes in Kaiser Crater** — ESA
   The orbiter captured a wide field of wind-sculpted dunes that appear metallic in tone inside the ancient crater. These features formed over billions of years under persistent Martian winds. The image reveals fine-scale ripples across the dune field that trace past atmospheric patterns. Scientists will compare the new view with earlier orbital passes to track any seasonal changes. The observation adds another data point to ongoing robotic surveys of Mars surface evolution.

3. **First Diagnostic X-rays Taken Aboard an Orbiting Spacecraft** — AOL.co.uk
   Crew members performed the first medical X-ray imaging during a spaceflight. The procedure demonstrates that routine diagnostic capability can now be maintained without returning samples to Earth. Equipment was adapted to operate in microgravity while meeting radiation safety standards. Future expeditions may expand the technique to monitor bone density and other crew health metrics. This milestone supports sustained human presence by enabling in-situ medical assessment.

4. **NASA Funds 41 Technologies for Future Moon and Mars Missions** — ScienceDaily
   The selected projects cover propulsion, life support, resource extraction, and navigation systems. Each technology targets specific challenges for sustained operations beyond low Earth orbit. Teams will advance the concepts through ground testing and prototype development. Progress reports will feed into broader planning for crewed lunar and Martian surface activities. The selections directly support the Artemis architecture and future robotic precursor missions.

5. **Curiosity Examines Shifting Geological Units in Gale Crater** — NASA Science
   The rover has moved through several distinct rock layers mapped from orbit. Each unit shows different textures and mineral signatures that record changing environmental conditions. Atmospheric measurements continue alongside the geological work. The campaign will help reconstruct how the crater floor evolved over time. This phase of the mission builds on prior traverses by sampling a new sequence of mapped units.

6. **New Paper Explores 'Noosignatures' as Missing Link in Astrobiology** — Phys.org Space
   The study argues that current searches focus too narrowly on either simple life or technological civilizations. It proposes looking for intermediate signs of intelligence that could appear after billions of years of biological evolution. The concept fills a gap between microbial biosignatures and radio signals. Follow-up work will identify observable markers that future telescopes could target. The approach opens a new search category between established biosignature and technosignature frameworks.

7. **VLA Detects Relativistic Jet from Intermediate-Mass Black Hole** — Universe Today
   The array recorded an off-axis radio afterglow after the black hole disrupted a passing star. The event provides rare evidence that intermediate-mass black holes can launch powerful jets. Observations constrain the mass range and accretion behaviour of this elusive class of objects. Additional radio monitoring will track how the jet evolves over coming months. This detection supplies new constraints on black-hole demographics within the Cosmology & Astrophysics research arc.

8. **Astronomers Find Most Distant Compact Star-Forming Core Yet** — Phys.org Space
   Durham University researchers identified a dense stellar nursery at the heart of a galaxy more than nine billion years old. The structure offers a direct view of early galaxy assembly processes. High-resolution data reveal rapid star formation within a compact region. Further observations will test whether similar cores appear in even older systems. The finding extends the timeline for observing the earliest phases of galactic core development.

9. **Oldest Galactic Stars Align with Standard Cosmology Timeline** — Universe Today
   Measurements of more than 150,000 ancient stars show the oldest formed at the epoch predicted by the standard model. The result adds weight against certain proposed solutions to the Hubble tension. Precise age determinations came from combining spectroscopic and photometric surveys. Continued surveys will refine the age distribution across different galactic regions. The data set strengthens the standard cosmological timeline within ongoing Hubble-tension investigations.

10. **Brown University Maps Thickness of Lunar Regolith** — Phys.org Space
    New measurements quantify the loose surface layer that covers the Moon. Thickness varies with location and influences landing site selection and resource extraction planning. The study combines orbital radar and surface sampling data. Future landers will test the maps during site characterization activities. These maps provide practical inputs for Artemis landing-site evaluations and resource prospecting.

11. **Four White Dwarfs Found Hidden Behind Red Dwarf Companions** — Space.com
    The stars were identified through their gravitational influence rather than direct light. Each object is denser than typical white dwarfs yet remains invisible in standard surveys. The discovery expands the known population of nearby compact remnants. Additional radial-velocity monitoring will determine their exact masses and cooling ages. The detections increase the census of nearby white dwarfs available for detailed follow-up.

12. **NASA Outlines SkyFall Mission Concept for Mars** — NASA Science
    The proposal would send three aircraft to survey regions inaccessible to rovers. The aircraft would map climate history and identify landing sites for future human missions. The design builds on lessons from the Ingenuity helicopter. Concept studies will assess power and communication requirements for extended flights. SkyFall would extend the Robotic Exploration program by adding aerial reconnaissance capabilities to Mars surface studies.

13. **Artemis II Crew Reviews Orion Service Module at ESTEC** — European Space Agency
    The astronauts met the European teams responsible for the module that will power their lunar flyby. The visit covered integration status and operational procedures. Data from the meeting will inform final checks before the mission. The service module remains a critical element for life support and propulsion during the flight. This review advances the Artemis & the Moon program by confirming hardware readiness for the crewed lunar trajectory.

14. **New Crew Joins Expedition 74 Aboard the Space Station** — NASA Science
    NASA astronaut Anil Menon and two Roscosmos cosmonauts entered the laboratory after docking. They will conduct experiments during an eight-month stay. Daily updates will appear on the NASA Space Station blog. The crew is scheduled to return in April 2027. The arrival continues the cadence of crew rotations supporting ongoing commercial and international operations in low Earth orbit.

15. **Vanguard 1 Fragment Receives Final Pre-Rollover Catalog Number** — Jonathan McDowell via orbital records
    Object 69999 has been assigned to a previously uncatalogued piece of the 1958 satellite. The fragment now carries the international designation 1958-002D. The assignment marks the last entry before the catalog numbering system rolls over. Analysts will continue to track the object’s orbit with existing radar assets. The cataloguing step closes the pre-rollover sequence for debris from one of the earliest orbital missions.

### Cosmic Spotlight
China’s Tianwen-2 probe has closed to twenty kilometres of its target asteroid after traveling a billion kilometres. The spacecraft will now map surface composition and rotation to test whether the body originated as lunar ejecta. Confirmation would reshape models of how material moves between the Earth-Moon system and near-Earth orbits. Next data releases are expected once initial spectra and imaging sequences are processed. The encounter supplies the first direct compositional measurements that could distinguish between captured lunar material and native asteroid origins.

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Intermediate-Mass Black Holes Launch Jets
If the Sun were compressed into a sphere the size of a large city, an intermediate-mass black hole would still be ten times more massive yet occupy a volume no larger than a modest mountain range. When such an object passes near a star, tidal forces stretch the star into a thin stream that spirals inward, releasing enormous energy as the material crosses the event horizon. In the recent VLA detection, that inflow apparently powered a relativistic jet pointed slightly away from our line of sight, producing the observed radio afterglow. The jet itself travels at nearly the speed of light and can remain detectable for months as it expands into surrounding gas. Radio observations captured the afterglow at multiple frequencies, revealing how the outflow interacts with the interstellar medium over time. We still do not know what fraction of intermediate-mass black holes can generate such outflows or why some disruptions produce jets while others remain radio-quiet.
