### Top 12 Space & Astronomy Stories

1. **Nuclear Pathfinder Would Refuel Missions From Ceres** — Universe Today
   Two aerospace engineers propose a nuclear thermal propulsion mission to the dwarf planet Ceres that would land, extract water ice, and return samples within five years of a 2037 launch. The Pathfinder combines a Dawn-class orbiter with a five-metric-ton lander and a new nuclear engine that heats liquid hydrogen to 2,700 Kelvin. Nuclear thermal rockets operate at two to three times the efficiency of chemical rockets, cutting the Earth-to-Ceres trip to roughly one year. The vehicle would map volatiles for four to five months, then deploy the lander at a high-latitude ice-rich site to demonstrate water extraction before returning samples to Earth in 2041. A transport node at Ceres would sell propellant to passing vehicles, allowing a refueled craft to launch 60 percent less mass per round trip. The reactor physics was proven on the ground during NASA’s Rover and NERVA programs that ran more than twenty reactors between 1959 and 1972. Source: [universetoday.com](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/nuclear-pathfinder-mission-to-ceres-would-usher-in-era-of-interplanetary-transport)

2. **Not Every Gap in a Protoplanetary Disk Comes From a Planet** — Universe Today
   Astronomers used existing ALMA data on V1094 Sco and a new super-resolution imaging technique called PRIISM to map four narrow ring-gap pairs between 100 and 230 AU from the star. The disk extends to 380 AU, with an even larger gas disk reaching 760 AU in Keplerian rotation. Comparing gap widths and depths against the one-planet-per-gap model showed that planets alone cannot explain the full pattern. The team concludes that secular gravitational instability likely works alongside at least one low-mass planet to carve the observed structure. V1094 Sco lies about 500 light-years away and was first recognized as a giant multi-ringed disk in a 2018 Lupus survey paper. Source: [universetoday.com](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/all-the-gaps-we-see-in-protoplanetary-disks-arent-from-planets)

3. **New Model Estimates Ages of Lunar Impact Craters** — Phys.org Space
   Researchers at the Aerospace Information Research Institute developed a mathematical model that uses morphometric parameters to estimate both the degradation state and age of lunar craters. They applied the model to 140 impact craters in the Chang’e-6 landing area. The approach allows rapid assessment without waiting for new high-resolution imagery of every site. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-mathematical-age-degradation-lunar-impact.html)

4. **Faintest Supernova Ever Seen Refused to Fade for a Year** — Phys.org Space
   An international team identified the faintest supernova observed to date and found it remained visible for nearly a year. The explosion appears to have been produced by a white dwarf paired with a relatively small companion star. The discovery carries implications for models of binary-star evolution and for the use of supernovae in measuring cosmic expansion. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-faintest-supernova-staying-visible-year.html)

5. **Venus Meteor Scars Point to Slow Volcanic Resurfacing** — Phys.org Space
   A radar image from NASA’s Magellan spacecraft shows a dark spot roughly 20 kilometers wide inside two bright halos on Venus, the fainter outer halo spanning 180 kilometers. No obvious crater sits at the center, suggesting the impactor exploded in the planet’s thick atmosphere. The pattern offers new evidence that Venus experiences gradual volcanic resurfacing rather than catastrophic global events. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-meteor-blast-scars-hint-venus.html)

6. **Two European Firms Study Orbital Missile Concept** — Space.com
   Atmos Space Cargo and MBDA signed an agreement to assess the feasibility of Precision Orbital Deep Strike, a system that would drop weapons from orbit. The initial work focuses on design, feasibility, and technology integration, with any future demonstrations subject to governmental approval. Atmos’s Phoenix reusable reentry vehicle is already scheduled for a spring 2027 test flight on a SpaceX rideshare mission. Source: [space.com](https://www.space.com/technology/dropping-missiles-from-orbit-2-european-companies-sign-agreement-to-study-the-idea)

7. **UK Issues First Lunar Operations Licensing Framework** — CASRAI
   The UK Space Agency released its Technical Framework on Lunar Operations on 23 November 2026, establishing standards for trajectory deconfliction, planetary protection, spectrum allocation, and space nuclear systems. All missions that have not passed a Concept Design Review by that date must follow the new rules. The framework applies to licences under both the Space Industry Act 2018 and the Outer Space Act 1986. Spectrum coordination is governed by Ofcom under ITU regulations that protect the Shielded Zone of the Moon. Source: [casrai.org](https://casrai.org/news/uk-space-agency-technical-framework-lunar-operations-unlocking-space-2026)

8. **Stoke Space Moves Nova Pathfinder Booster to Launch Site** — NASASpaceflight
   Stoke Space’s Nova Pathfinder booster has arrived at SLC-14, positioning the vehicle for an early 2027 inaugural launch. The move marks a concrete hardware milestone toward the company’s reusable upper-stage architecture. Source: [nasaspaceflight.com](https://www.nasaspaceflight.com/2026/11/stoke-nova-booster-slc-14-2027-debut/)

### Cosmic Spotlight
Astronomers at the European Southern Observatory expressed concern that one million additional satellites approved for low-Earth orbit would raise the diffuse sky background enough to affect observations at major facilities including the Extremely Large Telescope. Their peer-reviewed analysis showed that constellations exceeding roughly 60,000 satellites produce measurable losses in field of view and increased scattered light. The study quantifies both direct satellite trails and the cumulative background component that cannot be masked. Source: [universetoday.com](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/the-eso-is-deeply-concerned-about-space-data-centers-and-the-rest-of-us-should-be-too)
