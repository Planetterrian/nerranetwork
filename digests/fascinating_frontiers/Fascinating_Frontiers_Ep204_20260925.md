1. **Côte d'Ivoire Joins Artemis Accords as 75th Signatory — NASA**

NASA issued the formal announcement of the signing. Signatories agree to common standards for exploration on the Moon and beyond. The step adds another partner to coordinated lunar efforts. Four astronauts will travel to the International Space Station on a long-duration expedition. SpaceX released imagery showing the spacecraft at the pad. The company confirmed the upcoming launch target remains on schedule. Source: [spacex.com](https://www.spacex.com/launches/crew13)

4. **NASA Astronaut Victor Glover Signs With CAA — The Hollywood Reporter**

Glover also served as pilot on the Artemis II flight around the Moon. He joined California Polytechnic State University as an executive advisor. Source: [hollywoodreporter.com](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/nasa-astronaut-victor-glover-caa-1236709704/)

The vehicles also performed engine relight sequences after separation. The developments narrow the gap with established reusable systems operated by other nations. Source: [newscientist.com](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2589931-is-china-catching-up-to-the-us-and-spacex-on-reusable-rockets/)

   The Artemis Accords establish shared guidelines for resource use. The rules also cover safety zones and debris mitigation on the lunar surface. Multiple nations have adopted the framework to coordinate future landings. The agreement adds practical operational rules to the Outer Space Treaty. Infrastructure planning now follows these coordinated standards. Source: [earthsky.org](https://earthsky.org/space/what-are-the-artemis-accords/)

The images reveal temperature variations across channels and islands in northern Italy. ESA released the image as part of its regular Earth observation series. The observations support ongoing monitoring of coastal environments. Source: [esa.int](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2026/09/Earth_from_Space_Venetian_Lagoon)

12. **NASA Unveils Winning Designs for Mars Space Food Systems Challenge — NASA Breaking**
    Chinyere Ukeje of Philadelphia received the top three hundred thousand dollar prize. The award recognized the Adaptive Nourishment Infrastructure concept. The challenge sought integrated systems that grow, process, and prepare food for long-duration Mars missions. Eight additional teams earned smaller awards for complementary technologies. The winning designs address nutrition and sustainability needs for future crews. Source: [nasa.gov](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/stmd/prizes-challenges-crowdsourcing-program/centennial-challenges/nasa-unveils-winning-designs-for-mars-space-food-systems-challenge/)

13. **NASA’s Machines for Mars Make Beer Bubbly — NASA Breaking**
    Carbon-capture hardware originally developed for Mars habitats is now purifying carbon dioxide in craft breweries. The technology removes unwanted gases during fermentation. It improves consistency of the final product. Several breweries have adopted the systems through NASA technology transfer agreements. The adaptation demonstrates practical Earth applications of space hardware. Source: [nasa.gov](https://www.nasa.gov/technology/tech-transfer-spinoffs/nasas-machines-for-mars-make-beer-bubbly/)

14. **Practicing for Safe Landings on the Moon and Beyond — NASA Breaking**
    An Alta-X drone tested the Safe and Precise Landing Integrated Capabilities Evolution system. The test occurred near NASA Armstrong Flight Research Center. The experiment simulated lunar descent profiles using sensors and algorithms. The algorithms were developed at Johnson Space Center. Results will refine guidance software for future landers on the Moon and other bodies. Source: [nasa.gov](https://www.nasa.gov/image-article/practicing-for-safe-landings-on-the-moon-and-beyond/)

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Close Asteroid Flybys Reveal Internal Structure
Apophis will pass Earth at a distance roughly one tenth the average Earth-Moon separation, close enough for tidal forces to flex the asteroid’s crust by several millimeters. Picture a city block of solid rock bending like a steel beam under its own weight; that is the scale of deformation expected during the April twenty twenty-nine encounter. Radio tracking and laser ranging from the Ramses spacecraft will measure those tiny shifts in real time, turning the flyby into a natural seismic experiment. The data should reveal whether Apophis is a single monolithic body or a loosely bound rubble pile, something current ground observations cannot resolve. Yet scientists still cannot predict exactly how much energy will couple into surface motion or whether dust will lift off and form a temporary coma. The unanswered question is whether the same tidal stresses that expose internal layers also trigger unpredictable surface changes that could affect future deflection planning.
