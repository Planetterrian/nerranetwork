### Top 12 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **ESA plans lunar service station to power experiments through the night** — European Space Agency
   The Outpost for Advanced Scientific Investigation Station will supply at least 75 watts of continuous power during the lunar day and 15 watts at night. It will also provide communications, data handling and storage for multiple payloads. OHB in Germany is leading feasibility studies, with a systems requirement review already under way. The station is designed to be modular and extendable, potentially offering sheltered spots for rovers during the night. One early payload, the Environment Characterisation and Heliophysics Observatory, will test the infrastructure while studying the lunar environment. The system draws on the Apollo-era ALSEP approach of connecting instruments to a central station. OASIS collects solar energy during the day and stores it in a battery unit to survive the nearly 15-Earth-day lunar night. It eliminates the need for each payload to carry its own heavy night-survival systems. Future versions could support science or technology demonstrators deployed farther from the lander or provide sheltered locations where rovers can survive the lunar night. The concept uses an Argonaut lander launched on a four-booster Ariane 6 rocket with communications through ESA’s Moonlight infrastructure starting with Lunar Pathfinder. Source: [esa.int](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Human_and_Robotic_Exploration/OASIS_a_European_service_station_for_the_Moon)

2. **Uranus and Neptune show different chemical makeups, hinting at separate origins** — Phys.org Space
   An international team found Neptune more enriched in oxygen than Uranus by probing their atmospheres indirectly. The contrast suggests the ice giants formed in different regions of the early solar system before migrating to their current orbits. The finding challenges the long-held view that the two planets share a common birthplace. By measuring chemical composition through atmospheric observations, the researchers revealed a significant difference in oxygen content. Neptune’s greater enrichment points to formation zones that were chemically distinct. Models of the primitive solar system must now account for this separation rather than assuming identical starting conditions for both worlds. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-planetary-twins-uranus-neptune-stories.html)

3. **Curiosity rover records first high-altitude twilight clouds of the Martian autumn** — NASA Breaking
   The rover captured zenith movies showing wispy clouds above Gale Crater shortly before sunset on sol 5037. These clouds are bright enough to appear in raw Navcam images even though most atmospheric clouds at the site remain too thin to see. The observations mark the start of seasonal twilight cloud activity as Mars enters its northern spring. The new Mars year began at solar longitude zero degrees at the end of September on Earth. Curiosity wrapped up drill activities at Basque Lakes while collecting additional GCMS data and remote-sensing observations from Mastcam and ChemCam. The team plans to drive back toward crystal forms seen in the Chiu Chiu region after completing contact science on drill tailings. Source: [science.nasa.gov](https://science.nasa.gov/blog/curiosity-blog-sols-5036-5042-watching-the-clouds-go-by/)

7. **Webb data show metal-rich gas orbiting the Milky Way’s central black hole** — Phys.org Space
   McGill University astronomers measured infrared emission from gas clouds near Sagittarius A* and found elevated levels of elements heavier than helium. The composition matches stars orbiting the black hole, suggesting the gas and stars formed together. The enrichment likely came from earlier generations of exploding stars. The James Webb Space Telescope’s high spectral and spatial resolution allowed the team to distinguish heat signatures of multiple gas clouds and precisely measure their metal content. Nicole Ford, lead author, noted that understanding why the black hole consumes material more slowly requires examining the properties of available gas, dust and stars. A companion study tested whether the gas preserved evidence of previous energy bursts from the black hole but found local conditions dominate the emission. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-webb-reveals-metal-rich-gas.html)

### Cosmic Spotlight
The European Space Agency’s proposed OASIS station would remove the need for every lunar payload to carry its own heavy night-survival systems. By supplying shared power, communications and data storage, the station lets experiment teams focus on science rather than engineering. One early payload, ECHO, will both characterise the lunar environment and demonstrate the infrastructure in action. Future versions could include rover garages or extendable harnesses reaching farther from the lander. The modular design draws directly from the Apollo ALSEP model while adapting it for robotic, long-duration operations. OASIS is intended as a recurring capability rather than a one-off mission, with plans to operate as part of a European lunar architecture using an Argonaut lander and Moonlight communications. The baseline design consists of a service station with a solar array and a separate battery unit capable of delivering at least 75 watts during the day and 15 watts at night. This shared infrastructure approach mirrors the philosophy of the Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Package but scales it for multiple simultaneous robotic payloads.

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Ice Giants May Have Swapped Places
Uranus sits 2.9 billion kilometres from the Sun while Neptune lies another 1.6 billion kilometres farther out, a gap wide enough to contain the entire inner solar system twice over. That vast separation once seemed to support the idea that the two planets formed as twins in the same region. New atmospheric measurements now suggest otherwise. Researchers compared the amount of oxygen locked in each planet’s deep layers and found Neptune noticeably richer. The difference points to formation zones that were chemically distinct before the planets migrated to their present orbits.

Imagine riding along with the building blocks of these worlds as the young solar system stirred. Closer to the Sun, temperatures allowed more oxygen-bearing ices to condense, while farther out the mix favoured other compounds. A planet that assembled in the oxygen-rich zone would carry a different bulk composition than one born farther away. Once giant planets began flinging one another around through gravitational encounters, those original locations became scrambled. The surviving chemical fingerprints in today’s atmospheres are the only record left of where each world started. The international team used indirect atmospheric probing techniques to reveal the oxygen contrast without sending probes into the planets themselves. Their results show Neptune carries a measurably higher oxygen enrichment, forcing planetary-formation models to incorporate separate birth regions rather than a single shared nursery.

We still do not know exactly how far apart those birthplaces were or what triggered the migration. Models can reproduce the current orbits, yet they cannot yet match the measured oxygen contrast without fine-tuning assumptions about the early disk. A single, high-precision measurement of another heavy element in both atmospheres could decide whether the planets truly formed on opposite sides of a chemical boundary or whether some later process erased the expected similarity. The same data might also clarify whether the migration happened early, while the disk was still gas-rich, or later, after most of the gas had dissipated. Either scenario would reshape how we reconstruct the dynamical history of the outer solar system and the delivery of volatiles to the inner planets.
