### Cosmic Spotlight
NASA’s 200-day mark inside the simulated Mars habitat shows how crews manage daily life without fresh air or resupply. The closed environment forces constant monitoring of water, oxygen, and waste while psychologists track mood and performance. Data from this phase will shape habitat layouts and crew schedules for actual Mars missions years from now. The test also reveals which small comforts and routines become unexpectedly important over months of isolation. ### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Infrared Eyes Reveal Hidden Galactic Bars
If you could shrink the Milky Way to the size of a dinner plate, the central bar would still stretch across most of that plate like a cosmic axle driving the galaxy’s rotation. Infrared light slips past the dust that blocks visible starlight, letting telescopes map the dense lanes of older stars that form these bars. In galaxy M77 the near-infrared view shows the bar feeding gas into a tight ring where new stars ignite at furious rates. The same wavelengths also expose how the bar’s gravitational pull funnels material inward on timescales of just a few hundred million years. We can watch the process in thousands of galaxies, yet no one has fully explained why some spirals develop strong bars while others remain smooth and featureless disks. What hidden trigger decides whether a galaxy grows a bar or stays bar-free?
