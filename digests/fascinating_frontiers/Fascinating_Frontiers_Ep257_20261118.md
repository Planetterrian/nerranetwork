### Top 12 Space & Astronomy Stories

1. **Nammo completes hot-fire tests for Argonaut lander engine — space & defense**
   Nammo UK finished a hot-fire test campaign on a prototype RELIANCE lunar landing engine. The 6 kN bi-propellant engine uses hypergolic propellants and a pintle injector with electrically driven centrifugal pumps. Testing showed throttle control between 50 and 100 percent thrust. The company completed a Preliminary Design Review that confirmed design maturity. An engineering model build and test campaign is scheduled for 2027. ESA Argonaut Team Leader Ludovic Duvet called RELIANCE a key technology step for Europe that also opens applications for future lunar return missions. Source: [spaceanddefense.io](https://spaceanddefense.io/nammo-uk-reports-engine-test-milestone-for-esa-argonaut-lunar-lander/)

2. **NASA sets December 7 date for Roman telescope first images — NASA**
   The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope will deliver its first images during a livestream from the American Geophysical Union meeting in San Francisco. Roman launched August 30 on a Falcon Heavy and is completing a three-month commissioning process at the Sun-Earth L2 point. The mission will use its large field of view and infrared vision to map vast sky areas and study dark energy, dark matter, and exoplanets. A coronagraph technology demonstration will attempt direct imaging of planets around other stars. NASA will also offer media briefings and a fireside chat with Administrator Jared Isaacman. Source: [nasa.gov](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-invites-media-public-to-view-roman-telescopes-first-images/)

3. **Dragon spacecraft carries science samples home from station — NASA**
   A SpaceX Dragon will undock from the International Space Station no earlier than November 18 and splash down off California no earlier than November 19. The spacecraft returns a 3D-bioprinted blood vessel construct used to study vascular aging, frozen tumor organoids for cancer research, and microbial samples to examine antibiotic-resistant bacteria in space. A lighting system tested for astronaut sleep and well-being over three years is also coming back. The uncrewed Dragon arrived October 15 with more than 6,300 pounds of supplies. Source: [nasa.gov](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-to-cover-35th-spacex-resupply-mission-space-station-departure/)

4. **Chandra releases sonification album of forming stars — NASA**
   NASA’s Chandra X-ray Observatory has turned four star-forming regions into sound. The album combines Chandra X-ray data with Hubble optical and Webb and Spitzer infrared views of M82, the Orion Nebula, the Carina Nebula, and Rho Ophiuchi. In the Orion Nebula sonification, more than a thousand energetic young stars appear as twinkling sounds while diffuse X-ray emission maps to high tones. The Carina “Cosmic Cliffs” data assign short string sounds to individual baby stars and longer notes to the diffuse X-ray glow. Source: [science.nasa.gov](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/chandra/new-album-of-baby-star-sounds-shared-by-nasas-chandra/)

5. **Curiosity data point to thin surface water films on Mars — Phys.org Space**
   More than a decade of atmospheric measurements from the Curiosity rover suggest small amounts of surface water exist on Mars. Researchers analyzed daily and seasonal changes in the carbon dioxide-rich atmosphere and found that known processes such as polar freezing of carbon dioxide cannot fully explain the observations. The remaining signal is consistent with water attached to soil grains or present in extremely thin liquid-like films at the surface. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-decade-atmospheric-nasa-curiosity-rover.html)

6. **Radio telescopes map nearby binary star system in detail — Phys.org Space**
   Astronomers used the NSF Very Long Baseline Array to measure the orbit, masses, and distance of the red dwarf binary DG CVn with high precision. The two stars orbit each other every 4.511 years on an elliptical path that brings them as close as 0.59 times the Earth-Sun distance. The more massive star contains 39 percent of the Sun’s mass and its companion 36 percent. The system lies 59.3 light-years from Earth. Both stars emit detectable radio waves, allowing separate tracking of each component across 27 position measurements spanning 18 years. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-astronomers-track-flaring-dwarf-star.html)

7. **NICER telescope ends science operations on station — NASA**
   NASA’s NICER X-ray telescope on the International Space Station has concluded science observations after six and a half years beyond its original mission. An internal motor failure in June 2025 left the instrument unable to track targets beyond its current orientation. NICER measured neutron star properties, demonstrated pulsar-based navigation, and supported studies of black holes and active galaxies. The team will now archive eight years of data and plan decommissioning. Source: [nasa.gov](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/nicer-status-updates/)

8. **New supernova spotted in nearby spiral galaxy — Phys.org Space**
   A Type Ia supernova has been discovered in NGC 1433, a spiral galaxy roughly 50 million light-years away. The event offers astronomers a relatively nearby target for detailed follow-up observations. Type Ia supernovae occur in binary systems when a white dwarf explodes after accreting material from a companion star. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-supernova-million-years-astronomers.html)

9. **China outlines plans for Callisto lander — Universe Today**
   China has signaled its intention to place a small lander on Jupiter’s moon Callisto. The mission would mark only the second landing on a Solar System body beyond Mars. The lander is described as carrying two instruments, with the scientific return expected to be substantial even at that scale. Source: [universetoday.com](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/china-signals-its-intention-to-land-on-jupiters-moon-callisto)

10. **Reflect Orbital readies first sunlight-beaming mirror — Space.com**
    British company Reflect Orbital plans to launch its first orbital mirror next year as part of a larger constellation intended to redirect sunlight after dark. The company has completed an orbital test of a demonstration version of its laser-reflecting technology aimed at addressing space debris concerns. Source: [space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/satellites/reflect-orbital-wants-to-launch-thousands-of-sunlight-beaming-mirrors-to-space-and-the-1st-will-go-up-next-year)
