### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **NASA to Name Artemis III Crew Next Week — WKMG**
   The agency will hold a briefing on June 9 at 11 a.m. ET to introduce the astronauts assigned to the mission. Artemis III will use the third European Service Module to power Orion and will demonstrate docking with commercial lunar landers in low Earth orbit. This step directly supports the goal of returning humans to the Moon’s surface. The crew announcement advances planning for the first crewed landing under the current architecture. Observers will watch for details on training timelines and lander integration tests.

2. **Prada and Axiom Reveal Lunar Cooling Garment — Space.com**
   The liquid cooling ventilation garment features a design described as “very aesthetically pleasing” by project leads. It will be worn beneath the Axiom Space lunar spacesuit developed for NASA. The garment manages heat during surface operations on the Moon. This marks the first public reveal of a finished element from the Prada partnership. Next steps include further integration testing with the full suit assembly.

3. **Axiom Advances Full Lunar Spacesuit Design — SpaceNews**
   The company unveiled another completed component of the suit intended for NASA’s Artemis program. The design incorporates materials and construction techniques developed with Prada. This element joins the cooling garment in moving from concept to hardware. The partnership aims to deliver a suit ready for crewed lunar surface activity. Follow-on work will focus on certification and mobility testing.

4. **NASA Tests Spacecraft Network Switching Technology — SciTechDaily**
   The new system allows spacecraft to move between different communication networks without interruption. Engineers demonstrated the capability in recent ground trials. The technology addresses growing demand for flexible links as more commercial and government assets operate in orbit. It builds on existing relay infrastructure to improve data return rates. Future missions will incorporate the switching protocol during initial flight tests.

5. **ESA and EBRD Sign Earth Observation Agreement — European Space Agency**
   The partnership will apply satellite data to development and impact finance projects. EBRD clients will gain access to imagery and analysis for monitoring infrastructure and environmental conditions. The agreement expands use of European Earth observation assets beyond traditional science programs. Initial projects will target regions where EBRD operates. Progress will be tracked through joint pilot studies over the next two years.

6. **UK Startup NewOrbit Raises $18.5 Million — SpaceNews**
   NewOrbit Space secured Series A funding to develop satellites for very low Earth orbit operations. The capital will support construction of the first demonstration spacecraft. VLEO platforms promise lower latency and higher resolution imaging than traditional orbits. The round reflects continued investor interest in specialized orbital regimes. The company plans an initial launch within the next two years.

7. **OQ Technology Schedules Direct-to-Smartphone Test — SpaceNews**

Success would mark an early European entry into the direct-to-device market. The test targets standard smartphones without hardware modifications. Results will inform regulatory filings across additional countries.

The system provides a nearby laboratory for studying coherent radio emission. Data were collected with the Australian Square Kilometre Array Pathfinder. Continued monitoring will refine the orbital parameters and emission geometry.

### Cosmic Spotlight
The ASKAP J1745 binary offers the first clear periodic clock for a repeating radio burst source. Repeated observations tie the emission directly to the orbital motion of two compact stars, giving astronomers a predictable window to study the emission mechanism. Because the system is relatively nearby, sensitive arrays can capture fine time structure that distant fast-radio-burst sources wash out. The next observing season will test whether the burst strength varies with orbital phase or remains constant. What physical process in the stellar magnetospheres actually generates the coherent radio waves remains unknown.

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Binary Star Systems Produce Repeating Radio Bursts
A pair of neutron stars orbiting each other every few hours can release more radio energy in a single burst than the Sun emits across its entire surface in a day. Picture riding alongside one of the stars as its magnetic field sweeps through the companion’s wind; the interaction twists and reconnects field lines in milliseconds, accelerating particles that then radiate in phase like a natural laser. The entire process repeats with clock-like precision because the orbit itself sets the timer. In the case of ASKAP J1745 the bursts arrive every 1.5 hours, matching the measured orbital period to within seconds. Yet no model fully explains why only certain binaries produce this coherent emission while others remain silent. The missing piece is whether the radio waves require a minimum magnetic-field strength, a particular wind density, or an alignment we have not yet measured.
