### Top 12 Space & Astronomy Stories

1. **Milky Way bar drives unexpected star formation** — Phys.org Space
   A University of Sydney Ph.D. student uncovered evidence that the Milky Way is unusual in where its new stars form compared with nearby galaxies. A vast rotating structure of stars near the galactic center drives enormous shock fronts through gas clouds, compressing them and creating conditions for new stars. The discovery challenges the existence of the Norma spiral arm, suggesting at least part of it may instead be a region of star formation along the leading edge of the galactic bar. Lead author Dr. Hillary Davis reached the conclusion by comparing sophisticated computer simulations of galaxies evolving over billions of years with astronomical observations of masers. Masers are naturally occurring sources of concentrated microwave emissions associated with regions where massive stars are forming. The findings place further constraints on the size, angle and rotation speed of the central bar. Researchers estimate the bar extends approximately 4.3 kiloparsecs, about 14,000 light-years, from the galactic center along its long axis. The work was published in Nature Astronomy. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-galactic-discovery-milky.html)

2. **Mercury-bound X-ray telescope delivers first images** — Phys.org Space
   The Mercury Imaging X-ray Spectrometer aboard the BepiColombo spacecraft has provided its first observations of the Crab Nebula, one of the brightest X-ray sources in the sky. A team led by the University of Leicester made calibration measurements after the Mercury Transfer Module separated from the joined science orbiters in September. The main observation sequence consisted of 55 spacecraft slews with 39 hours of observations during Sept. 11–12. The team found the instrument's angular resolution to be slightly better than when last measured in the lab in 2015. Principal investigator Emma Bunce said the detector has suffered no radiation damage after eight years in space and that stray-light effects are absent from the images. MIXS is the first imaging X-ray telescope operating on a planetary science mission and will produce a global map of Mercury's surface atomic composition. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-mercury-bound-ray-telescope-images.html)

3. **NTT and SSC study global laser ground-station network** — Unite.AI
   NTT and SSC Space have signed a memorandum of understanding to examine how optical ground stations in different countries could operate as one network. The agreement covers market needs, technical compatibility, geographic coverage and links to terrestrial networks. SSC Space's stations in Australia and Chile each offer up to 10 gigabits per second. Optical links can move more data during a contact window than radio but remain vulnerable to cloud cover at any single station. NTT supplies optical networking and network control expertise while SSC Space operates satellite ground infrastructure. The first phase is a study of interoperability and commercial requirements rather than the launch of a joint service. Source: [unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/ntt-ssc-space-laser-ground-station-network/)

4. **Yukon students hear from CSA astronauts** — Education News Canada
   Around 300 students from secondary schools across Whitehorse gathered on November 18 to hear CSA astronaut Jenni Gibbons discuss space exploration and Canada's role in Artemis II and the Crew-13 mission. A special video message from CSA astronaut Colonel Joshua Kutryk was recorded aboard the International Space Station for the students. The event is part of a broader visit to Whitehorse by Gibbons that included outreach activities on November 17 and 18. Joshua Kutryk launched to the station on October 1 and has become the fifth CSA astronaut to conduct a spacewalk. The Canadian Space Agency uses the excitement of space missions to connect students with astronauts and bring STEM learning to life. Source: [educationnewscanada.com](https://educationnewscanada.com/article/education/level/k12/3/1229433/from-whitehorse-to-space-yukon-students-hear-from-csa-astronauts-jenni-gibbons-and-joshua-kutryk.html)

5. **Rare triple-star system found 28 light-years away** — Notebookcheck
   Astronomers led by the Trottier Institute for Research on Exoplanets at the University of Montreal have detected a triple-star system just 27.6 light-years from Earth. The system contains a red dwarf and two white dwarfs, one orbiting close to the red dwarf and the other more distant. The discovery was made while searching for planets around the red dwarf TYC 3980-1081-1 using the SPIRou instrument at the Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope. Velocity variations of about 10 miles per second prompted follow-up observations with the Gemini North telescope that directly detected the faint white-dwarf companion. Professor Patrick Dufour said the system should not exist and that gravitational interactions between the three stars remain poorly understood. Source: [notebookcheck.net](https://www.notebookcheck.net/A-rare-triple-star-system-has-been-detected-by-astronomers.1454773.0.html)

6. **BepiColombo spacecraft begins Mercury orbit insertion** — New Scientist
   The joint ESA/JAXA BepiColombo mission is scheduled to start its orbital insertion sequence on November 21 after an eight-year journey. The two spacecraft will separate and enter complementary orbits around Mercury to study its surface, magnetic field and exosphere. Arrival marks the end of the longest cruise phase for any planetary mission to date. Source: [newscientist.com](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2599799-the-bepicolombo-spacecraft-is-finally-entering-orbit-around-mercury/)

7. **Northrop Grumman cargo mission set for November 29** — NASA
   NASA, Northrop Grumman and SpaceX are targeting no earlier than 10:47 a.m. EST on November 29 for the CRS-25 mission to the International Space Station. The Cygnus XL spacecraft will carry approximately 11,000 pounds of science, equipment and supplies including fresh fruit and holiday treats. NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney will use the Canadarm2 to capture the spacecraft on December 1. Source: [nasa.gov](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-sets-coverage-for-northrop-grumman-crs-25-launch-prelaunch/?utm_source=TWITTER&utm_medium=NASA&utm_campaign=NASASocial&linkId=1040586353)

8. **Euclid begins strong hunt for gravitational lenses** — Phys.org Space
   The Euclid mission has identified 15,000 strong gravitational lens candidates in its first data release. These rare alignments let astronomers map the distribution of dark matter across cosmic scales. The wide-field survey is designed to build a three-dimensional map of the universe and measure its accelerating expansion. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-euclid-strong-gravitational-lenses.html)
