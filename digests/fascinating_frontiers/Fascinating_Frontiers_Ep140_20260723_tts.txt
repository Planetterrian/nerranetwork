A candidate Jupiter-sized moon orbiting far beyond our solar system is forcing astronomers to redraw the line between planets and moons.

Astronomers have found evidence for a satellite orbiting an exoplanet outside our solar system through transit timing variations and subtle changes in the host star's light.

The candidate object is roughly Jupiter-sized, which challenges traditional distinctions between planets and moons.

Further observations with larger telescopes are required to verify the signal and rule out other dynamical effects.

While astronomers hunt for these borderline worlds at interstellar distances, closer to home a major telescope has uncovered a hidden giant in a young planetary system.

NASA's James Webb Space Telescope has detected a giant planet orbiting within the debris disk of Beta Pictoris.

The planet was identified through direct imaging after earlier data sets were re-examined with improved calibration.

Its presence helps explain gaps and structures previously seen in the disk by other telescopes.

The discovery adds a third giant planet to the system, which is only about 20 million years old.

Follow-up observations will measure the planet's orbit and atmospheric composition.

Astronomers have confirmed an atmosphere on a rocky exoplanet located in the habitable zone of its star.

Transmission spectroscopy during transits revealed absorption features consistent with a substantial gaseous envelope.

The planet's size and density suggest a composition similar to Earth or Venus.

The detection relied on multiple transit observations to separate the atmospheric signal from stellar variability.

Additional spectra are planned to identify specific molecules.

NASA's New Horizons spacecraft has ended a year-long hibernation to begin new observations in the outer solar system.

The craft will study distant Kuiper Belt objects and measure the heliosphere's edge.

Instruments will collect data on cosmic rays and dust particles at distances never before sampled.

Mission controllers have confirmed all systems are operating normally after the long sleep.

The new phase extends the spacecraft's exploration well past its original Pluto encounter.

Data from these flights will inform certification for Artemis missions.

The team plans to compare these markers with data from future commercial crew flights.

The findings may help design targeted countermeasures for longer missions.

NASA is recruiting volunteers to participate in a simulated year-long mission to the Moon and Mars conducted entirely on the ground.

Results will inform crew selection and habitat design for future deep-space missions.

Observers can use the date to note the shift from its earlier westward drift against background stars.

Daily sky charts show Mercury's changing position relative to Castor and Pollux.

The finding adds to the inventory of prebiotic molecules available during planet formation.

Further surveys will search for related compounds in the same cloud.
