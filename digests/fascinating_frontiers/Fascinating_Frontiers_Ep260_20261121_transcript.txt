The European Space Agency has selected plasma observatory as its next medium-class mission, according to ESA.int. That selection gives the project formal approval to proceed into detailed development.
Formal approval is the step that lets plasma observatory move beyond a concept and into
the work of being built as a mission.
The mission will fly seven coordinated spacecraft.
Those seven spacecraft will measure how solar energy flows through Earth's magnetic
shield.
They will take those measurements at multiple scales of distance.
They will also take them at multiple scales of time, so the same energy flow can be tracked
as it changes from place to place and from moment to moment.
Israel has proposed a new space law on launches and asteroid mining, the Jerusalem Post Reports.
The draft, covered by JPOST.com, would require permits for satellite launches.
It would require permits for satellite sales.
Asteroid mining activities would require permits as well.
The draft aims to regulate commercial space operations and national space operations together.
Those commercial and national activities would sit under a single licensing framework rather
than under separate tracks.
Aldebaran, according to Space.com, looks like the brightest member of the Hyades yet
lies at a different distance.
The Hyades is a star cluster that appears as a group in the night sky.
Measurements of how the star drifts across the sky and of its parallax, the shift that
encodes distance, show it does not belong with the cluster.
The pairing is a line of sight coincidence that can fool the eye.
Lunar soil can be processed, Fizz.org reports, by pairing semiconductor manufacturing methods
with the chemistry of lunar regolith. Regolith is the loose dusty rock that blankets the
moon. Oxygen comes off in the process and metals that can be used come with it. Lunar
bases could then haul less oxygen up from Earth. The James Webb Space Telescope recorded
infrared pulses from magnetar 4U0142 plus 61, AAS NOVA reports. A magnetar is a dense
remnant of a collapsed star with an intense magnetic field. JWST detected pulsed infrared
emission from that magnetar. The infrared pulses align in time with the objects known X-ray pulses.
That overlap in timing is what the observations turn on. The overlap indicates the infrared
a mission originates inside the magnetosphere rather than from a surrounding disc.
The magnetosphere here is the magnetic zone around the remnant, not a disc of material
circling it.
The report is carried by AAS NOVA, with the underlying work pointed to at DOI.org.
Successive eruptions from one solar active region can combine into one more powerful outburst.
A coronal mass ejection is a burst of charged gas and magnetic field from the sun.
Models show the first blast opens a path, so the next one can race faster and travel farther.
Accounting for those mergers improves forecasts of geomagnetic storms at Earth.
Laboratory experiments reported by Fizz.org tested Venus-like conditions for preserving ancient water traces.
Those experiments expose minerals to high temperature, similar to Venus' surface.
They also expose the minerals to high pressure, similar to Venus' surface.
Certain minerals retained chemical signatures of past water even after prolonged
exposure. The result suggests that some Venus surface rocks could still hold evidence of
earlier habitable conditions. The question in the lab was whether those water traces can
survive the heat and pressure, not whether a given rock on Venus has already been sampled.
Low viscosity lava's offer clues to early Mars history according to Fizz.org. Perseverance
images of the Saita formation show textures consistent with very fluid lava flows.
The flows date to roughly 4 billion years ago. Their low viscosity implies higher eruption
temperatures than typical earth basalts. It also allows for a different composition than
typical earth basalts. Either way, the Saita textures are being read as a record of how
freely that lava moved when it was in place. Alma imaged a dying star casting off its
layers into a large expanding ring of gas. An hourglass shape is already
emerging in the inner regions. Those structures document the late evolution of
the star before it becomes a planetary nebula. A planetary nebula is the
glowing shell of gas left as a star dies. Russia is developing a nuclear
space weapon, a US report finds, space.com reports. The finding is tied to a
defense intelligence agency assessment. That assessment describes Russian work on
a satellite. The satellite in the assessment's description would carry a nuclear device.
Space.com is the outlet carrying that assessment. Astronomers are tracking shifting pulsation
modes in the Delta Scoody Star, KIC 473720, Fizz.org reports. A Delta Scoody star is one
whose outer layers swell and shrink, so its light rises and falls. KIC 473770 shows multiple
pulsation modes at the same time. Those modes change over time rather than
holding still. High precision brightness measurements are what make the tracking
possible. The brightness records reveal how the modes interact inside the
star. The modes couple deep inside below the visible surface. That coupling is a
direct look at layers of density below the surface. It is also a direct look at
layers of temperature below the surface. The data provide a direct probe of the
stars internal density and temperature structure.
One measurement of this kind can quietly rewrite a familiar patch of sky.
