The European Space Agency has selected Plasma Observatory as its next medium-class mission, according to esa.int.

That selection gives the project formal approval to proceed into detailed development.

Formal approval is the step that lets Plasma Observatory move beyond a concept and into the work of being built as a mission.

The mission will fly seven coordinated spacecraft.

Those seven spacecraft will measure how solar energy flows through Earth’s magnetic shield.

They will take those measurements at multiple scales of distance.

They will also take them at multiple scales of time, so the same energy flow can be tracked as it changes from place to place and from moment to moment.

Israel has proposed a new space law on launches and asteroid mining, The Jerusalem Post reports.

The draft, covered by jpost.com, would require permits for satellite launches.

It would require permits for satellite sales.

Asteroid mining activities would require permits as well.

The draft aims to regulate commercial space operations and national space operations together.

Those commercial and national activities would sit under a single licensing framework rather than under separate tracks.

Aldebaran, according to Space.com, looks like the brightest member of the Hyades yet lies at a different distance.

The Hyades is a star cluster that appears as a group in the night sky.

Measurements of how the star drifts across the sky, and of its parallax, the shift that encodes distance, show it does not belong with the cluster.

The pairing is a line-of-sight coincidence that can fool the eye.

Lunar soil can be processed, Phys.org reports, by pairing semiconductor manufacturing methods with the chemistry of lunar regolith.

Regolith is the loose dusty rock that blankets the Moon.

Oxygen comes off in the process, and metals that can be used come with it.

Lunar bases could then haul less oxygen up from Earth.

The James Webb Space Telescope recorded infrared pulses from magnetar 4U 0142+61, AAS Nova reports.

A magnetar is a dense remnant of a collapsed star with an intense magnetic field.

JWST detected pulsed infrared emission from that magnetar.

The infrared pulses align in time with the object’s known X-ray pulses.

That overlap in timing is what the observations turn on.

The overlap indicates the infrared emission originates inside the magnetosphere rather than from a surrounding disk.

The magnetosphere, here, is the magnetic zone around the remnant, not a disk of material circling it.

The report is carried by AAS Nova, with the underlying work pointed to at doi.org.

Successive eruptions from one solar active region can combine into one more powerful outburst.

A coronal mass ejection is a burst of charged gas and magnetic field from the Sun.

Models show the first blast opens a path, so the next one can race faster and travel farther.

Accounting for those mergers improves forecasts of geomagnetic storms at Earth.

Laboratory experiments reported by Phys.org tested Venus-like conditions for preserving ancient water traces.

Those experiments exposed minerals to high temperature similar to Venus’s surface.

They also exposed the minerals to high pressure similar to Venus’s surface.

Certain minerals retained chemical signatures of past water even after prolonged exposure.

The results suggest that some Venus surface rocks could still hold evidence of earlier habitable conditions.

The question in the lab was whether those water traces can survive the heat and pressure, not whether a given rock on Venus has already been sampled.

Low-viscosity lavas offer clues to early Mars history, according to Phys.org.

Perseverance images of the Séítah formation show textures consistent with very fluid lava flows.

The flows date to roughly four billion years ago.

Their low viscosity implies higher eruption temperatures than typical Earth basalts.

It also allows for a different composition than typical Earth basalts.

Either way, the Séítah textures are being read as a record of how freely that lava moved when it was emplaced.

ALMA imaged a dying star casting off its outer layers into a large expanding ring of gas.

An hourglass shape is already emerging in the inner regions.

Those structures document the late evolution of the star before it becomes a planetary nebula.

A planetary nebula is the glowing shell of gas left as a star dies.

Russia is developing a nuclear space weapon, a U.S. report finds, Space.com reports.

The finding is tied to a Defense Intelligence Agency assessment.

That assessment describes Russian work on a satellite.

The satellite, in the assessment’s description, would carry a nuclear device.

Space.com is the outlet carrying that assessment.

Astronomers are tracking shifting pulsation modes in the δ Scuti star KIC 4737720, Phys.org reports.

A delta Scuti star is one whose outer layers swell and shrink, so its light rises and falls.

KIC 4737720 shows multiple pulsation modes at the same time.

Those modes change over time rather than holding still.

High-precision brightness measurements are what make the tracking possible.

The brightness records reveal how the modes interact inside the star.

The modes couple deep inside, below the visible surface.

That coupling is a direct look at layers of density below the surface.

It is also a direct look at layers of temperature below the surface.

The data provide a direct probe of the star’s internal density and temperature structure.

One measurement of this kind can quietly rewrite a familiar patch of sky.
