### Top 12 Space & Astronomy Stories

1. **Potatoes Complete Life Cycle in Simulated Lunar Soil — Phys.org Space**
Researchers showed potatoes grown in simulated lunar soil amended with organic matter are safe to eat and achieve growth and yields comparable to Earth. "We found that potatoes can complete their entire life cycle in simulated lunar soil when supplemented with organic matter," said Aymeric Goyer of Oregon State University's Department of Botany and Plant Pathology. Plants grown with 30 percent vermicompost matched Earth-soil performance while those without amendment were severely stunted. The organic matter binds heavy metals and reduces plant exposure to harmful compounds. Researchers tested five regolith simulants representing lunar mare and highland regions plus soil from Hermiston, Oregon. Potatoes were selected because they produce more calories per unit of land than many other crops. A forthcoming paper will evaluate ten potato varieties in the same simulants. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-potatoes-grown-simulated-lunar-soil.html)

2. **Webb Captures First Stellar Merger in Andromeda Galaxy — NASA**
Researchers identified a star slightly less massive than the Sun that spiraled into a supergiant at the outskirts of the Andromeda galaxy and nicknamed the result Bao. The smaller star merged after only seven orbits lasting about seven years. Webb observed the system 47 days after the eruption peak, the earliest follow-up of such an event. The primary star was seven times the Sun's mass, 350 times its radius and 18,000 times brighter before the merger. The companion was approximately 0.7 solar masses. The team used mid-infrared data to measure mass ejected during common-envelope evolution and cross-referenced ultraviolet, visible and near-infrared observations from multiple telescopes. Source: [science.nasa.gov](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/webb/nasa-data-clinches-first-of-its-kind-stellar-merger/)

3. **First Lunar-Specific Ground Motion Model Developed for Moonquakes — Phys.org Space**
Civil engineers at Slate Geotechnical Consultants created the first ground-motion model tailored to shallow moonquakes using Apollo seismic recordings and physics-based simulations. The model predicts significantly higher ground motions at large distances than terrestrial models. Shaking at 200 kilometres on the Moon equals a short-to-moderate distance by lunar standards while the same distance on Earth would show mostly attenuated motion. Lunar recordings show strong shaking persisting past 1,000 kilometres even for small-magnitude events. The colder, more solid lunar interior lets seismic waves lose less energy while the fractured surface scatters energy into long codas. Apollo data show an average strong-shaking duration of 54 minutes compared with under one minute for shallow crustal earthquakes on Earth. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-lunar-specific-ground-motion-seismic.html)

4. **Korean Researchers Test AI and Supercomputers for Future Missions — Asiae**
The Korea Aerospace Administration held a workshop on numerical models of astrophysical phenomena and artificial intelligence attended by about 50 researchers from universities and institutes. Participants presented simulations of galaxy formation, gravitational waves, planetary collisions and the effects of lunar-lander exhaust plumes on regolith. Researchers discussed using these models to pre-validate lander safety and observation strategies for space telescopes and lunar surface instruments. The Korea Institute of Science and Technology Information and the Institute for Basic Science shared updates on national supercomputing resources while the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute reported progress on an AI collaboration platform. KASA plans to incorporate the proposals into future mission planning and policy. Source: [asiae.co.kr](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/science/2026111912015841273)

7. **ULA Traces Vulcan Failure to Inadequate Fuel Supply — Spaceflight Now**

The six solid rocket boosters and Centaur upper stage performed as expected following earlier nozzle improvements. The FAA is overseeing the ongoing investigation. The spacecraft launched in 1977 and continues returning data from interstellar space. Source: [nasa.gov](https://www.nasa.gov/image-detail/nasas-voyager-1-spacecraft-light-day/)

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Seismic Waves Behave on the Moon
The Moon’s interior is colder and more solid than Earth’s, so seismic waves lose less energy as they travel. Fractured rubble near the surface scatters those waves repeatedly, spreading energy into long codas that keep the ground moving for tens of minutes. Apollo seismometers recorded an average strong-shaking duration of 54 minutes after even modest moonquakes, compared with under one minute for similar events on Earth. Because the scattering is so efficient, shaking at 200 kilometres on the Moon is still intense, whereas the same distance on Earth would already show strong attenuation. Researchers combined the four-station Apollo data set with physics-based simulations that used lunar-specific inputs for temperature, rigidity and surface roughness. The resulting model predicts ground motions that remain elevated well past 1,000 kilometres, a distance at which terrestrial models expect almost no detectable shaking. The same scattering that lengthens the signal also makes it harder to pinpoint the exact source location with the limited station geometry available from the Apollo era. Modern broadband instruments placed at varied depths and distances would capture a wider frequency range and allow direct measurement of how wave energy decays with distance and time. Those measurements would tighten the model and give engineers the numbers they need to design structures that can withstand prolonged lunar shaking.
