### Top 12 Space & Astronomy Stories

1. **Next-Generation Laser Ranging Debuts in Arctic Norway — NASA Breaking**
   A faster satellite laser ranging system began operations on October 12 at the Ny-Ålesund Geodetic Observatory in Svalbard. The instrument fires thousands of pulses per second and detects single photons, allowing more accurate orbit tracking than older systems that fired only tens of times per second. It is the first NASA next-generation SLR station and the northernmost facility of its kind. The remote Arctic location improves coverage of polar-orbiting satellites such as ICESat-2 and GRACE-FO, which the station can observe on nearly every orbit. The system also supports the terrestrial reference frame used for GPS, sea-level measurements, and tectonic studies. Automation improvements were driven by the site's proximity to a bird sanctuary that restricts summer access. Source: [science.nasa.gov](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/laser-focus-at-the-top-of-the-world/)

2. **Solar Sails Encounter Unexpected Drag Near Relativistic Speeds — ScienceDaily Space & Time**
   Researchers modeled how laser-driven solar sails behave once they reach 75 percent of the speed of light. At that velocity, relativistic aberration concentrates diffusely scattered light in the forward direction, producing a recoil force that acts as drag. The study examined three momentum-transfer mechanisms: incident light, specular reflection, and diffuse scattering. Incident light remains the strongest contributor, yet all three lose effectiveness as the incoming laser beam redshifts from the sail's perspective. The finding applies to concepts such as Breakthrough Starshot that aim to accelerate small probes to a substantial fraction of light speed. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261120071305.htm)

3. **Mass Gap in Black Hole Mergers Points to Failed Supernovae — ScienceDaily Space & Time**
   Analysis of 257 black hole mergers detected by LIGO, Virgo, and KAGRA revealed that objects near 10 solar masses appear in roughly three-quarters of events. A clear shortage exists between 12 and 16 solar masses. Black holes near 10 solar masses also show more uniform spin rates than the overall population. The pattern matches predictions for stars that collapse directly into black holes without exploding. The study was led by Caltech physicist Katerina Chatziioannou with lead author Isaac Legred. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261121221126.htm)

4. **AI-Accelerated Simulation Models Entire Milky Way at Star Level — Phys.org Space**
   The PHEASANT simulation combines three supercomputers and an AI model that predicts gas evolution after supernovae. The AI replaces direct calculations that would otherwise require 100-year time steps, allowing the full galaxy run to advance in 100,000-year increments. The approach runs roughly 20 times faster than exhaustive computation. It was presented at the SC 2026 conference and developed by teams including the University of Tokyo and RIKEN. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-milky-simulation-resolution-supercomputers.html)

5. **Europa's Sodium Cloud Remains Stable Over Two Decades — Phys.org Space**
   Observations from the W. M. Keck Observatory show that the diffuse sodium cloud around Jupiter's moon Europa has stayed remarkably constant despite the dynamic Jovian environment. The stability supports an oceanic source for the sodium rather than sporadic surface processes. Data span more than twenty years. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-astronomers-reveal-oceanic-europa-sodium.html)

6. **Vera C. Rubin Observatory Begins Legacy Survey of Space and Time — Astronomy**
   The Legacy Survey of Space and Time has started operations at the Vera C. Rubin Observatory. The 8.4-meter telescope with its 3.2-gigapixel camera will capture about 1,000 images each night. The survey follows more than twenty years of planning and construction. Source: [astronomy.com](https://www.astronomy.com/science/rubin-brings-the-universe-into-focus/)

7. **Sound Waves From Sunspots Drive Plasma Jets — Universe Today**
   A new study in The Astrophysical Journal tracked roughly two hundred jet-like plasma bursts called dynamic fibrils originating from sunspots. The jets are launched by sound waves generated in the spots. The observations add detail to explanations for why the corona is hundreds of times hotter than the Sun's surface. Source: [universetoday.com](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/sound-waves-from-sunspots-launch-fiery-plasma-jets-across-the-sun)

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Dune Grains Reveal Planetary Wind Speeds
On Earth we judge wind by watching flags or feeling it on our faces, yet on distant worlds the only record often comes from patterns left in sand. Dunes form wherever loose grains are moved by wind or other flows, and their overall shape and orientation have long told researchers which way the wind blows. The new work shifts attention from the dunes themselves to the individual grains that build them. Grains of different sizes respond differently to the same wind; smaller particles lift more easily and travel farther, while larger ones require stronger gusts. By measuring how far and how fast grains of known sizes move across a dune field, researchers can back-calculate the wind speeds that produced those movements. The Stanford analysis used laboratory experiments and numerical models to map grain trajectories under controlled winds, then showed that the same relationships appear in orbital images of dunes on Mars and Titan. Because the method relies only on visible grain motion and dune morphology, it can be applied wherever high-resolution images exist. What remains unknown is how well the calibration holds when atmospheric density, grain composition, or electrostatic forces differ sharply from the tested cases; direct wind measurements from future landers would provide the ground truth needed to refine the speed estimates across the solar system.
