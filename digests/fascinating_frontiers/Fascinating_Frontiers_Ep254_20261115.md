### Top 12 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **Planet's orbit tilts until transit vanishes — Mashable**
   NASA's TESS watched TOI-2535 b, a hot Jupiter 1,000 light-years away, cross its star repeatedly for seven years. The crossings arrived as much as six hours early or late and grew shorter and fainter before stopping entirely earlier this year. The planet's path has slid from the middle of the star toward its edge. A second, unseen world at least as massive as Saturn on a tilted orbit is pulling on it. The two planets will continue to tug on each other, and TOI-2535 b is expected to swing back into view in 30 to 50 years. "The planet's orbit is tilting so quickly that its transit disappeared while we were watching," said Samuel Yee of UCLA. Source: [mashable.com](https://mashable.com/science/disappearing-planet-hidden-world-tilts-orbit)

2. **Euclid finds 31 ancient quasars, two at record redshifts — ScienceDaily Space & Time**
   ESA's Euclid telescope has identified 31 quasars from when the universe was only 670 million years old. Two of them, EUCL J172902.75+641018.1 at redshift 7.77 and EUCL J125308.55+705432.3 at redshift 7.69, set new records. Both objects shone with the brightness of a trillion Suns and lie just over 13 billion light-years away. The finds more than double the number of known quasars with redshift 7 or greater. "This finding more than doubles the number of quasars we know of that are so ancient," said Antonio La Marca of ESA. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261112230504.htm)

3. **Independent method confirms M87 black hole ring — Phys.org Space**
   A new technique called closure invariants was applied to the original Event Horizon Telescope data of the M87 black hole. The method combines triplets of telescope measurements to remove atmospheric and instrumental errors. A machine-learning imaging approach called GenDIReCT, trained only on everyday images, then produced the reconstruction. The ring diameter and orientation match the 2019 result for a 6.5-billion-solar-mass black hole viewed nearly face-on. Fine details of ring width and brightness vary slightly from earlier images. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-iconic-black-hole-image-accurate.html)

4. **Moon dust sintered into interlocking bricks without binder — SciTechDaily**
   University of Arkansas researchers used laser powder bed fusion on Lunar Highlands Regolith Simulant LHS-1. The process heats selected areas of the powder so particles bond directly to one another. Blocks a few centimetres across were printed in cross and H shapes that fit together like LEGO pieces. The resulting material is more porous than binder-added prints. Further tests are needed in vacuum and microgravity before the bricks can support structural loads. Source: [scitechdaily.com](https://scitechdaily.com/researchers-turn-moon-dust-into-lego-like-bricks-that-could-build-future-lunar-bases/)

5. **Candidate sixth dwarf planet found beyond Pluto — ScienceDaily Space & Time**

Confirmation would expand the solar system's official dwarf-planet count to six. The find also suggests more large worlds may remain undetected beyond Neptune. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261112230447.htm)

6. **Long-period radio transient linked to white-dwarf binary — Phys.org Space**
   Since 2022 astronomers have traced several long-period radio transients to binaries containing a white dwarf and a red dwarf. The systems produce bright radio flashes every few minutes to every few hours. The white dwarf's magnetic field and interaction with the companion star are thought to drive the emission. Similar objects continue to be found as surveys improve sensitivity at low frequencies. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-dead-star-radio-minutes-partner.html)

7. **Voyager 1 nears one light-day from Earth — Phys.org Space**
   NASA's Voyager 1 is approaching the milestone of one light-day's distance from Earth while travelling through interstellar space. The spacecraft continues to return data on the heliopause and interstellar medium. No other human-made object has reached this range. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-nasa-voyager-day-interstellar-space.html)

8. **Lensed supernova expected to reappear twice more — Phys.org Space**
   Gravitational lensing by a massive galaxy cluster created multiple images of supernova SN Ares when the universe was 4.9 billion years old. JWST high-resolution imaging captured the distorted views. A paper posted to arXiv on 23 October predicts when the remaining images will arrive, roughly 70 years apart. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-cosmic-magnifying-glass-reveal-supernova.html)

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Gravitational Lensing Creates Multiple Supernova Images
When a massive galaxy cluster lies between Earth and a distant exploding star, its gravity bends the star's light along several curved paths. Each path can produce a separate image that reaches us at different times because the light travels different distances. In the case of SN Ares the universe was 4.9 billion years old when the star exploded, and JWST captured the first distorted views created by the cluster's lensing. The discovery paper posted to arXiv on 23 October calculates that the remaining images will arrive roughly 70 years apart as the light from the other paths finally reaches us. The timing difference arises because the extra distance light travels along the longer lensed routes can stretch to years or decades even though the actual explosion was a single event. Astronomers still need repeated high-resolution imaging over coming decades to confirm the predicted arrival windows and to measure how precisely the cluster's mass distribution matches the models. Those future observations would also test whether small changes in the lens model alter the expected delays.

The universe keeps showing us the same cosmic events from slightly different angles and moments in time.
