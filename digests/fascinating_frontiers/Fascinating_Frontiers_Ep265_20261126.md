### Top 12 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **FCC Approves One Million Starmind Satellites** — Phys.org Space
   The U.S. Federal Communications Commission authorized SpaceX to deploy up to one million orbital compute modules forming the Starmind constellation. The order requires at least one authorized space station by 2033 and full deployment by 2040. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr called the move a historic first in the artificial intelligence race. Scientists including astrophysicist Adam Becker called the plan a fantasy citing basic physical limits on radiating heat in vacuum. The American Astronomical Society said the larger and more numerous objects would undermine decades of investment in astronomical facilities and the ability to study the night sky. The regulator dismissed environmental concerns outside its authority while maintaining the constellation serves the public interest. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-fcc-spacex-deploy-million-satellites.html)

2. **Europa Clipper Swings Past Earth Toward Jupiter** — Phys.org Space
   NASA’s Europa Clipper will fly within 1,950 to 2,133 miles of Earth on December 3 to gain a gravity-assist boost toward Jupiter. The 12,800-pound spacecraft will turn on all nine instruments during the flyby to calibrate them against known Earth data. The magnetometer will use Earth observations to pin its measurements to an absolute reference before reaching Europa. The E-THEMIS thermal imager will scan Earth every eight hours while the ultraviolet spectrograph will study the geocorona alongside Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter observations. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-nasa-europa-clipper-earth-jupiter.html)

3. **Lunar Craters Could Shield Moon-Base Reactors** — Phys.org Space
   Researchers simulated placing a 40-kilowatt nuclear reactor 1.5 meters above the lunar surface on a rover. A three-meter-deep crater or three-meter-high dirt hill reduced radiation to roughly one-tenth of a rem per year. At Connecting Ridge near Shackleton Crater a 120-meter crater wall dropped habitat radiation to four-hundredths of a rem per year. The simulations used Monte Carlo N-Particle software to track millions of particles from the core. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-lunar-craters-free-shields-moon.html)

4. **Hubble Maps Galactic Bulge for Roman Telescope** — Phys.org Space
   Hubble observed the Milky Way’s galactic bulge in red and near-infrared wavelengths that overlap with the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope. The data will help characterize stars before they produce microlensing events that Roman will use to find exoplanets. Hubble’s high resolution records brightness, ages, composition and motion of individual stars in the crowded region. The observations add to legacy surveys including the WFC3 Galactic Bulge Treasury Program and the Sagittarius Window Eclipsing Extrasolar Planet Search. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-nasa-hubble-views-ocean-stars.html)

5. **NASA Tests Strawberries, Cucumbers and Lemon Basil for Space** — NASA Breaking
   Researchers grew strawberries in flight-like chambers and found the Delizz variety outperformed a wild type that produced too many runners. Cucumbers showed steady edible fruit yield under simulated spaceflight conditions despite some leaf enlargement. Lemon basil passed seed sanitization and preliminary growth tests and is now moving to plant-pillow evaluation. Space crops absorb carbon dioxide, produce oxygen and recycle water on long-duration missions. Source: [science.nasa.gov](https://science.nasa.gov/directorates/smd/nasa-eyes-crop-candidates-for-future-menus/)

7. **Landsat Captures Resolution Island Sanctuary** — NASA Breaking
   An Operational Land Imager image from Landsat 9 on December 12, 2025 shows Resolution Island rising to Mount Clerke at 1,069 meters amid Fiordland’s glacier-carved valleys. Thomas Henry Potts proposed the island as a refuge for kiwi and kākāpō in 1872. Caretaker Richard Henry transported more than 700 birds by rowboat after the 1891 reserve designation. A Department of Conservation program begun in 2008 continues stoat control on the island that lies within swimming distance of the mainland. Source: [science.nasa.gov](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/seeking-refuge-on-resolution-island/)

### Cosmic Deep Dive: Density Patterns in Cosmic Web Filaments
Cosmic web filaments are the largest structures in the universe, vast bridges of dark matter and gas that connect galaxy clusters across hundreds of millions of light-years. New research published by the American Astronomical Society refines how dark matter is distributed inside these filaments and points to a previously hidden universal pattern in their density. Astronomers have long mapped the web through galaxy surveys and weak-lensing measurements, yet the precise way mass concentrates along the filaments has remained difficult to pin down because the structures are so diffuse and distant. The latest analysis untangles conflicting signals from earlier studies by comparing density profiles across multiple filament samples and simulation runs. It shows that the central density of filaments follows a consistent scaling relation once projection effects and galaxy bias are carefully removed. The work draws on both observational catalogs and large-scale cosmological simulations to isolate the underlying dark-matter distribution. What remains unknown is whether this scaling holds at the highest redshifts or in the most massive filaments, and whether a single physical mechanism sets the pattern across all environments. A larger sample of filaments mapped at higher resolution with upcoming surveys would settle whether the relation is truly universal or varies with cosmic time and local density. Source: [aasnova.org](https://aasnova.org/2026/11/25/hints-of-a-universal-pattern-in-the-density-of-cosmic-web-filaments/)
