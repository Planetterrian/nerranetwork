### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **ESA Authorises Airbus to Develop Aeolus-2 Mission** — European Space Agency
   The European Space Agency has given Airbus Defence and Space authorisation to proceed with Aeolus-2, the follow-on to the original wind-measuring satellite. The new mission will continue global wind observations from orbit and feed data directly into weather forecast models. It is being developed in partnership with Eumetsat to strengthen both short-term forecasting and long-term climate records. The authorisation moves the project from study into full development. Next, Airbus will begin detailed design and component procurement for the spacecraft.

2. **SpaceX Completes 1,000th Merlin 1D Engine** — Spaceflight Now
   SpaceX teams have finished building and acceptance testing the 1,000th Merlin 1D engine for Falcon first stages. Reusability has allowed the company to recover and inspect engines after flight, revealing ways to improve reliability over successive builds. The milestone reflects steady production gains since the engine family entered service. Each new engine incorporates lessons from prior flights and ground tests. The company continues to fly the same core design on both Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy.

3. **Artemis II Swatch Returns to Air and Space Museum** — NASA
   A small piece of fabric from the Wright Flyer flew aboard Artemis II during its lunar flyby and has now been placed on display at the Smithsonian’s National Air and Space Museum. The swatch joins other mission keepsakes that highlight connections between early aviation and current human spaceflight. NASA has published a full list of the mementos carried by the crew. The return of the artifact marks the completion of the first crewed Artemis flight’s post-mission activities. Future Artemis missions are expected to carry additional historical items.

4. **Hubble Birthday Lookup Tool Gets New Design** — NASA
   The popular Hubble “What did Hubble see on your birthday?” page has been refreshed with a new interface. Users can now select any date and receive five images of objects the telescope was observing that day. The update makes it easier to explore the archive without prior knowledge of specific targets. The tool draws from Hubble’s long-term observation logs stretching back more than three decades. It remains one of NASA’s most visited public astronomy resources.

5. **Blue Origin Advances After New Glenn Setback** — Ars Technica
   One month after the New Glenn launch failure, Blue Origin has resumed hardware testing and recovery operations. NASA Administrator Bill Nelson publicly noted the company’s rapid progress on the next vehicle. Work includes structural inspections and engine testing ahead of a planned return-to-flight attempt. The company is applying lessons from the mishap to both the booster and upper stage. Observers are watching for the next integrated vehicle test.

6. **Final Atlas 5 Carries 29 Amazon Satellites** — Spaceflight Now
   An Atlas 5 551 lifted off from Cape Canaveral at 0430 UTC on 2 July carrying 29 Amazon Leo satellites. The flight marked the last time the 551 configuration will fly with a customer payload. United Launch Alliance completed the mission successfully, ending a long run of Atlas 5 commercial launches. The satellites will join Amazon’s expanding broadband constellation in low Earth orbit. Future Amazon launches will shift to other vehicles.

7. **CubeSat Contracted to Land on Asteroid Apophis** — European Space Agency
   ESA has awarded a contract to Spanish firm EMXYS to build a shoebox-sized CubeSat that will touch down on asteroid Apophis. The spacecraft, named Don Quijote, will be released by the Ramses mission before the asteroid’s close Earth approach in April 2029. It will be the first CubeSat designed to operate on an asteroid surface. The lander will carry instruments to study surface properties during the flyby. Development begins immediately under the new contract.

8. **LSST Begins Full Science Operations** — Phys.org
   The Vera C. Rubin Observatory has started the Legacy Survey of Space and Time, one of the largest astronomical imaging programs ever attempted. More than 80 Japanese researchers are already contributing to data analysis and software development, drawing on experience from the Subaru Telescope. The survey will repeatedly image the southern sky to detect transients, map dark matter, and track solar-system objects. Japanese teams are providing key algorithms for image processing and calibration. Early data releases are expected within the first year of operations.

9. **July Skywatching Highlights Include Comet and Milky Way** — NASA
   NASA’s monthly skywatching guide notes a predawn alignment of the Moon with planets, the return of a comet, and excellent Milky Way viewing conditions. Saturn’s rings are also presented at a new viewing angle this month. Observers in the northern hemisphere can look for the comet in the early morning hours. The Milky Way stretches high across the southern sky after midnight. The guide includes specific rise and set times for each featured object.

10. **Jupiter-Sized Super-Puff Planets Found** — EWN
    Astronomers have identified giant planets whose average density is lower than cotton candy despite radii similar to Jupiter. The worlds orbit stars at distances that allow detailed atmospheric measurements with current telescopes. Their extremely low densities suggest large, extended atmospheres with very little solid material at the core. Follow-up observations are planned to determine how such tenuous structures remain stable. The discoveries add new test cases for models of giant-planet formation.

11. **Cosmic Dust May Heat Solar Corona** — Phys.org
    A University of Alabama in Huntsville study proposes that charged dust grains near the Sun help transport energy into the corona. The research, published in The Astrophysical Journal, models how these grains interact with the surrounding plasma. If confirmed, the mechanism would help explain why the corona reaches millions of degrees while the surface remains far cooler. The team used spacecraft measurements of dust populations to constrain the model. Additional observations from upcoming solar missions are expected to test the idea.

12. **Chandra Maps Outer Milky Way Arms** — NASA
    New Chandra X-ray Observatory data indicate the Milky Way’s outer spiral arms may extend farther than earlier maps suggested. Astronomers measured precise distances to dust clouds that absorb X-rays, revealing arm segments previously thought to be closer. The finding could revise models of the galaxy’s overall structure and mass distribution. The observations cover regions several thousand light-years from the Sun. Further Chandra pointings are scheduled to trace additional arm segments.

13. **Webb Examines Planet Around White Dwarf** — Reddit r/space
    JWST observations have captured a planet that survived the red-giant phase of its host star. The data show the planet’s atmosphere remains intact despite the intense radiation and mass loss that occurred during the star’s death. Researchers are using the spectrum to measure changes in atmospheric composition caused by the stellar evolution. The system offers a rare look at post-main-sequence planetary survival. Additional JWST visits are planned to monitor seasonal or orbital variations.

14. **Report Examines Starliner Development Issues** — SpaceNews
    An independent review links Boeing’s Starliner technical problems to overly optimistic schedules and insufficient NASA oversight during development. The report details how early confidence in the vehicle’s design reduced the margin for addressing integration challenges. NASA has already begun implementing several of the review’s recommendations. The findings cover the period from contract award through the most recent uncrewed test flight. A follow-up assessment is expected later this year.

15. **Deaf Engineers Shaped Early NASA Human Spaceflight** — Reddit r/space
    A new historical account details how eleven deaf men contributed to NASA’s human spaceflight program in the 1960s. Their work included precision manufacturing and testing of components for Mercury and Gemini spacecraft. The article draws on oral histories and NASA archives that had not been widely published before. Their contributions helped establish quality-control standards still used today. The story highlights an under-recognized chapter in aerospace workforce history.

### Cosmic Spotlight
The discovery of multiple Jupiter-sized planets with densities lower than cotton candy forces a re-examination of how giant planets assemble. These “super-puff” worlds must maintain enormous, extended atmospheres with almost no rocky or icy core to provide gravity. Current formation models struggle to explain how such diffuse envelopes avoid collapsing or being stripped away during migration. JWST transmission spectra of the atmospheres could reveal whether the gas is primordial or replenished. How do these structures remain stable over billions of years?

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Charged Dust Grains Could Heat the Solar Corona
If the Sun were scaled to the size of a basketball sitting on a Toronto street, the corona would extend outward like an invisible, million-degree halo reaching past the CN Tower and into the suburbs. Charged dust grains drifting through this region pick up energy from the turbulent magnetic field and then collide with ions and electrons, transferring that heat outward. Spacecraft have measured dust densities near the Sun at roughly one grain per cubic kilometre, yet the cumulative effect over millions of kilometres is enough to help raise temperatures from 6,000 K at the surface to over 1 million K in the corona. The process happens on timescales of hours to days as grains spiral inward on Poynting-Robertson drag before sublimating. We can map the dust and measure the resulting X-ray emission, but nobody yet knows whether the grains dominate the heating or merely supplement waves and reconnection events that scientists have tracked for decades.
