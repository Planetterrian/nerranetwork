### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **Mars passes within 5 degrees of the Pleiades cluster** — Astronomy Magazine
   The Red Planet currently sits in Taurus and appears in the predawn sky. Today it lies less than 5° from the famous Pleiades star cluster (M45). Observers who rise early can spot the pairing low in the east before sunrise washes it out. The geometry places Mars on a line of sight that brings it unusually near the cluster’s bright stars from our viewpoint. Watch for the pair to separate gradually over the next several mornings as Mars continues its eastward motion.

2. **Rocket reentry events leave measurable metal traces in the upper atmosphere** — r/space
   Researchers have linked specific reentry events to sudden spikes in atmospheric metal concentrations. The data come from ground-based sampling that captured elevated levels of elements commonly used in spacecraft construction. These measurements show how material from returning stages disperses at altitudes where it can interact with the stratosphere. Follow-up studies aim to quantify how frequently such injections occur as launch rates rise.

3. **New true-scale mode reveals how empty low Earth orbit actually is** — r/space
   A satellite-tracking tool now includes an option that removes artificial magnification so objects appear at their real sizes. Users can select the ISS and watch the view zoom out until the station shrinks to a single pixel against the globe. The update incorporates accurate 3D models for the ISS, Hubble, Tiangong, and thousands of Starlink satellites. Real-time propagation still runs on CelesTrak data, but the visual now matches the physical spacing of objects in orbit.

4. **NOVA documentary examines plans for a sustained return to the Moon** — r/space
   Season 53, Episode 7 of the long-running science series focuses on current lunar exploration architecture. The program covers lander development, surface operations, and the role of commercial partners in establishing a presence. Interviews highlight engineering challenges that must be solved before crews can stay for extended periods.

5. **A sizable asteroid is set to pass Earth this weekend** — r/space
   Observers are tracking an object large enough to be monitored by both professional and amateur telescopes. The flyby offers a chance to refine orbital predictions and test detection networks. Details and viewing guidance appear in regular space-weather updates.

6. **Two companies advance designs for faster-deploying lunar terrain vehicles** — Spaceflight Now
   NASA selected providers to build iterative versions of earlier lunar rover concepts. The goal is to deliver operational rovers ahead of the Artemis 4 crew arrival so astronauts have mobility from the start of surface operations. The contracts emphasize reduced development time and lower costs compared with previous vehicle programs.

7. **Loudest gravitational-wave signal yet helps probe black-hole event horizons** — Space.com
   The detection supplies new data on the boundary beyond which nothing escapes a black hole’s gravity. Analysts examined the waveform’s fine structure to extract information about spacetime geometry right at the horizon. Continued observations of similar loud events could tighten constraints on how matter and energy behave in these extreme regions.

8. **Artemis II crew nominated for Congressional Gold Medal** — collectSPACE.com
   Lawmakers have introduced legislation to honor the four astronauts selected for the first crewed flight of the Orion spacecraft. The medal would recognize the mission’s role in returning humans to the vicinity of the Moon. If approved, it would join a short list of spaceflight achievements recognized at this level.

9. **BalNeO project will fly a balloon network to sample stratospheric aerosols** — NASA
   The initiative aims to gather regular, in-situ measurements of particles that influence global temperatures and the water cycle. Multiple balloon platforms will operate at different longitudes to capture variability that satellites see only intermittently. Data will feed into climate models that currently rely heavily on remote-sensing averages.

10. **NASA tests a new cryogenic fluid coupling for future in-space refueling** — NASA
    Engineers evaluated a prototype nozzle and receiver system designed to transfer propellants between spacecraft. The hardware must maintain seals at extremely low temperatures while allowing rapid connection and disconnection. Successful tests move the technology closer to demonstrations that could support longer deep-space missions.

11. **Hubble images an ancient globular cluster showing two distinct star-formation episodes** — phys.org
    The cluster contains tens of thousands to millions of stars packed under mutual gravity. Observations reveal one population of stars that formed early and a second group that appeared later, suggesting the cluster experienced separate bursts of star birth. More than 150 such clusters are known in the Milky Way, though others may remain hidden behind dust.

12. **University teams win NASA’s 2026 Human Lander Challenge for life-support concepts** — NASA
    Student groups presented environmental-control and life-support designs intended for future crewed lunar landers. The winning entries addressed air revitalization, water recovery, and thermal management under lunar gravity conditions. The competition feeds directly into technology development supporting the Artemis program.

13. **May 2024 geomagnetic superstorm pulled most ring-current ions from Earth’s own atmosphere** — phys.org
    Measurements showed that the intense event drew ions primarily from the planet rather than the solar wind. The finding revises earlier assumptions about how ring-current populations are replenished during major storms. Continued monitoring will test whether this source dominance holds for future events.

14. **Pegasus rocket prepared to launch orbit-raising spacecraft for Swift observatory** — phys.org
    The mission targets a no-earlier-than June 30 liftoff from Kwajalein Atoll. The small upper-stage vehicle will dock with the aging Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory and gradually increase its altitude over several months. The effort marks the first attempt to extend an operational astrophysics mission through in-space propulsion assistance.

15. **Stoke Space launch site had already reached an advanced stage of construction last year** — Ars Technica
    Site visits more than six months ago showed substantial progress on infrastructure needed for full rocket reuse. The company continues work on a vehicle designed for rapid turnaround between flights. Observers are watching for the next visible milestones in testing and assembly.

### Cosmic Spotlight
The true-scale satellite tracker makes the emptiness of low Earth orbit visible in a way conventional maps hide. When magnification drops away, even the crowded shells of Starlink satellites become widely separated points against the planet’s curve. The same view shows why collision warnings remain rare despite thousands of objects: physical spacing, not software, does most of the work. One open question is how future mega-constellations will alter that spacing once every orbital shell fills to higher densities.

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Reentering Hardware Seeds the Upper Atmosphere
If every reentering rocket stage were compressed into a single layer, the metal it releases would form a film only a few atoms thick spread across the entire stratosphere. Yet that tiny addition occurs at altitudes where chemistry proceeds slowly and where natural sources of those same elements are scarce. A single large stage can inject kilograms of aluminum, titanium, and iron compounds directly into the mesosphere during its fiery passage. Those atoms then drift downward over weeks or months, mixing into the aerosol layer that influences both ozone chemistry and the planet’s radiation balance. No one yet knows whether the cumulative effect of rising launch rates will eventually rival volcanic or meteoritic contributions on a global scale.
