> **A star long used to calibrate the James Webb Space Telescope is actually a binary system whose companion alters its measured flux.**

1. **Hidden binary throws off JWST calibration fluxes** — Universe Today
   Researchers at the Xinjiang Astronomical Observatory identified HD 158485 as a binary while reviewing Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite observations. The two stars orbit every 382.8 days with the companion roughly 80 percent the mass of the primary. A two-star atmosphere model showed flux differences of 1.6 percent between one and five micrometers and 2.5 percent between five and 28 micrometers compared with a single-star model. The discovery was confirmed by an independent orbital solution built from earlier TESS data. The team published the first orbital solution and dynamical mass ratio in The Astrophysical Journal Letters. Source: [universetoday.com](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/astronomers-discover-a-hidden-binary-in-a-star-system-used-to-calibrate-the-jwst)

2. **New model tracks chemistry on uneven interstellar ice grains** — Phys.org Space
   Astrophysicists at the University of Liège built the DESTINY model using Markov chains with absorbing states to follow molecules on amorphous ice surfaces. The model assigns binding energies to individual surface sites instead of a single average value and accounts for encounters that change binding strength without a reaction. Methane population trends already differ from earlier single-energy models. The approach was detailed in Astronomy & Astrophysics. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-destiny-tracks-molecular-chemistry-uneven.html)

3. **Runaway pulsar found outside its supernova remnant shell** — Phys.org Space
   Astronomers combining radio survey data discovered a pulsar whose tail points directly back to the center of a newly identified supernova remnant shell. Timing and dispersion measures yielded a transverse velocity above 1,400 kilometers per second. The shell and tail were first seen in MeerKAT images. The result has been accepted for publication in Astronomy & Astrophysics. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-runaway-pulsar-remnant-supernova.html)

4. **JWST confirms one of the most metal-poor galaxies known** — Phys.org Space
   JWST NIRSpec integral-field spectroscopy of the strongly lensed galaxy LAP2 detected no oxygen emission across the full system. The integrated spectrum places the oxygen abundance below 0.5 percent of the solar value. The galaxy’s stellar mass is approximately 80,000 solar masses and its stars formed in a dust-free burst about 7 million years ago. The findings appear on the arXiv preprint server. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-jwst-tiny-metal-poor-galaxy.html)

5. **Titanium oxide vapor detected around a young massive star** — Universe Today
   Astronomers recorded the first gas-phase titanium oxide signature around a forming high-mass star. The detection captures conditions before planets or asteroids have assembled. The observation provides a direct window into the earliest chemistry of a solar-system analogue. Source: [universetoday.com](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/detection-of-vaporized-rock-reveals-the-birth-of-a-solar-system)

The data constrain both the basin’s formation age and the timing of later craters across the solar system. The study was published in Science Advances. The magnetometer team in particular will use Earth’s magnetosphere to anchor measurements needed later at Europa. ### Cosmic Spotlight
Astronomers using long-term Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite observations found that a star previously treated as single for James Webb Space Telescope calibration is in fact a binary whose orbital motion produces measurable light-travel-time variations between different pulsation modes. The companion orbits every 382.8 days and is about 80 percent the mass of the primary, creating flux offsets of 1.6 percent at shorter infrared wavelengths and 2.5 percent at longer ones. How might other calibration stars be affected once similar checks become routine? ### Cosmic Deep Dive: How binding-energy variations shape chemistry on interstellar dust
Interstellar dust grains are coated in amorphous ice whose surface offers binding sites of different strengths rather than one uniform value. Molecules arriving from the gas phase can land on a sticky site or a slippery one, and a simple encounter with another molecule can change how tightly either is held without any chemical reaction occurring. The scale of the effect appears in laboratory and modeling work that tracks populations across many sites instead of averaging them.

The DESTINY model developed at the University of Liège follows each adsorbed species with Markov chains that include four possible outcomes at every step: staying in place, diffusing to a new site, reacting, or desorbing. By classifying sites according to measured binding energies and letting encounters alter those energies, the model produces methane surface populations that already diverge from earlier single-energy calculations. The same framework reproduces the observed competition between adsorption, diffusion, and desorption on realistic disordered ice.

What remains unknown is how large the cumulative effect becomes once many species and many grain types are included together. Direct comparison of the model output with new James Webb Space Telescope spectra of ice features in dense clouds would test whether the site-by-site treatment is required or whether simpler averages still suffice at the precision now achievable. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-11-destiny-tracks-molecular-chemistry-uneven.html)
