**Lunar fault maps reveal how much the moon is shrinking — Phys.org Space**
A new study of young tectonic landforms on the moon offers the most detailed estimate yet of how much the moon has contracted as its interior gradually cooled over time. The work focuses on recent fault activity rather than older, degraded features. Researchers mapped scarps and other structures formed as the moon lost heat and its crust adjusted. This approach yields tighter bounds on total radial contraction than earlier global surveys. The findings refine models of the moon’s thermal evolution and interior structure. High-resolution orbital images allowed separation of fresh scarps from ancient terrain. Latitude comparisons helped establish the timing of the most recent shortening episodes. Additional crater counting on the scarps themselves supplies relative ages that place the youngest faults in geologically recent epochs. The data show that contraction has persisted at a modest rate into the present era rather than halting after the moon’s early history. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-lunar-fault-reveal-moon.html)

**Scientists detect rare type of wood alcohol in space — Universe Today**
An international research team has detected a rare form of methanol in space for the first time as part of the ALMA COMPASS Large program. The detection adds a new data point to inventories of complex organic molecules in star-forming regions. ALMA’s high-resolution spectra allowed the team to distinguish this isotopic variant from more common methanol. The result expands the known chemical inventory available for planet-forming disks. Observations targeted regions where new stars and planets are assembling. The rare variant provides a fresh tracer for temperature and density conditions during molecule formation. Multiple spectral lines confirmed the identification and ruled out contamination from terrestrial sources. The finding demonstrates that even uncommon molecular species can be tracked across vast distances with current radio arrays. Source: [universetoday.com](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/scientists-detect-rare-type-of-wood-alcohol-in-space)

**Rigorous analysis confirms NASA’s Artemis II heat shield performance — Phys.org Space**
NASA’s Artemis II mission sent four astronauts around the moon and brought them safely back to Earth. Post-flight analysis of the heat shield data has now been completed and confirms expected performance. The review examined temperature, ablation, and structural integrity throughout re-entry. Results support continued use of the same shield design on future crewed lunar flights. Engineers compared recorded temperatures against pre-flight predictions. Structural integrity checks showed no unexpected degradation after the high-speed return. The data set includes measurements from multiple sensors embedded in the shield material. These readings matched model outputs within acceptable margins across the entire entry profile. Source: [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-10-rigorous-analysis-nasa-artemis-ii.html)

**Astronomers discover the 1st ever green star, a cosmic zombie just in time for Halloween — Space.com**
Scientists have discovered that our star may go green if it turns into a cosmic zombie after its death in billions of years. The finding comes from models of white-dwarf cooling and atmospheric chemistry. A green hue would appear under specific temperature and composition conditions not previously modeled in detail. The work shows how stellar remnants can produce unexpected colors during late evolutionary stages. The green phase would occur only after the white dwarf has cooled for an extended period. Earlier models had not accounted for the precise molecular interactions that could favor green wavelengths. Refined calculations now indicate the color arises when certain atomic transitions dominate the visible spectrum for a narrow temperature window. The stage is expected to be brief compared with the white dwarf’s overall cooling timeline. Source: [space.com](https://www.space.com/astronomy/stars/astronomers-discover-the-1st-ever-green-star-a-cosmic-zombie-just-in-time-for-halloween)

**Host stars shape their planetary systems into two families — Universe Today**
New research draws a link between a host star and the characteristics of its planetary system. Higher mass stars create one family of systems, while lower mass stars create another. The study used observed orbital architectures and stellar properties to separate the populations. These results provide a new, more detailed template for understanding exoplanet systems. The division appears tied to the mass of the central star at formation. Systems around more massive stars show different spacing and size patterns than those around lower-mass hosts. The separation emerges from statistical analysis of thousands of confirmed multi-planet systems. Orbital period ratios and planet radius distributions form the primary discriminants between the two groups. Source: [universetoday.com](https://www.universetoday.com/articles/host-stars-shape-their-planetary-systems-into-two-families)

**Chandra finds possible origin of ‘Treble Clef’ waves — NASA Image of the Day**
Astronomers have used NASA’s Chandra X-ray Observatory to break down the cause of a remarkable formation of radio waves they have nicknamed the “Treble Clef” because its distinct shape strongly resembles the musical symbol. The X-ray data reveal the underlying cluster collision dynamics that produce the radio structure. The collision between galaxy clusters generates shocks and turbulence visible in X-rays. These processes shape the radio emission into the distinctive clef-like pattern. The radio feature spans tens of millions of light-years across the merged cluster. Chandra’s imaging resolves temperature variations that trace the shock fronts responsible for accelerating particles. The match between X-ray morphology and radio contours supports the collision-driven origin model. Source: [nasa.gov](https://www.nasa.gov/image-detail/shape-3601-3601/)

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Young Faults Record a Moon’s Slow Contraction
Picture a body the size of a modest continent that has been shedding internal heat for more than four billion years, its outer shell slowly wrinkling under the strain of that cooling. On the moon those wrinkles appear as networks of young fault scarps only a few meters high, each one marking a moment when the crust shortened and buckled. Because these scarps remain sharp-edged against the older, impact-gardened surface, they stand out clearly in orbital images and let scientists isolate the most recent increments of planetary shrinkage.

High-resolution topography from orbiting spacecraft captures the scarps at meter scales. By counting the small craters that have accumulated on the scarps themselves and comparing their freshness across different latitudes, researchers build a timeline of contraction. Younger faults display crisp slopes and minimal erosion, while older ones have been softened by later bombardment. This relative dating turns the lunar surface into a geological clock that records how the rate of radial shortening has changed through time. The method sidesteps the blurring effect of ancient, heavily cratered terrain and focuses instead on structures formed after the moon’s early heavy bombardment ended.

The measurements indicate that contraction has continued into geologically recent epochs, although the total amount of shortening remains modest compared with the moon’s overall radius. What remains unknown is whether this cooling has proceeded at a steady pace or whether brief episodes of faster heat loss occurred when large impacts or shifts in mantle convection released stored energy. Future missions carrying even finer-resolution topographic instruments could reveal whether the youngest scarps formed in clusters separated by long quiet intervals, revealing whether the moon’s interior cooled smoothly or in pulses.
