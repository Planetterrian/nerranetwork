### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **Falcon 9 Sends 24 Starlink Satellites to Orbit** — Spaceflight Now
   A Falcon 9 lifted off from Vandenberg Space Force Base at 1635 UTC on August 8 carrying 24 Starlink satellites. The mission marked another step in the ongoing west-coast launch cadence for the constellation. Deployment of the satellites was confirmed shortly after separation from the second stage. The flight used a previously flown booster that returned to a droneship in the Pacific. Liftoff occurred at 9:35 a.m. PDT, which converts to 12:35 p.m. EDT. The launch is designated Starlink Group 17-38.

2. **Self-Driving Mars Rover Breaks Distance Records** — Ars Technica
   NASA’s newest Mars rover employs autonomous driving software that lets it cover more ground each day without constant commands from Earth. The system has already allowed the vehicle to surpass every prior rover’s total distance traveled. Onboard navigation uses cameras and hazard-detection algorithms to plot safe paths across rough terrain. Engineers report the rover continues to operate well beyond its original design goals. Remember, we covered robotic exploration yesterday — today’s news moves that forward because the autonomy is now delivering record distances.

3. **Sun’s Magnetic Poles Set to Reverse This Year** — r/space
   The Sun undergoes a full magnetic pole reversal roughly every 11 years as part of its natural cycle. The upcoming flip will affect the structure of the heliosphere but is not expected to disrupt life on Earth. Strong solar activity during the reversal can still produce intense geomagnetic storms capable of damaging satellites and power grids. Observers note that widespread power outages could briefly reduce light pollution and reveal brighter auroras. The inversion occurs as part of the regular 11-year solar cycle.

4. **Andromeda Galaxy Captured in New Image** — r/space
   The Andromeda Galaxy lies 2.5 million light-years from Earth and is the nearest large spiral galaxy to the Milky Way. A recent photograph highlights its bright central bulge and sweeping spiral arms. The image was taken with standard camera equipment under dark skies. Andromeda remains a key target for studying how galaxies similar to our own evolve over time. The photo was submitted by user Resolute_Protector6 and credited to Arthur Debons via Unsplash.

6. **Webb Telescope Detects Hidden Exoplanet Beta Pictoris d** — curiosmos.com
   The James Webb Space Telescope has identified a previously unseen planet orbiting the young star Beta Pictoris. The world appears in infrared data that penetrates the dusty debris disk surrounding the star. Its presence helps astronomers map the architecture of this nearby planetary system. Follow-up observations are planned to refine the planet’s orbit and mass. The discovery was reported through the curiosmos.com news feed.

7. **Intel Proposes Orbital Data Centers for Satellite Networks** — r/space
   A concept from Intel envisions placing powerful computing nodes in higher orbits to manage thousands of simple low-Earth-orbit satellites. The two-tier architecture would move data processing away from the crowded lower constellation layers. Proponents argue the approach could reduce latency and power demands on individual satellites. The idea remains in early study phase. The proposal was submitted by user esporx on r/space.

8. **Brown Dwarf Hosts 0.9-Jupiter-Mass Companion** — r/space
   Astronomers report a planetary-mass object of roughly 0.9 Jupiter masses circling a brown dwarf that itself contains 29 Jupiter masses. No consensus yet exists on whether the companion should be classified as a planet or a moon. The detection adds to the small sample of substellar systems with known companions. Further spectroscopy may clarify formation pathways. The abstract of the paper appeared in Nature and was discussed by user CurtisLeow.

11. **Reduced Gravity Alters Human Movement Patterns** — Phys.org Space

12. **Hipparcos Satellite Launched 37 Years Ago** — Astronomy
    ESA’s Hipparcos mission lifted off on August 8, 1989, to perform high-precision astrometry. A rocket-engine failure left the satellite in the wrong orbit, yet operators still extracted valuable star-position data. The mission pioneered space-based measurement of stellar distances and motions. Its catalog remains a foundational reference for modern astronomy. The article continues on the Astronomy Magazine website.

### Cosmic Deep Dive: The Sun’s 11-Year Magnetic Flip
The Sun’s magnetic field reverses direction every 11 years, a cycle whose scale is hard to picture until you map it onto something familiar. Picture a star whose surface alone spans 1.4 million kilometres flipping its entire magnetic layout the way a handheld compass needle swings, except this star holds 330,000 times Earth’s mass and its field lines extend past Neptune. During the reversal the Sun’s north and south magnetic poles trade places, driven by the twisting motion of plasma deep inside the star where differential rotation winds up the field until it becomes unstable and snaps into the opposite orientation. One concrete number captures the stakes: the reversal coincides with solar maximum, when the number of sunspots can climb above 150 and the solar wind carries roughly twice the magnetic flux it does at minimum. Magnetographs track the poles migrating across the surface, and forecasters can time the flip to within months, yet no model fully explains why the cycle length stays so steady while the strength of each reversal varies.
