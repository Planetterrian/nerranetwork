### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **Comet Tempel 2 Brightens in Aquila** — Astronomy Magazine
   Comet 10P/Tempel 2 is currently glowing at roughly magnitude 15 while moving through the constellation Aquila. Observers with larger telescopes can track it as it rises later in the night and continues to brighten.

2. **Rescue Spacecraft Clears Final Tests for Aging Satellites** — Space Daily
   A commercial vehicle has passed its last ground trials ahead of an unprecedented NASA mission to capture and relocate defunct satellites. Success would establish a repeatable method for managing crowded and hazardous orbital zones.

3. **NASA Launches New Recruitment Drive for Engineers** — Space Coast Daily
   The agency and the Office of Personnel Management have begun “NASA Force,” a targeted campaign to attract technologists and engineers. The effort focuses on filling critical roles that support ongoing exploration programs.

4. **Western University Team Traces Cosmic Buckyballs to Their Source** — Universe Today
   Fifteen years after the first detection of buckyball molecules in space, new James Webb Space Telescope data have pinpointed their formation sites. The observations supply both images and spectra that reveal how these carbon structures arise in stellar environments.

5. **MDA Space Advances Robotic Arm for Lunar Gateway** — SpaceNews
   Engineers continue hardware development on the Canadarm3-style arm while the Canadian Space Agency reviews the project’s next phase. The arm is intended to perform maintenance and logistics tasks on the planned lunar outpost.

6. **New Circumbinary Planet Candidates Found Around Twin Stars** — Eurasia Review
   Researchers have identified several planet candidates that appear to orbit pairs of stars, reminiscent of Tatooine from Star Wars. The detections rely on repeated dimming patterns recorded in the combined starlight.

7. **Saturn’s Rings Likely Came From a Destroyed Moon** — Universe Today
   Fresh modeling indicates the planet’s prominent icy rings formed after a lost moon called Chrysalis was torn apart roughly 100 million years ago. The study links the rings’ current mass and composition to debris from that single catastrophic event.

8. **Early Galaxy Shows No Sign of Rotation** — PHYS.Org
   Astronomers report a surprisingly non-rotating galaxy observed in the early universe, contradicting standard formation models. The finding, published in Nature Astronomy, suggests some primordial systems assembled through different dynamical paths.

### Cosmic Spotlight
Astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope have located the birthplaces of buckyball molecules in space. These soccer-ball-shaped carbon cages were first spotted fifteen years ago, yet only now do we have the spatial resolution and spectral detail to watch them form around aging stars. The data show the molecules condensing in dense, carbon-rich shells where temperatures and radiation levels allow complex chemistry to proceed. This discovery tightens the link between stellar evolution and the organic building blocks that later seed planets. What remains unclear is whether these same environments also produce the larger carbon compounds that could contribute to prebiotic chemistry elsewhere in the galaxy.

### Cosmic Deep Dive: How Early Galaxies Can Assemble Without Spin
If you could shrink the observable universe to the size of a football field, the first galaxies would have appeared as faint specks within the first few metres from the goal line. Those early systems condensed from turbulent gas clouds whose random motions never settled into the orderly rotation we see in today’s spirals. Instead of a spinning disk, material fell inward along chaotic filaments, building dense cores through repeated mergers and inflows. One surprising number stands out: some of these galaxies already held stellar masses equivalent to the Milky Way’s bulge when the universe was less than 600 million years old. We can map their light profiles and velocity fields with current telescopes, yet no one has explained why certain pockets of gas avoided acquiring net angular momentum while others did not. The puzzle is whether these non-rotating objects later gained spin through later mergers or whether they represent a genuinely separate formation channel that simply faded from view.
