The Cosmic Deep Dive examines how black holes slowly evaporate.

If the Sun were compressed into a black hole, its event horizon would span only six kilometres yet contain the mass of two octillion tonnes.

Virtual particle pairs constantly pop into existence near that horizon; one falls inward while the other escapes as Hawking radiation, carrying away energy.

Over time the black hole loses mass and its temperature rises, accelerating the process until the final stages release a burst of particles.

A solar-mass black hole would require roughly 10 to the 67 years to vanish completely, far longer than the current age of the universe.

The radiation spectrum follows a perfect blackbody curve whose peak wavelength shrinks as the hole shrinks.
