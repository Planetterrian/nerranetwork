A meteorite recovered from the Sahara desert contains minerals that form only in the presence of liquid water.

Universe Today reports that the rock's chemistry matches surface conditions that persisted for extended periods early in Martian history.

Isotopic measurements indicate the water was not a brief, localized event.

Ancient Martian meteorites arrive on Earth after impacts launch them from the Martian surface into space.

The Sahara specimen adds weight to models showing widespread liquid water rather than isolated pockets during the planet's first billion years.

The find also supports habitable environments beyond the sites already visited by rovers.

Space-grown glioblastoma organoids more closely resemble human tumors than identical cultures grown on Earth.

A report on Reddit describes three-dimensional structures formed in microgravity that preserve cell-to-cell interactions Earth-based cultures often lose, building on prior orbital biology experiments that showed similar advantages for other tissue types.

Microgravity removes sedimentation and convection that distort cell layering on the ground, and it reduces shear forces that can damage delicate cell junctions during handling.

That preservation lets the organoids maintain realistic gene-expression patterns and native architecture for weeks, longer than standard lab dishes allow.

Growing the models aboard the International Space Station lets investigators observe how glioblastoma cells interact with surrounding stroma over extended periods that Earth cultures cannot sustain.

Researchers expect the models to speed drug screening, reduce false leads, and filter out compounds that only work in simplified Earth-based assays, so results translate more reliably to clinical settings.

Follow-on flights are planned to expose the same models to candidate therapies while still in orbit.

Students at Queen's University Belfast led an international team that identified new solar-system bodies sharing Neptune's orbit.

Universe Today says the objects occupy stable Lagrange points that have remained sparsely sampled until now.

The discoveries help map the distribution of material left over from the giant planets' migration.

Orbital inclinations and sizes provide fresh constraints on the timing of Neptune's outward movement during the solar system's early dynamical rearrangement.

Precise tracking of the objects' paths will refine models of how the outer solar system settled into its current configuration.

Follow-up observations are already scheduled with ground-based telescopes.

India placed the NVS-zero three navigation satellite into orbit today atop a GSLV rocket.

It is the fourth orbital launch from Indian soil this year and strengthens the regional NavIC constellation.

The atomic clocks offer higher stability than previous NavIC spacecraft, reducing timing errors over extended periods.

The inter-satellite link allows data exchange between constellation members without constant ground-station contact, increasing operational resilience in remote regions.

The satellite will improve positioning accuracy for civilian and military users across South Asia.

The National Aeronautics and Space Administration, or NASA, issued a Request for Information on October twenty-ninth seeking industry ideas for boosting Hubble's orbit or performing other servicing tasks.

The agency wants to extend the telescope's science lifetime before its predicted re-entry in the twenty thirty-three to twenty thirty-four timeframe.

A mission would need to occur by twenty thirty-two to raise the orbit meaningfully.

Hubble lacks onboard propulsion, so any orbit-raising maneuver must come from an external servicer.

The request covers three options: an altitude boost, a capability enhancement, or a controlled re-entry.

Responses are due by November thirtieth.

A millimeter-scale glass chip now combines light from two separate telescopes into a single higher-resolution instrument.

Researchers at Heriot-Watt University developed the photonic circuit, Phys dot org reports, performing beam combination on a scale of millimeters rather than the meters required by traditional optical interferometers.

Early tests produced images with angular resolution previously achievable only by much larger arrays.

The photonic circuit maintains phase stability across temperature fluctuations that would destabilize bulk-optics systems.

Integration with existing telescope mounts requires only minor mechanical adapters, lowering the barrier for smaller research institutions.

The approach could let modest observatories reach the detail of facilities many times their size.

Fermi observations from March twenty twenty-six initially recorded an unusually faint gamma-ray burst.

Re-analysis reported by Universe Today showed the event was viewed from the side, so only the dimmer edges of the jet reached Earth.

The true energy release matched roughly one billion years of the Sun's output concentrated into a narrow beam.

Side-on geometry reduces observed flux by orders of magnitude compared with on-axis detections.

The delayed afterglow peaks arise from the jet's lateral expansion becoming visible only after the core has decelerated.

The finding explains why some bursts appear faint and demonstrates the importance of viewing angle in interpreting high-energy transients.

New calculations of cometary delivery of prebiotic molecules find the process would have been at best a one-off event.

Cambridge researchers, in a report on Reddit, showed that a steady rain of organic material from comets is unlikely under realistic early-solar-system conditions.

The models incorporate updated impact velocities and cometary compositions derived from recent spacecraft missions.

Delivery rates drop sharply once the solar system's dynamical clearing phase ends, limiting cumulative organic input to negligible levels.

The work challenges a long-standing hypothesis that cometary impacts supplied Earth's first building blocks for life.

Alternative sources such as atmospheric chemistry or hydrothermal vents now appear more plausible.

Developer rachzera released an open-source NPM package called Larawag that lets users build and visualize hypothetical star systems in three dimensions using three.js.

In brief, radio telescopes may soon help trace the origins of high-energy neutrinos that pass through Earth unnoticed.

E S A leadership is exploring joint lunar projects with the United Arab Emirates.

A single neutrino can cross the entire diameter of Earth in a fraction of a second while interacting with almost nothing, yet the same particle can carry information from the edge of the observable universe that no other messenger survives to deliver.

When a high-energy neutrino strikes an ice molecule in Antarctica or a water molecule in the deep ocean, it generates a cascade of secondary particles that emit a brief radio pulse traveling outward at the speed of light.

Large antenna arrays listen for that pulse across thousands of cubic kilometers, turning the planet itself into a detector whose effective volume far exceeds any optical telescope ever built.

One measurement already in hand shows that the highest-energy neutrinos arrive from directions that do not line up with known gamma-ray sources, suggesting either undiscovered accelerators or entirely new particle-physics processes operating at extreme energies.

The remaining puzzle is why these arrival directions look almost uniform across the sky even though most candidate sources—blazars, gamma-ray bursts, or star-forming galaxies—should cluster along the plane of the Milky Way.

Until larger radio arrays come online and deliver statistically robust sky maps, astronomers cannot decide whether the neutrinos are produced uniformly throughout cosmic time or whether current instruments are simply tuned to the wrong wavelengths.

The radio technique also sidesteps the optical opacity that hides the universe's earliest epochs, letting scientists peer back to times when the first stars and galaxies were still forming.

Each detected pulse carries directional information accurate to a few degrees, enough to begin cross-matching with catalogs from gamma-ray and X-ray observatories.

The next generation of arrays will increase sensitivity by another order of magnitude, pushing the detectable energy threshold downward and revealing whether the current isotropic pattern persists at lower energies or finally reveals clustering that matches known source populations.

Until then, every new radio detection adds one more data point to a map that remains mostly blank.

Ground-based telescopes are already booked to track the new Neptune Trojans, and those orbits will tighten the timing of the planet's outward migration.
