### Top 15 Space & Astronomy Stories
1. **Moon Guides Early Risers to Mars** — Astronomy Magazine
   The crescent Moon sits just 7° from Mars in the predawn eastern sky. This pairing offers a simple visual marker for locating the Red Planet before sunrise.

2. **Halo Appears Around Eclipsed Sun in Orion Images** — r/space
   An unexpected ring of light encircles the Moon during a solar eclipse captured from the Orion spacecraft. The feature remains visible even where the Moon blocks direct sunlight, ruling out simple camera or atmospheric effects.

3. **Elephant’s Trunk Nebula Captured in Deep Exposure** — r/astronomy
   A new amateur image reveals intricate dark lanes and bright emission within the Elephant’s Trunk Nebula after 400 minutes of integration. The result demonstrates how compact smart telescopes can now reach complex deep-sky targets from suburban skies.

4. **Perseverance Captures Selfie at Lac de Charmes** — r/astronomy
   The rover assembled 61 frames into a self-portrait showing an abrasion patch on a rocky outcrop named Arathusa. The scene marks the farthest west the vehicle has traveled beyond Jezero Crater during its current science campaign.

5. **Hong Kong Museum Debuts Shenzhou 13 Sky Show** — bastillepost.com
   The Hong Kong Space Museum opens a new planetarium program focused on the Shenzhou 13 mission. The show highlights crew activities and orbital views for public audiences starting tomorrow.

6. **Cosmic Ray Origins Stay Elusive** — r/space
   Ultra-high-energy particles continue to strike Earth after traveling from distant cosmic accelerators. Magnetic fields scramble their arrival directions, leaving scientists without clear source identifications despite decades of observation.

7. **Space Elevators Move From Concept to Engineering** — Tech Times
   Recent design studies examine tether materials and climber systems capable of reaching geostationary orbit. The approach could reduce launch costs dramatically if structural and safety hurdles are solved.

8. **Galactic Center Mapped in H-Alpha and Continuum** — r/astronomy
   A six-hour exposure through an astromodified DSLR reveals ionized hydrogen clouds and dust lanes around Sagittarius A*. The image separates emission structures from the dense stellar background in the Milky Way’s core.

9. **Black Aerospace Pioneers Look Ahead** — Precinct Reporter News
   Early contributors to American rocketry and aviation reflect on expanding access to spaceflight. Their perspectives emphasize both technical progress and the need for broader participation in future programs.

### Cosmic Spotlight
NASA’s Perseverance rover paused on the western rim of Jezero Crater to document its own work on a rocky target called Arathusa. Sixty-one individual frames were stitched into an animated selfie that shows both the fresh abrasion patch and the sweeping ancient terrain behind it. The image captures a moment in the rover’s fifth science campaign, where it is systematically sampling rocks that may record Mars’ wetter past. The detailed documentation helps scientists correlate surface textures with mineral data collected by the rover’s instruments. What will the next abrasion patch reveal about the timeline of water on the Red Planet?

### Cosmic Deep Dive: Cosmic Rays and Their Scrambled Journeys
If you lined up every proton that hits Earth’s atmosphere in a single second, their combined energy would rival a small nuclear blast spread across the entire planet. These particles race in at nearly the speed of light after being flung from supernova remnants, black-hole jets, or distant active galaxies. Along the way they encounter magnetic fields that twist their paths like river currents deflecting floating leaves, erasing any straight-line memory of where they started. One rare event carries the energy of a well-thrown baseball packed into a single subatomic particle. Detectors on the ground and in orbit record arrival times and energies, yet the exact launch sites remain hidden because the deflections are too complex to reverse. Scientists still cannot explain why the very highest-energy rays appear to come from directions that do not match any known powerful objects in the sky.
