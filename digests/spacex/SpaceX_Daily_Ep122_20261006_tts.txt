From an engineering standpoint the record Dragon docking shows how orbital mechanics can be leveraged when the launch window aligns the vehicle with the station’s position.

Dragon reached orbit and began closing the range almost immediately because the station was already near the right phasing angle.

That geometry removed the need for multiple days of phasing burns that most missions still perform with the Draco thrusters.

The approach trades schedule flexibility for speed: future flights will need the full series of burns unless the station happens to be in a similar spot.

The same principle applies to any rendezvous where the chaser starts with a favorable relative position vector.

Reducing the number of burns also lowers propellant consumption on the vehicle that must later return to Earth.

Over many missions the cumulative savings in fuel and ground-support time become meaningful even if each individual flight cannot repeat the seven-hour-fifty-five-minute mark.
