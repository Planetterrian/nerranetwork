If you want the engineering angle on why Super Heavy carries 33 Raptors, start with the
mass it has to lift.
The booster is a roughly 5,000 ton vehicle that has to leave the pad with engine out margin.
The ability to lose an engine and still fly, then shut down a subset of chambers for
boost back, the burn that turns it toward the tower, and for the landing burn, without
losing control authority.
Each Raptor 3 makes between 230 and 250 metric tons of thrust at sea level, so the full
set supplies the 18 million pounds required at lift-off.
A back-of-envelope figure, 33 times 240 metric tons is about 7,900 metric tons of thrust
on a 5,000 ton stack, a lift-off thrust to weight on the order of 1.6.
Many smaller chambers avoid the combustion instability and fabrication limits that can
strain single chamber designs, such as Saturn 5's F1, which made on the order of 1.5 million
pounds each. Five of them on that first stage. High volume production of smaller engines
is how per unit cost falls, while turbo machinery and chamber materials iterate quickly. Super
Heavy is 9 meters wide and 72.3 meters tall in Block 3.
