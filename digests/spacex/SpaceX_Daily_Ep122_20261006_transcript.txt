From an engineering standpoint,
the record drag and docking shows
how orbital mechanics can be leveraged when the launch window aligns the vehicle with the
station's position. Dragon reached orbit and began closing the range almost immediately because the
station was already near the right phasing angle. That geometry removed the need from multiple days
of phasing burns that most missions still perform with the Draco thrusters. The approach trades
schedule flexibility for speed. Future flights will need the full series of burns unless the station
happens to be in a similar spot. The same principle applies to any rendezvous where the
chaser starts with a favorable relative position of actor. Reducing the number of burns also lowers
propellant consumption on the vehicle that must later return to Earth. Over many missions,
the cumulative savings in fuel and ground support pine become meaningful, even if each individual
flight cannot repeat the 7-hour 55-minute mark. A quick look at the tape shows SPCX finished
