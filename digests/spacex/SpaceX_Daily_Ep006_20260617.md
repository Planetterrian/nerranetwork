### Engineering Deep Dive
The 2026 Mars window plan rests on a simple propellant-mass budget. A Starship departing Earth orbit needs roughly 1,200 t of propellant after the initial boost to reach trans-Mars injection; each tanker can deliver about 100 t per rendezvous. Two carefully timed tanker flights therefore close the energy gap without requiring a larger vehicle. The same architecture scales directly to crewed missions once boil-off is controlled below 0.1 % per day through multi-layer insulation and solar-reflective coatings. Success here collapses the “dry-mass penalty” that has historically made interplanetary round trips uneconomical; every kilogram of structure saved on orbit multiplies into hundreds of kilograms of delivered payload at Mars.
