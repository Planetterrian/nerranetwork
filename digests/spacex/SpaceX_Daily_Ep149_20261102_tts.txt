Starship Block 3 is the engineering reality behind a one hundred twenty-four meter stack, nine meters in diameter, with a reusable low-Earth-orbit cargo target above one hundred tonnes.

Super Heavy, the first stage, stretches seventy-two meters and carries three thousand six hundred fifty tonnes of propellant. Thirty-three Raptor 3 engines make eight thousand two hundred forty tonnes of thrust at sea level.

That cluster is an engine-out design: lose one Raptor on the way up and the rest can still lift a wet mass of thousands of tonnes.

Ship, the upper stage, measures fifty-two meters and holds one thousand six hundred tonnes of propellant. It flies three Raptors built for sea level and three built for vacuum, one thousand six hundred fourteen tonnes of thrust in all, each sea-level Raptor 3 near two hundred fifty tonnes.

A closed-cycle methalox system, methane and liquid oxygen, pushes fuel and oxidizer through the turbopumps first, then into the chamber. That is full-flow staged combustion: no turbine exhaust dumped overboard, so every kilogram does work in the chamber.

The cycle raises chamber pressure and specific impulse, the push per kilogram of propellant, while cutting parts.

Methane stays liquid at friendlier temperatures than hydrogen, which keeps tank volume down, and it does not leave the carbon soot kerosene leaves in a reused chamber.

Flight fourteen held orbit long enough to deploy twenty-six Starlink V3 satellites, each designed for roughly one terabit per second of downlink, something earlier blocks did not complete.

Heat-shield tiles in a hexagonal layout on the Ship survived re-entry and left the vehicle in shape to fly again.

The booster's engine count supplied the thrust-to-weight for Super Heavy return and tower catch on prior flights, proving the propellant load and engine count deliver the delta-v margin, leftover velocity, to come home rather than fall short.

A common bulkhead, a single wall between oxygen and methane tanks on both stages, saves structural mass versus separate tanks. At this diameter a second dome is on the order of several tonnes that become payload fraction instead of steel, and the rocket equation punishes extra dry mass all the way to orbit.

That mass saving compounds with higher chamber pressure, so the same propellant load delivers more total impulse than an equivalent open-cycle engine.

Raptor 3 deleted external lines and valves, makes about thirty-five percent more thrust than Raptor 1 with fewer parts, and shrinks the Idiot Index, how many times the finished engine costs over its raw inputs.

The gap is not the nickel or the methane, which are industrial commodities, but the machining, the tolerances, and the active cooling passages that keep a few-hundred-bar chamber from melting.

Those tolerances are met with additive manufacturing, metal printing, and precision machining Space X has scaled in its own facilities.

Fewer external leak points is why post-flight inspection can be far lighter, and that is what lets a Super Heavy booster be caught, looked over, and reflown, and a Ship return, closer to airline-style turnaround, instead of expending the hardware.

Magic-wand accounting for this stack is stainless tank metal plus on the order of five thousand tonnes of industrial methane and oxygen across both stages. Reuse is how the flown vehicle stops paying a painful multiple of that wand number on every ascent.
