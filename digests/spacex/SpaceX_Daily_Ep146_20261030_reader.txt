The Federal Communications Commission voted Thursday to advance a proposal raising eligible satellite direct-to-phone bands from five to thirteen, according to Keep Track, though no bands have been assigned yet.

AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile, through the wireless trade group CTIA, asked the commission to drop the two point five gigahertz and three point four five gigahertz bands, which account for two hundred ninety megahertz of the total.

T-Mobile argued satellite interference could eliminate up to ninety-six percent of a cell site's uplink coverage area.

SpaceX asked the FCC to add the nine hundred two to nine hundred twenty-eight megahertz band.

A companion item proposes auctioning twenty-five megahertz in the one thousand six hundred seventy-five to one thousand six hundred ninety-five megahertz and two thousand twenty to two thousand twenty-five megahertz bands, usable for terrestrial service, direct-to-device service, or both, with comments due sixty days after the notice appears in the Federal Register.

Cursor disclosed a security incident involving a maliciously modified version of the Anthropic Claude Code extension in its VS Code marketplace.

Business Standard reports the package was not developed by Anthropic, ran code attempting to install a persistent Secure Shell, or SSH, backdoor, and affected at most five thousand users.

The malicious package was available for roughly eleven hours on October twenty-ninth.

It collected host and user information including hostname, username, operating system, and the machine's public IP address, tried to add an attacker-controlled SSH key and open a reverse tunnel — a step the company said would fail unless Cursor was started with administrator privileges — and still installed persistence and sent host details.

For persistence, the payload installs a Mac login agent called a LaunchAgent or, on Windows, a scheduled task that attempts to run at the highest system privilege, with background scripts retrying the tunnel roughly every sixty seconds.

Cursor removed the malicious version, shut down the entire marketplace as a precaution, confirmed no other extensions were tampered with, rotated its keys, rebuilt the marketplace, and worked with the cloud provider to take the attacker's server offline, leaving the reverse shell non-functional.

Closing a phone-to-satellite link, from an engineering standpoint, is a fight against distance and milliwatts, not a shortage of towers.

A handheld phone puts out only a few hundred milliwatts of radio energy, while a terrestrial cell site sits a few hundred meters away and can run a few tens of watts.

Free-space path loss at a couple of gigahertz across hundreds of kilometers is, on a back-of-envelope figure, on the order of one hundred fifty decibels, so received power shrinks by a factor of about ten to the fifteen.

Thermal noise in a one-hertz bandwidth sits near minus one hundred seventy-four dBm, a textbook floor, so every extra decibel of path loss eats into the bits you can push.

Shannon's formula says that in the moderate signal-to-noise regime, more hertz at the same ratio raises the data rate without asking the handset to shout louder.

In the extremely power-limited limit, capacity approaches a ceiling set by received power over noise density, so extra spectrum helps more as cleaner channels and as reuse of the same hertz in non-overlapping spot beams, meaning narrow radio footprints on the ground.

Each new megahertz can be painted onto a different patch of Earth at the same time, so system capacity scales with both bandwidth and the number of simultaneous beams the satellite's phased-array antennas can form.

Because the satellites already carry inter-satellite laser links, a direct-to-cell user's bits can leave the serving beam and ride the mesh to a ground station instead of fighting for a return path in the same frequency.

The magic-wand number is hertz reused across beams rather than extra milliwatts in your pocket, and the Idiot Index is that fifteen-orders-of-magnitude gap that coding, antenna gain, and frequency reuse have to close.

Wider contiguous channels let the modem extract more bits per joule at the handset, the only lever that does not raise handset output, while lower-frequency allocations penetrate buildings and foliage with less loss.
