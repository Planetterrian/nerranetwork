Nine-meter scale is the engineering reality that ended carbon-fiber Starship prototypes and put stainless steel under both Starship and Super Heavy.

Carbon fiber fought the factory at that diameter, because a nine-meter composite barrel needs mandrels, autoclaves, and void-free cures that grow nastier with every extra meter, while stainless plate rolls and welds on tooling shipyards already own.

The raw sheet also costs roughly fifteen to twenty times less than equivalent titanium, so the metal stops being the exotic line item and starts being a commodity bought as plate.

Cool that steel in liquid oxygen and methane and yield strength climbs by about fifty percent, so the same wall holds about one and a half times the original load, and it does so without the brittleness risk titanium faces at cryogenic temperature.

Coming back through the atmosphere, the steel still carries load at roughly eight hundred fifty degrees Celsius, which lets the heat shield go thinner or use fewer tiles than a titanium primary structure would need, because the airframe itself can take the soak.

Those same traits let a team cut, weld, and change a prototype in weeks rather than months, collapsing the gap between plate price and finished-stage price.

Manufacturing speed and that thermal margin together are what make rapid, repeated reuse of an entire one-hundred-twenty-meter stack feasible at the volumes Starship is designed to fly.

Early Atlas tanks made a similar move from exotic alloys to stainless once the performance margin was there, because cost and fabricability started to beat pedigree.

The Idiot Index, how many times a finished stage costs over the raw metal and the propellant, therefore drops sharply: the dominant expense leaves exotic forming and joining and lands on welding and heat treatment that existing industrial sites already know how to do.

With a Starship upper stage now caught by the tower, the next constraint is how fast the factory can turn out vehicles whose per-flight cost approaches the price of the propellant alone.
