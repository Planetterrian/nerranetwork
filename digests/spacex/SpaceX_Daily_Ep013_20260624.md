### Engineering Deep Dive
Starmind’s orbital placement removes the dominant terrestrial constraint of convective and conductive heat rejection by operating in vacuum, where only radiation remains. A satellite node can therefore sustain higher sustained power density than an equivalent rack on Earth without the multi-megawatt chiller plants that currently dominate data-center energy budgets. The design trades launch mass and radiation hardening for elimination of those cooling systems, lowering the Idiot Index of delivered compute from the roughly 3–4× raw silicon-plus-power cost on the ground to something closer to the mass and radiation-shielding overhead alone. Once the first batch of nodes reaches orbit, the marginal cost per additional inference token falls primarily to incremental Starship flights rather than continuous terrestrial power and water infrastructure expansion. That shift also opens the possibility of co-locating training clusters with the inference layer in the same orbital shell, collapsing the round-trip latency that currently separates the two workloads.
