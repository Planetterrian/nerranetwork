For that flight 15 upper stage catch, the engineering reality is converting hypersonic
high drag flight into a vertical capture with body flaps alone, only seconds before contact.
fact. Those flaps have to raise pitch and yaw while the starship upper stage is still
moving at hundreds of meters per second. A few hundred milliseconds of timing error puts
it outside the arm geometry envelope. Horizontal velocity and leftover rates have to die to roughly
a meter and a degree, while the stage is still on aerodynamic control. The upper stage
has no super heavy style grid fins, and body flap bite collapses below a few kilopascals
of dynamic pressure. That pressure is 1.5 times air density times velocity squared.
Near the ground, density is about 1.2 kilograms per cubic meter. At 100 meters per second,
you get on the order of 6 kilopascals. At 50 meters per second, you get about 1.5 kilopascals.
Authority falls with the square of speed, so the flaps go mushy faster than the vehicle
slows. Right into the flip. That square law is why the last
second is the whole game. Hypersonic means faster than Mach 5 on the order of a kilometer
and a half per second or more, before drag bleeds it down. At vertical residual horizontal
speed must sit under 1 meter per second and pitch and yaw rates under 1 degree per second,
or the arms and the skirt yield. 2 meters per second is not twice the problem. Energy and
therefore load go with velocity squared, so it is 4 times the hit. 1 extra degree per
second walks a compressive stack into a peel on the arm pins.
Structural steel yields on the order of a few hundred megapascals, so a side slip at
remaining descent speed is a spike, not a tap.
The plan uses body flaps rather than engines for those last seconds, which keeps a plume
off the tower and makes the catch a purely mechanical take of a tiny leftover state.
The controller still modulates flaps into the last half-second before capture.
At 40 meters per second of descent, that half-second is on the order of 20 meters of travel.
The arms are a fixed geometry and cannot chase a miss, so the vehicle has to fly into
a box measured in meters.
