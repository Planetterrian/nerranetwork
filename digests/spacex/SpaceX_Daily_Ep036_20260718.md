### Engineering Deep Dive
The Raptor 3 ignition gap that halted Flight 13 at T-0 illustrates a classic start-transient problem in staged-combustion engines. At ignition, the turbopumps must reach self-sustaining speed before the main propellant valves open fully; any mismatch in spark timing, chamber pressure rise, or gas-generator light-off creates a window where the engine either fails to start or experiences a hard start that damages hardware. SpaceX’s choice to replace the two affected engines rather than attempt an in-place fix reflects the priority on preserving the remainder of the test objectives and the vehicle stack. The decision also preserves schedule margin for the July 20 window while the data team reviews the precise timing logs.

From first principles, the cost of that decision is dominated by the marginal expense of two additional Raptor 3 units rather than the raw materials inside them. If each engine’s bill of materials—turbopumps, injectors, chamber liners, valves—is roughly one-tenth the assembled price, the Idiot Index for a single Raptor sits near 10×. Reusing the same engines across multiple flights collapses that multiplier by amortizing the precision-machined components over dozens of missions instead of one. Falcon 9’s 2025 record of 165 flights on reused boosters already shows how that amortization works at scale; Starship must eventually match or exceed that reuse intensity to deliver comparable economics.

The immediate engineering question is whether the ignition timing margin can be widened through software sequencing or minor valve-profile changes before the next countdown. A successful July 20 flight would move the program one step closer to demonstrating the engine reliability needed for rapid booster turnaround; continued start-sequence issues would point to a deeper requirement for hardware redesign or additional ground-test campaigns before orbital refueling attempts can be scheduled with confidence. Observers will watch whether the replacement engines undergo a dedicated static-fire acceptance test or are installed directly, as that choice will reveal how much schedule pressure the team is willing to accept to keep the test campaign moving.
