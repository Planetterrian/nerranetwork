### Top News

1. **SpaceX informs NASA of end to Crew Dragon missions after 2030**
   SpaceX has informed NASA that it will end Crew Dragon and Falcon 9 crewed missions to low Earth orbit after 2030.
   The company will continue supporting the International Space Station through 2030 but plans to retire Dragon afterward.
   SpaceX has also told private space station developers including Axiom Space, Voyager Space and Vast Space that it will not provide Crew Dragon missions.
   The shift follows SpaceX earning most revenue from Starlink, with future plans centered on Starlink and orbital data centers.
   NASA spent $3.1 billion through its commercial crew program, yet SpaceX has already flown 13 crewed missions beyond the original six-mission contract.
   SpaceX told NASA it has no current interest in developing Starship for crewed low Earth orbit launches.
   Crewed ascent and re-entry raise additional safety and regulatory issues that Starship has not yet addressed.
   NASA would have limited ability to require continued Dragon flights beyond the existing contract commitments. Source: [digitaltoday.co.kr](https://www.digitaltoday.co.kr/en/view/108922/spacex-moves-to-withdraw-dragon-falcon-9-crewed-missions-after-2030-widening-nasa-low-earth-orbit-gap)

2. **Hanwha Aerospace begins assembly of reusable demonstrator**
   Hanwha Aerospace has begun final assembly of a reusable technology demonstrator under a Korea Aerospace Research Institute project.
   The 12.2 meter tall, 1.5 meter wide vehicle carries a single throttleable staged combustion cycle turbopump engine.
   A 2028 flight test will see the demonstrator lift off vertically, reach 500 meters altitude in about 90 seconds, and land vertically.
   The project will verify engine thrust control, attitude control, and guidance and control systems.
   Hanwha aims to apply the results to Korea's next-generation launch vehicle planned for the mid-2030s.
   The company seeks to secure core reuse technologies similar to those behind SpaceX's Falcon 9. Source: [koreajoongangdaily.com](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/business/hanwhasnbspkorean-spacex-ambitions-grow-as-worknbspbegins-on-reusable-rocket-test-vehiclenbsp/12897399)

3. **Falcon Heavy rolls out for NROL-97 classified mission**
   SpaceX rolled Falcon Heavy to Launch Complex 39A at Kennedy Space Center for the NROL-97 mission.
   Liftoff is targeted for October 1 at 11:53 p.m. ET, with a backup window the following night.
   One side booster is on its second flight and the other on its fourth flight after supporting prior missions including GOES-U and the Roman Space Telescope.
   The center core will be expended in the Atlantic Ocean rather than recovered.
   The mission carries a classified payload for the National Reconnaissance Office.
   Both side boosters are planned to land simultaneously at Landing Zones 1 and 2 at Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. Source: [basenor.com](https://www.basenor.com/blogs/news/falcon-heavy-nrol-97-5-details-that-matter-before-launch)

4. **Anthropic signs up to $84.5 billion in SpaceX data center deals**
   Anthropic has entered agreements worth up to $84.5 billion with SpaceX for data center capacity.
   The deals cover capacity at Colossus 1 and Colossus 2 through May 2029 at $1.25 billion per month.
   Anthropic can cancel the agreements with three months notice.
   SpaceX's IPO filings had disclosed at least $42.5 billion from the same agreements.
   The agreements allow SpaceX to monetize unused compute capacity in its infrastructure. Source: [wccftech.com](https://wccftech.com/spacex-set-to-receive-84-5-billion-from-anthropic-in-ai-capacity-payments-says-report-figure-2x-of-what-spacex-disclosed-in-ipo-filings/amp/)

5. **SpaceWit launches Space String orbital computing constellation**
   SpaceWit launched the "Space String" computing constellation program targeting the global market.
   The roadmap calls for more than 1,000 space agents deployed long term.
   The G1 satellite is scheduled for 2027 launch with an 11.5 kW computing load and 40-60P computing power.
   Earthii provides AI capability support while Oriental Starlink handles overall design and operation.
   The program aims to create integrated space agents that combine chips, algorithms and models. Source: [eu.36kr.com](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/4005156110389120)

6. **Boeing Starliner delays compound NASA crew transport concerns**
   Boeing's Starliner remains behind schedule and has not yet flown an operational crew rotation to the ISS.
   The combination of Dragon retirement and Starliner's delays could produce a narrower commercial human-spaceflight market in the 2030s. ### Engineering Deep Dive
The engineering angle on Hanwha Aerospace's demonstrator centers on proving throttleable staged combustion cycle operation in a vertical takeoff and landing profile. The single engine must vary thrust across the full flight envelope while the vehicle maintains attitude control through a 90-second ascent to 500 meters. That requirement forces the turbopump and injector design to handle rapid mixture ratio changes without combustion instability, a constraint that grows harder at larger scales. The 12.2 meter height and 1.5 meter diameter keep structural mass low enough that the engine can land the stack on a single throttle setting rather than requiring multiple engines or grid fins. Once the 2028 test confirms guidance loop closure and engine restart reliability, the data directly informs first-stage reuse on Korea's mid-2030s vehicle, where recovering only the booster can cut per-kilogram launch cost by roughly half compared with expendable designs. The same throttle authority also reduces the Idiot Index on the engine itself: raw turbopump and chamber materials represent a small fraction of finished engine cost, yet the control software and test campaign multiply that number several times until reuse amortizes the investment across dozens of flights. Staged combustion cycle engines achieve higher specific impulse than gas-generator cycles by routing all propellant through the turbine before injection, yet they demand precise valve timing to avoid hard starts or chugging during throttle transients. The 500-meter target altitude keeps the vehicle inside visual range for telemetry validation while exposing it to enough dynamic pressure to test attitude authority without requiring full orbital energy. Vertical landing at that scale validates the same guidance algorithms later needed for booster recovery, where the vehicle must null both horizontal and vertical velocity within a few meters of the target pad. The 2028 flight therefore serves as a low-cost risk-reduction step before committing to the larger first stage planned for the mid-2030s vehicle, where the economic payoff of reuse becomes material only after the engine demonstrates repeated ignition and throttling without refurbishment.
