from an engineering standpoint because combustion instability grows with the size of the combustor.
The F1 remains the most powerful single chamber liquid rocket motor ever flown,
producing 6.77 MN of sea level thrust, and combustion instability during its development forced
extensive redesigns and testing that ultimately limited further scaling of single chamber designs.
A large combustor supports low frequency acoustic modes that couple with the burn.
If a pressure wave hits the injector just as fresh propellant arrives, the burn spikes,
the wave grows, and the engine can tear itself apart, which is why F1 engineers spent
years on baffles and injectors instead of simply making the chamber bigger.
Raptor avoids that trap by using smaller chambers that are easier to stabilize, throttle,
and replace after a flight.
Sea level variants produce between 230 and 250 tons of thrust, and vacuum-optimized versions
reach 258 to 275 tons.

out capability, the ability to keep flying after one engine fails and rapid reusability.
Lose one chamber in a cluster and you still have most of your thrust, lose the only chamber
and the stage is done.
