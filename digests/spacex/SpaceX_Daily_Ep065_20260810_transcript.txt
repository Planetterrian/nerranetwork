Mechanical fasteners reduce ground turnaround time because individual tiles can be swapped without rebuilding large sections of the vehicle. That speed advantage only holds if the fasteners themselves survive the full thermal and mechanical load spectrum across repeated missions.
Ablative materials by contrast erode predictably during each reentry and therefore remove the need for precise alignment after every flight.
The raw material cost of an able to layer is low yet the added mass directly reduces payload capacity to orbit and raises the propellant load required for every subsequent mission.
Engineers must therefore weigh the recurring mass penalty against the one time qualification cost of a fastener system that may never reach the needed cycle count.
