### Top News

1. **SpaceX selects Qualcomm Dragonwing for Starlink Mobile nationwide 5G network**
   SpaceX chose Qualcomm's Dragonwing QRU100 and QDU100 platforms plus Layer 1 software to equip Massive MIMO radio units with 64 transmit and 64 receive paths. The multi-year deal covers tens of thousands of terrestrial cell sites, with deployment beginning in 2027. The hardware targets improved coverage, capacity, spectral efficiency, and performance per watt at power-constrained tower locations. Starlink Mobile will combine the new ground network with its satellite constellation to pursue fourth-carrier status in the United States. SpaceX acquired roughly 65 MHz of mid-band spectrum from EchoStar and up to 14 MHz of low-band spectrum from Grain Management to support the build. The terrestrial layer is intended to complement satellite direct-to-cell service rather than replace it, with small cells or femtocells potentially co-located alongside existing Starlink user terminals. Qualcomm described the agreement as its largest 5G deal to date. ### Engineering Deep Dive
The Qualcomm Dragonwing QRU100 radio units and QDU100 baseband units selected for Starlink Mobile integrate radio-frequency processing and latency-sensitive Layer 1 functions into a compact hardware set. This consolidation reduces the number of discrete enclosures that require power and cooling at each tower site, lowering the kilowatt-hour draw when the network scales to tens of thousands of locations. Massive MIMO arrays configured for 64 transmit and 64 receive paths form narrower, higher-gain beams that reuse the same frequency channels more densely than earlier 8T8R or 32T32R systems. The efficiency gain is especially relevant for the 65 MHz mid-band block SpaceX acquired from EchoStar, where capacity rather than coverage sets the binding constraint. Because many tower sites already operate under strict utility limits, every watt saved at the radio and distributed-unit layer compounds across the nationwide footprint and improves the economics of adding small cells or co-located Starlink terminals for capacity relief. The same performance-per-watt metric also determines how quickly the hybrid satellite-terrestrial handoff can scale without requiring new grid connections at every cell.

Spectral efficiency improvements from 64T64R beamforming directly affect the number of simultaneous users that can be served within a given channel bandwidth. In mid-band spectrum the wavelength allows practical array sizes that still deliver the spatial multiplexing needed for dense urban and suburban deployments. The Dragonwing platforms also supply commercial-grade Layer 1 software that handles physical-layer signal processing, further trimming overhead compared with separate software stacks running on general-purpose servers. When the terrestrial network begins rolling out in 2027 alongside the existing satellite constellation, the combined architecture will need to manage seamless handoffs between orbital and ground links while maintaining consistent latency and throughput. Power savings at each site therefore translate into both lower operating expense and faster permitting, since utilities and local authorities often scrutinize total facility draw before approving new installations. The design choice to standardize on these specific Qualcomm platforms reflects a calculation that integrated hardware will deliver better watts-per-bit than piecemeal solutions assembled from multiple vendors.

The low-band 14 MHz allocation acquired from Grain Management complements the mid-band layer by improving indoor penetration and range, allowing fewer macro sites to cover the same geography. This dual-layer spectrum strategy mirrors the approach used by established carriers, yet SpaceX can leverage its satellite backhaul to reach sites that would otherwise lack fiber or microwave connectivity. The resulting network economics depend on how quickly the performance-per-watt advantage of the Dragonwing hardware offsets the capital cost of tens of thousands of radios and baseband units. If the efficiency targets are met, the hybrid system could serve as a credible fourth national carrier by filling coverage gaps that satellites alone cannot address while adding capacity where terrestrial demand is highest. The 2027 start date implies that site acquisition, permitting, and backhaul planning are already under way, even though the first satellites for direct-to-cell service are still ramping.
