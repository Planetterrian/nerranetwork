SpaceX, Spaceflight Now reports, now holds the first license of this type for an AI compute network in orbit, and the Starmind grant does not require every planned satellite to fly.

The company had filed on January thirtieth, two thousand twenty-six; spacecraft now have to start reaching orbit by November twenty-fifth, two thousand thirty-three, with one hundred thousand in orbit by two thousand thirty-five and five hundred thousand by two thousand thirty-eight, and the authorization runs until two thousand forty.

The vehicles will fly in low-Earth orbit between five hundred and one thousand two kilometers, in fifty-kilometer-thick shells at thirty-degree and sun-synchronous inclinations that hold a steady sun angle, taking power from the sun, dumping waste heat by radiation, and sending finished compute over Starlink's optical crosslinks.

First flight could come as soon as the third quarter of two thousand twenty-seven on Starship, SpaceX is building the vehicles around Nvidia Vera Rubin processors, and the commission also cleared extra communications hardware for the company's growing optical network.

Chairman Brendan Carr said the project backs American leadership in super-intelligence infrastructure, while Amazon's Kuiper, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society objected over orbital debris, congestion and harm to optical and radio astronomy.

Elon Musk replied on X that the constellation "will be a great asset to America and the world."

Musk wrote in a separate post on X that SpaceX is beating the rest of the world by an order of magnitude and that the lead appears to be widening.

He said no advantage is permanent, yet described current margins as durable.

A classified United States Space Force payload is scheduled to lift off from Vandenberg Space Force Base tonight or Saturday, the Lompoc Record reports, under the designation USSF-266.

Cochise County's supercompute campus will use neither a gas plant nor an industrial water draw, SpaceX said on X, and it relies on closed-loop dry cooling that keeps coolant in the pipes and rejects heat without evaporating water, while the company pays for its own electricity and puts three point six billion dollars into grid upgrades.

SpaceX plans to invest more than fifty billion dollars in the area, bringing one thousand local permanent jobs and up to five thousand five hundred construction jobs, and it promised up to forty percent lower residential rates for local SSVEC customers.

The company stated that people who already live there will not be asked to subsidize the work, and a public meeting is scheduled for December ninth to address community questions.

SpaceX will donate one new Class A share into each active Trump Account set up for American children, SSBCrack reports, with Musk matching that company gift himself.

President and chief operating officer Gwynne Shotwell separately promised more than two million shares she already owns, and the gifts from Musk and Shotwell do not raise the number of shares outstanding.

Teams wrapped the Cygnus XL spacecraft for Northrop Grumman's last commercial cargo run of two thousand twenty-six to the International Space Station, or ISS, SpaceX said, with Falcon 9 liftoff targeted for ten forty-seven a.m. Eastern on Sunday, November twenty-ninth from Cape Canaveral.

Elsewhere, former xAI employees, Zamin reports, have launched a new startup focused on predictive AI models that aim to forecast future events using advanced machine-learning techniques.

Worth keeping an eye on, astronomers and rival operators argue the Starmind constellation will increase orbital debris risk, radio-frequency interference and light pollution that degrades both optical and radio observations.

Those objections are the reason the FCC order limits the license to backup Ka-band radio links and requires an accepted debris-mitigation plan before any satellite flies above six hundred kilometers.

Amazon called the timeline and the size technically and economically unrealistic, and the order drops the financial surety bond that usually backstops debris risk while leaving the deployment deadlines in force.

For a Starmind spacecraft, the engineering reality is a trade of radiator area and shielding mass against the land, water and grid a ground hall has to buy.

A satellite in those shells sees sunlight for nearly the entire orbit except brief eclipse periods, which removes the grid-scale battery or gas-turbine buffering terrestrial facilities need at peak demand.

Waste heat leaves through large radiator surfaces rather than evaporative cooling towers or chillers, and the reported seventy-five-meter wingspan and thirty-meter deployed height supply the area for that radiation balance.

Heat dumped into space rises steeply as the surface warms, so that extra area lets processors run at higher power density than air or water loops allow, holding junction temperatures within commercial-grade limits without active refrigeration.

Chips still take a slow radiation soak and sudden bit flips from trapped protons and electrons, so the design adds aluminum or tantalum shielding or uses silicon-on-insulator processes — chips built on an insulating layer — that raise per-die cost but cut upset rates.

Raw metal for that shielding costs far less than the finished packaged die, which is the gap the idiot index measures: how many times delivered compute costs over the silicon, metal and kilowatt-hours inside.

That index can fall if Starship launch cost per kilogram drops enough to offset radiator and shielding mass, compared with terrestrial sites that also pay for land, water rights and grid interconnection.

Day-to-day traffic rides optical inter-satellite links already flying on Starlink, so the radio path stays a narrow backup and any detected interference takes that channel offline, shifting reliability onto those optical terminals.

The same architecture also frees site selection from fights over where to put a power plant on the ground.

The plan is to start small in two thousand twenty-seven, increase through two thousand twenty-eight, and hit very large volume in two thousand twenty-nine once Starship flight rate and payload capacity stabilize, moving the bottleneck from silicon fabrication to assembly at Gigabay.

Filling incremental planes rather than requiring simultaneous global coverage cuts peak manufacturing load, the same staged method that grew Starlink from hundreds of spacecraft to more than eleven thousand without ever halting service.
