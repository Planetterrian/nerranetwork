The engineering angle on the final IROSA solar arrays centers on how roll-out flexible blankets solve mass and volume constraints that rigid panels cannot meet at the same power level.

Each array deploys from a compact canister using stored strain energy in the mast and blanket, avoiding the need for heavy motors or hinges that would add mass to every resupply flight.

The design trades initial complexity in the deployment mechanism for a power-to-mass ratio that supports the station’s growing experiment load and eventual controlled deorbit.

At roughly 20 kilowatts per pair once installed, the arrays reduce reliance on the original rigid arrays whose degradation has already limited available power margins.

The choice also reflects a broader pattern in which Space X and NASA favor lightweight deployables that fit inside Dragon’s trunk volume rather than requiring dedicated heavy-lift flights solely for power hardware.

This approach keeps the marginal cost of each additional kilowatt low enough that the station can maintain operations through 2030 without a full redesign of the power architecture.
