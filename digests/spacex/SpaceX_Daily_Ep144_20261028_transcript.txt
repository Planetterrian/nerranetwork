For the engineering angle on that golf coast cadence, every ground operation has to become
a repeatable industrial process rather than a one-off campaign.
At that rate, the pad and tower have to take a returning vehicle, load propellant, and
lift off in about 50 minutes on average, with almost no spare time for inspection or repair.
A day holds 1,440 minutes, so a 50-minute cycle is the entire clock if the tower never
sits idle. The mechanical arms that caught the super heavy booster on earlier flights
already show one piece of that speed. Extending the same approach to the ship upper stage removes

How many times the finished stack costs over its raw inputs?
The raw material floor is stainless steel sheet, liquid oxygen and methane, plus the silicon
and rare earth content inside avionics and engines.
The magic wand number, metal plus propellant, plus those minerals, is a few hundred thousand
dollars per flight.
The finished vehicle still carries an idiot index of roughly 50 to 100 times that floor
once labor, tooling, testing, and certification are included.
A few hundred thousand dollars times 50 is on the order of tens of millions for a flight
ready stack before reuse, as a back of envelope figure, not a company price.
Fly the same stack many times and that labor and certification burden spreads across flights,
which is the only way the ratio comes down at scale.
