### Engineering Deep Dive
The IFT-12 dry-mass data reveal a 24 t reduction on the booster and an 11 t reduction on the ship relative to Block 2 averages. From first principles, structural mass directly subtracts from the propellant load that can be lifted to orbit; every kilogram saved therefore multiplies the number of tanker flights needed to fill a downstream vehicle.  

The observed savings come from thinner tank walls and a redesigned interstage. If the new wall thickness still satisfies buckling margins under the same axial and bending loads, the design change has collapsed part of the “Idiot Index” between raw aluminum-lithium sheet and the finished tank barrel.  

That margin expansion matters for the open refueling question: fewer tankers per mission reduces the total launch rate required before a Mars cargo manifest becomes feasible. Watch the next flight test for confirmation that the lighter structure survives re-entry heating at the same reliability as prior blocks.
