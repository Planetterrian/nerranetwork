SpaceX Q2 2026 Earnings Special: Revenue reached $7.814 billion, up 92 percent from a year earlier, in the company's first earnings report as a public company.

SpaceX reported revenue of seven point eight one four billion dollars for the second quarter, up ninety two percent from four point zero seven one billion dollars a year ago and ahead of the roughly six point nine billion dollars analysts had expected.

Net loss narrowed to five hundred forty one million dollars from the one point zero zero eight billion dollar loss recorded in the same quarter last year, while loss per share came in at nine cents against an expected loss of roughly twenty six cents.

Adjusted EBITDA rose one hundred ninety one percent to three point five three eight billion dollars, and loss from operations improved to one hundred forty three million dollars from nine hundred seventy million dollars, placing the company close to operating breakeven on a quarterly basis.

For the first half of the year the net loss reached four point eight one seven billion dollars, but that figure includes a one point five four five billion dollar loss on debt extinguishment and a six hundred seventy one million dollar deemed dividend tied to the pre-IPO capital structure.

Those one-time items explain much of the headline six-month loss and should be set aside when comparing ongoing performance.

In the Space segment revenue reached nine hundred sixty two million dollars, up twenty nine percent year-over-year and fifty five percent sequentially, while operating loss widened to five hundred forty two million dollars as Starship research-and-development spending accelerated to one point zero seven six billion dollars, fifty five percent higher than a year earlier.

The company conducted thirty eight launches in the quarter, ten for customers and twenty eight internal, lifting four hundred eighty five metric tons to orbit.

The operating loss grew because development costs for the next-generation vehicle outpaced launch revenue growth in the period.

Connectivity delivered the clearest profit contribution, with revenue of four point two nine one billion dollars, up sixty six percent year-over-year, operating income of one point six five six billion dollars, up seventy nine percent, and Adjusted EBITDA of two point five nine seven billion dollars.

Starlink subscribers reached twelve point zero million, doubling from a year earlier and adding one point seven million in the quarter, while average revenue per user held steady at sixty six dollars per month after the deliberate reduction from eighty five dollars a year ago to expand the addressable market.

Consumer revenue grew forty four percent to two point four eight five billion dollars, while Enterprise & Government revenue more than doubled to one point eight zero six billion dollars.

The AI segment posted revenue of two point five six one billion dollars, up two hundred forty seven percent year-over-year and two hundred thirteen percent sequentially, driven by new Cloud Services Agreements that lifted AI solutions and infrastructure revenue to two point one nine four billion dollars from three hundred eleven million dollars.

The AI segment recorded its first positive Adjusted EBITDA of one point one four six billion dollars, compared with a negative two hundred seventy six million dollars a year earlier, even as it posted an operating loss of one point two five seven billion dollars because of heavy depreciation and continued research-and-development outlays.

Two successful Starship V3 flight tests occurred in the quarter, Flight 12 in May and Flight 13 in July, both from the new Starbase pad.

Flight 12 achieved the first suborbital mission profile and a precision upper-stage landing along with modified V2 Starlink satellite deployment.

Flight 13 met all objectives including deployment of twenty production V3 satellites, an in-space Raptor relight, and the softest Starship splashdown recorded with an intact heat shield.

On the earnings call, Musk stated the next flight, tentatively scheduled for the end of August, will attempt to catch the ship with the tower pending regulatory approval and projected a cadence of at least one flight a day, possibly more, within a year.

Cloud Services Agreements totaling fourteen point one billion dollars in contracted sales drove one point six billion dollars of incremental AI infrastructure revenue, while nameplate compute capacity expanded to one point four gigawatts from one point zero gigawatts in the prior quarter and zero point four gigawatts a year earlier as Colossus II construction continued.

Reporting on the call noted a Google agreement for AI capacity and that Anthropic contracted all of Colossus 1 capacity in Memphis.

The company announced an agreement to acquire Cursor for sixty billion dollars, expected to close in the third quarter of twenty twenty six, to accelerate AI enterprise offerings.

Grok four point five was released in July as the largest model to date, trained alongside Cursor and incorporating the one point five trillion parameter V9 foundation model.

More than six billion dollars in multi-year U.S. government Starshield contracts were secured, primarily two Space Force awards for low-Earth-orbit communications and sensing.

Additional connectivity wins included an American Airlines agreement plus activations on Southwest, Virgin Atlantic, Iberia, and Aer Lingus, plus mobile partnerships with SoftBank, NTT Docomo, and Spark NZ, and FCC approval of the EchoStar license transfer covering sixty five megahertz of U.S. spectrum and global mobile-satellite-service licenses.

One thing worth watching is the quarterly capital expenditures that reached eighteen point three six nine billion dollars, of which fifteen point eight two eight billion dollars went to AI infrastructure, roughly thirty nine percent above analyst estimates, with management indicating the next two quarters could show similar levels.

Operating cash flow for the first half totaled three point four six six billion dollars against twenty eight point four seven six billion dollars of first-half capex, so the spending is being funded by the IPO and bond proceeds rather than current operations.

After the print, SPCX fell roughly seven percent in after-hours trading and hovered near one hundred seventeen dollars, below the one hundred thirty five dollar IPO price and the one hundred fifty dollar opening trade.

The first major share-lockup expiration begins August sixth, creating a supply overhang to monitor.

Two customers accounted for approximately nineteen point five percent and eighteen point three percent of second-quarter revenue, illustrating concentration risk tied to the new Cloud Services Agreements.

Starlink average revenue per user declined from eighty five dollars to sixty six dollars year-over-year as part of the scale strategy.

The CFO's statement that the company is on a trajectory to reach one hundred billion dollars of annualized recurring revenue by year end should be treated as a management projection whose definition and timing carry the usual caveats around backlog conversion and contract ramp.

Commentary following the release highlighted the revenue beat and subscriber growth as impressive while focusing on the scale of AI-related capex as the primary driver of the after-hours decline.

Outlets such as CNBC noted that soaring AI costs weighed on the stock despite the top-line outperformance, with investors emphasizing near-term cash burn.

X commentary from accounts including @homotrades described the revenue trajectory as strong yet flagged the capex debate as the dominant concern.

Other posts observed that Nvidia remains positioned as a direct beneficiary of the infrastructure build-out.

The sharpest takes centered on whether the AI revenue quality and long-term utilization justify the quarterly spend rate rather than on the launch or Starlink results themselves.

From an engineering standpoint the fifteen point eight two eight billion dollars in quarterly AI infrastructure capex appears on the balance sheet as servers and networking equipment rising from twenty two point seven billion dollars to thirty four point eight billion dollars in six months and construction in progress nearly tripling to twelve point six billion dollars, while nameplate compute capacity scaled from zero point four gigawatts to one point four gigawatts in a single year.

A gigawatt-class datacenter draws continuous power on the order of a large city, whereas a single Starship launch consumes energy measured in minutes.

The depreciation line in the AI segment reached one point eight eight five billion dollars per quarter because chips lose value on a schedule measured in years rather than the flight-proven reusability of rocket engines.

Connectivity operating leverage stems from the fact that twenty eight of the thirty eight second-quarter launches were internal Falcon flights carrying Starlink satellites at marginal cost once the vehicle and pad are already operating.

The same first-principles approach that drove Raptor engine cost reduction is now being applied to datacenter power density and networking, with the vertical integration of launch, satellite production, and compute hardware intended to compress the cost curve across both orbital and terrestrial infrastructure.

SPCX traded near one hundred seventeen dollars in the sessions after the release, below the IPO reference levels.

Forward items include the Flight fourteen tower-catch attempt window at the end of August, the Cursor acquisition close expected in the third quarter, the next two quarters of elevated capex, the ongoing lockup expiration calendar, and the trajectories for Starlink subscriber additions and compute capacity expansion toward the stated two gigawatt target by year end.

The quarter showed a launch cadence supporting internal Starlink deployment, a connectivity business generating operating income, and an AI segment achieving its first positive Adjusted EBITDA on the back of large contracted agreements.

The same period also revealed quarterly AI capex running well ahead of operating cash flow and customer concentration in the new revenue streams.

The open question left by the filing is whether the infrastructure build rate and the Starship flight cadence can both scale fast enough to convert the backlog and contracted compute into sustained cash generation.
