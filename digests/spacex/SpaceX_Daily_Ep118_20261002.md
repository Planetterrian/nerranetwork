### Engineering Deep Dive
The Google TPU test satellite illustrates the core thermal and radiation constraints of orbital compute.  
In vacuum, heat can only leave through radiation or conduction along heat pipes, so the team sized radiators to keep junction temperatures inside the Trillium operating window.  
Each chip must survive 50-to-100 g launch loads and cumulative proton fluence that can flip bits in memory; pre-flight proton-beam tests quantified the error rate and the mitigation code that will run on orbit.  
If the four-chip stack stays within power and thermal limits, the next design step becomes a larger array linked by optical inter-satellite links that could move raw training data at tens of terabits per second without ground stations.  
That architecture trades the high cost of lifting mass for the eightfold increase in solar energy available in sun-synchronous orbit, exactly the trade SpaceX’s Starship mass-to-orbit numbers are meant to improve.  
The Idiot Index here is the ratio of finished satellite cost to the raw silicon, solar cells, and aluminum radiator mass; every gram of radiation shielding or extra radiator area multiplies that index until reuse or higher launch cadence brings it down.
