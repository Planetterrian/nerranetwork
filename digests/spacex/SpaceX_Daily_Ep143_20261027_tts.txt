Holding the engine's mass line while pulling more thrust, from an engineering standpoint, is a vehicle-level choice, not a bay-level one.

Each Raptor 4's thrust-to-weight rises by about twenty percent, the equivalent of several extra Raptor 3-class engines with no added dry mass.

That extra push shows up in the first sixty to ninety seconds, when the stack is still in thick air and deep in the gravity well.

Earth's gravity pulls at about nine point eight meters per second squared.

A back-of-envelope gravity loss is on the order of six hundred to nine hundred meters per second if that opening stretch is spent fighting straight up.

Low Earth orbit is roughly seven point eight kilometers per second, so hundreds of meters per second not spent on the climb can go to payload.

Air density falls by about two-thirds every eight point five kilometers of altitude, a textbook scale height, so seconds saved early are seconds not spent in dense air.
