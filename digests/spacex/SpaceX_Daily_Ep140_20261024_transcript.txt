Starships refusal to fly in Apollo style one-shot moon trip is the engineering angle behind
those lander interiors.
Apollo's Saturn V put roughly 47 tons on a trans lunar path by discarding stages after
each burn.
Starship must return its entire 100-plus tonne upper stage and land that ship intact.
Keep that much hardware through every firing and you are accelerating a vehicle on the order
of a loaded jetliner, not a tiny capsule.
The rocket equation is exponential.
The fueled mass over the empty mass equals e to the power of delta v divided by exhaust
velocity.
Vacuum methane engines deliver an exhaust velocity on the order of 3.5 kilometers per
second.
Translunar injection, the burn out of Earth orbit, is roughly 3 kilometers per second,
so that burn alone wants a mass ratio near 2.5 before you even count landing or ascent.
Throw the tanks away, as Saturn did, and each later burn only has to push what remains.
Keep the ship and the propellant to land, leave, and still have a vehicle worth flying
again.
We'll not fit in a stack you can lift from Earth in one go.
That is why tankers exist.
They aggregate propellant in low Earth orbit, then the crude lander leaves.

in lunar orbit.
