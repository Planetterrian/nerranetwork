TakeMe2Space will fly an orbital AI computer on a Falcon 9 in three days.

This is SpaceX Daily, episode 114.

TakeMe2Space will send its MOI-1A satellite to orbit on October 1 aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 on the Transporter-18 rideshare mission.

The Hyderabad startup describes MOI-1A as India’s first orbital computing satellite because it processes data in space and returns only results rather than raw files.

The satellite weighs less than 50 kilograms and carries Nvidia Orin NX chips built for edge computing.

Twenty-three customers have already booked capacity, including mapping firms, schools, and businesses in farming, mining, supply chains, and insurance.

One customer is the U.S. firm Little Place Labs.

The satellite replaces an earlier unit lost in January when an ISRO PSLV third stage failed.

According to The Next Web, the mission lifts off in three days.

On the AI front, TakeMe2Space’s MOI-1A satellite will use Nvidia Orin NX chips to run customer-loaded AI models in orbit and return only processed results.

Twenty-three customers have booked capacity ahead of the October 1 Falcon 9 launch.

Google’s upcoming satellite test will collect the first orbital performance data on Trillium TPUs, focusing on heat rejection through pipes and radiators in vacuum.

xAI expects its models to reach parity with OpenAI and Anthropic systems by year-end as development continues.

Engineering deep dive on the orbital edge computer shows a clear power-capability trade-off.

With roughly 150 watts available, the satellite cannot host full data-center GPUs and instead uses Nvidia Orin NX chips optimized for low-power inference.

That choice keeps the finished satellite close to the raw cost of its silicon, solar cells, and structure rather than adding heavy radiators or high-wattage processors.

The 23 booked customers each load their own models, so the same 50-kilogram bus serves mapping, farming, mining, and insurance workloads without dedicated hardware per user.

In three days the mission will show whether 150 watts is enough to deliver useful results over a single pass.
