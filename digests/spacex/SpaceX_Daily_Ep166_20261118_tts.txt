For the Starship upper stage Musk walked, the engineering reality sits in the heat-shield tiles, not in the catch hardware.

Each tile has to live through the hottest stretch of reentry and still stay bonded while the airstream loads and shakes the vehicle, because a lost bond lets hot plasma reach the structure behind it.

Heating is the energy the tile must refuse; dynamic pressure is the force of the airflow trying to peel it off, and those are two different ways the same array can fail.

When the tiles come through intact, ground work shrinks to spot checks, a little sealant, and a sign-off on the thermal protection system, instead of pulling the array and building it again.

Ship forty-two completed fourteen orbits on Flight fifteen and spent twenty-two point five hours in space before the tower caught the Starship upper stage, giving engineers flight data on those tiles under real reentry heating.

Kinetic energy is one half mass times velocity squared, so at roughly seven point eight kilometers per second that is on the order of thirty megajoules per kilogram the shield must keep out of the structure.

That load is why a missing tile is not cosmetic; it is a hole in the only system standing between orbital speed and the vehicle underneath.

An intact array decides the cost, because the dominant bill on an expendable upper stage is fabricating the structure and the propulsion system once, and that bill is paid again on every flight if the vehicle is thrown away.

Fly the same Ship and each extra kilogram to orbit is mostly propellant and range time, and the Idiot Index — how many times the finished stage costs over the raw metal and propellant — falls once you stop buying a new vehicle for each payload.

Kit and procedures on the ground are scaled to flow a flown upper stage through inspection and out again without new facilities.

Repeat that cycle and upper-stage reuse stops being a structural rebuild and becomes a question of how fast you can load propellant and mate the payload.

Whether the next recovered Ship can match that inspection flow without extra tile work or swapping the flight computers is the next data point.
