From an engineering standpoint, the shift from gas generator Merlin engines to full flow
staged combustion raptors changes how propellant energy converts to thrust.
In a gas generator cycle, a separate turbine burns a small propellant stream to drive
the pumps, discarding that exhaust and limiting overall efficiency. Full flow staged combustion
routes all propellant through the turbines first, extracting more work before the main chamber burns
it, which raises specific impulse and allows higher chamber pressures without extra hardware mass.
The Raptors 617,000 pounds of thrust per engine, versus the Merlin's 190,000 pounds,
reflects that thermodynamic gain plus the switch to denser methane-oxygen propellant.
Manufacturing the more complex Raptor cycle requires tighter tolerances on turbo pump
seals and injector elements.
Yet the payoff appears in Starship's ability to carry 33 engines on a single booster,
while still targeting rapid reuse.
The same cycle also reduces the number of engine variants needed across sea level and vacuum
versions, because the core architecture stays consistent.
