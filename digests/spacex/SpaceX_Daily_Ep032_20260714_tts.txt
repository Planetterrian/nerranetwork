From an engineering standpoint, Falcon 9 first-stage reuse centers on a tightly coupled set of thermal, structural, and propulsion trades that keep the booster within its certified envelope after each flight.

The octagonal landing legs, grid fins, and Merlin 1D-Vac engines must survive repeated entry heating and landing loads while the stage’s propellant tanks remain leak-tight.

Each reuse cycle therefore reduces the marginal cost of the booster to the price of inspection, refurbishment, and the small amount of additional propellant needed for the recovery burns.

The engineering limit is not thrust or propellant load but the cumulative damage to the interstage, raceway covers, and tank domes from repeated entry heating and saltwater exposure.
