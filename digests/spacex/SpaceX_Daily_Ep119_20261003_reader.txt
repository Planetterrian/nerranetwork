From an engineering standpoint the shift from gas-generator Merlin engines to full-flow staged combustion Raptors changes how propellant energy converts to thrust.

In a gas-generator cycle a separate turbine burns a small propellant stream to drive the pumps, discarding that exhaust and limiting overall efficiency.

Full-flow staged combustion routes all propellant through the turbines first, extracting more work before the main chamber burns it, which raises specific impulse and allows higher chamber pressures without extra hardware mass.

The Raptor’s 617,000 pounds of thrust per engine versus the Merlin’s 190,000 pounds reflects that thermodynamic gain plus the switch to denser methane-oxygen propellant.

Manufacturing the more complex Raptor cycle requires tighter tolerances on turbopump seals and injector elements, yet the payoff appears in Starship’s ability to carry thirty-three engines on a single booster while still targeting rapid reuse.

The same cycle also reduces the number of engine variants needed across sea-level and vacuum versions because the core architecture stays consistent.

Raw material costs for a single Raptor sit far below the finished engine price, creating an Idiot Index that SpaceX aims to compress through iteration and volume production.
