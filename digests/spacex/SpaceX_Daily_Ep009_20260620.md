### Engineering Deep Dive
The Mechazilla tower arms are being modified with tighter alignment tolerances and faster quick-disconnect hardware. From first principles, the catch sequence must absorb the booster’s residual horizontal velocity while the vehicle is still descending at several meters per second; any misalignment of even a few centimeters multiplies the lateral loads the tower legs must carry.  

Reducing the time required to reset the arms after each attempt directly shrinks the “Idiot Index” of the entire ground system—the ratio of total facility cost to the raw steel, hydraulics, and sensors inside it. Faster resets also raise the number of catch attempts that can be scheduled inside a single launch window, which is the practical limit on how quickly the program can close the rapid-reuse learning loop.  

If the updated geometry proves reliable, the next measurable milestone will be a successful catch on a flight test rather than a hover or soft-landing demonstration; that single data point would collapse the uncertainty around whether tower capture can ever become routine.
