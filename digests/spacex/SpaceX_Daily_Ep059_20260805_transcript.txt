For the first half of the year, the net loss reached $4.817 billion, but that figure includes
a $1.545 billion loss on debt extinguishment and a $671 million deemed dividend tied to

The company conducted 38 launches in the quarter, 10 for customers and 28 internal, lifting
485 metric tons to orbit. The operating loss grew because development
costs for the next-generation vehicle outpaced launch revenue growth in the period.
Connectivity delivered the clearest profit contribution, with revenue of $4.291 billion,
up 66% year over year, operating income of $1.656 billion, up 79%, and adjusted E. Bittda

doubling from a year earlier and adding $1.7 million in the quarter, while average revenue
per user held steady at $66 per month after the deliberate reduction from $85 a year ago
to expand the addressable market. Consumer revenue grew 44% to $2.485 billion, while
enterprise and government revenue more than doubled to $1.806 billion.
The AI segment posted revenue of $2.561 billion, up 247% year over year, and 213% sequentially
driven by new cloud services agreements that lifted AI solutions and infrastructure revenue

E.E. Bittda of $1.146 billion, compared with a negative $276 million a year earlier, even

research and development outlays. Two successful starship V3 flight tests occurred in the quarter,

the first suborbital mission profile, and a precision upper-stage landing along with
modified V2 Starlink satellite deployment. Flight 13 met all objectives including deployment

splashdown recorded with an intact heat shield. On the earnings call, Musk stated the next flight,

From an engineering standpoint, the $15.828 billion in quarterly AI infrastructure

A gigawatt class data center draws continuous power on the order of a large city,
whereas a single starship launch consumes energy measured in minutes.
