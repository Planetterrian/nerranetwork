### Top News
**Thousands of Nvidia staff sign to bring Huang back: The Information**
A petition urging Nvidia’s board to reinstate Jensen Huang passed 100,000 signatures on Saturday, The Information reported, including 5,248 people who said they are current employees. Signers named there include Elon Musk, Epic Games’ Tim Sweeney and Y Combinator’s Garry Tan. By Saturday afternoon TechCrunch put the total above 115,000, with the petition site listing 5,965 current employees, 1,108 former employees and 63,063 shareholders, plus Replit CEO Amjad Masad among notable supporters. The text says signers are concerned Huang was removed without meaningful due process, demands that the board bring him back while an independent investigation establishes the facts, and says he deserves due process, not mob justice. Huang resigned Thursday, four days after confessing to an affair with a chip executive suspected by U.S. intelligence of being a Chinese spy, and he has agreed to testify before Congress. Nvidia named CFO Colette Kress interim CEO while it searches for a permanent replacement. ### The Thread: A capacity upgrade, then a thundering herd
Microsoft’s Microsoft 365 failure lasted about 30 hours, and the company now says the break was introduced during an earlier network capacity upgrade, then showed up after a more recent network change. GeekWire, reading Microsoft’s incident log, puts the disruption at about 30 hours across more than 70 countries. TechXplore puts products offline in at least 74 countries and territories in the Americas, Europe, Asia and Africa. The services named were Outlook, Teams and Copilot. Microsoft called it a technical issue, not cyber and not related to AI or agents, and on Saturday said the remaining customers and regions had service again.

Charles Lamanna, executive vice president of Copilot, Agents and Platform, said cascading failures and thundering herds at the network perimeter made recovery harder than normal. In his account a thundering herd is huge numbers of devices reconnecting at once and overwhelming systems as they return. Microsoft told customers a preliminary post-incident report would come within two business days and a final report within five. He said the write-up would include a step-by-step timeline and many repair items, and that the company would be very specific.

The load on that perimeter is a January number Microsoft reported: more than 450 million paid commercial Microsoft 365 seats. GeekWire’s reading is roughly one seat for every 18 people on Earth. Unity Health Toronto declared a Code Grey and said patient care continued. Pennsylvania lawmakers went about 18 hours without email. A Colorado jail could not take online bond payments. Business Upturn, citing Nikkei, said securities firms and asset managers in Japan fell back on fax machines when trade-confirmation email stopped. New York’s Emergency Management office warned of delays reaching city agencies. In Singapore some government systems, including email, were disrupted. In London the UK Supreme Court was not receiving email.

It was at least the fourth significant outage of this kind this year. A January outage lasted nearly 10 hours. In July a bug in Microsoft’s automated network maintenance system disrupted Azure and Microsoft 365 for hours. On August 31 an Exchange Online outage also hit Teams, OneDrive and SharePoint. GeekWire sets those failures next to earlier incidents at Amazon Web Services: a second cloud operator, the same class of customer dependence. Friday’s FAA ground stop was not part of the Microsoft incident. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no connection, and Microsoft said it had no indication the ground stop involved any Microsoft service. Federal officials blamed aging FAA telecom gear.

What the two companies share is the reconnect problem, not this week’s fault. The incident account gives Amazon no role in the upgrade. Customers still treat both perimeters as one risk, because a network change at either can drop mail, chat and an assistant together, then worsen as devices rush back. Microsoft owes the specific repair list its Copilot executive already promised. Amazon inherits the comparison buyers will use the next time its own change window fails.
