### Top News
**4.4 gigawatts on a retired Pennsylvania coal plant: The Information**
The Information visited an Amazon data-center project this week near the economically depressed borough of Homer City, on a site that once held a 2-gigawatt coal-fired plant. The old plant employed thousands and released coal dust and arsenic. The project is described as gas-fired power into 36 buildings for the AI of Anthropic or others, the kind of site few companies can marshal resources for as readily as Amazon. Source: [theinformation.com](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/amazon-plays-long-expensive-data-center-game-neoclouds)

**Meta AI chief says superintelligence is coming: Calcalist**
Meta AI chief Alexandr Wang says AI systems could soon surpass human intelligence across almost every field and eventually improve themselves at a pace people cannot control, and that next year will be critical. He pointed to a reported proof of the Riemann Hypothesis — a problem he said occupied leading mathematicians for about 150 years, and a harder intellectual challenge, in his telling, than the atomic bomb he grew up beside in Los Alamos — and compared it with OpenAI's o3 model solving hard AIME problems in December 2024. He said coding, cybersecurity and limited-oversight task systems have advanced, with the remaining barriers in specialist knowledge, infrastructure, experimentation and data, and that AI could outperform human specialists in AI research, biological research, weapons development and economics. Maintaining control over unrestricted self-improvement, or over systems potentially 1,000 times more capable than humans, would be difficult, he said; he rejected the idea that superintelligence is inevitable and said society should not hand control to machines. **Siri layoffs spared newer AI systems: MacRumors**
Bloomberg's Mark Gurman says Apple's recent Siri layoffs hit people working on the assistant's older infrastructure, not its newer AI systems, and that employees have yet to notice a major change in how Apple operates. Inside Craig Federighi's software engineering organization, an internal release calendar labeled A through G ties work to software versions; depending on the release, the roadmap for a major operating-system update is largely set by late summer or early fall of the year before, which Gurman says leaves little room to answer rivals that ship far more often than Apple's annual cycle. He also described Siri as slow, thinly tied to third-party apps, and worse at dictation. A more advanced Apple AI agent is not expected before late 2027; multistep control of Apple's own apps is expected in December, with third-party app support in 2027. Gurman said Apple's privacy reputation and deeper platform access could still help it compete as rival agents collect preferences and connected accounts. ### Company Desk
**Samsung and Google set a December 10 US launch for AI glasses: UC Today**
Samsung says its intelligent eyewear, built with Google, will launch in the US on December 10, after pre-orders that opened on November 12, with Gemini, real-time translation, messaging, navigation, visual assistance and image capture. Cortney Harding, founder and CEO of Friends With Holograms, told UC Today the capabilities are not fully new, but that wearability and a deep tie to Google's own workplace tools set the glasses apart from Meta and Snap for companies already on Google Workspace. She expects office workers, not industrial crews, among the earliest users — the design is lightweight and not rugged, with what she called a good battery life — and pointed to translation, plus a Samsung scenario in which a wearer captures a whiteboard, asks Gemini to summarise it, and saves the notes to Samsung Notes. Gemini alone is not the deciding feature, she said; the larger step would be letting a company attach its own internal models, which she expects over time. Person recognition, she added, stays contentious unless it is a clear double opt-in in a controlled setting. Source: [uctoday.com](https://uctoday.com/are-samsung-and-googles-ai-glasses-ready-for-work)

### The Thread: The screen an assistant can still read
On 20 November, Signal president Meredith Whittaker announced protections that stop Apple's Siri and Google's Gemini from reading chats displayed on a phone. On iPhones running iOS 27 or later, Signal 8.37 or later enables the block by default, under Settings, Privacy, Block Siri AI. That control also blocks screenshots and screen recordings in the app and hides Signal content in the App Switcher. If the app blocks a screenshot, a prompt offers ten minutes with protection off, and during that interval Siri may scan the visible chat. On Android the build is Signal 8.35.1. Screen security switches on the first time the app detects an assistant trying to read the screen, or a user can enable it in advance under Settings, Privacy, App security, Screen security. Turn it off through Signal's prompt and the app restores it after 90 days; turn it off manually in Settings and Signal says it will leave that choice alone. Active protection on Android also blocks screenshots and obscures the conversation in the recent-apps view. Microsoft met this problem earlier: in May 2025 Signal introduced screenshot blocking on Windows 11 to keep Recall from saving images of conversations. For macOS 27, Signal says it cannot provide equivalent screenshot blocking and recommends adding Signal under System Settings, Siri, Excluded Apps.

Those version lines sit on one mechanism. End-to-end encryption protects a Signal message only while it travels. Once the recipient opens the thread, the phone must show the words. An assistant that can see the screen can read the same words, without intercepting the message or obtaining a key. In a group chat, one person's assistant can capture lines written by several other people who never chose that assistant. Google's guide to Gemini screen actions says that with Intelligent Screen Context enabled, Gemini can infer when to use what is visible, and Google provides controls for screen text and screenshots. Apple's guide lists onscreen awareness among Siri's capabilities on compatible iPhones. Whittaker says engineers spent months identifying routes they could verify, and that other routes may exist, so a developer still cannot give users a complete account of what an assistant can reach. Signal is asking Apple, Google and Microsoft to document those routes and to let developers exclude an app's content across screens, notifications, files and software interfaces.

Apple's phone block does not follow the user to the Mac, so a person who shut Siri out of the mobile app still has to exclude Signal on the desktop or leave proactive access in place. Google's screen-aware Gemini hits a stop inside this app once Signal detects the read, unless the user opts out in the way the app has said it will remember. Microsoft's earlier desktop rule is the template the phones now follow: the price of keeping an assistant off the conversation is that an ordinary screenshot is treated as a leak. What changes is the limit of a privacy promise that used to stop at encryption. Apple, Google and Microsoft can still ship assistants that see a screen; users of this one app can refuse that sight; and nobody in a group thread can treat another member's assistant settings as someone else's problem. The step Whittaker wants from the three platform owners is a documented exclusion that covers every route an assistant uses. Source: [linkedin.com](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/signal-now-blocks-siri-gemini-from-reading-private-htgee)
