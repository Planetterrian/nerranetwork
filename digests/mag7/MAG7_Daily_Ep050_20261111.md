### Top News
**Three contract workers die at Tata's iPhone-parts plant in Hosur: MacRumors**
Three contract workers died Monday at a Tata Electronics factory in Hosur, Tamil Nadu, that manufactures iPhone components, Reuters reported. They were performing maintenance at a wastewater treatment plant and inhaled gas while inspecting a nano-filtration tank. Apple, Tata Electronics, and state officials will look into the incident, and Apple is sending a team to assist. In 2024 the same facility had a fire in a chemical storage area that disrupted production for several days. Tata Electronics was also the target of a cyberattack earlier this year in which hackers stole confidential Apple files. Source: [macrumors.com](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/11/10/india-tata-electronics-gas-incident/)

**Google Cloud announces Gemini for Healthcare: Distilled Post**
Google Cloud's Gemini for Healthcare, announced in November 2026, is built to connect fragmented clinical and administrative systems and to retrieve and synthesise information across infrastructure organisations already run, rather than forcing a replacement of electronic health records. Stated capabilities include preparing patient records, identifying medication discrepancies, and automating documentation for insurance authorisations and appeals. Google estimates that clinicians spend nearly 28 hours each week on manual documentation and administrative tasks; Distilled Post notes that figure comes from Google's own research on the US system and should not be treated as an NHS measurement. England's 10 Year Health Plan, published in July 2025, puts a single patient record, greater use of the NHS App, and wider AI-supported admin tools at the centre of the shift from analogue to digital. Unite.AI reports that Google Cloud has opened a Gemini for Healthcare preview in the United States. Distilled Post adds that an inaccurate clinical summary can create extra verification work, so the test it urges on NHS adopters is documentation time, referral processing, and time spent with patients, not the fact of automation. Source: [distilledpost.com](https://www.distilledpost.com/post/what-googles-gemini-for-healthcare-means-for-digital-reform) Source: [unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/google-cloud-opens-gemini-for-healthcare-preview-in-the-u-s/)

### The Thread: The filing prices a stake, not a vote
Around 20 percent of Anthropic is the share Amazon could own if a ceiling the company set for itself did not apply. The Information, reading Anthropic's updated IPO filing from Tuesday, says Amazon could hold around 20 percent of outstanding shares, and will not hold more than 12 percent because of that self-imposed cap. The portion above the cap is not discarded. It sits in convertible notes that can turn into equity over time, so the economic interest can keep growing after the share ceiling binds. Set the cap aside, The Information calculates, and that 20-percent-or-so position would be worth about $258 billion at the midpoint of Anthropic's proposed IPO pricing range. The capital behind the position totals around $18.6 billion. A Financial Times report the same night suggested the final IPO price may come in higher than the preliminary range. That is a report about a range that has not cleared, not a price Anthropic has set.

Google is the other of the seven in the same filing. The Information says the Tuesday update shows how many shares each of the two will own, and that both stakes are significant. It also says neither Amazon nor Google will own voting shares. The piece draws a line from that structure to antitrust: because there is no vote, authorities would not have to worry about either company's influence over Anthropic's management. That is The Information's reading of the filing, not a ruling. What the two companies share is the lab they funded early, and a public-market holding that does not come with control.

The cap is the operational fact. Amazon can be a very large economic holder and still stay at or under 12 percent of the shares that count toward the ceiling, with the overflow parked in notes that convert later. A convertible note is a promise to become stock, not stock today, so the cap can hold on the shares Amazon is allowed to keep while the notes wait. Google, on this account, is in the same non-voting category. A vote would have raised the management question the filing's structure is written to avoid. An economic stake does not, by itself, put either company's executives in the room when Anthropic decides a model, a price, or a customer. The Information is explicit that the non-voting design is what keeps the antitrust worry about influence over management off the table. It does not describe a board seat, a veto, or a joint operating agreement in the excerpt of the filing it walks through.

What changes for Amazon is the form of a commitment already made. The $18.6 billion is spent capital. The $258 billion is The Information's midpoint math on a proposed range, and that mark moves if the range's final print moves. The Financial Times report is the only indication in today's material that the print might be higher, and it does not replace the filing. What changes for Google is parallel: a significant stake, and no vote of the kind that would carry management rights. Neither company, on the filing as The Information describes it, picks up control of Anthropic in exchange for the early cheques. The report names no listing day. Source: [theinformation.com](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/amazon-google-win-big-anthropic-ipo)
