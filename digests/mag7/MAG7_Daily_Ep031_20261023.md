> **Only one iPhone got iOS 27.1, so every other model skips straight to 27.2.**

**What You Need to Know:** Only one iPhone got iOS 27.1, so every other model skips straight to 27.2. Apple pushed that build to the iPhone Duo alone and pointed the rest of the lineup at iOS 27.2 next week. Elsewhere, Microsoft pulled eight Copilot actions out of Windows 11 File Explorer, and a Ninth Circuit ruling left an Apple TV copyright verdict in place.

### Top News
**iOS 27.1 is exclusive to iPhone Duo: 9to5Mac**
Apple released iOS 27.1 over the air, and 9to5Mac reports it is exclusive to the iPhone Duo. On other iPhones, Software Update still shows iOS 27.0.1 as the newest build, and those models skip 27.1 for iOS 27.2. 9to5Mac says 27.2 has been in beta for the last month and ships to all users next week. The same report gives two reasons for the split: skipping a beta cycle kept Duo details hidden until launch, and custom Duo designs were easier to manage without other models in the mix. In 27.2, Dual Capture — front and rear cameras at once, started from the Flip button — extends from one-on-one FaceTime to Group FaceTime. Apple Podcasts splits the old Up Next queue into New Episodes, for the latest releases of shows you follow, and Continue Playing, for in-progress shows. The TV app adds user profiles on iPhone, including a child profile limited to age-appropriate series and movies, and the selection screen can be turned off. 9to5Mac also lists an Apple Music app icon and updated design, plus the Podcasts queue overhaul, among the 27.2 features. **CSIRO queries genomic variants on AWS without a central copy: InfoQ**
CSIRO’s Serverless Beacon, sBeacon, queries genomic variant data on Amazon Web Services without copying large datasets into a central database. Variant Call Format files stay in Amazon S3; metadata such as disease status, age, and location is indexed separately, with Athena tables built at onboarding and ontology terms handled through CSIRO’s Ontoserver. API Gateway sends requests to Lambda; the Variant Querying Module uses bcftools and htslib to pull file ranges over HTTP byte-range requests, then returns results through the GA4GH Beacon API. On chromosome 1 from the 1000 Genomes Project — 2,504 samples in a 1.1 GB compressed VCF — onboarding took 18 seconds. A query covering 10,000 base pairs averaged 1.52 seconds at an estimated compute cost of 0.00013 dollars, and latency stayed broadly consistent as matching variants rose from four to 400. Anuradha Wickramarachchi, solutions architect for bioinformatics products at CSIRO, said querying data while it stays compressed is greatly beneficial. Earlier sBeacon research in Nature Biotechnology reported support for up to 40 million individuals and 3 billion genomic locations. Source: [infoq.com](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/10/csiro-serverless-genomic-variant/)

### The Thread: Four signatures, one lawful-purpose clause
Three of the seven signed the same kind of door this year. Wocult, citing the BBC, reports that Google, Microsoft and Amazon — and OpenAI, which is not one of the seven — signed agreements with the Pentagon in 2026 to deploy AI in classified military environments. The Google deal, signed earlier this year, allows its models to be used for classified work including what the contract describes as any lawful governmental purpose. US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth has made AI integration in the armed forces a stated priority. The shared fact is permission: a public-company model, once inside that environment, is available for lawful governmental use, and the companies named are the ones that already sell cloud and model access at scale.

What is new against yesterday’s resignations is who left and what they said the contract does not let them stop. Research engineer Sofia Liguori, based in London, left this week after three years. She told the BBC, in a personal capacity, that she had lost faith in Google’s stated ethical values over growing military work, and that her employment contract restricts her from criticising the company in public effectively for life. AI researcher Andreas Kirsch resigned earlier in October. He told the BBC that Google’s governance and oversight were not sufficient or meaningful enough to deploy AI safely in military and classified settings, and that the company appeared to have no right to control or veto how the Pentagon uses its technology. In June, René Mayrhofer, then a director for Android platform security, resigned and published a farewell letter saying leaving had become unavoidable and that Google management had lost its moral compass. Current employees told the BBC they fear retaliation and feel unable to speak openly about AI safety or the military direction. Google told the BBC it follows a consistent process for reviewing public-sector contracts in line with its acceptable-use policies and AI principles, and that it has dedicated channels for staff to raise concerns with protections against retaliation.

The split inside the seven is not a split in the signature list. Alphabet is the company whose staff are leaving in public. Microsoft and Amazon are named in the same BBC account as signatories of Pentagon agreements this year, without the named resignations attached to them in this report. OpenAI is the fourth name on that list. For Alphabet, the operational change is a classified-use right the company told the BBC it reviews under existing principles, set against three dated exits — June, earlier in October, and this week — and a claim, from Kirsch, that Google cannot veto Pentagon use. For Microsoft and Amazon, the report’s change is the same class of agreement: models in classified military environments, tied to Hegseth’s priority, with no separate remedy or opt-out described in the piece. The next dated mark the sources actually give is not a hearing. It is the pattern of 2026 signatures already on the page, and the October exits that are still being named.
