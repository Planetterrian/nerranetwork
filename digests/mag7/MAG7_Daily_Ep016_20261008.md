### Top News
**Google releases new AI detection tool: The Independent**
Google has released SynthID Detector so people can check whether text, image, audio, or video was made with AI, The Independent reports. The check reads imperceptible watermarks Google says it has already applied to more than 180 billion images and videos, plus 240,000 years of audio. The tool is available through Google DeepMind. Marks are meant to stay readable after a crop or a filter, without changing what a person sees or hears. The release lines up with the EU AI Act, which requires generative platforms to make outputs identifiable to automated systems. Source: [independent.co.uk](https://www.independent.co.uk/bulletin/news/google-synthid-detector-ai-detection-tool-b3063389.html)

**AWS takes aim at runaway AI agent behavior with Strands Box: InfoWorld**
Amazon Web Services has put out Strands Box, an open-source sandbox that pairs operating-system isolation with policies on what an agent may do next, InfoWorld reports. It landed in developer preview on October 7 under the Apache 2.0 license, and it currently runs on Macs with Apple silicon on macOS 15 or later. The Dogwood policy language and its evaluation engine can use an agent's recorded activity across tools, so a file read through a shell can trigger limits on later network requests. In AWS's own example, an incident agent may post to a Slack channel no more than three times every 10 minutes, and Box enforces the cap. Checks cover the shell, Python interpreters, and the Model Context Protocol broker; the network gateway can attach credentials to approved requests without showing those secrets to the agent. Pareekh Jain, CEO of Pareekh Consulting, said the underlying technologies are not new and that the gain is consistent enforcement across agent frameworks. Files opened through a harness's own tools stay under operating-system limits and are not scored by Dogwood. Source: [infoworld.com](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4232439/aws-takes-aim-at-runaway-ai-agent-behavior-with-strands-box.html)

**Meta AI ad checks put destinations under scrutiny: ContentGrip**
Meta on October 7 added AI checks to ad review aimed at ads that look harmless and then send people to prohibited material off the platform, ContentGrip reports. Meta said it had investigated ads used to point people to child sexual exploitation material outside Facebook and Instagram, disabled violating accounts, and blocked the external links. The new layer includes large-language-model detection for suspected covert signposting, a clearer read on where an ad leads, extra AI sweeps for misses, an AI agent that tests Meta's defenses, and stronger checks for people who return after an account is removed. Meta also reported 33.2 million pieces of violating content actioned globally on Facebook and Instagram from January to June 2026. Those totals cover more than paid ads and predate the October announcement, so they are not a score for the new ad tools. Source: [contentgrip.com](https://www.contentgrip.com/meta-ai-ad-destinations/)

### The Counterpoint
**The public check stops at five named generators: The Independent**
The detector currently works on media from Google, OpenAI, Nvidia, Kakao, and Apple. That list is the bound on a tool that reads watermarks those platforms embedded, not a scan of every synthetic file on the web. The EU AI Act's identify-the-output rule is what the launch is lined up against; reach beyond those five names is the open question, and the partner list is the number that decides it. Source: [independent.co.uk](https://www.independent.co.uk/bulletin/news/google-synthid-detector-ai-detection-tool-b3063389.html)

### The Thread: Eight hundred developers, still a human in the loop
Nearly 800 software developers filed into a San Francisco hall last week for a conference hosted by Modal, and the argument on stage is a split that now runs across the seven. Scott Wu, whose coding-agent startup Cognition reached a $48 billion valuation last month, told them to be ambitious: virtual employees that work on outcomes instead of tasks. His turning point, he said, was the AIME moment last year, when models began outperforming humans on the high-level math contests he used to enter. The Information reports that OpenAI said Tuesday an unreleased model had solved or made major progress on hundreds of significant problems in math and computer science. Later that afternoon Diogo Almeida, a former OpenAI researcher who now runs TypeSafe AI, asked the same room where the automation is. His account was that large language models today are optimized for assistance with reinforcement learning from human feedback, so they are trained to please a person in the loop and fail at unattended work. Cat Wu, head of product for Claude Code at Anthropic, put a ratio on the stall: the model does 80 percent of the work, a human still does 20 percent, and that 20 percent repeats. Dax Raad, who built OpenCode, said the failure he is fighting is human — engineers not paying enough attention to code the models write. Microsoft on Wednesday unveiled its latest effort to run AI in Windows PCs rather than in the cloud, which the company said would bring costs down for customers. Anjney Midha, a former general partner at Andreessen Horowitz, and former executives at Google, Apple, and Nvidia have launched a company aimed at making that compute easier and more affordable for smaller companies and startups.
