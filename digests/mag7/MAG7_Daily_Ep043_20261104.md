### Top News
**Google will turn student Search and Gemini off unless schools opt in: The SOFX Report**
Starting next year, Google will leave Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom, and Google Search off for student accounts aged five to eighteen unless an administrator turns a service on. Chris Phillips, vice president of education, told The New York Times that Search and AI are deeply integrated, and that a school which bans AI will not have access to Search. New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani said Google is trying to use monopoly power to force AI into public schools. Former Federal Trade Commission chair Lina Khan said Google is using its search monopoly to coerce schools into adopting AI, and that it will punish those who do not fall in line. The Decoder put the Search consequence in 2027. Google said schools should get more information on the change in the coming months. ### Company Desk
**Washington notice lists 95 Amazon Stores cuts, not a new round: GeekWire**
A notice made public Tuesday lists about 95 Amazon job cuts in Washington state, and a spokesperson told GeekWire they are part of previously reported Stores layoffs, not a new round. The filing names software engineers, product managers, and program managers, mostly in Seattle, with others in Bellevue, Redmond, Sumner, Pasco, or remote. Reuters had reported fewer than 1,000 corporate cuts, mainly in Stores, affecting the United States, India, and the United Kingdom. Amazon eliminated about 14,000 corporate roles globally in October 2025, including 2,303 in Washington, then a January round that included 2,198 Washington employees. Spokesperson Brad Glasser said the company adjusted parts of Stores so the structure would better match its priorities. Separately, Amazon announced roughly 300,000 seasonal openings in U.S. operations, with health coverage from January 1, 2027, for seasonal employees working at least 30 hours a week, plans starting at $5 a week with $5 copays, after a September announcement of more than $1.5 billion in additional pay for U.S. operations employees. ### The Counterpoint
**Google says schools can switch Search on without taking every AI service: The SOFX Report**
Against the reading that a school which refuses AI loses Search, Google’s news account said the day’s changes give schools more choice, not less: Gemini, Gemini Notebook, and Search start off, and administrators can turn on one, two, or all three individually. The same post said AI Mode and AI Overviews in school Search will offer step-by-step guided learning so students do not get quick answers to schoolwork. That clarification is the fact that decides whether Phillips’s “deeply integrated” line still means Search cannot be separated from Gemini. Source: [sofx.com](https://www.sofx.com/google-to-block-search-access-for-schools-that-disable-ai-features/)

### The Thread: Cheques, sites, and a chip book that will not fill
Alphabet’s Google plans to spend $15 billion to expand data-center and cloud operations in Malaysia, Bloomberg reported, with the AI boom cited as the demand pulling that infrastructure. The same country, the same day, got a different Amazon move: BusinessToday said AWS launched Semarak AI Malaysia, a program with three pillars to drive AI adoption. One figure is a construction budget. The other is an adoption program, and the launch line does not attach a dollar total to those three pillars.

Meta is raising money rather than breaking ground in that story. Bloomberg said Meta has started its first euro-denominated bond sale and is set to pay generous yields, after a $15 billion dollar deal. The wire put that debut inside a race in which the biggest technology companies are financing trillions of dollars of investment in artificial intelligence. The euro sale does not add a Malaysian hall. It adds a second currency after the dollar raise, and it prices the funding at a yield Bloomberg called generous.

Nvidia’s bind is production. Yesterday the company told cloud clients to take on less China business. Today Bloomberg reported that Chinese AI developers expect to receive 1 percent of the $70 billion in advanced RTX chips they have ordered over the next several months, because Nvidia is struggling to lift output against soaring demand. AMD, which the same wire called a key challenger to Nvidia in AI chips, forecast fourth-quarter revenue of $17 billion, plus or minus 3 percent. Bloomberg read that outlook as fresh evidence that data-center processor demand is still strong. The AMD figure is a rival’s sales line, not a count of Nvidia boxes landing in China.

On the U.S. side of the land rush, MyNorthwest identified AWS as the buyer behind a $264 million data-center land deal in West Richland, Washington. That is a site purchase. It is not a switched-on cluster, and it is not the Malaysian program.

The shared constraint is the AI capacity race: power and buildings on one side, chips and the debt that pays for both on the other. Google is committing owned cloud capacity in Malaysia. Amazon is buying land in Washington and, separately, wrapping an adoption push around Malaysia. Meta is testing whether euro investors will fund the same build after the dollar deal. Nvidia cannot turn the China slice of that RTX order book into shipments at anything like the order size, even as a challenger’s quarterly outlook says buyers who can get processors are still spending.

What changes is the queue, not a single campus opening. Malaysian capacity becomes a Google spend. Euro bond buyers become a new price on Meta’s AI funding. Chinese developers holding RTX orders are the customers for whom almost the whole book does not convert on this run. Amazon’s West Richland outlay secures dirt, not racks. No source today dates a ribbon-cutting on either site. The clock a wire actually set is the delivery window on those fractional Nvidia shipments.
