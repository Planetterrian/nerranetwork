Google DeepMind's Koray Kavukcuoglu, in Mashable's account of Thursday's announcement, describes Hydrogen as a speed cut of Gemini 4 Carbon, built to write code, run tests and do ordinary work, or to sit beside Carbon and Argon as the fast engine.

Analytics India Magazine has Google's lab card under that claim: at the extra-high thinking setting the index score rises to 54, and at the default high level Google says the model finishes tasks two and a half times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash. On Terminal-Bench 4.1 it scored 57 percent, against 18.2 percent for that Flash model.

The sticker is one dollar and fifty cents per million input tokens and seven dollars and fifty cents per million output tokens, across a window of up to one million tokens, and it is going into Search, the Gemini app, the API, AI Studio and Gemini for business.

An Orchestrator in Antigravity switches models by task and, on Terminal-Bench 4.0, stayed near Carbon's success rate while burning 24 percent less quota and finishing 19 percent faster. Google also says it is twice as unlikely as 3.8 Flash to step past a permission line, and that it still invents things.

Apple is putting HomeView and a fourth-generation Apple TV 4K in buyers' hands, MacRumors reports, each at two hundred ninety-nine dollars after pre-orders that started in mid-October. HomeView is the smart-home display, tabletop or on the wall, running Apple Intelligence and Siri AI, and it can tell up to six faces apart, each with its own Home Screen.

The new Apple TV box adds Siri AI, the A19 chip, a Siri Remote that works with Find My, one hundred twenty-eight gigabytes of storage, and four-speaker surround with HomePod mini 2. Apple announced the pair on October thirteenth, the same day as HomePod mini 2, which reached stores on October thirtieth.

HomeView is not for sale in the European Union, because Siri AI is still unavailable on iOS there under the current rules, and Apple says it is working constructively with the EU to find a path forward.

Nvidia's board says Jensen Huang has resigned at once as president, chief executive and director. Yesterday the company was already hunting a chief executive; today Colette Kress, finance chief since September 2013 after nearly twenty-five years in finance at Microsoft and Cisco, holds the job on an interim basis and keeps the finance role, while a formal search looks inside and outside.

Huang co-founded the company in 1993, and the exit followed reports of an alleged affair with a suspected Chinese spy. The Information says general counsel Tim Teter went to Huang's home, asked whether that report was true, and was told it was.

Harvey Jones, former chief of Synopsys, becomes Nvidia's first chair, and he says the board expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue without a break. UC Today runs the Exchange incident from sixteen seventeen GMT Thursday to a zero seven twenty-five GMT Friday note that still had the suite degraded, more than fifteen hours in. At seventeen fifty-four GMT Microsoft called it an Americas problem; by eighteen oh seven it said any region could lose connectivity; at eighteen twenty-six it opened an incident for the rest of the suite.

The extra load was not extra traffic. By nineteen eighteen GMT Microsoft had decided a large set of servers failed automated health checks and dropped out, dumping work on whatever was left. A process that sets up secure connections got overwhelmed, and those servers stopped taking requests.

At nineteen oh six GMT deployments and configuration changes were paused, and engineers started rolling back a network change that lined up with the start of the break. Boxes they brought back seized again as soon as traffic returned. At four ten GMT Friday, recovery steps in some regions were not sticking.

At five oh one the mitigations had thrown a surge of calls from routing services at the backend, and some machines were stuck, which blocked a force-sync of that network change. At six fifty-six GMT the company had only marginal success after patches and reboots.

Teams came apart along the Exchange seam. Joining calls and meetings, group chats, channel messages and ordinary channels mostly kept going. Calendars, scheduling, invites, meeting edits, Live and Virtual events, contact sync, and creating or deleting private or shared channels did not.

Sharing a SharePoint file with someone outside the organization failed, even while the files themselves stayed put. A separate North America Teams incident, fifteen forty to seventeen oh nine GMT Thursday, was fully closed. Azure's public status page showed no Azure trouble.

Dynamics 365 locked contact-center agents out for just under eight hours, and Microsoft gave that access back at zero fifty-seven GMT Friday, with email already down as the backup channel.
