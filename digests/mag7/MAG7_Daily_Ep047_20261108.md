# MAG 7 Daily
> **A five-hundred-forty-nine-dollar leaked iPad twelve would put Apple Intelligence on the budget tablet.**

**What You Need to Know:** A leak reported by Geeky Gadgets and BigGo Finance puts Apple’s next entry iPad at a $549 start so the cheap tablet can finally run Apple Intelligence. New disclosures, the Financial Times says, show Apple saved $2.7 billion in tax by booking profits in Singapore, which has overtaken Ireland as its biggest shelter. Beside that: an empty office on Nvidia’s China chip list, and a basic Kindle Amazon has made lighter and clearer while charging more.

### Top News
**Leaked iPad 12 specs add an A19, 8GB and a $549 start: BigGo Finance**
Geeky Gadgets, citing a SaranByte video, and BigGo Finance describe an unconfirmed leak for an iPad 12 aimed at a spring 2027 window. The device is expected to use an A19 processor and 8GB of RAM, against the iPad 11’s A16 and 6GB, a combination the reports say clears Apple’s hardware bar for Apple Intelligence, including real-time language translation, predictive text and enhanced photo editing. Connectivity in the leak is Apple’s C1X modem for faster 5G and an N1 chipset with Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6 and Thread. The panel stays a non-laminated LCD. Analysts cited in the reports expect the start price at $549. Apple has not commented; BigGo Finance says the specifications are leaked and analyst projections, not confirmed by Apple, and that the spring 2027 window could shift. Source: [finance.biggo.com](https://finance.biggo.com/news/1c8ae464-c605-4cfc-8f71-f5a990aafdbc) · [geeky-gadgets.com](https://www.geeky-gadgets.com/ipad-12-release-date/)

**Apple saves $2.7 billion in tax as Singapore overtakes Ireland: Financial Times**
The Financial Times reports that Apple saved $2.7 billion in taxes by booking profits in Singapore. New disclosures, the paper says, show the city-state has overtaken Ireland as the iPhone maker’s biggest tax shelter. Source: [ft.com](https://www.ft.com/content/49453406-99f2-4f5b-8f5d-01dc8cac5ede?syn-25a6b1a6=1)

### The Counterpoint
**The budget case fails if buyers step up $150 to OLED**
The leak’s own price path is the case against treating a $549 iPad 12 as the volume on-ramp. BigGo Finance says the iPad 11 already climbed from $349 to $449, and that a $549 start would be a 57 percent increase over the entry price of two generations ago, a pace it says risks the students, families and casual users who made the base iPad the default. The same report keeps a non-laminated LCD on the entry model while the OLED iPad Mini sits at $699, shrinking that gap to $150, and says a buyer may decide the step up is worth it — higher average selling prices, a thinner budget tier. Source: [finance.biggo.com](https://finance.biggo.com/news/1c8ae464-c605-4cfc-8f71-f5a990aafdbc)

### The Thread: A Malaysia cheque, a loan sale, and a Texas olive branch
Google said on Wednesday it would spend $15 billion to expand data-center and cloud computing operations in Malaysia. In a weekend letter for The Information, Abram Brown sets that overseas commitment against Tuesday’s election, which he reads in part as a voter referendum against the AI era, and against the fear in Silicon Valley that Democrats will halt construction. He reports that a Dutch bank is trying to quickly offload a half-billion-dollar chunk of data-center loans, including some tied to OpenAI’s Project Stargate, and that Anthropic investors are gloomy about an IPO because they worry the company will not secure a sought-after $1.5 trillion valuation. Tesla appears in the same letter as Elon Musk, not as a site. Texas’s new Democratic senator, James Talarico, on Wednesday — the day after he won a race in which Musk spent heavily to defeat him — called Musk “a great business leader and entrepreneur and innovator” and vowed to work with him. Musk replied on X, “Nice of him to say that.” Nvidia is named on The Information’s own account by SK Ventures general partner Paul Kedrosky, who said a slowdown is “very bad” for a data-center supplier because “a significant fraction of the queue” is, at the very least, pushed out.

The shared object is that queue. Google can write a Malaysia cheque while a lender tries to sell exposure that already includes Stargate-linked paper. A delay does not have to cancel an order to matter to Nvidia; on Kedrosky’s account, pushing the queue out is enough to hit the supplier investors had treated as the beneficiary of the build. Musk’s link is the election week itself: heavy spending against a candidate, then a next-day compliment, which Brown offers as evidence that flattery can calm the same nerves the vote rattled. None of the three companies is described as sharing a contract. They share a political constraint on where concrete gets poured, and a capital market already trying to move the loans.

Brown’s conclusion is that anti-AI campaign rhetoric fades once lawmakers take office. He cites AI investor Gavin Baker’s optimistic analysis of data-center plans from Texas’s new governor, Gina Hinojosa, and Baker’s line that she “sounds reasonable.” He notes that New York still has none of Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s city-run grocery stores, and he suspects that by next year AI companies will have found enough room to move many projects past Democrats. Nikita Bier, a former product lead at Musk’s X, posted that the period ahead would be “like the Nuremberg trials for AI researchers”; Brown treats that comparison as a warning about how the industry talks, not as a docket. The letter’s other geography is orbit. On Friday, Ron Baron told CNBC that data centers in space could have significant capacity as soon as 2028. Brown does not adopt that year as a schedule, and he undercuts the economics by comparing them to life on Saturn. The Malaysia commitment, the loan sale, and Kedrosky’s pushed queue do not wait on that date.
