### Top News
**Amazon cloud capacity sold out through 2027, CEO Jassy says: Yahoo Finance**
On a video podcast with Andreessen Horowitz co-founder Ben Horowitz and general partner Erik Torenberg, Amazon chief executive Andy Jassy said Friday that Amazon Web Services is struggling to match demand from AI labs and enterprises. He said 2026 cloud-computing capacity is sold out, 2027 is largely sold out, and a good chunk of 2028 is sold out. Amazon is scheduled to report third-quarter results on October 29. Earlier this year, Amazon said it would spend about $220 billion in capital expenditure for 2026. Jassy said the company could invest a lot more and it would not be a bad use of capital. **Updated Google privacy policy says your Gmail might be used for advertising: Ars Technica**
On October 16, Google changed the privacy-policy line that had said it does not show personalized ads based on content from Drive, Gmail or Photos. The updated text adds that this holds unless a person chooses to make data from those services available to other Google services that support personalized ads. Ars Technica reports that turning on Personal Intelligence, released in early 2026, is the mechanism that hands data from several apps to Google's AI products. A policy example says Search may process data a person allows it to access in order to personalize content, including ads. Personal Intelligence remains opt-in, and Google still says advertisers will not see a name or email address without permission. **Apple Pushes Back on DOJ Request for More Evidence: MacRumors**
A court filing this week shows Apple and the U.S. Department of Justice split on how much newer evidence Apple must produce in their antitrust case. The sides had agreed to a June 20, 2025 cutoff for Apple's records. Evidence-gathering is now scheduled to end in late May 2027, and the Justice Department's lawyers want documents created up to 30 days before that deadline, arguing newer records could show whether the alleged practices are continuing. Apple says a wider search would delay the case by several months. The company says it has already reviewed more than 11 million documents and handed over more than two million, covering 18 years. The lawsuit, filed in March 2024, accuses Apple of illegally protecting a smartphone monopoly by restricting competing apps, services and devices. Apple denies the allegations. The filing asks the court to record that the sides cannot settle the document fight themselves. Source: [macrumors.com](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/24/apple-pushes-back-on-doj-request/)

### The Thread: Half a million paid rides, two stacks
By March 2026, Waymo's weekly paid driverless orders stood at about 500,000. TradingKey sets that operating base next to Tesla as the other company it tracks in robotaxi commercialization. Waymo is owned by Google parent Alphabet. Service or deployment already covers 10 major U.S. cities, and Waymo plans to move toward 1 million weekly orders by the end of the year.

Waymo plans a driverless ride-hailing service in Toronto in 2028, its first Canadian market. It will soon put vehicles on local roads for manually driven mapping and testing, with Element Fleet Management handling fleet maintenance, customer service and daily operations. Official commercialization still depends on local regulatory approval. Local testing goes into Ontario's Automated Vehicle Pilot Program. The city is also a systems test for ice, snow, low temperatures and complex urban traffic, and a 2028 start for passengers still depends on test results and regulatory policy.

Tesla's service has already entered multiple cities across Texas and Florida. The first batch of Cybercabs, with no steering wheel or pedals, went into operation in Austin in September. The existing network keeps using the Model Y so early expansion does not rest only on dedicated vehicle production. TradingKey describes Tesla's position as vertical integration: the company controls vehicle manufacturing, autonomous-driving software, charging networks and the ride-hailing platform at once. The same piece says that if the Cybercab can be manufactured below $30,000, and if existing factories convert to large-scale robotaxi production, per-vehicle cost could sit significantly below Waymo vehicles that carry multiple sensors. That cost figure is TradingKey's condition, not a Tesla disclosure in the article.

The shared contest is paid driverless rides, run on opposite stacks. Waymo uses a geofenced Level 4 model: cameras, lidar and millimeter-wave radar together, plus high-definition maps and remote operation. Each new city needs advance road mapping, edge-case validation and regulatory communication, which TradingKey says raises hardware and operating cost and limits how fast the map can grow. Tesla relies more on a camera-centric vision system and neural networks. The Cybercab is designed directly around driverless operation, which TradingKey says creates significant regulatory hurdles for vehicles that lack traditional controls. Waymo can take orders in the Waymo app or on Uber, depending on the market.

What each company can claim is different, and so is the bill attached to it. Waymo's published comparison says the accident rate involving serious injury or fatality for the Waymo Driver is about 95 percent lower than human-driving benchmarks under identical road conditions. TradingKey also notes related research showing a lower incidence in certain crashes involving pedestrians, cyclists and intersections, and says those results still have to be read with operating areas, road types and data methods. That safety file is the foundation TradingKey credits for regulatory approval, bought with a slower city-by-city build. Tesla's file in the piece is a lower-cost copy: factories, software, charging and a Model Y fleet already in Texas and Florida, against a thinner current operating scale and an unresolved controls question. The next dated step the article names is Toronto in 2028, and only if tests and local rules let passenger operations begin on that schedule. Source: [tradingkey.com](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/262237212-tesla-robotaxi-vs-google-waymo-who-holds-autonomous-ride-commercialization-tradingkey)
