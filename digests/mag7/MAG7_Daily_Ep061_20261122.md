**AWS Lambda runs GPU inference, forty-eight calls to a model: InfoQ**
AWS has added GPU support to Lambda: event functions on Lambda Managed Instances can run on EC2 G-series instances — G4dn, G5, G6, G6f, Gr6, Gr6f and G6e at launch — as container images, triggered by the same event sources as any other function. The feature is generally available in commercial regions where both Managed Instances and those G-series instances exist, priced at the EC2 On-Demand rate plus a 9.75 percent management fee and $0.20 per million requests. A capacity provider lists allowed instance types, the VPC and a vCPU ceiling; Lambda picks the newest generation with capacity and falls back when newer ones are short. Publishing a version provisions environments, downloads the weights and warms the engine before the first event; Seekable OCI lazy loading can cut environment start time by up to 70 percent. Launch limits include container images up to 32 GB, 32 GB of ephemeral storage, 2 to 32 GB of memory, 3 to 48 GB of accelerator memory, 15,000 vCPUs per capacity provider, and a timeout of up to 90 minutes for asynchronous and event-source mapping invocations. AWS ships five GPU base images — CUDA only, CuPy, PyTorch, vLLM and SGLang — and the two inference servers need no handler code. Source: [infoq.com](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/aws-lambda-gpu-managed-instances/)

### The Counterpoint
**The GPU function still waits on a zero quota and a cold publish: InfoQ**
Classmethod’s Akaike tried every accelerated instance type in us-east-1 and found 37 of 81 accepted, all G-family; P-family, G7, Inferentia, Trainium and the Graviton-based g5g were refused, as were zip packages. A PyTorch benchmark ran about 66 times faster on the L4 than on that instance’s CPU, but neither Terraform nor the Cloud Control provider knew AcceleratorConfig yet, and the GPU vCPU quota in a fresh account is zero — a g6.xlarge has 4 vCPUs, so the default three instances need a quota of 12. A second test put Gemma 4 E2B on one g6.2xlarge through the vLLM image, with Google’s 4-bit QAT weights and a speculative-decoding drafter in a 9 GB image: 417 calls succeeded, at roughly 130 tokens a second for a single request and 3,400 to 3,600 at full concurrency, after 484 seconds from publish to an active version. Source: [infoq.com](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/11/aws-lambda-gpu-managed-instances/)

### The Thread: Search’s slower quarter, Meta’s larger bill
Up to $145 billion is the 2026 capital spending Meta has committed, and advertising is the business that has to earn it back. Yahoo Finance, citing the company’s results, says advertising was 98 percent of total revenue in the third quarter of 2026. Daily active users across the family of apps stood at 3.64 billion and were still growing. Ad revenue rose 25.9 percent year over year to $63.1 billion in that quarter. Average price per ad increased 14 percent, and impressions rose 10 percent.

The same piece sets that ad revenue just slightly below what Alphabet’s Google Search generated in the third quarter, and it says Search is growing at a much slower pace. That comparison is the shared ground. Both companies sell attention to advertisers. Only one of them, in this account, has turned free cash flow negative and paused share buybacks in order to fund the build. The article says spending is likely to be much higher next year. Susan Li, chief financial officer, said Meta is leveraging AI to make it easier for businesses to manage campaigns, develop creative, and act on performance opportunities. The piece adds that better recommendations drive engagement, engagement produces data on interests, and that data draws advertisers, with revenue then pushed back into the technology stack. Outside the ad line, Meta sells AI tools to small businesses and enterprises, has said it can sell excess compute to outside customers at premium prices, and offers a Muse personal agent that can bring subscription sales and a share of commerce revenue. Those lines are immaterial right now.

The dependency runs through the advertiser, not through a joint product. Alphabet’s Search business is the yardstick, so a slower Search growth rate is Alphabet’s number, not a cost Meta can hand it. Meta’s number is whether price and impressions keep rising once cash that used to repurchase shares is going into data centers instead. The article’s test is narrow on purpose. A thriving ad segment is the evidence it wants that the infrastructure spend is showing up in core operations. Tool sales, outside compute, and Muse are not, in its telling, large enough to answer the question.

The consequence splits. Meta is exposed if the ad gains stall, because the piece already describes cash flow turned negative and buybacks stopped, with a higher outlay still ahead. Alphabet is exposed differently: Search remains the slightly larger ad business in the quarter being compared, so Alphabet does not need Meta’s build to work in order to hold that position, but it does compete for the same campaign budget Li says Meta is making easier to run. Neither company gets a date in the article for the next set of figures. The deciding read is whether ad price and impressions repeat this quarter’s direction, and whether anything outside advertising stops being too small to matter. Source: [finance.yahoo.com](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/mark-zuckerberg-meta-platforms-pouring-043101493.html)
