**Geely puts Gemini, Maps and Play on overseas cars from late 2027: CarNewsChina**

**British Airways writes Meta AI glasses into its filming ban: PYOK**
British Airways amended its General Conditions of Carriage so passengers may not use Meta AI glasses to covertly film staff or fellow seatmates. Section 7A.10 lets the airline refuse carriage for filming, live-streaming or photographing BA crew or other colleagues without consent, folded into harassment and abusive-conduct grounds. Section 11A lets the airline make a passenger leave the aircraft, refuse another flight including a pre-booked return, and report the passenger to the police for filming crew without consent. Passengers may still wear the glasses; an LED is meant to show recording, and PYOK notes reports of wearers covering that light. Cabin crew and other customer-facing BA workers are already banned from using smart glasses. The change follows, by months, a ban on photographing or filming those workers without express consent, and on live-streaming anyone without permission. In June, Volotea ground staff at Oviedo refused to engage a travel vlogger who would not stop recording a service exchange in Meta AI glasses; the airline’s reply warned that the conduct potentially breached Spanish privacy laws. Source: [paddleyourownkanoo.com](https://www.paddleyourownkanoo.com/2026/10/16/british-airways-bans-passengers-from-using-meta-ai-glasses-on-board-its-aircraft/)

**Gemini 4 Argon is live in Antigravity for paid subscribers: 9to5Google**
Google is rolling Gemini 4 Argon into Antigravity, its agentic coding tool, for AI Pro and AI Ultra subscribers, after a wider rollout to users earlier this week. A separate weekly-only Argon quota sits on top of the existing quota for Gemini 3.8 Flash. Google says Argon is designed to tackle the hardest problems. On YouTube’s comments frontend, Argon traced production profiles, found redundant emoji rendering at nearly 45 percent of server overhead, and drove 12 optimizations that cut the CPU footprint in half, cut server comment-load latency by 30 milliseconds and cut client end-to-end latency by 20 milliseconds. On an on-call bug queue of 134 open reports, agents closed 97 stale or duplicate tickets in under 3 hours, a 72.4 percent reduction, and left 37 sorted by owner. Auditing a batch of access-control changes, Argon found four critical security bugs after two earlier frontier models had reported zero on the same code. A Fortran-to-Rust rewrite through a custom skill delivered a 1.8-times performance boost, and an Ads sales workflow cut prospect research time by 90 percent, about 10 hours a week. ### The Thread: A tenth of a second on two laptops’ chips
Amazon Web Services put a two-billion-parameter decision model in the open, and the only speed numbers it published were measured on an NVIDIA graphics card and an Apple laptop chip. Strands Decider 2B is built to pick among choices a developer already listed — model routing, tool selection, classification, guardrails, context management — and to return a decision with confidence scores instead of a long generated answer. The base is Qwen3.5-2B. AWS replaced the language-model head with a pointer head that scores candidate answers, then fine-tuned with a rank-16 LoRA adapter. Weights, training data and scripts are out, so a developer can run and change the model locally through the strands-decider package, on the command line or inside Strands agents.

The clock AWS cites is short. Median decision latency was about 115 milliseconds on an NVIDIA RTX 3090. Small tasks took 153 milliseconds on an Apple M3 MacBook. On JevBench, the v19 checkpoint scored 72.3 percent accuracy across 231 public tasks. Those two machines are not AWS products. They are the hardware a developer already has on a desk: an NVIDIA consumer GPU in one test, an Apple silicon laptop in the other. The model’s job is to sit in front of a larger system and choose a tool or a route. If that choice takes a tenth of a second on a 3090 and about a sixth of a second on an M3, the wait is in the same band as a sluggish click, not a cloud round trip the article never claims to have measured.

InfoQ places Decider against TypeSafe AI’s Jev, another model aimed at structured decisions, classification, routing and tool selection, also returning confidence scores. The split InfoQ draws is deployment, not a winner on the benchmark: Decider is open source and can run locally; Jev is a managed API. AWS evaluated Decider on JevBench. The published results, InfoQ says, do not establish a direct performance advantage over Jev. So the 72.3 percent figure is a score on 231 tasks for one checkpoint, not a claim that Amazon beat the specialist it borrowed a bench from.

The same announcement pairs the small model with Strands Box, in developer preview, as the execution cage around whatever model is chosen. Box combines operating-system isolation with Dogwood, an open-source policy language, and routes shell commands, Python, MCP tools, filesystem operations and network access through a policy layer outside the language model. A rule can require tests before a push, cap how often an agent calls an external API, or block outbound HTTP after the agent reads a customer file. AWS says Box can sit on different models and agent frameworks, local or in the cloud. NVIDIA and Apple do not ship that policy layer. They show up because the decision step was timed on their silicon, and because GitHub discussion already flags Apple Silicon compatibility, concurrent requests and cold-start latency as problems to check before production. AWS Community Builder Yuuki Yamashita, in a hands-on tutorial, reported that Decider 2B could stop an agent from making an unsupported weather-tool call.

What changes is where the choice is made. Amazon is offering a two-billion-parameter pointer model that developers can fine-tune and run beside the NVIDIA and Apple hardware they already own, then wrap the chosen action in a kernel policy instead of a prompt. The numbers that decide whether that is faster than calling a managed decider are the ones AWS printed: 115 milliseconds, 153 milliseconds, 72.3 percent on 231 tasks, rank-16 on a Qwen3.5-2B base, and no published head-to-head win over Jev.
