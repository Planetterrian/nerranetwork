### Top News
**A New AI Tool From Google DeepMind Proved the Signal Protocol Is Secure: WIRED**
Google DeepMind researchers used agentic AI and a framework called AlphaProof Code to formally verify a set of the Signal protocol’s key cryptographic guarantees, in a proof-of-concept that ran four weeks. The work is further independent validation of cryptography that protects communications on Signal, Google Messages, and WhatsApp. The analysis found no new vulnerabilities in the portions examined. Run against older Signal versions that contain known, long-fixed vulnerabilities, AlphaProof Code identified them and showed those versions could not be formally verified. The researchers are releasing the specification and proof they developed. Signal chief technology officer Ehren Kret said the approach enabled a significant expansion beyond prior formal verification of smaller portions of code. **Meta Expands AI Age-Estimation Tech to Protect Teens on Instagram: Analytics India Magazine**
Meta is expanding AI age estimation globally on Instagram to find accounts it suspects belong to teenagers, including people who registered with an adult birthday, and to place them in Teen Accounts. Teen Accounts limit who can contact a teen, what content they see, and how much time they spend; Meta says the global step will bring millions more teenagers under those protections. The company is continuing the rollout on Facebook and plans to introduce similar safeguards globally on Threads later this year. In Australia, Meta has started using Apple’s age-range signal: when a parent sets up a phone, the app store can confirm an age range and pass it to a developer when the teen downloads an app. This month, parents in the United States start getting an Instagram notification on how Meta places teens in age-appropriate protections, linking to an article by Larry Magid, CEO of ConnectSafely. Meta said the expansion builds on an agreement with 52 attorneys general across US states, territories, and the District of Columbia, and it called on TikTok and YouTube to adopt similar age-identification measures. ### The Thread: Credits for the clinic, three clouds in the waiting room
Amazon Web Services is putting $100 million of artificial intelligence, agentic services, and cloud infrastructure into a five-year Health Impact Initiative for global health nonprofits, government agencies, research institutions, healthcare delivery systems, and health-tech firms working with underserved populations. The line continues the Health Equity Initiative AWS launched in 2021, which distributed $60 million across more than 400 awardees. With other AWS health social-impact programs, the company has supported more than 600 organizations in more than 80 countries. An independent evaluation of 83 past Health Equity Initiative awardees found that 60 percent named cloud hosting and compute costs as the single largest barrier to modernization where they operate, and ranked promotional credits as their highest-priority operational need. The new fund is written for more than hosting. HIT Consultant lists autonomous patient-outreach bots, automated call-center triage, serverless predictive analytics, and ambient workflow tools meant to extend constrained clinical staff. Three domains set the priority: expanding access to quality health services, women’s and children’s health research and innovation, and global nutrition. Dr. Rowland Illing, chief medical officer and director of healthcare and life sciences at AWS, said agents and AI are revolutionizing health services but too often do not reach the communities that need them most.

HIT Consultant places Microsoft and Google in the same contest, on different products. Google Cloud is framed around generative healthcare search through Vertex AI. Microsoft Azure is framed around an OpenAI integration inside Epic. AWS, in this account, is using the credits to seat HealthOmics, Amazon Bedrock, and Amazon Comprehend Medical inside research institutes and health NGOs while those groups are still choosing a stack. The piece’s stickiness claim is plain: once data pipelines and agents sit on one cloud, moving them is costly. The shared constraint is staffing. Safety-net hospitals, federally qualified health centers, and rural clinics cannot close shifts with signing bonuses. For those sites, an autonomous scheduling agent, a voice-based multilingual intake coordinator, or an automated triage bot is described as how continuous operations stay possible, and the grant is what pays the compute so the software does not eat operating cash. Proposals are audited weekly against projected lives impacted, computational feasibility, and implementation roadmaps. Organizations are expected to price the build in the AWS Pricing Calculator so an award becomes a production deployment rather than an open research note.

What shifts is who owns the default health agent in organizations that never sign a commercial cloud contract on their own. A group that stands up outreach and triage on Amazon’s credits is a harder later sale for Microsoft, even where Azure already sits inside the Epic record, and a harder later sale for Google, even where search is the clinical front door. Amazon gets a path into care networks that do not show up in a standard cloud forecast. Microsoft and Google keep the wedges this account assigns them — the record-system model and healthcare search — but they are not funding this round. The filter that decides who actually ships is that weekly audit: a plan that cannot show a live deployment does not stay on the credit line.
