# MAG 7 Daily
> **Ninety-six accelerators in one rack will run Microsoft's Copilot replies from 2027.**

**What You Need to Know:** Microsoft's Maia 300 puts 96 accelerators in one rack for Copilot replies, with data-center rollout starting in 2027. Apple's iPhone still took a record slice of a much smaller phone market, Meta confirmed adult chats inside a private Muse virtual machine, and a South Korean pension fund cut a policy read from eight hours to ten minutes on AWS.

### Top News
**Maia 300 packs 96 accelerators for Copilot replies: The Mac Observer**
Microsoft announced the Maia 300 and plans to roll it into data centers starting in 2027 so Teams, Copilot and Cowork return faster answers. The Mac Observer reports that one rack holds 96 accelerators, with integrated liquid cooling and a unified Ethernet network so more AI interactions fit in a smaller footprint while energy use stays managed. Satya Nadella has called the project the company's biggest bet yet on custom hardware, instead of relying entirely on outside vendors. Source: [macobserver.com](https://www.macobserver.com/news/microsoft-reveals-new-maia-300-chip-to-power-faster-teams-and-copilot/)

**iPhone share hits a third-quarter record as shipments fall: MacRumors**
Counterpoint Research's preliminary figures put Apple's share of worldwide smartphone shipments at 22% in the third quarter of 2026, a third-quarter record, even though Apple's own iPhone shipments declined. Samsung accounted for 23%. Manufacturers shipped 20% fewer smartphones than a year earlier, and the July-to-September total was the lowest since 2012. With no standard iPhone 18 beside the Pro models in September, Counterpoint said Apple supplied fewer new devices to retail channels than at last year's iPhone 17 launch. The firm still reported a better debut for the iPhone 18 Pro than for the previous Pro generation, with China and India standing out. Counterpoint estimates smartphone retail prices have risen 20% globally so far this year and links the downturn to memory-chip shortages and higher memory prices. Source: [macrumors.com](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/13/iphone-takes-bigger-slice-of-shrinking-market/)

### The Counterpoint
**The leap is versus Maia 200, and the chip answers prompts rather than training models: The Mac Observer**
The rack does not, on Microsoft's own description, take over model training. The Mac Observer says the Maia 300 is built primarily as an inference engine, specialized in quick answers to prompts rather than training models from scratch. The stated gain is internal: two and a half times the memory speed and a third more total capacity than the Maia 200. Outside vendors remain the path for the training work this design leaves aside. Source: [macobserver.com](https://www.macobserver.com/news/microsoft-reveals-new-maia-300-chip-to-power-faster-teams-and-copilot/)

### The Thread: A two-hundred-billion-dollar check and a Costco pitch
Up to $200 billion is the check Benzinga says Mark Zuckerberg is ready to "write," defending Meta's AI capital spending at that ceiling to dominate the decades ahead. The headcount move is already done. In May, Meta cut about 10% of its global workforce and reassigned roughly 7,000 employees to AI-focused teams. Alexandr Wang, Meta's chief AI officer, said on a David Senra podcast published Monday that he took Elon Musk's small-team lesson to Zuckerberg early in his time at the company. Wang said a small, flat, highly technical team with very high talent density can get a lot done quickly, that Zuckerberg was excited, and that the idea became a tentpole of Meta's strategy. Benzinga, citing Reuters, reports Zuckerberg later told employees the reorganization did not unfold as clean as planned. Andrej Karpathy, a former AI director at Tesla, said in 2024 that Musk was a force against growth, minimizing middle management and treating engineers as the source of truth. In 2022, after taking over Twitter, now X, Musk slashed that company's global workforce by thousands. Microsoft's parallel line, in Aaron Holmes's account for The Information, is a token price rather than a headcount rule. Holmes describes Charles Lamanna's vision of Copilot as the Costco of AI: a broke college student and a millionaire would both use it as the best value they can get without spending too much on tokens. Meta is naming a ceiling on the check and a thinner org. Microsoft is naming the token bill as the reason to stay.
