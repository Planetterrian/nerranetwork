### Top News
**Deep Review opens after conference pilots on more than 16,000 manuscripts: Google Research**
Rajesh Jayaram and Vincent Cohen-Addad of Google Research say Deep Review, the public successor to the Paper Assistant Tool, is now available worldwide after opted-in authors received private pre-submission feedback on more than 16,000 manuscripts at STOC 2026, ICML 2026, NeurIPS 2026, and ICLR 2027. The system checks logical consistency and mathematical statements, suggests improvements, grounds facts, and compares a document with existing work. Inputs are PDFs and plain text at launch, with multi-file inputs coming soon. Users who need more than the free tier can attach a Gemini API key for higher limits and for the Deep Review API. Uploaded documents are never used to train, fine-tune, or improve Google models. Further detail on the pilots is in the manuscript Towards Automating Scientific Review with Google’s Paper Assistant Tool. Source: [research.google](https://research.google/blog/analyzing-technical-documents-with-deep-review/)

**Microsoft launches Copilot for Legal with more than 20 skills: KuCoin**
On October 16, Microsoft President and Vice Chairman Brad Smith announced Copilot for Legal, with more than 20 specialized AI skills for commercial customers worldwide, built as legal plugins inside Copilot. Each skill maps to a task such as drafting contracts, reviewing agreements, tracking obligations, or organizing case timelines. Microsoft’s blog says those capabilities sit mainly in desktop Word and in Cowork, which can pull obligations, deadlines, and trigger conditions from contracts, emails, minutes, and documents, compare terms across agreements, build case timelines, or turn a meeting into minutes and resolutions. Partner plugins named in the launch include Bloomberg Law, Thomson Reuters HighQ, DocuSign, Harvey, Relativity, iManage, and NetDocuments, covering legal research, document management, e-signatures, e-discovery, and transaction management. Plugins roll out gradually through the Microsoft Plugin Registry, and availability can vary by region, licensing, and administrator settings. Microsoft says the tool assists lawyers and does not replace legal judgment, so citations, conclusions, and revision suggestions still need manual checks, and an enterprise needs a commercial Microsoft 365 Copilot license plus a supported Word setup. Source: [kucoin.com](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/microsoft-launches-legal-version-of-copilot-with-over-20-ai-legal-skills)

**App Store rules now target AI nudify apps and hidden features: MacRumors**
Apple expanded App Store rules to cover AI-generated nonconsensual intimate imagery. Sexually explicit content was already banned; developers must now proactively prevent and remove AI deepfakes, and apps can be removed if they do not. Updated guideline 1.2 names AI content-creation apps. New guideline 2.5.19 says features that were not in the version submitted for review can mean removal from the App Store and the Apple Developer Program, aimed at “nudification” apps that hid that function until after approval. Section 5’s ban on apps that facilitate human trafficking or child exploitation now also covers alternative app distribution. MacRumors reports that earlier this year Apple told xAI it would remove Grok if nude deepfakes continued, xAI added safeguards, and Apple approved an updated app. The Verge reports that Apple did not pull X or Grok, after criticism that X users had used Grok to make nonconsensual sexualized deepfakes, including of minors. ### The Counterpoint
**Two free reviews a week against 74,000 submissions, and the official verdict stays human: Google Research**
Deep Review does not enter the official peer-review decision. Google Research says this year’s submissions to ICLR, NeurIPS, and ICML topped 74,000, up from about 45,000 in 2025, 32,000 in 2024, and 23,000 in 2023, while every user gets two complimentary document reviews a week. In the conference pilots, feedback was completely decoupled from the official review, and papers were deleted from Google systems promptly after each program; the public product stores uploads for one year so the user can see them in the Deep Review interface. The system is built to take on consistency checks and leave higher-level judgements to humans. Source: [research.google](https://research.google/blog/analyzing-technical-documents-with-deep-review/)

### The Thread: One UK gateway, three of the seven
In May 2026, the U.K. Competition and Markets Authority opened an investigation into whether Microsoft should be designated as having Strategic Market Status in its business software ecosystem. On October 16, 2026, the authority proposed that designation. The CMA says Strategic Market Status does not find or assume wrongdoing. It is a gateway into the country’s digital markets competition regime, and it could lead to measures that affect how Microsoft bundles Excel, Teams, and Copilot.

Alphabet is already inside the same regime. Last year the CMA designated Google with Strategic Market Status in general search and advertising services after confirming that the company met the legal criteria. Amazon sits in the adjacent file. In March the regulator began consulting stakeholders on whether Amazon Web Services and Microsoft were following through on commitments to improve customer choice in cloud services, and on whether further action was necessary. Friday’s proposal stacks workplace software and Copilot onto a cloud check that already names Amazon and Microsoft, and onto a search designation that already names Google.

The CMA says Microsoft has held a dominant position in U.K. business software for decades, first through operating systems and productivity tools, then cybersecurity and database management, with AI products and cloud services now adding further reach. U.K. businesses and public-sector organizations can benefit from buying several digital products from Microsoft, and they may also face barriers when they try to switch. The products are closely integrated, which the authority says offers a more seamless experience than combining services from different companies. Control over key APIs, technical interfaces, and data flows can make it harder for competing providers to deliver the same functionality.

The examination the CMA described is about choice, not a penalty already imposed. It will look at whether customers can choose their preferred AI tools and whether competing providers can compete effectively with Copilot. It will assess whether bundling of security products restricted customer choice, and how licensing practices affected cloud options. Sarah Cardell, chief executive of the CMA, said organizations should be able to choose the products that work best for them, switch provider when they want to, and combine services from different suppliers. A Microsoft spokesperson told Reuters that the company’s products, including Copilot, are “open and interoperable, giving customers the ability to use them alongside competing services.” Microsoft did not immediately respond to Benzinga’s request for comment.

Benzinga wrote that analysts highlight 30 million Copilot seats, Azure momentum, and Maia 300 inference chips ahead of fiscal Q1 2027 earnings. That seat figure is an analyst highlight in the piece, not a number Microsoft stated to the CMA. Comments on the proposed decision are invited by November 13, and the authority will take a final decision by February 13, 2027. Source: [benzinga.com](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/10/62427164/uk-watchdog-microsoft-competition-scrutiny)
