**AWS Emulator: Open-source tool for testing AWS apps locally, built by autonomous AI agents: Help Net Security**
Amazon Web Services has released AWS Emulator, a free tool that simulates AWS services on a laptop, a development server, or in a continuous-integration pipeline, Help Net Security reported. The company says autonomous agents wrote it entirely. Senior principal engineer Matthew Goldberg said, "The emulator was written entirely by autonomous agents. Our team hasn’t written or reviewed any of the code," because guardrails kept the agents inside set lines. Three agent teams divide the work: one builds tests from public API specifications and checks them against real AWS, one runs those tests on the emulator and groups failures, and one fixes a single problem per agent. Hundreds of agents fix thousands of issues a day against a library of tens of thousands of tests, and a change enters the main codebase only after build checks and agent reviews for coding standards, security, and fidelity. Developers can start it through an AI coding agent, a Linux package, Docker, or an embedded mode inside a Rust test process. There is no extra service fee — users pay for AWS resources when they deploy — and the code and fidelity tests are on GitHub under a CC0 public-domain dedication, with per-service fidelity scores published at each release. **Google’s Gemini AI Assistant Now Works On Apple CarPlay: Yahoo Autos**
Google is rolling Gemini out on Apple CarPlay so an iPhone user can open the app from the car screen and continue a conversation, Yahoo Autos reported, and The Mac Observer dates the global start to November 11. The driver has to tap the icon. Gemini does not wake on a voice command, because Apple does not allow third-party wake phrases or an always-on microphone. Only Siri has that privilege, though a driver can ask Siri to launch Gemini. Once it is open it runs full screen, so the navigation route is not visible during the chat, and the driver can interrupt, change subject, or pause. With permissions it can read Gmail and Google Calendar, and CarPlay chats sync back to the Gemini app on the phone. The app is free, covers supported Gemini Live languages, and appears after the latest Gemini app is installed, the microphone is allowed, and Gemini Live has been used at least once on the phone. Last month Google also put Gemini into the CarPlay mode of Google Maps. Apple opened the door to third-party conversational apps in iOS 26.4 without letting them replace Siri or take over vehicle functions. **Commerce Department Finalizing Long-Awaited AI Remote Access Rule: The Information**
The Commerce Department is finalizing a rule to stop Chinese AI firms from remotely using advanced AI chips through data centers in other countries, The Information reported, citing two people familiar with the process. The rule would expand existing semiconductor controls at the Bureau of Industry and Security to seven more countries, including Malaysia, Singapore, and Thailand, to block remote access and chip smuggling into China. Commerce is still consulting other agencies before it can be finished. The Information said the step could answer criticism that the department has been slow on limits that influential companies, including Nvidia, have opposed. ### The Counterpoint
**The emulator will not tell you whether a permission holds: Help Net Security**
AWS Emulator is the wrong tool for the question that matters in a real account: whether a person or a policy is allowed to touch a resource. Help Net Security reported that it does not evaluate AWS Identity and Access Management or resource-based policies the way AWS does, so it should not be used to test security, and it is not intended for production workloads. Fidelity varies by service and feature. Behavior is checked against AWS in US West (Oregon), except global services, which are checked in US East (N. Virginia). All emulated resources sit in memory and disappear when the emulator stops. AWS has not tested it for long-running use or load testing, and the documentation says to use one region and one account because the emulator does not keep resources separate by region or account. Source: [helpnetsecurity.com](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/11/12/aws-ai-agents-built-emulator/)

### The Thread: The AWS operator Meta wants on its own machines
Santosh Janardhan joined Facebook in October 2009. More than seventeen years later, Meta is taking him off day-to-day leadership of infrastructure and installing Dave Brown, who spent nearly twenty years at Amazon Web Services, with Brown assuming that organization in early 2027. Citybiz reported the change from a Bloomberg account of an internal employee memo. Janardhan helped oversee the computing systems behind Meta’s global operations, including its data centers, networking infrastructure, engineering platforms, and custom-designed chips. He remains through the first quarter of 2027 to assist the transition, then becomes chief technology officer of Meta Compute, where he will advise on technical strategy and maintain relationships with key technology partners. Brown joined Meta earlier in 2026 as vice president of infrastructure. At AWS he most recently served as senior vice president responsible for compute and machine-learning services.

Under chief executive Mark Zuckerberg, Meta is committing hundreds of billions of dollars to data centers, advanced processors, networking equipment, and engineering talent. In the memo, Janardhan described the infrastructure organization as having evolved from a supporting technology function into a competitive advantage. During his tenure Meta expanded its global data center network and invested in proprietary silicon, advanced networking technology, and systems designed to improve computing efficiency. The stated aim is capacity for more sophisticated models and for AI-powered services across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and the wider ecosystem. The appointment runs through Amazon and Meta because the executive who managed large-scale compute and machine-learning services at AWS is the person Meta is placing over its own build. Amazon is the company that developed that operating experience inside the cloud business that rents capacity. Meta is recruiting it while it designs its own chips and grows its own sites. Citybiz sets the hire against competition among major technology companies to secure the computing power those models require. One firm is the training ground. The other is turning that training into an internal leadership seat rather than only buying the machines from outside.

The shift puts cloud and machine-learning operations experience at the center of Meta’s next infrastructure expansion, while the longtime chief moves into an advisory technology role and keeps the partner relationships. Amazon’s compute and machine-learning organization is the background Meta selected, so experience that ran rented capacity now sits inside a company building its own silicon and its own sites. For Amazon, a senior infrastructure leader has moved to a customer that is also constructing capacity of its own. For Meta, the memo’s language already treats infrastructure as an advantage, and the person accountable for it is being chosen for having run that kind of fleet. The contest described in the report is for computing power, and this hire says the scarce input includes people who have already operated it, not only processors and buildings.
