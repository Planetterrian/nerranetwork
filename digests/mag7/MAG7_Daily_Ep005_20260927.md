### Top News
**San Diego jury awards Taction over $5.7 billion against Apple: The Verge**
Haptics company Taction sued Apple in 2021. The Verge, citing CNBC, reports a federal jury in San Diego awarded Taction over $5.7 billion in damages. The suit centered on vibration-based tactile transducer technology. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1001118/apple-hit-with-5-7-billion-in-damages-over-haptic-patents)

### The Counterpoint
**Two vibration patents carry the whole damages number: The Verge**
The open question on the Apple award is whether two patents can hold a damages figure of that size. The Verge says the case turned on U.S. Patent Nos. 10,659,885 and 10,820,117, both for vibration-based tactile transducer technology meant to help users feel a signal. That pair, from the 2021 suit, is the record the $5.7 billion number sits on. Source: [theverge.com](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1001118/apple-hit-with-5-7-billion-in-damages-over-haptic-patents)

### The Thread: Who gets to finish the purchase
About $350 million is what Google put into Flipkart in 2024, in a funding round led by Walmart, and that stake now sits under a live checkout test. Google has begun letting shoppers in India buy Walmart-owned Flipkart goods from inside Gemini and AI Mode. A Buy button shows on select Flipkart listings and routes the shopper into a Flipkart checkout without leaving the AI interface. People familiar with the matter, and an experience observed by TechCrunch, put the test with a small group of users and a narrow range of products. The covered goods are smartphones, electronics, and mobile accessories — three categories — while other users still see a standard Flipkart listing with no direct purchase option. Google plans a broader expansion later in October, timed to India's festive shopping season, one of the highest-traffic periods for the country's e-commerce sector. A Google spokesperson told TechCrunch the company is always testing new features so people can discover and connect with businesses more easily. Earlier in 2026 Google introduced the Universal Commerce Protocol, an open standard for agents to move through a retail journey including checkout, and later expanded it so a shopper can transfer items to a retailer's own site. The Flipkart flow observed by TechCrunch is distinct from that Google-hosted checkout: tapping Buy opens a Flipkart-branded payment flow. Google holds the financial stake on top of the technology partnership. GeekWire's live show on Amazon, Meta, and the fight over AI reports Amazon is blocking Meta's Muse assistant from shopping on Amazon.com because the agent does not identify itself. October is Google's expansion date. Amazon's condition is identification. Muse has not met it.
