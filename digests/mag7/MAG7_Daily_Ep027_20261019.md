### Top News
**Google Gemini 4 Agents Just Beat Minecraft With No Human Intervention: Engadget**
Four Gemini 4 Argon agents beat Minecraft's Ender Dragon in a sandboxed, randomized survival world with no roles, strategies, or game-specific tools assigned. Each agent drove a player through the open-source Mineflayer library and coordinated by in-game chat and shared memory. They split tasks, warned one another about threats, passed coordinates, and recovered items from dead teammates. In the paper's account, janesick made tools, gyldenia gathered food and supplies, auratian lit a key portal, and bulicame built Enderman shelters and landed the final blow. The run took 27.23 hours, most of it on the Ender Dragon. The agents wrote and ran 2,783 programs, exchanged 229 messages, and a fifth agent, the Observer, watched for cheating and published reports. The team wrote that this was, to their knowledge, the first end-to-end autonomous Minecraft completion by AI agents using only the standard Mineflayer library, without pre-scouted seeds or other game-specific tools written by humans, and that winning required a coherent plan across thousands of dependent steps for more than a day. Source: [engadget.com](https://www.engadget.com/2289944/google-gemini-4-agents-just-beat-minecraft-with-no-human-intervention/)

### The Counterpoint
**605 deaths and a four-figure token bill sit under the Minecraft win: Engadget**
The same paper that calls the Ender Dragon run a first also records 605 deaths. The agents used 1.35 billion input tokens. The final cost was $810 at Gemini's list price, or $1,620 at the standard price. Those three figures are the open test of the result: a day-long plan that dies hundreds of times and bills four figures is a completed run, not a clean one, and the paper does not show the same stack finishing a second world. ### The Thread: Twenty million seats, five products, one February call
Microsoft's business software reaches more than a million UK businesses and public bodies and around 20 million users a month. The Competition and Markets Authority said on 16 October that this ecosystem should be given strategic market status. That view is provisional. The regulator is consulting, and a final decision is due by February 2027.

The CMA examined five digital activities and found Microsoft strong in each: relational databases, security tools, operating systems for PCs, operating systems for servers, and productivity suites. The products named in that reach are Word, Excel, Outlook, PowerPoint, and Teams, a growing presence for Copilot, and Microsoft's security and server products. The provisional findings say the strength rests on how hard it is for customers to switch, which limits choice and weakens the pressure rival developers can apply. Commercial terms and integration advantages add further barriers.

Amazon and Google are in the cloud half of the same picture, not in the software designation itself. In separate, informal talks, Microsoft and AWS have begun connecting their data centres to competitors' networks, Google first, and both have scrapped the charges for moving data out when a customer switches provider. A further cloud update is due in spring 2027. The CMA says it is still talking to both companies about further improvements.

If the designation goes ahead, it does not imply wrongdoing. It would let the regulator consider conduct requirements or pro-competition interventions. The draft roadmap lists three areas for more evidence. One is whether buyers are free to pick the AI tools and features they want, and whether alternatives to Copilot get a fair chance to compete. A second is whether Microsoft's bundling and integration of security products restricts choice. A third is whether customers can decide which cloud runs their software licences, and how Microsoft's licensing affects that.

Chief executive Sarah Cardell said organisations should be able to choose the products that work best for them, switch provider when they want to, and combine services from different suppliers. For Microsoft, the February 2027 decision is the gate on whether Copilot keeps its integration advantage inside those suites or whether a rival assistant has to be given a level field. For Amazon, the informal track has already moved two commercial terms: interconnects to rival networks are underway, and the egress charge on a switch is gone, with more actions still under discussion. For Google, the recorded change is positional. It was first onto those competitor links, ahead of the spring 2027 cloud update. The AI question is the one the CMA flags for UK organisations. It is inviting views on both the proposed designation and the roadmap, so that line of inquiry is open to challenge before the February 2027 decision.
