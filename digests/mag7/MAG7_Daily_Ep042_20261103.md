**EU court adviser says Gemini's use of press may infringe publisher rights: Reuters**
Advocate General Maciej Szpunar told Europe's highest court on Tuesday that Google's Gemini may infringe press publishers' rights in certain circumstances. He said the right may be infringed when an LLM-driven chatbot retrieves a publication from a press publisher's website, reuses it to generate an answer, and gives that answer to the user. Copyright, he said, does not protect the mere facts in a news report, only the specific form of expression. Use of copyrighted material to train large language models is, in his view, freely permitted under the text and data mining exception. He was advising in a case brought by Hungarian site operator Like Company against Google, and advocate-general opinions are usually followed by the judges. ### The Counterpoint
**The majority rule and the "some customers" account are not the same size of constraint: The Information**
The number that decides how hard Nvidia's instruction bites is whether a majority-of-clients rule is what was actually imposed. The Information's account, from three people familiar with the matter, is that some cloud customers were told to reduce Chinese-company exposure, which would narrow one route to advanced compute without stating a client-mix quota. Bloomberg's retelling, from unidentified people, is an instruction to scale back exposure. No named cloud, and no filing in today's reports, states the majority threshold as a requirement those clients have accepted. Source: [theinformation.com](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/nvidia-tells-cloud-clients-take-less-china-business)

### The Thread: The September scoreboard under the memory bid
Apple's iPhone business booked $57.6 billion in the September quarter, up 17% and a new September-quarter record, and that line is only the start of a split that also runs through Amazon's cloud, across the seven only where the same memory and compute bid shows up. Mac revenue was $10.8 billion, up from $8.7 billion, despite supply constraints. Full-year Mac revenue was $37.9 billion, up from $33.7 billion in 2025, and Ternus said Apple has never sold more Macs in a fiscal year than it did in 2026. iPad revenue fell to $6.2 billion from $7 billion, which Apple tied to supply constraints and a difficult compare with last year's A16 iPad. Services revenue was $31.6 billion, up from $28.8 billion, a new all-time record, and $123.3 billion for the full year of 2026. The App Store saw nearly a billion average weekly visitors, and the number of users paying for at least one service grew. Wearables, Home and Accessories were $8.3 billion, down 8%, against the prior-year launches of Apple Watch SE 3 and AirPods Pro 3. The new Apple TV 4K, HomePod mini and Apple HomeView did not ship in the September quarter. The installed base is more than 2.5 billion active devices.

The foldable sits on the same constrained shelf. The Verge reported the iPhone Duo launched last month at $1,999, with a 5.4-inch outer display and a 7.6-inch inner screen, that preorders sold out, that some shipments are expected in 2027, and that a limited number were available in stores at launch. TechCrunch reported Apple is deepening a partnership with Perryman, a titanium producer, for materials for the foldable, and that Verizon has been offering gift cards while apologizing for the delays. An October Financial Times report, cited there, quoted Apple calling Duo demand nothing short of extraordinary after the device sold out on Apple's site within hours.

The shared constraint is memory, and TechCrunch tied it to soaring demand for memory chips from the AI industry, which has pushed component costs up for hardware makers across the board. Parekh said Apple's price increases were done reluctantly. Those June and September increases on iPads, Macs and iPhones, plus lower prices on some non-memory parts and memory bought earlier, offset part of the September pressure. Ternus said the benefit of that pre-bought memory declines over time. Khan said iPhone, iPad and Mac were constrained in the September quarter by demand plus limited availability of the advanced nodes the processors are made on, and that those node constraints are easing as more of the lineup moves to 2-nanometer chips. Mac and iPad supplies are expected to stay constrained in the December quarter, primarily because of memory, with demand higher than expected. Services revenue in that quarter is expected to grow 8% to 9%, and Apple expects the quarter to be the largest for revenue in its history.

Amazon is the other named side of the compute bid. BankInfoSecurity reported that AWS posted its fastest growth in five years, and attributed that pace to demand for AI agents. That is a separate measurement from Apple's device mix, not a figure Apple put on its own bill. What changes for Apple is which lines already show the squeeze: iPhone and Mac set records, iPad and wearables did not, and December is the quarter Apple named memory as the main constraint on Mac and iPad. What changes for Amazon is that agent demand is the workload a trade report credits for the fastest AWS growth in five years, the same class of compute demand TechCrunch says is bidding up the memory device makers now have to buy. The next dated window in these reports is that December quarter.
