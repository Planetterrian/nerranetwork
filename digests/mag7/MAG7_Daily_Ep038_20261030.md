> **Two senators set a November 25 deadline for Meta to account for glasses footage.**

**What You Need to Know:** Two senators set a November 25 deadline for Meta to account for glasses footage. ### Top News
**Senators question Meta on AI glasses privacy: Tech Times**
Senators Ron Wyden and Edward Markey wrote to chief executive Mark Zuckerberg after human reviewers reportedly watched people undressing, using the bathroom, and engaging in sexual activity in footage from Meta’s AI glasses, including people who did not know they were being filmed. They want Meta to explain privacy checks before launch, how captured imagery is used, and whether people can have data deleted. An earlier investigation by two Swedish news outlets, as reported via Gizmodo, found third-party data labellers in Kenya viewed sexual content recorded through the glasses; the senators’ letter says contractors described that footage. Meta disclosures cited by the senators state that imagery processed through the glasses’ visual AI features may be retained and used to train AI systems, including with human reviewers. A wearer option, reported via VitalLaw, lets owners stop visual data from being retained for AI training. The senators write that “individuals captured without their consent have no corresponding control over the processing of their images or data,” and they asked Zuckerberg to respond by 25 November. Source: [techtimes.co.uk](https://www.techtimes.co.uk/senators-question-meta-privacy-ai-smart-glasses-1809567)

**Amazon Q3 sales rise 12% to $201.9B as AWS growth hits 39%: Türkiye Today**
Amazon reported third-quarter net sales of $201.9 billion, up 12 percent from $180.2 billion, or 13 percent excluding a $900 million unfavorable foreign-exchange impact. Operating income rose 61 percent to $28.1 billion from $17.4 billion, and the operating margin reached a record 13.9 percent. AWS net sales rose 39.3 percent to $46 billion, the fastest pace in nearly five years, with an annualized run rate of $184 billion; AWS operating income rose 56 percent to $17.8 billion from $11.4 billion. North America sales grew 6 percent to $112.9 billion; international sales rose 5 percent to $43.1 billion, or 8 percent excluding currency. Net income slipped 2 percent to $20.7 billion, or $1.89 per diluted share, from $21.2 billion, or $1.95, primarily because the year-ago quarter included a $9.5 billion pre-tax gain on Anthropic. NewsCord’s comparison of outlets says earnings per share of $1.89 missed a FactSet consensus of $1.97, and that free cash flow was negative $42.4 billion for the 12 months through September, against positive $14.8 billion a year earlier, with net cash outlays for property and equipment up $94.8 billion. Andy Jassy said AWS growth is projected above 40 percent in both 2027 and 2028, that no business has reached a trillion dollars of annual revenue, and that Amazon may have two such businesses in five to ten years. Fourth-quarter net sales are guided at $240 billion to $247 billion, growth of 12 percent to 16 percent, and operating income at $28.5 billion to $33.5 billion, against $25 billion a year earlier. Cash capital expenditure for 2026 remains about $220 billion. Jassy also cited a contracted backlog of $578 billion and said 2027 capital spending will be meaningfully higher; GeekWire, via that same roundup, reported CFO Brian Olsavsky saying Amazon Leo satellite costs are weighing on the fourth-quarter profit forecast. The Register reported that AWS is installing an AI accelerator every 14 seconds. **The contractor line is already cut, and the recording light now blocks capture: Tech Times**
The same reporting that carries the senators’ letter also says Meta ended the contractor relationship after the Swedish investigation, and that the company said in August it had updated the glasses so recording stops when a wearer covers the light meant to show that recording is on. Those two steps sit on contractor access and on whether a clip is made at all; they do not answer the November 25 request for deletion rights for people who never wore the device. ### The Thread: One inference bill, Meta and Nvidia on the customer list
AWS signed new agreements this quarter with Meta and with NVIDIA, on a list that also names OpenAI, Salesforce, Oracle, Qualcomm, Verizon, ADP, Stripe, and NATO. The product those names sit on is Amazon Bedrock, not a new campus announcement.

Fortune 500 companies spent nearly 7 times more on Bedrock year over year. Total Bedrock token volume grew nearly 17 times year over year. The quarter’s model additions on Bedrock include OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Sol, GPT-6 Luna, and GPT-6.1 Sol; Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.1, Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, and Haiku 5.5; Moonshot AI’s Kimi K3; SpaceXAI’s Grok 4.7; Z.ai’s GLM 5.3; and two purpose-built cybersecurity models from OpenAI. Built-in web search for OpenAI models on Bedrock is meant to ground answers in current information without a customer-built retrieval pipeline. Bedrock Managed Agents, a public preview powered by OpenAI, is the managed path Amazon is offering for production agents.

Strands harness, released as open source, starts with a single line of code and can run locally, on Bedrock AgentCore, or on another provider. AWS says it lowers the cost of agent tasks by 32 percent through token efficiency versus other harnesses on the same frontier models, with equal or better accuracy. Strands Box, in preview, is an open-source sandbox for rules on agent behavior and access, meant to hold on a desktop, in AgentCore, or elsewhere.

AgentCore is in use by tens of thousands of customers, who deployed 2 times more agents in the third quarter than in the prior three quarters combined. Lambda topped a $1 billion annualized revenue run rate; named users include Samsung, Volkswagen, United Airlines, Mercedes-Benz, and Booking.com. Lambda Web Functions adds web apps on that same serverless base, with Lambda handling scaling, multi-region distribution, and active CPU billing.

AWS Continuum, which discovers, prioritizes, validates, and remediates software vulnerabilities at machine speed, is generally available, and AWS is bringing it into Anthropic’s Claude Code, OpenAI’s Codex, and AWS’s Kiro. Named users include Capital One, IBM, Infor, Luminate, and Robinhood. Amazon Quick now reaches Outlook, Word, PowerPoint, and Excel, and can keep running when a laptop is closed.

The shared dependency is the runtime, not the chip invoice. Meta and NVIDIA appear here as AWS customers in the same quarter they show up elsewhere as infrastructure partners and suppliers. Amazon’s measurable claim in this release is usage: 7 times the Fortune 500 Bedrock spend, 17 times the tokens, a 32 percent cut in agent-task cost, twice the agents versus the prior three quarters combined, and a $1 billion Lambda run rate. Meta gets a contracted inference path beside its own buildout. NVIDIA is on the buyer side of AWS while still selling accelerators into everyone else’s factories. The About Amazon note names no next dated step for these agreements. Source: [aboutamazon.com](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-q3-2026-earnings-preview?utm_source=rss)
