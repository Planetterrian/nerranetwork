# MAG 7 Daily
> **Gemini 4 Carbon scores 59, ahead of Claude and GPT-6, for cyber teams first.**

**What You Need to Know:** Google’s Gemini 4 Carbon posts 59 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5.0, just ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra, and the first copies go to cyber defenders rather than the open Gemini app. Apple’s first touchscreen MacBook Pro is on a November 6 launch, and Amazon put five new Alexa devices on sale the same week it confirmed device-unit job cuts.

### Top News
**Google's Gemini 4 Carbon promises a leap in coding and cyber defence: Engadget**
Google released Gemini 4 Carbon on top of Gemini 4 Argon, which it launched two weeks earlier, and Koray Kavukcuoglu, SVP at Google DeepMind and chief AI architect at Google, called Carbon the company’s strongest model, with state-of-the-art cybersecurity and long-horizon work. Access starts this week with selected cyber-defence teams, then trusted partners through the Fairwind programme, while Google says it is in the US government’s voluntary pre-release testing process; Argon is already in the Gemini app and AI search mode, and no wide Carbon date was set. Google says Carbon scored 59 on version 5.0 of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, ahead of Claude Opus 5.5 at 58 and GPT-6 Astra at 57, up from Argon’s 50. On tests Google cited, Carbon scored 88.2 percent on SEC-bench Pro, 54.8 percent on ExploitGym and 71.0 percent on CWE-bench, with secure-code success more than double Argon’s and the Chrome team reporting patch quality more than ten times that of another lab’s large frontier model. Introductory pricing matches Argon at 2 dollars per million input tokens and 10 dollars per million output tokens, cached input 95 percent below the standard input rate, and Basic Tutorials reported those rates later rise to 4 dollars and 20 dollars. ### The Counterpoint
**First on the composite index, second or worse on several jobs that buyers actually run: BigGo Finance**
BigGo Finance’s read of the same launch cuts the “near sweep” claim: paired with Google’s Antigravity tool, Carbon scored 59 on an agentic-coding index, short of Claude Code with Opus 5.5 at 62, and on FrontierSWE 2, GPT-6 Astra led at 65.5 percent against Carbon’s 61.2 percent. On the Vals Index, covering finance, coding, legal and tax tasks, Carbon ranked second at 70.36 percent, behind Sonnet 5.5 at 70.85 percent, and on WebDev Arena it placed fourth. The three headline cyber scores, including 54.8 percent on a 10-hour ExploitGym test, were measured without safety guardrails enabled, so the defensive chain Google is selling was not scored in the configuration customers would actually be allowed to run. ### The Thread: Two cloud lines, one September quarter
Google Cloud revenue more than doubled in the September quarter, to over 30 billion dollars, and that unit’s operating income tripled to 11.12 billion dollars. Azure revenue in the same period was 33 billion dollars, up 48 percent. Yesterday the open question on this show was still the growth rate; those two lines are the answer, and they leave the long-time number-three cloud business a few billion dollars from Azure.

Alphabet reported a 25 percent rise in company revenue to 127.58 billion dollars, slightly short of Wall Street forecasts, The Information wrote. Free cash flow turned negative by more than 9 billion dollars, against positive free cash flow of more than 24 billion dollars a year earlier. Search advertising grew 12 percent to 63.35 billion dollars, the softest growth in that business since the second quarter of 2025. Sundar Pichai said year-over-year search-query growth is the highest in five years, and he credited AI investment. Philipp Schindler told analysts that strong growth in AI Mode queries weighed on the result because monetization there is lower: Google is not showing as many ads in AI Mode as it does in ordinary search.

The cloud comparison is not a pure hosting match. Google Cloud’s revenue includes tensor-processing-unit systems that customers install in their own data centres. It did call those sales a meaningful contributor to revenue growth. Thomas Kurian, Google Cloud’s chief executive, said the company is seeing extraordinary demand, and executives told analysts to expect TPU sales to count for even more of Google Cloud next year.

Microsoft’s September-quarter revenue rose 19 percent to 92.3 billion dollars, one percentage point faster than the growth rate reported for the prior quarter, a figure Digitimes also carried. Microsoft 365 Commercial Cloud, the line that includes Copilot and the broader Office suite, rose 17 percent to 27.7 billion dollars, a slight acceleration from the June quarter. What Microsoft did not repeat was the paid Copilot seat count it had published before: 30 million at the end of June, up from 15 million at the end of 2025, each paying 30 dollars a month on top of the application fees. Satya Nadella said the number of customers paying for usage-based Copilot features grew 75 percent in the September quarter. CFO Amy Hood said average revenue per user rose because of that pay-as-you-go use. Late last month Microsoft folded Copilot features into one application, in part to steer people toward agents and usage billing, including tools set against Meta’s Muse.

Both companies are funding the same kind of build, and the quarter shows different proofs. Google’s cash flow went negative while cloud operating income tripled, and part of the cloud line is now chip hardware leaving the building. Microsoft kept commercial-cloud growth in the teens and stopped publishing the seat count that had been the simple evidence subscriptions were absorbing the spend. The next marker Kurian gave analysts is next year, when TPU sales are expected to weigh more on Google Cloud.
