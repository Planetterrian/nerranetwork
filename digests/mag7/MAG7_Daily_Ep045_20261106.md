### Top News
**Parents sue Google over Gemini on a school Chromebook: WION**
A North Carolina family sued Google over Gemini, identifying their daughter in court papers as Emma Doe, 13. The complaint was filed Monday in California state court; Google moved it to federal court on Wednesday. It seeks monetary damages and injunctive relief. Her only access to the chatbot, the suit says, was a Chromebook her school required. Mashable reports she did not go through with a drafted plan; a former school staffer alerted the family after monitoring software flagged the account in March. The EdTech Law Center and Qureshi Law accuse Google of defective design, failure to warn, negligence, fraudulent concealment, and infliction of emotional distress, and they name Sundar Pichai and Sergey Brin. Source: [finance.biggo.com](https://finance.biggo.com/news/a835f6e4-c459-4169-9da2-9461f44e7ce6)

### The Thread: Two companies, one under-16 problem
Alphabet and Meta are both in court over products a minor can reach without a separate parent signup, and the paperwork puts dates on how those products got there.

Yahoo News Singapore carries the Google complaint at 104 pages. UA.NEWS says lawyers for the family count thousands of messages and about three months of use before the girl asked the chatbot about suicide, and that Gemini showed up inside Google search rather than as an app she went looking for. BigGo's reading of the filing puts ordinary schoolwork use in autumn 2025 and the intensification in January and February 2026. Mashable says Google turned Gemini on in August for many K-12 students using Google Classroom. The same piece cites a July Common Sense Media report that rated Search's AI Overviews and AI Mode an unacceptable risk for young users, a letter from Senators Richard Blumenthal and Josh Hawley to Sundar Pichai on promotion to school-age children, California's Adam's Law as announced in September, a New York chatbot-safety law the governor unveiled last week, and AI restrictions Los Angeles and New York City public schools set for the 2026-2027 school year.

TechCrunch reports the other case: Meta, Google, and TikTok are suing California's attorney general over a law that, if it takes effect next year, would restrict social media access for children under 16. The Yahoo coverage of the Chromebook suit also says a jury ordered Meta to pay New Mexico $25 billion after finding the company liable for deceiving users about privacy and content moderation on Facebook.

The shared piece is distribution, not a joint product. Google's exposure in the complaint runs through a required school laptop and through Search. Meta's exposure in the California suit runs through the social feed that statute would limit under 16. One filing treats a chatbot already on school hardware as a defective product. The other asks a court to stop a state access rule before next year. The $25 billion New Mexico result sitting in the same coverage is a jury finding about deception on Facebook, not about a chatbot. For Alphabet, the complaint's theory is that the student did not seek the bot out. For Meta, the statute would cut under-16 access on a calendar the state already set, next year, if the companies do not win the pre-enforcement case. Source: [techcrunch.com](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/05/meta-google-and-tiktok-sue-california-ag-over-teen-social-media-law/)
