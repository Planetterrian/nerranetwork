After the tablet leak, a Singapore tax disclosure, an empty office on an Nvidia approval list, and a lighter basic Kindle, then the counterpoint, a thread on where the data-center build can still go, and a look back at the week.

Apple’s leaked twelfth-generation iPad, in a SaranByte video that Geeky Gadgets and BigGo Finance both carry, pairs an A19 processor with eight gigabytes of memory, against the eleventh generation’s A16 and six gigabytes.

That pairing is what the reports say finally clears the hardware bar for on-device intelligence, including live translation, predictive text and heavier photo edits, which the current cheap iPad misses entirely, Siri included.

The leak also puts Apple’s C1X modem on the tablet for faster 5G, plus an N1 chipset with Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6 and Thread, the same wireless set already on the expensive hardware. The screen does not move with the silicon. It stays a non-laminated LCD.

Apple has not commented, the specs are projections rather than a company sheet, and BigGo Finance says the spring twenty twenty-seven window itself could slip.

The Financial Times, working from new disclosures, says Apple saved two point seven billion dollars in tax by booking profits in Singapore. On those disclosures the city-state has overtaken Ireland as the iPhone maker’s biggest tax shelter.

The Verge, citing The New York Times, says a firm cleared to handle Nvidia’s H200 chips in China has nothing in the office. New Matrix is on the approved list, and the rooms are a hollow gray shell. Earlier this week the constraint on those chips was who the cloud client’s customers could be. The new question is whether Commerce or Nvidia checked the handler at all.

Amazon’s basic Kindle has been redesigned, TechCrunch reports, and the new one costs more than the old one. The review’s case for it is weight and the screen: lighter in the hand, clearer on the page, enough that the pricier Kindles are easier to pass up.

AWS Continuum is generally available, tech-insider.org lists, at two hundred US dollars per million lines, for code vulnerabilities. The same listing has no Australia region.

Google DeepMind economist Alex Imas, BigGo Finance reports, says even millions of AI agents will not deliver double-digit growth. The claim as carried is that headcount of agents is not the same thing as that growth rate.

Microsoft has taken Copilot off smart TVs, BGR reports. One fewer assistant sits on the set.

The price path cuts against the cheap-tablet pitch. BigGo Finance says the eleventh-generation iPad already climbed from three hundred forty-nine dollars to four hundred forty-nine, and that the next start would be a fifty-seven percent increase over the entry price of two generations ago.

That pace, the report says, hits the students, families and casual buyers who treated the base iPad as the default. The OLED Mini sits at six hundred ninety-nine dollars, so the gap shrinks to one hundred fifty, and a shopper may decide the step up is the rational buy. Average selling prices rise. The budget tier thins.

Google, Amazon’s cloud rival in this queue and Tesla’s political neighbor this week, said Wednesday it would spend fifteen billion dollars expanding data-center and cloud operations in Malaysia.

Abram Brown’s weekend letter in The Information sets that cheque against Tuesday’s election, which he reads in part as a voter referendum on the AI era, and against a Silicon Valley fear that Democrats will stop the pour. A Dutch bank, he writes, is trying to unload a half-billion-dollar slice of data-center loans in a hurry, some of it tied to OpenAI’s Project Stargate.

Anthropic’s investors, in the same piece, are gloomy about an IPO because they doubt a sought-after one point five trillion dollar valuation will hold. Tesla shows up as Elon Musk, not as a site.

James Talarico, Texas’s new Democratic senator, spoke the day after winning a race Musk had spent heavily to swing the other way, called him a great business leader, entrepreneur and innovator, and said he would work with him. Musk’s reply was “Nice of him to say that.” Nvidia is the supplier in Paul Kedrosky’s remark on The Information’s account.

The SK Ventures general partner said a slowdown is very bad for a data-center supplier because a significant fraction of the queue is, at the very least, pushed out.

What they share is the queue, not a contract. Google can fund a Malaysia build while a lender tries to sell paper that already includes Stargate-linked loans. Kedrosky’s point is that Nvidia does not need a cancellation to feel it. A pushed delivery date is enough to hit the vendor investors had cast as the winner of the build.

Musk’s link is the week of the vote itself: money against a candidate, then a compliment the next day, which Brown offers as proof that flattery can settle the same nerves. Brown’s own bet is that the campaign language fades once people are in office. He cites Gavin Baker’s read of data-center plans from Texas’s new governor, Gina Hinojosa, and Baker’s line that she sounds reasonable.

He notes New York still has none of Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s city-run grocery stores. Nikita Bier, a former product lead at Musk’s X, posted that what is coming will be like the Nuremberg trials for AI researchers. Brown treats that as a warning about how the industry talks, not as a case name. The other map in the letter is orbit.

On Friday Ron Baron told CNBC space data centers could have significant capacity as soon as twenty twenty-eight. Brown does not take that year as a schedule, and he compares the economics to life on Saturn.

Apple has put the count of apps optimized for iPhone Duo above one hundred thousand. Veterans in Rosemont are getting free Meta AI glasses meant for vision loss, Hoodline reports.

On the calendar, Apple’s HomeView hub and the updated Apple TV 4K are set for Friday, November thirteenth. The revamped Health app, back in the iOS twenty-seven point three beta after it was pulled before the last public release, is slated to launch to the public before the end of twenty twenty-six.

Before we go, Abram Brown’s marker for whether the rhetoric actually fades is next year, when he expects many of those projects to find a way through.
