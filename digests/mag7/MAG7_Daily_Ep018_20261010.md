### Top News
**Unpatched Muse key lets local Mac software redirect dictation: 36Kr**
Patrick Wardle, founder of the Objective-See Foundation, has disclosed an unpatched flaw in Meta's new Muse macOS client, 36Kr reports. Software running as an ordinary user can overwrite an undocumented preference, endo_voyager_dictation_endpoint, without an administrator password or a macOS authorization prompt. That key names the server that receives dictation audio and returns the transcript. Change it, and the client sends raw microphone audio and the Muse account's authentication token to an endpoint the attacker controls. Wardle ran a proxy that forwarded traffic on to Meta while keeping the token and the audio. With the session, hidden instructions can be added to voice requests so the assistant acts in the background, including taking local documents or WhatsApp message records. Mark Zuckerberg had said the assistant was built with privacy and security at its core from the start. Source: [eu.36kr.com](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/4019730918363011)

**SandboxAQ's AQPotency opens on the AWS Marketplace: Quantum Zeitgeist**
SandboxAQ's AQPotency is available to AWS commercial customers in North America through the AWS Marketplace, and it deploys as an Amazon SageMaker model package. It predicts how strongly a candidate compound binds a protein from a UniProt ID and a ligand SMILES string, with no 3D crystal structure, which brings in targets such as membrane proteins, mutant panels, and proteins that have not been crystallized. Teams can rank millions of compounds in a few hours on standard CPU instances inside their own AWS environment, through a real-time endpoint or large asynchronous jobs. SandboxAQ says it collects no metadata on users' targets or molecules. The model has also been reachable through Claude via the Model Context Protocol since August. Source: [quantumzeitgeist.com](https://quantumzeitgeist.com/aqpotency-available-aws-customers-north/)

**Google tests Gemini 4 Carbon on internal coding: The Decoder**
Google has put a Gemini 4 checkpoint called Carbon on its internal coding platform Jetski over the past few days, where it is said to beat still-unshipped Argon mainly on programming tasks, The Decoder reports from documents, screenshots, and internal chats Business Insider reviewed. One employee compared Carbon to Anthropic's Opus 5.5 and said it still needs more testing; early Argon builds reminded another employee of the older Opus 5 on some coding tasks. Google's own map calls Argon the frontier reasoning model, Flash the speed-and-volume model, Omni the generative-media model, and Gemma the lightweight open-weights edge model. Carbon and Barium read as checkpoints inside the Argon family: an employee called Carbon "Gemini pro next model," and internal documents had labeled the now-unveiled Argon as "Barium-B." DeepMind's Vedant Misra replied on X, "Have you heard of recursive self improvement." There is still no official Gemini 4 launch date. Logan Kilpatrick has confirmed the team is working on getting the most out of Argon. Source: [the-decoder.com](https://the-decoder.com/googles-gemini-4-carbon-model-is-reportedly-matching-anthropics-opus-5-5-coding-performance/)

### The Counterpoint
**The Muse hijack still needs software already on the Mac, and dictation switched on: 36Kr**
Wardle's path is not a remote break-in by itself. The rewrite is open to unprivileged local processes and arbitrary scripts already running as the user, and the microphone audio and token leave only when dictation is enabled. Meta has not issued a security advisory, the flaw has no CVE ID, and the open question is whether that preference stays writable before a patch lands. Source: [eu.36kr.com](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/4019730918363011)

### The Thread: Three home platforms, seven bulbs
A roundup of seven smart bulbs treats Alexa, Google Home, and Apple Home as the test a lamp has to pass. Geeky Gadgets, drawing on a Smart Home 101 breakdown, highlights the ABRA bulb at a color rendering index of 90, with tunable white from 2700K to 6500K. ABRA comes as an A19 rated at 800 lumens and a BR30 rated at 750 lumens, and the write-up says it works with Alexa, Google Home, and Siri without a hub. LIFX is the brightness and wide-color option, also hub-free. Philips Hue is the customization lane: Chroma Sync, plus an optional Hue Bridge when the three voice platforms are not enough control. Govee is listed for music sync, 16 million colors, and 54 preset scenes. The Amazon Basics bulb is described as streamlined Alexa integration, not as the lamp built to treat Google and Apple the same way. Third Reality's ZL1 is the Zigbee bulb, a mesh radio aimed at network reliability instead of depending only on the Wi-Fi path those assistants usually use. Alphabet, Amazon, and Apple are not the lamp makers in this comparison. They are the compatibility gate. A bulb that misses one assistant drops out of a list organized around exactly those three. The spec that splits the field is the hub. ABRA, LIFX, and the Basics path are sold as hub-free. Hue's bridge is optional, and the breakdown calls that bridge the route to maximum control. Zigbee on the ZL1 is the pitch when a Wi-Fi link to an assistant is the failure point. Three of the seven set the checkbox. The bulb makers spend lumens, color counts, and radio choices proving they clear it. Source: [geeky-gadgets.com](https://www.geeky-gadgets.com/best-smart-bulb-2026/)
