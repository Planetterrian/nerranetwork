# MAG 7 Daily
> **Nvidia's $2,399 RTX Spark laptops put up to 128GB in a portable Windows PC.**

**What You Need to Know:** Nvidia says RTX Spark laptops are on sale now, with Blackwell RTX graphics, as much as 128GB of unified memory, and a starting price of $2,399. Microsoft has walked away from renting 23,000 Nvidia chips at a delayed Essex hall, and a Financial Times report carries Bernstein's view that only about 35 percent of the planned US data-center pipeline is credible. Google has put a $100 Home Speaker on sale, built for Gemini for Home, six years after the last Nest speakers.

### Top News
**RTX Spark laptops go on sale with up to 128GB: NVIDIA Newsroom**

Nvidia's newsroom says RTX Spark laptops are available now. The machines bring Blackwell RTX graphics, up to 128GB of unified memory, and the full Nvidia AI platform into slim, premium Windows PCs. The starting price is $2,399. The makers Nvidia names are ASUS, Dell, HP, Lenovo, Microsoft, and MSI. Nvidia describes the use as local AI, content creation, and gaming in a portable device. Microsoft appears here as one of the laptop brands, not as the buyer of a data-center lease. Source: [blogs.nvidia.com](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/local-ai-rtx-spark-windows-laptops-celebration/)

**Google releases a $100 Home Speaker for Gemini: New York Post**

The New York Post's Nishka Dhawan reported that Google has released a Home Speaker priced at $100, six years after the prior Nest speakers, and that this model replaces that older generation. She wrote that it is built to function with Gemini for Home. The dimensions she listed are 3.4 inches high by 4.2 inches in diameter, weight 0.9 pounds including the power cable, a 58mm full-range driver, and 360-degree sound. Connectivity is Wi-Fi 6, Bluetooth 5.4, and Thread 1.3, with three far-field microphones and a 30W USB-C power adapter on a cord. Colors are Berry, Porcelain, Hazel, and Jade. Setup ran through the Google Home app and took her less than five minutes. She said Gemini let her set schedules, have the calendar read out, hold a conversation, and interrupt the assistant mid-conversation, and that the speaker can sync to other smart devices in the home. Some of those smart features need Google Home Premium. With the Advanced plan, she wrote, a user can search camera history or get a Home Brief recapping what happened at home while they were out. Source: [nypost.com](https://nypost.com/shopping/google-home-speaker-review/)

**Gurman says iPhone Duo walk-in calls are premature: Bloomberg**

He added that he is not saying it will not happen, only that it has not. Separately he wrote that in China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, and Korea the same message applies, but only to the iPhone 18 Pro Max. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/markgurman/status/2111635252775141633)

### The Counterpoint
**A 23,000-chip rental is off, and Bernstein calls 35 percent of the US pipeline credible: The Telegraph**

A laptop on a store shelf is not a rented hall full of the same vendor's accelerators. The Telegraph, as carried by Techmeme, reported that Microsoft has cancelled its deal with Nscale to serve as anchor tenant and to rent 23,000 Nvidia AI chips at a planned data center in Essex that has faced repeated delays. Bloomberg, citing the Telegraph, reported that Microsoft has pulled out of that UK data-center deal with British neocloud Nscale at a critical time for the startup's initial-public-offering hopes. On the wider map, Techmeme's note of a Financial Times report says RegLayer counted 47 or more large US data-center projects delayed, withdrawn, or blocked in 2026. In that same report, Bernstein says about 35 percent of the planned US data-center pipeline is credible. That share is the figure that decides how much announced hall capacity sitting behind Nvidia chips should be treated as real, against a product launch that puts Blackwell graphics in a Windows laptop rather than in Essex. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/Techmeme/status/2111700272468967765)

### The Thread: An hourly gap, a bond quote, a chip with 120 cores
Tech-insider put a $5.41-an-hour gap on Nvidia H100 prices across Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud. The New York Times, via Techmeme, reported that CoreWeave's bond yield has topped 15 percent, that insuring SoftBank debt is getting pricier, and that some Oracle debt trades near 80 to 90 cents on the dollar. InfoQ reported that AWS will run its European Sovereign Cloud without the global backbone for several hours. A shattered.io item described a Google Cloud U4 chip with 120 cores aimed at high-frequency trading.

The shared object is compute a customer can rent or a region can keep running. Amazon, Microsoft, and Alphabet are the three clouds in the H100 comparison, so a buyer of that Nvidia part can be shown different hourly prices at each of them. Nvidia is in the comparison as the maker of the H100, not as the author of the price survey. The gap is a rental spread. On the survey's own terms it does not add a hall, a power feed, or a permitted site. CoreWeave, SoftBank, and Oracle sit outside the seven. The Times account still sits beside Amazon, Microsoft, and Alphabet because the borrowing it describes is the kind that has been paying for AI buildings and the accelerators inside them. A yield through the level the Times printed is a costly coupon. Paper changing hands in the 80-to-90-cent range the Times printed is below face value. Either print makes the next unbuilt hall a harder financing job than chips already installed and already posted by the hour.

Amazon's sovereign-cloud exercise asks a different version of the same scarcity question. Taking that European region off the global backbone tests whether it still runs when the usual worldwide network is absent, for a stretch measured in hours rather than in a price per hour. The exercise does not add H100 capacity. They are named, with Amazon, in the price comparison, so they share the commercial spread and not the disconnection test.

What changes follows from which figure touches which company. Amazon carries a place in the H100 spread and the sovereign-cloud hours: one is a price a customer pays, the other a timed resilience test of a European region. Microsoft carries Azure's place in that same spread, so a workload that can move can use the gap against Azure or in Azure's favor. The UK lease Microsoft dropped is a building contract, separate from this rate card. Alphabet carries Google Cloud's place in the spread and the U4's 120 cores, a trading chip rather than the H100 being priced by the hour. A short path through a market and a rental gap are different offers. They are both Google Cloud putting a number on specialized compute while Amazon prices an H100 hour and schedules a disconnection. Nvidia carries the H100 as the part all three clouds are pricing, so the spread is what those clouds charge for Nvidia hardware, not a list price Nvidia posted in today's material. Apple's figure in this set is a device price: MacRumors reported the iPhone Duo starts at 1,999 in the United States, a purchase rather than an hour of rental. The Times yield and the Oracle cents remain a backdrop for all of them and a filing from none of them. Source: [tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/aws-vs-azure-vs-gcp-gpu-cloud-pricing-2026/)
