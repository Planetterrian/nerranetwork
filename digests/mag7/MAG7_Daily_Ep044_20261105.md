**Amazon S3 Tables now offer managed migration of Apache Hive tables to Apache Iceberg: Amazon Web Services**
Amazon S3 Tables now run a managed migration of Apache Hive tables to Apache Iceberg for Parquet tables in Amazon S3 that are registered in the AWS Glue Data Catalog. AWS says the path needs no pipelines to build and no compute to provision, and that production workloads keep running. Before any copy, S3 Tables identify every source object in scope and generate a compatibility report, then handle validation, compatibility checks, type conversions, file recompression, and compaction. Syncs can run as often as needed without stopping writes to the source, except for a few minutes at cutover. The destination stays read-only during migration and becomes read-write once migration is completed. Customers pay only for data copied, with no charge for the Hive-to-Iceberg conversion, and source objects in S3 Intelligent-Tiering stay in their current access tier. It is available in US East (Northern Virginia and Ohio), US West (Oregon), Asia Pacific (Mumbai, Singapore, Sydney, and Tokyo), and Europe (Frankfurt, Ireland, and Stockholm), for Glue-registered Parquet Hive tables in the same account and Region. Query paths named in the post include Amazon Athena and Amazon SageMaker Unified Studio. Source: [aws.amazon.com](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/amazon-s3-tables-now-offer-managed-migration-of-apache-hive-tables-to-apache-iceberg/)

**Apple now lets developers offer discounts when users move to cancel subscriptions: 9to5Mac**
Apple has turned on Retention Messaging, a subscription tool it announced at WWDC earlier this year. Messages appear in the cancellation flow and can include text, images, or a special offer, configured in App Store Connect with subscriber eligibility and a wait time between messages. A Retention Messaging API, access by request, lets a developer pick which message a subscriber sees from that subscriber's details. Developers can create up to 10 retention messages per app, each tied to one or more subscriptions. This is not the 2024 Win-Back Offers, which discounted former subscribers after a cancel or expiry. These messages reach people before the cancellation is finished. Source: [9to5mac.com](https://9to5mac.com/2026/11/04/apple-now-lets-developers-offer-discounts-when-users-move-to-cancel-subscriptions/)

### The Thread: Debt taken, a Texas option refused
Meta's first euro bonds drew €53 billion in investor orders, TokenPost reported, record demand for that debut euro sale. Bloomberg, on the first day of trading, said the freshly minted euro bonds were being quoted above face value after a sale that drew record investor orders for a corporate bond in Europe. Yesterday the sale was the news. Today the paper is already above par, and the order book has a published size.

Microsoft made the opposite capital call. The Financial Times reported that Microsoft rejected a multibillion-dollar AI deal with the UK start-up Nscale, turning down an option to more than double a Texas data-centre contract. The paper called it a fresh blow ahead of the start-up's IPO. One of the seven is raising a new currency of debt into the build. Another is leaving a doubling option on the table in Texas rather than taking the extra hall.

Amazon and Meta show up again as the customers a phone-chip company wants next. On Qualcomm's fourth-quarter 2026 earnings call, The Verge reported, the company laid out a push into AI data centers through partnerships with companies including Amazon and Meta. The same piece said smartphone sales were tanking by around 20 percent compared with 2025. The data-center pitch is the replacement story for a phone market that shrank by about a fifth in a year.

The chip-shipment tally points the same way, and it pulls Apple in. Counterpoint Research, as reported by 9to5Mac, found Apple and Samsung were the only major smartphone-chip vendors to gain share in the third quarter of 2026, while global shipments declined 21 percent year over year. Share gained inside a falling total is not a larger phone market. It is a reshuffle while Qualcomm talks up halls instead of handsets.

What is shared is the bill for AI sites and the slump in the phone business that used to fund these suppliers. Meta is paying for the build with euro debt that buyers bid to a record and then marked above par on day one. Microsoft is choosing which contracted expansion not to take, and that choice hits Nscale before an IPO rather than adding a doubled Texas site to Microsoft's own book. Amazon and Meta are named partners for a chip vendor whose phone sales are down hard against last year. Apple's gain is mix: more of a smaller shipment pool, not a phone cycle that rescues the old line.

What changes, without treating the assembler print as the payoff, is who holds the next constraint. Meta has a euro curve it did not have. Microsoft's refusal means the extra Texas capacity in that option is not Microsoft's to switch on. Amazon and Meta become the design-in targets for a vendor exiting a weak phone year. Apple can take vendor share and still sit in a market whose units fell. None of these filings dates the quarter when the data-center spend The Verge called unrewarded starts to throw off the returns the earnings calls promised.
