# MAG 7 Daily
> **Three model filings now mark China iPhones, moving Apple Intelligence toward a mainland launch.**

**What You Need to Know:** Apple’s China-market iPhones now carry network-access filings for Apple Intelligence and two Alibaba models, the clearest regulatory step yet toward a mainland Apple Intelligence rollout. The same week Apple put a $299 home hub on the calendar, Microsoft’s Azure leadership asked for cost-cut ideas of at least 20%, and Google opened paid access to Gemini 4 Argon.

### Top News
**China-market iPhones add three AI model filings as Apple Intelligence nears a mainland launch: BigGo Finance**
BigGo Finance reported that on October 14 users found MIIT telecommunications equipment network-access records showing Apple completed license renewals on September 29 for several China-market iPhones, adding filings for Apple Intelligence, Tongyi Wanxiang and Tongyi Qianwen. The iPhone Duo, model A3721, received trial approval on September 2 and the September 29 renewal; the same filing set also covers the iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 16 Pro, iPhone Air, iPhone 17 and iPhone 18 Pro series under model A3718. Apple Technology Development (Shanghai) completed generative AI service registration for iPhones on July 8, and on July 15 the Cyberspace Administration of China said Apple Intelligence and six other mobile-device generative AI services — from Huawei, OPPO, vivo, Xiaomi, Samsung and ZTE — had completed registration. In February 2025 Alibaba chairman Joe Tsai confirmed China iPhones would use Alibaba’s AI, and Alibaba said Qwen would be integrated into Apple Intelligence on iOS, iPadOS, macOS and visionOS. Tim Cook, whom the report describes as executive chairman after stepping down as CEO, was in Guangzhou on October 10 and at Apple’s Sanlitun store in Beijing on October 11. iPhone Duo pre-sales begin October 16, with sales on October 23. Source: [finance.biggo.com](https://finance.biggo.com/news/f60fc00b-ee25-4abb-88c6-fe3245401ed4)

**Apple unveils HomeView at $299 and a Home Collection with LG and Schneider: Yahoo Finance**
Yahoo Finance reported that Apple announced HomeView on Tuesday, CEO John Ternus’s first launch outside iPhones since he took over from Tim Cook in September. The hub has a 7-inch screen for wall or table, uses facial recognition to show up to six users their own Messages, Photos, Calendar and Reminders, runs an A18 chip with Siri AI that can continue on an iPhone or iPad, and also handles FaceTime, music and photo-frame use. Pricing starts at $299, with shipping on November 13; it can show camera feeds, adjust a thermostat or lock doors, setup is one tap from an iPhone, and Apple says Home Collection accessories encrypt data so only the user and people they share with can see it. Apple also launched a new Apple TV 4K at $299, up from $199, and a HomePod Mini at $149, up from $129; Dataconomy identifies that speaker as HomePod Mini 2 with an S10 chip. Homekit News and Reviews reported Apple Home Collection, developed with LG Electronics and Schneider Electric: indoor and outdoor cameras, thermostats, temperature sensors, a video doorbell and a smart deadbolt, availability beginning in December, selected products in early 2027, set up from an iPhone with no separate manufacturer app or account. Every product in the range supports Thread; the deadbolt, thermostats and sensors are described as Matter compatible; LG cameras record at up to 2.5K, a supported iCloud+ plan holds 10 days of event video history, and Apple says camera footage and Home app data are end-to-end encrypted. ### Company Desk
**Google opens Gemini 4 Argon to paying users, with rates that double on December 1: WinBuzzer**
WinBuzzer reported that Google opened Gemini 4 Argon on October 13 to paying developers, businesses and higher-tier subscribers, extending access past an initial cybersecurity-partner group. Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers get Argon in the Gemini app and in AI Mode in Search; Ultra subscribers also get it in Antigravity, and Google is offering 10,000 scientists six months of the highest Antigravity usage tier free, subject to eligibility, capacity and selection. The combined context window holds 1,048,576 tokens, shared by the prompt, internal thinking and the answer. Developer rates of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens double on December 1. Google’s engineers shipped two of six proposed code optimizations after an overnight Argon run; Artificial Analysis’s September 30 evaluation gave Argon at high reasoning a composite score of 53, and as of October 13 Argon’s aggregate on the Vals Index was 68.90%, a first for a Gemini model in Vals’s September 30 announcement. ### The Counterpoint

On August 9 Apple removed a Simplified Chinese Mac user-guide page titled “Using Qwen with Apple Intelligence on Mac,” and the page reverted to “Using ChatGPT with Apple Intelligence.” The September 29 renewals put filing numbers on network-access licenses; they are not, in this report, a stated date when mainland buyers can turn the features on. ### The Thread: Credits for labs, money for agent safety
Up to 200 European scientific researchers are the headcount in Google’s Dublin commitment, and the cash attached to them is $3 million in computing credits. TradingView, citing the companies, reported that Google DeepMind and Google Cloud will give that group access to frontier AI models plus the credits, as support for scientific research in the region and for the European Union’s Resource for AI Science in Europe, RAISE, which aims to expand computing resources and AI tools for researchers. The named tools are AlphaFold 3 for predicting molecular structures, WeatherNext for weather forecasting, AlphaGenome for studying DNA, AlphaEvolve for algorithmic discovery, and AlphaEarth Foundations for understanding changes on Earth. Five tools, one credit pool, one researcher cap. Google said, in a statement released at an AI summit in Dublin, that delivering compute and state-of-the-art tools directly to research teams is meant to accelerate European innovation and reinforce the continent’s scientific competitiveness.

NVIDIA’s own newsroom, the same day, put a different number on a different European ledger: $250 million over the next five years to advance UK agentic safety and security research. A NVIDIA blog the same day describes the UK ecosystem moving from ambition to application with NVIDIA. Another NVIDIA blog says European cities are deploying NVIDIA-powered AI agents to run urban operations. The Neuron’s October 14 roundup added a third figure, reported rather than posted on NVIDIA’s newsroom pages in this set: Nvidia plans about €2 billion in new European AI deals. The same roundup said about 400 scientists urged a global AI oversight body. That appeal is not a Google or NVIDIA program, but it sits on the same day’s page as the €2 billion line.

The shared piece is Europe as a buyer of access and a site for agent work, not a single contract linking the seven. Google’s Dublin package is scientific models and cloud credits for a capped set of researchers under RAISE. NVIDIA’s published commitment is safety and security research money in the UK, spread across five years, plus city-agent deployments described in its blog and a larger European deal pipeline that The Neuron reported at about €2 billion. The dependency runs through whoever supplies the models, the credits and the agent stack the labs and cities actually get to run.

What changes is narrow and dated to today’s posts. Up to 200 researchers get named scientific systems and $3 million in credits, not a consumer chatbot tier. NVIDIA’s $250 million is a five-year research commitment on agentic safety and security, not a data-centre groundbreaking in the posts supplied today. The €2 billion is a plan The Neuron reported, distinct from the $250 million NVIDIA itself announced. The about 400 scientists are an oversight appeal in that same roundup, not a headcount inside either company’s program. The public step these sources date is October 14 itself: the Dublin statement, the NVIDIA newsroom commitment, and The Neuron’s report of the deal pipeline.
