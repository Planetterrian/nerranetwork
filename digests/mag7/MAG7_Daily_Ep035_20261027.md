### Top News
**Nvidia puts $3 billion into Meta and expands the Muse partnership: NVIDIA Newsroom**
Nvidia will invest $3 billion in Meta’s equity offering, a planned commitment rather than a completed sale, as the companies expand work on Muse and the Muse family of models to improve training and inference and to cut the cost of running those systems. Meta intends to expand deployment of Nvidia Vera Rubin systems and Vera CPUs for next-generation agents, plus Spectrum-X Ethernet networking and BlueField-4. The companies are also considering Nvidia’s OpenShell Reference Architecture for safer agent deployment and Sentry for monitoring agent behavior. Jensen Huang said agents are a new computing paradigm and that the partnership is meant to optimize infrastructure so Muse can serve billions of people. Mark Zuckerberg said Muse is how Meta brings personal superintelligence to everyone. Nvidia said Muse models and agents on its infrastructure have delivered frontier-level benchmark performance and surging consumer downloads since launch. **Microsoft opens a voluntary exit to about 60,000 employees: Instant Gaming News**
An internal memo from chief people officer Amy Coleman launched a time-limited voluntary separation programme for eligible employees at level 70 and below on participating teams in Canada, India, the United Kingdom and the United States, about 60,000 people, roughly a quarter of the workforce. Coleman said it lets people who want a future outside Microsoft leave with support and gives participating organizations another way to match staffing to changing needs; some organizations are not offering it, and it builds on a voluntary programme earlier this year. Eligible people were to get an email with the terms. In India, where Microsoft has about 23,000 employees and eligibility was not broken out, the decision deadline is December 1, and the package is understood to match standard severance: one month of base salary per completed year, plus health insurance and career-transition services. A Microsoft spokesperson said the company is backing India’s AI ambitions with $20.5 billion committed from 2025 through 2029 and is on track to equip 20 million people in India with AI skills by 2030. ### The Thread: Three cheques, one crowded shelf
Almost a quarter of a trillion dollars is the sum The Information put on three fundraising plans unveiled the same Monday, from Anthropic, SpaceX and Meta. SpaceX signed a $40 billion financing agreement with an entity managed by Apollo Global Management to lease graphics processors, which SpaceX called the largest GPU financing in history. Colleague Alex Eichenstein reported that the money is expected to run through a special purpose vehicle kept off SpaceX’s balance sheet, a structure that could limit any hit to its credit rating. Meta’s board committee, in a filing last week, said timing mattered so the company could secure capital before competitors saturate funding options, and it named a congested pipeline that includes Alphabet, SpaceX, Anthropic and OpenAI. Microsoft is the other of the seven in that piece, and the new number is take-up rather than eligibility: the global workforce is 223,000, an earlier voluntary offer went to 9,000 people and about a third accepted, and Microsoft is expected to see a smaller share this time, fewer than 20,000.

The shared constraint is the funding shelf, not a shared product. Meta’s directors treated Alphabet’s deals as occupying the same equity and debt options Meta wants to use before they fill up. Microsoft is offering exits while that window is still described as open. The Apollo lease is the article’s exhibit that a company with Elon Musk’s platform is choosing an off-balance-sheet GPU structure rather than a plain borrowing. Musk, in a post on X that The Information quotes, said debt markets in recent weeks have closed to firms whose revenue is only from AI data centers, and that more than 200 neoclouds face what he called guppy-level death and consolidation. The Information notes that the argument is self-serving: fewer neoclouds could leave more capital for SpaceX and other large names later. Apollo’s chief economist, Torsten Slok, wrote over the weekend that despite the investment and the hype, the technology has delivered zero financial payoff to corporate America outside of tech.

For Meta, the filing turns the raise into a race against other people’s calendars, including Alphabet’s, rather than a quiet registration. For Microsoft, the buyout math is the operational twin of the same squeeze: on the take-up the company is expected to see, the offer does not empty a quarter of a 223,000-person company. The risk the piece leaves on the table is Slok’s zero, and Musk’s warning that investor disfavor could spread from neoclouds to larger AI companies if customers keep seeing no payoff. Source: [theinformation.com](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/anthropic-spacex-meta-cash-grabs)
