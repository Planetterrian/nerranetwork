# MAG 7 Daily
> **Apple says affected iPhone 18 Pro Max phones on AT&T must be replaced, not patched.**

**What You Need to Know:** Apple says iPhone 18 Pro Max phones on AT&T that have already lost cellular service cannot be fixed in software and must be replaced. A software update and a carrier-settings update are meant to stop the failure from spreading. Elsewhere, Apple is tightening Mac disk access as Meta gives Muse’s code away, and Google is cutting which Gemini models free and Plus users can open.

### Top News
**Affected iPhone 18 Pro Max phones on AT&T must be replaced: MacRumors**
Apple said on October 2 that a small number of iPhone 18 Pro Max users on AT&T can lose cellular service, so calls, texts and mobile data stop. Bloomberg reported the bug affects customers who recently upgraded. Apple released iOS 27.0.1 earlier in the week and an AT&T carrier-settings update on October 2, and it wants every iPhone 18 Pro Max user to install the software update now through Settings, General, then Software Update. The carrier-settings update is set to download on its own over the next 14 days; opening Settings, General, then About can trigger it. Apple says phones that have already lost service cannot have that service turned back on with software, and it tells those owners to contact Apple Support or AT&T, or to visit an Apple Store or an AT&T store. Apple says it has seen the failure only on the iPhone 18 Pro Max on AT&T. Source: [macrumors.com](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/02/apple-statement-on-iphone-18-pro-max-att-issue/)

MacStories described the plan as new full-disk access controls on Mac developers. Ars Technica set the move against Meta, writing that Meta says FDA is not sufficient for Muse reading messages and that Apple disagrees. **Free and Plus Gemini users lose models in October: XDA**
XDA reported that Google is cutting Gemini model access on the lower tiers in October. On October 9, free users lose Flash and keep only Flash-Lite. Google AI Plus loses Gemini Pro but keeps Flash and Flash-Lite, and Google said Plus subscribers will get an email naming when Pro access ends. AI Pro and Ultra keep Pro, and Google said every other subscription tier is unchanged. Plus subscribers still keep the storage benefits on that tier. Source: [xda-developers.com](https://www.xda-developers.com/google-ai-plus-users-are-losing-access-to-geminis-pro-model-and-its-happening-very-soon/)

### The Thread: A robot brain at ten billion, with Nvidia and Meta already in the room
FieldAI reportedly raised seven hundred million dollars at a ten-billion-dollar valuation, more than quadrupling a two-billion-dollar valuation in just over a year, Benzinga reported from a Friday Business Insider account. Which venture firm led the round is not clear. A person familiar with the deal told that account FieldAI has added at least thirty-five million dollars in revenue and customer contracts since June, taking the total above one hundred thirty-five million dollars. Founded in 2023, the California company is building what it calls a universal general-purpose brain for humanoid robots, robot dogs, drones and industrial rovers. Last year it secured four hundred five million dollars across two rounds at the two-billion-dollar valuation. Backers then included NVentures, the venture arm of NVIDIA, and Intel Capital, along with Bezos Expeditions, Gates Frontier, Samsung, Khosla Ventures, Temasek, Prysm Capital and Canaan Partners. FieldAI did not immediately respond to Benzinga. Chief executive Ali Agha told Business Insider in June, “We have seen very, very fast growth in the last several months.” The new mark sits near rivals Physical Intelligence, valued at roughly eleven billion dollars, and Skild AI, valued at more than fourteen billion dollars. Meta is already in the same market: it bought humanoid startup Assured Robot Intelligence for an undisclosed sum in May. The valuation is a bet that the brain works in unpredictable settings, not a single task in a controlled room. A Citrini Research report in September, after meetings with robotics labs, engineers and investors in the San Francisco Bay Area, found most humanoid robots still in development, training or tele-operated demonstrations, with real-world deployments closer to pilots than clear return-on-investment cases. That report said basic humanoid locomotion is effectively solved and complex dexterity is not. Source: [benzinga.com](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/tech/26/10/62150844/fieldai-700-million-10-billion-valuation-physical-ai)
