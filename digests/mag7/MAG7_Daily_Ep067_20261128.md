### Top News
**OpenAI countersues Apple, alleging trade-secret theft and supplier threats: Financial Times**
OpenAI filed Friday in a California court, accusing Apple of stealing trade secrets and blocking access to hardware suppliers in an effort to "compete unfairly by any means available." The Financial Times reports that OpenAI accuses Apple of "coercing and threatening" suppliers that work with both companies. Bloomberg reports the complaint also alleges unfair employment agreements meant to keep talent from leaving. The hardware work follows last year's acquisition of Jony Ive's io firm. Apple's July suit names OpenAI, io Products, and former Apple employees Chang Liu and Tang Tan, who previously led iPhone and Apple Watch product design. OpenAI says an employee who returned to Apple in October sent more than 100 confidential files on unreleased hardware, including device images, engineering data, and supplier information, to personal accounts; Apple said then that its investigation had not located OpenAI confidential information. Apple has sought a preliminary injunction and won expanded access to evidence. The original case is scheduled for trial on July 19, 2027. **Waystar integrates revenue cycle intelligence with Gemini for Healthcare: Digital Health News**
Waystar is extending a multi-year Google Cloud collaboration by putting AltitudeAI claims analytics and insurance-eligibility checks inside Gemini for Healthcare, the workspace unveiled on November 10, 2026. Providers can query claims and payer-payment data, and start real-time eligibility checks, in natural language inside a governed workspace, using open standards and Waystar's Model Context Protocol connector. The connector lets Gemini agents trigger standardized revenue-cycle actions, not only retrieve data, so front-office staff can run eligibility checks without switching applications. Waystar says early-adopter providers cut time spent analyzing complex claims and payer-payment datasets by up to 75 percent with its conversational analytics. Chief executive Matt Hawkins said advancing a more autonomous revenue cycle "requires trusted intelligence, deep connectivity, and the ability to take action." Source: [digitalhealthnews.com](https://www.digitalhealthnews.com/waystar-integrates-revenue-cycle-intelligence-with-google-cloud-s-gemini-for-healthcare)

### The Thread: The run rate beside the old cloud
Twenty-five billion dollars is the annualized run rate Andy Jassy says Amazon's AI business has already passed, three years in. Crypto Briefing, citing remarks on November 17, 2026, puts early Amazon Web Services at an annualized run rate of about fifty-seven million dollars after its own first three years, the gap he calls 440 times. Those accounts say AWS posted 46 billion dollars of revenue in the third quarter of 2026, up 39.3 percent year over year, its fastest growth in nearly five years, which annualizes to a run rate of roughly 184 billion dollars. A contracted backlog of 578 billion dollars, he said, gives visibility on demand through 2029. A large amount of AI compute is already reserved for 2027, with portions extending into 2028 and 2029. On the third-quarter 2026 earnings call he projected that AWS could exceed 1 trillion dollars in annual revenue within five to ten years. In March 2026 he had projected at least 600 billion dollars within a decade; roughly seven months later both the target and the clock had moved. He extended the same 1-trillion-dollar ambition to physical stores. Cash capital expenditure for 2026 is approximately 220 billion dollars, most of it aimed at AWS-related work, and he expects a substantial increase in 2027. He has also indicated AWS is expected to grow by over 40 percent in both 2027 and 2028.

The comparison is Amazon measuring a new line against its own origin story. Companies training and running models need computing power, and many would rather rent it than build it; the reports call AWS one of the largest landlords in that market. The early cloud business started from nothing in a market that barely existed. The AI line launched on an already enormous customer base and a global data-center footprint, so a faster multiple is not a repeat of the same starting conditions. Jassy's phrase for the market is "incredibly early innings." He argues that spending now should eventually become stronger free cash flow and a better return on invested capital once new data centers are fully monetized. An annualized run rate, the reports note, projects current revenue across a year. It measures momentum. It is not a booked annual total. No company, those reports say, has had even one division at the trillion-dollar scale he is now describing twice.

What changes is the size of the destination he is willing to say out loud: a cloud line and a stores line each aimed at a scale no division has reached, paid for by a capital plan he says is mostly for the cloud and set to rise again. The risk left on the table is demand. The backlog and the reservations pay off only if customers keep the capacity they have already spoken for, and if the new buildings do not sit idle. The checks inside his own account are the two growth years he indicated and the capital step he expects after this year's bill.
