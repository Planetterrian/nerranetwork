> **House House is keeping August's Big Walk off Xbox over Microsoft's Israel ties.**

**What You Need to Know:** House House is keeping its August co-op, Big Walk, off Xbox, citing Microsoft's involvement with Israel. Tim Cook now says Apple Intelligence is approved in China, with a test version later this month and no AI Siri in the first batch. Google is also putting Gemini on unknown-sender texts in Android Messages, while scammers keep walking people toward WhatsApp.

### Top News
**House House keeps Big Walk off Xbox over Microsoft and Israel: The Verge**
House House is not shipping Big Walk, its co-op multiplayer walking adventure, on Xbox consoles. The Verge reports the game has proved popular since it launched in August, and Xbox players have been left out. The studio said in a blog post that it decided against an Xbox release in line with a Palestinian-led boycott of Microsoft's involvement with Israel. **Cook says Apple Intelligence is approved in China; Siri AI waits: The News International**
After years of regulatory delay, Tim Cook told The Paper, in a Thursday interview in Shanghai, that Apple Intelligence has been approved in China. A test version is expected later in October. He said the first batch covers writing tools, real-time translation and image cleanup, and he ruled out AI-powered Siri in that first introduction. Reuters carried the same account from The Paper. The same day in Beijing, Commerce Minister Wang Wentao met Cook; a Ministry of Commerce statement says Cook told Wang that Apple is optimistic about China's prospects and will further expand its operations there. Wang said he hopes Apple will act as a communication bridge on China-US trade and on AI that is safe, controllable and governed through cooperation. TechNode, separately, reports that Apple plans a December China launch using Alibaba models. ### The Thread: The text leaves Google's phone
Google is turning Gemini loose on scam texts, and the handoff the researchers describe runs straight out of Google Messages toward WhatsApp. The Neuron says Google is expanding Gemini-powered scam detection in Android's Messages app with an opt-in mode called Enhanced Protection, rolling out over the coming weeks. When a number that is not in your contacts texts you, cloud-based Gemini models can read the conversation and judge whether it is a scam. Notebookcheck, citing Google's October 14 post, says those chats go to cloud Gemini models meant to read the wider context, which is the point for slow-burn fraud that asks for money only after a long run of harmless messages. Google says the analysis happens in Private AI Compute, where data is encrypted and processed in a secure dedicated space and stays inaccessible to anyone else, including Google. The Neuron says it could not verify the privacy claim.

A second layer stays on the phone. Gemini Nano checks unfamiliar links against real-time web signals before a tap. Google limits that check to the Pixel 10 and newer Pixels, the Galaxy S26 series, the Galaxy Z Fold8, the Galaxy Z Flip8 and other select devices. Redesigned warnings for links from numbers outside your contacts reach further: Android 6 and later. They warn. They do not block. The final tap is still the user's. Coverage is only unknown senders.

The numbers around the problem are larger than one settings toggle. A University of Florida study of 175,430 user-reported scam texts found that link-based scams, such as fake toll and postal notices, reuse near-identical scripts and are easier to filter. Reply-based scams, including romance and wrong-number texts, adapt to each victim and grew much faster. A wrong-number text with no link gives a keyword filter nothing to catch. Text scam losses reported to the FTC in 2024 were $470 million at minimum. Total reported fraud losses in 2025 were $15.9 billion, a record, and texting was the top reported contact method. In a Google-released 2025 survey of users in the US, India and Brazil, Android users were 58 percent likelier to report zero scam texts in a week. That figure is self-reported. Google says Scam Detection in Phone by Google and Google Messages already flags more than 5 million suspected scam calls and texts every month. Notebookcheck notes that figure comes from Google and has not been checked independently. In the Florida team's test of general chatbots, GPT-5 missed 18.8 percent of scams.

The shared limit is the next app. The Florida study, as The Neuron reports it, found scammers often move victims to WhatsApp or Telegram. Enhanced Protection does not follow that move. Meta's messaging app is where a Google warning can end and a fresh chat can start, with no Gemini read of the thread the user was just talked into opening. That is the platform dependency: Google can screen an unknown sender inside Messages, and the scam's design is to leave Messages.

The rest of the package is dated, and it is still Google's stack. Chrome and Android WebView will hide login fields and payment screens during screen sharing, rolling out in October to devices on Android 15 and later. Android will block apps from switching on call forwarding in the background, and it will warn before a forwarding code takes effect if a caller talks someone into typing it. That starts in early December on Pixel phones and other select devices. Scam alerts in the notifications of popular chat apps have been a Pixel feature; Google plans to bring them to Motorola, OnePlus, Oppo, Xiaomi, Vivo, Honor and other phone makers later this year. Reporting a text as spam still sends the message and its contents to Google, and that step is optional, on the Pixel 6 and later and on the Galaxy S24 series and later. The setting that reads unknown-sender chats is opt-in. Leaving it off keeps the older on-device detection.
