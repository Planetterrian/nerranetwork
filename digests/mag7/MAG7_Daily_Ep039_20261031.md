# MAG 7 Daily
> **More than fifty apps now run inside Gemini and Search, and Google bars their ads.**

**What You Need to Know:** Google’s Connected Apps preview puts outside tools inside Gemini and Search and forbids those partners from serving third-party ads. Apple set a November 4 developer beta for the first Apple Intelligence features in mainland China, with Alibaba’s Qwen handling some requests. Counterpoint Research measured the iPhone 18 Pro launch as a China-led gain against declines in the United States and Japan.

### Top News
**Google Opens Gemini and Search to Apps, Bars Their Ads: FourWeekMBA**
Google said on October 28 it is launching a preview of the Connected Apps Platform so developers can bring capabilities into Gemini and Search, with more than 50 Connected Apps already live between the two. Developers integrate a cloud-hosted MCP server or Android MCP using the AppFunctions APIs, register through the Google Connected Console, and choose before review whether an app goes live in Search, the Gemini app, or both; a remote MCP server reaches Gemini and Search on web, Android, and iOS, while Android MCP reaches Gemini users on Android. The category chosen at registration cannot be changed later, developers must list at least three capabilities, and Google reviews each app, requiring test accounts with sample data where sign-in is needed. The Connected Apps Policy says partners must not serve any third-party advertising or promotional material, must offer direct utility free of advertising, and may still include their own brand elements; an app also must not use overly broad or deceptive capability descriptions to get the model to select it over another app, and Google can reject the app, suspend it, remove it, or suspend or terminate the developer account. Adobe, Experian, Localiza, and Peloton are live in Gemini, with those apps coming to Search in the coming weeks, plus a new Calm integration on both; Search’s AI Mode connected apps in the United States, in English, include Instacart, Canva, YouTube Music, Yahoo Fantasy Football, and Sleeper, and AI Mode may default to the last app used for a task. Sundar Pichai’s figure this week was one billion Gemini app users, and Google said it is opening access in phases, adding developers, markets, and Google products over the coming months, with live apps able to track traffic, latency, and error rates. **Apple Confirms iOS 27.3 Beta Release Date With Apple Intelligence in China: The Mac Observer**
After Apple Intelligence stayed off iPhones sold in mainland China, Apple said testing of the first wave starts November 4, 2026, with the latest iOS developer beta, and the public release comes with iOS 27.3 in December 2026. The package includes Writing Tools, Live Translation in Messages, FaceTime, Phone, and supported AirPods, Visual Intelligence, Genmoji, Photos Clean Up, notification summaries, and related Mail and Notes tools, on the iPhone 15 Pro and Pro Max, iPhone 16 and later, iPhone Air, and iPhone Duo, with the device and Siri set to English or Simplified Chinese. Apple has partnered with Alibaba so Siri, Writing Tools, and Visual Intelligence can hand some requests to Qwen, including questions about photos and documents; access uses a Qwen account or Sign in with Apple. Alibaba said Apple asks for consent before a question, document, or image is sent to Qwen, that the integration is opt-in, and that user requests and generated replies will not be used to train Alibaba’s models. Siri AI, Image Playground, and the other next-generation Apple Intelligence features from WWDC 2026 stay out of this release, and iPad, Mac, and other devices wait on further approvals. Outside mainland China, Apple Intelligence and the ChatGPT option stay as they are. **iPhone 18 Pro Sales Surge in China, Offsetting Decline in U.S. and Japan: MacRumors**
Counterpoint Research said combined global sales of the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max rose 3.1 percent in their first three weeks versus the equivalent launch period last year. China sales increased 29 percent, which Counterpoint attributed to better initial supply and higher prices for competing Android flagships, while U.S. sales fell 4 percent and Japan fell 27 percent. In the United States, Counterpoint said buyers perceived insufficient changes to features and design despite additions such as the variable aperture camera. Apple raised starting prices by 100 dollars in the U.S., bringing the Pro to 1,199 dollars and the Pro Max to 1,299 dollars, and Counterpoint said exchange rates compounded price increases in Japan. The Pro Max share of sales slipped from 57 percent to 56 percent, with growth in India and Southeast Asia favoring the smaller model. The tracking period predates the iPhone Duo’s launch on October 23. Source: [macrumors.com](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/31/iphone-18-pro-sales-surge-in-china/)

**HomeView Software 27.2: Wallpapers, Control Center, and More: MacRumors**

The update includes 20 default wallpapers in light and dark variations, and users will also likely be able to set their own photo wallpaper. The software shows a Hello screen at startup, Control Center in a setup animation, automatic Light and Dark Mode, and Snoopy animations on the Snoopy face. A hidden HomeView Software 26 graphic adds to evidence that Apple had planned an earlier launch. HomeView launches alongside the fourth-generation Apple TV 4K on November 13. ### The Counterpoint
**Google is expanding its own AI Mode ads while partner apps cannot carry any: FourWeekMBA**
The same week Google barred Connected Apps from third-party advertising, Alphabet said on its October 29 earnings call that it is expanding ads in AI Mode into international markets, alongside multi-turn ads in AI Mode in the U.S. On that call the company also said strong growth in AI Mode queries had an impact because of the lower monetization it was seeing in AI Mode. The apps sit at the moment of intent; the ad inventory Google is adding is Google’s, and the queries driving the new surface are the ones Alphabet said monetize less. ### The Thread: The flash shelf flipped
Two years ago Apple bought nearly twice the flash storage of Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, and Google combined, according to TechInsights estimates in a Wall Street Journal report carried by MacRumors. Hyperscalers are now buying double Apple’s share. That is the shared component under iPhones and under the data centers those four companies are filling.

As the iPhone took off, Apple became the world’s largest buyer of flash storage and used the leverage. The Journal account says that after ordering flash at rock-bottom prices, Apple would refuse delivery at times if prices were falling, leaving suppliers holding unsold chips, including ones custom-made for Apple. Kioxia, previously Toshiba Memory, had executives who joked they felt like Charlie Brown trying to kick the football. Apple is also said to have pressed flash makers to open their books so it could monitor sales and check that no one else got a better deal. Around the end of 2015, Sandisk refused requests for additional supply because it already had customers willing to pay more. Tony Blevins, then head of procurement, threatened a Sandisk salesman and said he would have Sandisk’s chief executive, Sanjay Mehrotra, crawl on his knees back to Apple, according to a person familiar with the call. SK Hynix and Samsung were not in those examples.

The AI data-center build is what reversed the table. Flash storage and RAM now go into those centers in volume, and the buyers on the other side of TechInsights’ old comparison — Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, and Google — are the hyperscalers whose combined purchases have passed Apple. Apple has raised prices on more than a dozen products this year, including iPhones, iPads, Macs, HomePods, and the Apple TV, and attributed the increases to the memory chip shortage. Former CEO Tim Cook said the price increases were unavoidable. An Apple spokesperson told the Journal that Apple has worked with many suppliers for decades, values the partnership, and looks forward to continuing the work.

The margin on the upgrade customers actually buy is already thinner. TechInsights estimated that Apple makes a gross profit of 134 dollars when a customer spends 200 dollars to move an iPhone 18 Pro from 256 gigabytes to 512 gigabytes, down from 185 dollars on the equivalent upgrade for the iPhone 17 Pro last year. Higher shelf prices can mean lower demand, so Apple is not fully insulated from the shortage it is passing on. On an earnings call this week, SK Hynix forecast that the memory chip shortage will persist through 2030. MacRumors read that as the bargaining pendulum staying away from Apple for at least four more years. The dated marker in the supplier’s own forecast is 2030.
