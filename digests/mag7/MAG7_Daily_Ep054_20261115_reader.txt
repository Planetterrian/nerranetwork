After the board fight, Italy’s fine on Meta, Apple’s China approval, and a private raise that still names Nvidia and Meta, then the counterpoint and a thread on Microsoft’s mail outage.

Nvidia’s board is staring at a later Saturday count from TechCrunch, past one hundred fifteen thousand names, with the petition site listing five thousand nine hundred sixty-five current employees, one thousand one hundred eight former employees, and sixty-three thousand sixty-three shareholders.

The Information’s earlier Saturday tally is the one hundred thousand, and the five thousand two hundred forty-eight staff, and it names Elon Musk, Epic Games’ Tim Sweeney, and Y Combinator’s Garry Tan. TechCrunch adds Replit chief Amjad Masad.

The text says signers think Huang was removed without meaningful due process, asks the board to bring him back while an independent investigation sets the facts, and says he deserves due process, not mob justice. He resigned Thursday, four days after confessing to an affair with a chip executive U.S. intelligence suspects of being a Chinese spy, and he has agreed to testify before Congress.

CFO Colette Kress is interim chief while the search for a permanent replacement runs.

Italy’s data-protection authority fined Meta eight hundred five million euros, eight hundred eighty-seven million dollars, for opening photos and videos in Android users’ galleries without explicit consent. Bloomberg has the euro figure and the dollar conversion. Reuters carries the same penalty as eight hundred eighty-seven million dollars over Android users’ data access.

Apple Intelligence is approved for iPad and Mac in China, Softonic reports, ahead of a December rollout.

Google’s corporate development team has explored buying AI tools that create ads, The Information reports, and Google is paying startup Hightouch to move advertisers onto its customer data tools. The point is ads inside AI features, after advertisers complained they cannot see how those ads perform.

Firmus Grid is planning a three billion dollar private raise after its IPO collapsed, Bloomberg says, citing the Australian Financial Review. The plan mixes new equity and debt with prepayments locked in from customers, including backer Nvidia and Meta.

Google’s Gemini glasses now have a starting price of three hundred ninety-five dollars, the new commercial term after face-hiding limits already set for Gemini on the glasses. The sale date is later.

Apple has rewritten the clock for iOS 28, macOS 28, and the matching updates, Mark Gurman reported through MacRumors. Each yearly release still has three stages. Coding inside each stage now runs six weeks instead of four, and convergence shrinks from four weeks to two, under new chief executive John Ternus.

Nvidia’s security chief quietly left in August, TechCrunch reports, before Huang stepped down over relationships that include an executive at a company suspected of funneling chips to China. A former intelligence officer told the outlet this is the sort of leak that travels in ordinary conversation, which most corporate security teams are poorly equipped to catch.

The petition’s remedy is putting Huang back. A Financial Times account says the chip company has only itself to blame for a long list of adversaries, and the column Techmeme circulated says a post-Jensen Nvidia must reshuffle leadership, reset Washington ties, and accept that even the most valuable American company cannot dictate American foreign policy.

On that account a board vote to restore him does not clear the policy fight those adversaries are already using.

Microsoft and Amazon share a customer fear that showed up in a thirty-hour Microsoft 365 failure, which yesterday was a restored service and which Microsoft now traces to an earlier network capacity upgrade that surfaced after a later network change. GeekWire, from the incident log, puts it at about thirty hours and more than seventy countries.

TechXplore puts products offline in at least seventy-four countries and territories across the Americas, Europe, Asia, and Africa. Outlook, Teams, and Copilot were the services named. Microsoft called it technical, not cyber, and not related to AI or agents, and on Saturday said the remaining customers and regions were back.

Charles Lamanna, executive vice president for Copilot, Agents, and Platform, said cascading failures and thundering herds at the network perimeter made recovery harder than normal. A thundering herd, in his usage, is huge numbers of devices reconnecting at once and swamping systems as they return.

Customers were promised a preliminary post-incident report within two business days and a final report within five, with a step-by-step timeline and many repair items, and he said Microsoft would be very specific.

The load behind that perimeter is a January figure Microsoft reported, more than four hundred fifty million paid commercial Microsoft 365 seats, which GeekWire reads as roughly one seat for every eighteen people on Earth. Unity Health Toronto declared a Code Grey and said patient care continued. Pennsylvania lawmakers went about eighteen hours without email.

A Colorado jail could not take online bond payments. Business Upturn, citing Nikkei, said securities firms and asset managers in Japan fell back on fax machines when trade-confirmation mail stopped. New York’s Emergency Management office warned of delays reaching city agencies. In Singapore some government systems, including email, were disrupted.

In London the UK Supreme Court was not receiving email. It was at least the fourth significant outage of this kind this year. A January outage lasted nearly ten hours. In July a bug in automated network maintenance disrupted Azure and Microsoft 365 for hours. On August thirty-first an Exchange Online outage also hit Teams, OneDrive, and SharePoint.

GeekWire sets that record beside earlier Amazon Web Services incidents, a second cloud operator with the same class of customer dependence. Friday’s FAA ground stop was not part of this. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no connection, Microsoft said it had no indication its services were involved, and federal officials blamed aging FAA telecom gear.

The incident account gives Amazon no role in the upgrade. Customers still treat both perimeters as one risk, because a network change at either can drop mail, chat, and an assistant together, then worsen as devices rush back. Microsoft owes the specific repair list Lamanna already promised. Amazon inherits the comparison buyers will use the next time its own change window fails.
