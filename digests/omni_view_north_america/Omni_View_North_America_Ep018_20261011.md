### Across the Region
**France’s envoy says Carney admired for response to ‘extremely radical’ U.S.: The Lethbridge Herald**
French Ambassador Hélène Farnaud-Defromont told The Canadian Press on Friday that France admires Prime Minister Mark Carney’s response to American demands she called “extremely radical.” She called his handling of trade talks with President Donald Trump’s administration “exemplary,” and described Canadian leaders’ stance as calm and determined. Carney halted those talks in August and accused the Trump administration of bringing fresh demands at the last minute; he has also vowed to protect Canada’s language sovereignty and its power to regulate big tech firms. Farnaud-Defromont said her mission is to make France one of Canada’s closest allies in Europe in every sector, and she said a promise made or a treaty signed can no longer be assumed to hold. French President Emmanuel Macron has criticized a European Union deal, reached this summer, that accepts a 15 percent American tariff on many European goods but not on American ones; the EU handles most of France’s trade talks with Washington. **Mexican cartels are building luxury fleets by stealing U.S. rental cars: Los Angeles Times**
Operatives with the Jalisco cartel and other Mexican criminal organizations are renting trucks and cars in the United States and driving them across the border, the Los Angeles Times reports. About 2,000 vehicles stolen from California end up in Mexico each year, the California Highway Patrol says, and California is the top target. LAPD Sgt. Juvey Mejia said the groups want heavy-duty pickups and Jeeps that can cross rough ground and move drugs past Border Patrol. Recovery specialists also name Cadillac Escalades, BMW SUVs, Chevy Suburbans, Ford Raptors and Toyota pickups, some fitted with ballistic-proof steel, firing ports and gun mounts. Sharky Laguana, owner of Bandago Van Rentals in San Francisco, said California requires agencies to wait to switch on GPS until the return deadline has passed and the renter has been contacted, and by then most of the vehicles are in Mexico. Thefts have also been traced to New York, Chicago and Miami. Source: [latimes.com](https://www.latimes.com/world-nation/story/2026-10-11/mexican-cartels-are-building-luxury-fleets-by-stealing-u-s-rental-cars)

### The Region and the World
**Mark Carney’s Plans Are Bigger Than Trump: The New Republic**
Since President Donald Trump imposed tariffs of up to 50 percent on hundreds of Canadian goods, Prime Minister Mark Carney has spent more than a year calling European leaders and pitching Canada as open for business, The New Republic reports, citing The Wall Street Journal. Norman Hillmer of Carleton University told the magazine that Canadian-American relations have “never been like this” and are “right off the charts,” as the White House has threatened to use “economic force” to annex Canada. In her September State of the Union address, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen proposed a new “associate member” category for Canada. Officials on both sides then played the idea down: Carney spoke only of “an alliance for the future,” European Council President Antonio Costa said Europe wanted the closest possible relationship, and German MEP Siegbert Frank Droese called the proposal absurd and unrealistic. Source: [newrepublic.com](https://newrepublic.com/article/216347/mark-carney-plans-trump-europe-global-game)

### Both Sides: Hawaii’s judicial confirmation clock
Hawaiʻi voters on November 3 will decide whether to amend the state constitution so the Senate has 60 days, not 30, to act on judicial appointments made from April 1 through December 31. Supporters of the measure, Honolulu Civil Beat reports, argue it will give senators more scheduling flexibility and save the state money. Their reason is the calendar already in law: the Legislature meets from about the middle of January to late April or early May, and April is dominated by a two-week conference period on hundreds of bills, so an appointment in that stretch forces a special session. The 25-member Senate, the body that confirms judges, has held two special sessions in recent years and three in 2022. Civil Beat reports those sessions cost about $5,000 in airfare and per diem for neighbor-island senators, plus senator and staff time. The Senate says moving the window to 60 days cannot be done by ordinary statute; it requires this constitutional amendment, which is why the question is on the ballot. Rejecting it would leave the present rule in place: when an appointment comes between April 1 and December 31, the Senate must meet within 30 calendar days. Both the current text and the proposed change sit beside the same vacancy facts Civil Beat reports. Justices and judges must retire at 70. Since March the judiciary has advertised an associate justice seat on the Hawaiʻi Supreme Court, Circuit and District Court seats on Maui, a family court judgeship, and a District Court seat on Kauaʻi. The Judicial Selection Commission has extended application deadlines when too few people apply. The next step is the November 3 vote, one of two constitutional questions that day. The other asks voters to approve a type of bond financing for county infrastructure. Source: [civilbeat.org](https://www.civilbeat.org/2026/10/senate-wants-more-time-for-judicial-picks-should-you-vote-yes/)
