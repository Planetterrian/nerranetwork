### Lead
**Tennessee governor halts executions after second botched lethal injection attempt this year: The Guardian US**
Tennessee Governor Bill Lee has halted the state's remaining executions for the rest of the year after officials were unable to put Christa Gail Pike to death on Wednesday. Pike, sentenced to death over a 1995 murder of a schoolmate, was taken to hospital after surviving two lethal injections. Witnesses said she was still breathing and was heard snoring about 40 minutes after the doses meant to kill her. The Guardian reports it is the second botched execution in Tennessee this year. The pause covers the executions still scheduled for the rest of 2026. **Trump officials abandon effort to investigate election threats, experts warn: The Guardian US**
Former officials and a recent report say the Trump administration has abandoned key bodies meant to investigate direct threats to U.S. elections by foreign actors and domestic terrorists, while officials have focused on unsubstantiated claims of widespread voter fraud by noncitizens. Nikki Fisher, former chief election security officer for the Oregon secretary of state, said the Justice Department's election-threats task force prosecuted 21 cases from 2021 to 2024, including Solomon Peña and Quintez Brown, and now appears defunct in practice, with no new cases prosecuted. Her June paper, written at the Naval Postgraduate School, said the task force received more than 2,000 reports and opened more than 100 investigations in those years. Asked about the task force, the department pointed to nine releases in the past five weeks on indictments of foreign nationals for election fraud and stolen identities. The day after Pam Bondi's confirmation as attorney general, she eliminated the FBI's foreign-influence task force. ### Both Sides: Who pays when a police chase that broke the rules ends in a death
The question is whether Chicago must pay all of a $10.5 million jury award when officers broke pursuit rules and the driver they chased was also found at fault. The Illinois Supreme Court on Wednesday refused to hear the city's appeal. That refusal becomes final in 21 days and leaves standing a First District Appellate Court ruling that the city pays the entire verdict. Tuong Lam, 61, died on April 13, 2018, near Devon and Kedzie avenues in Lincolnwood, struck by the driver police were chasing. A Cook County jury set the $10.5 million figure in 2023 for his wife and three children.

Lawyers for the city argue the chased driver should pay a share. Their strongest reason is that he was found partially responsible for the crash, so taxpayers should not cover fault a jury assigned to him.

Lawyers for Lam's family argue the city should pay the whole sum. Their strongest reason is that officers violated the Chicago Police Department's 2018 pursuit policy: they chased a driver wanted only on suspicion of a traffic violation, in an unmarked vehicle, without telling dispatchers, and without weighing whether an immediate arrest outweighed the danger of the chase. The appellate court agreed the city owes the full amount.

Both sides accept the jury's $10.5 million figure and the finding that the driver was partly at fault. In February a city lawyer told the City Council Finance Committee that the Supreme Court outcome would profoundly affect other pursuit lawsuits. A spokesperson for the Chicago Department of Law declined to comment. WTTW reports the rejection is likely to leave Chicago paying the full cost of similar suits when a pursuit broke policy, whether or not the person chased was at fault. Source: [news.wttw.com](https://news.wttw.com/2026/10/01/chicago-must-pay-105m-family-man-killed-driver-being-chased-police-supreme-court-ruling)
