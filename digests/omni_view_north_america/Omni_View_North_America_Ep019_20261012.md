**What You Need to Know:** The Federal Communications Commission is asking the U.S. Supreme Court, in an eleven-page filing, to keep discounted rates for certain political party ads and joint fundraising committees. ### Lead
**FCC urges Supreme Court to reverse lower court’s ruling on political ad rates: TheDesk.net**
Solicitor General D. John Sauer, in a filing submitted over the weekend, is urging the Supreme Court to block a lower-court order that would require the FCC to unwind guidance on what local radio and television stations may charge for political advertising. The dispute concerns a March 30 Public Notice extending the lowest unit charge to qualifying political party advertisements and certain joint fundraising committees. Chief Justice John Roberts on Thursday temporarily stayed a Fourth Circuit order that had required the FCC to decide the dispute by noon Eastern Time on Friday, after the federal government sought emergency relief. Former Ohio Senator Sherrod Brown, Georgia Senator Jon Ossoff, former North Carolina Governor Roy Cooper and Michigan Representative Kristen McDonald Rivet argue the FCC unlawfully expanded a benefit federal law reserves for legally qualified candidates — the lowest available rate in the 45 days before a primary and the 60 days before a general election. The FCC says the March guidance clarifies existing requirements rather than creating new rules. Opponents of the change say it could benefit Republican candidates in battleground states ahead of the midterms and would cut revenue stations need to operate. In August the Fourth Circuit struck down the guidance, finding that political parties and joint fundraising committees with non-candidate members were not entitled to the discounts. Source: [thedesk.net](https://thedesk.net/2026/10/fcc-supreme-court-brief-lower-political-ad-rates-rule/)

**Colorado voters may steer roughly $200M a year to land management, but there’s no guarantee it gets there: The Colorado Sun**
Proposition 137 would, starting next year, send all state sales tax collected on sporting goods to land conservation, access and wildfire mitigation, an amount The Colorado Sun says is estimated at about $175 million at the start. The diversion would shrink or turn off in any year it would affect the Family Affordability Tax Credit or the Earned Income Tax Credit. Those two credits totaled nearly $1 billion in 2024, with the family credit accounting for $866 million; the family credit offers households earning as much as $96,000 up to $3,200 per child under 6 and up to $2,400 per child ages 6 to 16. Tarn Udall, an attorney with Western Resource Advocates, a main backer, says the measure was built to fund protection from wildfire and drought while recognizing budget pressure, and that any money for land management is a gain. The Colorado Sun reports Democrats in the legislature have signaled they will ignore the measure’s directives in tough fiscal years, and that supporters have all but said they accept that. Source: [coloradosun.com](https://coloradosun.com/2026/10/12/proposition-137-colorado-state-budget-impacts/)
