### Across the Region
**B.C. universities and colleges in limbo as they await release of independent review: The Globe and Mail**
The Globe and Mail reports that colleges and universities across British Columbia have been cutting programs since the federal government began curtailing international enrolment in early 2024, in the province that relied on foreign-student fees more than any jurisdiction except Ontario. Carissa Wilson, 46, learned that the cabinetmaking certificate she was to start this fall at North Island College had been suspended; the college board voted last February to pause that Port Alberni program and 14 others. Joy Johnson, president and vice-chancellor of Simon Fraser University, said the university lost nearly $80-million in revenue and that a number of the province’s institutions are very close to the edge of sustainability. Most schools are still cutting, or weighing cuts, while hoping for political attention before the October 24 provincial vote. Source: [theglobeandmail.com](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-bc-universities-colleges-independent-review/)

**Suicide memorial in Chicago signals compassionate shift by Catholic church: The Guardian**
The Guardian reports that the Chicago archdiocese earlier this year dedicated At Peace, a memorial at Queen of Heaven Catholic cemetery in Hillside, Illinois, which the archdiocese says is the first of its kind by any United States archdiocese, in honour of the Rev Charles Rubey and of lives lost to suicide. Rubey founded Loving Outreach to Survivors of Suicide in 1979, a counselling program run by Catholic Charities of the archdiocese. He said the church has made a complete turnaround and that families who lost someone to suicide are no longer condemned or thought to be in hell; Catholic teaching still considers killing oneself a sin, while acknowledging that mental health problems can lead to suicide and that salvation is possible. Until a 1917 church law was revised in 1983, people who died by suicide were denied Catholic burials on consecrated grounds. Chicago-born Pope Leo XIV has dedicated monthly prayer intentions to people with mental health problems and suicidal thoughts and has called on the church to listen without judging; the Rev James Martin, editor at large of the Jesuit magazine America, says the church’s approach now mirrors the broader society’s sympathy for everyone affected. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/10/suicide-memorial-chicago-catholic-church)

### Both Sides: Alberta’s classroom neutrality rules
What is being decided is how Alberta teachers may handle contested issues in class, and the decision has already taken effect. This fall, Bill 25 — An Act to Remove Politics and Ideology from Classrooms and Amend the Education Act — came into force. It requires teachers to present all issues in an impartial and neutral manner. The act never defines those two words.

The strongest case for the rules is the one the statute states in its title and its demand. The act is written to take politics and ideology out of classrooms and to require teachers to present issues without taking a side. Conner Huey, a Fulbright scholar who researched education policy, writing in The Globe and Mail, grants the aim behind that demand: there is nothing wrong, he writes, with wanting open and tolerant classrooms where students encounter a range of views.

The strongest case against is Huey’s. He argues that when a government holds the power to decide what counts as ideological teaching, the result is censorship of a different flavour rather than pure neutrality. In his account, posters may become contraband, and which ideas count as political is settled by whoever holds power in Edmonton. He points to a year in which the government ordered books off school library shelves, including a graphic-novel adaptation of George Orwell’s 1984. He writes that outside organizations delivering child-abuse-prevention presentations have already been forced to suspend that programming in Alberta schools while they await ministerial approval, so a policy sold as shielding children has left them with less protection, with students from marginalized communities most deeply affected. Scholars who study these policies, he writes, have documented that classroom neutrality mandates especially strip those students of the affirmation and safety schools are supposed to provide, and weaken their ability to learn.

The fact both the statute and Huey’s account accept is that the impartiality rule is now in force and that “impartial” and “neutral” are not defined in the act. On Huey’s account, the outside prevention programs stay suspended until the minister approves them. Source: [theglobeandmail.com](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/opinion/article-albertas-new-classroom-rules-wont-benefit-students/)

**Post-its, homework breaks and karaoke: inside the Women’s Pro Baseball League’s debut season: The Guardian**
The Guardian reports that the Women’s Pro Baseball League has completed an eight-week inaugural season, the first league of its kind in 70 years, played entirely in Springfield, Illinois, at Robin Roberts Stadium. Players came from across the United States and 11 other countries to four teams: the New York Heights, the Boston Hunters, the San Francisco Firebells and the Los Angeles Queens. Addisyn Baird, 18, the youngest player in the league, was called up to the Los Angeles Queens in late August, weeks before her senior fall semester at the Hill School in Pennsylvania.
