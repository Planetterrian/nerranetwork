### Lead
**Pennsylvania health officials brace themselves as measles outbreak expected to spread: The Guardian**
Last week Pennsylvania reported its fifth measles-associated death amid 943 confirmed cases across 39 counties, The Guardian reports. Some public health officials say it is only a matter of time before the disease moves beyond the outbreak's epicenter. Only seven of the state's 67 counties have their own health department. Lancaster County, which has none, has the most cases, 381, believed to be mostly among Amish or similar religious groups with historically low uptake of the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine, and local doctors and community members are trying to create a county health department. Jeanne Franklin, director of the Chester County health department, said her county has the third-highest total, 83 cases, shares a long border with Lancaster, and is preparing for the virus to reach people outside Plain communities even as the county tries to stop that. She said many Amish residents were unvaccinated because they were unaware of the shot or seek care less often, not because they reject vaccines, and that they will decide from what is happening to neighbors. Chester County is running surveillance, contact tracing and community vaccination sites, and working to counter misinformation about vaccine safety. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/04/pennsylvania-health-officials-measles-outbreak)

### Across the Region
**Christine Fréchette faces first provincial election test as CAQ leader: Yahoo Finance Canada**
Christine Fréchette is seeking her first electoral mandate as Quebec premier, less than six months after succeeding François Legault at the head of the Coalition Avenir Québec, in power since 2018. Across a 39-day campaign she has argued for a change in leadership without a change in government, and at the launch she named US President Donald Trump her real opponent in the trade war, saying she was asking for a mandate to move forward through the storm. She has campaigned on affordability, health care and support for families and seniors, including more money for home care and school-related expenses. She has appealed to sovereigntists who do not want a referendum amid economic uncertainty and to Liberal voters worried about a Parti Québécois government, calling herself an autonomist and declining to say how she would vote on sovereignty. After the third leaders' debate she called for an urgent meeting with Prime Minister Mark Carney and warned that Americans might secretly be trying to support the Parti Québécois to destabilize Canada; hours later she said there was no evidence of foreign interference. **Beth Fukumoto: When Politicians Run Unopposed, Everyone Loses: Honolulu Civil Beat**
Of Hawaii's 98 general-election races this year, from Congress to county council, more than a third will feature a candidate with no opposition, Honolulu Civil Beat reports. Senate President Ron Kouchi and House Speaker Nadine Nakamura have already secured their seats with no opponent, and in 24 races only one candidate ran from start to finish. In 27 percent of this year's legislative races only one candidate ever ran, so voters never had a choice, and nearly half of those legislative races were settled without a general-election contest. When only one candidate advances from the primary, Hawaii declares that person the winner and the race never appears on the November ballot, and the state is one of six that do not allow write-in votes, according to a 2023 report from the US Election Assistance Commission. Ballotpedia found that 28,032 of 40,800 elections it tracked through August, about 69 percent, were uncontested. Source: [civilbeat.org](https://www.civilbeat.org/2026/10/how-hawaii-can-combat-uncontested-elections/)

### Both Sides: Whether state courts can hear climate suits against oil companies
The US Supreme Court hears oral arguments Monday in Suncor Energy versus Boulder County, on whether federal law stops the city and county of Boulder, Colorado, from suing Suncor Energy and Exxon Mobil in state court. Boulder sued under state law to hold the companies responsible for costs tied to climate disasters, arguing they deceived the public about the danger of their products. The Colorado Supreme Court ruled that federal law does not stand in the way of the claims moving forward in state court. Jonathan Adler, a professor at William and Mary Law School who filed a brief, said that if this path closes too, seeking court remedies will be largely off the table, because a 2011 Supreme Court decision already left little future for similar cases in federal court. Suncor and Exxon told the justices they have a chance to decide whether the litigation can proceed before the energy industry is threatened with potentially enormous judgments. Similar claims have been filed in Vermont, Minnesota and Hawaii, and courts have split on whether federal law preempts them. The companies' filing and Boulder's suit both treat the procedural answer as one that could shape claims from dozens of states, counties and cities seeking billions of dollars for fires, floods and other climate-disaster costs. Monday's argument is the next step the reporting names. Source: [notus.org](https://www.notus.org/climate-environment/supreme-court-fossil-fuel-lawsuit-change)
