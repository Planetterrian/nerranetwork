**Top Democrats slam ‘Trump’s failing economic agenda’ after weaker-than-expected jobs growth: The Guardian**

### Across the Region
**Ontario launches public consultation on new measures to limit protests blocking ‘key infrastructure’: CP24**
Ontario is inviting the public to weigh in on possible legislative and non-legislative changes aimed at what the province calls growing concerns about demonstrations blocking key infrastructure, CP24 reports. The consultation asks whether the province should prohibit unauthorized, willful obstruction of a roadway, including overpasses and bridges, when the block is intentional, cannot be easily avoided, and stops travel. Other questions cover a new provincial offence, a requirement to give identifying information for a provincial offence notice, new police authorities, and new arrest powers for failing to comply. The province said the sites could include religious institutions, culturally specific community centres, and schools. Solicitor General Michael Kerzner told Newstalk 1010 that the aim is barrier-free access without intimidation, that this is not about restricting protests, and that people should not block a Gardiner Expressway on-ramp or an ambulance bound for Mount Sinai Hospital or Hennick Humber Hospital. He said he does not know what courts would do with constitutional challenges, but that he and the premier have had calls from Ontarians and that asking the public is the right way to proceed. Source: [cp24.com](https://www.cp24.com/local/toronto/2026/10/02/ontario-launches-public-consultation-on-new-measures-to-limit-protests-blocking-key-infrastructure/)

**Ex-sports doctor Larry Nassar removed from Michigan’s sex offender registry: WCVB**
Larry Nassar, convicted of sexually abusing Olympic and college gymnasts, is among more than 20,000 offenders removed from Michigan’s sex offender registry after a September 9 Michigan Supreme Court ruling, WCVB reports. The court held that the state cannot retroactively apply current registration requirements to people who committed sex offenses before July 1, 2011, under 2021 amendments to the Sex Offender Registration Act. The Eaton County Prosecuting Attorney’s Office said the decision is deeply concerning because victims and the community expected the registry to provide protection and awareness. Nassar, 63, was sentenced in state court in 2018 to 40 to 175 years after admitting he sexually assaulted athletes at Michigan State University and at USA Gymnastics in Indianapolis, and he received a 60-year federal sentence for child pornography crimes. The ruling does not change those prison sentences. Source: [wcvb.com](https://www.wcvb.com/article/michigan-supreme-court-larry-nassar-sex-offender-registry/73994702)

### Both Sides: Maui wildfire attorney fee caps
The Hawaii Supreme Court decided on Wednesday whether a circuit judge may limit lawyers’ fees in a mass wildfire settlement. Associate Justice Todd Eddins, in an opinion joined by his colleagues, wrote that the appellants’ central attack — that the circuit court had no power to enter the fee order — fails, and that “Judge Cahill had authority to act.” The justices upheld fee limits set in a June ruling by Maui Circuit Judge Peter Cahill. The panel was unanimous: Acting Chief Justice Sabrina McKenna, Associate Justice Lisa Ginoza, and Circuit Court judges Kevin Morikone and Taryn Tomasa, the last two sitting as substitutes because of a vacancy and a recusal by Chief Justice Vladimir Devens. Three attorneys who represent more than 1,000 victims, together with one fire victim, argued the other way. They said Cahill’s order was unlawful and unconstitutional, and that even if he had the power, using it was an abuse of discretion. The court accepted their appeal on August 5, and they argued on September 15. Both sides are contesting fees inside a 4 billion dollar settlement covering 21,750 people with claims from the August 8, 2023 fire, which killed 102 people and destroyed most of Lahaina. The Honolulu Star-Advertiser reports that the rejected appeal had been holding up initial payments to victims. The ruling leaves the fee limits in place. Source: [staradvertiser.com](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2026/10/02/hawaii-news/hawaii-supreme-court-upholds-maui-wildfire-attorney-fee-caps/)
