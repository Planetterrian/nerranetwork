> **Two years later, Hochul moves Cornell's alleged gang-rape case to Letitia James.**

**What You Need to Know:** Governor Kathy Hochul has taken the Cornell investigation of an alleged 2024 gang rape away from the Tompkins County district attorney and given it to Attorney General Letitia James. The Pentagon is studying a larger fleet of small autonomous boats on the Rio Grande. In Ontario, twelve Jewish educators have asked the Human Rights Tribunal to hear discrimination claims against school boards and unions.

### Lead
**Hochul appoints Letitia James special prosecutor in Cornell case: BBC**
Students and faculty at Cornell University told the BBC that an alleged gang rape from 2024 has hung a "dark cloud" over the upstate New York campus since the case resurfaced in recent weeks. At a student assembly on Thursday, undergraduate Sophia Riley Sim said, "We deserve to know that our institution values our safety more than its reputation," and said she had been too upset to attend classes for the past week. The accuser, named in court papers as Jane Doe, filed a civil lawsuit against Cornell on 16 September, saying she was drugged and raped at a fraternity house in 2024 and that the university failed to protect her or adequately punish the men, dubbed the Cornell Seven. Cornell has agreed to an independent investigation and said it ran an extensive inquiry lasting several months and followed applicable federal civil rights law. Shortly after Thursday's assembly, Governor Kathy Hochul said she had lost faith in Tompkins County District Attorney Matthew Van Houten and appointed Attorney General Letitia James as special prosecutor, so James takes the case over from Van Houten. Source: [bbc.co.uk](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cm0e3ed1er1yo?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss)

### Across the Region
**Pentagon may expand canoe-sized drones on the Rio Grande: The Straits Times**
The Pentagon may widen use of autonomous surveillance boats on the Rio Grande and in the Gulf of Mexico after a pilot that began in January, a US official told The Straits Times. That official said the canoe-sized craft helped authorities detect and deter illegal border crossings and narcotics trafficking. Defense officials want the test group of 10 vessels increased by as many as 50, to ease strain on Coast Guard cutters and crewed aircraft along the border with Mexico as part of President Donald Trump's crackdown on illegal immigration. San Diego-based Seasats' Lightfish is 11 feet long, weighs 340 pounds, costs roughly $240,000, runs on solar power and can stay out for months; Saronic, Saildrone and Splash offer similar boats. M. Roosevelt Ditlevson, assistant secretary of defense for Homeland Defense and Americas Security Affairs, said maritime drones are "cost-effective solutions to interdict and put people in these areas that narco traffickers are now moving towards." Source: [straitstimes.com](https://www.straitstimes.com/world/us-looks-to-expand-use-of-canoe-sized-drones-to-deter-migrant-crossings)

**Jewish teachers file Ontario human rights applications: The Globe and Mail**
Eleven Jewish teachers in Ontario schools and one recently retired Jewish teacher have filed applications with the Ontario Human Rights Tribunal against four school boards and three teachers' unions, lawyer Kathryn Marshall said on Thursday. The applicants say the boards and unions failed to act on antisemitic incidents and, in some cases, penalized teachers who reported them. The accounts include an unpunished swastika on a classroom chart, a swastika on a desk in the Toronto District School Board, a Star of David and a cross repeatedly taken off an anti-hate bulletin board, children playing a game about Hitler at recess, and a teacher publicly reprimanded by a union vice-president after speaking for mainstream Jewish organizations. Marshall said the aim is accountability and institutional change. The boards and unions may now file written responses; the Elementary Teachers' Federation of Ontario said it does not comment on tribunal matters, and the York Region District School Board said it wants inclusive workplaces but cannot comment on matters before the tribunal. Source: [theglobeandmail.com](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-jewish-teachers-ontario-human-rights-alleged-discrimination/)

### The Region and the World
**US and Russian lawyers weaken a human-review line on AI weapons: The Guardian**
Kenneth Roth writes in The Guardian that lawyers sent by Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin watered down a draft that could underpin a binding treaty on autonomous weapons. In early September, 128 governments met in Geneva to approve a report that might found that treaty. Roth writes that those lawyers weakened a provision requiring humans to review military targets developed by artificial intelligence before a strike, and that the agreed text refers only to war, not repression. Fully autonomous weapons remain the focus; using AI to pick targets for conventional weapons does not. Roth writes that, in Gaza, the Israeli military is using AI to target Palestinians. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/oct/02/ai-weapons-systems-algorithms-war)

### Both Sides: Should states bill fossil fuel companies for climate damage?
New York's climate superfund law, signed in 2024, sought $75 billion from fossil fuel companies for extreme-weather recovery and projects such as upgraded stormwater drainage and cooling systems in public buildings. Last week another federal judge blocked it. Two rulings found the law preempted by the Clean Air Act; Judge Brenda Sannes issued the first. The Supreme Court hears Suncor v. Boulder County beginning next week. A decision may not come until next spring.

Seven lawmakers from Connecticut, Hawaiʻi, Rhode Island, New Jersey and New Hampshire told Grist the New York decisions have limited relevance to their states. Most expressed continued interest in their own superfund bills. Their case is that companies should help pay costs linked to their activities, on the model of the federal Superfund Act of 1980, which made chemical and petroleum companies financially responsible for cleaning heavily polluted sites.

The court findings state the case against: federal law, through the Clean Air Act, already occupies this field, so New York cannot impose this payment scheme. Pat Parenteau, an emeritus professor at Vermont Law School, said the worst outcome in Suncor would be a ruling that federal statutory and constitutional law preempts these cases and also consumer-rights, racketeering and antitrust claims, leaving states with billions of dollars in costs and no recourse.

Both sides accept that New York's law has lost twice and that the Supreme Court argument starts next week. They do not accept that those losses already settle every other state's bill. Source: [civilbeat.org](https://www.civilbeat.org/2026/10/will-courts-block-climate-suits-by-hawaii-other-states/)
