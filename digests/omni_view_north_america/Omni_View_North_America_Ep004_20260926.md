> **A Pentagon office would own a third of a Venezuelan oil firm Trump says will refill US reserves.**

**What You Need to Know:** A Pentagon office would own a third of a Venezuelan oil firm that President Donald Trump says will refill US reserves, a structure experts told The Guardian the law blocks and that will not restock the reserve soon. The paper ties the August announcement to the January seizure of Nicolás Maduro and to claims it says do not survive Venezuelan or US legal limits. Elsewhere, Ottawa is sealing a Havana diplomats’ lawsuit, and Florida’s District 46 is a three-way rematch.

### Lead
**The truth behind Donald Trump’s ‘biggest oil deal in world history’: The Guardian**
The Guardian reports that an August plan for the Pentagon’s office of strategic capital to hold a third of North American Blue Energy Partners — a deal President Donald Trump called “the biggest oil deal in world history” — is almost certainly doomed on legal and logistical grounds. US forces seized Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro in January, the paper reports, moved him to a federal holding facility in the United States, and left Delcy Rodríguez in his place. Experts and officials who spoke with The Guardian said virtually everything Trump and his cabinet have said about the deal is false or misleading: Venezuelan law does not allow oil deals longer than 25 years, and the office is not authorized in US law to own company shares. They said the structure will not bring new capital into Venezuela’s oil sector and, contrary to Trump, will not refill the Strategic Petroleum Reserve any time soon; The Guardian reports the reserve fell to a longtime low after the US invaded Iran.

### Across the Region
**Canadians were spying for U.S. in Cuba at time of alleged sonic attacks: The Globe and Mail**
The Globe and Mail reports that Ottawa denies Canadians were spying in Cuba, while two unnamed sources told the paper a Canadian diplomat in Havana gathered human intelligence and sometimes shared it with American spies, and that Canadians used satellite equipment the United States brought in, between 2016 and 2018, as diplomats began reporting nosebleeds, dizziness, headaches, blurred vision and memory loss. The Department of Justice confirmed Friday that the government invoked Section 38 of the Canada Evidence Act to keep new details of the diplomats’ multimillion-dollar lawsuit secret; the paper says the sources are unnamed because disclosure could bring charges under the Foreign Interference and Security of Information Act. A court order sealing information was issued in April 2025, after an amended claim in August 2024. Lead lawyer Paul Miller declined specifics under that order and said there was a fear that things said in court could be of national security sensitivity. **Threeway contest for Osceola-Orange state House seat a rematch of 2024: Orlando Sentinel**
On November 3, state House District 46 — northwest Osceola County, including Kissimmee, Poinciana and St. Cloud, plus a small section of Orange County — again lists Democrat Jose Alvarez, Republican Michael Cruz and nonpartisan Ivan Rivera. Alvarez won in 2024 with 57 percent, Cruz 39.6 percent and Rivera 3.4 percent; on August 18 he took 66 percent of the Democratic primary against Robert LeWayne Johnson. State records cited by the Orlando Sentinel put Democrats at 42 percent of registered voters and Republicans at about 21 percent. Alvarez, a former Kissimmee mayor and a Biden-era regional administrator at the US Department of Housing and Urban Development for the Southeast and Caribbean, says a second term would stay on affordable housing, healthcare and public safety, citing a veterans housing bill, vertiport funding and a recall process for community development boards. He opposes Amendment 3, which needs 60 percent of voters to create a $150,000 homestead exemption in 2027, rising to $250,000 in 2028, and to limit local spending to core services; he says legislative backers could not explain how governments would offset lost revenue, and he expects the November vote to shape the session that starts in March. Source: [orlandosentinel.com](https://www.orlandosentinel.com/2026/09/26/threeway-contest-for-osceola-orange-state-house-seat-a-rematch-of-2024/)

### The Region and the World
**Carney races to build a safer future with Europe after Trump shattered Canada’s old U.S. relationship: Milwaukee Independent**
Two days after reports that he had studied a risk of US force, Prime Minister Mark Carney is pressing a September plan for Canada to become the European Union’s first associate member, not as a step to full membership but to reduce dependence on any one country without ceding control of Canadian decisions. He told the European Parliament, “There is now a price to be paid for access to the United States market,” pointing to tariffs, US investment commitments and demands for domestic policy changes. Standing with French President Emmanuel Macron in Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon, he said Canada and Europe will build an alliance so “we can live our lives as we choose based on the values that we share.” President Donald Trump has suggested Canada’s closer relationship with the EU could amount to a “hostile act” and has threatened “very heavy tariffs” on Europe if he judged the move unfriendly. ### Both Sides: Ontario's property assessment freeze
Premier Doug Ford’s government still has to decide whether to restart Ontario property assessments or leave municipal tax bills tied to January 2016 values. In 2020, at the height of COVID-19, the province ordered the Municipal Property Assessment Corporation to pause. The Globe and Mail reports the system was never switched back on.

Paul Sullivan, a British Columbia property-tax consultant and national tax leader at Ryan ULC, wants the freeze lifted. His best reason is quiet overpayment. Owners whose property rose less than the local average, or fell, have paid more than they otherwise would, sometimes thousands of dollars a year, because the base never moved. He told The Globe and Mail the biggest problem is education and transparency: people do not know, and if they did there would be outrage. He has launched a campaign to end the pause.

Critics of lifting it warn, as The Globe and Mail reports, of explosive political cost. Owners whose property rose faster than their community’s average could face higher bills. The freeze did not stop municipalities from raising rates; it froze the values those rates use, normally reset every four years and phased in over four. MPAC was about to issue a 2020 reassessment when the pandemic hit.

Both sides share one roll: six years of bills on the fully phased-in 2016 value, now a decade old, after other pandemic rules ended. Sullivan’s campaign is the live push, and Ford’s government has left the 2020 order in place. MPAC, in that same account, says almost half of Ontario condo units are worth less than $500,000 as prices plunge, a change the frozen roll does not capture. Source: [theglobeandmail.com](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-ontarios-taxing-problem-with-property-owners/)
