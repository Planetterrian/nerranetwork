Enforcement begins October 1, while most states wait until January 1, and NPR reports the early start is sowing confusion and anxiety. Ontario has spent $140 million from a $5 billion tariff account, and the Senate is holding procedural votes on a data-center energy bill the House passed 417-3.

### Lead
**Montana starts enforcing Medicaid work requirements on Oct 1. Patients are confused: NPR**
Montana begins enforcing Medicaid work requirements on October 1. In most states, people with Medicaid coverage must prove they are working, volunteering, or studying starting January 1. Montana is starting that requirement three months sooner. NPR reports the early start is sowing confusion and anxiety among patients. Source: [npr.org](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/30/nx-s1-5946766/montana-early-medicaid-work-requirements-patients-confused)

### Across the Region
**Ford government ‘Protect Ontario’ tariff program spent $140 million so far: Global News**
Nearly two years into an escalating trade battle with the United States, Premier Doug Ford’s $5 billion Protect Ontario account has spent $140 million to shore up tariff-hit businesses, Global News reported. At a Tuesday committee at Queen’s Park, Finance Minister Peter Bethlenfalvy tried to treat questions about that spending as outside his scope, then left the figures to Deputy Minister of Finance Gadi Mayman. Mayman said 36 applications totaling just under $100 million had been received, 18 had been approved for about $40 million, and Algoma Steel support added $100 million. Liberal finance critic Stephanie Bowman said only $40 million had gone to help businesses — 0.01 per cent of provincial GDP, in her telling — and that the program is not working. Bethlenfalvy pointed to other supports he said had seen uptake, and the government has said it would move billions from the account into an investment fund run by a private-sector partner. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12077838/ford-government-protect-ontario-tariff-program-spent-140-million-so-far/)

### Both Sides: Should the Senate advance the data-center ratepayer bill before November?
The question on September 30 is whether the Senate should advance the House-passed Ratepayer Protection Act, on data-center energy costs, before November’s midterms. A second House bill, cracking down on congressional stock trading and carrying a voter-identification mandate, is on the same procedural slate.

Senator Jon Husted, an Ohio Republican and the ratepayer bill’s author, said the measure cleared the House 417-3 and is “the only bill that has a chance to get to the president’s desk before we get to the election.” He said it will be up to Democrats “whether or not they want to play politics or they want to solve affordability issues,” and he tied that choice to power costs in Ohio. Republican leaders hope the votes let vulnerable senators say they backed cost-of-living and corruption measures. Punchbowl News reported that even some Republican senators doubt that will matter this late.

Punchbowl News reported Senate Democrats are likely to block both bills. Senator John Hickenlooper, a Colorado Democrat, said that in the House “it’s so easy and people go along with these symbolic things there that have no teeth,” and that “the Senate doesn’t work that way.” The outlet reported Democrats do not appear worried about rejecting a bill nearly all House Democrats supported.

President Donald Trump, at a Tuesday White House news conference with technology executives including Anthropic’s Dario Amodei and Meta’s Mark Zuckerberg, said the companies would be “very generous with communities.” He declared that once data centers arrived, “taxes have gone down,” “incomes have gone up,” and “the value of their housing has gone up.” Punchbowl News reported that earlier this year he got technology giants to pledge to shield residents from higher energy costs — the idea behind the bill — and has since defended the facilities as voters have soured on them.

Both sides are dealing with a bill the House already passed 417-3. Three procedural votes were set for 11:30 a.m., one on each bill and one Cabinet nomination, after which senators could be free until mid-November. Source: [punchbowl.news](https://punchbowl.news/article/senate/senate-votes-midterm-elections/)
