The Commons is debating Bill C-twenty-eight, the Canadian Space Launch Act, sponsored by Transport Minister Steven MacKinnon. The bill would authorize, license, and regulate the safety of commercial rocket launches from Canada.

No dedicated federal law governs those launches now. Companies that want to put payloads into orbit have generally had to use foreign sites.

The case for passing the bill as written, as Liberal parliamentary secretary Mike Kelloway put it in the House on April twenty-eighth, is sovereignty in a growing industry.

He argued that Bill C-twenty-eight is about whether Canada becomes a sovereign, space-faring nation that launches from its own soil, builds its own capabilities, and competes in one of the fastest-growing sectors on earth, or whether it continues to rely on others.

The case for rewriting the text before licences go out, as Conservative Dan Albas told the House in that debate, is a security gap. The New Democratic Party has questioned how much of the framework is left to regulation rather than written into the act.
