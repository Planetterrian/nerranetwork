The case on both sides of a college-sports bill is now in the House, after the Senate passed the Protect College Sports Act last week.

The text, as the Guardian lays it out, would cap what a school can steer to athletes at about forty-nine million dollars.

It would also allow one free transfer. Move again, outside a few listed exceptions, and the player sits a year.

Deion Sanders cut an ad for the bill. He says it lets student-athletes shine on the field and off it, strengthens historically Black colleges, and keeps men’s and women’s sports strong.

He still says athletes should be paid for name, image, and likeness. In June he told reporter Baker Machado the market has become pay-per-view, not NIL.

The Guardian says the bill would not abolish NIL or put a dollar cap on it. A deal would have to match roughly what a similar figure would earn, and show a real commercial purpose. That test could kill payments that are really just salary.

What both readings share is this. The Senate has passed a bill that keeps some NIL, then adds a commercial-purpose test, a school payment ceiling, and a harder transfer rule. The House has not approved it.
