### Lead
**Trump Once Vowed to ‘Own’ Greenland. He Settled for Something Much Less.: The New York Times**
President Donald Trump signed a security agreement with Denmark and Greenland that expands the U.S. military presence but stops short of taking control of the territory, The New York Times reports. The Times says the agreement was reached on the sidelines of the U.N. General Assembly amid prolonged tensions, and that it falls short of earlier demands by Trump to own Greenland. The Associated Press also reported the signing of the U.S.-Denmark-Greenland security deal at the United Nations. For Denmark and Greenland, the territory is not transferred; for the United States, the change is a larger military footprint, not ownership. Source: [nytimes.com](https://www.nytimes.com/topic/subject/united-states-politics-and-government)

### Across the Region
**Battle over Missouri's congressional map reaches US Supreme Court for third time: Reuters**
Challengers to a redrawn congressional map in Missouri, which Reuters describes as aimed at favoring Republicans, returned to the U.S. Supreme Court on Tuesday and urged the court to halt the map’s use in November’s midterm elections. Reuters reports that a St. Louis-based federal court again ordered the map reinstated, despite two previous Supreme Court orders to the contrary. Reuters headlines the filing as the third time the battle has reached the Supreme Court. Source: [reuters.com](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/battle-over-missouris-congressional-map-reaches-us-supreme-court-third-time-2026-09-23/)

### Both Sides: A ban on artificial superintelligence
The decision before Congress is whether the United States should ban artificial superintelligence, pause development of advanced artificial-intelligence systems, and create a Department of Artificial Intelligence. Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar are introducing legislation that would do all three, the Associated Press reports. It is a proposal being introduced, not a law that has been passed.

Sanders’s case, as the AP reports it, starts with risk. He argues that the potential dangers demand immediate action and international cooperation. The AP says the proposal comes as prominent artificial-intelligence executives and researchers increasingly warn about potentially catastrophic risks from rapidly advancing systems, and that Congress has struggled to agree on safeguards. The reason he puts first is that those dangers justify a ban, a pause, a new department, and cooperation beyond one country.

The case against that approach, as the AP attributes it to President Donald Trump, is competitive position. He has pushed back on new regulations and emphasized maintaining an advantage over China. The reason in that reported position is the edge over China, and resistance to new rules, rather than a pause on advanced systems or a ban on superintelligence.

The fact neither account disputes is that Sanders and Casar are introducing a bill, and that it is not yet law. What happens next is that introduction. The AP frames the open choice as Sanders’s call for a ban and international cooperation against Trump’s reported resistance to new regulation in the name of an advantage over China.
