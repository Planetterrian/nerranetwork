### Lead
**As referendum looms, 70% of Albertans want province to remain in Canada: Global News**
An Ipsos poll conducted online for Global News from September 18 to 22 interviewed 1,500 Canadian adults, including 600 Alberta residents, ahead of the October 19 referendum. Seven in ten Albertans said they plan to vote for Alberta to remain a province of Canada, down two points from Ipsos polling between May 28 and June 1, a change Ipsos calls statistically insignificant. Twenty percent said they would vote for Alberta to commence a binding independence referendum, with support highest among men at 25 percent, Gen Z respondents born from 1997 to 2012 at 28 percent, and Albertans outside Calgary and Edmonton at 28 percent. Ipsos Public Affairs senior vice-president Gregory Jack said the stay-or-go numbers have not really changed through the campaign, and that turnout matters because the side that wants to leave is more motivated than the side that wants to stay. Ipsos found 76 percent of Albertans know the ballot has several questions, and 62 percent plan to vote on all 10. Premier Danielle Smith added the separatism question on May 21 to a referendum announced February 19 that began with nine questions on immigration and the Constitution. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12072083/alberta-referendum-ipsos-september-2026/)

### Across the Region
**Senate plots its final week before elections with potential data center electricity vote: Roll Call**
Senate Majority Leader John Thune, Republican of South Dakota, told reporters Thursday that the Senate returns next week to finish business before the November midterms, with a test vote on the Ratepayer Protection Act front and center. The bill would require state regulators to consider standards so large data centers pay for electric-grid upgrades tied to rising electricity prices. The House passed it last week, 417 to 3, and Democrats blocked Senator Jon Husted, Republican of Ohio, from clearing it for President Donald Trump’s signature. Husted, whom Inside Elections with Nathan L. Gonzales rates in a toss-up against Democratic former senator Sherrod Brown, said the president, House Democrats and Republicans, consumers, and the business community want the bill. Senate Minority Leader Charles E. Schumer, Democrat of New York, said Wednesday that Husted’s bill is voluntary and “a fraud,” and that the Democratic bill is mandatory and stronger. Source: [rollcall.com](https://rollcall.com/2026/09/25/senate-plots-its-final-week-before-elections-with-potential-data-center-electricity-vote/)

**Why so many Republicans are rejecting Trump’s AI vision for education: Vox**
Six months ago, first lady Melania Trump told a White House education summit to imagine an AI humanoid educator named Plato giving children classical studies at home. The administration’s showcase is Alpha School, whose student was a State of the Union guest: children there do reading and math on AI software for two hours a day, then workshops with adults the school calls guides rather than teachers. Alpha says students learn 2.6 times faster; Vox reports no outside researcher has verified that. After leaders at Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google DeepMind urged a slowdown, President Donald Trump called the panic a hoax and said whoever wins AI wins. Republican-led Florida, Iowa, and Utah have passed limits on AI and screens in schools, and Vox describes a fight inside the GOP over parental choice, innovation, and whether publicly funded schools must prove they work. ### Both Sides: Sending the narrowed Strong Borders Act to committee
The House of Commons is resuming second reading of Bill C-2, the Strong Borders Act, Public Safety Minister Gary Anandasangaree’s package on the Canada–US border and related security measures. The motion would send the bill to the Standing Committee on Public Safety and National Security. The government has removed provisions that would have allowed law enforcement warrantless access, narrowing the text from the version first called for second reading on September 17, 2025.

Anandasangaree told the House in Thursday’s Question Period that since taking office last year the government has made “an unprecedented amount of legislative changes” for Canadians’ safety, citing Bill C-14 on bail and sentencing, Bill C-16 on victims’ rights, and Bill C-9 on community safety. Justice Minister Sean Fraser said the government is advancing legislation to fight serious crime and toughen the penal system, and would act on the advice of experts and police. The Hub reports that, with a majority government, the second-reading vote is not in doubt, and that the live questions are the shape of the bill that reaches committee and how long debate runs before the government moves it along.

Conservatives, as The Hub describes the fight, are pressing ministers on violent offences and sentencing, treating the border bill as part of an unfinished argument over crime rather than a file closed by bills already passed or in progress. That is the case the opposition is putting against letting the package move on the government’s timetable.

Both sides are working from the same order paper: C-2 leads Friday’s government business, ahead of Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne’s four tax motions, Bill C-28, the Canadian Space Launch Act, and Bill C-34, the Safe Social Media Act, with eight private members’ bills also on the order of precedence. The next step named in the debate is the motion to send the narrowed bill to the public safety committee. Source: [thehub.ca](https://thehub.ca/2026/09/25/parliament-today-strong-borders-act-returns-to-second-reading/?hubcta)
