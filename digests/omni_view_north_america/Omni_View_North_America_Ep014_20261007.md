**Skepticism greets a planned $15 billion Iowa steel plant: The Guardian**
The Trump administration has announced plans for what it calls the largest steel plant in the United States, a $15 billion plant in Lee County, Iowa, to be built by India-based Mesabi Metallics, The Guardian reports. The Guardian says the project has drawn doubt over whether it will be built as promised, citing President Trump's record and the financial history of Mesabi's parent, Essar Global. It says the plant had been scheduled for Minnesota, and that the shift to Iowa has drawn attention. Doug May, a steelworker for 43 years at a mill in Granite City, Illinois, called the announcement political pandering and pointed to tariffs he says are hurting Iowa farmers. May said Trump did the same in 2018 with a $10 billion Foxconn project; The Guardian says that Wisconsin project failed to produce the jobs Trump and Foxconn had promised. Essar Global first proposed a $1.65 billion plant in the Mesabi iron range of northeast Minnesota when it bought Minnesota Steel in 2007, May credited steelworkers with more than 150 trade cases, and The Guardian notes that more than 40 domestic steel producers filed for bankruptcy in the late 1990s. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/oct/07/trump-steel-plant-iowa-skepticism)

### Both Sides: Idaho Medicaid work requirements
Idaho's legislature set January 1 as the start of federally mandated Medicaid work requirements, through House Bill 913. The decision is whether people who already qualify keep their care once that paperwork begins. Legislators have said eligible Idahoans will not lose coverage over the requirements. A commentary in the Idaho Capital Sun says they should be held to that, and argues the red tape will cut the other way.

The legislators' best reason, as that commentary records it, is that eligibility is the guardrail. People who qualify stay covered, including those whose serious conditions should bring a medical-frailty exemption. The commentary notes that Idahoans adopted expansion themselves in 2018, with more than 60 percent of voters backing a ballot initiative, and that more than 80,000 people relied on it. When later bills tried to repeal it or add work rules, it says, supporters packed the Capitol and outnumbered advocates of cuts by about 100 to 1.

The commentary's best reason against confidence in that assurance is what a lapse would look like in a clinic. Someone with cancer could find out, while booking a scan or during treatment or recovery, that coverage had stopped, then delay care or skip a prescription, with no other plan waiting. It says the cost would land on families and on providers treating patients who cannot pay.

Both accept the January 1 start under House Bill 913 and the 2018 voter adoption of expansion. The next step the commentary names is that January date. Source: [idahocapitalsun.com](https://idahocapitalsun.com/2026/10/07/legislators-said-eligible-idahoans-wont-lose-care-over-work-requirements-lets-hold-them-to-that/)
