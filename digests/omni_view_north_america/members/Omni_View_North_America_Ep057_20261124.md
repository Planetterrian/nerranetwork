**Officials certify election results, set recount for East Alabama’s Senate District 10: Alabama Daily News**
Alabama’s State Elections Canvassing Board certified the November 3 results on Monday and left Senate District 10 out, because a 72-vote margin is under half of 1 percent and triggers an automatic recount on Wednesday. Elections director Jeff Elrod said the official winner will not be included in what is submitted to the secretary of the Senate. Unofficial election-night totals had independent Jesse Battles at 20,341 and Republican Senator Andrew Jones of Centre at 20,289; Elrod’s totals, with provisional and write-in ballots, widened Battles’ lead. Battles has declared victory and was sworn in on November 10 by an Etowah County circuit judge after the Alabama Republican Party removed him from the primary ballot; Jones has not conceded and wrote that every vote needs to be accurately counted. Secretary of State Wes Allen said Cherokee, Etowah and DeKalb counties will recount with the electronic-voting vendor and sheriffs. Certified results in the other races, Alabama Daily News reported, solidify four seats Democrats flipped while Republicans keep a supermajority in both chambers and won every statewide contest; new constitutional officers are to be inaugurated on January 18, 2027, and the session begins February 2, 2027. ### The Closer Look
**Galloway’s lawyers say he deserves one of Canada’s largest defamation awards: The Globe and Mail**
Steven Galloway’s lawyers began closing arguments Monday in British Columbia Supreme Court, asking Justice Neena Sharma for what they call one of the largest defamation awards in Canadian history. The Globe and Mail reports they want millions from four defendants they allege ruined the bestselling author’s career by spreading sexual assault allegations.

Lead counsel Daniel Burnett asked for at least $2.5-million in general, aggravated and punitive damages from a former creative-writing student, known as A.B. under a publication ban, tied to allegations she made in 2015. Arguments continue Tuesday on special damages against A.B. alone, a claim the lawyers expect will add millions more for a decade of lost income and for future earnings they say were wiped out. Daniel Coles told the court one expert put lost income to date and earnings to age 70 at $7.1-million, and another at $11.7-million. Burnett said A.B. “orchestrated lies to support the lies,” was “tactical,” and sought “maximum damage,” causing “years of suffering.”

He asked that Mandi Gray pay at least $250,000, Glynnis Kirchmeier at least $80,000, and Dana Kagis $30,000, for posts the lawyers say repeated the claim that Galloway sexually assaulted A.B. A.B. did not testify. At the start of the trial a lawyer said she was withdrawing defences of truth and fair comment. She denies spreading lies about Galloway, with whom she had a two-year relationship. Her lawyers have told the court she tried to kill herself twice this year because of the stress of the case.

Galloway was never charged with a crime. The University of British Columbia suspended him when the allegations surfaced in November 2015. In 2016 a former Supreme Court justice hired by the university found that, on a balance of probabilities, it could not be established that he committed sexual assault or assault. She did find that he had sexually harassed A.B. and committed other misconduct, and he was dismissed that year. He sued almost two dozen defendants in 2018.

Burnett asked the judge to rule the allegations false, which he said would help restore Galloway’s reputation and would matter for Kirchmeier, who lives in the United States and, he explained, is not typically liable for a foreign ruling unless a defamatory claim is proven false. He said that in correspondence with other professors A.B. claimed CBC News had reviewed her evidence and intended to cover the story, which he said she has admitted was not the case, and that a producer for CBC’s the fifth estate was copied on her email to the university president. He argued she, Gray and Kirchmeier acted out of malice, described Gray’s posts as treating Galloway like “roadkill,” and said Kirchmeier should be penalized for blurting out in court “other bad acts” alleged by a different former student who settled with Galloway on the eve of trial.

Closing arguments continue this week from A.B.’s counsel, Gray’s lawyer, and Kirchmeier, who is attending law school and representing herself remotely. Kagis has not participated and has no representation. Some time in the new year, the court will hear a further matter concerning A.B. ### The Region and the World
**Montreal touts aerospace, AI in bid to host NATO defence bank: Global News**
Montreal is officially bidding to be the Canadian home of the Defence, Security and Resilience Bank, which a federal statement says would provide long-term, low-cost financing for defence projects by NATO members and allies. Global News reports that Canada was named in April as host country, and that nine countries, including Canada, signed an agreement this year to launch the institution. Mayor Soraya Martinez Ferrada and Premier Paul St-Pierre Plamondon launched the city’s pitch at city hall; Martinez Ferrada pointed to the city’s population and business ecosystem, and the premier said Montreal has the arguments to win. Melissa Denis, interim chief executive of Montreal International, cited finance, aerospace, defence, artificial intelligence, diplomacy and a bilingual workforce; Michael Nault of Bell Textron Canada said the aerospace industry that would use the bank is already in Montreal. The bank is expected to launch next year with about 50 employees and grow to as many as 700 within a decade; Toronto, Ottawa, Vancouver and Montreal are competing, applications are due December 3, and the federal government will decide. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12163120/montreal-defence-security-and-resilience-bank-bid/)
