### The Closer Look
**North Atlantic right whale population declines as U.S. weakens protections: The Globe and Mail**
The North Atlantic right whale population has slipped back into decline. The North Atlantic Right Whale Consortium, meeting in Halifax, released an estimate on Monday of about 382 animals at the end of 2025 — seven fewer than a year earlier, 20 percent below the most recent peak of 483 in 2011, and the end of four years of fragile growth. The species lost about a quarter of its numbers in less than a decade after that peak. Heather Pettis of the New England Aquarium, who chairs the consortium, said this population cannot afford another slide like that. Entanglements in fishing gear and vessel strikes remain the leading threats to animals that migrate between Canadian and U.S. waters to feed and give birth. Climate change has pushed the whales into new feeding grounds, and ocean noise adds to the pressure. Scientists say recovery depends on both countries, at a time when Pettis says their approaches are diverging. In the United States, she said, there are movements to deregulate and backslide on protective measures.

Congress has barred new rules for U.S. lobster and crab fisheries to prevent entanglement through 2028, and the House of Representatives has passed a bill, still awaiting Senate approval, to extend that pause to 2035. Pettis said that could stall talks on the next round of rules, and that a delay is the last thing needed. The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration is considering deregulatory action on its 2008 rule requiring large vessels to slow in seasonal right whale zones along the U.S. East Coast, including swapping speed limits for acoustic or other technology that detects whales and alerts ships in real time. NOAA Fisheries spokesperson John Chu said the 2008 rule remains in effect while the agency reviews public comments. Pettis said the new technology, while promising, is not ready to replace the vessel-speed rule — an "and," not an "or." A separate federal rule that came into effect on Sept. 14 narrows the definition of "harm" under the Endangered Species Act. Because right whale deaths are incidental, Pettis called that change cause for alarm, though the whales are still covered by the Marine Mammal Protection Act. Chu said the change targets unnecessary or duplicative permitting requirements for projects and industries, not protections, and that risk reduction must be a shared responsibility.

Canada, Pettis said, has been much more nimble, and Sean Brillant of the Canadian Wildlife Federation said that with the United States pulling back, Ottawa needs to hold or step up its efforts. Canada first brought in fishing closures and vessel speed limits after a wave of deaths in the Gulf of St. Lawrence in 2017. Brillant said that at best ended the nosedive, and that the species is still on a knife's edge. Two of the three whales found entangled with fishing gear this year were in the gulf, the most recent on Oct. 21, said Lindsey Jones, who manages the North Atlantic Right Whale Catalog at the New England Aquarium. In February, Fisheries and Oceans Canada rolled out its Whalesafe Fishing Gear Strategy, which aims to keep fishing areas open when right whales are present and allows harvesters to use on-demand, or ropeless, gear that keeps buoy lines on the sea floor until traps are hauled. Alexis Van Bemmel of the department said Canadian harvesters and industry have been the real leaders in testing it. After closures near North Lake, Prince Edward Island, and Cheticamp, Nova Scotia, the Canadian Wildlife Federation's CanFISH gear-lending program trained about 85 harvesters and deckhands, nearly as many as in all the years since 2022, and it was the first year lobster was landed commercially from a right whale closure in Canada. Lost gear is a separate problem: Division, number 5217 in the catalog, a four-year-old male found dead off North Carolina in January, was entangled in rope from a snow crab area in the southern Gulf of St. Lawrence that had not been fished in five years. Marina Petrovic of Fisheries and Oceans Canada said gear-marking rules let the department trace it. Since 2019, the department's ghost-gear program has removed more than 2,620 tonnes of lost fishing gear and more than 1,040 kilometres of rope, and a national ghost-gear action plan is expected next spring. The 2025-26 season also brought 24 calves, the fourth-highest on record. ### The Region and the World
**Russia won’t be able to compete in World Juniors, IIHF says: Global News**
The International Ice Hockey Federation has confirmed that Russia will not have a team at the world junior championship starting Dec. 26 in Edmonton and Red Deer, citing Canadian government restrictions, and says Canada will remain the host even though that conflicts with a Court of Arbitration for Sport mandate. The CAS ordered Russia's immediate reinstatement on Oct. 30, after Russia and Belarus were banned from international hockey tournaments in 2022 following Russia's invasion of Ukraine; the ruling would also have covered later events, including the women's world championship set for November 2027 in Quebec City. Prime Minister Mark Carney said on Nov. 3, at a Commonwealth meeting in Antigua, that Russians and Belarusians would not compete for their countries in Canada while that war continues, and Ottawa later said even players with valid visas would be barred from the world juniors. Finland's federation said on Nov. 2 that its teams would not play Russia while the war continued, and Sweden, Czechia and Latvia followed; Sweden's federation said its line was crystal clear that it will not play Russia in the present situation. Russia's hockey federation calls the IIHF decision unlawful and plans to appeal to the International Olympic Committee; ambassador Oleg Stepanov accused Ottawa of bringing politics into sports, and federation president Vladislav Tretiak threatened to seek compensation from Canada and to push for the Alberta tournament to be relocated or cancelled, while Norway's federation said it would be bumped in Russia's favour, a step the IIHF never confirmed publicly. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12152003/russia-cant-compete-world-juniors-iihf/) Source: [kitchener.citynews.ca](https://kitchener.citynews.ca/2026/11/15/iihf-confirms-russia-will-not-compete-at-world-juniors-in-canada/)
