### The Closer Look
**Quebec farmers say proposed high-speed rail threatens their land, livelihoods: Global News**
Farmers and local elected officials in Repentigny, Quebec, are calling for a proposed high-speed rail project to be scrapped. They fear the line could cut through agricultural land and threaten their livelihoods. Guillaume Alary, a third-generation crop farmer, told Global News on Thursday that he fears the proposed rail line could divide his property and fundamentally change the life his family has built there. He said he is concerned about his land, his family, and the land and families of his neighbours. Alary owns more than 260 hectares, where he grows wheat, corn and soybeans and produces hay. His family’s farming history on the property dates back to his grandfather. He said he is left wondering how much of that land he could lose, and he asked whether he will lose half his land or his house. He said he learned about the project at the beginning of the year but was never consulted, and that he is upset about a project that is not transparent.

Alto, the federal agency overseeing the project, is proposing a high-speed rail network between Quebec City and Toronto. Much of the proposed route is mapped along agricultural property. Simon Duval, regional president of the Union des producteurs agricoles in Lanaudière, said that what farmers have to do right now is stop the project. He said that if half a farm ends up on the other side of the line, the detour required to reach it is going to totally ruin the financial aspect of the operation. In a statement to Global News, an Alto spokesperson said the agency held consultations earlier this year and received 20,000 comments. The spokesperson said that as the corridor approaches more urbanized areas such as Repentigny, the priority is to identify a route that minimizes impacts on residential neighbourhoods and agricultural lands.

Bloc Québécois MP Patrick Bonin, who represents Repentigny, said he cannot support the project. He said there is no proper public consultation and environmental assessment. Repentigny city councillor Normand Urbain raised the question of moving equipment. He asked listeners to imagine a train passing in the middle of farmland, and said a farmer cannot bring tractors from one side to the other because the farmer cannot pass over the rails. For Alary, the uncertainty reaches a way of life passed down through generations. He said he would be losing a piece of his reason to live. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12126696/quebec-farmers-high-speed-rail-alto/)
