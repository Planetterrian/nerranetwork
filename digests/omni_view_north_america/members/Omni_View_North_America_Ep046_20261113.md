### The Closer Look
**Toronto orthodox priest criticized for accepting medal from Putin: Global News**
A Toronto priest accepted a Russian state medal from Vladimir Putin at the Kremlin on November 4, and the Ukrainian Canadian Congress called that choice disgraceful. Global News reported that Putin presented the Order of Friendship to Father Vladimir Malchenko, head priest at Toronto’s Holy Trinity Russian Orthodox Church, during a ceremony on Russia’s National Unity Day. Video the network obtained shows Malchenko walking onto a stage to greet Putin, who shakes his hand and pins the honour onto his frock. Putin commended him for helping Canadian parishioners maintain their connection with their historical homeland, and in the same remarks celebrated Russian troops he said were bravely fighting in Ukraine. Malchenko’s acceptance speech lauded the Fatherland. He said Russians live in all cities and countries but have one Fatherland — Russia, Holy Rus — and asked that God bless it with prosperity and well-being forever.

The Order of Friendship is awarded for outstanding contributions to strengthening friendship and fostering peace and friendly relations among countries. After Russia’s 2022 assault on Kyiv, Canada placed Putin on its Russia sanctions list, citing his grave breach of international peace and security. Kirill, patriarch of the Russian Orthodox Church, whom Global News described as a cheerleader of the attack and who has called it a holy war, was also sanctioned. Global News reported that the International Criminal Court wants Putin for war crimes for the abduction of Ukrainian children. Former prime minister Jean Chrétien received the same order before Putin sent troops into Ukraine in 2014.

Reached by phone after returning to Toronto, Malchenko said he had no regrets. He told Global News he did not see the medal as a political act but as recognition of pastoral duties in Canada, and that his community was grateful for recognition of work to comfort people’s lives there. He said the church was above politics, did not support any wars, and was greatly concerned about human suffering caused by armed conflicts. He said he did not raise the war in Ukraine with Putin because it was not time for a conversation, and that the church never talks about the war.

The Ukrainian Canadian Congress took the opposite view. Chief executive Danylo Korbabicz called the acceptance disgraceful and a textbook case of moral hypocrisy unbecoming of someone who purports to promote Christian teaching. Two days before the ceremony, Russia bombed a school in Ukraine’s Zaporizhzhia region that was used as a humanitarian hub, killing more than 50 people. The night before that attack, a hospital complex was struck, injuring at least 73, and the bombing of a hospice in Dnipro forced the evacuation of dozens of terminally ill and elderly patients. On Wednesday, Canada joined G7 allies in condemning attacks on residential buildings, civilian infrastructure, and cultural heritage. Global Affairs Canada did not respond to requests for comment.

The church’s website links to Russian online platforms that claim Ukraine is an imaginary country and that Ukrainians are imaginary people, language Global News said lines up with propaganda used to justify the military campaign. The church is a registered charity that receives government tax breaks. In 2012, the Conservative government awarded Malchenko the Queen’s Diamond Jubilee Medal. Putin, using his term special military operation, said fighters were defending Russia’s interests and statehood, and he pointed to past wars he said drove invaders from what he called Russia’s native lands. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12145277/toronto-orthodox-priest-criticized-for-accepting-medal-from-putin/)

### Progress Watch
**Akwesasne elder makes history as first graduate of McGill’s Indigenous languages PhD program: Global News**
Mary Onwá Tekahawáhkwen Macdonald, 73, of the Mohawk community of Akwesasne, is the first graduate of McGill University’s Indigenous language revitalization PhD program, which was created in 2023. Global News reported she finished in three years, two years ahead of the usual five-year timeline, after retiring at 70 from 45 years of teaching Kanyen’kéha in Akwesasne. Linguistics professor Jessica Coon said the program is meant to give students who already speak an Indigenous language tools for documentation, revitalization and reclamation in home communities where those languages are threatened. Macdonald has become a mentor to master’s students and hopes to continue as an elder in residence; among the students is Jasmine Jimerson, an Oneida PhD student from Wisconsin who said she wants to carry on a dictionary produced by her great-grandmother, Maria Hinton. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12149031/elder-first-graduate-mcgill-indigenous-languages-phd-program/)
