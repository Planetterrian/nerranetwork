### Lead
**The Reward for Black Women's Work in Georgia: Leaders Who Look Like Them: The New York Times**
The New York Times reports that Black women, all Democrats, will hold seven of the top eight elected leadership roles in Georgia. Mary-Pat Hector, 28, volunteered on Election Day to help get dozens of voters to the polls after months spent trying to energize other young people behind Democratic candidates. The Times calls her a Gen Z torchbearer for organizing traditions handed down over generations of Black women, and says she was prepared for a good night. She was hopeful that Keisha Lance Bottoms would win, which would make Georgia the first state to elect a Black woman as governor. The Times reports that history kept being made after that hope: up and down the ballot, Black women, all Democrats, claimed positions that had long been occupied by Republican men. Hector called a mentor, a veteran organizer in the state; both women were stunned, and a future they had thought might be attainable someday was suddenly materializing. "We both just cried on the phone," Hector said, "because we have been doing this work for so long." She added that they could at last "see the fruits of our labor." The Times describes Black women as, for decades, a trusted base and the most relied-upon foot soldiers for Democrats in Georgia: knocking on doors, registering friends and neighbors, driving strangers to the polls, and coaxing people who had all but written off the government to show up anyway. That incremental work, the Times writes, revived Democratic fortunes in the state after years of Republican control. Source: [nytimes.com](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/15/us/black-women-georgia-leaders.html)

**Two Planes Collide on a Taxiway at Miami International Airport: The New York Times**
The wingtip of a Copa Airlines Boeing 737 struck the nose of a UPS Boeing 767 on a taxiway at Miami International Airport around 9 p.m. Saturday, the Federal Aviation Administration said. The Copa plane was taxiing to the gate after arriving from Panama City; the UPS plane was taxiing for departure to Bogotá, Colombia, and air traffic controllers had told the UPS crew to give way to the Copa flight. Greg Chin, a spokesman for the Miami-Dade Aviation Department, said the planes made contact at the intersection of two taxiways. Two people were treated at the scene for minor injuries, and flights were routed to other taxiways without disrupting airport operations, Chin said. The FAA is investigating. The collision came a day after a nationwide ground stop disrupted air travel, and just over two months after an Amazon cargo jet overran a runway at the same airport, struck two vehicles and killed five people; a federal report found an air-traffic controller had failed to warn those pilots about windy conditions when giving them permission to land. ### The Region and the World
**Europe and NATO will determine their own security, not Russia - Canadian Prime Minister Carney: Censor**
Prime Minister Mark Carney told journalists that the security architecture of a free Europe will be determined by a free Europe and NATO, not by Russia or the revised Minsk agreements, Censor.NET reported, citing Ukrinform. He said decisions on the security of a free Europe should be made by European countries themselves, together with NATO, and that Canada will continue to support its allies and will not leave other Alliance members without support. About 2,500 Canadian troops are stationed in Latvia, where Canada leads a NATO multinational brigade. Commenting on Russia's demand that NATO troops be withdrawn from the Baltic states and Poland, Carney said Russia will not get a cordon sanitaire, adding, "Everyone knows how 'well' that idea worked in the 1940s." Censor.NET notes that "cordon sanitaire" is a geopolitical term for a system of states joined to create a barrier, coined by French Prime Minister Georges Clemenceau in 1919 in reference to the former Russian Empire. ### Both Sides: Closing East Potomac Park for a championship golf course
Judge Ana C. Reyes of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia is weighing whether the Trump administration may close East Potomac Park in Washington, including its municipal golf course, in order to start work on a championship course. On Saturday she temporarily blocked the administration from shuttering the park. NPR reports that the order is a two-week delay to any work at the historic public course, not far from the White House. President Trump had announced that the municipal golf course and park would close on Sunday, ahead of renovation work he had called for. The Times reports that her ruling means the park will remain open for now.

President Trump's stated course is the renovation he called for. The New York Times reports he announced the Sunday closure ahead of transforming the area into a championship golf course, and Judge Reyes found that the proposed closure appeared intended to start that process.

Against closure, a preservationist organization and local golfers sued in February. Their argument, as the Times reports it, is that the project threatened to destroy a historic course that remained a cheap and accessible option for recreational players.

In a 30-page order, Judge Reyes said the Trump administration would not be responsible for operating the course while the case proceeded, and she required the golf courses, paths and picnic areas to remain open and accessible to the public. She has overseen the litigation for months, including a series of earlier rulings limited to whether the administration could clear trees at the site. Saturday's order was the first blocking work at the park more generally. The Times reports she has repeatedly accused administration officials of concealing their efforts to move forward with the president's plans. The Sunday closure does not proceed, and the courses, paths and picnic areas stay open to the public while the case goes on. Source: [nytimes.com](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/14/us/politics/trump-east-potomac-golf-course.html) Source: [npr.org](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/14/nx-s1-6041152/judge-trump-golf-course-blocks)
