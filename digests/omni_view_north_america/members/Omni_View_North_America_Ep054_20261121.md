### Lead
**Draft report finds catastrophic training failures as ICE hiring surged: The New York Times**
Officials found widespread training gaps in a draft report as the Department of Homeland Security sought to rapidly hire and deploy 10,000 new officers for the Trump administration’s immigration crackdown, The New York Times reports. A draft copy of a Department of Homeland Security inspector general’s report, obtained by the Times, says the administration graduated thousands of new Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers through a training regimen one instructor called a “catastrophic failure.” The Times’ immigration reporter Hamed Aleaziz explains the draft in a separate video account of the same findings. The report is described as a draft, not a finished inspector general conclusion. The hiring target is a rapid field deployment of those 10,000 new officers. The instructor’s phrase, “catastrophic failure,” is attached in the draft account to the training regimen used to graduate the new officers. The gaps are presented as a cascade of problems found while that surge was underway. The two Times accounts — the written report and the video — describe the same draft and the same hiring surge. ### Across the Region
**Federal court halts pregnant workers’ protection law: The New York Times**
The Fifth Circuit Court of Appeals said a pregnant workers’ protection law was unconstitutional because it was passed with proxy voting during the Covid pandemic, The New York Times reports. The court halted the law. The Washington Post reports the same appeals-court holding: the pandemic-era law was passed unconstitutionally because of proxy voting. The Times says the ruling is expected to be appealed to the Supreme Court. The constitutional defect the court named is the method of passage, proxy voting in the pandemic, not a finding about the substance of workplace protections. The halt is a court result; the expected Supreme Court appeal is what the Times says comes next, not a filing the articles date. ### The Closer Look
**Contractors must tell the White House first: theunionstar.com**
President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Friday that tells every federal contractor to notify the White House before answering Congress. The order, Executive Order 14438, was signed on 20 November. It says that anything the White House considers protected by executive privilege is not to be shared without its say-so.

Under the order, contractors must “NOTIFY FIRST,” informing the White House and their contracting agencies about relevant requests from Congress, and must follow “WITHHOLDING INSTRUCTIONS” for communications deemed protected by executive privilege. The order establishes “NO UNCOORDINATED DISCLOSURES” as United States policy. The requirements apply to subcontractors working on federal contracts as well. The duty is notice before an answer, not a ban on Congress asking. The withholding rule is limited, in the order’s own terms as reported, to communications the White House considers protected by executive privilege.

The White House said the order was issued on Friday. An explainer of its terms notes that the Office of Management and Budget will issue guidance on how it works in practice. Noncompliance could trigger termination of the contract, debarment or suspension. Implementation remains subject to applicable law and available appropriations, which the account of the order describes as the order’s own acknowledgement that it cannot override statutes or the Constitution. The appropriations clause is the order’s limit on itself, as that account reads it.

The order sits on the fault line between Congress and the presidency. Congress’s power to investigate the executive branch depends on getting information from the people who do the government’s work, and a large share of that work is done by contractors. What the order does not spell out in the material published so far is who decides, and how quickly, whether a given document is protected. That question is where the account says the fights will happen.

NewsBytes reports the same Friday order: contractors must clear congressional disclosures with the White House. Traders Union reports that the White House expanded oversight limits for federal contractors. The three accounts describe one action — Friday’s order putting the White House and contracting agencies in front of a contractor’s answer to Congress — and the fullest terms are the ones theunionstar.com publishes: Executive Order 14438, signed 20 November, notice first, withholding instructions, no uncoordinated disclosures, subcontractors included, and penalties of termination, debarment or suspension, subject to law and appropriations.
