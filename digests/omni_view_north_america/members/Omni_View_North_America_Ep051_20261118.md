### The Closer Look
**Carney pitches proposed Pacific Link crude pipeline to business leaders at Asian summit: The Globe and Mail**
Prime Minister Mark Carney on Wednesday pitched a proposed new oil pipeline to the West Coast at the Asia Pacific Economic Cooperation CEO summit in Shenzhen. He said Canada is advancing Pacific Link, a pipeline to the Pacific that will carry an additional one million barrels a day to growing markets in Asia, diversifying trade and reinforcing Canada as a reliable energy partner for the region. The Globe and Mail reports crude oil exports to China hit $3.5-billion in the third quarter of 2026, up nearly 159 per cent from the same period a year earlier, according to the University of Alberta's China Institute. Volumes were nearly double the same 2025 period at about 327,000 barrels a day. Much of that flow, the paper reports, is due to extra capacity from the Trans Mountain expansion that began operating in 2024.

Ottawa said earlier this fall it would expedite federal approval for Pacific Link, a decision that is attracting legal challenges from numerous First Nations. At the start of October the federal government announced a national-interest designation for the line, from the Edmonton area to a port in the Vancouver region. That was the first such move under the Building Canada Act, which The Globe and Mail calls a centrepiece of Carney's work to shift Canada's economy away from the United States. The designation gives Ottawa's backing at the start and is meant to let the government grant many permits much more quickly than in the past. In Shenzhen, Carney pitched chief executives on clearing red tape from approvals for major resource and infrastructure projects. He said Canada, which for much of its history looked to the United States and Europe, is also looking to Asia.

He said that two years ago Canada had never shipped a major liquefied natural gas cargo to Asia, and that since LNG Canada's first shipment in June 2025 the country has shipped more than 11 million tonnes. He said Canada will quadruple that by the end of the decade and double it again in the next one. He noted that the port of Vancouver is already Canada's largest, and that APEC economies are already four of Canada's five largest trading partners. He described cooperation as less paperwork at the border, more reliable food on the shelves, and the power to fuel later innovation. The Globe and Mail reports that touting the pipeline at APEC suggests he is willing to spend political capital to see it built. Asian buyers are being offered more Canadian crude on top of oil already moving to China. First Nations bringing legal challenges face a faster federal permit track they are contesting. Communities and firms between the Edmonton area and a Vancouver-region port face a project Ottawa has marked as in the national interest before those permits are finished. Source: [theglobeandmail.com](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/politics/article-carney-pitches-proposed-pacific-link-crude-pipeline-to-business/)
