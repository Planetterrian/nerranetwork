### Lead
**Learjet Plummets to Ground in Florida, Killing 9: The New York Times**
A business jet hit a South Florida neighborhood on Sunday, exploded into a fireball, and killed everyone on board. The New York Times reported that photos and video posted online showed the jet diving nose first to the ground and exploding. In a radio call, a pilot said the plane was descending at twenty thousand feet per minute. A second Times account called the aircraft a midsize jet and said it plummeted on Sunday, exploding into a fireball and killing all on board. The Times headlines identify the jet as a Learjet. NPR reported that Transportation Secretary Duffy said all nine people aboard died. Duffy, in that account, said the business jet crashed into a Florida neighborhood. The aircraft had departed Austin, Texas, and was headed to the Bahamas. NPR placed the crash near a busy roadway. The death toll both newsrooms report is nine, and both describe the impact as a fireball or an explosion after a nose-first dive. **Democrats Show Strength in Final Days of Race for the Senate, New Polls Find: The New York Times**
New York Times/Siena polls found Democrats up in Michigan, Texas, Kansas, Maine, and Ohio. The same polls found Democrats with an edge in Alaska. They found Democrats effectively tied with Republicans in Iowa. A companion Times/Siena survey found Democrats leading or tied in seven battleground governor’s races. The Times said those governor findings underscore how a favorable political environment may be lifting Democrats, which is the paper’s reading of its own polls. Polling reporter Ruth Igielnik said Democrats have the lead in most of seven tossup Senate races, though some contests remain close. The Times said the country is one day away from the midterms. These are poll findings, not results, and no outcome is settled by them. Nate Cohn, the Times chief political analyst, said polls show some kind of wave seems likely for the Democrats, and he outlined four possible scenarios for whether that would flip the House and the Senate. The paper’s separate account of closing arguments said Republicans, worried about losing control of Congress, are sending dire warnings about the opposing party, while Democrats are trying to keep their focus on affordability, corruption, and President Trump.
