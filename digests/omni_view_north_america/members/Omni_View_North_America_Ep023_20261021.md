### The Closer Look
**Canada orders deportation of Toronto man who helped Iran evade sanctions: Global News**
Canada is ordering the deportation of Amin Yousefijam, a former Tehran police lieutenant living in Toronto, after the Immigration and Refugee Board found he played an important and critical role in a crime group that helped Iran evade sanctions. The 36-page decision, released to Global News on Tuesday, says the Iranian citizen, in Canada since 2016, was the group’s logistician and oversaw shipping for a transnational procurement operation. Global News reported that the electronics, motors and other materials he exported were dual-use, with civilian and military applications, and could be harnessed to manufacture weapons of mass destruction. Board member Christopher Marcinkiewicz wrote that Yousefijam belonged to an organization whose criminal activity directly contravened Canadian and U.S. sanctions on certain exports to Iran, and wrote, “I issue a deportation order against him.”

Yousefijam arrived from Tehran a decade ago under the name Amin Riki. He was arrested in Toronto in 2021, extradited to Michigan to face allegations tied to a sanctions-evasion gang led by his brother Arash, pleaded guilty, and was deported to Ontario, where he took a third name, Ameen Cohen. The board found he intentionally failed to declare that U.S. conviction on an Ontario legal name-change form, rejecting his claim that a government employee told him foreign convictions need not be reported, and called the omission the conduct of someone who will say whatever serves his immediate purpose. Hearings ran from September 2024 to February 2025. The order was issued in July 2026 and released to Global News this week. Marcinkiewicz wrote that Yousefijam joined voluntarily, knowing he meant to defeat sanctions, and that a more limited role than his brother’s was still critical because it focused on shipping sanctioned goods from the United States to Iran.

Yousefijam told Global News he was still in Canada, would not say whether he had appealed, and said he was considering his options while the minister had options of their own. “Right now I’m just, you know, living my life peacefully,” he said. The Canada Border Services Agency did not answer Global News by deadline. The report places the case amid Ottawa’s effort to expel members of Iran’s regime: senior Iranian officials were banned in 2022, and as of October 1 the border agency said it had found 40 top-level regime officials in Canadian cities, with two deported, eight gone voluntarily, two awaiting removal, and the rest before the board or already through hearings. Global News also reported last month that the son of Iran’s former vice president has lived in Edmonton as Sal Ghafouri, received almost 2 million dollars in federal contracts, and is under investigation over alleged work for Iran’s military. ### Both Sides: British Columbia's $10-a-day child care
British Columbia votes on Saturday with a signature child-care promise still short of what the B.C. NDP pledged. The question is whether the province should keep a pause on the $10-a-day program into at least early 2027, or put new money in now.

The case for waiting comes from Education and Child Care Minister Lisa Beare, who told The Globe and Mail the program as structured is not sustainable at $10 a day because there is not enough money in the system. The province paused it in February until at least early 2027. Spaces at $10 a day are about 10 percent of licensed spaces, the $10aDay Child Care Campaign says, and just over 17,000 program spaces exist against a July 2021 promise, under the Canada-wide agreement, of 30,000 new affordable spaces within five years and an average of $10 a day by March 2026.

The case for pushing ahead is that families outside the program still pay some of the country’s highest fees, and that scarcity is the failure. Sharon Gregson of the Coalition of Childcare Advocates of B.C. said new spaces, a 50 percent fee cut for publicly funded licensed providers, and higher educator wages are real, and that the government has still not delivered and has stalled. Martha Friendly of the Childcare Resource and Research Unit called the policy a mess and said the question is why there is not more affordable care, after critics attacked Premier David Eby for having a child in a $10-a-day space. A Canadian Centre for Policy Alternatives report she co-authored put the median infant fee at 52 dollars a day in Richmond and 40 dollars in Vancouver, against 22 dollars in Toronto and 15 dollars in Calgary. On Saturday the campaigning NDP said that if re-elected it would spend 80 million dollars on 7,200 before- and after-school spaces and 250 million dollars more, with Ottawa, on universal care. Conservative spokesperson Alex Shiff said a Conservative government is fully committed to opening more spaces, available when and where people need them.
