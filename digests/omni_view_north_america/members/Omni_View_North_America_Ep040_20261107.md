### The Closer Look
**One of Okanagan’s most colourful characters dies at 96: Global News Canada**
Eddy Haymour died on Wednesday at the age of 96. For decades, a distinctive castle overlooking Highway 97 in Peachland has served as a reminder of him. Global News reports that he leaves a story of ambition, controversy and a dream that never quite came to fruition. For many locals, his name is synonymous with both the castle and Rattlesnake Island. Okanagan resident Don Scramstad put it this way: “Rattlesnake island and a bit of trouble, a lot of trouble.”

Haymour, a Lebanese immigrant, purchased Rattlesnake Island in 1971 with plans to transform the five-acre property into a Middle Eastern-themed amusement park. That vision caught the attention of filmmaker Greg Crompton, who produced the 2019 documentary “Eddy’s Kingdom.” Crompton told Global News that, when ideas were being kicked around, this one seemed a compelling story to tell. “So I was quite motivated personally but also as a filmmaker, I want to tell interesting stories. This is a very interesting story,” Crompton said.

The dispute with the provincial government escalated in 1974, when Haymour and his relatives took hostages at the Canadian embassy in Beirut. Crompton said Haymour’s rationale was the most interesting part of him, and described a thought process in Haymour’s own framing: wronged by the government, then “you were going to get some grenades and chuck it into the legislature and and cause mayhem.” Crompton said that thought process was maybe the most compelling part. At the Peachland Museum, Haymour remains a prominent figure in the community’s history. Assistant curator Jeff Green said, “He was zealously committed to his dream,” and that it did not matter what obstacle was in his way; he pursued it to the end.

Haymour eventually won his legal battle with the province. His dream of developing the island never materialized. He did build the castle in Peachland, and beside it a statue of himself pointing across the lake toward the island he had big dreams for. Tracy Janes, a customer service agent at the museum, said, “He was a visionary. He was impassioned about what he was doing,” and that everything about the man was larger than life. For Peachland, the castle on Highway 97 and the statue aimed at Rattlesnake Island are what remain of a five-acre plan from 1971, a 1974 hostage-taking in Beirut, a legal win against the province, and a development that never rose on the island. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12140696/okanagans-colourful-character-dies-96-eddy-haymour/)
