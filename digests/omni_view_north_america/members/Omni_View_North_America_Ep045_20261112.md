### Lead
**Ottawa mandates a merger of EDC and BDC: The Globe and Mail**
Export Development Canada and the Business Development Bank of Canada, both Crown corporations that finance businesses, are under a mandate to combine, one of the steps Ottawa promised in the federal budget unveiled earlier this month. Together they hold $128-billion in assets, 5,100 employees and a footprint in more than two dozen countries. Alison Nankivell, EDC’s chief executive, said the aim is more Canadian companies of a certain size with a real presence in several markets, and that the combination is not a cost cut but a larger balance sheet that can move resources. Two people with knowledge of the plans told The Globe and Mail the new body will report to the Minister of Finance, part of a wider consolidation of federally backed financing agencies; the Globe is not naming them because they were not authorized to speak publicly. In a September white paper, EDC ranked Canada 35th for the complexity of goods it produces, behind Japan, South Korea, Germany, China, Sweden, Italy, the United States and France, and estimated Canada could add $100-billion to GDP by making more complex products. Nankivell pointed to critical minerals, the energy transition, artificial intelligence, agri-food and forestry, and said ruptures in trade with the United States mean firms can no longer assume supply chains will cross that border without friction; Tushar Handiekar, EDC’s vice-president of structured and project finance, said he expects other countries’ export agencies or insurers to take slices of the lending risk, and Nankivell said the assets overseen should keep growing with the number of firms financed abroad. Source: [theglobeandmail.com](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-edc-bdc-merger-corporate-champions-global-footprints/)

**Younger Canadians shop retro tech for the holidays: Global News**
A Best Buy Canada survey conducted by Angus Reid finds 40 percent of Canadians likely to buy a holiday gift out of nostalgia, rising to 53 percent of millennials and 49 percent of Gen Z. Kushal Samy, the chain’s director of brand marketing, said younger buyers are rediscovering older formats because they want technology that feels personal, not nostalgia alone. One-third of respondents said they are drawn to old tech because they value owning physical media; 23 percent bought music, movies or games in a physical format in the last year, including 32 percent of millennials, 30 percent of Gen Z and 12 percent of boomers. Gen Z showed the most interest in the Apple iPod, then the Blackberry; millennials favoured consoles such as the Nintendo 64 or Super Nintendo; boomers were most nostalgic for VCRs and VHS. A separate Angus Reid survey released last month found 37 percent of Canadians had cancelled at least one streaming service in the past six months, up from 32 percent four years ago. Best Buy said headphones, earbuds, game consoles and turntables or vinyl are popular categories this year. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12146554/retro-nostalgia-tech-best-buy/)

### The Closer Look
**Ontario minister open to changes on the landfill veto: The Globe and Mail**
Ontario Environment Minister Todd McCarthy says he is open to changes to his proposal to eliminate a municipal veto on new landfills. He had argued the change is necessary because the province is short of places to put its garbage. Unveiling the plan last month in an omnibus red-tape bill, he said Ontario has less than 10 years of landfill space left. He warned that a new 50-per-cent U.S. tariff imposed by President Donald Trump on trash that crosses the border would make the shortage worse. He said Ontario sends 40 per cent of its commercial waste to Michigan and New York State.

The bill would remove an Environmental Assessment Act section that makes proponents show support from the host municipality and from others within 3.5 kilometres of the site. The same Progressive Conservative government inserted that section in 2020, siding with rural municipalities that opposed taking big-city waste against their will. Ford and his party campaigned in 2018 on a local veto after a proposal for 17.4 million tonnes of waste in a quarry two kilometres from Ingersoll rallied a coalition of 150 municipalities.

McCarthy told The Globe and Mail the deletion would be a housekeeping amendment and that he is open to tweaks. He said that technically the veto is being removed, that this is still a proposal, and that a legislative committee will consider alternatives. Municipalities would keep input and permitting powers, which the province’s land tribunal can override. He expects enough willing communities for new landfills and for energy-from-waste incinerators, a faster process that environmentalists say brings pollution and greenhouse gas emissions.

Late last month he left the door open on forcing a municipality to accept a site; the next day he said no municipality would be forced to accept trash. Last year special legislation let proponents revive and expand a landfill in Dresden while skipping an environmental assessment, over local opposition.

On Nov. 2, 12 Oxford County mayors and mayors-elect warned the changes would leave municipalities without leverage or a meaningful voice and with little chance to negotiate safeguards. Marcus Ryan, outgoing warden and mayor of Zorra Township, said he will meet McCarthy to try to preserve the veto and blamed waste-industry lobbying. He said there is no indication the quarry owners intend to revive their proposal. Bryan Smith, chair of the Oxford Coalition for Social Justice, said the government appears to be talking out of both sides of its mouth. Source: [theglobeandmail.com](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-ontario-landfill-veto-changes-todd-mccarthy-environment-minister/)
