> **A majority ends nearly a century out of power for British Columbia’s Conservatives, and David Eby resigns.**

**What You Need to Know:** British Columbia’s Conservatives have a majority after nearly a century out of power, and NDP Leader David Eby has resigned after calling the vote early. A shooting near Florida A&M left adults and children wounded, and First Nations leaders meet Canada’s first ministers on Monday. ### Lead
**British Columbia Conservatives win a majority and David Eby resigns: The Globe and Mail**
After earlier reporting of a narrow race, The Globe and Mail now reports that the British Columbia Conservatives have won a majority government. The paper says the party has returned to power after nearly a century. The Globe and Mail reports that NDP Leader David Eby resigned Saturday night. It says his snap election call backfired. Global News reports that British Columbians voted on October 24. Global News reports that the provincial election had been set to take place in 2028. David Eby called the snap election rather than waiting for that date. A separate Global News report says he called that election in September. The majority means the Conservatives, not the NDP, will form the government. The New Democrats enter that change without Eby as leader. The result closes a campaign that began as an early vote and ended with the governing party’s leader out of office on the night the ballots were counted. ### Both Sides: Should Berkeley start a public bank with taxpayer dollars?
Berkeley is considering whether to start a public bank with taxpayer dollars, The New York Times reports. The bank would offer low-interest loans to community projects in the East Bay region of Northern California. It remains a proposal the city is weighing, not a statute already in force. No final vote is recorded in today’s reporting.

Proponents say a public bank would allow the city to keep its tax dollars local. Their strongest reason is use of money Berkeley already collects. Those dollars would stay in the East Bay. They would be lent at low interest to community projects, rather than leaving the local economy for outside institutions. Proponents present the bank as a tool for projects the private market may not finance on those terms. The people who would use it, on that account, are East Bay community projects seeking cheaper credit from a city-owned lender.

Opponents say it would be a high-risk experiment with tax dollars. Their strongest reason is exposure. Capitalizing a new public bank with taxpayer money would put public funds at risk if the institution or its loans failed. That loss would fall on the same residents whose taxes supplied the capital. The Times reports that objection as a warning about the experiment, not as a record of a bank that has already failed. The people who would bear it, on that account, are Berkeley taxpayers.

The choice sits with Berkeley’s city government. Proponents are asking it to build a local lender for East Bay projects. Opponents are asking it to keep taxpayer dollars out of that experiment. Today’s reporting does not record a date for a final vote. Source: [nytimes.com](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/25/us/berkeley-california-public-bank.html)
