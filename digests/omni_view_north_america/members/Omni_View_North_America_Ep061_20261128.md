### The Closer Look
**Calgarian with Tourette syndrome interrupted by councillor during budget public hearing: Global News**
A public hearing at Calgary city hall on the proposed four-year budget went into a lengthy recess Friday afternoon after Ward 10 Councillor Andre Chabot interrupted a presenter whose tics from Tourette syndrome included profane language and slurs. Matthew Riley had joined by phone to speak against a proposed 15 per cent property-tax increase and significant hikes to utility rates. Chabot raised a point of privilege and said he did not care what affliction Riley had, that inappropriate language was not acceptable in the chambers, that children were in the audience, and that if Riley had to say something inappropriate he should cover his mouth. Riley told council he had written the city clerk's office about his condition and involuntary tics, and that he took part by phone rather than in person to limit disruption. He accused Chabot of discriminating against him and of interfering with his right to participate, and said the interruption had primed worse tics. When the language continued, Chabot argued that council could disqualify a speaker who could not comply, affliction notwithstanding, and insisted Riley not be allowed to continue. Council recessed for roughly an hour while councillors consulted one another and city lawyers.

When the meeting resumed, Mayor Jeromy Farkas apologized to Riley for the delay and said council has an obligation to protect the psychological safety of city staff and of the public in the hearing. Reading from a statement, Farkas said involuntary expressions associated with a disability are different from deliberately disrespectful language. He said he had verified that Riley notified the clerks in advance and asked for accommodation, and that another member of council had confirmed the disability. He added that accommodation is required to the extent it does not cause undue hardship on others, and he suggested council hear Riley in a closed session. Riley declined that offer and finished in public after a warning to people in the hearing. He later told Global News the episode left him hurt and embarrassed, that the tics are not a reflection of how he feels about anybody, and that he is thinking twice about speaking to council again. During the interruption he told council he would pursue a lawsuit, and he told Global News he plans to seek advice on his next steps.

Ward 2 Councillor Jennifer Wyness criticized the handling and the closed-session offer, saying it would remove Riley's right to be heard by his peers and the community, and that talk of inclusive communities is tested in a moment like this. Chabot told reporters his concern was that the public had not been warned the presentation could include offensive language, and that a warning next time would let the city advise people if a similar condition came up again. He said he personally did not have an issue with the language and that his point was protecting the interests of the public. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12169576/calgarian-with-tourette-syndrome-interrupted-by-councillor-during-budget-public-hearing/)

### Both Sides: Should Alberta restrict abortion access
Alberta is reviewing access to abortion, Adriana LaGrange, Minister for Hospital and Surgical Health Services, told United Conservative Party members on Friday in Calgary. She said the province must keep the procedure available because the federal government protects it, and she called it a disgrace that Canada has no law protecting the unborn. She said she is Catholic and spent 26 years as president or educator with a Red Deer organization that opposes abortion, that much can still be done, and that she expects to be briefed on the review in the very near future.

Sarah Hoffman, a New Democratic Party MLA and former health minister, said the remarks are about energizing conservative activists, that Premier Danielle Smith had found another front in what Hoffman called pointless culture wars, and that the comments attack a woman's right to choose. Hoffman said Alberta's NDP has always stood, and will stand, with women. The Globe and Mail reports that Smith has resisted pressure to legislate changes to abortion access, that some UCP MLAs openly support restricting access, and that the government has not passed legislation reflecting a 2025 member vote to end public funding for late-term abortions except when there is serious risk to a mother's health. That topic is not on this year's agenda. About 2,000 members were in the room when LaGrange spoke; the party expects roughly 8,000 in Calgary this weekend. Smith's spokesman, Ryan Fournier, did not immediately respond to questions about her statements. LaGrange also said human life begins at conception and ends at natural death, and she tied that view to steps the UCP took on medical assistance in dying, including that no one should have access solely because of mental illness. The next step she named is a briefing she anticipates in the very near future.
