**US supreme court clears way for Trump’s border wall in Texas’s Big Bend: The Guardian**
The US Supreme Court allowed the Trump administration to resume a border wall in Texas’s Big Bend, including parts of Big Bend National Park, in an unsigned five-page ruling that suspended a lower-court order from last month. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson was the only dissent. The justices wrote that a 1996 immigration law lets the homeland security secretary waive environmental and conservation requirements, and that Congress’s preference for expeditious border construction outweighs the aesthetic, recreational, and environmental harms claimed by the Center for Biological Diversity, a Big Bend river guide, and a church preservation group. The administration plans more than 300 miles of six-foot vehicle barriers and roads; Customs and Border Protection has awarded five contracts worth more than $6 billion for upgrades that include 30-foot steel fencing, vehicle barriers, and surveillance technology it calls a “smart wall.” Commissioner Rodney Scott said the wall is going up at about 12 miles a week and is expected to be finished by the end of President Trump’s second term. The Center called the ruling a “horrific decision” that lets contractors bulldoze desert and dynamite canyon walls before legality is decided, and it said a CBP map covers remote park canyons with no documented unauthorized vehicle crossings; after a federal judge paused work in October, CBP said it remained confident in its authority “to secure the border while preserving the unique landscape of the Big Bend region.” Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/09/us-supreme-court-border-wall-texas-big-bend) Source: [usatoday.com](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2026/11/09/texans-supreme-court-decision-big-bend-border-wall/92698754007/)

### The Closer Look
**US Senate advances New Mexico US Sen. Heinrich’s controversial permit reform bill: Source New Mexico**
On Monday evening the United States Senate voted 73 to 25 to end debate on the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act, the permitting bill New Mexico Democrat Martin Heinrich has championed with a bipartisan group of senators. Ending debate took 60 yes votes. The bill now goes to the Senate floor, where senators may add amendments, before a final vote expected in the coming days. It aims to hasten federal environmental reviews of energy projects, and it has drawn criticism from other members of New Mexico’s congressional delegation over provisions they say will weaken environmental protections and exclude tribes from a meaningful role.

Heinrich and his cosponsors released an amended text on Saturday. On Monday morning he and Rhode Island Democrat Sheldon Whitehouse urged senators to pass the bill. Members of the All Pueblo Council of Governors, representing New Mexico Pueblos, flew to Washington that day to urge a no vote. They say the bill, including the Saturday amendment, makes harmful development on sacred sites more likely. Gov.-elect Deb Haaland opposed the amended bill. Attorney General Raúl Torrez opposed it in a letter to Senate leaders signed by attorneys general from several other states. Senator Ben Ray Luján, Democrat of New Mexico, cast one of the no votes. He said he supports faster clean-energy and infrastructure development but, after a month listening to tribal leaders and environmentalists, cannot support advancing the bill in its current form. Other members of Congress said they oppose the bill because they cannot trust President Trump’s administration to permit renewable projects along with fossil-fuel projects such as pipelines.

While the votes were cast, Trump said at a news conference that he fully supported the permitting bill and that he would not approve any new wind turbines, which the bill seeks to encourage. “The bill hasn’t been fully negotiated,” he told reporters, according to The Hill as quoted by Source New Mexico. “I will not approve wind turbines because wind turbines are a major loser.” Heinrich said the bill would reduce energy costs while many Americans are frustrated with rising prices, and would hold data centers financially liable for new costs they impose on energy grids. Erik Schlenker-Goodrich, executive director of the Western Environmental Law Center, said both chambers should wait until January, when a new Democratic majority could have a constructive conversation about renewable infrastructure. He said the sponsors’ urgency exists “to minimize scrutiny and prevent lawmakers and Americans from understanding the enormity of BAAJA’s power transfer away from the people and to the oligarch class.” If the Senate passes the bill, it heads to the House. ### Progress Watch
**Calgary water use remains in ‘green zone’ as Bearspaw feeder main work progresses: Global News**
Calgary and neighbouring communities have logged five straight days in the city’s “green zone,” with water use under the 500-million-litre daily target that keeps supply available for essential services and firefighting while the Bearspaw South Feeder Main is connected. Consumption was 482 million litres on Friday, 483 million on Saturday, and 487 million on Sunday. The city said Monday that draining of the existing main was on track to finish that day and that excavation had begun; from Thursday, crews are to work around the clock at the 89th Street N.W. site for about two weeks, and restrictions are expected for at least another three and a half weeks. Water chief operating officer Michael Thompson said that once this connection is done the city will turn the original main back on, lift restrictions, and use that pipe while the new one is commissioned, with a switch to the new pipe in mid-December. During the restrictions the Bearspaw plant supplies about 20 per cent of daily water and the Glenmore plant the other 80 per cent.
