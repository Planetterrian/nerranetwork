> **Eight points is the Democratic lead among likely voters, one week before control of Congress is decided.**

**What You Need to Know:** Democrats hold an eight-point lead among likely voters, 52 percent to 44 percent, in a PBS News/NPR/Marist poll with one week until Election Day. People who have already voted favor Democrats heavily, while those still waiting are split. In Canada, Olivia Chow won another term in Toronto, and the RCMP is examining Alberta health contracts for possible fraud and money laundering.

### Lead
**Poll: Likely voters favor Democrats as Trump disapproval drives early turnout: PBS**
With one week until Election Day, 52 percent of likely voters would back the Democratic candidate in their congressional district and 44 percent the Republican, an eight-point lead in the latest PBS News/NPR/Marist poll. Independents break for Democrats by 13 points, and 71 percent of Democrats say they are very enthusiastic about voting, against 61 percent of Republicans and 48 percent of independents. Likely voters say by a two-to-one margin that their midterm ballot is a vote against President Donald Trump rather than for him. Among registered voters the Democratic lead is 10 points, down from 12 last month, a shift within the poll's margin of error, and Democrats have led each time Marist has asked since last November. In October 2018 the same likely-voter measure showed a nine-point Democratic edge before the party picked up 40 House seats. More than 30 million ballots have already been cast, including more than 15.1 million by mail and 15.5 million in person, according to the University of Florida Election Lab; about one in five likely voters in the survey have already voted, and that group favors Democrats 68 percent to 31 percent, while those yet to vote split 48 percent apiece. Half of Republican likely voters plan to vote in person on November 3, compared with 31 percent of Democrats. Forty percent of likely voters approve of the job Mr. Trump is doing and 59 percent disapprove, including 53 percent who strongly disapprove; Lee Miringoff, director of the Marist Institute for Public Opinion, said a president whose approval is upside down does not usually put himself on the ballot, and that Mr. Trump has nationalized the race to the concern of some congressional Republicans putting distance between themselves and him. Source: [pbs.org](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/poll-likely-voters-favor-democrats-as-trump-disapproval-drives-early-turnout)

**A Tight Senate Race in Mississippi? Trump and Others Seem to Think So.: The New York Times**
President Trump campaigned Monday in Corinth, Mississippi, for Republican Senator Cindy Hyde-Smith, The New York Times reports, in a state he won by about 23 points in 2024. She has held the seat since 2018 and is still favored, but the Cook Political Report last week shifted the race to likely Republican from solid Republican after late spending and visits from prominent Democrats. Challenger Scott Colom, a Democratic district attorney, has narrowed her lead, polls show, campaigning on health care access, lower inflation and fighting corruption with a coalition of working-class Black and white voters. A Democrat has not won a Mississippi Senate seat since 1982; about 37 percent of residents are Black, the largest share of any state, and if Mr. Colom wins he would be the state's first Black senator since Reconstruction, a milestone he has played down. Former Vice President Kamala Harris campaigned for him last week, and he wrote that Ms. Hyde-Smith was scared, questioning her votes on healthcare, farmers and the Epstein files, while the Times reports Republicans are still projecting confidence. Source: [nytimes.com](https://www.nytimes.com/live/2026/10/26/us/midterms-elections-trump)

### The Closer Look
**Five takeaways from Ontario's municipal elections: The Globe and Mail**
Olivia Chow won re-election as mayor of Toronto with 54 percent of the vote to former councillor Brad Bradford's 41 percent, with almost all votes counted. In her victory speech, The Globe and Mail reports, she said Prime Minister Mark Carney and Ontario Premier Doug Ford had called to congratulate her, and that she looks forward to working with both governments. She pledged to keep investing in police, paramedics, emergency response and community programs, and to keep the city's water in public hands. The water line answered Mr. Bradford's promise to move Toronto Water into a separate, publicly owned company. Critics alleged that split was a step toward privatization. He denied it.

Key ward results appeared to go her way. Mike Layton, her stepson and son of the late federal NDP leader Jack Layton, won easily in University-Rosedale. Diana Chan McNally, an advocate for the homeless, will replace Gord Perks in Parkdale-High Park after he stepped down. In Toronto-Danforth, former school trustee Sara Ehrhardt, backed by long-time councillor Paula Fletcher, defeated former councillor Mary Fragedakis. In Toronto Centre, Curran Stikuts unseated Chris Moise. Mr. Stikuts, a former activist with The 519, had the endorsement of Kristyn Wong-Tam, the New Democratic Party member of provincial parliament.

Frances Nunziata lost York South-Weston to social worker Chiara Padovani, on Padovani's third attempt. Ms. Nunziata had spent 16 years as speaker of council and had been a councillor since amalgamation in 1998. In Scarborough North, former Liberal member of Parliament Shaun Chen defeated Chow ally Jamaal Myers, chair of the Toronto Transit Commission. In Beaches-East York, former Liberal MP Nate Erskine-Smith, who pledged to work with Ms. Chow, won the ward Mr. Bradford vacated, beating former CTV reporter Natalie Johnson.

Alvin Tedjo was elected mayor of Mississauga. The first-term councillor, 43 and a father of three, beat incumbent Carolyn Parrish and former mayor Bonnie Crombie, who returned after losing the 2025 provincial election. He ran on affordability and community safety. In Hamilton, incumbent Andrea Horwath, former leader of the Ontario NDP, came third with 14 percent in early returns, while Keanin Loomis won more than 40 percent. Mr. Loomis, former head of the Hamilton Chamber of Commerce, now works in steel construction, an industry the paper says U.S. tariffs have hammered, and he campaigned on respecting taxpayers and a responsive city hall. Source: [theglobeandmail.com](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-five-takeaways-from-ontarios-municipal-elections/)
