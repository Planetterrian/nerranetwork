### The Closer Look
**Ukrainian war veteran from Manitoba's Interlake walks the walk in fundraising campaign: Yahoo News Canada**
Peter Derksen, 60, a retired Winnipeg police officer from Manitoba's Interlake, has completed a nearly six-month hike of more than 4,000 kilometres on the Pacific Crest Trail, on foot from the U.S.-Mexico border to Canada, to raise money for Ukraine's war effort. He began in early April and reached the northern terminus in late September. On October 5, just over a month after he finished, he said he never doubted he would finish, even though it was a momentous task and incredibly difficult. He said nothing he put up with in his suffering equated to what soldiers are suffering in Ukraine.

The trail begins near Campo, California, and ends at Manning Provincial Park in British Columbia. Hikers who complete it cross 26 national forests and three U.S. states. Derksen averaged the equivalent of a marathon each day, carrying up to 50 pounds of camping gear, food and water. The trail officially spans about 4,265 kilometres; he estimated he walked over 5,000 because of fire closures, rerouting and diversions to restock. He faced the heat of Southern California, an American black bear and her cubs in the Sierra Nevada, and he summited Mount Whitney, the highest peak in the contiguous United States, on his 60th birthday. He witnessed multiple helicopter rescues of other hikers. Chris Rylee, communications director for the Pacific Crest Trail Association, called a single continuous hike an audacious proposal that very few attempt and even fewer complete. The association generally sees about 5,000 permit applications a year and around 1,000 to 2,000 self-reported completions; only a tiny handful are thru-hikers who finish without skipping sections.

The hike left swelling and calluses that grew his feet nearly three shoe sizes, plus stress fractures and numbness in his toes. He calls that numbness Christmas toe: the feeling, hikers say, comes back by Christmas. He raised over $7,000 for two charities, including the Canada-Ukraine Foundation. Valeriy Kostyuk, the foundation's executive director, said the impact goes beyond fundraising to keeping Ukraine relevant, and that the foundation has contributed more than $100 million in aid since the invasion, all of it from donations. Derksen had served about three years with the Royal Winnipeg Rifles reserve and specialized in security, weapons handling and tactical procedures with the Winnipeg Police Service. He travelled to Ukraine in September 2022 and stayed until September 2023, first with the International Legion for the Defence of Ukraine and later as a member of the Ukrainian army. His brigade, he said, operated near the front lines and often suffered multiple casualties each day. Soldiers who fought alongside him have reached out to thank him. He said he kept walking so the world would stay aware that Ukrainians are still struggling to live peacefully and free. **The number of young Canadians participating in cadet programs reaches record high: The Globe and Mail**
As of September, more than 72,000 Canadians aged 12 to 18 are in the Cadet and Junior Canadian Ranger programs, 34 per cent more than a decade ago, The Globe and Mail reports, with more than 16,000 young people on wait-lists. The programs are funded by the Department of National Defence, but cadets are not members of the military. Air Cadets are the largest stream, with more than 38,000 enrolled. Major Anthony Vukojevic, commanding officer of a Toronto air cadet squadron, said many parents choose the programs because there are no barriers to joining and everything is free, against youth sports that can cost families thousands of dollars. The wait-list is the measure of demand the record enrollment has not yet met.
