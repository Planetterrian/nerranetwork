**Election Dispatches: Trump Rallies in Texas for 3rd Time This Month as G.O.P. Anxieties Mount: The New York Times**
President Trump rallied in Corpus Christi on Tuesday, his third Texas stop this month, with an evening rally in Lubbock where the Senate rivals were also converging, the New York Times reported. Attorney General Ken Paxton, the Republican candidate for U.S. Senate, gave a shortened stump speech, urged Republicans to vote, and attacked Democrat James Talarico, saying, "I'm going to ask you to pray. The Times noted Trump once owned a United States Football League franchise. Gudea, analyzing nearly a million social-media posts, found much of the online talk about the Texas and Kansas Senate races coming from accounts that appear automated and that comment in more than one state. More than 85 percent of the Texas discourse about Talarico and Paxton was reshared posts. Chief executive Keith Presley said the topics and the framing were in place before most users showed up, that an ordinary person scrolling has no way to tell the difference, and that "volume is easy to manufacture, and right now it gets read as public opinion." The Times also noted that this year hundreds of fake pro-Trump avatars were generated by artificial intelligence and put on social media to attract conservative voters. ### The Region and the World
**Canada signs trade deal with UAE in push for non-US markets: Al Jazeera**
With a deal in Washington still out of reach, Canada and the United Arab Emirates said Tuesday they signed a comprehensive trade deal to lower barriers and boost investment. Prime Minister Mark Carney and Abu Dhabi Crown Prince Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan presided over the signing of the Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement, which aims to cut tariffs, reduce red tape and improve market access. The Prime Minister's Office said that once fully implemented the pact will reduce or eliminate tariffs on over 99 percent of current Canadian exports to the UAE, opening markets the office named for pulses and canola, industrial machinery and power-generation equipment, aerospace and other innovators, and that CEPA is expected to enter into force in 2027. The office said the agreement will also address non-tariff barriers and expand access for goods and services in the industrial, agricultural, forest, fish and seafood, environmental and engineering sectors. The two governments also signed preliminary agreements on energy, diplomatic and defence cooperation. Foreign Trade Minister Thani bin Ahmed Al Zeyoudi, who signed with Canadian counterpart Maninder Sidhu, said the UAE sees potential for exporters and for investment in logistics, communications, advanced technology and manufacturing. Al Jazeera reported that the UAE is the largest Middle East market for Canadian exports and a major buyer of Canadian motor vehicles and parts and of machinery. Two-way trade was worth 3.5 billion Canadian dollars, about $2.48 billion, last year, and has been growing at 10 percent annually for the last few years, according to government data. The UAE committed up to $50 billion in investment in Canada last year. Carney has pledged to double Canada's non-U.S. trade over the next decade after President Donald Trump's tariffs rippled through the economy and later attempts to reach a deal with Washington broke down, pushing Ottawa toward pacts from the Asia Pacific to Europe and the Middle East.
