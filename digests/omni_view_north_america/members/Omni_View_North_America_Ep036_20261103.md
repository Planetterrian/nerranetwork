### Lead
**Voters head to the polls as control of Congress is on the ballot: The New York Times**
Voters across the United States began heading to the polls on Tuesday in midterms that will decide which party controls Congress for the final two years of President Trump’s second term, the New York Times and The Guardian report. All 435 voting House seats are contested; The Guardian reports Republicans are defending a narrow majority and Democrats need a net gain of four seats to take the House. Thirty-five Senate seats are up — 33 in regular elections and special elections in Florida and Ohio — and Republicans hold 53 seats against 47 held by Democrats and independents who caucus with them; The Guardian reports Democrats need to gain four seats and defend all of their own to secure a majority. NBC News reports Democrats would have to flip at least two states President Trump won by double digits in 2024. Voters are also choosing governors in 36 states and three US territories, 39 governorships in all, plus thousands of state legislative seats, local offices, judicial contests and ballot measures on taxes, abortion rights and voting rules. The New York Times reports Mr. Trump has urged supporters to act as if he is on the ballot and has insisted Republicans would win if his base turned out; Democratic campaigns have blamed the president and his signature policies for household struggles; Republicans have tried to turn the election into a choice against opponents they cast as radical, far-left activists. The Times reports this is the most expensive midterm in political advertising since at least 2018, according to AdImpact, and that Elon Musk contributed $474 million to Republican candidates and their allies. The first polls close at 7 p.m. Eastern; the Times says full results could take days or weeks, and that in 2024 it took three weeks to determine winners in California’s closest House races. A federal judge turned down a request by Democratic lawmakers and party organizations to prohibit the Trump administration from coercing states into screening incoming ballots, and the Supreme Court signaled it was not interested in assisting Mr. Trump’s efforts to interfere with how states administer elections, the Times reports. The Guardian reports the president’s party has lost House seats in 18 of the past 20 midterms, a stretch back to 1946. ### Across the Region
**How a New Brunswick island is caught in the middle of the Canada-U.S. trade war: Global News**
About 900 people live on Campobello Island, New Brunswick, whose only road bridge runs to Lubec, Maine, not to the rest of Canada, Global News reports. There is no gas station on the island and grocery options are limited, so residents cross for basic goods; a student who lives on the island and attends high school in the United States said she crosses the border almost every morning. Residents paid a 25 per cent tariff on US goods brought across the border until Ottawa announced a special exemption for the island. Last month a truckload of supplies was turned back by US border officials, and a Campobello brewery has shut down because of the tariffs. Canadian tourism to Maine was cut in half last year following President Trump’s taunts and tariffs. New Brunswick MP John Williamson said decisions made in distant capitals hit Campobello immediately; Lubec resident Nelson Meaker said there is no reason to cause bad blood among good neighbours. American fisherman Dana Barker, who has lived on the island with his Canadian wife for 50 years, said the two sides will work it out. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12130717/island-caught-canada-u-s-tensions-midterms-campobello-nb/)

### The Closer Look
**Ohio’s Senate, governor and House races on a right-shifted map: PBS**
Voters in Ohio are choosing a US senator in a special election, a governor, and the state’s 15 US House seats on Tuesday, in a state the Associated Press, via PBS, describes as having shifted sharply toward the right over the last decade. Polls close at 7:30 p.m. Eastern. For Democrats nationally, PBS reports, Ohio may be crucial to a bid to retake Congress.

Republican Jon Husted faces Democrat Sherrod Brown for the Senate seat. Husted was appointed in 2025 by Republican Governor Mike DeWine to fill the vacancy JD Vance left when he became vice president. The winner serves the remaining two years of that term. Brown held Ohio’s other Senate seat for three terms until 2025, when Republican Bernie Moreno defeated him in a tight race. President Trump carried Ohio in the last three presidential elections, and Republicans have controlled the governor’s office and the state Legislature for more than a decade. Husted has the president’s endorsement.

The cost of living has been a core issue. Brown has tied Trump’s war in Iran to climbing gas prices and attacked Husted for repeated votes against war-powers resolutions that could have directed the president to end the conflict. In September, Husted said the war needs to come to an end as quickly as possible so gas prices can be lowered. Brown has labeled Husted “the face of data centers,” a reference to Husted’s support for data-center projects as lieutenant governor, a post he held until 2025. In August, the National Republican Senatorial Committee warned in a memo that Husted could lose because of data centers.

Brown raised over $68.4 million as of mid-October, against $19.9 million raised by Husted. Hundreds of millions of dollars more have been spent by outside groups to oppose both campaigns.

DeWine cannot run for reelection because of term limits. Democrat Amy Acton, a physician, faces Republican Vivek Ramaswamy, a billionaire biotech entrepreneur and Trump ally. Acton was Ohio’s public-health director, a post DeWine appointed her to early in the pandemic; she implemented strict public-health measures that prompted a backlash. In the weeks before the election, Ramaswamy defended his campaign’s decision to publish an ad in which Acton’s estranged mother calls Acton a “liar.” Acton was removed from her mother’s custody at 12 after her stepfather was indicted for raping her. Trump has stood by Ramaswamy since the ad made national news. DeWine called for the ad to be taken down. On Thursday, Ramaswamy said the ad’s planned run was over.

Certain House races became more competitive after mid-decade redistricting favoring Republicans. In the 9th District, Democratic Representative Marcy Kaptur, who has held the seat since 1983, faces a rematch with Republican Derek Merrin, a former state House member. In 2024, before that redistricting, Kaptur won by less than one percentage point. In the 7th District, Republican Representative Max Miller faces Brian Poindexter, a union organizer and ironworker. Miller’s ex-wife, Emily Moreno, alleged that Miller abused her. Her father, Senator Bernie Moreno, and Husted have called on Miller to drop out. Source: [pbs.org](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/live-results-ohio-governor-midterm-election)
