### Lead
**Alberta government to revise policy as school boards say new flag regulations could cost millions of dollars: CBC**
Alberta is preparing to revise new school flag regulations after education officials warned that compliance could place millions of dollars in added expenses on school boards, CBC and the Philippine Canadian Inquirer report. The regulation is scheduled to take effect in January 2027. It requires public schools to display both the Canadian flag and the Alberta flag outdoors, on separate flagpoles. Several divisions say many campuses have only one existing pole, so they would have to buy and install another. The Calgary Board of Education estimates it would need 223 additional flagpoles, with installation costs reaching approximately four million dollars. The Calgary Catholic School District expects to require another 122 poles, and it estimates each installation at between 20,000 and 30,000 dollars. Edmonton Public Schools reported that more than 170 school sites would need additional flagpoles. Edmonton Catholic Schools identified 77 locations requiring the same upgrades. Education and Childcare Minister Demetrios Nicolaides says the province is reviewing the regulation to prevent school divisions from shouldering unnecessary expenses. He has advised boards to hold off, for now, on purchasing new flagpoles while the government works on revised guidelines. One possible adjustment would let schools display both flags indoors instead of requiring separate outdoor poles. The Alberta School Boards Association called on the province to suspend the regulation, citing the financial burden and the need to preserve local decision-making. Premier Danielle Smith acknowledged that the government had not anticipated how many schools operated with only one flagpole. Opposition NDP representatives questioned the consultation process, arguing that education officials and school boards should have been involved before the policy was introduced. The proposed changes come as the new display rules also restrict which other flags may be shown on school grounds, particularly those considered to carry political or ideological messages. **Zohran Mamdani unveils plan to close Rikers Island jail complex: The Guardian**
New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani on Friday unveiled a plan to close the Rikers Island jail complex, The Guardian reports. The stated goal is to end all incarceration at the current facility and relocate its remaining jail population to smaller facilities across four boroughs by the end of 2032. Mamdani said, “It is time to finally close Rikers Island.” He said New Yorkers have for decades demanded a correctional system that is safe and humane, and that the plan puts the city on a path to deliver that. He said the plan would close Rikers, reduce pretrial detention, tackle case delays that deprive people of a right to a speedy trial, and invest 146 million dollars in supports he called evidence-based and said help break cycles of incarceration. NPR reports that city officials are moving forward with plans to close Rikers, the city’s main jail complex, after years of discussion. NPR says the jail has been plagued by assaults and inmate deaths. ### The Closer Look
**California’s coastal communities on alert as biggest waves in years hit state: The Guardian**
Coastal communities in California spent this week under hazard warnings as successive areas of low pressure generated powerful swells and large breaking waves, The Guardian reports. Multiple coastal hazard warnings were issued throughout the week. Beaches and piers were closed last weekend as large waves crashed into the coastline. Offshore National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration buoys recorded wave heights greater than 30 feet, or 9 metres, last Friday. The Monterey, California, buoy recorded a wave height of 34.1 feet. The Guardian says that reading would make it the highest there in 30 years. Coastal warnings that began last weekend extended through this week, with alerts running until Friday night. For San Luis Obispo County beaches and Santa Barbara County central-coast beaches, waves of 10 to 18 feet are expected, with local sets of 18 to 22 feet.

The Guardian links those swells to the same Pacific storm systems now bringing unsettled conditions across the western United States: heavy rain, mountain snow, and strong winds through the coming days. San Francisco already saw what The Guardian calls record-breaking rainfall on Wednesday. Heavy rain also caused 32,000 customers to lose power in Sacramento. Into this weekend, a further area of low pressure is expected to bring considerable moisture to southern Oregon and northern California. By Sunday night, more than 100 millimetres, or 4 inches, of rain is expected in places. Further snowfall is expected for areas such as Donner Pass across the northern Sierra Nevada.

For people on the coast, the week changed physical access and hazard status. Beaches and piers closed last weekend. Alerts were still set to run into Friday night. Another round of 10-to-18-foot waves, with local sets up to 22 feet, is forecast for San Luis Obispo County beaches and Santa Barbara County’s central coast. Inland, the same systems have already meant a Wednesday of record rain in San Francisco, as The Guardian describes it, and a power cut affecting 32,000 Sacramento customers. Through Sunday night, the forecast The Guardian cites still has more than 100 millimetres of rain in places in southern Oregon and northern California, plus more snow at Donner Pass. The account ends on those Friday-night alerts and that Sunday-night rain and snow forecast.
