### The Closer Look
**Canadian Blood Services says longtime plasma donor’s death not connected to donation: The Globe and Mail**
Canadian Blood Services says a plasma donor died a day after giving plasma at a CBS facility, and the charity believes the death was not connected to the donation. Collectors must report to Health Canada if a donor dies within 72 hours of a donation, and the first report must go out within 24 hours of the collector learning of the death. Chief executive Graham Sher told The Globe and Mail on Friday that the person was a long-time donor and volunteer. CBS learned of the death three weeks later, when the family told clinic staff, then reported it quickly to Health Canada and reviewed the records. Sher said nothing unusual happened during the donation and the donor appeared fine on leaving. The family told CBS that doctors described the death the next day as natural causes. Sher said a natural-cause death happened to be temporally related to a previous donation. He told the paper that donors, like anyone else, have illnesses, falls, trips, and motor vehicle accidents, and that this death “just happened to be temporally related to a previous donation.”

Health Canada said it received notice on September 25, which would suggest the death occurred in early September, and it would not give the date or location out of concern for privacy. CBS, which runs the blood system everywhere in Canada but Quebec, is also withholding where it happened and nearly all other details, saying only that it was earlier in the fall, to protect the donor’s privacy. It is the first time CBS has reported such an incident since at least 2018, the earliest year for which records are available. Plasma, a golden-coloured fluid used as a key ingredient of medicines such as immunoglobulin, has been under scrutiny after two donors died after giving plasma at Grifols, a Spanish pharmaceutical company that collects in Canada through a partnership with CBS. Health Canada found operational deficiencies at multiple Grifols locations, suspended the licence of one site in September, and Grifols paused Canadian operations before saying last week it would gradually reopen.

Health Canada said CBS holds the duty, under federal regulation, to conduct a thorough investigation. The regulator reviewed documentation the charity provided. Those documents, Health Canada said, showed that CBS followed appropriate procedures, with no suggestion of equipment failure or malfunction. The regulator did not say it was taking further action. Health Canada has previously told The Globe and Mail that, as a regulator of blood and plasma products, it does not determine the cause of death, including whether the donation process may have contributed. CBS reported 28 adverse reactions in the 2025-26 year, lower than previous years. An adverse reaction is defined in federal regulations as an undesirable response associated with the collection of blood or plasma. Sher said this death is not classified as one because the charity believes it is unrelated. Earlier this year the regulator gave the date and location of two other such deaths, in Winnipeg on October 25, 2025, and on January 30, 2026, and it also disclosed a June 4, 2018, report from Winnipeg. It did not immediately answer why those details were released then and not in this case. Grifols said in August it had revised its own reports of serious adverse reactions — incidents of injury or death — and believed there were only six such incidents at its facilities since 2018, down from 25 previously reported to Health Canada. Source: [theglobeandmail.com](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-canadian-blood-services-plasma-donor-death-not-connected-donation/)
