> **Trump now offers a ten-year stay deal so some long-term immigrants can remain.**

**What You Need to Know:** President Trump is offering a ten-year stay deal so some long-term undocumented immigrants can remain, after telling CBS's 60 Minutes that his deportation policy was too tough. He said people convicted of serious crimes should still be removed, days after Stephen Miller was elevated to White House chief of staff. He also linked much of the Republican midterm defeat to the war with Iran and higher petrol prices.

### Lead
**Trump admits deportation policy was 'too tough' despite promotion of Stephen Miller: The Guardian**
On CBS's 60 Minutes on Sunday, President Trump told correspondent Ross Douthat that he thought his deportation policy was "too tough," and that he was open to an arrangement letting some undocumented immigrants remain. Asked whether the policy had gone too far, he said, "I think it might be too tough, yeah," adding that a lot of people would say it is great and a lot of people think it is too tough. He said people here for 10 or 15 years should "work a deal," while removal continued for killers, rapists and drug dealers, and he called that "something that's actually doable." He said the policy "both helped and hurt": an "A+" for closing the border and "very high marks for getting the bad ones out," but political damage because people with good jobs were removed for the violation of entering illegally. The Guardian reports those comments sit against Friday's elevation of Stephen Miller, whom it calls the architect of the crackdown, to White House chief of staff. The same account says that since Trump returned to office, Immigration and Customs Enforcement raids at workplaces, homes and public spaces have ensnared people with no criminal records, separated families and spread fear in immigrant communities, and that federal agents fatally shot two US citizens, Renee Good and Alex Pretti, during operations in Minneapolis this year. Trump said some people believe undocumented immigrants should stay if they are "good people working in a landscaping company" and in some cases paying taxes; he said some Democrats had already contacted him, and that he could refrain from name-calling for six months "very easily." He agreed with Vice President JD Vance that the war with Iran was a significant factor in the Republican midterm defeat, also citing higher petrol prices and what he called a growing dislike of Israel, while saying he would make the same decision again because "you can't let Iran have a nuclear weapon," that the war had been won, and that a renewed assault was something he was not looking to escalate after the midterms. In a separate CNN interview he called a renewed assault "highly unlikely," depending on "whether or not they sign a very strong agreement"; on the economy he said the administration had failed to communicate despite his claims of record employment, investment and stock-market figures, and "it was bad messaging, maybe by me"; on artificial intelligence, "I don't mind guardrails," but not a "deep second position." Douthat cited a Forbes estimate that Trump's net worth rose by $3 billion from the autumn of 2024 to the autumn of 2025. ### The Region and the World
**Ottawa poised to seal trade deal with Philippines, now home to Canada's second-biggest embassy: The Globe and Mail**
Carney arrives Saturday on a trip that also includes a European leaders' meeting in Ireland, the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in China, and the Francophonie summit in Cambodia. Global Affairs spokesperson Aditee Kissoon said the Manila mission had about 300 positions as of early October, counting locally hired staff and officials from federal departments, provincial governments and Crown corporations, second only to Washington by headcount, with London, Mexico City and Delhi next. Under the 2022 Indo-Pacific strategy, Canada has shared Dark Vessel Detection satellite data to help Manila track illegal fishing and Chinese coast guard ships, including CCG-5901, which a Philippine Coast Guard spokesman publicly acknowledged in 2024 and which Manila has accused of intimidation; the Globe says the program's primary purpose is fighting overfishing, including vessels Manila believes are part of Beijing's maritime militia, and that the data has also helped track coast guard ships enforcing disputed claims in the South China Sea, where the Philippines has clashed with Beijing over reefs and shoals and where its northernmost islands sit near a gateway to the open Pacific, a short distance from Taiwan, which the Globe describes as a self-governed island of about 23 million people claimed by China. The embassy now hosts the Indo-Pacific Agriculture and Agri-Food Office, opened in 2024, and an Export Development Canada office opened the same year; Citizenship and Immigration operations in Manila handle visa work beyond the Philippines; close to one million people in Canada claim Filipino ancestry; former ambassador Peter MacArthur, who served in 2020 and 2022, said locally hired Filipino staff are particularly effective, that English is a working language, that the mission does back-office work for other Indo-Pacific embassies, and that it has become a global processing centre for Immigration Canada; and this year Ottawa named Lieutenant-General Derek Macaulay, the Chief of the Defence Staff's envoy to military leaders from Tokyo to Hanoi to Kuala Lumpur, as Canada's first senior military representative for the Indo-Pacific.
