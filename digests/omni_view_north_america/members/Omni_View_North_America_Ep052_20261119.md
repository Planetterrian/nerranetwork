### The Closer Look
**Menopause experts raise concerns after Pfizer discontinues drug in Canada: The Globe and Mail**
Pfizer is stopping supply of Premarin cream and Premarin oral estrogen tablets in Canada while continuing to offer them in the United States, and menopause specialists say Canadian patients will carry the consequences. The decision was made public last week. Existing inventory is expected to last until next year. Premarin was first developed and approved in Canada in 1941. Pfizer manufactures the global supply of the active ingredient in Brandon, Manitoba, from the urine of pregnant mares in Manitoba and Saskatchewan.

Pfizer first notified Health Canada on November 3 that it would stop supplying Premarin products, according to a federal drug-shortage database. The cream is being discontinued immediately. Pills will be available while inventory lasts, potentially until next June, the database notices say. Pfizer said the products were commercially unsustainable in Canada. Sophie Morin, a spokesperson for Pfizer Canada, said the company considered manufacturing and supply costs, the pricing and reimbursement environment, and the ability to maintain sustainable supply over the longer term. The federal database shows the cream has been in shortage four times since the beginning of 2025.

In the United States, demand for menopause hormone therapy has grown after the Food and Drug Administration initiated the removal of long-standing safety warnings on the treatments last November. Health Canada has not removed similar warnings on products in Canada. Demand is rising here as well. In September, a Manulife Canada report on its group benefits showed a 228 percent increase in hormone-replacement therapy claims over five years among women 45 to 54.

Canadian obstetrician-gynecologist Jen Gunter wrote on Wednesday on her blog that Pfizer’s decision should concern all Canadians, and that the issue is larger than one brand: people who care about access to medications should speak up. In cream form, Premarin applies estrogen directly to vaginal tissue. It is used to treat atrophic vaginitis, including drying of the vaginal walls and shrinkage of the skin of the vagina and vulva, and it can be used for dyspareunia, pain or discomfort during intercourse. Gunter wrote that some alternatives are available, but that Premarin may be better for some patients on side effects and efficacy. Physicians say stopping certain treatments can bring back hot flashes and vaginal irritation and can set patients back in their menopause care.

A tube of Premarin cream in Canada typically retails for around $50 because of federal drug rules, compared with around $400 to $500 in the United States. Some patients pay less if they have insurance. Rithvika Ramesh, a family physician in Toronto, said the discontinuation affects some of her patients. It is the only vaginal estrogen cream covered under the Ontario Drug Benefit program, which covers certain medications for eligible residents. She said the cream has long been used for genitourinary symptoms of menopause and to help prevent recurrent urinary tract infections. For older adults, she said, preventing those infections can be life-saving, because a seemingly uncomplicated infection can progress to a kidney infection, hospitalization, and sometimes sepsis — an extreme immune reaction that can shut organs down and, in some cases, lead to death. She said access to effective vaginal estrogen is not only a quality-of-life question but a part of preventive care.

Lily Barnes, a spokesperson for Ontario Health Minister Sylvia Jones, said vaginal estrogen products identified by the Canadian Menopause Society, including a vaginal ring, a tablet, and inserts, are still covered on the provincial drug formulary. She said the Canadian Pharmacists Association has developed a resource for health care providers to manage supply changes. Ramesh said people who rely on the cream may face additional out-of-pocket costs and may have to move to a product that does not match their symptoms, preferences, and needs. Source: [theglobeandmail.com](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-menopause-experts-raise-concerns-after-pfizer-discontinues-premarin/)
