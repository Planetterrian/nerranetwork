### Lead
**Bayer reaches Roundup settlement that could cost the company billions: NPR**
NPR reports that Bayer has reached a Roundup settlement that could cost the company billions. The money will go to thousands of people who have been diagnosed with non-Hodgkin lymphoma or who will be diagnosed with it. NPR describes that illness as a blood cancer linked to the herbicide. The people covered are not limited to those already diagnosed; the report says the money is also for people who will be diagnosed. NPR presents the settlement as the result now on the record. It does not, in the account used here, describe a new trial verdict sitting beside that settlement. What changes is the path for those thousands: payment is tied to the settlement NPR reports, and the cost to Bayer is framed as one that could run to billions. The herbicide at the center of the claims is Roundup. Source: [npr.org](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/21/nx-s1-6049684/roundup-bayer-hodgkins-settlement)

**Judge Blocks Trump Administration’s Effort to Transform Homelessness Policy: The New York Times**
A federal judge in Rhode Island has blocked the Trump administration’s effort to transform homelessness policy, The New York Times reports. The paper calls the decision a blow to an effort to shift aid away from housing subsidies. The shift the administration had pursued would have favored treatment for mental illness and addiction instead. The ruling stops that shift from going forward on the terms the administration had pursued. The actor is a federal judge in Rhode Island, and the policy under challenge is the administration’s homelessness-aid approach. People who receive housing subsidies are the ones the blocked shift would have moved away from, toward treatment-centered aid, according to the Times account. Source: [nytimes.com](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/21/us/politics/judge-trump-homeless-aid-addiction-treatment.html)

**The Penny Is Dead. Now It’s Official.: The New York Times**
President Trump signed a law on Friday formally putting the penny out of mint, The New York Times reports. The coin had already stopped being struck a year earlier. Friday’s signature is the formal end of minting, not the day the presses first went quiet. The Times presents the signing as making that earlier stop official. Anyone still using pennies already in circulation is not told, in this account, that existing coins were pulled. What changed on Friday is the legal status of further minting. Source: [nytimes.com](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/21/us/politics/penny-mint-trump-common-cent-act.html)

### The Closer Look
**‘He got a great a**’: Trump impersonator’s cheeky joke leaves Mark Carney in splits: WION**
At Ottawa’s annual Parliamentary Press Gallery Dinner on Saturday, a Donald Trump impersonator performed beside Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney and left him laughing, WION reports. Pretending to be the US president, the performer joked about having two Carneys on stage and described the Canadian leader as “almost as popular as me.” An explicit remark about Carney’s backside drew particular attention; the impersonator called it “incredible” and “beautiful,” then mimicked what WION calls Trump’s signature firm handshake. The same set turned to the trade fight. The performer joked about getting a deal done “before the nukes,” described himself as “crazy,” and said he was looking for an ambassador “less likeable than me.” He delivered the line “He puts the ass in ambassador,” and joked that he would reject press-secretary candidates who were “too smart.” On the Canada–US trade war, WION quotes him: “We’re gonna have a great deal. There’s only one thing standing in the way – me, because I’m crazy, but that’s ok.”

Carney used the same dinner to answer in kind. “This is a tough room. And I've been in the Oval Office. Alone. With its new interior decorator,” he said, drawing laughter, in a reference to the US president’s revamp of the Oval Office and his taste for elaborate decor. He also said he was prepared to work with “anyone—ideologues, tyrants, dictators,” and described that approach as “cooperative federalism.” Those lines are jokes at a press dinner, attributed by WION to Carney, not a new statute.

WION places the laughter against a feud it says has run for the past year, with Canada and the United States locked in a bitter trade dispute. Trade talks between the two countries collapsed in September, WION reports. Trump imposed 50 per cent tariffs on imports from Canada. Ottawa retaliated with what it described as “dollar for dollar” tariffs on American goods, which WION says further worsened relations. The people on the receiving end of that tariff pair are importers and exporters on both sides of the border: American goods faced Ottawa’s matching tariffs, and Canadian imports faced the 50 per cent US rate WION attributes to Trump. The dinner did not, in this account, restart the talks that collapsed in September. It put the collapsed talks, the tariff rates, and each leader’s public posture in the same room, with Carney speaking for himself and the impersonator speaking only as a performer. Source: [wionews.com](https://www.wionews.com/trending/he-got-a-great-a-trump-impersonator-s-cheeky-joke-leaves-mark-carney-in-splits-video-1795341302033)

**6 buyers won back their pre-construction apartment deposits: The Times of India**
Six buyers won back pre-construction apartment deposits after a developer moved completion forward by a year but notified them six months later, The Times of India reports. The Supreme Court of British Columbia ruled that the delay breached disclosure rules. The ruling returns those deposits to the six buyers, a completed court result rather than a promise of repayment. For those buyers, the measurable change is the return of money they had already put down on apartments whose completion date moved.
