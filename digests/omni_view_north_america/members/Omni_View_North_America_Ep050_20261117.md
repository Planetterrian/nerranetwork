> **Nine years on, Canada and China revive a currency swap, easing tariffs for exporters.**

**What You Need to Know:** Nine years on, Canada and China are reviving a currency swap and easing tariffs, a shift for exporters who move goods between the two countries. In Shenzhen on Tuesday, President Xi Jinping and Prime Minister Mark Carney paired that relief with more flights and a wider cooperation list ahead of the Asia-Pacific leaders' meeting. Outlets also reported looser Canadian steel rules and wider Chinese access for pork.

### Lead
**Carney and Xi Announce Trade Pact Ahead of Asia Summit: The New York Times**
In Shenzhen on Tuesday, Chinese President Xi Jinping met Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, who is in China for the 33rd Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation Economic Leaders' Meeting on November 18 and 19, Xinhua reported. The New York Times reported that the two announced a trade pact ahead of that summit. Anadolu Agency reported that China and Canada agreed to tariff relief. The South China Morning Post reported that the two sides will revive a bilateral currency swap after nine years. Yahoo Finance Canada reported that Xi is opening more pork access as Carney loosens steel restrictions. CBC reported partnerships on Carney's first full day in Shenzhen: an expansion of current tariff agreements, more flights between the two countries, and agreements covering education and the arts. Xi said improving the relationship is the right choice for both peoples and for world peace, stability, development and prosperity, and that China is ready to advance the new strategic partnership launched when the two met earlier this year. He said the countries should deepen cooperation in trade, energy, agriculture and finance, and expand it in the green economy, e-commerce and the digital economy. Carney said relations have been on the right track since that partnership began, and that Canada will pursue stronger cooperation in trade, investment, new energy, agricultural products, artificial intelligence and tourism, reduce the list of irritants and expand the list of cooperation. CFJC Today reported that Carney and Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum are set to meet in China. ### The Closer Look
**After 7 years in Canada, young student faces deportation: Global News**
Students and faculty at Dawson College in Montreal are rallying around Luisa Juliana Chaves Munoz, a young student ordered to leave Canada with her family by the end of December. Global News reported that her classmates and teachers argue she should at least be allowed to finish her studies first. Munoz said the family received the deportation notice on October 15, seven years after they arrived. On Monday the campus launched a letter-writing campaign directed at several members of Parliament, hoping political pressure could help the family remain. The office of Brossard-St-Lambert MP Alexandra Mendès said it is working with the family and declined further details, citing privacy protocols.

Munoz said the family came from Colombia seeking asylum because of violence and death threats against her mother. The asylum claim was denied. She said she left as a child, no longer has ties to Colombia, and that her only childhood friend was killed as an indirect result of the violence there. "Now that she's gone, I would have to relive the whole thing," Munoz said about returning. The family applied for permanent residency on humanitarian grounds two years ago. Canada's immigration department said that application does not cancel a deportation order and would still be reviewed, a process that could take several years.

The stake for Munoz is a credential and money already paid. She said the family has spent $28,000 on tuition and that her courses are not transferable outside Quebec. ### Both Sides: A religious-symbols ban for Quebec elementary students
Quebec's minority Parti Québécois government has proposed extending a ban on religious symbols to elementary school students, and it needs opposition votes to advance the plan. Conservative Leader Eric Duhaime has left the door open. He said his party is generally in favour of living in a secular society, and that the link with limits already placed on some public-sector workers is a wish to banish religious symbols in the public space, especially inside public institutions. He said Conservatives feel uneasy seeing a young girl arrive at school with a veil. He would not commit his party's votes. He said Conservatives want to know how a ban would be implemented, who would be responsible, and what the process would be, before they take a clear stand.

The Quebec Liberals and Québec solidaire have said they would vote against the proposal. Joe Ortona, chair of the English Montreal School Board, said legislating what children can wear would cross a line, and that the debate is pulling focus from real systemic problems in education. Harini Sivalinga of the Canadian Civil Liberties Association called the idea a new low and said people should be as outraged as the association that the government would go this far. Stephen Brown of the National Council of Canadian Muslims asked whether Quebec would deny children entry to math class over a turban or a kippah. The proposal would go further than the previous Coalition Avenir Québec ban on religious symbols for some public-sector workers, a law whose challenge has reached the Supreme Court. That court's decision on Bill 21 is expected in the coming weeks. Source: [globalnews.ca](https://globalnews.ca/news/12153880/quebec-conservatives-pq-religious-symbols-ban-children/)
