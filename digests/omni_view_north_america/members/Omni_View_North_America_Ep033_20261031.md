### The Closer Look
**How fraudsters got a slice of Canada’s bread price-fixing settlement: The Globe and Mail**
After Canada’s bread price-fixing payments went out in May, a market in forwarded e-transfer links appeared, and one bank customer deposited roughly 1,000 of those transfers into a single account, The Globe and Mail reports. Loblaw Cos. Ltd. and its parent, George Weston Ltd., had agreed to pay $500 million for their role in a scheme to fix bread prices. For most recipients the sum was $49.11, sent as an Interac e-transfer. Ken Wu, a Vancouver resident, told the Globe that a stranger on the Chinese app RedNote contacted him this past spring with a split: the stranger had registered with different email addresses, the links could be forwarded, and anyone with a Canadian bank account who opened them could deposit the money, after an upfront payment priced below the payout. Wu, a newcomer who had not bought bread at Loblaw stores in the period covered by the lawsuit, refused that offer and others. He said, “I don’t have that right.” Once people began posting that payments had arrived, the Globe reports, comments on RedNote filled with offers of links, some on generous terms for anyone willing to make, in some cases, hundreds of deposits. The paper says it is impossible to know how many of those posts sold working links and how many looked for someone who would pay and get nothing.

The settlement was the first to emerge from lawsuits launched after the companies reported an alleged industry-wide conspiracy to the Competition Bureau in 2015, in exchange for immunity from criminal prosecution. Other grocers have denied involvement. Canadians who bought bread between 2001 and 2021 were eligible. Court documents last year listed Avocats Inc. among administrators hired by the lawyers and responsible for security procedures meant to prevent fraud. Concilia Services Inc. was chosen by the settlement administrators to make the payments. Loblaw and George Weston worked with those administrators so the payments could be made.

Three small institutions then reported unusual account activity to the settlement administrator, Verita Global LLC, and to its banking partner, the Bank of Nova Scotia. Multiple bread payments were being funnelled into the same accounts, including the customer who deposited roughly 1,000 e-transfers. The Globe reports that when fake claims succeed, legitimate claimants receive a smaller share, and that official estimates of suspicious deposits into Canadian banks have grown. Many applications to the publicized fund, the paper says, were bogus. Source: [theglobeandmail.com](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/canada/article-loblaw-bread-price-fixing-settlement-payout-fraud-class-action-lawsuit/)
