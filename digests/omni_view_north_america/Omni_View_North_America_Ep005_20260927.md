### Both Sides: Hawaii’s handgun permit window and inspection rule
Hawaii’s handgun buyers are living with two rules a federal appeals court has just kept in force: thirty days to use a permit to acquire a handgun, and a requirement that some newly acquired guns be brought to a police station for inspection. The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit upheld both in a 6-5 opinion. The state Department of the Attorney General announced the result.

The strongest case for keeping the rules is the one the majority wrote. Hawaii, the ruling explains, is a shall-issue state: an applicant who satisfies objective requirements may obtain a permit. Guided by the U.S. Supreme Court’s 2022 decision in New York State Rifle and Pistol Association v. Bruen, the en banc court held that this system fits the Second Amendment because the plaintiffs failed to show that the two provisions, which the majority called presumptively lawful, have been put toward abusive ends that would effectively deny ordinary citizens their rights.

The strongest case against is the lawsuit Todd Yukutake and David Kikukawa filed in 2019. They challenged the narrow window as unconstitutional. They also challenged a form asking for information about the firearm, and the duty, in certain circumstances, to bring that firearm to a police station for inspection. Their reason is that those steps still limit acquiring a gun after the state has found that the applicant qualifies.

What both accounts share is the shall-issue frame the ruling sets out: meeting the state’s objective requirements is what opens the door to a permit. The opinion leaves the thirty-day clock and the inspection mandate standing. Source: [thegardenisland.com](https://www.thegardenisland.com/2026/09/27/hawaii-news/appeals-court-backs-hawaiis-gun-permit-window-inspection-mandate/)
