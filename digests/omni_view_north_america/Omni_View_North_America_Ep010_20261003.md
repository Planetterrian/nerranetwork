### Lead
**Trump Suffers Legal Setback As Texas Border Wall Blocked In National Park: Newsweek**
On Friday, Judge Kathleen Cardone of the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Texas ordered a temporary halt to border-wall construction and related infrastructure in the Big Bend region. The preliminary injunction says federal agencies shall not build a border barrier or related infrastructure, or do any construction or ground-disturbing work, in the Big Bend Sector until they comply with all applicable laws. The order covers the entire sector, about 500 miles and roughly a quarter of the U.S.-Mexico border, including Big Bend National Park, which the National Park Service says has 118 miles of border with Mexico. Cardone concluded that a coalition of environmental groups and local landowners is likely to succeed on at least one claim. The court did not decide the underlying case for construction in the sector. Source: [newsweek.com](https://www.newsweek.com/trump-suffers-legal-setback-as-texas-border-wall-blocked-in-national-park-12519989)

**B.C. candidates and voters scramble to get their minds around two elections, one week apart: The Globe and Mail**
British Columbia is holding municipal and provincial elections in the same month for the first time since the province’s Election Act was passed in 1995. **‘It’s enraging’: lawmakers call for action after Guardian investigation into convenience store overcharges: The Guardian**
Five members of the U.S. House have called out pricing at 7-Eleven and Circle K after The Guardian found the chains frequently charge more at the register or pump than shelf tags or outdoor fuel signs promise. Representative Becca Balint, a Democrat from Vermont, said customers are being forced to fact-check every price, and she asked the Federal Trade Commission to investigate and to hold the companies accountable if they are deliberately misleading customers. Representative Nanette Barragán, a Democrat whose district covers part of Los Angeles County, called the findings disturbing and said President Trump and Republicans are raising the cost of food and necessities. Between 2023 and 2025, The Guardian’s review of inspections found 7-Eleven failed 41 percent of price inspections in Arizona and 47 percent in both Colorado and Utah, while Circle K failed 35 percent in Florida, 39 percent in Arizona and 62 percent in North Carolina. The chains did not answer The Guardian’s questions; President Trump has called affordability concerns a “hoax” and a “con job,” and an AP-NORC poll released Thursday found 17 percent of Americans approve of his handling of the cost of living. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/03/convenience-store-overcharges-us-congress)

### Both Sides: Should wall work resume in Big Bend before the laws are met?
Whether federal agencies may build a border wall and related works in Texas’s Big Bend sector before they comply with environmental and other laws is the question left by Friday’s preliminary injunction from Judge Kathleen Cardone.

The plaintiffs, a coalition of environmental groups and local landowners, want the work kept stopped. The reason Cardone said carries them — that they are likely to succeed on at least one claim — is that they face serious, irreparable harm without an injunction, and that the balance of the equities and the public interest weigh in their favor until the government meets all applicable laws.

President Trump’s stated priority, as Newsweek describes it, is reducing illegal crossings of the southern border, which he has made one of the main aims of his presidency. His 2025 law, which the article calls the Big Beautiful Bill, earmarked $46.5 billion for fencing and other obstacles along the 1,954-mile southern border. Planned works include hundreds of miles of 30-foot wall, surveillance systems and river barriers, on private land as well as federal land. In August, bulldozers were seen clearing land in Big Bend National Park, which the article says sparked anger across parties.
