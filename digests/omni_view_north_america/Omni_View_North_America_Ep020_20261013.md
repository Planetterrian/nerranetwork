### Lead
**Federal judge orders halt to taxpayer-funded Trump ads: The Guardian**
US District Judge Jennifer L Rochon of the Southern District of New York ordered President Donald Trump's administration to temporarily halt the broadcast of taxpayer-funded television ads promoting the president ahead of the November 3 midterm elections. The temporary restraining order took effect at 6:45 p.m. Eastern Time on Sunday and is meant to stay in effect for at least 14 days. The order is aimed at those ads. For at least the next two weeks, the administration is barred from putting those taxpayer-funded spots on television before the midterms. ### Across the Region
**Deportation planes use quiet northeast Ohio airports: The Guardian**
Youngstown-Warren regional airport, which handles no commercial flights, saw 398 ICE-contracted shuffle and direct deportation flights last year, The Guardian reports. ICE Flights Monitor, an advocacy group, has recorded about 300 flights so far this year from that airport and about 300 from neighboring Akron-Canton; only seven other US airports have seen more deportation shuffle flights than the two combined. Planes on the ground, including Air Wisconsin aircraft and an Eastern Air Express Boeing 737, can handle about 300 passengers at a time, and The Guardian describes the fields as a cornerstone of the administration's Great Lakes deportation campaign, moving hundreds of immigrants daily among Wisconsin, Michigan, Ohio and Massachusetts. Erin Bracken-Sanchez, a military veteran from Massillon who monitors the planes, said the regional airports are being used to stay under the radar, and that if authorities come for immigrants they could come for others next. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/13/ohio-ice-deportation-flights)

### Both Sides: Who should pay for schools and local services in Wisconsin Senate District 33
Voters in Wisconsin Senate District 33 decide on November 3 who will represent a district covering roughly two-thirds of Waukesha County and portions of Jefferson, Dodge and Washington counties. The policy fight is whether the legislature should take more of the cost of schools and municipal services off local property taxpayers, or stay inside what the incumbent calls government's constitutional limits.

Mike Van Someren, the Democratic challenger, who has held no elected office, says his campaign boils down to affordability, including housing, health care, utilities, transportation, groceries and property taxes. His strongest reason for a larger state role is to free up family dollars: he favors a shift toward more state resources to fund public schools and municipal services instead of relying on local property taxpayers. He also favors expanding the housing supply and increasing health-care price transparency and competition, and he opposes legislative deals that subsidize powerful corporations. In a September email to news outlets, including the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, he wrote that he is running one race and his opponent is running two.

Chris Kapenga, the Republican incumbent, a manufacturing business owner first elected to the seat in 2015, says his main policy is to keep government within its constitutional limits so it remains subject to the will and authority of the people. That is the case against shifting local costs onto the state: if the legislature's job is to stay inside those limits, a broader state funding role is the wrong move. Kapenga will also appear on the December ballot in a special election for Waukesha County executive. The article says he has not left state legislative politics behind for that second race.

The next dated steps are the November 3 Senate election and the December county executive special election. Neither candidate's view is a law until a legislature acts on it. Source: [aol.com](https://www.aol.com/articles/kapenga-van-someren-view-role-100422000.html)
