Ontario's provincial police are looking at allegations about a handful of private career colleges, and they have not decided whether a formal criminal investigation starts.

Global News reports that the Ford government is under growing scrutiny over how those colleges used the Ontario Student Assistance Program.

Early this month, the Ministry of Colleges and Universities raised the alarm about six private career colleges.

The ministry said those schools were engaged in egregious use of taxpayer dollars, and it accused them of criminality by taking advantage of student aid.

Minister Nolan Quinn said an internal investigation found unusually high concentrations of aid applications sent from the same geographic locations.

He also pointed to immigration services firms tied directly to some of those colleges, or co-owned with them.

After sending the findings to KPMG for a deeper look, Quinn said the government reported the matter to the provincial police and the Auditor General.

The force confirmed it received a referral from the Ontario government and that the allegations are currently under review.

A spokesperson said no estimated timeline is available, and that no further detail or comment can be given while the review continues.

The Auditor General's office said it got a notification, not a request for a formal value-for-money audit.

It quoted Quinn's October sixth statement that the government notified the auditor last week and referred the matter to provincial police.

That notification, the office said, was separate from an audit request from Liberal member Tyler Watt.

A minister can request a special assignment. Why this government only notified the auditor, rather than asking for a performance audit of the kind Watt sought, is not clear in the reporting.

The auditor's office said it has been told about similar issues before, and that it expects to be informed because it is the independent auditor of Ontario's public accounts.

Two hundred forty-two career colleges in the province are eligible for this aid. Every one of them is now cut off while officials examine how wide the problem is.

Quinn has said the pause in grants stays until third-party auditors finish a wider probe.
