## Understanding the Issue: How competition regulators weigh broadband mergers when one firm is near collapse
Most coverage of telecom deals focuses on whether the buyer will gain too much market power, yet it often skips the legal test regulators actually apply when the target company is already failing. In the UK, the Competition and Markets Authority must compare the proposed merger against the realistic alternative—here, TalkTalk entering administration and losing its 2.5 million connections. That counterfactual matters because losing an entire provider can reduce choice and raise prices faster than a merger that keeps the customers on an existing network. The same logic has appeared in earlier UK cases where failing firms were allowed to combine rather than exit. Knowing this, when you hear claims that any increase in BT’s share is automatically harmful, you can check whether the regulator’s published reasoning shows the alternative would have been worse for customers. One concrete step is to read the CMA’s eventual decision document and note which scenario it treats as the baseline. Another useful check is to compare the number of wholesale customers affected against the number of retail connections that would disappear if TalkTalk collapsed. Regulators have previously accepted that preserving service continuity can outweigh concerns about concentration when the alternative is outright market exit. The CMA will therefore examine both the immediate impact on prices and the longer-term effect on infrastructure investment that supports critical national services.
