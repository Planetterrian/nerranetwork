To really understand how competition regulators weigh broadband mergers when one firm is
near-collapse, start with the legal test the UK's competition and market's authority
must apply.
The authority compares the proposed merger against the realistic alternative of talk-talk
entering administration and losing its 2.5 million connections.
That counterfactual matters because losing an entire provider can reduce choice and raise
prices faster than a merger that keeps the customers on an existing network.
The same logic has appeared in earlier UK cases where failing firms were allowed to combine
rather than exit.
When claims arise that any increase in BT's share is automatically harmful, check whether
the regulators published reasoning shows the alternative would have been worse for customers.
One concrete step is to read the CMA's eventual decision document and note which scenario it
treats as the baseline.
Another useful check is to compare the number of wholesale customers affected against
the number of retail connections that would disappear if talk-talk collapsed.
Regulators have previously accepted that preserving service continuity can outweigh concerns
