To really understand how major powers coordinate maritime strategies to secure energy routes, look at shared logistics costs, not at alliance slogans.

A common misunderstanding is that meetings of this kind are mainly military or geopolitical signalling.

The mechanism is more ordinary than that, and it starts with geography that does not overlap.

One partner can bring Arctic access; the other can bring an extensive port network.

By sharing data on safe corridors and coordinating patrols, they reduce duplication — two systems doing the same job at twice the cost.

That coordination lowers the cost of protecting tankers and bulk carriers that carry oil, gas, and minerals between the two economies and onward to other markets.

When sanctions or regional tensions raise insurance rates or force longer routes, joint planning can keep specific lanes open without each side building separate capacity.
