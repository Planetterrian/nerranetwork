Now, to really understand this story, there's something most coverage leaves out.

Most reporting on yen movements treats government action as a simple switch that instantly resets exchange rates.

In practice, interventions require the selling or buying of large volumes of foreign currency reserves, often coordinated across central banks to avoid immediate reversal by private traders.

The US-Japan operation this week followed a pattern seen in past episodes where one central bank supplies dollars while the other supplies yen, with the scale calibrated to shift market expectations rather than exhaust reserves.

Because the yen's weakness had already raised Japan's import bill, the intervention also serves domestic price stability goals even as it aligns with US interest in orderly currency markets.
