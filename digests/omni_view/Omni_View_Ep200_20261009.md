## Understanding the Issue: How US-China trade truces are negotiated through sequenced bilateral meetings
Most coverage of US-China trade talks treats each summit as a standalone event that either succeeds or fails on the day. In practice, the two sides maintain a rolling set of working groups and vice-ministerial channels that identify specific tariff lines and technology controls to pause or adjust before leaders meet. These lower-level talks produce draft texts that are then reviewed by economic and national-security teams in both capitals, creating a narrow window in which a truce extension can be locked in without reopening every dispute. The mechanism resembles the phased agreements reached in 2019–2020, when agriculture purchases and some tariff rollbacks were sequenced over months rather than announced at a single meeting. Knowing this, when you hear claims that one side “won” or “lost” a summit, you can check whether the reported outcome matches the list of items already advanced through the working groups or whether it simply restates positions that remain under discussion. The concrete thing to verify next time is whether any announced extension includes a published schedule for the next round of technical talks and which specific tariff headings are covered.
