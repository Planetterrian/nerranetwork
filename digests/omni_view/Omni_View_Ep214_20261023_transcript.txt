To really understand how a humanitarian corridor is arranged in an active war, look at the machinery
of a pause rather than the first public claim.
time. Most coverage of sudden ceasefire announcements treats them as straightforward diplomatic winds.
Yet the mechanics are more constrained. In practice, a corridor requires both sides to accept
a temporary pause at specific locations and times. That pause is often verified by a third
party that neither fully trusts. The arrangement usually begins with back-channel messages
through intermediaries rather than public statements, because each side fears appearing weak.
The historical examples from earlier wars show that even when terms are written down, violations
frequently occur within hours.
Field commanders retain discretion over enforcement, so a paper promise in a capital does not
by itself freeze every unit.
