## Understanding the Issue: How cross-border infrastructure projects between rivals actually advance
Most coverage treats the sudden activation of a long-idle bridge as simple diplomacy or sudden goodwill. In practice, such projects move forward only after both sides complete parallel technical and customs arrangements that are rarely announced together. The New Yalu River Bridge required synchronized changes to inspection protocols, currency settlement rules, and rail gauge compatibility on each side—steps that can take years even when political will exists. Historical parallels show the same pattern: the Friendship Bridge between North and South Korea remained unused for decades until rail and customs rules aligned in 2007. The New Yalu River Bridge was completed in 2014 yet required an additional twelve years before traffic signals and vehicle crossings were activated. Knowing this, when you hear claims that a single political meeting “unlocked” the crossing, you can check whether customs or transport ministries on both sides have published updated operating procedures. A useful next step is to look for the joint operating manual or tariff schedule that must be posted before commercial traffic can begin in volume. Observers can also track announcements from Xinhua and The Chosun Daily for matching details on inspection hours and permitted cargo types once the bridge begins regular use.

## Media-literacy note
Cross-check official statements against the actual operating documents released by transport and customs agencies rather than relying on ceremony coverage alone. Primary records from both countries’ ministries often reveal the practical constraints that political announcements omit.
