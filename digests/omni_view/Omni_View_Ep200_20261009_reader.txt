To really understand how US-China trade truces are negotiated through sequenced bilateral meetings, most coverage of US-China trade talks treats each summit as a standalone event that either succeeds or fails on the day.

In practice, the two sides maintain a rolling set of working groups and vice-ministerial channels that identify specific tariff lines and technology controls to pause or adjust before leaders meet.

These lower-level talks produce draft texts that are then reviewed by economic and national-security teams in both capitals, creating a narrow window in which a truce extension can be locked in without reopening every dispute.
