To really understand a diplomatic break, separate the person at the embassy from the paper that binds two states. People hear that an ambassador was sent home and picture a treaty ripped up. That mixes three acts, and this next stretch is my own explanation of the mechanism, not a finding from today’s reporting.

Under the Vienna practice most capitals still follow, a host country can declare a diplomat persona non grata, no longer welcome, and the sending country is expected to pull that person out. The embassy as an office can stay, sometimes under a lower-ranking stand-in.

Calling your own ambassador home is the matching move: you remove your representative without automatically shutting the building or stopping trade. Cancelling a named defence deal or a commercial contract is a third act, and it has to name the contract. Diplomats can also have been gone for months before any expulsion order, so the public rupture and the empty desk need not be the same day.

When you next see an expulsion lead the news, look for a separate list of cancelled agreements; if that list is missing, the legal relationship has not been described yet.
