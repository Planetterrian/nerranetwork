Now, to really understand the role of Federal Reserve chairs, most coverage treats the position as mainly about setting short-term interest rates.

In reality the position carries constitutional independence that lets one individual steer policy across multiple administrations without direct electoral oversight.

Successive chairs build credibility through consistent communication, which markets then price into everything from 30-year mortgages to corporate borrowing costs.

When that credibility holds, even small signals can calm or roil entire sectors.

When it erodes, the same tools lose effectiveness regardless of the underlying data.

Next time headlines emphasize one individual’s influence, ask what statutory term length and Senate confirmation process still govern that power, and which past decisions survived changes in elected leadership.
