To really understand how a consumer rule moves from a Brussels paragraph to a change on a shopping screen, start with the common mix-up that the Union's executive simply announces finished laws.

In practice a draft is published only after national regulators, consumer groups, and industry have been consulted, so the first public text is already the product of that argument, not the end of it.

The text then goes to two chambers, where elected members and national governments can amend it or block it, which is why a proposal can shrink, expand, or stall long after the first headlines.

That two-chamber process usually takes twelve to eighteen months and produces the final text that member states must turn into national law, and only after that turn does a shopper's screen have to change.
