## Understanding the Issue: How US House impeachment resolutions reach a floor vote
Most coverage of an impeachment filing treats the step as an automatic trigger for debate. In practice, the House Rules Committee and the majority leadership control whether a resolution is granted floor time. A single member can introduce a resolution, yet the Speaker or the Rules Committee must schedule it; without that step the measure stays in committee. The last three times a minority-party member filed an impeachment resolution against a cabinet official, the majority leadership declined to bring it to the floor. Knowing this, when you hear that a resolution “forces a vote,” you can check whether the majority has actually placed it on the calendar or referred it only to committee. One concrete thing to verify next time is whether the resolution number appears on the House floor schedule published by the Rules Committee. The process begins when any representative files the resolution with the clerk. It is then referred to the Judiciary Committee unless the Rules Committee intervenes to set terms for debate. Historical precedent shows that even resolutions with dozens of co-sponsors have remained in committee when leadership chose not to advance them. The constitutional requirement for a majority vote in the full House therefore depends first on procedural decisions made by the majority party. Checking the daily floor calendar and the Rules Committee website provides direct evidence of whether the measure has moved beyond introduction.
