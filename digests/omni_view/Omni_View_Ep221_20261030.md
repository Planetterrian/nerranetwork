## Understanding the Issue: How cryptocurrency exchanges manage large-scale thefts
Most coverage of major crypto hacks treats them as sudden technical failures, yet the underlying process involves a chain of custody decisions made long before any breach. Exchanges typically hold the majority of customer assets in offline “cold” wallets that require multiple human approvals to move funds, while keeping only a small fraction in online “hot” wallets for daily trading. When a theft occurs, the stolen tokens often move through mixers or cross-chain bridges that obscure ownership, forcing investigators to rely on blockchain analysis firms that track wallet addresses rather than real-world identities. North Korean-linked groups have repeatedly used the proceeds to purchase equipment and pay operatives, turning cyber theft into a steady revenue stream that bypasses traditional financial sanctions. In the Binance case, two analytics firms traced the activity to the TraderTraitor unit, illustrating how public ledgers allow post-incident mapping even when immediate recovery proves difficult. Knowing this mechanism lets readers distinguish between claims that an exchange “lost” funds and the reality that the assets remain visible on public ledgers even if recovery is difficult. When a report states that a particular unit carried out the attack, check whether the attribution rests on wallet clustering patterns published by the analytics firm or on broader intelligence assessments. The concrete detail to verify next time is whether the exchange has published an on-chain address for any recovered portion of the stolen assets.

## Media-literacy note
Cross-reference any single report of a large financial incident with at least one additional outlet from a different region before drawing conclusions about scale or responsibility. Primary documents such as exchange statements or regulatory filings often contain narrower claims than initial headlines suggest. Reading the methodology section of any attribution report reveals how much of the conclusion rests on publicly visible blockchain data versus private sources.
