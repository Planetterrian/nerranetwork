## Today's lead story (1)
### 1) Microsoft outage disrupts global services including UK hospitals and police
**What happened (neutral):** Microsoft reported early signs of stabilisation after a global outage that affected Teams, Outlook and other services for more than 12 hours, with no indication of a cyber-attack, according to The Guardian. The company extended the email timeout window to 48 hours while recovery efforts continued. The outage hit UK hospitals, GPs, schools and police forces, causing delays in prescriptions and cancelled meetings.

**Context & perspectives:** Microsoft said a portion of its infrastructure experienced elevated load that likely contributed to connectivity errors, and some servers became unexpectedly unavailable during automated checks. The company warned that restarted services could become unresponsive again once traffic returned. The UK government said it was working closely with Microsoft to assess impact on services while maintaining contingency plans.

## Understanding the Issue: How EU leaders coordinate on artificial intelligence policy
Many assume decisions on artificial intelligence rules in the European Union are made only by the European Commission without direct involvement from national heads of government. European Council President Antonio Costa wrote to leaders stating that a first dedicated discussion at leaders’ level is now needed because of the rapid pace of AI development. The summit took place in Dublin’s Convention Center, the same venue used for the European Political Community summit earlier in the week. French AI company Mistral’s CEO Arthur Mensch and Nobel-prize winner Geoffrey Hinton were scheduled to address the gathering. Estonia and Finland sent a letter ahead of the meeting calling for EU-level arrangements to respond to serious AI incidents while urging responsible governance. Mistral has argued against slowing development and pressed instead for Europe to advance its own capabilities. This direct leader-level format shows that national governments are engaging on both the economic opportunities and the risks of the technology, rather than leaving the file entirely to Brussels institutions. When reading future coverage, check which national letters or statements from heads of government are cited as shaping the summit outcomes.
