## Understanding the Issue: How Family Courts Assess Unregulated Expert Testimony
Most coverage of family-court decisions assumes that expert psychological reports carry formal professional oversight, yet many experts providing testimony operate without statutory regulation. In practice, courts rely on a mix of registered psychologists, counsellors and other practitioners whose qualifications are evaluated case-by-case rather than through a single licensing body. This structure developed historically to ensure timely access to specialist insight in urgent custody matters, but it leaves room for varying standards of training and methodology. When judges weigh these reports, they must balance the expert’s stated experience against the absence of mandatory peer review or continuing professional development requirements. Knowing this, readers can better evaluate claims that a particular outcome resulted from “expert advice” by asking whether the individual held recognised credentials or simply presented relevant experience. Next time you see reporting on a custody dispute involving psychological evidence, ask: what formal qualifications did the expert hold, was their methodology open to challenge, and what alternative sources of information were available to the court?
