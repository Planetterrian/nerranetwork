To really understand how a crossing between rival states finally carries traffic,
you have to look past the ceremony and at the parallel paperwork both sides must finish in private.
Most coverage treats a sudden activation as simple diplomacy or a burst of goodwill.
In practice, these projects move only after both sides complete technical and customs arrangements that are rarely announced together.
That usually means synchronized changes to inspection protocols, currency settlement rules,
and rail gauge compatibility on each bank of the border.
Those steps can take years even when political will already exists.
The friendship bridge between North and South Korea stayed unused for decades until rail
and customs rules aligned in 2007.
That is the same pattern, steel in place, traffic still forbidden, until the operating rules match.
When you hear that a single political meeting unlocked a crossing, the check is whether
customs or transport ministries on both sides have published updated operating procedures.
Ceremony coverage will not show you those constraints.
Primary records from the ministries often will.
Cross-check official statements against the actual operating documents released by transport
and customs agencies, not against the speeches alone.
The document worth looking for is a posted set of updated procedures from both ministries,
because that is what lets inspectors, banks, and rail operators work to the same clock.
