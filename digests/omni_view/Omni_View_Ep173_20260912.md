## Understanding the Issue: How BRICS coordinates positions among members with differing external ties
Most coverage of BRICS summits treats the group as a single bloc pushing back against Western institutions, yet the actual process works through consensus-based statements that avoid binding commitments on divisive issues. Each member brings its own set of bilateral relationships—India maintains close defense ties with the United States while also buying Russian oil, China pursues its own trade disputes with the U.S., and Brazil and South Africa focus on regional economic priorities—so formal declarations tend to emphasize broad goals such as expanded trade in local currencies rather than specific sanctions or military stances. This structure emerged from the group’s original focus on economic coordination among large emerging markets rather than security alignment. Knowing this helps readers see why a summit can produce a joint communiqué on development finance while remaining silent on the Ukraine war or the situation in the Middle East. When reports describe “BRICS unity” or “BRICS division,” the distinction often rests on whether the story highlights the agreed economic language or the topics left out of the final text. One concrete check is to compare the final summit declaration with the individual foreign-ministry readouts issued by each capital in the following days.
