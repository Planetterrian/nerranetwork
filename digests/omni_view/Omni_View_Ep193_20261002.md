## Major world stories (3)
### 2) Kenya High Court blocks removal process for seven Supreme Court judges
**What happened (neutral):** Kenya’s High Court ruled that the Judicial Service Commission failed to conduct a required preliminary assessment before issuing notices to seven Supreme Court judges facing removal complaints. The three-judge bench declared the commission’s communications unconstitutional and barred further steps on the same complaints. The decision affects complaints targeting Chief Justice Martha Koome, Deputy Chief Justice Philomena Mwilu and Justices Mohammed Ibrahim, Smokin Wanjala, Njoki Ndung’u, Isaac Lenaola and William Ouko.

**Context & perspectives:** AllAfrica’s Capital FM report details how the commission had required the judges, including Chief Justice Martha Koome, to respond within 21 days without first confirming that the petitions met constitutional grounds for removal. The judges had argued that such an appraisal is necessary to avoid frivolous proceedings. The court also found the process violated the judges’ right to fair administrative action under Article 47 of the Constitution. The decision affects complaints filed by former Law Society of Kenya president Nelson Havi and others. The notices issued on January 27, 2025, were found wanting because they largely communicated the complaints without sufficiently setting out the factual allegations and the specific constitutional grounds on which removal was being sought.

## Understanding the Issue: How media mergers preserve or alter studio brands
Most coverage of studio mergers assumes the acquiring company will simply absorb and rebrand the targets. In practice, the legal and commercial structure often keeps the acquired studios operating under separate production labels while the parent company handles financing, distribution, and corporate reporting. The Paramount and Warner Bros. deal illustrates this pattern: Ellison explicitly stated the new Skydance name gives the parent an identity without diminishing the historic studio names, allowing each to retain creative autonomy and brand recognition. This arrangement mirrors earlier consolidations in which legacy labels continued to market films independently even after ownership changed. The 35-film release slate planned for next year will still carry the individual studio names rather than a single merged banner. Knowing this distinction helps readers evaluate whether a merger announcement signals creative continuity or a deeper shift in decision-making power. When the next merger headline appears, check whether the reporting specifies separate production slates and brand licensing terms rather than focusing only on the parent company’s new name. The ticker symbol change to “SKYD” affects only investor-facing reporting and does not alter how individual films are marketed to audiences.
