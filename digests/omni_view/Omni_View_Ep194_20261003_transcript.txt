To really understand how proportional elections shape coalitions in small states begin with the seat allocation rule itself.
Seats are distributed roughly in line with each party's share of the total vote across 100 positions rather than awarding all seats to the largest vote getter.
This means even a party that finishes first usually falls short of the 51 seats needed for a majority on its own.
Coalition formation then requires negotiations that trade policy concessions and cabinet posts
among several groups.
The same pattern has appeared in other small European parliaments, where no single block
dominates, producing governments that reflect compromises rather than outright victories.
