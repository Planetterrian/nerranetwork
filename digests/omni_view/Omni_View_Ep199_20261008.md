## Today's lead story (1)
### 1) TSMC reports sharp rise in September revenue on AI chip demand
**What happened (neutral):** Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co recorded September net revenue of 511.86 billion New Taiwan dollars, a 54.6 percent increase from the same month a year earlier. The company, the world's largest contract chipmaker, supplies advanced chips to clients including Nvidia and Apple. Revenue for the third quarter reached approximately 1.49 trillion New Taiwan dollars.

**Context & perspectives:** TSMC has become a central supplier in the global semiconductor chain because it produces the most advanced chips used in artificial intelligence systems. The company also announced it will adopt ASML's High NA extreme ultraviolet lithography machines, a step that enables production of even smaller and more powerful chips. Coverage from CNBC frames the results as evidence that demand for AI hardware remains strong even as foundation models improve and pricing pressure grows. The report notes that sales did not set a new monthly record, correcting an earlier description.

## Understanding the Issue: How advanced chip production became concentrated in one company
Most coverage of rising AI chip sales treats the numbers as simple proof of demand growth. In reality, the vast majority of the world's most advanced semiconductors are made by a single company on one island because building and operating the necessary fabrication plants requires decades of accumulated expertise, enormous capital, and stable access to specialised equipment that few places have replicated at scale. Taiwan's government supported this industry through consistent policy, education investment, and infrastructure long before AI became prominent, creating a position that later market demand simply amplified. Other countries have tried to build similar capacity but face higher costs, longer timelines, and gaps in the supplier ecosystem that TSMC already solved. Knowing this, when reports highlight a single month's sales increase, readers can assess whether the story is mainly about short-term demand or about the structural risks that come from depending on one geographic point for critical components. A useful check is to ask which specific equipment or process steps remain difficult to duplicate elsewhere and how quickly new facilities outside Taiwan are actually coming online.
