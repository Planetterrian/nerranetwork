To really understand a climate draft that names or erases fossil fuels, you have to separate the electricity socket from the whole energy system. Deutsche Welle's account of whether the Paris limit can still be held is the page for that split. In twenty fifteen, governments in Paris agreed to do what they could to stop the world overheating.

Since then coal, oil and gas have fallen to a record-low share of primary energy, meaning all energy supplied, not only electricity, and they still make up around eighty-six percent of that supply. Mark Maslin, an earth-system scientist at University College London, told Deutsche Welle we are still burning stuff like the nineteenth century. Electricity moved faster.

Wind, solar and hydropower were around thirty-four percent of global electricity in twenty twenty-five. An Ember analysis found record solar growth last year let clean energy meet all new electricity demand, which held back new fossil generation. Maslin put investment in new renewable projects at about two trillion dollars, twice the money going into fossil fuels.

The United Nations now says overshooting the Paris temperature threshold is inevitable, and that the time above it should be as short as possible, with heating brought back down by the end of the century, because a longer overshoot raises the chance of self-reinforcing shifts such as large forest die-back and melting permafrost.

Next time a summit line says phaseout or stays silent, ask whether the number beside it is primary energy or electricity.
