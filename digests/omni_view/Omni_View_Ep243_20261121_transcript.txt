Most European Union countries, plus a few neighbors, dropped routine checks on the borders
between them and kept checks on the outside edge.
A short stay visa is usually issued by one state but written for the whole area, because
the point of abolishing internal controls was that a visitor cleared once should not be
stopped at every next frontier.
The issuing country does the paperwork and is supposed to be the main destination, yet
the document is not that country's private pass.
Any state on the external border can still refuse entry if the traveler fails the common
conditions.
Purpose, funds, a return ticket, security flags, even with a visa in the passport.
That is a refusal at the door, not a rewrite of the visa's map.
Changing the map so that a visa issued in one capital works only there is a change to
a shared instrument. It needs the states that issue the common visa to agree. Because
a government that cannot print a Europe-wide visa by itself also cannot alone, unprint
the Europe-wide visas other capitals have already issued. Next time a government says
it will confine that visa to one country, check whether the text is an instruction to
its own consulates or a proposal the other issuing states still have to adopt.
