Iraqi officials have described the process as the end of a foreign military footprint that began after 2003, while warning that the vacuum could also allow groups such as Islamic State to stage a comeback.

## Understanding the Issue: How troop withdrawal timelines are set and executed
Most coverage of a military exit focuses on the final date, yet the actual process begins years earlier with bilateral agreements that fix both the schedule and the handover of facilities. In the Iraq case the 2024 framework set the sequence of base closures and equipment transfers, giving each side predictable steps rather than an abrupt departure. Those agreements also include clauses on residual training missions or intelligence sharing that can continue after combat units leave. Knowing this, when reports emphasise a single “withdrawal day,” readers can check whether the underlying pact still allows limited cooperation or whether every function ends at once. The same structure appears in earlier US drawdowns from other countries, where the calendar was adjusted only after new security guarantees were negotiated. One concrete check is to look for the text of the original status-of-forces agreement and any later amendments that list the exact bases and dates involved. Another is to compare the original timeline with any public updates issued by the governments involved. Readers can also examine whether equipment transfers or training programmes are mentioned separately from the combat-unit exit. This approach reveals whether the withdrawal is truly complete or whether narrower forms of cooperation persist under the same legal umbrella.
