To really understand how index changes affect markets, consider the mechanical side of these additions.

When S&P Global adds a firm to the S&P 500, index-tracking funds and E T F's must buy the shares to match their benchmarks, creating predictable demand regardless of the company's fundamentals.

This mechanical buying can lift the stock price in the short term even if underlying business prospects remain unchanged.

Knowing this, when you hear claims that index inclusion signals lasting success, you can evaluate whether the move reflects genuine growth or simply forces passive capital to follow the rules.

Next time you see a headline about a company joining a major index, ask who is required to buy the shares, what is the size of that forced demand relative to daily trading volume, and how long such effects typically last.
