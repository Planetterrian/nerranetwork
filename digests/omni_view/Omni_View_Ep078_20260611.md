## Understanding the Issue: How US presidents authorize military action abroad
Most coverage of US strikes on Iran assumes each new round of bombing requires fresh congressional approval, yet the actual process often rests on older authorizations and executive interpretations of Article II powers. Presidents since the 1970s have relied on the 2001 and 2002 Authorizations for Use of Military Force, plus claims of inherent commander-in-chief authority, to conduct operations without new votes. This structure means limited strikes can continue for weeks or months while Congress receives only after-the-fact briefings. Knowing this, when you hear claims that “Congress must approve any further action,” you can check whether the administration is citing an existing statute or asserting unilateral power. Next time you see a headline about escalation, ask: which specific legal text is being invoked, what reporting requirements apply, and how previous administrations used the same authorities.
