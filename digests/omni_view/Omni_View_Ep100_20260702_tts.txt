Now, to really understand the Google ruling that opened today's episode, there is something most coverage leaves out.

Most reports assume the European Court of Justice simply re-examines whether a company broke the rules.

In practice the court reviews whether the Commission followed its own procedures, applied the correct legal tests, and set a proportionate penalty.

It rarely re-tries the underlying facts.

This structure means companies can succeed on narrow procedural grounds even when the core finding of dominance stands.

Knowing this distinction helps listeners separate headlines about Google winning from the narrower legal victories that sometimes occur.

Next time you see a report on a fine being upheld or reduced, ask which part of the Commission's reasoning was actually challenged and what precedent the ruling sets for future cases involving similar market power.
