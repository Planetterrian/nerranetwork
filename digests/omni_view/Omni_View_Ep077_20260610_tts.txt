Now, to really understand this story, there is something most coverage leaves out.

Most reports treat primary results as simple popularity contests, yet the actual mechanism involves state-by-state rules that allocate delegates rather than a single national vote.

Each state sets its own date, voting method, and delegate-allocation formula, which can be winner-take-all or proportional.

Candidates accumulate delegates across these separate contests until one reaches the required majority at the party convention.

This structure means early states exert disproportionate influence because momentum and media attention compound after the first few results.

Next time you see a headline about primary night, ask which state’s rules determined the delegate split, how many delegates are still unallocated, and what historical precedent exists for comebacks after early losses.
