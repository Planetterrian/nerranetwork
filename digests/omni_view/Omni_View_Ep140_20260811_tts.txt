Now, to really understand this story, there is something most coverage leaves out.

Most reporting on the Strait of Hormuz focuses on the simple fact of closure, yet the deeper mechanism is how a single narrow waterway concentrates leverage for both the coastal state and outside powers.

The strait sits between Iran and Oman.

Any vessel must pass through waters that can be monitored or restricted from shore-based systems, giving the coastal state physical options that wider oceans do not.

When a blockade is imposed, the immediate effect is rerouting of tankers around Africa, which raises shipping costs and delivery times for buyers in Asia and Europe.

Historical precedent shows that the last sustained closure, in the nineteen eighties, produced exactly these longer voyages and higher insurance rates without halting all trade.
