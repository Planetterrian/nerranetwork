To really understand a return order after a genocide finding, separate what a court can command from what a host country owes. A judgment can say one state’s forces committed genocide and can order that state to let people come back safely, by choice, and with dignity. That duty lands on the state that was sued. It does not, by itself, require every other capital to house those people without end.

A second rule lives in the refugee treaty: members generally may not push someone back into serious harm. A state that never joined does not carry that treaty bar in the same way, even when its ministers borrow the words safe and voluntary. Voluntary means the person decides, not that two governments publish a sailing date.

Safe and dignified are conditions on the receiving side, papers and no punishment for having left, not a label on a manifest. One report can truly say a flight matches a court’s adjectives while another truly says people are moving toward a country a court just found responsible.

Next time, ignore the adjective until you can find a record that the passengers agreed and the name of someone independent who will see them after they land.
