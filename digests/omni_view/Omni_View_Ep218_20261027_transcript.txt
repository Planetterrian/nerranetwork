To really understand how a party-list vote becomes a government, you have to look past
the night's projection and into the bargaining that starts once the count is certified.
The common misunderstanding is that the seat tally itself decides who won.
The system allocates seats strictly by party list vote share.
You vote for a party slate, not a local candidate, and seats follow that share.
A low threshold means even small parties can enter the kineset and hold leverage in coalition
talks.
The structure rewards parties that can deliver narrow but reliable blocks of seats to larger
alliances.
Historical patterns show that prime ministers have repeatedly traded policy concessions
on settlements, religious issues, or budgets to secure those seats.
The same low threshold rule has produced multi-party coalitions in every recent caness it.
That makes the post-election negotiation phase the true test of any reported lead, not
the first projection on television.
