To really understand how prisoner releases function inside political transitions, it helps to see them as sequenced bargaining steps rather than isolated humanitarian gestures.

In these settings each side typically trades names on verified lists for movement on sanctions relief or election timelines, with third parties often holding the lists to prevent either faction from claiming sole credit.

The pattern from earlier cases shows that releases without matching security guarantees tend to stall when one party faces domestic pushback.

External mediators usually require independent monitoring of names and conditions so that selective releases cannot tilt the balance toward one side.

Releases also tend to link directly to parallel steps such as easing asset freezes or scheduling electoral reviews, forming a chain of reciprocal actions.
