Now, to really understand the pension stories, there is something most coverage leaves out.

Governments first receive updated life-expectancy and workforce-participation data from national statistics offices, then feed those figures into long-term budget forecasts produced by independent watchdogs such as the Office for Budget Responsibility.

Once a new age threshold is set, legislation typically introduces equalisation periods so people within a few years of retirement face smaller step-ups while younger cohorts absorb larger ones.

Knowing this sequence shows why announcements about 2037 to 2039 implementation matter immediately to people in their late forties.

Next time you see a headline about pension reform, ask which birth cohorts are explicitly covered by the change, what independent body verified the demographic assumptions, and how much notice the affected workers received before the rules were locked in.
