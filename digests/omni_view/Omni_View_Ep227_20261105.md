## Understanding the Issue: How EU accession talks shape domestic police reform
Most coverage of police arrests in candidate countries treats them as simple law-and-order news. In reality, the process ties concrete institutional changes to the opening and closing of negotiation chapters on justice and home affairs. Serbia’s current talks remain frozen on several rule-of-law benchmarks; each chapter requires verifiable progress on independent oversight, training standards and prosecution of misconduct before the EU moves to the next stage. When a new government signals willingness to meet those benchmarks, Brussels can unlock funding and political support that previous administrations forfeited. The arrests announced this week therefore function as both a domestic signal and a diplomatic test: if prosecutors pursue the cases through transparent procedures, the EU can cite measurable improvement when deciding whether to resume formal talks.
