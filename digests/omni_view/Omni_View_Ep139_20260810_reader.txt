Now, to really understand this story, there’s something most coverage leaves out.

Most coverage treats a new central-bank governor as a simple personnel swap whose views will quickly dominate.

In practice, many central banks operate under multi-year mandates, staggered board terms and statutory inflation targets that limit how fast any single appointee can shift direction.

Indonesia’s central bank, for example, sets policy through a board whose members serve overlapping terms, so a new chair must still secure majority support for rate changes.

Historical cases in other emerging markets show that when a sole candidate is named, markets often price in smoother continuity rather than abrupt reversal.

Knowing this, when reports emphasize the new leader’s past statements, readers can check whether the bank’s legal framework actually allows rapid policy swings or whether the board structure and inflation target remain the stronger constraints.

One concrete step is to review the central bank’s published meeting calendar and voting records for the first two quarters after the appointment takes effect.
