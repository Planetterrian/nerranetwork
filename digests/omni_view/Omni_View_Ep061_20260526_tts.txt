Now, to really understand this story, there is something most coverage leaves out.

Most coverage of exam leaks treats them as simple failures of security, yet the real mechanism involves layers of paper handling, printing, transport, and local administration that create multiple points of vulnerability.

In systems like India’s NEET, question papers must be produced under strict controls, sealed, and distributed to thousands of centres, each requiring trusted personnel who may face intense local pressure or financial incentives to breach protocols.

Historical parallels in other large countries show that even modest leaks at a single printing facility or transport leg can compromise an entire cohort when digital backups or alternate versions are not immediately available.

Knowing this, when you hear claims that the system worked after a cancellation, you can evaluate whether the response addressed root distribution weaknesses or merely postponed the next sitting.

Next time you see a headline about exam integrity, ask where exactly the chain broke, who controlled each segment, and what independent verification exists beyond the organising body’s own assurances.
