To really understand the European Union budget process, begin with the constitutional requirement for unanimous approval among all member states.

The Multiannual Financial Framework sets spending ceilings for seven years and must be agreed before the period begins.

Failure to reach consensus by the deadline can trigger automatic extensions of the prior framework at reduced real terms.

Negotiations begin with a European Commission proposal that balances net contributors’ demands for restraint against net recipients’ claims for cohesion and agricultural funds.

Because every country holds a veto, deals typically emerge only after side payments, revised spending headings, and last minute summit bargaining that can stretch across multiple European Council meetings.
