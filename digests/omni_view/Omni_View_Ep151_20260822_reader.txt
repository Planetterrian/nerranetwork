Now, to really understand this story, there’s something most coverage leaves out.

Most coverage treats tariff announcements as sudden decisions.

Yet the process normally follows a set sequence of formal notifications, consultation periods, and last-minute adjustments.

In practice, one side tables a list of disputed practices.

The other responds with proposed remedies.

Talks extend when either side introduces new demands close to the deadline.

The United States and Canada have followed this pattern in past disputes.

An initial round of tariffs is often paired with an offer to resume talks once the measures take effect.

Historical examples show that duties announced on integrated supply chains frequently trigger immediate retaliation pledges.

Those pledges are often followed by quiet technical working groups.

Knowing this sequence helps distinguish between a genuine breakdown and a negotiating tactic.
