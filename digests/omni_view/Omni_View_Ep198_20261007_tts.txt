To really understand how a public condemnation works as a diplomatic signal, look at the sequence around it rather than the heat of the language.

A common misunderstanding is to treat every broadcast warning as a simple threat, as if volume equaled an imminent military move.

In this system, state media names a specific foreign action — an arms transfer is the usual example — and, in the same breath, highlights recent meetings between leaders.
