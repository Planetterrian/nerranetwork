**What happened (neutral):** The Parti Quebecois secured the most seats in Monday’s provincial election but fell short of a majority in the 127-seat National Assembly. ## Understanding the Issue: How minority governments pursue sovereignty referendums in Canada
Most coverage treats a separatist election win as an automatic path to a referendum, yet Canada’s parliamentary rules require the winning party to secure support from other legislators for major constitutional steps. In practice, a minority government must negotiate confidence-and-supply agreements or risk losing power before any referendum bill can pass the provincial assembly. Historical precedent shows that even majority separatist governments needed federal cooperation and court rulings to define the terms of a vote, as occurred after the 1995 referendum. Knowing this, when reports describe the Parti Quebecois victory as a direct mandate for independence, readers can check whether the party has actually secured cross-party backing for legislation rather than assuming the result alone triggers the next vote. One concrete check is whether the new government tables a formal referendum bill within its first legislative session and whether opposition parties indicate willingness to support it.
