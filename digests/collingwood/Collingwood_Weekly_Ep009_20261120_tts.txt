Grey County paramedics are being recognized for a twenty twenty-six ice rescue in Owen Sound, Bayshore Broadcasting reported from last week's council meeting. Grey Bruce OPP Staff Sergeant Henry Thalen praised the people involved, and on behalf of the OPP each Grey County paramedic who responded got a letter of commendation.

Kyle Stewart, wellness and support manager for Grey County Paramedic Services, told council that on March eighth a fast, life-threatening emergency opened up on Georgian Bay. Two large sections of ice broke from shore and carried twenty-three ice fishers about two kilometres, in two different directions, out into the bay. The ice kept fracturing as conditions got worse.

Paramedics, fire services, the OPP, Ornge, dispatch and other partners answered together. Stewart said that coordination brought all twenty-three safely to shore. Some patients had minor injuries and hypothermia. All of them recovered.

He called it an extraordinary, highly complex incident that pulled emergency partners from across the region, and said he had been there for part of the response and saw the professionalism, the teamwork and the commitment. He told council the morning shows how fast conditions change, and how much coordinated response matters for communities on the bay.

CTV News described the same operation as Canada's largest ice rescue.
