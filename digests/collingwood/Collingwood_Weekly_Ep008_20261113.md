> **More than four hundred fifty thousand dollars a year rides on Collingwood taking Oro leachate.**

**What You Need to Know:** Simcoe County is asking Collingwood to keep taking Oro Landfill leachate for at least another year, because staff say walking away adds more than $450,000 a year in hauling and treatment. Collingwood council voted unanimously in July to end that arrangement on about 60 days' notice. County council has backed a second letter asking the town to extend it.

### The Week's Stories
**County seeks extension from Collingwood to accept Oro Landfill leachate: CollingwoodToday**
Simcoe County wants the Town of Collingwood to keep accepting leachate from the Oro Landfill for at least one more year. Staff say the replacement arrangement, a site outside the county, is expected to raise annual hauling and treatment costs by more than $450,000, add transportation-related environmental impacts, and cut revenue the town had been receiving. Collingwood council voted unanimously in July to give two months' notice that it would no longer run Oro-Medonte landfill leachate through the town's wastewater treatment plant. Rob Elliott, the county's commissioner of engineering, planning and environment, told county council on Tuesday that staff understand Collingwood not wanting to be the only receiver, and that "that just hasn't given us the runway to find alternative homes." The county runs six landfill sites with leachate collection. Five have long been hauled to the Collingwood landfill under an agreement and a force-main; Oro's leachate had gone to Barrie until Barrie could no longer treat it, after which it was trucked to Collingwood under an agreement allowing up to 13 litres per second into the force-main. Elliott said few plants are sized or approved for this load, and that capital work or Ministry of the Environment approvals need at least a year. Collingwood Deputy Mayor Tim Fryer said he strongly supports sending the letter again as soon as possible and hopes the next council can repair the partnership. Elliott said capped, closing landfills should mean less leachate, and less contamination in it, over time. Council supported a second letter asking for at least one year. **Wasaga man's murder trial delayed until February 2028: CollingwoodToday**
The trial of David Zamida, 49, also called Michael Zamida in some court documents, is now set for Feb. 14, 2028, before Superior Court Justice Vanessa Christie and a jury in Barrie. He is charged with second-degree murder in the 2024 death of Peter McKinven and remains in custody. The trial had been ready to start; the Crown asked for a delay, citing unforeseen circumstances. Defence counsel's schedule and Christie's limited dates left the earliest date both sides could take about 15 months out. The case will come back periodically in case earlier time opens. The trial is expected to last three weeks. McKinven, 43, was found critically injured near the accused's Homewood Avenue home in Wasaga Beach on Feb. 25, 2024, and died about 10 weeks later. The charge began as aggravated assault and was upgraded after his death. Zamida was also charged separately with weapons and drug offences after Huronia West OPP executed a search warrant about 10 days later; officers say they seized guns and drugs at the Wasaga Beach home in March 2024. His counsel has not ruled out a bail review before trial. ### The Closer Look
**Abuse and Neglect Reports Rose at Grey County Long-Term Care Homes Over the Past Year: The Owen Sound Current**
Grey County's three long-term care homes filed 18 critical incident reports with the province between August 15 and October 14, according to a report to the county's Long-Term Care Committee of Management, as covered by The Owen Sound Current. Reports of improper care rose from four to 15 in a year at Grey Gables in Markdale, Lee Manor in Owen Sound and Rockwood Terrace in Durham. The figures are one combined total. The report does not assign each filing to a home, and it does not describe any incident.

Five of the 18 reports sit in abuse and neglect. That category covers any alleged, suspected or witnessed abuse of a resident by anyone, or neglect by the home or its staff. Two involve improper or incompetent treatment or care that resulted in harm or a risk of harm. Four residents went to hospital because of an injury. Two medication incidents sent a resident to hospital. One controlled substance was reported missing or unaccounted for. The homes also reported four disease outbreaks. Medication incidents, in this report, are those in which a resident was taken to hospital.

The committee received the report for information on November 10. Staff did not present the incident table, and no member asked about it. The report says the incidents "have been reviewed and internally investigated" and that "Action plans have been implemented along with providing additional training and education as required."

Ministry of Long-Term Care inspectors visited all three homes in September and issued seven written notices. A written notice records a finding that a home did not comply with a requirement. Lee Manor drew three notices after a critical incident inspection from September 15 to 24, tied to duty to report, general requirements, and altercations between residents. Grey Gables drew two notices after a critical incident inspection on September 23, 24 and 28, tied to reporting requirements and to continence and bowel management. Rockwood Terrace drew two notices related to food production, after a proactive compliance inspection. A separate check of that home's generator readiness found no problems. In each case the report says staff took follow-up action and "the issues have been addressed."

Teri Fischer, executive director of Rockwood Terrace, told the committee that inspectors gave the homes positive feedback that does not appear in the public reports. At Rockwood Terrace, she said, that included continence care, falls prevention and pain management. The Ministry of Labour inspected Lee Manor on October 14 as a follow-up to a critical injury and issued no orders. The ministry posts inspection reports for Lee Manor, Grey Gables and Rockwood Terrace online. Source: [owensoundcurrent.com](https://www.owensoundcurrent.com/p/abuse-and-neglect-reports-rose-at)

### Both Sides: Grey County nuisance-wildlife pay
Grey County's updated nuisance-wildlife rules are what five local trappers want reopened, and what county staff say provincial law now requires. Council passed updated bylaws in August. A September 18 letter from Byron Schwass, Brian Aitken, Tom Eastman, Dave McAleese and Bob Kranenburg was on the November 12 committee of the whole agenda. Payments double on January 1, 2027, from $25 to $50 per beaver; coyote and wolf payments go to $100, then rise with the consumer price index. Staff said those rates had not changed in more than 20 years.

County staff, at a November 9 information session, said the province no longer lets the county pay bounties for beavers, coyotes or wolves, which is why a claim now needs county authorization before any hunting or trapping begins. A staff member said, "The ministry was very clear that the county can no longer pay bounties for coyotes or beavers or wolves." The landowner and trapper file a form; the county then sets a six-week window. Staff said approval can take up to 10 days but is usually faster. The county no longer pays for animals taken on public land. Road-allowance work must be authorized and paid by the road authority, and staff said those authorities remain responsible for hazards in the allowance. The Owen Sound Current has not confirmed with the ministry what the ministry told the county. Staff said they could not speak for Bruce County, which trappers noted does not require the same pre-approval.

The five trappers welcome the higher rate. They argue, in the letter also reported by CKNX, that "The work is time-consuming and carries real operating costs," and that "Going from a one page form to five pages needs to be revisited." They say losing county pay on public land can make road calls unviable on municipal payments alone, reject the idea that pay from both a municipality and the county is improper double compensation, and warn the new rules could discourage responses to nuisance beavers on municipal roads and drainage. They want the changes revisited with experienced local trappers. The doubled rates are set for January 1, 2027.
