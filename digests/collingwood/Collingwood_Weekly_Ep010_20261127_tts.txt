CollingwoodToday reports the Simcoe County District School Board put that request in front of trustees on November 25. Chief executive Dawn Stephens said the Ministry of Education opened the capital call on November 13 and wants the business cases by January 5.

First in line is a replacement for Collingwood Collegiate Institute, then an addition at Huronia Centennial Elementary in Elmvale, then a new secondary school in Bradford West Gwillimbury. Past calls have typically held five hundred million dollars for the whole province, so this package is a large slice of a usual pot.

Orillia, Severn and Ramara trustee Jodi Lloyd told the meeting the board is excited and also realistic that it may get none of the three. She called it a big ask, and said the result will depend on how good the competition is.

Superintendent Corry Van Nispen said in October these three were picked because talks with the ministry suggested they could draw funding, and that the ministry still decides. The province prefers projects that are shovel-ready, on land the board already owns, with a finished design, and preferably a design copied from a school already built.

The Collegiate plan is two storeys and would hold one thousand one hundred thirty-four students. The board will not release that design before the province says yes. There has been interest in sharing the build with other agencies. The expected bill for that school alone is eighty-three million dollars.

The building on Hurontario Street went up in the nineteen-fifties and is the oldest board high school that has not already been approved for a rebuild. An inspection in December 2020 found fifty-seven per cent of its components at the end of their life. Fixing those components is put at twenty-one million dollars, and the board calls the building prohibitive to repair. A site has been selected.

Communications manager Sarah Kekewich said in October the board would be better positioned to say more after the plan goes forward. The board did not say whether that site is newly bought land or the ground the school already sits on. In Elmvale the ask is a single-storey addition, renovations and heavy site work.

An architect has that design about three-quarters done, and site-plan talks with the Township of Springwater are still open. Simcoe County's public board is back asking for student food money, and CollingwoodToday has the November 25 debate.

Chair Brandy Rafeek, trustee for Adjala-Tosorontio, Clearview, Canadian Forces Base Borden and Essa, told trustees that a commitment last year from the Ministry of Children, Community and Social Services and from Peterborough Child and Family Services is not turning out as the board hoped.

Trustees approved a new letter from Rafeek to both agencies, written for the board, saying again that the programs need more dollars. She also sits with the local Eat Well to Excel chapter. Before the board pushed, the ministry had not raised province-wide student food dollars since 2014, and newly opened schools sat on a wait-list with none of that funding.

Last year the ministry said it would pull new schools off that list using money from the federal one-billion-dollar National School Food Program, and it promised nutrition funding for new schools would continue. At the latest Eat Well to Excel meeting, Rafeek said, the chapter heard the opposite. Schools being built now would get no student nutrition dollars and would go onto a new wait-list.

She said Peterborough Child and Family Services was told around this time last year to clear every school off the old waiting list, and did, but no instruction for the future came with that order. She called it a hard decision for them. Fifteen new schools are coming to Simcoe County within the next few years. She said the pot itself has not grown. It has only been spread across more schools.

Trustee Jodi Lloyd pointed to advocacy from the Ontario Public School Board Association and to those federal dollars. She said that even though more money has been announced, and the intentions are well-meaning, there are still not sufficient funds, and that Simcoe County has significant challenges.

Public Health Ontario figures show twenty-six per cent of Simcoe County households experienced food insecurity in 2023-24, above the provincial average of twenty-four per cent. The Simcoe County Greenbelt Coalition says one in six children and youth in the county live with food insecurity.

Earlier this year trustees heard that about seventeen thousand of the board's fifty-seven thousand students use the programs, across one hundred five schools. Eat Well to Excel is the non-profit partnership that has backed volunteer breakfast, lunch and snack programs in every Simcoe County school for more than twenty years.

Around the table are the public board, the Simcoe Muskoka District Health Unit, United Way Simcoe Muskoka, the Simcoe Muskoka Catholic District School Board, the Sharing Place Food Centre, the Karma Project and Peterborough Child and Family Services. Provincial base funding moves through Peterborough Child and Family Services.

The ministry gave the public board seven hundred ninety thousand seven hundred seventeen dollars in the 2024-25 school year. The board estimates ministry and organizational money covers ten to fifteen per cent of what each school actually spends to run the program. Fundraising by the school and the community covers the rest, so one school can look nothing like the next.
