### The Week's Stories
**Collingwood endorses climate change adaptation plan: Ontario Construction News**
Council endorsed a Climate Change Adaptation Action Plan with 19 goals for heavier rainfall, flooding, extreme heat, shoreline erosion and warmer winters. The plan is organized around People and Community, Services and Operations, and Natural Environment, and is meant to guide planning and spending. Mayor Hamlin said in a statement, "Adaptation is about practical planning for weather conditions we are already seeing in Collingwood," and that preparing early protects residents, properties and services and can reduce emergency-repair costs. Local climate data, technical analysis, staff and regional partners, and community feedback shaped the risks and the measures. Endorsement moves the plan into implementation through work plans, budgets and asset management, with each investment still subject to the town's regular budget process. The plan also calls for stronger emergency planning and for information residents can use before extreme weather; the town encourages household emergency plans, clear drainage and eavestroughs, and checking on neighbours. **Arson charge laid in Wasaga Beach motel fire: Barrie 360**
Ontario Provincial Police have charged a person in connection with a fire at the Villa Nova Motel on Mosley Street in Wasaga Beach. Crews responded shortly before midnight on March 9. Everyone inside evacuated safely, and no injuries were reported. This week police arrested a 58-year-old Wasaga Beach resident and charged them with arson, disregard for human life, and failing to comply with an undertaking. The accused has a court date on October 27. **Name a Grey County Snowplow: Submissions Open Until Oct. 22: The Owen Sound Current**
Grey County is taking snowplow names through October 22 at 11:59 p.m. on ConnectGrey.ca, and the contest is the first project on that new engagement site. Each person may submit up to three names. The county will publish top suggestions, public voting runs October 23 to November 6, and winning names will be announced November 10 for plows in each of four patrol areas. The four top entries win prize packs. Only entrants with a registered ConnectGrey account and an email are eligible for credit and prizes, and if more than one person submits the same name, only the first is eligible. Names must suit all ages; puns about winter, snow, plowing or the area are encouraged. Source: [owensoundcurrent.com](https://www.owensoundcurrent.com/p/name-a-grey-county-snowplow-submissions)

### The Closer Look
**Grey County, Owen Sound Face Uncertain Infrastructure Funding as Five-Year Boost Ends: The Owen Sound Current**
Grey County and Owen Sound received $7.5 million in 2026 from a provincial fund for roads, bridges and water systems whose five-year doubling expires this year. On October 8, Grey County's committee of the whole voted to support a June 25 letter from the Municipal Finance Officers' Association of Ontario to Acting Infrastructure Minister Todd McCarthy. The letter asks the province to make the doubled Ontario Community Infrastructure Fund permanent at $400 million a year before it calculates 2027 allocations. The province doubled the fund from about $200 million to $400 million a year beginning with 2022 allocations. The resolution notes no public commitment to keep that level beyond 2026.

Grey County received $1,279,946 in 2021, the last year before the doubling, $2,645,238 in 2022, and $3,836,916 in 2026. Owen Sound's allocation grew from about $1.1 million in 2021 to $3,647,845 in 2026. The county's deputy treasurer told the committee the fund is a fixed $400-million pool reweighted every year on the condition and replacement value of assets in each asset management plan. Grey County's larger share, the deputy treasurer said, has effectively come out of other municipalities' allocations, because the province has not increased the total over the five years. The province announced 2026 allocations in September 2025.

At the Association of Municipalities of Ontario conference in August, McCarthy was asked about continuing the doubled fund. He pointed to investments already made, including a new stream for municipalities that do not charge development charges, and did not commit to $400 million, according to a report from the Association of Municipal Managers, Clerks and Treasurers of Ontario. Coun. Eccles, who pulled the item from the consent agenda, argued $400 million falls well short once divided among eligible communities and said he wanted the fund raised every year to keep pace with costs. CAO Randy Scherzer said council has previously asked the province to account for inflation in municipal funding, and that an asset-management update due at the October 22 committee of the whole will include updated figures on Grey County's infrastructure needs. Coun. Greig said Buy Ontario rules, which now apply to municipalities, tend to raise prices by requiring domestic purchasing, which strengthens the case for more provincial funding. A September 15 letter from Municipal Affairs and Housing Minister Rob Flack, received for information without discussion, said failing to comply with Buy Ontario requirements "will force us to reconsider provincial funding made available to municipalities." Scherzer said staff will contact the Municipal Finance Officers' Association about its next steps.
