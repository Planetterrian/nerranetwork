### Lead
**Situation remains tense in violence-hit Manipur as toll rises to nine, CM calls for peace: The Hindu**
The toll from fresh clashes between Kuki and Naga armed groups in Kamjong, Ukhrul and Kangpokpi has risen to nine, with the killings reported on Friday, October 9, and Saturday, October 10. Chief Minister Y. Khemchand Singh said he condemned the killing of civilians, instructed authorities to restore order and protect citizens, and appealed to all communities for restraint, dialogue and harmony. Officials said the violence followed the shooting, on the night of October 9 in Kuki-dominated Chassad village in Naga-majority Kamjong, of a 22-year-old mother of five by unknown assailants; a 42-year-old man was then killed and 10 houses torched. Prohibitory orders under Section 162 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita are in force in that village, and officials said extra forces have gone to Kangpokpi and Kamjong. Officials said suspected militants on Saturday killed Naga village guards M. Daimai and R. Gangmei, and another person identified as Ringkangmai, in attacks on Tapon and Makui villages in Kangpokpi. The Working Committee JTC Manipur, a Naga civil society organisation, claimed militants beheaded 52-year-old villager Ajinbou R. and demanded the severed head be returned for burial. ### Across the Region
**‘Cockroach’ party leader released as Delhi braces for second day of protests: The Guardian**
A court has ordered the release of Cockroach Janta Party founder Abhijeet Dipke and two other senior leaders after their detention on Saturday, as Delhi prepared for a second day of protests demanding that Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar resign. The Guardian, citing Reuters, reported that police detained more than 2,000 people on Saturday to stop a gathering at Jantar Mantar; police said most of those held that day were released on Saturday night, and on Sunday metro stations had reopened and mobile internet was back. Critics say a voter-list revision disproportionately benefits the Bharatiya Janata Party, in power for more than 12 years. UN human rights spokesperson Thameen Al-Kheetan said the office was concerned by mass detentions and called on authorities to respect peaceful assembly and to hold meaningful dialogue. India’s foreign ministry said the UN approach was selective and that the statement was an unwarranted interference in India’s internal affairs. Source: [theguardian.com](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/11/cockroach-party-leader-released-delhi-protests-second-day)

**‘What they’re doing isn’t working’: Opposition hits back at premier over poverty report: Sydney Morning Herald**
The Bankwest Curtin Economics Centre’s 2026 Poverty Snapshot estimates that 456,000 Western Australians, including almost 99,000 children, are living in poverty, 15.1 per cent of the state, up from 11.8 per cent in 2021, with housing costs and inadequate incomes cited as the push. Premier Roger Cook, speaking at Dumas House on Sunday, said his government has put a billion dollars of cost-of-living relief in this budget alone, covering public transport, school costs and fuel, and that every level of government has to keep people from falling through the cracks. Opposition Leader Basil Zempilas said that if 15 per cent are on or below the poverty line, what the state government is doing is not working, and that Western Australians want the focus on housing, health, police resources and the cost of living. Source: [smh.com.au](https://www.smh.com.au/national/western-australia/what-they-re-doing-isn-t-working-opposition-hits-back-at-premier-over-poverty-report-20261011-p614ki.html?ref=rss&utm_medium=rss&utm_source=rss_national)
