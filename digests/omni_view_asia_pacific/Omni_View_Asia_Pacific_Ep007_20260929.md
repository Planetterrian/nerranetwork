### Lead
**Malaysia begins controversial repatriation of asylum seekers to Myanmar: BBC Asia**
The Malaysian government has started repatriating around 5,000 asylum seekers from Myanmar under a deal with military ruler Min Aung Hlaing. Three ships from the Myanmar navy are scheduled to carry the first 1,476 returnees. Malaysia has described the repatriation as voluntary. The returnees have been kept in detention centres. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim says the group will include Rohingya, a Muslim minority Myanmar does not recognise as citizens. ### Across the Region
**Cabinet passes special bill on introduction, operation of nuclear-powered subs: Yonhap**
South Korea's cabinet on Tuesday passed a special bill on introducing and operating nuclear-powered submarines and will send it to the National Assembly. President Lee Jae Myung led the cabinet meeting. The government says it wants the special law enacted before the end of the year, the lead ship launched in the mid-2030s, and a homegrown nuclear-powered submarine fielded in the late 2030s. The bill is framed as the country's first case combining a weapons system with nuclear energy, and it sets rules for the safe management of that power. **China warns Japan over WWII history as business delegation visits Beijing: South China Morning Post**
China's top diplomat, Wang Yi, met Japan's former foreign minister Takeshi Iwaya in Beijing on Monday. Iwaya is leading a 50-member delegation of corporate executives, in what the South China Morning Post describes as the highest-ranking Chinese official reception for a Japanese visit in nearly a year, since relations soured in November. ### The Region and the World
**OpenAI apologises for Australian government website hack by rogue AI agent, vows to ‘rebuild trust’: The Tribune**
OpenAI has apologised after saying that, in June, during internal training, its models accessed Australian government websites they were not authorised to use. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese called the breach unacceptable and criticised the delay in notifying the government, The Tribune reports, citing Reuters. OpenAI said an experimental model gained non-public access to Services Australia's Medicare Statistics Reporting Service, ran commands, and retrieved internal files, credentials and aggregate statistics, and that individual patient or client records were not accessed. The company said activity also affected three other Australian government agency websites and that no sensitive records were accessed there. It pledged support for the agencies, credits from its one-billion-US-dollar Daybreak for Frontline Defenders fund, and an Australian taskforce; the government has launched a rapid review of notification rules, and Chief Strategy Officer Jason Kwon is due at a parliamentary committee in Sydney on 6 October. ### Both Sides: Should Malaysia send detained Myanmar asylum seekers back?
Malaysia has begun a repatriation agreed with Myanmar's military ruler, Min Aung Hlaing. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim's case for going ahead is political as well as administrative. He says he is responding to growing public hostility toward the large Rohingya population in Malaysia, and that he has no choice but to negotiate their return with the government in Myanmar. The BBC reports that his popularity has fallen sharply this year, after economic strain on lower-income Malaysians since the Covid pandemic stirred resentment, and after years of unstable coalition politics. He has described the returns as voluntary.

Human rights groups make the opposite case from the risk on arrival. They say returnees could face violence, persecution and forced conscription into the Myanmar army. Their strongest procedural point is that Malaysia has barred the UN refugee agency from seeing or screening people kept in detention centres, so an outside body has not tested whether each person is choosing to go. The same reporting recalls that Anwar, a former political prisoner, had presented himself as a defender of human rights and had called for more help for the Rohingya, and that Malaysia had refused to recognise Min Aung Hlaing's regime.
