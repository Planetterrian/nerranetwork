### Lead
**Bangkok floods force thousands into shelters as disaster declared: The Guardian**
Thousands of Bangkok residents were in shelters on Sunday after days of heavy rain inundated homes and roads, and the Bangkok Metropolitan Administration declared a disaster across the capital. The administration said nearly 4,900 people had been evacuated and about 52,000 households were affected. The weather agency said more than 330 millimetres of rain fell in some areas between Thursday and Sunday. Office worker Kosin Nilset, 44, who lives along the Premprachakorn canal, said his house was under water for about 30 hours and that the level was higher than in the 2011 flood, which killed more than 500 people. Governor Chadchart Sittipunt said several roads were still flooded, told residents to avoid unnecessary travel, and said about 1,000 people were at a temporary shelter in a school in the Kheha Romklao area. The administration said canal levels remained high and crews were speeding drainage, with more rain forecast through Sunday before it begins to taper on Monday. ### Both Sides: Should parts of Karnataka's Western Ghats be notified as an ecologically sensitive area?
On September 22 the Karnataka Legislature unanimously passed a resolution rejecting the K. Kasturirangan Committee report on the Western Ghats in its current form. The vote answered the seventh draft, published on July 27 by the Ministry of Environment, Ecology and Climate Change, which proposes notifying parts of the range as an Ecologically Sensitive Area and gave the state 60 days to file objections. Karnataka has now rejected a draft of this kind for the seventh time since the first version in 2014.

The case for notification is the High Level Working Group chaired by K. Kasturirangan, set up in 2012 to review the earlier Western Ghats Ecology Expert Panel headed by Madhav Gadgil. In 2013 the group recommended that 59,940 square kilometres of the range's 1.64 lakh square kilometres, covering 4,156 villages across Keralam, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Goa, Maharashtra and Gujarat, be declared an Ecologically Sensitive Area, to protect a region UNESCO recognises as an ecological hotspot. Of the six Western Ghats states, Karnataka has the largest area proposed for notification, spread across 10 districts, and the highest number of villages.

The case against is the legislature's, backed by residents who have protested. Successive state governments have acknowledged the Western Ghats as ecologically sensitive, and have still rejected the drafts because of the effect on lives and livelihoods, after widespread protests and public consultations in the Malnad region over fears of lost livelihood.

Both sides accept that the range is ecologically sensitive and that Karnataka would carry the largest notified area. The July 27 draft's 60-day objection window is the formal next step the ministry set; the September 22 resolution is the legislature's answer inside that process.
