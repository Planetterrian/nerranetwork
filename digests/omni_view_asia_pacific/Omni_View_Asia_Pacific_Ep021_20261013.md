### Lead
**Lee stresses importance of U.S. alliance, says submissive stance should be shunned: Yonhap**
President Lee Jae Myung told a Cabinet meeting on Tuesday that South Korea's alliance with the United States is very important and the main axis of its diplomacy, and that it needs to be further advanced. He said diplomacy should uphold the country's dignity and national interests, and that any submissive or kowtowing stance should be shunned. Yonhap reports his remarks come amid concerns over friction, highlighted by limitations on intelligence sharing by the United States, reportedly over the unification minister's earlier identification of North Korea's Kusong as a site housing a uranium enrichment facility. Washington is believed to have protested those remarks as a unilateral disclosure of intelligence shared by the United States, and some conservative South Korean outlets have accused the minister of jeopardizing the alliance. Lee said differences between countries should be debated and held to a manageable level. He said Seoul's recent deal with Washington on U.S. investment projects, reached through the longest negotiations, produced results that best served South Korea's national interests compared with other countries. ### Across the Region
**Japan could add study on nuclear subs in security policy update: Asahi**
Japan is expected to hold off on putting nuclear-powered submarines into the three key national security documents to be revised this year, because of costs and other challenges, Asahi reports, citing government and coalition sources. The leading option is instead to begin studying whether to adopt them, including research into next-generation power sources, including nuclear power, for submarines with vertical launch systems for long-range missiles. Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi has said the government will not rule out acquiring them. Maritime Self-Defense Force chief of staff Admiral Akira Saito said such submarines are expected to deliver a dramatic improvement in capabilities. The government plans to decide the wording after ruling-coalition talks that will begin as early as November. Source: [asahi.com](https://www.asahi.com/ajw/articles/16959996)

**'We grow with and for the world': Modi sets out India's economic vision at Bloomberg forum: The New Indian Express**
Prime Minister Narendra Modi told the Bloomberg New Economy Forum on Tuesday, held in India for the first time, that India does not grow at anyone's cost and grows with the world and for the world. He cited 7.8 percent growth in the April-June quarter, foreign-exchange reserves exceeding 700 billion U.S. dollars, and a current-account deficit he said had fallen from over 4 percent 12 to 13 years ago to about half a percent. He called the approach power to empower, and said India is sharing digital identity, payments and public infrastructure with partner countries, particularly in the Global South. The Tribune reports he also cited more than 100 languages, nearly 80,000 registered newspapers and publications, over 1,000 satellite television channels, and more than 500 million users each on YouTube and Instagram. ### The Region and the World
**Massive government spending blamed as IMF downgrades Australia: News24**
The International Monetary Fund's latest outlook projects Australia's GDP to grow 1.6 percent next year, down 0.1 percent from its April prediction, News24 reports. The Fund's forecast unemployment rate for next year has risen from 4.3 percent to 4.7 percent, which the report says would equal about 800,000 Australians without a job, while unemployment in most other advanced economies is expected to drop or stay flat in 2027. The IMF warned governments to rebuild fiscal space, saying any new spending or tax cuts should be well funded. News24 says Australian government spending is about 26.9 percent of GDP, the highest level outside the pandemic in about 40 years, and EQ Economics managing director Warren Hogan said that if governments do not cut recurrent spending, the cash rate might keep rising through next year. ### Both Sides: Should Japan's Cabinet raise its publicity budget tenfold?
The Takaichi administration is seeking a fiscal 2027 budget of 7.2 billion yen, about 45.5 million dollars, for the Cabinet Public Affairs Office, roughly ten times the previous fiscal year's amount. About 500 million yen, about 3.16 million dollars, would strengthen overseas messaging. Most of the request is for domestic audiences. The Mainichi says the government has explained that the money is to create and distribute easily understandable videos aimed primarily at young people, outsourced to private companies and strengthened in quality and quantity. Planned themes include the basic policy, the growth strategy, economic security and child-care support. A mid-September video, Honebuto Classroom, already on the prime minister's office YouTube channel, uses a former lawmaker and entertainers to explain the Basic Policy on Economic and Fiscal Management and Reform. The Mainichi says explaining disaster responses and publishing news conferences are necessary, and the government's case is that young people need those policies in a form they will watch. The Mainichi's own case against the request is that doubts cannot be dispelled that the aim is to promote government policy and lift support. It says most of the money sits under a framework for a strong and prosperous Japan, with no clear link to economic growth, and that there is no rational reason for a tenfold rise. The administration has said it will pursue policies that could divide public opinion. The Mainichi argues that spending taxpayers' money on explanations favourable to the administration risks becoming propaganda. The request is for fiscal 2027. The Mainichi does not report a date for a decision. Source: [mainichi.jp](http://mainichi.jp/english/articles/20261013/p2a/00m/0op/015000c)

**Ming Dynasty statue unearthed in WA back in Chinese hands: ABC News Australia**
A 15-centimetre Ming Dynasty bronze Buddha, found in 2018 under 20 centimetres of soil beside a highway in Shark Bay by prospectors Leon Deschamps and Shayne Thomson, was handed to Chinese consular officials on Tuesday at a ceremony in Denham. The pair had promised their late friend, Malgana man Nick Pedrocchi, that they would see it safely home. Australia China Friendship Society WA president Simon Wu called the return a gesture of respect and goodwill. Further testing will be done before the statue is displayed in a Beijing museum. It will travel with red dirt from Shark Bay and a golden pearl shell. Source: [abc.net.au](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-10-13/ming-dynasty-buddha-statue-returned-chinese-officials/107249044)
