### Lead
**Japan moves to tighten rules for foreigners, throwing futures into doubt: Al Jazeera**
Japan’s Immigration Services Agency is bringing tighter permanent-residency rules into effect in phases from October 1. Applicants must show annual household income above the Japanese average — a test applied retroactively to applications submitted since April — and must demonstrate Japanese-language proficiency. They must also hold a pension pot equivalent to 30 years of payouts. In the announcement, the agency said foreigners need to “live independently” and have “a stable life without the risk of becoming a burden.” Abdul, a Bangladeshi tech engineer who has lived in northern Tokyo for nearly eight years and asked not to be identified by his real name, is preparing applications for residency and citizenship and has started looking for work outside Japan. Sarah Nelkin, an American who has lived in Japan for 14 years and filed for residency shortly before the changes were announced, says that if she is rejected she will seek citizenship, which would mean giving up her United States citizenship because Japan does not recognise dual nationality. ### Across the Region
**Heavy rain lashes Odisha; over 8,000 people evacuated, ministers rush to affected districts: The Hindu**
A deep depression over the Bay of Bengal crossed the Andhra Pradesh coast near Kalingapatnam in Srikakulam on Thursday, the India Meteorological Department said, and heavy rain then lashed parts of Odisha. The Hindu reported that the Odisha government evacuated over 8,000 people from low-lying and landslide-prone areas after landslides, waterlogging, uprooted trees and house damage. The IMD said squally weather would prevail along the coast, with winds gusting up to 70 kmph, and advised fishermen not to venture into the sea until Friday morning. The Nagavali was expected to carry about 60,000 cusecs on Friday afternoon, keeping Vizianagaram, Srikakulam and Parvathipuram Manyam districts on alert. Initial estimates put crop damage at more than 10,000 acres in and around Rajam, with banana and vegetable crops among the worst hit, and Vizianagaram Collector S. Ramsundar Reddy said people in low-lying areas of Vangara mandal, near the Madduvalasa reservoir, had been moved as inflows reached 60,000 cusecs. **A ‘strikingly unusual’ condition. But this nurse knew instantly Joe Massa was very unwell: Sydney Morning Herald**
A coronial inquest has heard that two-year-old Joe Massa died in September 2024 of a “strikingly unusual” complication, and that senior nurse Felicity Melville recognised at once that he was critically ill and raised the alarm past his doctors. His death galvanised the Minns government’s plans to end the Northern Beaches Hospital’s public-private partnership after his mother, Elouise Massa, reported that she had repeatedly tried to alert staff. Melville, who started as team leader at 10am on September 14, almost three hours after Joe arrived, told the inquest on Thursday, through tears, that dark, sunken eyes and mottled skin made it instantly obvious he was “very, very unwell.” She could not record a heart rate after leaving the bedside twice for missing monitor cables and broken stickers, but she recorded a respiratory rate of 57. She said a registrar’s plan to wait for an ultrasound was reassurance when she wanted immediate action, so she escalated to her nurse unit manager, moved Joe toward resuscitation without the doctors asking her to, scooped him onto a smaller trolley, and told another doctor, “I’m really worried about this child,” after which that doctor came to the bedside. ### Both Sides: Disclosing the transfer of two North Korean soldiers
South Korea and Ukraine disagree on whether the handover of two North Korean prisoners of war to the South should have stayed secret. President Lee Jae Myung wrote on X on Thursday that Ukraine disclosed the transfer in breach of a non-disclosure agreement, a day after President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told the United Nations General Assembly in New York that Ukraine recently sent the two soldiers to South Korea.

Lee says secrecy was the responsible choice. He said it was appropriate in every aspect not to disclose, given the many problems disclosure may cause, and that the two sides had agreed to keep the transfer quiet. He said the issue is very sensitive and could immensely affect diplomacy, national security, and the security environment on the Korean Peninsula. He said it would be cruel and irresponsible to disclose the transfer regardless of the effect on inter-Korean ties, or of whether disclosure could lead to someone’s death or hamper national interests. Seoul had previously refused to confirm the soldiers’ arrival, citing potential dangers to the safety and lives of the prisoners and their families still in North Korea.

Ukraine says a total blackout was not credible. The presidential office told Yonhap News Agency that the country disclosed only the transfer, not what it called the long details of the process, and that it would have been strange to keep the entire transfer a secret. The office also said the transfer was carried out through direct dialogue with Seoul.

Both accounts treat the handover as having happened: Lee objects to disclosure of a transfer he says the governments agreed to handle confidentially, and Kyiv says the soldiers were sent through talks with Seoul. Lee said he does not know the reasons for the disclosure, and that he regrets it. Source: [en.yna.co.kr](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20260925000252315)
