The case on both sides in South Australia is whether sentencing courts should stop hearing good-character references for every criminal offence.

Attorney-General Kyam Maher says consultation starts in the coming days, and a bill should reach parliament in the first half of next year. ABC News Australia carries the proposal.

He says the Royal Commission into Domestic, Family and Sexual Violence recommended a review, and that the change would line South Australia up with New South Wales and Western Australia.

He described child sex offences as leaving a profound and lasting impact on victim-survivors.

Survivor advocate Harrison James co-founded the Your Reference Ain’t Relevant campaign four years ago, aimed first at child sex cases. He says a full ban would make a tangible difference for victim-survivors.

His reason is the room. He says nothing is more dehumanising than hearing the person who harmed you called kind, generous and a good person, after you lived through the worst thing that person did to you.

The allowance still on the books is the case for leaving a path open, and it is the state government that describes it. An offender may put in evidence of good character, or call friends, family or employers, and ask for leniency.

Judges already must not weigh that evidence when sentencing a child sex offender if the claimed good character helped the offence happen.

The same law still allows it if the offending did not use that reputation, or if the person had no prior convictions. The proposal would shut that door for every offence, not only child sex cases.

Both accounts start from that rule: outside the assisted-offending limit, good-character evidence can still reach the court. Next is Maher’s consultation, then the bill he expects next year.
