### Lead
**Taiwan's president calls for clear stance against China's threats: Yahoo**
On Saturday, President Lai Ching-te used his National Day address in Taipei to call on Indo-Pacific democracies to take a clear stance against China’s threats to Taiwan. He said there must be respect for the right of the people of Taiwan to exist, and that those democracies should join forces so the status quo of peace and stability is not changed. Lai said the question was not only Taiwan’s survival but whether the international community still believed force must not override rules; he said Taiwan cherishes peace but will not give up its freedom, is open to exchanges, and will not accept subordination. He said higher defence spending is meant to deter war, not to provoke, and Yahoo noted that the United States is Taiwan’s key arms supplier. China claims democratically governed Taiwan as its own territory and regularly sends military aircraft and warships around the island; observers are concerned about Chinese coastguard patrols east of Taiwan, pressure that stays short of open conflict. The Presidential Office translation of the speech marked the 30th anniversary of Taiwan’s first direct presidential election, which it said citizens cast under missile threats. ### Across the Region
**India's 'Cockroach' leaders detained ahead of major protest: DW**
Delhi Police said about 500 people arriving in separate groups were detained on Saturday before a Cockroach Janta Party rally police had not authorised, and that a further 50 people were arrested and described as anti-social elements with criminal antecedents. DW reported 43,000 police and security personnel deployed across Delhi, internet services cut in the centre, and heavy limits on metro and railway services. Founder Abhijeet Dipke and co-conveners Saurav Das and Ashutosh Ranka were detained after their flight landed; legal aid head Ratna Singh was also detained, a police spokesperson told The Straits Times that YouTube figure Dhruv Rathee was held outside Delhi, and a police source told that paper about 200 people were detained in the central area. The rally seeks the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar over a voter-list revision that critics say favours the Bharatiya Janata Party; before leaving Mumbai, Dipke asked ANI why the prime minister feared the country’s youth after railways, the metro, the internet and roads were closed. The party said the interception was meant to stop peaceful protest leaders reaching Jantar Mantar, and Singh said officers cited verbal orders and produced no warrant; advocate Shailendra Mani Tripathi has filed a contempt plea saying the transit shutdown breaks a Supreme Court order of October 9 against a blanket ban that harms ordinary commuters. ### Both Sides: Should Australia ban card surcharges?
Small businesses in Sydney are already changing how they take payment after the Reserve Bank of Australia decided it was in the public interest to ban surcharges on card payments from October 1. The bank expected the change to save consumers 1.6 billion dollars every year. Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said it could make a cup of coffee cheaper. The case for the ban is that the extra fee sits on the customer at the till, and the central bank judged that ending it served the public.

The case against is that the cost does not disappear. Gabrielle Franco, who owns La Botte Doro in Leichhardt, put up a sign calling cash king. He estimates he would lose between 12,000 and 16,000 dollars if he absorbed card costs, said he cannot raise prices, and said he already declares all income while working seven days. Geoffrey Ji, at Cranes Newsagency in Marrickville, imposed a 10-dollar minimum for EFTPOS, a 35-dollar minimum to top up an Opal card, and stopped taking credit cards. He estimates a loss of about 3,000 dollars a month, and said the shop makes about 20 cents on a newspaper. Economists warned that if shops lift prices to cover electronic payments, the ban could add to inflation rather than cut the cost of living. Franco also said suppliers may add delivery charges of their own.

Both sides accept that a card payment has a cost. In response to the surcharge ban, the Tax Office said it would no longer take credit cards from small businesses paying tax in instalments. After a backlash against that decision, the government said it would delay the ban for a year. ### Progress Watch
**Prefabricated Apartment Delivers 38 Social Homes in North Coogee: Mirage News**
Thirty-eight new social homes in North Coogee were completed in 14 months, eight months faster than a traditional build, in a 23.5 million dollar project jointly funded by the Cook and Albanese governments. Twelve one-bedroom, two studio and 24 two-bedroom apartments used prefabricated bathroom pods and masonry wall panels made in Western Australia by Tyrone Group while site works continued. Housing and Works Minister John Carey said the homes add to more than 4,300 social homes delivered since 2021. A community housing provider will manage them, and tenants are set to move in later this year.
