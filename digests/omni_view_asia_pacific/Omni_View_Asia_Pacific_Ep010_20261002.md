### Lead
**Kin of Manipur’s 49 people missing since 2023 ethnic conflict to get ₹10 lakh each: The Hindu**
The Manipur Cabinet has approved a one-time payment of ₹10 lakh for the kin of each of 49 people missing since the ethnic conflict that began on 3 May 2023. Government spokesperson Sapam Ranjan Singh called it a special humanitarian measure after a Cabinet meeting on Thursday chaired by Chief Minister Yunman Khemchand Singh. Home Minister Konthoujam Govindas told the Assembly in September that the conflicts — first between the Kuki-Zo and Meitei communities, then between the Kukis and Nagas — claimed 306 lives and displaced more than 60,000 people. Singh said the 49 remain untraced despite searches, and that families lack the death certificates and autopsy reports required for the applicable central assistance scheme because the law presumes a missing person dead only after seven years. The Cabinet also raised ex-gratia for Army and Central Armed Police Forces personnel killed or injured while operating in Manipur in connection with extremist violence, matching state police rates: ₹10 lakh for death, ₹2 lakh for permanent disability, ₹1 lakh for grievous injury and ₹20,000 for simple injury, against ₹2 lakh for death and ₹10,000 for injury fixed in 2006. **How the alleged Newington College rape was exposed: Sydney Morning Herald**
Deandra Kontou, 32, was charged last week with nine offences over the alleged abuse of a teenage boy across six months while she taught at Newington College in Sydney, the Sydney Morning Herald reports. Most of the alleged offences were during school hours in Stanmore, where she taught under her maiden name, Haralambidis; the allegation continued on a senior camp at Fitzroy Island on the Great Barrier Reef, where she is accused of twice sexually touching the boy during the day and sexually assaulting him between 10 p.m. and 11 p.m. Most charges are aggravated because she was his teacher. The student told another teacher on the tour; Kontou was removed from the travelling party, later suspended, and resigned when offered a chance to explain, after two years at the school and in the middle of term. Newington referred the allegation to NSW Police and the Office of the Children’s Guardian; detectives investigated soon after the alleged abuse but laid charges only after restarting the investigation last year. The school said on Thursday night that the matter is before the courts and that it will continue to support police. ### The Region and the World
**Rights group, US lawmakers condemn first Starbucks stores in China’s Xinjiang: The Straits Times**
Starbucks opened its first two cafes in Urumqi, Xinjiang, on 29 September, a step Chinese state media heralded and US lawmakers and a Uyghur advocacy group condemned. John Moolenaar, chairman of the US House Select Committee on China, called the opening “morally bankrupt” on 1 October and said it ties an American brand to the Chinese Communist Party’s campaign against Uyghurs. The World Uyghur Congress said Starbucks was ignoring a dire human rights situation and that companies should not contribute to an economy where state-imposed forced labour remains a serious concern. The United Nations warned in 2022 of possible crimes against humanity in Xinjiang, including detention of more than a million Uyghurs and other Muslim minorities; rights groups say serious abuses, including forced labour, continue. The Chinese government denies the accusations and says its policies have rooted out extremism and brought economic development. ### Both Sides: Should sitting Supreme Court judges advise a parliamentary committee on the One Nation, One Election Bills?
A Joint Parliamentary Committee is examining the Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024, and the Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2024, which seek a framework for holding Lok Sabha and state Assembly elections together. The Bills were introduced in the Lok Sabha in December 2024 and sent to the committee, headed by BJP MP P.P. Chaudhary. The committee had proposed a visit to the Supreme Court on 6 October 2026 for an informal interaction with the Chief Justice of India and other judges on simultaneous elections. That visit has been put off.

The case for going ahead rests on what parliamentary committees are for. They examine Bills closely and can seek views from experts and stakeholders, and committees have interacted with sitting judges before. Chaudhary’s programme treated the October meeting as an informal discussion of simultaneous elections, not as a court hearing. He later said the dates of committee members did not match and that he had other commitments.

The case against is that the subject was the merits and constitutional validity of Bills that could, once law, be challenged in the same court. Opposition members objected on that ground, not to committees visiting the court in general. Independent Rajya Sabha member Kapil Sibal called the proposed interaction unconstitutional, saying Parliament has the power to make laws and the judiciary has the power to examine their constitutional validity.

Both sides accept that the visit did not take place and that the committee is still examining the two Bills. Chaudhary has given scheduling, not a new date, as the reason the 6 October interaction was deferred.
