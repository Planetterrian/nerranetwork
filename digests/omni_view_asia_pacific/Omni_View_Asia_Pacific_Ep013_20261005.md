### Lead
**Union Cabinet approves ₹1.86 lakh crore Green Energy Corridor Phase-III: The Hindu**
The Union Cabinet has approved the ₹1.86 lakh crore Green Energy Corridor Phase-III, meant to close the geographic and temporal gap between renewable generation and use. Almost three-fourths of that sum, ₹1.36 lakh crore, is for intra-state transmission, and ₹50,000 crore is for 50 gigawatt-hours of battery storage. The Hindu reports the project is meant to move renewable power from generation-rich states to consuming centres, evacuate up to 135 gigawatts, and finish in financial year 2032-33. Central financial support is ₹54,082 crore, with viability-gap funding intended to draw private capital into storage. The International Energy Agency expects Indian electricity demand to grow 6.4 percent a year on average through 2030. India had commissioned 263 gigawatts of renewable capacity by January 2026, against a national policy target of 500 gigawatts by 2030, with plans also cited to integrate over 600 gigawatts by 2032. **Drought survey records 12,603 hectares of chilli loss in Karnataka: The Hindu**
A drought survey last month by the Karnataka horticulture department, the Revenue Department and the state disaster authority found major losses in chilli, turmeric, garlic and ginger. Chilli loss was 12,603 hectares, valued at ₹19,629 lakh, against 99,986.57 hectares sown and a target of 1,20,000.03 hectares. Dharwad lost 4,611 hectares of 12,291.96 sown, and Bagalkot lost 4,093 of 6,334.39; Bagalkot and Chamarajanagar led chilli and turmeric losses respectively. A horticulture official said the survey began in 124 taluks, another 53 have been added, and spice losses are likely to rise. Gadag trader Mallesh Hanjigi said Byadagi chilli prices have stayed at an all-time high for two years. **Trump cites an Indian presidential election that was not held: The Hindu**

### Both Sides: Should Western Australia open a coronial inquest into the 2022 Port Hedland child deaths?
The decision in dispute is whether Western Australia should hold a coronial inquest into the deaths of three children in Port Hedland in July 2022, after the coroner declined to open one.

Indigenous Australians Minister Malarndirri McCarthy told News 24 on Monday that she supports grandmother Dorothy Papertalk's campaign for an inquest into what happened and the circumstances leading to it. McCarthy said there is something not right in that region, and she urged the state Labor government to keep working with families on poverty and neglect. Opposition spokeswoman Libby Mettam asked what the Cook Labor government is afraid an independent inquest will find, and said the family deserves answers and the community deserves to know how the failures were allowed to happen. Aboriginal Elders have called for a Royal Commission into the state's child protection agency and for Child Protection Minister Jessica Stojkovski to resign, after reports last month in The Australian of more than a dozen children living unsupervised in squalor in a South Hedland home.

Attorney-General Tony Buti has said he cannot overrule the coroner's decision not to hold an inquest unless fresh evidence is brought forward. The Sydney Morning Herald reports that Margaret Dale Hawke is serving a life sentence for stabbing, strangling and suffocating her ten-year-old daughter and two sons, aged seven and four months, before burning their bodies in a house fire in July 2022.

Both arguments accept that the coroner has already declined an inquest and that the three children died in July 2022. Buti names fresh evidence as the condition for overriding that decision. McCarthy names continued work with families.
