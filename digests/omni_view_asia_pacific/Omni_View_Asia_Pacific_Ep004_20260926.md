### Lead
**Non-recovery of train ticket from deceased passenger can't defeat compensation claim: Supreme Court: The Hindu**
The Supreme Court said the failure to recover a train ticket from a dead passenger cannot defeat a compensation claim, and it restored the award to the parents of Maheshbhai. A bench of Justices Ujjal Bhuyan and A. S. Chandurkar set aside a Gujarat High Court order that had rejected the payout. Maheshbhai fell from a general compartment after a sudden jerk on 27 September 2017 while travelling to Surat via Ahmedabad, was hospitalised for more than a month, and died on 31 October 2017. On 17 June 2022 the Railways Claim Tribunal held that the fall was an untoward incident and awarded his parents ₹8 lakh with interest at nine per cent a year from the date of the incident until the date of that order. The bench said on Friday that the High Court gave undue weight to the missing ticket, against the father's statement on oath and the long hospitalisation, and that on the preponderance of probabilities he was a bona fide passenger. ### Across the Region
**Musician Rick Wakeman performs concert for 150 rescued bears in Vietnam — Asharq Al-Awsat: ua.news**
British musician Rick Wakeman played a piano concert for 150 rescued Himalayan bears at the Animals Asia sanctuary in Tam Dao National Park in Vietnam. He played a Yamaha grand piano outdoors and wrote new pieces for the animals, hoping they would come to the fence and listen. Wakeman said he has supported rescued bears since 2016 and recently adopted a 20-month-old male; he called for faster transfers, saying 125 bears remain caged on farms and four die every month. Animals Asia founder and chief executive Jill Robinson said staff wanted to see how the bears reacted to music written for them. The report says bile is drawn from gallbladders with a needle for traditional Chinese medicine; Vietnam bans the practice, but some bears are still kept in cramped cages on former farms. **US, China to set up 'communication channel' for AI incidents: CNA**
The United States and China will set up a communication channel for artificial-intelligence incidents, the White House said on Friday after President Donald Trump and President Xi Jinping concluded a summit. A White House fact sheet said the two sides established a US-China Super Intelligence Dialogue to exchange views on the risks and benefits of AI, and that the leaders agreed to use the term SI rather than AI. The next exchange is set to occur by November. Before the summit, Trump said on social media that super intelligence would be a big topic but that he wanted to leave it exactly where it is, and that this was China's position also. Xi, speaking at the White House on Thursday, said both countries have the capability and responsibility to develop and manage AI for good and to keep its development under human control. ### Both Sides: Should three states vote again on paper ballots?
Uddhav Thackeray, Shiv Sena (UBT) chief and a former Maharashtra chief minister, wants elections in Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu and West Bengal held again, and he wants future polls on ballot paper. He said so on Wednesday, 23 September 2026, at a press conference at Matoshree, his home in Mumbai.

His case is that the results cannot stand while Gyanesh Kumar remains Chief Election Commissioner. He said Kumar should be jailed immediately, that wrong Election Commission decisions should be revoked, and that every election in which Kumar intervened should be rerun. He appealed to opposition parties to come together to save democracy, accused Kumar of working on instructions from Union Home Minister Amit Shah, and accused him of murdering democracy. He thanked Election Commissioners Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi, called the reported differences a rebellion, and said the two commissioners now have a majority. He alleged that the Commission gave the Shiv Sena name and symbol to a rival faction, that about 40 lakh names were added before elections and removed after, and that the Special Intensive Revision deleted 90 lakh names in West Bengal and 70 lakh in Tamil Nadu.

The Election Commission has refuted the reports of differences between the commissioners. Its stated position is that inputs given at the draft stage should not be construed as final decisions. That answer treats the reported objections as unfinished comments rather than completed acts that void a poll. Thackeray and the Commission are both addressing those reports of internal differences. He reads them, together with the deletion figures he alleges, as reason to wipe results and return to paper ballots. The Commission says draft-stage inputs are not final decisions.
