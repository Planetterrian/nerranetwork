### Lead
**Vietnam mourns 700 war dead in major push to identify missing: The Straits Times**
Vietnam held funeral rites on October 14 in Ho Chi Minh City for 731 sets of remains unearthed in a national drive to identify people missing from wars with France and the United States. Tens of thousands lined the streets, some holding white flowers or praying, as buses carried caskets covered by the national flag to a cemetery for fallen soldiers on the city’s outskirts. The military said the remains came from individual and mass graves in Le Thi Rieng Park, of fighters killed when US-backed forces crushed a communist uprising in 1968. Deputy mayor Nguyen Manh Cuong said authorities “will not overlook any opportunity or method to continue the journey of searching for the fallen comrades,” and that most soldiers recovered in this phase still cannot be named or linked to a hometown. State media said one man, squad leader Huynh Van Quen, who fought in 1968, was identified in July from a scrap of paper bearing his name and from DNA that matched his relatives. Deputy Prime Minister Pham Thi Thanh Tra called the aim of recovering 7,000 sets of remains by July 2027 “a political task of special importance but also a command from the heart.” Up to 1.2 million Vietnamese soldiers are estimated to have been killed between 1945 and 1975, and there are 230,000 graves nationwide for unidentified soldiers. More than 1,500 people are searching old battlefields in Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia, while teams take samples from those graves and run DNA analysis on 18,000 samples of remains. ### Across the Region
**Lawmaker sentenced to 2 1/2 yrs in prison over cash-for-nomination scandal: Yonhap**
The Seoul Central District Court on Wednesday sentenced independent lawmaker Kang Sun-woo to two and a half years in prison for receiving money in exchange for an election nomination, and ordered her to forfeit 100 million won, about 75,000 US dollars. Yonhap reports she was charged with accepting that sum in January 2022 from former Seoul city councilor Kim Kyung in return for a Democratic Party nomination ahead of that year’s local elections, while she sat on the party’s nomination committee for Seoul city councilors. Kim was selected, and was elected. The court sentenced Kim to one year in prison, and a former aide to Kang to eight months, suspended for two years, for arranging the meeting. The court said the defendants exchanged illegal political funds, undermining election fairness and severely damaging public trust. Kang, formerly of the ruling Democratic Party, left the party at the start of this year after a recording surfaced of her discussing the money with former floor leader Kim Byung-kee. **F1 race at Malaysia’s Sepang generated nearly US$232 million: Sports ministry: CNA**
Malaysia’s youth and sports ministry said the Gulf Air Bahrain Grand Prix, staged at Sepang from October 2 to October 4 as a one-off, generated nearly 950 million ringgit, about 232 million US dollars, in economic impact. In a written parliamentary reply on Tuesday, Youth and Sports Minister Taufiq Johari said the event drew about 261,000 spectators over three days, including an estimated 103,000 on race day, above Sepang’s previous Formula 1 record of 126,690 at the 2008 Malaysian Grand Prix, as local media had reported. He cited Formula 1 management figures putting the worldwide audience at 827 million, and said more than 97,000 tickets were sold, with over 50,000 international tourists and about 5,000 foreign workers travelling to Malaysia. The race was moved from Bahrain after the April event could not go ahead because of the Iran war and wider tensions in the Middle East. Taufiq said the government has not yet decided whether hosting was worthwhile; a cost-benefit study is still under way, and Sepang International Circuit has appointed a consulting firm. Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said on October 4 that the government had held talks with Formula 1 about restoring the Malaysian Grand Prix as a permanent fixture, while acknowledging a return would take time. ### The Closer Look
**Rare earths company could be nationalised as China-linked investors fail to divest: ABC News Australia**
Resources Minister Madeleine King says nationalising Perth-based Northern Minerals is an option, because China-linked investors are still defying federal orders to sell out of the heavy rare earths company. She made the comments at the National Press Club on Wednesday.

In May, Treasurer Jim Chalmers ordered six investors holding around 17 per cent of Northern Minerals to divest by July 2. The company told the ASX later in July that those investors still held most of the shares they had been ordered to sell. King said that uncertainty was holding up the Browns Range project in Western Australia’s east Kimberley. She called it one of the most significant heavy rare earth deposits on Earth, and said it means a great deal to Australia’s national interest and national security.

The minerals at stake are dysprosium and terbium, used in renewable energy infrastructure and advanced military technology. Northern Minerals is already on track for about 500 million US dollars in funding from the Export-Import Bank of the United States. ABC News Australia reports the company is treated as a key player in efforts by the United States and Australia to loosen China’s hold on the critical minerals supply chain.

Asked if the government would nationalise the firm, King said it was one option, but that she would not want to advance on any decision, and she stressed that none had been made. ABC News reported she appeared to confirm that the companies had not paid fines imposed for non-compliance. She said failure to follow through on Australian government decisions was a serious matter under active review, and that Foreign Investment Review Board divestment decisions “has not been done by those who hold those shares.”

The standoff follows earlier interventions. In 2023, Chalmers blocked Chinese-linked investor Yuxiao Fund from boosting its stake. In 2025, he ordered another five China-linked companies to divest. One of those investors launched an unsuccessful bid earlier this year to topple the company’s leadership. The Foreign Investment Review Board wrote to Northern Minerals this year that three of the blocked investors had transferred their shares to Hong Kong Ying Tak Ltd, one of the six companies ordered to divest.

King said the saga showed why the government should press on with its review of foreign investment laws, which is due to wrap up next year. The ABC has approached Northern Minerals for comment. ### Both Sides: Should South Australia rotate birthing between Jamestown and Crystal Brook?
The Yorke and Northern Local Health Network has begun a six-month trial that alternates a one-team, one-site birthing service between Jamestown and Crystal Brook hospitals in South Australia’s Mid North, after ongoing midwifery shortages. The change took effect this week.

Four local GPs wrote to Health Minister Blair Boyer with what they called urgent concern, describing the model as unsafe and unsustainable. Their strongest reason is clinical: rotating sites stops doctors from covering maternity and emergency on-call at the same time, and complicates handovers. The letter said fragmented care and unintentional gaps in handover have been associated with 80 per cent of serious preventable medical errors, and that closing one maternity unit at a time is known to raise births before arrival and birth trauma, while forcing mothers to leave home communities. Jamestown GP Simon Jackson, a signatory, said he was restricted to obstetric care on the Monday the change began, leaving a doctor shortage, with possible later effects on emergency cover and on Adelaide’s already stressed hospitals. Shadow health minister Jack Batty said Boyer should explain how continuity, handovers and safety are being assessed, and should not settle for a timeshare that local doctors warn is unsafe.

Boyer said the change followed advice from then-SA Health chief Robyn Lawrence, and that neither hospital complied with the Maternity and Neonatal Clinical Service Capability Framework — a claim the GPs strongly dispute in their letter. His strongest reason is the risk he was warned of: a catastrophic event for a mother or baby if nothing changed. He said the rotation is a trial meant to keep birthing in the region while giving him a basis to tell prospective parents the sites are safe, and that the government will see how it goes. ### Progress Watch
**Vietnam’s DNA lab reports data from 54 of 58 degraded samples: The Straits Times**
At the Centre for DNA Identification in Hanoi, director Tran Trung Thanh told AFP that a pilot using US-funded technology through the International Commission on Missing Persons pulled genetic data from 54 of 58 severely degraded samples, under what he called a world-first process for remains in that condition. That hit rate matters to families still waiting on the 230,000 unidentified graves: Thanh said the work is lengthy, and that ageing relatives and remains decomposing in the ground are both shrinking the chance of a match.
