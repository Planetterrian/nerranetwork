> **After more than five years, Indonesia's president moves to replace the police chief.**

**What You Need to Know:** After more than five years, Indonesia's president moves to replace the police chief. President Prabowo Subianto has proposed Suyudi Ario Seto, head of the National Narcotics Agency, in place of Listyo Sigit Prabowo, and the House commission that oversees security has agreed by consensus to the legal process. The presidential letter is dated September 30, and Speaker Puan Maharani said the formal appointment was set for Thursday afternoon.

### Lead
**President Prabowo replaces Indonesia police chief Listyo Sigit with narcotics agency head: CNA**
President Prabowo Subianto has moved to replace national police chief Listyo Sigit Prabowo with Suyudi Ario Seto, head of the National Narcotics Agency since August last year, Channel News Asia reports. The House of Representatives received a presidential letter dated September 30 proposing the dismissal and the new appointment, Habiburokhman, chairman of Commission III, said on Thursday. House Speaker Puan Maharani confirmed that Suyudi would be formally appointed on Thursday afternoon, and the House has tasked Commission III with a fit-and-proper test. All factions on that commission agreed by consensus to the replacement process in accordance with the law, Habiburokhman said, as quoted by the Jakarta Globe. Listyo has held the post for over five years, since Joko Widodo’s administration, making him the second-longest serving national police chief, the Jakarta Post reported. In early August, local media reported, citing sources, that a presidential letter ordering Listyo’s replacement had already been sent, amid tensions between the police and the Attorney General’s Office over graft and money-laundering probes implicating senior officials at both institutions; Habiburokhman dismissed those reports at the time as unsubstantiated rumours. ### Both Sides: Should states supply the demographic committee’s religion-wise data?
The Ministry of Home Affairs set up the High-Level Committee on Demographic Changes on May 26, under retired Supreme Court judge Prakash Prabhakar Naolekar, and gave it a year to report, with a possible extension of six months. The Hindu reported that the panel has sent states a 35-point questionnaire on change over 15 years. A large share of the questions is sought district by district and by religion, covering Hindus, Muslims, Christians, Sikhs, Buddhists and Parsis.

The committee’s terms of reference are the case for collecting the data. The ministry constituted the panel to study demographic changes from illegal immigration and other reasons it describes as abnormal, to recommend measures, and to recommend an institutional mechanism for what it calls population stabilisation. The form asks for religion-wise figures on birth registrations, school enrolment, ration cards, vehicle registrations, property transactions and voter rolls. It also asks states to identify unusual rises in Aadhaar enrolments, voter identity cards and ration cards, to report forged identity documents, and to name districts where school enrolment looks inconsistent with historical birth rates, with possible reasons.

State officials who spoke to The Hindu make the case against. They say much of the information does not exist in the form requested, because ordinary administrative databases do not sort citizens by religion across many of those categories.

The questionnaire and the May 26 mandate are not in dispute. The next dated step in the source is the report, due in a year unless the ministry adds up to six months. Source: [thehindu.com](https://www.thehindu.com/news/national/conundrum-over-panels-questionnaire-seeking-extensive-data-on-demographic-changes-explained/article71523595.ece)
