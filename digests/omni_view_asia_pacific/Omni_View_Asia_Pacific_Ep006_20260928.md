### Lead
**Supreme Court takes suo motu cognisance of recent sexual assault cases in Delhi-NCR, flags ‘lax patrolling’ by authorities: The Hindu**
India's Supreme Court on Monday took suo motu cognisance of recent sexual assault cases against minors and women in Delhi-NCR and sought a response from the Delhi Police. A bench of Justices J.B. Pardiwala and K. Vinod Chandran cited the alleged rape of a 17-year-old at Astha Kunj park near Lady Shri Ram College, the gang-rape of a minor at Swaroop Nagar, and the sexual assault of another minor on a bus from Greater Noida to Delhi. The bench said public spaces, including parks, buses and Metro stations, cannot become “zones of high risk” because of “lax patrolling and other preventable administrative deficiencies.” It said the right to live with dignity, free from the fear of violence, is part of life and personal liberty under Article 21 of the Constitution. The court said an alleged gang-rape of a 17-year-old occurred on a bus that travelled nearly 47 kilometres from Greater Noida to Kashmere Gate without a stop at a police picket, and that it could not help but draw painful parallels with the 2012 Nirbhaya case. That journey, the bench said, called into question whether law-enforcement mechanisms had made meaningful progress in the decade since. ### Across the Region
**Eight dead in Thailand floods since mid-September: CNA**
Thailand’s Department of Disaster Prevention and Mitigation said Monday that eight people have died in flooding since September 16: five in Sa Kaeo, two in Chanthaburi and one in Saraburi. Not including Bangkok, more than 500,000 households and 1.7 million people across 27 provinces have been affected, the department said, with a further 52,000 households hit in the capital. Bangkok declared a city-wide disaster on Saturday after almost 48 hours of heavy rain; the department said no deaths have been officially reported there and that water levels had stabilised. Government spokesman Ekkapob Pianpises said some Bangkok areas still faced high water even as drainage let more people travel, and schools have been closed with people urged to work from home. **Record high support for New Zealand Greens boosts chance of centre-left coalition, poll shows: The Straits Times**
A 1News Verian poll published Monday put New Zealand’s Green Party at 16 percent, its highest support ever recorded, with National and Labour each at 28 percent ahead of a general election set for November 7. The poll suggested Labour, the Greens, Te Pati Maori and the Opportunity Party could govern with 65 seats, against 59 for National, ACT and New Zealand First. Labour’s 28 percent was its lowest since February 2024, down 2 points from August, and National’s was its lowest since 2021. The survey of 1,004 voters ran from September 23 to 27, with 13 percent undecided or declining to say. Reuters reported the result remains too close to call in a system that favours coalitions. Prime Minister Christopher Luxon has previously said his National Party was unlikely to work with Opportunity because of its tax policies. ### Both Sides: Should Kochi reimburse Samridhi for the Indira Canteen?
Samridhi@Kochi, the Kochi Corporation’s budget eatery, is again in a dispute between the ruling United Democratic Front and the opposition Left Democratic Front over who should cover subsidy gaps. The Left says the Indira Canteen opened at Edappally in March 2026 is being supplied with food and staff from Samridhi, and that more than six months later it has run up a liability of more than 25 lakh rupees.

V.A. Sreejith, the Left’s parliamentary party leader, says subsidised meals were served on an assurance that the spending difference would be reimbursed, and that the corporation has not paid. He says the canteen does about 65,000 rupees of business a day against spending of about 1 lakh rupees, that Kudumbashree staff salaries are slipping past the 10th of each month, and that a second Indira Canteen is planned at the Vyttila Mobility Hub. He says corporate social responsibility money has dried up after the launch allocation, the corporation cannot meet the dues from its own resources, and its plan fund has been cut. He says the Left will launch a mass protest to protect Samridhi and the livelihoods of the Kudumbashree women who work with it.

Mayor V.K. Minimol says the Left is shedding crocodile tears. She says it left subsidy arrears of more than 2 crore rupees when the United Democratic Front took office. She says that when the then Left government discontinued the subsidy for Janakeeya Hotels in 2023, hurting Samridhi, the previous council did not write to the government seeking restoration. She also says 1 crore rupees in corporate funding promised for a second Samridhi unit launched in Fort Kochi was never mobilised.

Both sides accept that subsidy money tied to these budget meals is outstanding and that Samridhi’s finances are under strain. ### Progress Watch
**A nearly extinct bird is making a comeback with Cincinnati Zoo's help: 91.7 WVXU**
Until 2024 the sihek, or Guam kingfisher, had been considered extinct in the wild for 40 years. Nine birds released that year on Palmyra Atoll, about 1,000 miles south of Honolulu, are still doing well, Cody Sowers of the Cincinnati Zoo’s bird team told WVXU, though none have hatched eggs and reared young. One of three Cincinnati birds in that first group laid a first egg on the atoll last year. Ten chicks raised in an international recovery program, including one born at the Cincinnati Zoo, are candidates for release after quarantine at Disney’s Animal Kingdom, and as many as eight could be selected. The birds are native to Guam and were nearly wiped out by the invasive brown tree snake; Guam remains unsuitable for return because that snake population is still high. Source: [wvxu.org](https://www.wvxu.org/environment/2026-09-28/cincinnati-zoo-guam-kingfisher-sihek)
