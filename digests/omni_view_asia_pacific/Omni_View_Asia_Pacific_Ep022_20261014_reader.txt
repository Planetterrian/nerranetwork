Ho Chi Minh City held the rites on October fourteenth for people still missing from the wars with France and the United States.

The Straits Times reports that tens of thousands stood along the streets, some with white flowers, some praying, while buses carried flag-covered caskets out to a cemetery for fallen soldiers.

The military says the graves, individual and mass, were in Le Thi Rieng Park, and the fighters died when US-backed forces crushed a communist uprising in nineteen sixty-eight.

Deputy mayor Nguyen Manh Cuong said the city will not overlook any opportunity or method to keep searching for fallen comrades.

He also said the war's ferocity, and the years since, mean most of those recovered in this phase still have no name and no hometown on record.

State media named one exception: squad leader Huynh Van Quen, who fought in nineteen sixty-eight, matched in July by a scrap of paper with his name and by DNA from his relatives.

Deputy Prime Minister Pham Thi Thanh Tra called the goal of recovering seven thousand sets of remains by July twenty twenty-seven a political task of special importance, and a command from the heart.

Up to one point two million Vietnamese soldiers are estimated killed between nineteen forty-five and nineteen seventy-five.

Two hundred thirty thousand graves nationwide hold unidentified soldiers.

More than fifteen hundred people are searching old battlefields in Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia, while other teams sample those graves and run DNA tests on eighteen thousand samples of remains.
