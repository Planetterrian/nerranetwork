> **A 63,701-vote BJP lead in Assam opens counting that tests Bengal and Tamil Nadu.**

**What You Need to Know:** A 63,701-vote lead for the BJP in Assam’s Nagaon seat opened Friday’s counting of bypolls that test the new governments in West Bengal and Tamil Nadu. Early rounds also put the ruling AINRC ahead in Puducherry and TVK ahead in one Tamil Nadu seat. None of those figures is a final result.

### Lead
**Bypoll results LIVE: BJP widens lead in West Bengal; TVK leads in Tamil Nadu: The Hindu**
Counting began on Friday, October 9, 2026, for bypolls to five Assembly seats in Tamil Nadu, West Bengal and Puducherry, and for Assam’s Nagaon Lok Sabha seat. The Hindu reported that BJP candidate Devasish Sharma led AIUDF’s Mohammed Badruddin Ajmal by 63,701 votes in Nagaon, with Congress candidate Sibamoni Bora third. In Tamil Nadu’s Dharapuram (SC) seat, the ninth round had TVK’s P. Sathyabama on 24,047, AIADMK’s K. Bhanumathi on 21,935, DMK’s S. Suganya on 19,972, Naam Tamilar Katchi’s M. Karthika on 1,891 and the CPI’s P. Lakshmanan on 442. In Puducherry’s Thattanchavady, ruling AINRC candidate Ashok Anand held a clear, consistent lead over the Congress and DMK candidates, and party workers began celebrating. In West Bengal, counting in Nandigram and Rejinagar started at 8 a.m. under tight security, the first electoral test since the BJP took office in May with 207 of 294 seats, a majority these bypolls will not change. Tuesday’s turnout was 80.89 percent in Nandigram and 80.08 percent in Rejinagar; the Election Commission called the polling more or less peaceful, while opposition parties alleged intimidation and demanded fresh polling, which the BJP rejected. ### Both Sides: Should Seoul send its national security adviser to Washington?
Seoul is weighing whether to send National Security Adviser Wi Sung-lac to Washington after the National Intelligence Service said on Thursday it had been excluded from a June meeting with the United States and Japan on North Korean cyber threats. A news report said Washington had asked that the NIS be left out. The NIS itself downplayed the incident. The case for sending Wi is that the snub has raised worry about the alliance and that someone senior should go and settle it. A senior ruling-bloc insider told Yonhap that various issues have already affected overall ties, that it is not good for the relationship to be shaky, and that problems in the intelligence sector are never desirable. That worry follows an earlier break: after Unification Minister Chung Dong-young disclosed a North Korean uranium enrichment facility in Kusong in March, saying he had shared only what was already public, Washington restricted Seoul’s access to some North Korea-related intelligence, in what Yonhap described as an apparent protest. Some have asked the presidential office to dispatch Wi to defuse the friction. The case against a trip now is practical. A ruling-bloc official told Yonhap there appear to be no such plans, while saying the situation still calls for other options. Separately, the industry ministry dismissed a local report that U.S. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick had voiced distrust of the NIS during talks on strategic investment. Both readings start from one admitted fact: the NIS was not in that June meeting. No date has been set for a Wi visit. The ruling Democratic Party says a U.S. visa for former leader Representative Jung Chung-rae remains under review, with no final decision, after he had planned a parliamentary audit of Seoul’s diplomatic missions. Senior spokesperson Park Sung-joon said he hopes the two governments resolve that quickly. Source: [en.yna.co.kr](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20261009003500315)

### Progress Watch
**Forum SG Waqa welcomes US$13.3m in new Pacific climate finance pledges ahead of COP31: Post Courier**
New pledges have lifted commitments to the Pacific Resilience Facility to US$190 million, Pacific Islands Forum Secretary General Baron Divavesi Waqa said. Fiji’s US$10 million, announced by Prime Minister Sitiveni Rabuka at the Pacific Pre-COP31 in Nadi, plus Denmark’s US$2.3 million and the Netherlands’ US$1 million, make up the latest US$13.3 million. The facility’s initial capitalisation target is US$500 million, with a longer-term goal of US$1.5 billion. Waqa said the money shows Pacific leadership, with Pacific priorities and ownership at the centre. Forum leaders reaffirmed the facility as the region’s top climate investment priority at their September meeting in Palau.
