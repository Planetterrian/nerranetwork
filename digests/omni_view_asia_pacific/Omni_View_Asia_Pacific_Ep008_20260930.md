> **13.6 percent more reference weight resets India's passenger-car fuel limits from 2027.**

**What You Need to Know:** India's government has notified CAFE-3 fuel and carbon limits that raise the reference car weight by about 13.6 percent and drop a draft break for the lightest petrol cars, covering new passenger vehicles from April 2027 through March 2032. A Bathurst childcare centre with 126 confirmed breaches will shut, and Seoul's spy agency has classed Kim Jong Un as extremely obese. Malaysia has started moving the first of 5,000 Myanmar nationals it says agreed to go back.

### Lead
**Government notifies new CAFE-3 norms for passenger vehicles: The Hindu**
The Power Ministry said on Wednesday that the government has notified Corporate Average Fuel Economy, or CAFE-3, norms setting fuel-efficiency and carbon-emission limits for passenger vehicles. Those limits run from April 1, 2027, to March 31, 2032, and apply to new passenger vehicles manufactured or imported for sale in India. They cover hatchbacks, sedans, special utility vehicles and MPVs with seating for eight passengers other than the driver. The final norms drop a draft concession for petrol cars under 909 kilograms and lift the reference weight from 1,082 kilograms to 1,229 kilograms. The revised target line is flatter, with relatively softer targets for lighter vehicles and greater fuel-efficiency requirements for heavier ones. Super credits, described as volume derogation multipliers, apply to battery electrics, range-extended electrics, plug-in hybrids, strong hybrids and flex-fuel vehicles when fleet-average emissions are calculated. ### Across the Region
**Childcare centre forced to close after recording 126 breaches: ABC News Australia**
The NSW Early Learning Commission has ordered Jenny's Kindergarten and Early Learning Centre in Bathurst to close permanently on October 13. Commissioner Daryl Currie said the provider, C & J Corporation Pty Ltd, was given two weeks' notice after persistent non-compliance despite support. Since opening in 2014 the centre has had 14 regulatory visits, 41 compliance actions and 126 confirmed breaches. An investigation began in March 2025 after three children left and a passer-by saw them near a road; the regulator said there is no current evidence a child suffered direct harm in a specific incident. In August the provider was fined $9,240 over unsafe furniture and equipment and exposed electrical cords, alongside concerns about sharp objects in garden beds and a staff personal device. **North Korean leader ‘extremely obese’: Seoul spy agency: The Straits Times**
Lawmaker Youn Kun-young said on Wednesday that Seoul's spy agency classifies Kim Jong Un as extremely obese, weighing over 140 kilograms, and at high risk of cardiovascular disease. The same assessment says he has shown no difficulty with physical activity, including running up stairs, and that a dedicated medical team has seen no signs of health problems. His health is watched because a sudden death could raise questions over succession and stability in the nuclear-armed state. He is described as a heavy smoker who has long been obese; his father Kim Jong Il and grandfather Kim Il Sung, also obese heavy smokers, died of heart attacks. In 2020 he missed a commemoration of his grandfather's birthday and was out of public view for about 20 days, which fed rumours of serious illness after surgery. ### The Region and the World
**Malaysia begins Myanmar repatriations despite warnings from UN and rights groups: The Guardian**
Malaysia on Tuesday began sending back about 1,500 Myanmar nationals, the first of 5,000 the government says agreed to voluntary repatriation from detention centres. AFP reporters saw three Myanmar navy ships leave Lumut naval base after the group was processed and taken by bus, under police escort, to the Majlis Perbandaran Manjung stadium. The interior ministry said the returns address a legacy of foreign asylum-seekers, 95 percent of them from Myanmar, which it said has imposed a financial burden, serious social crises and security threats. UN human rights chief Volker Turk urged an immediate halt, saying nobody should be forcibly returned to Myanmar given the abuses there and the risk of more violence on return. Neither government has clarified how many Rohingya are in the first group; the UN says Rohingya are two-thirds of the 190,000 Myanmar refugees and asylum seekers in Malaysia. ### Both Sides: Should Western Australia take Bullsbrook as a prison?
Western Australia has not decided whether the Bullsbrook Resilience Centre should become a jail. Corrective Services Commissioner Brad Royce said on Wednesday that he is always looking for more space and would be interested if the site became available. The case for considering it is crowding. In June, Inspector of Custodial Services Eamon Ryan issued a show-cause notice covering Hakea, Casuarina and Melaleuca, the first time such a notice covered three prisons at once. Some prisons have been triple-bunking inmates, and the prison officers' union has warned that the system is a tinderbox. The state is already planning to expand Acacia Prison by 480 beds by 2029. Standing with Royce, Corrective Services Minister Tony Buti said nothing is off the cards and that the government will look at Bullsbrook if it becomes available.

The case against comes from nearby residents, most of whom oppose the plan. In August a petition with 165 signatures was tabled in Parliament urging the Cook government not to proceed. In a June letter to those residents, then minister Paul Papalia said the Department of Justice had begun a feasibility study. Royce said the Resilience Centre is a Commonwealth asset and, at this stage, not for him. Its first use this year for the purpose it was built for — quarantining returning travellers — was housing six Australian and New Zealand passengers from the MV Hondius during a hantavirus outbreak.

Both the commissioner and the residents are dealing with the same unsettled fact: the 400-million-dollar facility is still Commonwealth property. Buti said that if the government reaches a solution, it will announce it.
