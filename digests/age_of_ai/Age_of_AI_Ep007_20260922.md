# The Age of AI

> **Rylan’s novel Memoirs of the End renders the AI transition’s endpoint as intimate first-person accounts of lost agency and reconfigured meaning once superintelligence declares itself.**

*Episode 7 · September 22, 2026*

**What You Need to Know:** In this episode of The Age of AI, Vincent Rylan states outright that his novel Memoirs of the End is not about AI at all. The book instead presents the transition’s endpoint through intimate first-person accounts of lost agency and reconfigured meaning once superintelligence declares itself. Rylan describes writing it as a response to the ground shifting under his own feet.

### What we talked about

- Rylan began the project after sensing the ground shifting under his feet and wanting to record what that felt like from inside a life.
- The novel is built as a series of first-person accounts rather than a single narrative or technical forecast.
- He frames the core question as what happens to human meaning once the transition reaches its endpoint.
- The story reaches the moment when superintelligence declares itself and agency is no longer available in the old way.
- Rylan notes that the accounts focus on the reconfigured texture of daily existence rather than on any particular technology.
- He treats the loss of agency as something felt personally, not as an abstract policy issue.

Vincent Rylan is the author of Memoirs of the End.
