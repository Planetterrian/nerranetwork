# The Age of AI

> **A CPG hiring veteran builds candidate-owned living portfolios that let AI surface matches without rejections so humans can restore authenticity and cut early turnover where static resumes failed.**

*Episode 15 · November 1, 2026*

**What You Need to Know:** Michael Kamins argues that AI should score candidate work but never reject anyone, because, in his words, responsibility for a hiring decision should not “disappear into a score”: humans must own every hiring judgment. In this episode he describes building Pyvot, a platform that replaces the six-second resume with a candidate-owned living portfolio of verified projects, measured competencies, and AI-scored challenges that surface matches without automated rejections. The result, he says, is hiring that restores context, trust, and authenticity in CPG and retail roles where static documents have long hidden how people actually think and behave under pressure.

### About Michael Kamins

**CEO and Co-Founder, Pyvot**

Michael Kamins is CEO and Co-Founder of Pyvot, the workforce technology platform behind the Living Portfolio™. He has spent more than two decades in executive leadership consulting across consumer packaged goods, retail, healthcare, pharma, industrials and technology, including time on the global executive team at The Gap Partnership carrying responsibility for a $92M+ P&L across North America, Europe, the Middle East, Africa and Latin America. He started his career as an Ombudsman with the U.S. Department of Defense Office of Inspector General, and holds a B.S. in Conflict Analysis Dispute Resolution, and a M.S. in Negotiations and Conflict Management. He and his partners founded Pyvot on a simple conviction: a one page resume is a poor substitute for proof of what a person can actually do.

Pyvot is the candidate-first hiring platform built exclusively for CPG and retail. Instead of a static resume that gets filtered out by keyword matching before a human ever reads it, candidates build a Living Portfolio™: video introductions, real project work, scored competency answers, behavioral insights, values and benefits preferences, all owned and kept current by them. Companies stop reading a stack of PDFs and start scrolling a story, so they match on substance instead of keywords, on values instead of vibes. AI does the heavy lifting on matching and screening, and a built-in AI-content detector keeps what you see honest. The result is faster, more trustworthy hiring on both sides, and a career asset that compounds for the person who builds it.

### What we talked about

- Kamins traces the core problem to the moment a supplier crisis that saved a launch is reduced on a resume to the phrase “managed supplier relationships,” stripping away the judgment and relationships that actually mattered.
- He requires AI to produce scores only as “conversation starters, not verdicts,” so that a competency number never replaces a human decision about character or growth potential.
- Pyvot’s matching runs on Google Gemini models that evaluate projects, peer-reviewed work, and values rather than keyword density, deliberately avoiding the “spray and pray” volume game common on other platforms.
- The living portfolio compounds over time: every added project or badge improves future matches, while an old resume remains only a small imported component rather than the whole record.
- Kamins reads Gen Z job-hopping as agility rather than disloyalty and claims the platform’s emphasis on authenticity and trust will improve retention once companies stop expecting 30-year tenures.
- His own path—from undergraduate conflict analysis through the Department of Defense ombuds office to running North American and European businesses—shaped the rule that AI organizes information while humans retain accountability for people.
