# The Age of AI

> **A mining engineer turned AI builder weighs how platforms guiding career choice and soft skills can keep human judgment central when the models risk automating the decisions they claim to augment.**

*Episode 8 · September 23, 2026*

**What You Need to Know:** Sameer Ranjan, a mining engineer turned AI builder, described how his platform normalizes personality and soft-skill scores inside separate cultural baselines instead of forcing a single universal scale. He stressed that the system supplies diagnostic signals only and leaves every hiring, retention, or assignment decision to a human. The exchange tested whether quantified soft skills can guide careers while keeping human judgment in front of the models.

### What we talked about

- Ranjan cited an internal hospital-system study in which attrition fell from 22 % to 12 % once managers received clarity on communication gaps rather than skill gaps.
- The patent US 12198213B2, assigned to Catenate Corp, quantifies soft skills and personality to recommend courses, colleges, and careers on the premise that personality shapes long-term outcomes more than job skills alone.
- To block gaming, the recommendation engine resets the question set for each user after the initial baseline is established, so no fixed right-or-wrong answers exist.
- Cultural baselines are built from open-source data plus native-language assessments; a score of four out of ten on communication is interpreted relative to the local distribution rather than against a global mean.
- The platform never frames problems or issues directives; it only flags gaps such as communication shortfalls, after which humans decide what action, if any, to take.
- Baseline values are treated as moving targets: new assessment data are compared with prior standard deviations, and shifts are reviewed by multiple human advisors before any update is accepted.
- Ranjan’s explicit design rule is that the AI remains a “decision-aiding tool, not a decisioning tool,” with follow-up assessments used to check whether any human-chosen intervention produced measurable change.

Sameer Ranjan is Co-Founder & CTO of Catenate, the company behind the MayaMaya platform for workplace personality and soft-skills analysis.
