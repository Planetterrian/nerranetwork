### The Teardown: One Laser, Many Wavelengths
Wavelength-division multiplexing puts several colours of light on one fibre so a data-centre link carries more traffic without another strand. The conventional build gives each colour its own laser, plus a driver and a control loop. Channel count and component count move together. Add a colour and you add another laser to power, tune, and watch fail.

An optical frequency comb breaks that pairing. One source produces many evenly spaced wavelengths. On a spectrum analyzer those lines look like the teeth of a comb. Data Center Knowledge reports that AI data centres are scaling toward 16 or more wavelengths per fibre, which is where a laser-per-channel build gets heavy: each added colour brings another laser, driver, and control loop, and with them more power, cost, and failure points.

Marcello Girardi, CEO and co-founder of Solinide, told Data Center Knowledge that four wavelengths is a solved problem and that arrays solve it well. He said the difficulty starts at eight, and that by 16 the component count is what limits you. His account of the comb is specific: one pump laser and one silicon nitride die produce all the wavelengths at once, on a grid set by the resonator itself, so the hardware does not grow as channels are added.

Frank Smyth, founder and CTO of Pilot Photonics, told Data Center Knowledge that comb lasers potentially pack many more wavelengths much tighter together without fear of interference. Spacing comes from the resonator, not from a rack of independently locked lasers drifting toward each other.

The same article draws a line on maturity. Comb sources have circulated in telecom research for years, but the co-packaged optics many of these designs envision remain closer to pilot than to volume production. That is the flip. At four wavelengths, an array already does the job, and a single pump plus still-pilot co-packaged optics is a harder bet. Toward 16 wavelengths per fibre, the array's part count — laser, driver, and lock loop per colour — is the constraint the comb is built to remove, provided the single source and its packaging actually reach volume. Source: [datacenterknowledge.com](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/networking/the-role-of-optical-frequency-comb-generators-in-ai-data-centers)
