> **TSMC is studying two paths into 3D DRAM Nvidia wants made in-house.**

**What You Need to Know:** TSMC is studying two paths into 3D DRAM after a request from Nvidia: build a new fab, or buy a plant and convert it. The Information’s November 6 report, carried by several outlets, describes cell-stacked memory, not an extension of the HBM4 base-die work TSMC already does with SK hynix. No investment decision has been made, and TSMC says its focus remains logic foundry.

### Top Story
The Information reported on November 6 that TSMC is considering manufacturing 3D DRAM, stacking memory cells vertically inside a die, and igor’sLAB says the push followed a request from Nvidia. Hsu Kuo-chin, a TSMC vice president in Advanced Packaging who previously worked at Micron in Taiwan, is said to be leading work on production concepts. The options described are a new fab or an acquired plant converted for memory; Seoul Economic Daily reports that no decision has been made on whether to invest, how much to spend, or when output would begin. TSMC told The Information it does not comment on market rumors and stays focused on logic foundry work, including the phrasing that it remains “focused on our core business at all times” and “the trusted technology and capacity provider” of logic chips. Nvidia did not reply, and Reuters could not independently verify the account. This is not the April 19, 2024 SK hynix collaboration, in which TSMC’s role is the HBM4 base die and CoWoS packaging, with HBM4 mass production planned for 2026 rather than recorded as a finished result. Cell-stacked 3D DRAM forms the cells in one die; HBM stacks finished DRAM dies. BigGo Finance, citing Counterpoint Research, puts global DRAM revenue in the third quarter of 2026 up 369 percent year on year, with Samsung at 38 percent, SK hynix at 29 percent, Micron at 21 percent, and CXMT at 9 percent, and cites TrendForce expecting conventional DRAM contract prices up another 10 to 15 percent quarter on quarter in the fourth quarter. TechPowerUp notes Micron has decided not to bring 4F² DRAM to market and is on a 3D DRAM path; BigGo cites Micron’s completed March purchase of Powerchip’s P5 fab in Tongluo, Miaoli, for 1.8 billion dollars, announced in January, as the kind of acquire-and-convert move now being discussed for TSMC. ### The Teardown: When Attention Is Sixty Percent of the Step
On a single Nvidia B200, the NVIDIA Technical Blog puts attention at about 60 percent of diffusion-transformer GPU kernel time for FLUX 3 Image Klein at 2048 by 2048 under NVFP4, with vanilla BF16 attention, for both the Base checkpoint and the distilled one. Generating the image is a loop. A text encoder, here Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct, conditions the job. A diffusion transformer then refines a noisy latent, step after step, and an autoencoder turns the latent into pixels, or encodes a reference image when the job is an edit. The loop, not the final decode, is where the clock goes. Higher resolution makes the attention matrices larger, so the same step count costs more. Blackwell’s NVFP4 format cuts the weight and activation traffic of the matrix multiplies that feed that attention. TensorRT LLM VisualGen then fuses those multiplies with their epilogues, including SwiGLU and the query-key-value projections that also apply normalization and rotary embeddings, through FlashInfer PrimsTS kernels.

The blog’s own ladder shows how the gains stack, and where they stop stacking cleanly. Against uncompiled BF16 on one B200 at 2048 by 2048, the combined optimizations reach up to 3.46 times faster text-to-image and 4.45 times faster image-to-image for the Base model. NVFP4 alone, before those extra layers, is up to 1.62 times faster text-to-image and 1.58 times faster image-to-image than BF16. The latency table that follows uses NVFP4, vanilla attention, and torch.compile, averaged over 10 requests after two warmup requests. Base runs 50 denoising steps with a classifier-free guidance scale of 3.5, which means a guided pass and an unguided pass. The distilled Klein runs 16 steps and drops guidance. That is why a format win and a step-count win are different levers: one shrinks each pass, the other deletes passes. Pinned NVFP4 PrimsTS kernels add only 1.02 to 1.10 times on text-to-image versus the autotuned TensorRT LLM NVFP4 GEMM, so kernel fusion is a small slice once the format is already NVFP4. Quantized attention is larger. VC-Attention FP8 with a repair budget of 0.005 reaches 1.18 times on both Base and distilled at 2048 by 2048, with LPIPS of 0.086 on Base and 0.151 on distilled. Sol-Attention at tau of 1.0 with 12 initial dense steps gives 1.30 times faster denoising plus decoding on Base; holding 16 dense steps for fidelity drops that to 1.24 times. Cache-DiT step caching nearly halves Base latency at both 1024 by 1024 and 2048 by 2048, and reaches 1.94 times at the higher resolution with an LPIPS of 0.033. Across eight B200 GPUs, classifier-free guidance parallelism and Ulysses parallelism cut median text-to-image latency from 14.90 seconds to 3.00 seconds on Base and from 2.73 seconds to 1.08 seconds on distilled, with denoising speedups of 6.01 times and 4.44 times. The model itself was trained on a GB200 NVL72.

The 12-step Sol-Attention schedule stops being the right schedule when the job needs the 16 dense steps that pull Base’s denoising-plus-decode gain from 1.30 times down to 1.24 times, or when an LPIPS of 0.151 on the distilled checkpoint, under the same 1.18 times VC-Attention setting that scores 0.086 on Base, is already past what the edit can accept. Source: [developer.nvidia.com](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/speed-up-image-generation-with-flux-3-image-klein-on-nvidia-blackwell-gpus/)
