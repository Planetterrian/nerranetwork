> **AMD's MI355X cut p99 latency about 42 percent versus B200, roughly doubling RAG user headroom.**

**What You Need to Know:** AMD's Instinct MI355X posted about 42 percent lower p99 latency than NVIDIA's B200 in Signal65 RAG testing hosted via TensorWave, with roughly twice the concurrent-user headroom before the evaluated SLA broke. Intel's Core Ultra 400-series Nova Lake platform has cleared USB4 and PCIe 5.0 compliance listings as a launch nears. A planned Los Angeles cable landing station, a cooling unit that drops evaporative water, and a Phoenix office purchase sit on the build side of the same week.

### Top Story
AMD pointed to Signal65 retrieval-augmented generation testing, hosted via TensorWave, of Instinct MI355X against NVIDIA's B200. In that evaluation the MI355X delivered about 42 percent lower p99 latency. It also delivered roughly twice the concurrent-user headroom before breaching the evaluated SLA. AMD published the result as a completed test comparison, not as a new accelerator shipment. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/AMD/status/2105019808509054986)

**Intel "Nova Lake" Launch Nears as Platform Passes USB4 and PCIe 5.0 Compliance Testing: TechPowerUp**
TechPowerUp reports that Intel's Core Ultra 400-series Nova Lake CPUs are nearing launch after the platform passed USB and PCIe compliance testing, which the outlet treats as confirmation that advertised specifications are working. USB-IF lists Nova Lake-H device IDs D331 and D333 as certified for USB4. USB-IF also lists ID 6E6E for the Nova Lake PCH-S desktop chipset, compliant with USB 3.2 Gen 2. PCI-SIG shows 900-series chipset IDs 6E38-6E3F, 6E30-6E35, and 6E40-6E47, added in April. In August, device IDs D461, D465, and D467-D46A certified a PCIe 5.0 x16 root complex; TechPowerUp says those IDs are likely Nova Lake-S CPUs or a mobile variant. The outlet calls compliance verification the final stage a platform usually clears before launch is finalized. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/353240/intel-nova-lake-launch-nears-as-platform-passes-usb4-and-pcie-5-0-compliance-testing)

**PCIe 8.0 Over UCIe 3.0: Bringing A Proven Protocol Into The Chiplet Era: Semiconductor Engineering**
Semiconductor Engineering describes PCIe 8.0 carried over UCIe 3.0 as a way to keep a familiar protocol when a design splits across chiplets. UCIe standardizes die-to-die communication inside a package. That path lets PCIe traffic cross chiplet boundaries with interoperability over the standardized interconnect. Source: [semiengineering.com](https://semiengineering.com/pcie-8-0-over-ucie-3-0-bringing-a-proven-rrotocol-into-the-chiplet-era/)

### The Teardown: Moving the job instead of the megawatt
Carbon intensity on a data-centre feeder is an hourly quantity, not a nameplate on the building. Take the flexible queue apart and the lever is which work is allowed to wait. Data Center Knowledge describes carbon-aware computing as scheduling compute-intensive but time-flexible tasks when grid carbon intensity is lower, or when on-site renewable or stored energy is available. The tasks it names are data backups, batch analytics, AI model training, report generation, and non-urgent patching. The comparison is emissions from running the workload at one time or one place versus another, then the option that cuts emissions inside the rules the operator already has.

Operators have to know which jobs can move without breaking service-level agreements, latency requirements, data-residency constraints, or change-control policies. A backup, a batch analytics pass, a training run, a report, or a non-urgent patch might move. The grid side of that comparison moves because generation moves. Solar production peaks in daylight. Wind output varies by season and by hour. Some regions sit on geothermal, hydro, or nuclear baseload. Demand, transmission constraints, and market dispatch then change what is actually serving the next kilowatt-hour, so the emissions intensity of that kilowatt-hour can swing significantly inside a single day.

The policy that falls out of the comparison is a filter on incremental demand. Flexible load is pointed at cleaner windows so it does not force extra consumption in higher-emitting hours. Data Center Knowledge puts that result next to long-term renewable procurement and efficiency programs: a practical path to lower Scope 2 emissions, and a way to show progress toward 24/7 carbon-free energy objectives while regulatory expectations and stakeholder scrutiny of data-centre electricity demand keep rising. The tradeoff flips the moment a cleaner hour would breach an SLA, a latency bound, a residency rule, or a change window. The job stays on the dirtier hour, and the carbon accounting follows the promise the service already made.
