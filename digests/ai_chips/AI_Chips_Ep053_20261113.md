# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **Six hundred megawatts of Texas power has a hyperscaler check, and Fermi's biggest lease is dead.**

**What You Need to Know:** An unnamed investment-grade hyperscaler is funding 600 megawatts of power at Fermi's planned Project Matador campus in Texas, while the TensorWave lease is terminated. Nscale's new prospectus puts active GPUs at 50,000 and active and contracted capacity at 1.37 gigawatts. OpenAI has told lawmakers it will back bills meant to tighten advanced-chip exports to China.

### Top Story
Fermi has a deal with an unnamed investment-grade hyperscaler to develop 600 megawatts of power at planned Project Matador in Texas, with that power to start coming online next year. The customer is providing $200 million up front and $15 million under an early construction and site-development agreement, and the two sides are finalizing a $90 million purchase of structural steel and long-lead building materials Fermi had already secured. No lease is signed; Fermi says the client is making financial and development commitments ahead of a lease so power stays on the customer's schedule. The same update ends the August 2026 TensorWave lease, which had been set for up to 650 megawatts and carried AMD credit support, so Matador is again without a tenant. Fermi still describes a possible 17-gigawatt campus near Amarillo on Texas Tech University system land, and chief executive Lee McIntire said converting the current pipeline into leases would fill 2.2 gigawatts of capacity the company already counts. For the three months to the end of September, Fermi reported a $141.6 million net loss, and debt stood at $958.4 million. Source: [datacenterdynamics.com](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/fermi-does-600mw-power-deal-with-hyperscaler-but-tensorwave-lease-is-officially-dead/)

### Silicon
**Kioxia and Dell Build a 157 PB Rack With 640 SSDs: TechPowerUp**
Kioxia and Dell have put 157 petabytes of raw flash in one 42U rack, running Dell ObjectScale on 16 PowerEdge R7725xd servers that together hold 640 of Kioxia's 245.76-terabyte LC9 SSDs. Each 2U server takes 40 E3.L drives, the same layout the two showed in May at 9.8 petabytes per server, so the 16 servers occupy 32U and leave 10U free. The LC9 parts are PCIe 5.0 NVMe drives on Kioxia's eighth-generation BiCS FLASH QLC with a 32-die stack; the full 245.76-terabyte capacity is only in the E3.L form factor. The 2.5-inch and E3.S versions top out at 122.88 terabytes, which would cut a rack built the same way to about 78.6 petabytes. Dell's George Papadopoulos said the point is to consolidate data into fewer systems and racks and cut cabling, power, and cooling. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/354767/kioxia-and-dell-build-a-157-pb-rack-with-640-ssds)

**Nscale brings online two data centers and 25,000 GPUs in 2.5 months: Data Center Dynamics**
Nscale has brought two more data centers and about 25,000 GPUs online since August 31, taking active GPUs to 50,000 of a planned 461,000. The August 31 prospectus had listed 25,000 active GPUs, about five percent of the goal; active sites are now seven, up from five that then carried 55 megawatts, with ten more in the pipeline. Active and contracted capacity is 1.37 gigawatts: 1 gigawatt at owned sites, 200 megawatts at leased sites, and 165 megawatts in colocation, with what the company calls a line of sight to 10 gigawatts. The new prospectus reports full-year 2025 revenue of $33 million, up 73 percent from $19.1 million in 2024, after an earlier prospectus had put the six months to the end of June 2026 at $140.6 million of revenue and losses of $1.02 billion, against $368.9 million a year earlier. Active total contract value is $6.4 billion, and active plus contracted TCV is $105.8 billion, compared with $0.5 billion and $38.0 billion at December 31, 2025, under long-term take-or-pay contracts tied to those 461,000 GPUs. Anthropic has exercised a right to more compute in Building 2 at the Monarch campus in West Virginia, on top of the $45 billion agreement signed in August; Nscale expects to fund that build with external financing and operating cash flow, and a missed GPU timeline can let Anthropic end that tranche without liability. Source: [datacenterdynamics.com](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nscale-brings-online-two-data-centers-and-25000-gpus-in-25-months/)

**Labor Shortages Threaten Data Center Schedules: Data Center Knowledge**
JLL's 2027 US Construction Perspective forecasts national tender prices up 4.2 percent in 2027, from 3.1 percent in 2026, above a pre-2020 norm of 2 to 3 percent, and that projection is for construction broadly, not data centers alone. The South is expected to add more nonresidential construction growth than any other US region and to carry the heaviest workload per worker by 2028, driven by data centers, power projects, and public civil works. JLL's Matthew Giddings said the cost of a crew runs past wages into overtime, travel, lodging, and relocation, and that extra crews can raise cost without raising output if design, materials, and sequencing are not coordinated. He said utility crews for lines and substations and the electrical trades that energize a hall are not interchangeable, and that JLL has seen halls substantially complete, with power available, still months from operation because commissioning staff were not there. Prefabricated electrical assemblies and standard cooling plant cut on-site labor, but the manufacturers of those assemblies have their own staffing limits; Giddings said repeatable campus designs do more than one-off prefabrication, and that overtime does not move an opening date if the constraint is a transformer, utility energization, or commissioning. Source: [datacenterknowledge.com](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-construction/labor-shortages-threaten-data-center-schedules)

### Supply Chain & Policy
**OpenAI backs US bills to tighten AI chip export controls to China: Chosunbiz**
OpenAI has told US lawmakers it will support a bipartisan package to restrict China's access to advanced AI chips and chipmaking tools, after months of review, Chosunbiz reported from Bloomberg. The three bills would limit exports of advanced AI semiconductors to China, block indirect shipments of Nvidia AI chips, and limit China's access to advanced equipment from ASML; lawmakers want them inside the annual National Defense Authorization Act and passed by year-end. Nvidia and related trade groups have opposed tighter controls. Jensen Huang, who had opposed stricter China rules, resigned on November 12, and the report says that has weakened Nvidia's position on Capitol Hill. The outcome is still open: Congress and the administration are negotiating the text, and a Trump veto signal could collapse the effort. ### The Teardown: One Pass Across the Wafer
A line-scan camera does not frame a wafer. It holds one row of pixels in the optical field, and motion turns each readout into the next strip of the map. Semiconductor Digest, covering SEMICON Europa, reports Teledyne Vision Solutions demonstrating that geometry on a 300-millimetre wafer with the Linea ML 32K, which Teledyne calls the world's first true 32K line-scan camera, inspecting the wafer in a single pass at 10 micrometre resolution.

The row has to span the diameter, or the tool is back to stitching. A second swipe adds travel, an overlap band, and a seam the defect software must not call a crack. Teledyne's width claim rests on two figures that sit on different planes of the same lens. The Linea ML 32K is specified at 32K resolution with 2.5 micrometre pixels. The Munich demonstration inspects at 10 micrometres on the wafer. The 2.5 micrometre number is the photosite. The 10 micrometre number is the patch of silicon that photosite represents in this setup. When the wafer-side patch is coarser than the photosite, the lens is folding a wide strip onto that long sensor, and a 32K row is the sample count Teledyne is using so the strip covers a 300-millimetre wafer without a second index.

Pace lives on the same row. The camera is rated at imaging speeds of up to 150 kilohertz, so it can emit up to 150,000 lines a second while the stage moves. In this demonstration each new line is one step of the 10 micrometre grid. That rate is the budget for stage speed before pixels smear, and it is also the budget for the other modes Teledyne lists on the Linea platform: multiline operation, high dynamic range, and time-delay integration. Multiline and TDI spend successive readouts on the same feature so a faint scratch or a thin resist edge collects more signal before the image is committed. HDR holds a bright pad and a dark neighbourhood in one line. Those modes do not widen the sensor. They spend the 150 kilohertz. A line that must run at the full rate for throughput is not, in that same pass, banking a long TDI sum.

The demonstration walks in four steps, all of them Teledyne's figures. The wafer is 300 millimetres across. The inspection grid in the single-pass demo is 10 micrometres. One line is a 32K sensor whose photosites are 2.5 micrometres, so optical scale, not a second mechanical pass, is what fits the diameter into the row. That row is clocked at up to 150 kilohertz so the stage can keep feeding lines without stopping to reposition. Width from the 32K line, scale from the 10 micrometre grid, pace from the 150 kilohertz rating: that is the single-pass claim.

The Z3 on the same stand is a different instrument. Semiconductor Digest lists spatial resolution up to 1.25 micrometres, height repeatability up to 35 nanometres, and throughput up to two seconds per wafer. The jobs named for it are micro ball-grid arrays, bumps and pillars, through-glass and through-silicon vias, wafer-to-wafer bonding, flatness, coplanarity, and defect analysis, on a die, a wafer, or a stretch frame. A 10 micrometre line can show that a bump is missing or far from its pad. It cannot certify a height error at the Z3's 35-nanometre repeatability, because that error is a sliver of the lateral pixel.

Single-pass 32K imaging loses the job when the scrap signature is vertical: bump height, via depth, bond-line tilt, a coplanarity stack. Then the two-second Z3 measurement is the step that closes the lot, and another swipe of the line camera only reprints a map that was already wide enough. The demonstration is in Hall C2, Stand 136, at Messe München, November 10 through 13. Source: [semiconductor-digest.com](https://www.semiconductor-digest.com/teledyne-vision-solutions-demonstrates-single-pass-300mm-wafer-inspection/?utm_source=rss&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=teledyne-vision-solutions-demonstrates-single-pass-300mm-wafer-inspection)
