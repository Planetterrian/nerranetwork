# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **Applied Digital's $3.2 billion plan places 210 megawatts near Brookwood, Alabama.**

**What You Need to Know:** Applied Digital's $3.2 billion plan places 210 megawatts near Brookwood, Alabama. The campus is Delta Forge 2, with operations expected in 2028 on a 1,300-acre site. Vertiv is also buying Irish fluid-management firm King Environmental Services to extend liquid-cooling field work into EMEA.

### Top Story
Applied Digital has announced a 210-megawatt AI data center, Delta Forge 2, near Brookwood in Alabama’s Tuscaloosa County, and puts the project at $3.2 billion. The campus sits on 1,300 acres and spans 1.2 million square feet at full build-out, with operations expected to begin in 2028. It is set to support up to 1,000 construction jobs and 100 permanent roles. Applied Digital describes the customer as an investment-grade hyperscaler; in June it had said that customer was secured for a 210-megawatt center without naming this site. The company is committing $270 million in community benefits to the Tuscaloosa County Economic Development Authority over 20 years, alongside an estimated $131 million in tax revenue. Its contracted portfolio now spans five AI Factory campuses, totaling 1.4 gigawatts of critical IT load and approximately 2.15 gigawatts of grid-connected utility power. Source: [datacenterdynamics.com](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/applied-digital-reveals-32bn-delta-forge-2-ai-data-center-will-be-built-in-alabama/)

Micron’s Jeremy Werner, senior vice president and general manager of the Core Data Center Business Unit, says agentic AI runs on two compute stacks rather than one. ### Data Centres & Power
**Vertiv agrees to buy King Environmental Services: Data Center Dynamics**
Vertiv has agreed to acquire Ireland-based King Environmental Services, which provides fluid management, commissioning and load-testing for high-density, liquid-cooled data centers. The deal is meant to extend Vertiv’s thermal-management services into EMEA; the company already offers fluid management in North America. Last year Vertiv bought PurgeRite, another firm in the same work, for more than $1 billion. Chief executive Gio Albertazzi said that as computing density increases, cooling and power performance “will depend as much on execution in the field as on engineering and system design.” KES, founded in 2002 and headquartered near Dublin, serves customers across EMEA, and its site lists projects for Microsoft and Equinix. Source: [datacenterdynamics.com](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/vertiv-to-acquire-data-center-fluid-management-firm-king-environmental-services/)

### The Teardown: Rack Mass Versus Rack Price
You take it apart at the floor plate, not at the silicon. A data-centre slab is rated for a point load and a uniform load, and the rack designer spends that allowance on busbar, manifold, cold plates and coolant until the frame is as heavy as the tile allows. The invoice is not part of that drawing. Dylan Patel wrote that a fully populated GB300 NVL72 weighs roughly 1,580 kilograms, that recent purchase orders put the rack at 5 million dollars, that the GB200 NVL72 was 4 million dollars, and that Vera Rubin NVL72 is already quoted at up to 8.8 million dollars at essentially the same rack mass. Those four figures sit on one chassis envelope. High-bandwidth memory, the CoWoS package that bonds it to the GPU, and the power stages that feed both add little mass and most of the price. Patel's point is that those three stay supply-limited, so dollars per kilogram rise while floor loading holds the kilogram count still. The unit you accept on the dock is the populated rack, which is why he prices the frame rather than the bare packages. A higher quote does not buy a heavier rack, and a heavier rack would not clear a slab already at its rating. The tradeoff flips when packaging allocation or feeder capacity, not steel, is what you lack: price can move inside a fixed tile, and the tile cannot grow to match the quote.
