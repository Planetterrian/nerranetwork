### The Teardown: Through-Glass Vias in Glass Interposers
Take it apart and the bottleneck appears at the moment a femtosecond laser must drill a precise via through glass without cracking the surrounding material or leaving debris that blocks later copper plating. The laser fires pulses lasting only femtoseconds so heat does not propagate into the glass matrix, allowing the beam to ablate a clean cylindrical hole while the rest of the interposer stays intact. Litlit’s factory in Vilnius will produce up to 3,000 such lasers annually once it opens in October, a volume that directly limits how many advanced packages can receive through-glass vias each year. RayVen’s two-micron systems add the ability to focus inside silicon itself, turning the same short-pulse mechanism into a tool for backside structuring and stacked-chip signaling without chemical etch steps. Menhir’s 1.5-micron units target the synchronization role in co-packaged optics, where the laser acts as a timing reference rather than a drill, yet still relies on the same femtosecond stability to keep jitter low enough for 6G and AI fabric links. The global ultrafast-laser market sits at roughly $2.4 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach over $10 billion by the early 2030s, with semiconductor miniaturization cited as the leading driver. When demand spikes, the constraint flips from laser power to the number of field-deployed units that can run 24 hours without maintenance, which is exactly the qualification Litlit, RayVen, and Menhir are now racing to complete.
