A two-phase cold plate takes heat off a processor by boiling liquid at the chip, then carries vapor to a condenser that dumps the heat and sends liquid back. Sensible cooling has to warm a stream of fluid. Boiling spends that energy as latent heat, so less fluid moves more load, and the plate can sit on the package instead of asking the room air to do it. The condenser is still required.

Heat is not gone until the far side of the loop rejects it, and in a rack that far side is a coolant distribution unit tied into whatever water or air the building already runs.

HPCwire's write-up of Accelsius puts that loop in one frame, the NeuCool IR150, a fully integrated IT rack plus CDU. Accelsius calls it the first of that kind and is handing it to Villanova as shared infrastructure, not as a bench demo. The company is in the NVIDIA Inception program. The recipient is the National Science Foundation Center for Energy-Smart Electronic Systems.

Villanova is one of three university sites in that center. Accelsius already backs a separate COOLERCHIPS project at the University of Texas at Arlington under Dereje Agonafer. Villanova's thermal lab is also on the NVIDIA-led OMNICOOL project inside ARPA-E's COOLERCHIPS program. Google, Microsoft and Meta are among the industry partners behind the center.

The number to walk is the center's target, not a saving this donation has posted. ES2 wants U.S. data-center energy efficiency up by twenty to thirty-five percent. By the center's own estimate, that band equals removing one to two power plants every three years. Read it as a goal tied to a percent range.

The IR150 is the instrument for studying that range at rack scale, not the proof the range has been hit. Richard Bonner, Accelsius's chief technology officer, cast the gift as joint research with the companies building AI infrastructure. Alfonso Ortega, the ES2 site director at Villanova, said researchers get a rack-level look at two-phase direct-to-chip as thermal demand rises.

The article's install line, early October 2026, falls before the November third announcement, so the partnership and the hardware class are the facts you can use.

That integrated two-phase rack stops being the right install when the hall can hold junction temperature with the air or single-phase loop it already has. The IR150 exists so a lab can treat the rack, boiling plate and CDU together, as the thermal object.

If the cabinet never reaches the load behind that twenty-to-thirty-five-percent problem, the frame is a research tool in a room that did not need a phase change.
