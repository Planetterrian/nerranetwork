> **TSMC booked $5.10 billion in foreign subsidies, funding fabs from Arizona to Nanjing.**

**What You Need to Know:** TSMC booked $5.10 billion in foreign subsidies, funding fabs from Arizona to Nanjing. The nine-month haul, NT$167.586 billion from the United States, Japan, Germany and China, landed almost entirely in the third quarter and is tied to property, construction and tools at the overseas subsidiaries. Arizona’s second fab is scheduled for 3-nanometer mass production in the third quarter of 2027.

### Top Story
Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing’s Friday financial report puts combined subsidies from the United States, Japan, Germany and China at NT$167.586 billion (US$5.10 billion) for the first nine months of this year, a record for that stretch and cover for property purchases, plant construction and production equipment. The third quarter alone was NT$166.996 billion, after NT$590 million in the first half. The recipients are TSMC Arizona, European Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. in Dresden, Japan Advanced Semiconductor Manufacturing in Kumamoto, and TSMC Nanjing, each under agreements with local governments. Nanjing runs a 12-inch wafer fab. Arizona’s first fab began commercial production in the fourth quarter of 2024 on 4-nanometer; construction of the second fab is complete, equipment installation is underway, and mass production is scheduled for the third quarter of 2027 on 3-nanometer. Kumamoto’s first fab began commercial production at the end of 2024, with a second fab under construction for mass production in 2028; Dresden is scheduled to begin mass production next year. Over the nine months, TSMC Arizona posted a profit of NT$53.986 billion and JASM a profit of NT$2.396 billion, while ESMC reported a loss of NT$1.079 billion. **Intel’s ISA Reference Confirms Razor Lake Desktop and Laptop Model IDs: TechPowerUp**
Intel’s November Architecture Instruction Set Extensions Programming Reference, revision 063, adds Razor Lake and Coral Rapids. Razor Lake gets four model values under family 18 (12H), the same family as Nova Lake: 12_06H and 12_0AH for desktop, 12_07H and 12_09H for laptops. Coral Rapids Xeon is 13_08H, after Diamond Rapids at 13_01H. InstLatX64 spotted the update. Razor Lake picks up SSBD_PSFD_U_S, described as separate user-mode and supervisor-mode control over certain speculative memory behavior; both families gain APIC-Timer Virtualization; Coral Rapids adds VPMLDSABFLYDQ, aimed at ML-DSA. GCC patches posted this week give Razor Lake the same ISA feature set as Nova Lake, which should mean AVX10.2 and APX carry over, while Coral Rapids stays on AVX10.2 rather than AVX10.3 for now. **European Union offers to build data center for Moldova: Data Center Dynamics**
The European Union has offered to build a data center for Moldova, proposed by enlargement commissioner Marta Kos on a visit last week, to help protect citizens’ personal data from cyberattacks. If accepted, the facility would be built with assistance from Sweden, Europa Libera reported. Prime minister Vasile Tofan welcomed the offer and said preparatory work is advancing for a secure data center and a stronger link to the European artificial intelligence ecosystem. The offer came at the first meeting of the High-Level Digital Connectivity Platform in Chișinău. The European Commission said the summit’s purpose was to invest up to €623 million ($689 million), pledged by nations and private-sector partners, in digital connectivity between Europe and Central Asia, including 5G, space-based connectivity and data center infrastructure in the Black Sea region, Turkey and the southern Caucasus. A separate Logos Press estimate put the sum required to build a government facility consolidating public digital services at €50 million ($55 million). The offer follows an October 2026 cyberattack that exposed personal data of an estimated 450,000 Moldovan citizens. DataCenterMap lists six facilities in the country, all in Chișinău. **NTT Docomo to pilot data center heat-reuse for on-shore shrimp farm in Japan: Data Center Dynamics**
NTT is set to pilot waste heat from a data center to warm an on-land shrimp farm, under a Tokyo Metropolitan Government program for unused data-center heat. IMT Engineering and NTT Docomo announced “Construction of a Waste Heat Utilization Type Land-Based Aquaculture System,” starting next year with heat from an NTT Docomo Business data center in or near Tokyo and running until March 2028. Recovered heat will warm rearing water for Pacific white shrimp, a use NTT framed for waste heat that has been hard to apply because it is relatively low temperature. Hibiya General Equipment will design and build the heat-recovery and supply gear; NTT Green & Food, formed in 2023 with Regional Fish, will run the aquaculture and judge whether production costs fall. The four firms want a model that can move to other data centers. Elsewhere in the same city program, KDDI and ElleThermo are deploying a self-powered temperature sensor that can generate electricity from heat around 20–40°C, and Kratwerk and Tokyu Construction are taking waste heat from a small modular data center under a railway overpass for server cooling. NTT Docomo’s Nexcenter sites are seven around Tokyo, one in Yokohama, two in Osaka, one in Kagawa, one in Shanghai and two in Hong Kong. ### The Teardown: The Copy After Every Layer
A training step is arithmetic plus a copy, and the copy is what infra.bea.ai times when it asks whether a consumer GPU can stand in for a data-center accelerator. The guide puts an RTX 4090 at 82.6 teraflops of BF16, then draws three gaps compute does not close: 24 gigabytes of memory against 80, an interconnect of 32 gigabytes per second against 900, and no error-correcting memory where training expects it. Parameters, optimizer state and activations all have to live in that 24 gigabytes. The link has to move a slice of those numbers after every layer if the model is split across cards. PCI Express is the only path on a consumer board. There is no NVLink, so tensor-parallel all-reduce cannot hide inside a dedicated GPU fabric.

Take the guide’s 7-billion-parameter case, sharded across eight 4090s. With a micro-batch of one, a sequence length of 2048 and full checkpointing, activations are about 2 gigabytes and the running total is 18 gigabytes. Headroom is 6 gigabytes. That remainder is the budget for a longer sequence. At 4096 tokens the same card needs gradient checkpointing and a micro-batch of one. Extra sequence length is not a software preference. It is the memory that is left after weights and optimizer state have taken their share.

The interconnect numbers show why adding cards does not refill that budget. Tensor-parallel all-reduce has to finish after every layer. infra.bea.ai times it at 640 milliseconds on PCI Express and 22 milliseconds on NVLink. If the forward and backward pass takes 500 milliseconds, the PCI Express copy more than doubles the step. Fully sharded data parallel does not dodge the wire: an all-gather at the start of each layer and a reduce-scatter at the end add about 900 milliseconds on PCI Express to a 7-billion-parameter step that would spend about 32 milliseconds on the fast link. Eight cards therefore buy eight small memories and a bus that spends more time synchronizing than the arithmetic spends computing.

That design stops being the right one when either budget is gone. When sequence length consumes the 6 gigabytes of headroom, another 4090 does not create one larger address space. When the 640-millisecond all-reduce exceeds the 500-millisecond compute step, another card adds a participant to the copy, not a shorter step. The 82.6 teraflops still sit on the board. They wait on 32 gigabytes per second, and on 24 gigabytes that cannot see the other 24. Source: [infra.bea.ai](https://infra.bea.ai/guide/distributed/parallelism-planning/consumer-gpu-feasibility/)
