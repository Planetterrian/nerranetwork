> **Amazon scraps four Loudoun data centers, turning 122 acres toward housing.**

**What You Need to Know:** Amazon scraps four Loudoun data centers, turning 122 acres toward housing. The former George Washington University campus will mostly become mixed-use and residential land, with 25 acres and $5 million going to the county. Kazakhstan’s licensed GPU build, a split over the next DRAM cell, and a new gas-engine order fill the rest of the day.

### Top Story
Tom's Hardware reports that Amazon will not build four new data centers on the former George Washington University campus in Loudoun County, just outside Washington, D.C. Most of the 122-acre property is to be repurposed for mixed-use and residential development. Amazon will donate 25 acres to Loudoun County for a community arts center and a recreational facility, and give $5 million to the county’s housing fund. The company submitted the data-center proposal in July and met Board of Supervisors Chair Phyllis J. Randall in September. Josh Weissman, Amazon’s vice president for data center development, said the county had “pointed concerns about data center development at this particular site,” and that “the best outcome for all is to come together on a shared vision for this campus rather than engage in costly litigation.” Randall said the agreement will “provide more attainable homes for working families.” Source: [tomshardware.com](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/data-centers/amazon-bows-to-local-backlash-over-ai-data-centers-scraps-plans-to-build-four-new-facilities-in-northern-virginia-site-to-be-repurposed-for-mixed-use-and-residential-development)

### Silicon
**YMTC to Start Mass Production of LPDDR5X with 9,600 MT/s Speed: TechPowerUp**
YMTC is preparing volume production of LPDDR5X at 9,600 MT/s at Fab 3, using a vertical-channel-transistor 4F² cell. Counterpoint Research says the company has made about 5,000 wafers a month of 16 Gb LPDDR5X at 7,500 MT/s at Fab 2 since October 2025. Xtacking 4.0 separates the memory-cell wafer from peripheral logic and joins them by hybrid bonding. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/354058/ymtc-to-start-mass-production-of-lpddr5x-with-9-600-mt-s-speed)

**Micron Walks Away From 4F² DRAM After Two Decades of Research, Bets on 3D DRAM Instead: TechPowerUp**
Scott DeBoer, Micron’s president and chief technology and products officer, said on an October 21 webinar that Micron will not bring 4F² DRAM to market. On paper the cell cuts footprint by about a third versus today’s 6F² layout, but DeBoer said real density is limited by how tightly capacitors pack, and that 4F² costs more and runs into scaling limits at leading-edge nodes. Samsung and SK hynix are still developing 4F², and CXMT says its first 4F² product, a server DDR5 RDIMM, is due by the end of this year. Micron will keep scaling 6F², with the 1-delta node set to ramp in Boise in the second half of 2027 and 1-epsilon after that moving to wafer bonding. 3D DRAM is a parallel path with no timeline, and Micron says it will switch only when the economics make sense. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/354042/micron-walks-away-from-4f-dram-after-two-decades-of-research-bets-on-3d-dram-instead)

### The Teardown: When the Fourth Mask Loses the Exposure
A dense layer does not have to be drawn in a single pass. Break the pattern into simpler pieces, expose each piece on a deep-ultraviolet scanner, and the stack can approximate a feature that one shot of that light cannot print. Tom's Hardware put that workaround to Chris Mack, co-founder and chief technology officer of Fractilia, because export rules have kept ASML’s extreme-ultraviolet tools out of China. Those machines print with 13.5-nanometre light. Without them, the path left is older deep-ultraviolet lithography plus multiple patterning.

What that path has already printed is in shipping phones. A teardown of Huawei’s Mate 60 Pro found the Kirin 9000S made by SMIC on a second-generation 7-nanometre-class process with no EUV. Later analysis of the Kirin 9030 found SMIC’s DUV flow pushed closer to densities associated with 5-nanometre manufacturing, while still lagging the leading processes at TSMC and Samsung. Mack’s point is that a node label hides the cost of getting there. Every added exposure has to land on patterns already etched. More masks mean more overlay error and more process variation. Designers buy a printable layer by leaving extra space around features. The die grows. A larger die means fewer candidates on each wafer, and wafers cost tens of thousands of dollars. The thing sold is the good die, not the wafer.

Mack’s cost line is the worked case. He told Tom's Hardware that industry convention held three exposures on an immersion tool at about the same cost as one EUV exposure. At four masks standing in for that single EUV exposure, EUV becomes the cheaper shot, and the cleaner one, because four overlay stacks accumulate defects one exposure never introduces. Memory shows the same gap in a June Morgan Stanley estimate: CXMT was producing only about one-third as many bits per wafer as Western competitors. Analysts put only about a third of that shortfall on the older process node and called lithography access a hard ceiling. Micron’s 1-alpha DRAM, built without EUV, is the counter-example — competitive chips from deep-ultraviolet light, multiple patterning and tight control, while Samsung and SK hynix used many EUV layers for the same generation. Douglas Fuller of Copenhagen Business School told Tom's Hardware that other routes around EUV for advanced logic are unlikely to be efficient, and that by the end of the decade advanced packaging could still let DUV print the latest DRAM generations.

Four masks against one EUV exposure is where this choice stops paying for a logic layer. Overlay error, a fatter die and fewer good dice per wafer then dominate the scanner that was never delivered. Source: [tomshardware.com](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/china-can-make-advanced-chips-without-euv-but-the-bill-keeps-growing-duv-multiple-patterning-means-bigger-dies-lower-yields-and-longer-manufacturing-times)
