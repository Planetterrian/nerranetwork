### Top Story
Semiconductor Engineering reports that Lam Research has proposed a self-aligned CFET architecture meant to extend CMOS scaling below 10 angstroms. Digital-twin modeling produced a 6T SRAM bit cell of 0.0094 square micrometres, 52.7 percent smaller than an N3 bit cell. The model keeps at least a 10-nanometre overlay window across critical contact layers. The result is a proposed architecture and a modeled cell, not a process in volume production. ### Data Centres & Power
**From Opposition to Buy-In: Data Center Knowledge**
Data Center Knowledge reports that sessions at Data Center World Power in Dallas, September 21–23, dealt with community opposition to data centers over water, power, and noise, and with bans and moratoria. Power availability and possible electricity rate hikes are among the biggest worries, and in some regions data centers are being blamed for higher rates. Many developers are willing to fund the upgrades their projects need; some have paid for entire battery energy storage systems that serve both the grid and the data center. Others are contributing to transmission upgrades or buying transformers, substations, and other power electronics so those costs are not passed to ratepayers, and some grid planners and utilities are formalizing such arrangements as standard practice. Diane Sullivan, chief development officer of Hecate Energy, a Chicago-based developer of utility-scale energy parks, tied part of the backlash to non-disclosure agreements with city officials and to projects developed under the radar, and said AI data centers are now large enough that engaging the community upfront is in the developer's long-term interest. Source: [datacenterknowledge.com](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-site-selection/from-opposition-to-buy-in-tactics-for-data-center-developers)

### The Teardown: Why a New Node Is Still a Sliver of Wafer Revenue
In the second quarter of 2026, 2-nanometre product accounted for 3 percent of TSMC wafer revenue, Spherical Insights reports, against 33 percent for 5-nanometre wafers and 30 percent for 3-nanometre wafers. Revenue for the quarter was 40.2 billion dollars. A new node does not take the paycheck on announcement day. Three physical queues have to finish, and they finish on different clocks. Design teams must close libraries, memory compilers, and a tape-out on the new rules. The fab must install and qualify the lithography, etch, and deposition set before it can offer a monthly wafer start. Yield must rise until a good die costs less than staying on the prior node. Customers with masks, intellectual property, and package designs already qualified on the older nodes move only the chips that need the density or the power. The rest stays where yield is already known. A node can therefore be in production and still be a sliver of the income statement. Wafers on the newest node are shipping, and the two older nodes still hold most of what the foundry sold. The 40.2 billion dollars means that sliver is cut from a very large quarter, not from a pilot line. The tradeoff flips when good-die cost on the new node, at the volume a buyer can actually book, undercuts the older node on performance per watt or on the wafers per month that buyer can get. Until that slot is bookable, the revenue mix marks where the tools and the masks already sit.
