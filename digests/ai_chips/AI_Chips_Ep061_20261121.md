### Top Story
Apollo Global Management and Goldman Sachs priced about $44 billion in senior and subordinated notes to buy GPUs and related equipment for SpaceX, citybiz reported, after the book was oversubscribed and the financing was increased. The companies call it the largest GPU financing executed to date. The notes back SpaceX's terrestrial compute build with Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips. Funds and accounts managed by BlackRock and Goldman Sachs Asset Management, plus capital accounts advised by KKR, joined as anchor commitments. A BASENOR account of the same announcement says the hardware sits in Saturn Funding Trust, which leases it back to SpaceX so the debt stays off the corporate balance sheet, and that lenders required the GPUs — the collateral — to remain on Earth in U.S. data centers majority-owned, controlled, and operated by SpaceX. Lease schedules start when the equipment is delivered in the first half of 2027. Apollo partner Jamshid Ehsani said SpaceX is "one of the most ambitious builders of compute in the world." ### Silicon
**HBM Memory Shortage: 23% of DRAM Wafers, 30% by 2027: shattered.io**
TrendForce projects that high-bandwidth memory will take 23 percent of DRAM wafer input at Samsung, SK hynix, and Micron in 2026 and nearly 30 percent in 2027, shattered.io reported, because HBM needs three to four times as much wafer capacity as general-purpose DRAM. A shortage tracker summarized by Futu News puts HBM supply at roughly 58 percent of estimated first-quarter demand. TrendForce expects conventional DRAM contract prices to rise 10 to 15 percent quarter over quarter in the fourth quarter, while NAND is falling. SK hynix says its HBM and related DRAM are sold out through 2029, and Micron reportedly covers only half to two-thirds of customer demand. SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won said the current shortage could continue until 2030, and SK hynix chief executive Kwak Noh-jung said customer demand will remain higher than supply capacity even beyond 2030. New capacity from China's CXMT clouds the 2028 outlook, and major new memory fabs are not expected to add meaningful output before around 2028. Source: [shattered.io](https://shattered.io/hbm-memory-shortage-23-percent-dram-wafers-nears-30-percent-2027-trendforce/)

### Supply Chain & Policy
**AI Server Boards Are Now Draining the PCB Supply Chain: TechPowerUp**
The Global Electronics Association, formerly IPC, warned that AI servers are taking board capacity and materials, TechPowerUp reported. North American PCB bookings are up 56.1 percent this year through September, while shipments have grown 13.3 percent. Lead times for high-layer boards are 16 to 20 weeks or more; the outlet said some PCB orders were taking up to 30 months, and that motherboard prices rose roughly 15 to 50 percent. A typical board has about 12 layers, while AI server boards are moving to 40 to 60 layers or more, so each board consumes more glass fabric, resin, copper foil, and laminate. At least $10 billion has gone into PCB plants in Southeast Asia and China over the past three years, and the association expects the shortage to last through at least the first half of 2027, with meaningful relief unlikely before mid-2028. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/355065/ai-server-boards-are-now-draining-the-pcb-supply-chain)

### The Teardown: Reading another PC's memory over USB4
Remote Direct Memory Access lets a workload on one machine touch memory on another without the usual copy through the operating system. Microsoft has turned that path on for Windows 11 over USB4, so several PCs can be daisy-chained and share a job. TechPowerUp's account of the work is a latency measurement, not a bandwidth bake-off, and the number that matters is how long a message takes to go and come back.

USB4 is the pipe, rated at up to 80 gigabits per second. Ethernet can already run over that same cable, and a UDP packet on that Ethernet path is the comparison Microsoft rejected. UDP still climbs the network stack: the message is wrapped, queued, copied, and unwrapped on the far side before the other PC's memory is involved. RDMA is built so the transfer lands in the other machine's memory with the CPU on that machine mostly out of the way. The engineering bet is that the stack, not the copper, was the wait.

The worked example is two PCs and a sweep of message size. TechPowerUp reports round-trip latency of 28 to 50 microseconds for RDMA over USB4. The same sweep on Ethernet over USB4 using UDP landed between 116 and 194 microseconds. At the fast end, 28 against 116; at the slow end, 50 against 194. The outlet calls the RDMA path roughly four times faster. Those four bounds are the whole published result: two machines, one cable family, two protocols, latency versus message size. The illustration of DGX Spark boxes linked by Ethernet is explicitly not this USB4 setup, so it does not add a third data point.

Eighty gigabits per second is the other limit, and it does not show up in a microsecond measurement of a short message. A short message finishes before the pipe is full, so the 28-to-50-microsecond access is what the user feels, and the 116-to-194-microsecond UDP path is the tax of the stack. Stretch the message and the bytes have to occupy that 80-gigabit USB4 pipe for longer, while the protocol gap stays in the same band the measurement already drew. Daisy-chained Windows 11 PCs stop being the right fabric when the job is limited by how many bytes that 80-gigabit pipe can move, not by the 28-to-50-microsecond memory access — a wider network then finishes the step, and the fourfold latency win no longer decides it. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/355062/microsoft-brings-rdma-over-usb4-to-windows-11-for-daisy-chained-pcs)
