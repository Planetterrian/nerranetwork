Samsung, TechPowerUp reports from Seoul Economic Daily and industry sources, is converting NAND inside the second Xi'an fab rather than building a new one. The X2 line now runs about ten thousand three-hundred-millimetre wafers a month of two-hundred-eighty-six-layer V9, and the extra output is aimed at enterprise drives for AI data centers.

An electroplating line would fill empty cleanroom space, equipment would move in around February twenty twenty-seven, and the conversion would finish that November. Twenty to thirty percent of the existing V6 line would switch to V9 as well.

Samsung has previously denied that it was expanding production capacity, so this reads as a reshuffle even while the wafer count jumps, and NAND spot prices have already started falling.

A published weight file can still name the machine that first filled it. Training does not open on an empty tensor. A library writes a starting pattern, and that pattern depends on how the initialization kernel was launched, including how many streaming multiprocessors took part and how the random streams were mapped across them.

Israel Defense, on a forensic study by Unemploymentlabs, says researchers rebuilt those initial values from public model weights and then hunted the hardware and initialization setups that reproduce the numbers exactly. The trace is in the weights, not in a leak.

The split they publish is the core count. In this method a Blackwell B200 or B300 signature is one hundred forty-eight streaming multiprocessors, and a GB200 or GB300 signature is one hundred fifty-two.

Six models out since April twenty twenty-six, from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group, carry patterns consistent with Blackwell, while earlier models from those same firms line up with Hopper. DeepSeek-V4-Flash and MiniMax-M3 match the one-hundred-forty-eight-core pattern. DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3, Kimi K3.1-Pro and Ling-3.1-flash match the one-hundred-fifty-two-core pattern.

For DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Kimi K3 the match was checked on physical Blackwell chips, not only in a search. DeepSeek-V3, Kimi K2.5 and Ling-3.0-flash stay on the Hopper side. Kimi K2.7-Code sits between them: Hopper-era weights, plus a minor slice consistent with B200 or B300.

The control set is what makes the count usable. The same test was run on twenty-four models whose developers had already named the hardware, and every one matched, including Nvidia's Nemotron-3 Ultra at that same one-hundred-fifty-two-core signature. Israel Defense says the correlations sit far above a chance explanation.

The only other setup the researchers found that could mimic the pattern is custom-configured AMD hardware, itself restricted for export to China. That alternative was ruled out for DeepSeek-V4-Flash and called highly improbable for the other five.

Recent models from Alibaba, Zhipu, Tencent, Xiaomi and StepFun still indicated Hopper, and Meituan's LongCat-2.0, which the company said was trained on an AI ASIC superpod, showed no NVIDIA fingerprint.

The fingerprint names how the weights were generated. It supports the idea that Blackwell also ran the training, and it does not prove that. It does not say where the chips sat, how access was obtained, or whether a rule was broken.

Treating a reproduced initialization, checked on silicon for two models and against twenty-four disclosed cases, as evidence of Blackwell use stops being the right instrument when the question is a place, a shipment, or a violation, or when a later training stack could have left the initialization's machine behind.

At that point the one-hundred-forty-eight versus one-hundred-fifty-two split still describes the first fill, and it no longer identifies the training run.
