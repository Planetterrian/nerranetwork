To take it apart, picture a sixty-four megabyte SRAM die bonded onto a compute chiplet, which is the whole of AMD’s 3D V-Cache trick.

Extra last-level memory sits on the vertical path, so a core that keeps reusing one working set can stay on the package instead of leaving for DRAM.

The bond is also a lid. Heat has to leave through that extra silicon, and the chiplet underneath has to accept the voltage the stack will tolerate.

That lid is why the boost clock moves when you cover both chiplets.
