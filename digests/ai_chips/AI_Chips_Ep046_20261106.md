> **Reported China orders of six million RTX PRO 5500s exceed a reported three-million 2027 build.**

**What You Need to Know:** Reported orders from China for Nvidia’s RTX PRO 5500 top six million cards, against a source’s ceiling of three million units that can be built in 2027, while a US listing prices the cut-down card above the flagship RTX PRO 6000. Reuters, via Semiconductor Engineering, puts about 600,000 H200 chips in Hong Kong awaiting approval for mainland shipment as Beijing limits imports. A European Commission analysis puts EU fab production costs 40% to 50% above Taiwan, South Korea, and China, and US voters moved to kill or slow data-center projects near them.

### Top Story
Chinese buyers have ordered Nvidia’s RTX PRO 5500 past a reported 2027 build ceiling, and a US government reseller lists the cut-down card above the flagship. TechPowerUp reports that SHI Government Solutions listed a PNY-built RTX PRO 5500 at an MSRP of $17,999 and a discounted $17,408.63, while Nvidia’s own US marketplace lists the RTX PRO 6000 at $16,000 even though that card has 24,064 CUDA cores and 96 GB of memory against the 5500’s 21,760 cores and 84 GB. At the discounted listing the lesser card is still around $1,400 more, and Nvidia has not posted an official price since quietly adding the 5500 in September, so TechPowerUp says it is unclear whether $17,999 is Nvidia’s figure or PNY’s. Videocardz, citing Reuters, reports that ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent have ordered more than 3 million cards at roughly $11,400 each, with total orders from China reportedly topping 6 million, while one source said Nvidia can make no more than 3 million of them in 2027. With the B300 barred from China and the H200 reviewed case by case, TechPowerUp calls the 5500 one of the strongest Nvidia GPUs Chinese firms can still buy; the card carries 28 GDDR7 modules, and the outlet says orders ahead of supply are a reason to expect the price to stay high. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/354527/nvidia-rtx-pro-5500-listing-spotted-at-usd-17-999-more-than-the-flagship-rtx-pro-6000)

**Intel Core Ultra 4000 Series "Nova Lake-H" SoC Surfaces: TechPowerUp**
A reported die photo adds a measured package map for the Core Ultra 4000 Nova Lake-H part already tied to Intel’s 18A-P process. TechPowerUp describes a 4+8+4 configuration — 4 P-cores, 8 E-cores, and 4 LPE-cores — on a compute die made on Intel 18A-P, in a package of about 12 by 23 mm and 276 mm². The graphics tile, on Intel 3-E, holds 4 Xe GPU cores in roughly 26 mm², beside a 21 mm² dummy die; a 72 mm² 18A-P die hosts the 4 LPE-cores as a central hub, a peripheral control tile measures 58 mm², and the 4P+8E compute tile measures 55 mm², both on 18A-P. The outlet presents this as a raw picture that surfaced, not as an Intel launch. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/354506/intel-core-ultra-4000-series-nova-lake-h-soc-surfaces)

**AMD "Medusa Point" With 10-Core, 20-Thread CPU Configuration Surfaces: TechPowerUp**
An AMD Medusa Point sample has surfaced on Geekbench as a 10-core, 20-thread part split into 4 Zen 6 cores and 6 Zen 6c cores, not as a formal launch. Each core shows 32 KB of L1 instruction cache, 48 KB of L1 data cache, and 1 MB of L2, with a shared 16 MB L3; the listing gives a 2.0 GHz base and a maximum of 1996 MHz, which TechPowerUp treats as a misread of early silicon. The instruction set on the sample includes AVX-512, AVX512-FP16, and AVX512-VNNI, and the test system had 64 GB of DDR5, 55.58 GB usable, at 5,188 MT/s. Talk of an RDNA 4m mobile graphics block remains rumor in the same report; what the listing measured is the CPU configuration and those instruction extensions. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/354504/amd-medusa-point-with-10-core-20-thread-cpu-configuration-surfaces)

**Europe’s Chip Industry: Perfect Storm or Perfect Timing?: EE Times**
SEMICON Europa 2026, co-located with electronica in Munich from November 10 to 13, is framed as “Europe’s Next Era: Navigating the Perfect Storm.” A European Commission analysis published in June 2026 finds total fab production costs in EU fabs 40% to 50% higher than in Taiwan, South Korea, and China; in 2024 the EU consumed €55 billion of semiconductors and imported €43.6 billion from outside the bloc, with the sharpest exposure in leading-edge chips for AI and high-performance computing. The European Court of Auditors concluded in 2025 that the first Chips Act strategy is very unlikely to deliver a 20% EU share of global market value by 2030, and the share is now projected at 11.7% by 2030. Chips Act 2.0, published in June 2026 and targeted for adoption in the second quarter of 2027, keeps advanced manufacturing but widens the frame to design, equipment, materials, and advanced packaging, pilot lines, and customers in automotive, industrial systems, and data centers. SEMI Europe president Laith Altimime said, “Europe’s semiconductor competitiveness depends on the strength of the entire ecosystem, from design and materials to equipment, manufacturing, and advanced packaging.” Source: [eetimes.com](https://www.eetimes.com/europe-chip-industry-perfect-storm-or-perfect-timing/)

### The Teardown: The Decoder Beside the Qubit
Error correction is a split job. The quantum chip holds a fragile state; a classical machine has to decide, continuously, which error just happened and which fix to apply before that state drifts. HPCwire’s account of the November 5, 2026 announcement from IBM, the Quantum Science Center at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, and AMD describes a bench built for that split. IBM’s piece is the Nighthawk processor: 120 qubits, square-lattice connectivity, a high rate of circuits per second, and pulse control that can act while a circuit is still running. Oak Ridge’s piece is Umbra, powered by AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs and 5th Gen AMD EPYC CPUs. They are co-located so a team can score each error-correction method by qubits, time, samples, and classical cost.

On that quantum hardware a researcher can measure qubits mid-calculation and branch on the result immediately, set the timing of the pulses that guard the qubits, see what the machine actually ran, and work from the raw measurement signal. A code uses many noisy physical qubits to stand in for fewer logical qubits a scientist can trust. The classical decoder reads the syndrome — the pattern of those stabilizer checks — and names the correction. If the name arrives late, it refers to a state that has already moved. Umbra is there so that decoder can be tried on GPU nodes large enough to resemble the classical side a later machine would need, and so competing codes can be ranked by the classical burden they impose. The announcement is explicit that the open question is no longer whether error correction can work on quantum hardware, but how to accelerate and optimize it.

The figures in the HPCwire account separate a modest qubit step from a large operations step. IBM’s recorded experiments have reached up to 70 logical qubits and 438 hard operations. The Department of Energy’s Quantum Genesis mission, through its Q Competition, targets 100 logical qubits running 100,000 hard operations by late 2028. From 70 logical qubits to 100 is 30 more logical qubits, under half again the demonstrated count. From 438 hard operations to 100,000 is the gap the announcement calls roughly 230-fold. Closing that gap, the write-up says, is what the field still has to do. A decoder that keeps up for a few hundred operations has not shown it can keep up when each added operation is another round of measurement and another classical decision. Stretch the circuit by that roughly 230-fold factor and a decoder that was merely fast enough beside Nighthawk becomes the limit, even though the logical-qubit count only had to rise by 30.

This first pairing stops being the right instrument the day the code needs a 121st physical qubit, because 120 is all Nighthawk brings, or the day the question is 100,000 hard operations rather than 438. The announcement says next approaches need those pulse-level controls together on systems large enough to test real codes, and it lists the stack that has to arrive as one piece: concatenated codes, space-time codes, algorithm-aware decoders, hardware-aware compilation, and software orchestration. Until a larger quantum processor shares the bench, Umbra’s job is to price methods in qubits, time, samples, and classical resources, not to deliver the late-2028 operation count. Source: [hpcwire.com](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/ornl-ibm-and-amd-launch-new-co-design-platform-for-quantum-error-correction/)
