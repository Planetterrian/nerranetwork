# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **Fifty gigawatts in 2027 would equal every data centre built through 2024.**

**What You Need to Know:** SemiAnalysis argues that about fifty gigawatts of data-centre capacity in 2027 would match the entire global data-centre market built through 2024, and it points to more than twenty-four gigawatts already in advanced construction for that year. A separate supply read puts next year’s high-bandwidth memory demand near 61.5 billion gigabits against expected shipments near 54.6 billion gigabits. Packaging is the other capacity bet: a U.S. interposer line for TSMC, and higher back-end spending at ASE, Amkor and Micron.

### Top Story
Datacenters 2027: The Year of Acceleration, +50GW in a Single Year: SemiAnalysis
SemiAnalysis says one photograph set covers more than 24 gigawatts of data centres in an advanced stage of construction, across 40 campuses, most of them more than halfway done with exterior cooling and electrical equipment already installed, and all of them to be delivered in 2027. Those 40 campuses are described as a small share of the several hundred sites the firm tracks in an advanced stage of construction. Year to date, slightly under 1 trillion dollars of binding, non-cancellable data-centre leases have been signed. A typical contract runs 15 to 20 years, with a total contract value of 30 to 50 billion dollars per gigawatt, and developers typically spend only 5 to 10 percent of project capital without a customer. The same piece also refers to a plus-43-gigawatt 2027 forecast that Altimeter’s Brad Gerstner called too aggressive against his own figure of about 25 gigawatts. Source: [newsletter.semianalysis.com](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/datacenters-2027-the-year-of-acceleration)

### Silicon
Breaking The Memory Bottleneck In AI And HPC SoCs: Semiconductor Engineering
Semiconductor Engineering’s write-up of a Synopsys paper says embedded memory, not compute, now consumes more than half of total die area on AI and HPC system-on-chips and constrains bandwidth, latency and energy. Silicon results from SRAM and register-file test chips on Intel’s 18A process, using RibbonFET and PowerVia, show density improvements in the 5 to 10 percent range and iso-power performance gains of up to about 4 percent. Source: [semiengineering.com](https://semiengineering.com/breaking-the-memory-bottleneck-in-ai-and-hpc-socs/)

### Data Centres & Power
Why Data Centers Rarely Reuse Cooling Water: Data Center Knowledge
Data Center Knowledge says a 100-megawatt facility can consume about 2.5 billion litres of water a year, roughly as much as 5,000 or more homes, mostly to reject heat. The most common method is direct evaporative cooling, drawing air through wetted media so evaporation removes heat from the airstream. Most sites do not recycle that water, the piece says, because alternatives carry higher capital cost and more operational complexity than the economics currently justify. Uptime Institute adds that water use has no standard template: each location has its own water signature, legacy halls often rely on open evaporative cooling towers, and new halls can be built to use little or no water for cooling. Source: [datacenterknowledge.com](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/cooling/why-data-centers-rarely-reuse-cooling-water)

### Supply Chain & Policy
Samsung Works with TSMC on Nvidia’s Custom HBM: SammyGuru
SammyGuru, citing industry estimates, says high-bandwidth memory demand next year is about 61.5 billion gigabits, up 91 percent from this year, while memory makers may ship only 54.6 billion gigabits, a gap of about 10 percent. The same account says Nvidia’s custom stack, NVHBM, uses a physical layer 60 percent smaller than standard HBM, and that TSMC’s 3-nanometer yield is estimated above 90 percent against about 60 percent for Samsung’s comparable 2-nanometer SF2 process. Samsung’s own 4-nanometer base-die output is limited to Hwaseong Line 17 and part of Pyeongtaek P3. Benzinga, citing Seoul Economic Daily, adds that NVHBM can deliver up to 30 percent more memory bandwidth and 15 percent lower power than standard HBM4E, that Nvidia wants the core die standardized on sixth-generation 1c DRAM, and that Samsung’s Device Solutions division accepted the 3-nanometer base-die request after an internal review. TSMC declined to comment on individual customers. Source: [sammyguru.com](https://sammyguru.com/samsung-works-with-tsmc-on-nvidia-custom-hbm/)

GlobalFoundries to build TSMC silicon interposers in Malta: Silicon UK
GlobalFoundries will manufacture the silicon interposer for TSMC’s CoWoS packaging under a deal valued at 2 billion dollars over five years, expanding capacity at its plant in Malta, New York, so TSMC customers can market products as manufactured and packaged entirely in the United States. Ed Kaste, senior vice president of CMOS at GlobalFoundries, said the deal would create a secure source of advanced-packaging elements. Silicon UK also reports that TSMC last week said it would invest 100 billion dollars to expand existing Phoenix operations, on top of 165 billion dollars already committed, and that many advanced chips built in Arizona are still sent back to Taiwan for final assembly. TSMC began 4-nanometer production in Arizona in January of last year. Source: [silicon.co.uk](https://www.silicon.co.uk/workspace/components/globalfoundries-tsmc-631852)

Advanced packaging capex rises as the EUV lesson moves to the back end: BigGo Finance
BigGo Finance reports TSMC is keeping this year’s capital-expenditure forecast at 60 to 64 billion dollars, with 10 to 20 percent of that going to advanced packaging, testing and photomask production, and CoWoS capacity the focus. ASE Technology raised this year’s capital-expenditure plan from 8.5 billion dollars to 10.5 billion dollars. Amkor’s second-phase expansion of its Arizona campus lifts total investment to about 12 billion dollars. Micron is investing about 7 billion dollars in Singapore for an HBM packaging hub. Yole Group projects the advanced-packaging market from 55 billion dollars in 2025 to more than 120 billion dollars by 2031, and more than half of total semiconductor packaging revenue by 2031. Source: [finance.biggo.com](https://finance.biggo.com/news/f6021721-ea46-4577-847e-68fe4d33346e)

### The Teardown: When the Pad Has to Land Inside Forty Nanometres
Hybrid bonding mates a dielectric face and a metal pad in the same step, so the interconnect can sit on a pitch a solder bump or a thermocompression bond has trouble holding. The copper has to land on copper. Alignment is the bond, not a check that happens after the chemistry.

The surface has to arrive flat, clean, and in a known material state. BigGo Finance quotes Applied Materials executive Prabu Raja: “Advanced packaging is no longer just physical assembly in the back end—it is a materials engineering revolution that demands wafer-level precision and cleanliness.” A particle, a recessed copper pad, or an unstable surface from an earlier step can make the bonder’s own positioning accuracy meaningless. That is the reason Applied Materials and Besi, starting from a joint R&D project in Singapore in 2020, built the Kinex die-to-wafer system to pull surface treatment, cleaning, activation and alignment into one tighter flow.

The measured case in that account is a May result from Imec and EV Group. They reached a 200-nanometre copper interconnect pitch in wafer-to-wafer hybrid-bonding research. After bonding, overlay deviation of the copper pads stayed below 40 nanometres across all test chips on 300-millimetre wafers. Forty nanometres is one fifth of a 200-nanometre pitch. If the pad is on the order of that pitch, a 40-nanometre miss already consumes a large share of the contact before corrosion or dishing is counted. Imec, in the same report, says further scaling still has to solve surface topography from chemical mechanical polishing, copper corrosion, dielectric reliability, and metal diffusion along the bond interface.

The lithography tool beside that problem is already shipping, and it is not a bonder. ASML delivered the TWINSCAN XT:260 i-line system for 3D integration and advanced packaging in 2025, specified at 270 wafers an hour. Scanning 270 wafers an hour prints packaging layers. It does not join the pads. An international patent published on 15 May, with ASML Netherlands as applicant, discusses improving bonding-alignment deviation by controlling the surface state of the substrate support. BigGo Finance draws the line in plain terms: ASML has not publicly confirmed a commercial delivery timeline, customer qualification, or mass-production performance for hybrid-bonding equipment. A patent position is not a qualified tool on a high-bandwidth-memory line.

The R&D money shows how early the integrated flow still is. On 1 October, Besi became an innovation partner at Applied Materials’ EPIC centre in Silicon Valley, planned at up to about 5 billion dollars and spent as customer projects advance. The scope moves past die-to-wafer hybrid bonding into thermocompression-bond scaling, die-to-die, die-to-panel, and optical interconnects. Five days later, Applied Materials deepened work with Intel that includes Foveros 3D packaging. Lam Research’s path stays on the hole and the fill: Syndion deep reactive-ion etch for high-aspect-ratio through-silicon vias, and the SABRE 3D copper-plating platform for via fill, wafer-level packaging and redistribution layers. That coverage does not replace the bonder.

Hybrid bonding stops being the right next step on an HBM flow when the added surface-control steps raise the cost of the whole stack faster than the pitch gain pays back, or when polishing topography, copper corrosion, dielectric reliability or interface diffusion cannot be held inside the overlay the pitch requires. BigGo Finance says Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron still have to decide which HBM generation and which stack architecture split traditional thermocompression bonding from hybrid bonding. Until that yield, capacity and reliability sum closes, the 200-nanometre pitch remains a research result on 300-millimetre wafers, not a committed process split. Source: [finance.biggo.com](https://finance.biggo.com/news/1e451252-79da-45e8-9f64-39e900da59c1)
