# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **Black Hills will build 564 megawatts of gas in Cheyenne for Google's data centre.**

**What You Need to Know:** Black Hills will build 564 megawatts of gas-fired generation in Cheyenne, Wyoming, for Google's planned data centre, inside a resource mix it puts at about 2.7 gigawatts. Samsung's high-bandwidth memory share is reported at 33 percent, and GlobalFoundries has unveiled an FD-SOI platform aimed at machines that sense and act rather than at data-centre GPUs. A council in northern Italy has rejected an 80-megawatt data centre.

### Top Story
Black Hills Corp., the South Dakota-based utility holding company, has announced 564 megawatts of new natural-gas generation at its existing Cheyenne Prairie Generating Station in Cheyenne, Wyoming, to serve Google's planned data-centre load, with the units to be built between 2027 and 2029. Power Engineering reports the new capacity would cost 1.8 billion dollars and would be owned by a non-regulated Black Hills affiliate, not the Wyoming utility. Black Hills would manage about 2.1 gigawatts of third-party resources — the project release suggests turbines and fuel cells — on a privately managed microgrid under Wyoming's Large Power Contract Service tariff, taking a management fee rather than owning those assets. The company said the project would draw on a total resource mix of about 2.7 gigawatts, including reserve margins and excluding transmission expansion. Google would cover the cost of serving the data centre, with contractual protections on stranded assets, early termination and unexpected costs intended to shield existing utility customers, and has advanced 399 million dollars for long-lead equipment that Black Hills expects to reimburse before July 2027. Black Hills projects about 150 million dollars of net income from the project in 2030 and 2.4 billion dollars of unlevered free cash flow through 2048, has secured an air-quality permit for the Cheyenne Prairie expansion, and is pursuing additional siting and transmission approvals. Source: [power-eng.com](https://www.power-eng.com/onsite-power/microgrids/google-wyoming-data-center-black-hills-natural-gas/)

**Samsung's HBM Market Share Jumps to 33%; Hybrid Bonding Technology Could Be Key to Overtaking Rivals: BigGo Finance**
BigGo Finance reports Samsung's high-bandwidth memory market share at 33 percent, drawing on an expert interview published by Damnang Research on October 6. The September 19 interview was with a process-development engineer at SK Hynix's Future Technology Research Institute; the notes say the remarks are personal views, not the company's position, and the full transcript is behind a paywall. That interviewee said SK Hynix is having difficulty developing hybrid bonding for next-generation HBM, while Samsung is reportedly already delivering samples that use the technology. On June 18, SK Hynix said it had delivered 12-layer HBM4E samples of 48 gigabytes to major customers on its existing Advanced MR-MUF packaging, with a 17 percent improvement in thermal resistance compared with HBM4. **Manfred Horstmann: GlobalFoundries Bets on FDX Fusion for Physical AI: EE Times**
EE Times reports that GlobalFoundries on October 9 unveiled FDX Fusion in Dresden, a strained-silicon fully depleted silicon-on-insulator platform it says can deliver 7-nanometre-class digital performance without extreme ultraviolet lithography, at more than twice the density of its first-generation FDX, and that combines RF, analog, mixed-signal, embedded nonvolatile memory, in-memory computing and dense logic. Manfred Horstmann, senior vice president and general manager of European fabs, told EE Times that data-centre AI is extremely power hungry, and that physical AI needs sensors, actuators, communications, low power and cost efficiency — what GlobalFoundries calls STAC, for sense, think, act, communicate. GlobalFoundries expects to invest roughly 1 billion dollars over the coming years in FDX Fusion development, including about 250 million dollars for the initial capability toolset. The transistor channel sits in an approximately 70-angstrom, or 7-nanometre, silicon film above a buried oxide, thin enough that the channel is inherently fully depleted. The same day the company marked the topping-off of SPRINT, its Dresden fab expansion supported under the European Chips Act, adding cleanroom, laboratory and manufacturing capacity. ### The Teardown: When the Module Price Is the Allocation
A finished memory module does not record whether its die was bid against an accelerator. The choice is made at wafer start and in the back-end that turns a die into something a board can accept. High-bandwidth parts take advanced DRAM, stacking and test time. Ordinary DRAM and NAND still need cleanroom hours, probe capacity, substrates and module assembly from an industry already steering people and tools toward the higher-margin AI package. When that shared capacity tightens, the line that used to be a small item on a bill of materials becomes the line that sets the shelf price.

TechPowerUp, reporting Circana figures that analyst Mat Piscatella shared on Bluesky, measured the US console market for 2026 through August. Xbox hardware unit sales were down 33 percent from a year earlier. The average price paid for a new Xbox was 529 dollars. A new PlayStation averaged 597 dollars. Total hardware unit sales in August were 559,000, the lowest August total since 2013. Piscatella tied the slump to price hikes that followed the memory and storage shortage.

The mechanism is a queue. A fab books a wafer start well ahead of the shelf. A start aimed at a stacked DRAM die does not later become a console chip. A start that is standard DRAM or NAND still competes for testers, engineers and often the same substrate vendors the AI package is already loading. The device maker then meets two constraints at once: a higher contract price, and a shipment slot that can slip after that price is accepted. Unit sales fall when either constraint binds. The average selling price in the Circana data is what remains after the buyer who would not pay has already left the sample.

That pass-through stops clearing the market when the missing constraint is no longer price. If the module is simply not on the line — testers full, substrates late, NAND held for data-centre drives — a discount does not create a unit. Availability, not the sticker, is what then decides whether a launch weekend ships.
