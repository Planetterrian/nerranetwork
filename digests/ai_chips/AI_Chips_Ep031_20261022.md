# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **NVIDIA plans a 2.5-times substrate build so AI factory capacity can actually ship.**

**What You Need to Know:** NVIDIA has announced a 2.5-times increase in substrate capacity over three years, with named partners expanding packaging, test and optical engines. Samsung and SK hynix are pointing the memory roadmap at stacked HBM and a finished HBM4 design, while a one-gigawatt solid-state-transformer order and a wafer-shipment forecast set the supply picture around the chips.

### Top Story
NVIDIA announced plans with Amkor Technology, IBIDEN, King Yuan Electronics, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Siliconware Precision Industries and Unimicron to expand substrates, advanced packaging, testing and optical engines for its AI factory platform. Jensen Huang said demand is extraordinary and capacity is the constraint. Substrate capacity is planned to rise 2.5 times over the next three years: under an agreement IBIDEN signed and announced earlier this year, NVIDIA is providing a multibillion-dollar capacity reservation advance for the Ono Plant in Japan, targeting three times current levels there; Samsung Electro-Mechanics is joining from existing South Korean lines, with upgrades and new factories in Sejong and Busan; and NVIDIA is backing Unimicron’s KF substrate expansion in Taiwan with a capacity reservation. With Amkor, the plan targets a greater than 300 percent increase in interposer wafer capacity in South Korea by the end of 2028. NVIDIA expects total semiconductor testing capacity to double from calendar 2026 to 2027, including a $1.1 billion capacity reservation advance to King Yuan Electronics for new U.S. testing facilities and equipment, with further buildouts in Taiwan and Singapore. With SPIL, manufacturing capacity in Taiwan for optical engines used in co-packaged optics is targeted to rise 15 times. ### Silicon
**What Samsung Unveiled at FMS 2026: zHBM, zNAND-O and V10 BV-NAND: shattered.io**
At FMS 2026 in Santa Clara on August 4, Samsung showed three memory concepts rather than a shipping flagship, shattered.io reports from Samsung’s technical announcement. zHBM stacks memory vertically above the accelerator instead of placing HBM beside it on an interposer. Kim Kyoung-ryun said zHBM delivers up to eight times the performance per GPU and up to three times the performance per watt compared with HBM5, a generation that has not reached commercial shipment. zNAND-O, in four- and eight-layer versions of stacked V-NAND with through-silicon vias, is aimed at on-device AI, and V10 bonding-vertical NAND is described as exceeding 400 layers. SK hynix, in the same write-up, says it has finished HBM4 development and is preparing mass production. Source: [shattered.io](https://shattered.io/what-samsung-unveiled-at-fms-2026-zhbm-znand-o-and-v10-bv-nand/)

**Navitas Unveils Next-Generation Ultra-High-Voltage SiC Power Module: Semiconductor Digest**
Navitas announced the G4H08MT23GB4, a 2300-volt full-bridge silicon-carbide module in the SiCPAK form factor with an on-resistance of 8 milliohms, and said samples are available immediately. The part extends a 2300-volt and 3300-volt GeneSiC portfolio and is aimed at solid-state transformers that take grid power to the high-voltage DC rails inside AI data-centre racks, as well as grid storage, solar inverters and megawatt EV charging. An aluminum-nitride active-metal-braze substrate supports isolation of over 5 kilovolts, and epoxy-resin potting is described as delivering 10 times the thermal conductivity of silicone gel. Press-fit pins are rated at twice the current per pin of standard press-fit connections, with optional pre-applied thermal interface material and high-current pin variants. ### Data Centres & Power
**Data Centers Can Delay, Not Dodge, the Power Crunch: Data Center Knowledge**
An Infosys analysis of AI energy demand through 2030, reported by Data Center Knowledge, argues that efficiency and flexible workloads can free usable capacity sooner than new generation, transmission and grid upgrades, without removing the need for that infrastructure. Mit Majumdar of Infosys said those measures are more likely to delay than eliminate new build, and that the path is dual-track: use what exists while accelerating generation, transmission and grid modernization. Model training, retraining and batch work can often move with grid conditions; real-time inference and customer-facing applications generally cannot. Utilities, he said, should require historical performance, real-time monitoring and contractual commitments, with operators guaranteeing claims and utilities setting the verification standard. Source: [datacenterknowledge.com](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/data-centers-can-delay-not-dodge-the-power-crunch)

### The Teardown: When the Panel Gives the Count Back
A large AI package is a rectangle, and the workpiece under it has spent decades as a circle. Semiconductor Engineering’s account of the wafer-to-panel shift, drawing on ASE and Intel Foundry engineers, starts from that mismatch. As the package grows, more of a 300-millimetre wafer’s process time lands outside the circle of usable silicon. A panel gives the corners back. It also exposes how much of “the process” was really a habit of the round part: spin, stepper, single-sided handling, a hot plate sized for a wafer.

Teck Chong Lee of ASE put the practical limit in one flow. Running assembly on a 600-millimetre panel is high risk, and the equipment to do the whole job is not all in place. One option is to finish the interposer at 600 millimetres, then cut it into four pieces before assembly and the C4 bump process. The area win is taken where the tools exist, then partly handed back where they do not.

The same study counts a 5.5-times-reticle interposer. A 300-millimetre wafer yields 9. A 300-millimetre panel yields 16. A 600-millimetre panel divided into four 300-millimetre pieces yields 64. Leaving that 600-millimetre panel intact yields 72. Seventy-two against nine is eight times the wafer, more than the roughly fivefold gap in raw area, because the rectangle wastes almost nothing at the rim. Intel’s Tarik Ibrahim, in a conference presentation covered in the same piece, used a 510-by-515-millimetre panel: a package of roughly four times a reticle field is about 10 units on a 300-millimetre wafer and approximately 48 on the panel. The largest square that fits the wafer is only about 210 by 210 millimetres, so past that outline the wafer is not merely less economical. It cannot hold the part.

What breaks is not the arithmetic. At wafer level the process is single-sided. On a panel it is dual-sided, and uniformity and process capability have to hold across a sheet the tools were not born for. Spin coating needs radial symmetry; on a rectangle the film thins toward the corners and most of the resist is thrown off. In a 600-millimetre flow, photoresist goes down by slit or slot-die coating across an active area of roughly 586 by 586 millimetres. Lithography often leaves the wafer stepper for maskless direct imaging. Fine-line redistribution on glass is being pushed from roughly 5 micrometres line and space toward 2 micrometres and below, which asks for something closer to front-end overlay and temperature control. Front-end tools that already do that precision still assume a round 300-millimetre wafer. Older substrate tools were built for coarser features. The panel sits between those two equipment worlds, and neither hands over a complete line.

Ibrahim’s split is the decision rule. For some form factors the wafer is still the cheaper workpiece, because the panel’s extra area does not yet buy extra good units after yield, handling and tool qualification. Past a size the presentation does not pin to a single number, the panel wins because the unit count jumps — 10 to about 48 in Intel’s four-reticle example, 9 to 72 if a 5.5-reticle interposer can stay intact. The intact panel stops being the right workpiece at the step that has no qualified tool. ASE’s four-way cut is that step: assembly and C4. Seventy-two falls to 64, and the rectangles you spent the interposer module earning are sawn back into pieces the existing line can hold. Source: [semiengineering.com](https://semiengineering.com/what-breaks-when-packaging-moves-from-wafer-to-panel/)
