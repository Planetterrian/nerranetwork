# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **More than ten thousand jobs ride on TSMC's Arizona expansion groundbreaking December fourth.**

**What You Need to Know:** More than ten thousand direct jobs are attached to the expansion groundbreaking President Donald Trump is set to attend on December fourth at TSMC’s Phoenix site, where twelve thousand to fifteen thousand construction and support workers are already on the ground. Huawei and ChangXin Memory Technologies have papers on the February ISSCC draft program, including a high-bandwidth memory design US media describe as twice the data-pin density of Samsung and SK hynix parts now in mass production. Nvidia has told Dell and Supermicro to stop shipping servers into Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia under last week’s US licence rules.

### Top Story
President Donald Trump will attend a groundbreaking on December fourth for an expansion of TSMC’s semiconductor plant in Phoenix, a company spokesperson confirmed, and Bloomberg reported a White House official expects TSMC executives plus supplier and customer representatives at the ceremony. Whether Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang attends has not been decided. TSMC already manufactures Nvidia’s Blackwell chips, and chips for AMD and Apple, at the first Arizona plant. The White House official said the expansion is expected to create more than ten thousand direct jobs once completed, and that twelve thousand to fifteen thousand construction and support workers are currently at the site. TSMC first announced the Arizona plant near the end of Trump’s first administration and, in March last year, unveiled a one hundred sixty-five billion dollar US investment plan to expand advanced semiconductor production in the country. The December fourth event is a groundbreaking for the expansion, not an energized new fab. Source: [biz.chosun.com](https://biz.chosun.com/en/en-it/2026/11/26/FVJ6OVBMQBEMJB2M7QJL4S4ZCE/)

### Silicon
**China's Chipmakers Step Up Efforts to Catch Samsung Electronics and SK hynix: Fnnews**
IEEE’s draft program for the International Solid-State Circuits Conference, set for San Francisco next February, previews papers from Huawei and ChangXin Memory Technologies. Huawei plans to present next-generation high-bandwidth memory; US media reported the data-pin density is twice that of products Samsung Electronics and SK hynix currently have in mass production, a design that in theory would raise memory bandwidth. A memory-industry official said the deals are mainly to avoid legal disputes after YMTC filed broadly, and that YMTC’s approach has also made it less exposed to US controls on advanced manufacturing equipment, with some leading firms now licensing the technology. ### The Teardown: Years of Capacity Against Months of Permission
A supply commitment is a dated promise to take wafers, memory and buildings that do not exist yet. Longbridge, reading Nvidia’s CFO commentary, puts those supply and capacity commitments at three hundred ninety billion dollars. About two hundred three billion dollars falls in fiscal 2028, roughly February 2027 through January 2028, and about ninety-two billion dollars in fiscal 2029. Together that is about three quarters of the book. Inventory has risen to thirty-nine point seven billion dollars. The bottleneck Longbridge describes is no longer the processor alone. SK hynix said in October 2026 that, counting long-term agreements and demand still under negotiation, its DRAM and high-bandwidth memory output through 2029 is largely booked, and Nvidia’s commitments grew mainly through multi-year memory purchases. Power and credit sit on the same timeline. Nvidia has guarantees tied to customers’ and partners’ land, power, buildings and systems, with maximum gross exposure of one hundred eighteen billion dollars. Its neocloud agreements carry financing, deployment and acceptance conditions. The International Energy Agency’s Energy and AI outlook, as Longbridge cites it, projects data-centre electricity use of about nine hundred forty-five terawatt-hours by 2030, and says about twenty percent of planned projects could be delayed by grid constraints.

Walk the commitment book without treating it as cash already spent. Start at three hundred ninety billion dollars. Fiscal 2028’s two hundred three billion and fiscal 2029’s ninety-two billion add to two hundred ninety-five billion, the three-quarter slice that sits two fiscal years out. The nearer quarter is real, but the commentary Longbridge uses does not split it by quarter, so it stays an unnamed remainder. The one hundred eighteen billion dollars of guarantees is a different stack. It is not a purchase order for HBM. It is a backstop on land, power, buildings and systems, and it is gross exposure, a ceiling, not a forecast of what will be drawn. Acceptance conditions matter because a neocloud that cannot take delivery does not convert a guarantee into a running cluster. The twenty percent figure is a project-delay share in the IEA projection, not a slice of the one hundred eighteen billion. Multiplying them would invent a loss the source never calculated. What the two numbers do show, side by side, is that chip commitments can be fully placed while a fifth of the buildings those chips were meant to fill are still waiting on a grid connection. Memory makes the same split in time: a book through 2029 at SK hynix is a physical allocation, and it does not move if the building slips.

That guarantee-for-the-shell choice stops being the right tool when the thing that fails is not the customer’s ability to finance a building. Longbridge’s own observation is that commitments are counted in years while recent policy windows are counted in months: the latest US-China truce extension was only two months, and the export rules carry a sixty-day comment period. A prepayment to a memory supplier, which management told Tom’s Hardware it may offer, clears a supply queue. It does not clear a licence, and it does not energize a substation. When the binding limit is who may lawfully use the compute, or whether the interconnect arrives before the acceptance window, the one hundred eighteen billion dollars of building guarantees is aimed at the constraint that is no longer the one in front of the cluster.
