# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **Samsung plans fivefold Xi'an V9 NAND growth, to fifty thousand wafers, for AI SSDs.**

**What You Need to Know:** Samsung is planning to lift 286-layer V9 NAND at its Xi'an X2 fab from about 10,000 wafers a month to about 50,000 by the end of 2027, aimed at enterprise SSDs for AI data centers. Chinese cloud buyers are cutting 2027 memory orders, and TrendForce now puts that year's NAND revenue near $419.3 billion and DRAM revenue at $1.15 trillion. In Phoenix, a utility contract would take TSMC toward 1.88 gigawatts.

### Top Story
Samsung is planning a large internal conversion of V9 NAND at its second Xi'an fab, not a new factory, TechPowerUp reports from Seoul Economic Daily and industry sources. The X2 line now turns out about 10,000 300 mm wafers a month of 286-layer V9; the plan is about 50,000 by the end of 2027, aimed at enterprise SSDs for AI data centers. An electroplating line would go into empty cleanroom space, with equipment moving in around February 2027 and the conversion finished by November 2027, while 20% to 30% of the fab's existing V6 line would also switch to V9. Samsung has previously denied claims of expanding production capacity, so the report reads as a reshuffle inside an existing fab even as the wafer count rises sharply. NAND spot prices have already started falling. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/355189/samsung-doubles-down-on-v9-nand-in-china)

### Data Centres & Power
**TSMC Arizona offers a look at what could come to Brazos County: KBTX**
TSMC's north Phoenix campus is under construction toward a contracted load of about 1.88 gigawatts, KBTX reports, with the first fab already in production since late 2024. Arizona Public Service signed a direct pricing contract on October 16 to ramp service to about 1.88 gigawatts for fabs and advanced packaging over an initial 25-year term. Engineers at the site's water resource center say the campus uses 4.75 million gallons of water a day, mostly reclaimed wastewater plus a small amount of groundwater, all through the City of Phoenix, and TSMC says it recycles 65% of intake, about 3.5 million gallons a day. Arizona's Family, cited by KBTX, put the first fab at about 5,300 acre-feet a year, roughly 1.7 billion gallons, enough for about 15,000 Phoenix households; Sarah Porter of Arizona State University's Kyl Center estimated about 15% or slightly more evaporates. The campus also makes nearly 15 megawatts of solar on site, with panels doubling as covered parking for about 4,000 vehicles. The first three fabs are estimated to employ 6,000 people, up from about 3,500; the county lists the project as an active Title V air-permit source; the 2025 sustainability report says most pollutants were reduced 95% or more and volatile organic compounds by 98.9%; and TSMC says it recycles 96% of waste, with less than 1% reaching a landfill. Source: [kbtx.com](https://www.kbtx.com/2026/11/24/tsmc-arizona-offers-look-what-could-come-brazos-county/)

### The Teardown: The Core Count Left in the First Weights
A published weight file still carries a trace of the machine that first filled it. Training does not start from a blank tensor. A library writes an initial pattern, and that pattern is a function of how the initialization kernel was launched: how many streaming multiprocessors took part, and how the random streams were mapped onto them. Israel Defense, reporting a forensic study by Unemploymentlabs, says researchers reconstructed those initial values from publicly released model weights and then searched for the hardware and initialization configurations that reproduce the numbers exactly. The evidence is in the weights themselves, not in a leak or an insider account.

The discriminator they publish is the core count. A Blackwell B200 or B300 signature in this method is 148 streaming multiprocessors. A GB200 or GB300 signature is 152. Six models released since April 2026 by DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group contain patterns consistent with Blackwell, while earlier models from the same firms line up with Hopper. DeepSeek-V4-Flash and MiniMax-M3 match the 148-core B200 or B300 pattern. DeepSeek-V4-Pro, Kimi K3, Kimi K3.1-Pro and Ling-3.1-flash match the 152-core GB200 or GB300 pattern. For DeepSeek-V4-Flash and Kimi K3, the match was checked on physical Blackwell chips, not only in a search over configurations. DeepSeek-V3, Kimi K2.5 and Ling-3.0-flash stay on the Hopper side of the same test. Kimi K2.7-Code sits in between: Hopper-era weights, plus a minor portion consistent with B200 or B300. That is a generation change with a date window, not a single ambiguous file.

The control set is the part that makes the count usable. The method was run on 24 models whose developers had already disclosed the hardware. Every one matched the disclosure, including Nvidia's Nemotron-3 Ultra, which showed the same 152-core signature. Israel Defense says those correlations are far stronger than a statistical-chance explanation. The only other configuration the researchers identified that could mimic the pattern is custom-configured AMD hardware, which is itself restricted for export to China. That alternative was ruled out for DeepSeek-V4-Flash and called highly improbable for the other five of the six.

The fingerprint names how the weights were generated. It supports the hypothesis that Blackwell also ran the training, and it does not prove that hypothesis. It also does not say where the chips sat, how access was obtained, or whether an export rule was broken. The design choice — treat a reproduced initialization, verified on silicon for two models and against 24 disclosed cases, as evidence of Blackwell use — stops being the right instrument when the question is a place, a shipment, or a violation, or when a later training stack could have left the initialization's machine behind. At that point the 148-versus-152 split still describes the first fill, and it no longer identifies the training run. Source: [israeldefense.co.il](https://www.israeldefense.co.il/en/node/73236)
