# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **More than 400 gigawatts of planned data-center load is now chasing power.**

**What You Need to Know:** More than 400 gigawatts of new data-center load is in planning, and power — not the announcement — decides what gets built. Industrial Info Resources puts Microsoft, Meta, Amazon and Google on pace to announce more than $700 billion of data-center spending this year, against a US fleet that is already 53 gigawatts. A reported CoWoS price rise and Anthropic’s IPO filing put packaging and Taiwan manufacturing on the same bill.

### Top Story
Industrial Info Resources, speaking at Data Center World Power in September and reported by Data Center Knowledge, expects Microsoft, Meta, Amazon and Google to announce more than $700 billion of data-center spending this year, up from a combined $64 billion in 2018. The US now has 53 gigawatts of operational data-center load, up from 23 gigawatts in 2023, with another 42 gigawatts approved or under construction; IIR frames demand as moving from 35 gigawatts in 2024 to 125 gigawatts by 2030. More than 400 gigawatts of new data-center load is in planning, almost 100 US sites are designed for at least 1 gigawatt, and the US and Canada pipeline is about $4.75 trillion across more than 4,250 projects. IIR counts more than 500 data-center moratoria across 42 states and $130 billion of projects rejected in the first quarter of 2026; New York has a temporary ban on new data centers larger than 50 megawatts, and Texas has paused new grid interconnections while it works through about 200 gigawatts of requests. GE Vernova, Mitsubishi Power and Siemens Energy are sold out of larger gas turbines through 2029, with some orders stretching into 2031. Shane Mullins of IIR said that if a project needs more than 300 megawatts and the utility queue says 2034, building or contracting a gas plant is the fastest path; IIR also counts $228 billion of gas-plant projects across 347 grassroots, expansion and conversion jobs, 111 nuclear projects worth $311.4 billion including 61 small modular reactors, and 701 battery projects worth almost $200 billion. Source: [datacenterknowledge.com](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/the-power-challenge-behind-hyperscale-ai-expansion)

### Silicon
**Micron Plans 400-Layer 3D NAND with Wafer-to-Wafer Bonding in 2027: TechPowerUp**
Micron has announced a plan, not a shipment, to bring G11 NAND to production and to ship 400-layer NAND in the second half of 2027. G9, which is shipping across the product line, uses more than 200 layers; G11 is meant to double module capacity by stacking a memory-cell wafer on a peripheral-logic wafer. The join is hybrid bonding, wafer to wafer, the structural approach YMTC has already used in Xtacking: metal bumps in the two wafers fuse into one chip. ### Supply Chain & Policy
**Demand and Complexities of AI GPUs and Custom ASICs Have Stressed TSMC’s CoWoS Supply: Wccftech**
China Times supply-chain sources, reported by Wccftech and Yahoo Finance and not confirmed by TSMC, say the foundry is discussing a CoWoS price increase of 10 to 20 percent. The reported pressure is AI GPUs, custom ASICs and server CPUs that put more compute dies and HBM stacks on one package, which enlarges each unit, cuts hourly tool output and lengthens test. Wccftech says reported capacity is 130,000 monthly wafers in 2026 and 200,000 units in 2027; Yahoo, citing the same China Times report, says monthly capacity is expected to exceed 130,000 units in 2026 and 200,000 units next year, so the two accounts do not use the same unit word. Industry sources in the Wccftech account say a new advanced-packaging plant takes about three years to reach full load at its bottleneck stations, and equipment lead times have stretched from 3–6 months to 6–8 months. TSMC’s 5x-reticle CoWoS packages are in volume production, 9x and 14x versions are in development, and panel-based CoPoS is aimed at larger packages and lower cost; Yahoo names Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Qualcomm and MediaTek among customers who would feel a higher packaging price. Source: [wccftech.com](https://wccftech.com/tsmc-cowos-price-hike-20-percent-ai-gpu-asic-demand/)

**Anthropic Warns Disruptions to TSMC’s Operations Could Hit AI Chip Supply: BusinessLine**
In a registration statement filed with the US SEC on October 26, Anthropic said most of the world’s most advanced chip manufacturing sits at TSMC in Taiwan, and that a disruption there would hit its ability to train and deploy models. The filing says military conflict, tariffs and trade restrictions, economic coercion, a blockade, a natural disaster, or a loss of electricity or water could severely disrupt advanced-chip supply, and that capacity outside Taiwan, including in the US, is still early-stage. It also says TSMC has at times been unable to meet full demand, leaving Anthropic short of chips on time. Seoul Economic Daily, reading the same filing, says Anthropic names SK hynix, Samsung Electronics and Micron as the main HBM producers and warns they may not deliver needed volumes on time, especially if other large buyers lock a supplier’s output. That paper’s simple sum of seven disclosed contracts is about $637 billion, led by a Broadcom AI-accelerator lease of about $161.2 billion, Google cloud commitments of at least $114.5 billion, Amazon of at least $110 billion and Microsoft of at least $44.9 billion, plus SpaceXAI, Nscale and Lambda; the paper says the total mixes minimums, equipment leases and volume-based amounts, so it is not one fixed investment. Anthropic’s computing and infrastructure costs were $8.945 billion in the first half of this year, up 283 percent, while revenue rose to $16.286 billion from $1.163 billion. Source: [thehindubusinessline.com](https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/info-tech/anthropic-warns-disruptions-to-tsmcs-operations-could-hit-ai-chip-supply-threaten-business/article71629778.ece)

### The Teardown: When the Advance Payment Buys the Slot
Wafer, high-bandwidth memory, a packaging substrate and a test socket can each stop an AI chip, so the delivery date is no longer set by the foundry handoff alone. 36Kr describes the older chain as a defined split: design houses specified the part, wafer foundries manufactured it, and packaging and test plants took the next step, joined by orders and shared standards. That split assumed each link could be booked after the one before it. AI parts broke the sequence because the performance ceiling is set together by the advanced process, the memory and the package. A larger die and a larger package also consume more wafer and more packaging area per chip, so a shortage in one input idles the others.

The wafer side is already spoken for. TrendForce estimated in March 2026 that global foundry revenue would rise 24.8 percent for the full year, to about $218.8 billion, and that TSMC’s 5-nanometer, 4-nanometer and more advanced capacity would stay fully loaded through the end of 2026. 36Kr says that load is Nvidia and AMD GPUs plus custom chips from Google, AWS and Meta. A full advanced-node line does not finish the accelerator if the package, the substrate or the test insertion is late. The scarce object stops being a wafer start and becomes a reserved place in every downstream tool.

The reservation is now paid before the tools exist. In July 2026 Amkor signed a $1.5 billion multi-year advanced-packaging and development agreement with Nvidia, and Nvidia’s advance payment was to support Amkor’s US capacity build. On October 21 the two added a South Korea expansion; 36Kr puts the US and Korea agreements together at $3 billion. Test follows the same pattern. Nvidia provided $1.1 billion to King Yuan Electronics as a capacity-reservation advance, funding a US test factory and the equipment to run it. On substrates, the October 21 arrangements with Ibiden, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and XinXing Electronics are planned to take the relevant substrate capacity to 2.5 times the current level, and Ibiden’s expansion of its Ono plant in Japan is also backed by an Nvidia advance. Cash plus a multi-year volume promise moves a supplier’s build plan earlier than a spot order would. The supplier gets a demand signal it can borrow against; the customer gets a claim on capacity that a later purchase order might not secure.

That choice stops being the right way to buy a chip when the check lands on a link that is not the late one. 36Kr notes that 2.5D packaging of the CoWoS type has been tight since 2023 and that the squeeze has spread to packaging equipment, substrates and key materials, so more wafers, or even a larger packaging house, still leave delivery gated by whichever input did not get the money. It also stops working for anyone but the largest buyers once the line is allocated by who can write the advance. 36Kr’s warning is polarization: firms with capital and the ability to commit long orders take scarce capacity first. A reservation that clears the bottleneck only for those buyers fails the moment the missing part is a tool, a substrate or a material the prepaid supplier cannot pull forward, and it fails the buyer who needs the chip but cannot fund the factory that has to build it. Source: [eu.36kr.com](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/4043465786889350)
