# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **Only 2.3 gigawatts looks delayed despite more than 300 local data-center bans.**

**What You Need to Know:** SemiAnalysis finds that more than 300 local data-center moratoriums are likely to delay only about 2.3 gigawatts, inside roughly 21 gigawatts sitting in moratorium areas. The same audit still has 38 gigawatts of U.S. data-center IT capacity coming online in 2027. Separately, Samsung is set to build Nvidia’s custom high-bandwidth-memory base dies on TSMC’s three-nanometre process.

### Top Story
Tom's Hardware reports a SemiAnalysis audit that finds more than 300 local data-center moratoriums likely to delay only three projects. Those three account for about 2.3 gigawatts of roughly 21 gigawatts located in moratorium areas, and that delay total leaves out Texas’s TCEQ permit pause. The firm’s forecast of 38 gigawatts of U.S. data-center IT capacity in 2027, more than double 2026, has barely moved over the last 12 months. Of that 2027 total, 22 gigawatts is under vertical construction and 16 gigawatts is mostly financed, with site work underway. Enacted local moratoriums cover about 20 gigawatts, of which about 1.5 gigawatts is delayed, and New York’s pause adds 0.8 gigawatts. A moratorium here is a temporary legal pause, almost always a freeze on new applications, and it moves a delivery date only if it reaches the parcel, stays in force, overlaps the schedule, and covers a use the project cannot work around. ### Silicon
**Samsung to use TSMC’s 3nm process for Nvidia NVHBM base dies: The Edge Malaysia**
Samsung will use TSMC’s three-nanometre process for base dies on Nvidia’s custom high-bandwidth memory, NVHBM, the first time Samsung has worked with TSMC at the HBM manufacturing stage, the Seoul Economic Daily reported. Nvidia has asked Samsung, SK hynix and Micron to use that process for the base dies and sixth-generation 1c DRAM for the core dies. NVHBM, unveiled in August and built on HBM4E, puts Nvidia’s memory controller on the base die. BigGo Finance reports the physical-layer area cut by 60 percent versus standard HBM, for up to 30 percent more bandwidth and 15 percent lower power than standard HBM4E. Industry estimates put TSMC’s 3nm yield above 90 percent and Samsung Foundry’s 2nm SF2 process near 60 percent. Samsung keeps its own 4nm turnkey base dies for standard HBM4 and HBM4E and for custom parts for other customers, including AMD, and is validating an NVHBM base die on its 2nm process if TSMC capacity fills up. **Intel Razor Lake-AX quoted at 700 TOPS: TechPowerUp**
Intel senior vice president Cho Min-sung said at the Semiconductors Powering Humanoids summit in South Korea that Razor Lake-AX will carry 700 TOPS of on-die AI compute at INT8/FP8. TechPowerUp puts that at 3.88 times the 180 TOPS it attributes to current Panther Lake mobile chips. Earlier reports cited by the outlet describe a 16-core integrated GPU and a 32-core flagship integrated GPU on Xe3, and a GPU die of about 162.84 square millimetres. The same reports say on-package LPDDR5X or LPDDR6 returns with this AX part, and the image with the story is an illustration, not the package. ### Data Centres & Power
**Local pauses miss the parcel, the permit, or the clock: Tom's Hardware**
SemiAnalysis is tracking more than 400 local instruments across 17 states as of September, including a few permanent zoning bans, and New York, Texas, Pennsylvania and Oregon each acted in under two months this summer. Michigan has 45 enacted moratoriums and effectively zero pipeline exposure, with a Google campus on industrial land already zoned for data processing. SpaceX’s planned data center in Brownsville, Texas, sits on land the city removed from its limits, so the city moratorium does not reach it. In Pennsylvania a township pause landed before NorthPoint filed its special exception, pushing local approval to next year, and the company is still pursuing the project. The Economist’s Archie Hall, cited from a Michigan and Ohio trip, calls the moratoriums much more bark than bite for now. An August poll by the firm found more Americans negative than positive on data centers, and the analysis says pauses would need to be materially more widespread and restrictive to delay the buildout. **Texas tells its environmental regulator to stop data-center permits: Tom's Hardware**
Texas Governor Greg Abbott told the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality to issue no permits for data-center projects. Law firm Bracewell says that instruction applies whether or not a project is in ERCOT’s queue. SemiAnalysis analyst Jeremie Eliahou Ontiveros said on the firm’s podcast last month that a strict state-level moratorium blocking even off-grid projects is the case that would worry the forecast, and he cited Texas. New York Governor Kathy Hochul, like Abbott, bypassed the legislature for a pause the governor controls and can revoke. **UBS puts next year’s HBM bits 11 percent short: BigGo Finance**
UBS, cited by BigGo Finance, projects next year’s HBM demand at about 61.5 billion gigabits, up 91 percent, against new shipments of 54.6 billion gigabits, up 49 percent, an 11 percent shortfall. Samsung’s 1c core dies run at Hwaseong Line 17 and Pyeongtaek P2, P3 and P4. The P5-1 facility, under construction, is slated to center on 1c lines. Samsung’s 4nm base-die lines are limited to portions of Hwaseong Line 17 and Pyeongtaek P3. TSMC, at its 15 October earnings announcement, said it will add two 3nm lines at Fab 18 in Tainan and also plans 3nm lines at its second Arizona fab and its second Kumamoto fab. ### The Teardown: When One Pool Has to Hold the Scene
A core that cannot see its weights in the same address space has to wait for a copy. iTWire’s account of Nvidia’s RTX Spark laptops describes the machine that tries to delete that wait: one superchip, a Blackwell RTX GPU with up to 6,144 CUDA cores beside a Grace CPU with up to 20 cores, joined by NVLink-C2C, sharing up to 128 gigabytes of unified memory and rated at up to 1 petaflop of FP4. MediaTek’s piece of that CPU, the same report says, was power efficiency and connectivity. The mechanism is the pool, not the logo on the lid. On a split PC the GPU owns a private frame buffer. A tensor that begins in system memory is copied across a peripheral link, used, and often copied back before the CPU can trust it. Unified memory makes the CPU and the GPU clients of one physical pool. A load from either side is an address in that pool. Coherence is the interconnect’s job. If the line is already there, nobody stages a second copy to make the other processor legal. FP4 is the precision tied to the 1-petaflop rating. Four-bit weights are how 128 gigabytes holds a larger network than a wider format would allow in the same bytes. That rating is a peak at FP4. It is not a second number for FP8 or FP16, where the same cores move less work per watt and the same pool holds fewer parameters.

Walk only the figures iTWire prints. Systems from Asus, Dell, HP, Lenovo, Microsoft and MSI start at $2,399, the figure in Nvidia's announcement; iTWire's report put it at $2,399. Set the memory ceiling at 128 gigabytes and set Nvidia’s reported scene claim beside it: 3D scenes of up to 90 gigabytes. That scene is not a side buffer. It is most of the only pool the chip has. The operating system, the tool, and any agent mapped into the same addresses have to live in what the scene does not already occupy. There is no second tier in the description to spill into. An edit and a local model, if both are open, are roommates in that 128 gigabytes, which is the product claim of one PC for creation, gaming and personal agents. The gaming line next to it is AAA titles at 1440p at over 100 frames per second, a shader-and-bandwidth claim on those 6,144 CUDA cores, not evidence of a private graphics memory beside the pool. Adobe is optimising Photoshop, Premiere and Substance 3D for the platform, with up to 2x gains in beta builds at launch. A gain of that size on tools that stream large frames is what shows up when a peripheral copy was a large share of the old timeline. The Grace side, up to 20 cores, is the control plane: scheduling, I/O, the agent loop. It is not a second accelerator hiding in the same package. At a $2,399 start the comparison is not a data-centre GPU tray. It is whether one coherent pool, capped at these figures, replaces a desktop that split CPU DRAM from GPU memory and paid the copy on every handoff.

The single superchip stops being the right box when the live working set no longer fits in 128 gigabytes, or when the result required is not an FP4 peak of 1 petaflop. A 90-gigabyte scene already takes most of the pool. A second scene, a weight that will not quantise to four bits, or a context that has to stay wide, turns the next address into a miss this package cannot cure by adding a card. The partner list and the $2,399 floor do not move that ceiling. The ceiling is the pool.
