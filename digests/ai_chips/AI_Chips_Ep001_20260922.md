# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **New Era signed a 20-year gas PPA to power a 250 MW data center in Texas from an adjacent plant.**

**What You Need to Know:** Three data-center power and capacity announcements moved forward today alongside new photonics and memory IP launches. Grid and generation deals continue to set the pace for where large AI loads can actually operate. Packaging capacity forecasts and new validation hubs add further constraints on accelerator supply timelines.

### What This Show Covers
The physical layer of AI breaks into three layers: the chips themselves, the systems they sit in, and the sites plus power infrastructure that house them. The quantity the show tracks most closely is gigawatts of AI capacity announced versus under construction versus energized. Models and software belong to the sister show Models & Agents. This show covers the hardware, the buildings, the power, and the money. AI Chips & Data Centres Daily is part of the Nerra Network, an independent, ad-free network of shows at nerranetwork.com.

### Top Story
New Era signed a 20-year power purchase agreement with a Vistra subsidiary to supply a 250 MW data center in Ector County, Texas. The facility will draw power from an adjacent natural gas plant in Odessa. The deal secures long-term generation directly tied to the site load. The agreement covers the full 250 MW requirement through the dedicated on-site plant without reliance on broader grid interconnection queues. No energization date was stated in the announcement. The 20-year term provides multi-decade visibility on both power pricing and availability for the operator. Source: [datacenterdynamics.com](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/new-era-signs-20-year-gas-ppa-with-vistra-subsidiary-to-power-250mw-data-center-in-ector-county-texas/)

### The Teardown: CoWoS Capacity Scaling Limits
CoWoS advanced packaging throughput is constrained by the number of usable interposer reticles that can be processed per month on 300 mm wafers and the subsequent TSV reveal and hybrid bonding steps. A single 300 mm wafer yields roughly 70–90 reticle fields depending on interposer size; each field supports one large GPU-plus-HBM stack. Current industry CoWoS lines run at 8 000–12 000 wafers per month across all suppliers, translating to roughly 600 000–1 000 000 packaged units annually before yield losses. Adding a second bonding layer for larger interposers drops effective throughput by 25–35 % because alignment and thermal-compression steps lengthen cycle time. The 2027 demand forecast nearly doubling from 2026 therefore requires either new clean-room bays or a shift to larger 510 mm panels that are still in pilot. Until those lines reach volume, allocation remains the binding constraint on accelerator shipments rather than front-end wafer starts. Reticle field utilization also varies with the exact interposer dimensions chosen by each GPU vendor, further tightening available capacity when designs move to multi-reticle interposers. Hybrid bonding overlay tolerances below 1 µm add another process-time multiplier that has not yet been fully offset by parallel tooling.
