# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **One hundred forty-four SSDs extend one Nvidia rack past high-bandwidth memory to two trillion vectors.**

**What You Need to Know:** Kioxia and Nvidia demonstrated a GB200 NVL72 fitted with one hundred forty-four SSDs addressing a database of two trillion vectors, using software to stretch effective GPU memory toward petabyte scale. TSMC’s A14 design kit is open for a 1.4-nanometre node with mass production set for 2028. A 500-megawatt campus is planned outside San Diego.

### Top Story
Kioxia and Nvidia have demonstrated, not shipped as a catalog product, a way to stretch effective GPU memory toward petabyte scale by putting NVMe SSDs behind Nvidia’s SCADA software under the Storage-Next initiative. On an Nvidia GB200 NVL72 rack-scale system, the setup processed a database of two trillion vectors using 144 Kioxia XD8 Series 7.68 TB E1.S NVMe SSDs. SCADA, Scaled Accelerated Data Access, is a software library meant to extend effective GPU memory with NVMe drives, including fine-grained reads at small block sizes. With high-bandwidth memory alone, the same growth in AI datasets can force a workload to be split and the data moved repeatedly between memory and slower storage. The demonstration is aimed at making larger sets reachable without that split. ### The Teardown: The Stitch Past One Reticle
A lithography scanner prints one field at a time, and that field is the reticle. Anything larger than one field has to be built by placing fields next to each other and keeping the seams aligned. Yahoo Finance, reporting TSMC’s account of its own packaging, says CoWoS is already in production at five times reticle size, with what TSMC calls great yields. That package is the floor an AI accelerator and its high-bandwidth memory sit on: the compute die and the memory stacks share one interposer, and the wires between them stay on that substrate instead of leaving the package for a board.

The multiple is a count of those fields, not a new transistor. Five times means the production package is five reticle fields stitched into one. Each added field is more area for a larger processor or for more memory stacks, and it is also another seam the bond, the via, and the warpage of the wafer have to survive. Yield at five times is the claim that those seams are already under control in volume, not a lab coupon. Yahoo Finance says TSMC is working on solutions for nine-times and fourteen-times reticle designs, because AI systems are combining processors and memory in larger packages. Five times is what a customer can order as a production flow. Nine times and fourteen times are the flows still being built.

Take the three multiples as the only scale the source gives. The production package is the five-times field. The next design the factory is working is nine times, and the one after that is fourteen times. The source does not publish a square-millimetre size for one field, so the jump is the multiple itself: from the size already yielding, to a package nearly twice that count of fields, to one approaching three times that count. The customer who needs more memory stacks beside the compute die is waiting on that count, not on a smaller gate.

Yahoo Finance describes a second packaging track, CoPoS, aimed at reducing packaging cost over time, while CoWoS remains the customer’s choice now. The same account says customer activity on the A14 process is already ahead of what TSMC saw for its 2-nanometre node at a similar stage, with risk production due in 2027. Two-nanometre contributed 8 percent of wafer revenue in the third quarter and still had a negative impact on gross margin during its ramp. JPMorgan, in that same piece, expects AI-related capital expenditure to reach 1.4 trillion dollars in 2027. The package and the wafer are one system: a die that is still dragging margin has to land on a stitch that already yields, or the larger multiple is a drawing, not a shipment.

Five-times CoWoS stops being the package to order when the design needs the nine-times or fourteen-times floor and that flow is the one customers are actually choosing, or when CoPoS has cut cost far enough that buyers leave the wafer stitch. Until one of those is true, the production choice Yahoo Finance records is the five-times CoWoS package. Source: [finance.yahoo.com](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/tsmc-tsm-building-semiconductor-moat-031114980.html)
