> **A two-point-five-billion-dollar Nvidia-server diversion now has one guilty plea.**

**What You Need to Know:** A two-point-five-billion-dollar Nvidia-server diversion now has one guilty plea. Broker Ting-Wei Sun admitted the export case; Super Micro co-founder Wally Liaw still faces trial on November 2. A second drone strike left Yandex modules in Kaluga out of service, and Bernstein cited HBM4 supply trouble as SK hynix widens memory output.

### Top Story
Tom's Hardware reports that Ting-Wei "Willy" Sun, a third-party broker, pleaded guilty under an agreement with prosecutors, with any cooperation terms still undisclosed. He was charged with Super Micro co-founder Yih-Shyan "Wally" Liaw and sales manager Ruei-Tsang "Steven" Chang, and sentencing is set for September 8, 2027. The alleged path ran through a Southeast Asian company that faked paperwork and built dummy servers; workers used hair dryers to move serial numbers onto those shells, a Thailand-based firm took the real servers, and Alibaba denied receiving the hardware. Super Micro denied knowledge of the operation; the report puts sales since the activity began in 2024 at $2.5 billion, and shareholders have sued, accusing the company of concealing dependence on illicit sales to China. Liaw has pleaded not guilty, is free on a $5 million bond, and is set for trial on November 2, while Chang remains a fugitive. Taiwan has indicted nine more people over illegal shipments of Nvidia B300 GPUs; Super Micro says it cooperated with U.S. and Taiwanese authorities, opened employee workstations, fired several employees after its own inquiry, and denies that its premises were raided. SK hynix is continuing a sizable share repurchase while widening its high-bandwidth memory production footprint. Reports that Solidigm mandated a Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley consortium for a U.S. listing sized at roughly $10 billion are unconfirmed by SK hynix. ### The Teardown: Why the Replay Never Sees the Missing Call
A graphics trace benchmark does not launch the application. It replays a recorded stream of graphics API calls and times how quickly the driver and the GPU finish that fixed script. The result is a playback rate. It is not a training step, and it is not a measure of how a chip moves tensors.

ServeTheHome reports that the Standard Performance Evaluation Corporation has released SPECviewperf 15.0.1 Linux Edition, the first Linux Edition in almost half a decade. SPEC first shipped a Linux build in 2019, as SPECviewperf 13, because many workstation applications are OpenGL-based or are not available on Linux at all. The new Linux suite carries 8 of the Windows edition's 11 benchmarks. The traces are from Blender 3.6, 3DEXPERIENCE CATIA and CATIA V5, Creo 9, Maya 2025, Solidworks 2024, and NX 2406, plus visualizations from OpendTect and the Tuvok library. Against the previous Linux suite, 2020 3.0, Blender is new and the CATIA, Creo, Maya, Solidworks, and NX workloads are updated. There is no DirectX path.

That gap is the mechanism. A replay can time only calls that were captured. If the application never issued the call on the platform SPEC ported, the call is not in the script, and no driver optimization on the machine under test can make it appear. The Linux score therefore describes the OpenGL subset in those eight traces, on the CPU architecture the binary actually runs. This release adds native Arm AArch64 support, the first time the graphics benchmark has been natively available on an Arm-based system, so the timed path need not include an x86 translation layer. The score still says nothing about a collective, a memory stack, or a rack.

The Linux edition stops being the right instrument when the frames that matter live in DirectX, or in one of the three Windows traces this port leaves out. Those calls never enter the script, so they cannot show up in the result. Source: [servethehome.com](https://www.servethehome.com/specviewperf-15-0-1-linux-edition-released-linuxs-key-graphics-benchmark-adds-arm-support/)
