> **Taiwan plans a Texas supplier park worth up to four billion dollars while tightening AI-chip export rules.**

**What You Need to Know:** Taiwan’s economics minister says the government plans a Texas industrial park worth up to four billion dollars for chip suppliers, in the same interview that sets criminal penalties for restricted AI-chip exports to China. On the show floor, an open AMD Helios switch tray shows why 224G in-rack copper still needs retimers. Finland’s grid rules, as described in Vienna this week, would put data centres under one megawatt ahead of larger builds.

### Top Story
Kung Ming-hsin, Taiwan’s Minister of Economic Affairs, told Bloomberg in an interview Asiae published Friday that Taipei will align some export-control measures with U.S. measures after commitments in the reciprocal trade agreement with the United States. The government plans to add AI servers and advanced semiconductors to the controlled-export list. Once those measures take effect, exporting restricted AI semiconductors to China will be a criminal offense under the Foreign Trade Act; today that export is not itself a criminal offense, so prosecutors use other laws. U.S. authorities have claimed that Nvidia products worth billions of dollars have been smuggled into China in recent years. The same interview reaffirmed a Texas industrial park worth up to $4 billion for suppliers working with Nvidia, TSMC and Foxconn, with loan guarantees and help on visas and land. Source: [asiae.co.kr](https://www.asiae.co.kr/en/article/2026101616242905832)

### Data Centres & Power
**Workload Value Enters the Data-Centre Policy Argument: Data Center Knowledge**
Data Center Knowledge reports that workload value is still an emerging policy question, not a live permitting rule, after Informa’s Data Center Europe event in Vienna on October 13–15. Sandvine’s 2024 Global Internet Phenomena Report puts video streaming, social media and related applications from Meta, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet and Netflix at about 65 percent of internet volume. Goldman Sachs has projected that AI’s share of the overall data-centre market will double to 30 percent over the next two years. Branko Jovanović, speaking on a panel, pointed to Finnish grid-connection amendments that would prioritize data centres under 1 MW, which could favor smaller enterprise or research sites over larger AI, colocation or hyperscale builds without explicitly ranking workloads by social value. The article notes there have been no significant attempts yet to write those distinctions into approvals. Source: [datacenterknowledge.com](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/regulations/could-the-societal-value-of-workloads-shape-data-center-policy-)

**Press-Connect Piping as a Cooling-Install Method: Data Center Dynamics**
In a sponsored Data Center Dynamics interview, Viega’s Stephen McAfee calls press technology the fastest way on the market to connect piping, against processes that can take hours. He ties cooling-loop reliability to five nines, about five minutes of downtime in a year. Welding or brazing is hot work: the space is isolated and other trades pause. On small-diameter pipe, he says welded joints fail more often, with X-rays showing cracks, porosity, inclusions or incomplete fusion, and a bad joint can force the system apart and back together. Contamination in secondary loops that feed processor cold plates can clog micro-fin vanes. Viega’s seal sits outside the flow line, so liquid does not contact a non-metallic surface; the same press systems are specified for chilled water, technical cooling loops, facility water and fuel. Source: [datacenterdynamics.com](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/marketwatch/the-future-of-data-center-efficiency-starts-with-pipes/)

### The Teardown: Why the Helios Switch Tray Still Needs a Retimer
Two Broadcom Tomahawk 6 switch chips, each rated 102.6T, sit side by side in a double-wide 1OU tray. ServeTheHome opened that tray on the HPE booth floor at OCP 2026. It is the HPE Juniper QFX5252, built as an AMD Helios switch tray, and the layout is the opposite of a chassis switch whose value is a wall of front-panel ports. The fabric connectors are at the back. They blind-mate into the rack’s cable cartridges. The electrical problem is the run from each switch chip to those connectors at the SerDes rate Helios actually uses.

That rate is 224G PAM4. The switch that sounds like the scale-up part is Tomahawk Ultra, and ServeTheHome spells out why it is not in this tray. The current Tomahawk Ultra is a 112G PAM4 SerDes switch: 512 lanes of PAM4-112G. A lane specified at 112G does not carry a 224G PAM4 signal, so those 512 lanes are not a Helios fabric. The tray uses Tomahawk 6 because Helios is on 224G PAM4 SerDes. Two of those 102.6T devices in a 1OU tray are why the assembly is liquid cooled. Internal manifolds feed the cold blocks on the chips.

The connectors do not sit next to the silicon. ServeTheHome notes that the cabled connections to the rear high-density fabric connectors are quite far from the Tomahawk 6 chips, and some are very far. The liquid-cooling blocks in that gap are retimers. A retimer takes a degraded high-speed waveform, samples it, and launches a clean edge so the next stretch of copper can still meet the eye opening the SerDes requires. At 224G PAM4 the channel budget is short enough that a long board-plus-cable run inside one rack fails unless something rebuilds the edge. AMD is using retimers for that reach. NVIDIA has worked to remove them. After the retimer, the tray still needs what ServeTheHome calls an enormous array of copper cables into the cartridges. Retimers buy the reach; copper still carries the lanes. That combination is the picture the site says it will use when explaining why co-packaged optics is the next simplification: move the optical engine next to the switch chip and the long electrical run goes away.

The cooling split matches the power split. The two switch chips and the retimer blocks are on liquid. Fans and airflow guides remain over heatsinks on other components. NVIDIA has been pushing fans out of the AI rack; this Helios tray still air-cools those other bits. The front latch levers are very long because the back panel is long, has to blind-mate, and the insertion force into the Helios rack is significant. ServeTheHome had already covered the QFX5252 at HPE Discover 2026. The show-floor difference is the assembled tray, built to scale up inside the rack rather than out of front-panel connectors.

The copper-plus-retimer choice stops being the right one when the cartridge connectors have to sit so far from the Tomahawk 6 that a 224G PAM4 lane only closes inside the rack if a retimer rebuilds the edge and a full copper array finishes the run. That is the condition already sitting in the open QFX5252: the retimers are there because the connectors are far, and co-packaged optics is the simplification once that distance is what in-rack copper cannot close on its own.
