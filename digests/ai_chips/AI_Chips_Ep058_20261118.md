**What You Need to Know:** Kinsus will expand advanced ABF package-substrate capacity dedicated to Nvidia by more than 3.5 times at its Youth campus in Taoyuan, with production expected to begin in the second half of 2027. ### Top Story
Nvidia is locking manufacturing capacity to demand for its accelerated-computing platforms through expanded agreements with Amkor Technology, Foxconn, Kinsus Interconnect Technology, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Siliconware Precision Industries (SPIL) and TSMC, covering substrates, advanced packaging, wafer manufacturing, contract manufacturing and system assembly. Kinsus plans to expand advanced ABF package-substrate capacity dedicated to Nvidia by more than 3.5 times at its Youth campus in Taoyuan, Taiwan, with production expected to begin in the second half of 2027. Nvidia is backing Amkor’s advanced-packaging expansion in Arizona and South Korea through multiyear agreements announced in July and October 2026, totalling nearly $3 billion and including prepayments. In Taiwan, a multiyear SPIL agreement backed by multibillion-dollar funding covers bumping, interposer wafer fabrication, assembly and testing; Digitimes reports that deal is set to triple advanced packaging capacity by 2029. Samsung Electro-Mechanics amended an existing substrate agreement to improve factory operations in South Korea, building on work in Sejong and Busan for computing and high-speed networking, while Foxconn is extending collaboration on next-generation GPU and networking platforms across Asia, North America and Europe. Nvidia said Vera Rubin NVL72 and Vera CPUs began volume shipments in the third quarter, using TSMC’s 5nm, 3nm and CoWoS advanced packaging. ### Supply Chain & Policy
**NVIDIA's Supply Chain Chief Deb Shoquist: Allocating TSMC Chips Weekly, Delivery Cycles Can Be Compressed to Two Weeks: BigGo Finance**
Deb Shoquist, Nvidia’s executive vice president of operations, described a supplier screen of three conditions — ramp cadence that matches Nvidia, technical precision for complex designs, and the balance sheet to invest ahead — and said Nvidia will inject capital if a partner has only two of the three. The network she named runs through TSMC (a partner since 1998), Foxconn, Wistron, SPIL, KYEC (packaging and test partners for more than two decades), Unimicron, Amphenol, Coherent and Lumentum; she meets major suppliers quarterly and spends six weeks a year on the road in Taiwan, mainland China, South Korea, Vietnam and the United States. Optics partners had technology but not the factory scale Nvidia needed, which is why Nvidia entered investment partnerships with Coherent and Lumentum, and why co-packaged optics is being built as a new supply chain. Amphenol’s cable assembly has 1,500 branches, each assembled to keep the defect rate below one per million, on more than a hundred automated lines in Vietnam, and is now in full production. Wafers are allocated weekly after packaging: a plant with low output or a short holding period does not get more chips that week, and every die is traced from its identification number to the customer invoice. When required, the cycle from chips leaving TSMC to systems shipping from Foxconn and Wistron can be cut from five weeks to two. On one compute-board ramp, automated test-handling throughput came in below plan; the line was paused, manual loading was restored with as many as several hundred operators, and an internal effort called Swarm redesigned tray, hardware, software and yield issues with Foxconn’s automation engineers before scaling resumed. ### The Teardown: The Film Between the Resin and the Mold
Release film is the sheet that keeps epoxy off the tool. In compression molding for IC packaging, hot resin is pressed around the die and the substrate. If that resin wets the mold, the next shot is contaminated and the demold can tear the package. BigGo Finance, reporting chairman Yang Yun-pin’s pre-listing presentation, describes Suregiant’s film as the layer that isolates epoxy from the mold under high temperature and high pressure so the part releases clean.

The process choice is how the film is made. Yang said Japanese peers mostly extrude film. Suregiant coats it, drawing on more than 25 years of precision coating and formulation, from an optoelectronics start that entered semiconductors in 2014. Coating is what lets a supplier cut a custom sample for one customer’s automated line. The line cares about three outcomes: non-stick sheeting, no adhesive residue, and a release force that matches the tool. An extruded film can post the same force number and still fail if the surface chemistry is not the one that mold, resin and cycle were qualified against. The sample, not a catalog roll, is the product.

The package stack changed the mold face the film has to leave clean. Suregiant’s release film entered mass production for 2.5D advanced packaging in 2024, was applied to panel-level fan-out in 2025, and is advancing to 3D packaging in 2026. A 2.5D compression is wide and relatively flat. Fan-out on a panel moves more resin across a larger area. A 3D stack adds height and more interfaces that can trap residue. Residue that would pass a lab peel test can still stop an automated visual check or contaminate the next shot. Suregiant says it is a qualified supplier to the world’s top five semiconductor manufacturers, with film already in mass production at first-tier advanced packaging. Order visibility runs 2 to 3 years, and customer forecasts reach 5 to 6 years, so the coating line is booked against a package roadmap, not a spot order.

Plant 1 is at full load. Taoyuan Plant 2 completed trial production and is expected to begin official production in December, at 2.5 to 3 times the scale of Plant 1, with a full year of contribution in 2027. Against that, the first three quarters of 2026 produced NT$333 million of revenue, about $10.1 million, NT$229 million of gross profit, NT$175 million of operating profit, NT$140 million of after-tax profit and earnings per share of NT$6.35. Third-quarter revenue was NT$159 million, up 80.76 percent quarter on quarter, and quarterly after-tax profit was NT$66.51 million, up 92.72 percent. Multiplying Plant 1 by 2.5 to 3 does not by itself multiply profit; Yang is counting on a rising share of high-margin advanced-packaging film inside that volume.

That coating film ceases to be the consumable that gates output when the critical step is no longer demolding a compressed package. Grinding tape, supplied to the world’s top ten packaging plants for more than 12 years, is the second product, and a thick single-adhesive-layer version went to first-tier verification in the fourth quarter of 2026 for taller bumps and thinner wafers. The third is hydrolyzable tape, meant to replace temporary bonding adhesives that need laser or ultraviolet debond, using a plant’s existing pure-water clean and skipping the chemical clean. Official samples are expected in mid-2027, with a gradual ramp in 2028. When the line is releasing a thinned, bonded wafer in pure water rather than peeling resin off a compression mold, the extrusion-versus-coating contest on release film no longer decides who can ship.
