# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **Nvidia ships CUDA 13.5, taking Vera Rubin from early access into production deployments.**

**What You Need to Know:** Nvidia has shipped CUDA 13.5, moving Vera Rubin into production on a platform it has described as up to 144 GPUs per rack. Samsung’s second Taylor, Texas fab is reported fully booked, mostly by AMD, for 2 nm HBM base dies. SEMI counted 3,756 million square inches of silicon wafers shipped in the third quarter.

### Top Story
Nvidia on Wednesday shipped CUDA Toolkit 13.5, moving the Vera Rubin platform from early developer access to general availability for enterprise production, and shipped it with Linux driver 620.70.06. The platform pairs Rubin GPUs with Vera CPUs over NVLink-C2C. Nvidia said in June 2026 that Vera Rubin systems would pack up to 144 GPUs per rack and deliver more than 7 exaflops of AI-for-science throughput and 5 petaflops of native FP64. CUDA 13.5 adds tuned matrix-multiplication heuristics and kernel adjustments for the platform’s NVFP4 and MXFP8 operations, which Nvidia says lift throughput without per-shape autotuning. Rubin and later GPUs support the Windows Display Driver Model and Microsoft Compute Driver Model but no longer support Tesla Compute Cluster mode, so applications and deployment scripts built around TCC need reworking. The release also extends Windows on Arm support to DGX Station for Windows, and it adds Multi-Process Service v3 weighted timeslicing plus locality-domain controls that let an application choose uniform streaming-multiprocessor partitions or partitions that draw on every available SM. Source: [finance.biggo.com](https://finance.biggo.com/news/ce0054f3-0d38-4ea1-81c5-6016102ce678) · [developer.nvidia.com](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-cuda-13-5-brings-nvidia-vera-rubin-ga-support/)

### Silicon
**Samsung's 2 nm Taylor Fab 2 Reportedly Fully Booked, Mostly by AMD: TechPowerUp**
Korea’s Maeil Business Newspaper, citing industry sources, reports that Samsung has booked the entire planned output of Taylor Fab 2 in Texas, with most of it going to AMD. The fab will make HBM base dies for AMD and NVIDIA on a 2 nm process, and AMD’s volume alone is described as enough to stretch the fab’s capacity. Samsung declined to comment, saying it cannot confirm customer-related details. The base die is the logic chip at the bottom of an HBM stack; Samsung plans to pair the Texas-made dies with DRAM produced in South Korea and ship finished HBM. Current HBM4 and sampled HBM4E use 4 nm base dies, so 2 nm or smaller is aimed at later HBM generations. The report follows Lisa Su’s October visit to Samsung, when Samsung reportedly wanted foundry orders from AMD in return for HBM AMD needs, and a March memorandum that made Samsung the primary HBM4 supplier for the Instinct MI455X. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/354477/samsungs-2-nm-taylor-fab-2-reportedly-fully-booked-mostly-by-amd)

### The Teardown: When the Unterminated Link Runs Out of Millimetres
A die-to-die wire inside an advanced package is not a shorter version of a board trace. Semiconductor Engineering, in a system-level signal-integrity piece by Muhammad Hassan, Mahmoud Mahany, and Mohamed Saleh, treats the channel as a stack of discontinuities: microbumps, through-silicon vias, redistribution layers, a silicon interposer or a silicon bridge, then the package transition into the next die. Each of those steps changes impedance and disturbs the return path. At multi-gigahertz rates the loss on those microscopic wires is dominated by skin effect, so insertion loss falls steeply and almost linearly with frequency. The same channel also changes character. At low frequency it behaves like a resistive-capacitive lump. At high frequency it behaves like a transmission line. That shift disperses the edge, spreads symbols into their neighbors, and shows up at the receiver pad as a shorter eye and more jitter.

Crosstalk, not the dielectric, is the louder noise source. Routing gaps of 0.5 micrometres to 2 micrometres let capacitive and inductive coupling inject more noise than the physical dielectric attenuates. The copper itself is on that same scale: traces of 0.5 to 2 micrometres, embedded in deposited silicon dioxide or a low-k film that the foundry has planarized flat, because any waviness misaligns the mask and snaps the wire. A conventional board, by contrast, tolerates thickness variation and uses coarse geometries above 50 micrometres. Checking the die, the interposer, and the package as three separate models misses the coupling that only exists when they are stacked.

Universal Chiplet Interconnect Express is the spec that turns that physics into two different electrical contracts. Semiconductor Engineering walks the split. Advanced packaging, the 2.5D interposer or bridge tier, is specified for an ultra-short reach of 2 millimetres or less, at up to 64 gigatransfers per second per pin under UCIe 3.0, with 64 single-ended mainband data lanes per module. Those signals are left unterminated at the receiver, and the energy target is 0.25 picojoules per bit. Standard packaging, the 2D laminate tier, stretches the channel to 25 millimetres, at 16 to 32 gigatransfers per second per pin, across 16 single-ended mainband lanes per module. The receiver uses 50-ohm parallel ground terminations to kill reflections off the longer trace, and the energy cost is 0.5 picojoules per bit. Physical-layer latency on the open spec is described as up to sub-2 nanoseconds. Compliance is not a lab courtesy: the extracted insertion loss, S21, has to stay strictly above a mandatory voltage-transfer-function mask.

Take the energy numbers as a worked budget, using only those published targets. One bit on the advanced-package path is budgeted at 0.25 picojoules. The same bit on the laminate path is budgeted at 0.5 picojoules, twice the energy, because the termination has to absorb the reflection the short unterminated link never builds up. A module on the advanced path also carries 64 mainband lanes against 16 on the standard path, four times the lane count, at a peak rate of 64 gigatransfers per second against a standard-package band of 16 to 32. The bargain is the reach. Two millimetres is the advanced-package ceiling in that description; 25 millimetres is what the terminated laminate is allowed to span. Stretch the unterminated, 0.25-picojoule link toward that longer run and the reflection the termination was invented to kill comes back, the eye that already lost height to the linear roll-off closes further, and the S21 curve fails the mask even if the arithmetic of the link still “works” on a short channel.

The unterminated advanced-package choice stops being the right electrical design once the physical span leaves that 2-millimetre reach, or once a bump map cannot keep aggressors inside the 0.5-to-2-micrometre gap regime without the coupling exceeding dielectric loss. At that point the designer is on the standard-package contract: fewer lanes, a lower transfer rate, a 50-ohm termination, and 0.5 picojoules per bit. Source: [semiengineering.com](https://semiengineering.com/a-system-level-approach-to-protecting-3d-ic-signal-integrity/)
