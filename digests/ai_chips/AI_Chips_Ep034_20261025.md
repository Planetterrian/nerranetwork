# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **Seventeen Texas data-center permits are suspended, freezing stormwater work every project needs.**

**What You Need to Know:** Seventeen Texas data-center construction permits are suspended, and the automatic portal that had kept issuing them is shut. The freeze lands on stormwater filings, the ground-raising step every project in the state has to clear. A pulled Geekbench run is the first same-clock look at dense Zen 6, and leaked factory video shows new GeForce RTX 5090 boards being rebuilt as server cards.

### Top Story
Texas has suspended construction permits on 17 data-center projects and disabled the STEERS portal that had kept granting automatic authorizations for a month after Governor Greg Abbott asked, in September 2026, for new permits to be halted. Tom's Hardware, citing 404 Media, reports that FracTracker Alliance and Public Citizen had already documented more than two dozen permits issued after that request, and FracTracker researcher Sam Law says OpenAI and Amazon filings went through earlier this week. All 17 suspended permits are stormwater construction authorizations, the step in which crews raise and level the ground. Abbott had put the brakes on 1,800 data-center projects while they were audited for power use and generation, water consumption, and community impact. He wrote that any project failing to comply with Public Utility Commission of Texas and ERCOT requirements, and with state law, must be denied connection to the Texas grid, and that "Texans must come first." The same report notes New York's year-long ban on data-center projects larger than 50 megawatts, enacted as Senate Bill S10642 and signed by Governor Kathy Hochul in July, and a October 2026 mandate from President Trump that AI data centers pay dividends to communities, after a July 2025 AI Action Plan aimed at speeding infrastructure. ### Silicon
**AMD EPYC 9996 Geekbench Listing Offers First Look at Zen 6 Single-Core Gains: TechPowerUp**
A Geekbench 7.1.0 listing spotted by leaker HXL put the 256-core EPYC 9996, the Venice flagship AMD launched in July, at 2,847 single-core points on a Dell PowerEdge R9815 running Linux. A Ryzen 7 9700X locked to 4.1 gigahertz, matching that EPYC part's maximum boost, scored 2,444 on the same Geekbench version, about 16.5 percent lower. Zen 6 led all 15 single-core subtests in that comparison, from 8.2 percent in File Compression to 25.7 percent in HTML5 Browser. The EPYC 9996 uses dense Zen 6c cores, not the full Zen 6 cores expected in desktop Ryzen, and the desktop side of the comparison was a 9700X on DDR5-6000 CL34 under CachyOS. The EPYC listing has since been pulled from the Geekbench database. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/354103/amd-epyc-9996-geekbench-listing-offers-first-look-at-zen-6-single-core-gains)

### Supply Chain & Policy
**Leaked video exposes how industrial-scale Chinese factories are dismantling RTX 5090s by the thousands to fuel AI servers: Tom's Hardware**
Tom's Hardware reports leaked footage of Chinese workshops desoldering GB202 silicon and GDDR7 from new GeForce RTX 5090 cards, including Founders Editions, and transplanting those parts onto dual-slot blower boards for high-density server racks. The cards are not officially sold in China. The report describes production lines converting retail shipments, then testing and repackaging the blower cards for data-center or lab customers, and says the conversion is probably running to thousands of cards a day. Nvidia has reportedly halted GeForce RTX 5090 production and is redirecting that silicon toward the RTX Pro 6000 Blackwell. Custom GeForce RTX 5090 cards now start at $8,500, and some prebuilt systems cost less than a standalone card. ### The Teardown: The Shell Is the Duct
A U.2 drive on the ADT R27A-BK3 does not sit on an open riser. ServeTheHome describes a metal shell large enough to enclose a full-length U.2 drive, a blower, and the adapter printed-circuit board. The cover is not decoration. It is the duct wall. Vents in the I/O plate let air in, the blower drags that air across the drive, and the shell keeps the stream from spilling into the chassis before it has picked up heat. The rear of the card carries screws and a fan cable on a standard 4-pin PWM connector, so the host can idle the blower and spin it up when a long write starts. The gold fingers are PCIe Gen5 x4. ADT also sells a Gen4 version of the same adapter, which is a link choice, not a cooler choice. Inside, the board is labeled as a PCIe x4-to-U.2 adapter and the drive plugs into an SFF-8639 connector. That plug is U.2 only. The EDSFF sibling in the same family uses an SFF-TA-1002 connector for E1.S and E3.S drives. ServeTheHome had already seen thermal throttling on that EDSFF shell. This U.2 shell did not repeat it in the runs below.

The worked example is two 7.68-terabyte drives, each held under load for 20 minutes. The Samsung MZQLB7T6HMLA-00007 is a PCIe Gen3 x4 part. It peaked at 2,252 megabytes per second and sustained 2,179 megabytes per second. Peak to sustained is a drop of about 3.2 percent. Throughput slipped around the eight-minute mark, then held near 2.18 gigabytes per second for the rest of the run. Temperature peaked at 78 C. The drive's warning threshold is 86 C, so the shell finished 8 degrees under the warning line on a link that cannot ask the cooler to clear more heat than Gen3 can generate. The second drive, a Solidigm D7-PS1010, is also 7.68 terabytes, but it is PCIe Gen5 x4. The test was sustained 128-kilobyte sequential writes, the case that stresses both the adapter and the blower. Peak throughput was 7,946 megabytes per second. Sustained throughput was 7,838 megabytes per second. That drop is 1.4 percent. Brief dips recovered to about 7.8 gigabytes per second. Temperature leveled at 70 C. The warning threshold on this drive is 77 C, so the margin was 7 degrees while the link moved a bit more than three and a half times the Samsung's sustained rate: 7,838 divided by 2,179. Neither drive showed sustained thermal throttling. Both stayed under their own warning lines for the full 20 minutes.

The blower-and-shell card stops being the right adapter when the drive is hotter than these two, which is the check ServeTheHome leaves with the buyer: verify it in the machine rather than trust the 20-minute pair. It also stops being the right card when the drive is E1.S or E3.S, because SFF-8639 will not take that connector, and the EDSFF shell that will take it is the one that already throttled. A Gen5 drive on the Gen4 edge connector fails a different test: the 7.8 gigabytes per second the Solidigm just held is a link result, and the slower fingers cannot deliver it no matter how cool the shell stays. Source: [servethehome.com](https://www.servethehome.com/adt-r27a-bk3-u-2-to-pcie-slot-review/)
