# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **Seven Virginia sites hold one thousand eighty-five megawatts, but Ohio's bigger map emits twice the carbon.**

**What You Need to Know:** TRG DataCenters' August 2026 ranking scores Virginia at 100 on seven mapped AI sites totaling 1,085 megawatts, while Ohio's larger mapped fleet is tied to roughly twice the annual CO2. Ohio, Tennessee, Georgia and Texas follow on the same 24-state score. Water stress sits in that score beside power and carbon, and the megawatts are TRG's AI dataset, not each state's whole data-centre market.

### Top Story
Data Center Knowledge reports that TRG DataCenters' August 2026 ranking of 24 US states puts Virginia first, with a readiness score of 100. Ohio scores 88.2, Tennessee 83.7, Georgia 81.8 and Texas 80.8. TRG's dataset maps seven large AI sites in Virginia totaling 1,085 MW, against 1,410 MW in Ohio and 1,373 MW in Tennessee; those figures are the AI infrastructure in the dataset, not each state's total data-centre market. Chris Hinkle, CEO of TRG DataCenters, said Ohio's mapped AI capacity was around 30 percent greater, while the grid serving Virginia's mapped facilities had roughly one-third lower carbon intensity. He tied the dataset's AI facilities to around 2.3 million tons of CO2 a year in Virginia, compared with roughly 4.5 million tons in Ohio and 4.3 million tons in Tennessee. Hinkle told Data Center Knowledge, "When planning infrastructure for the long term, that balance between scale and resource efficiency matters as much as raw megawatts." The same analysis treats water as a constraint on Virginia's lead and includes the share of mapped facilities in high water-stress areas. Source: [datacenterknowledge.com](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-site-selection/virginia-tops-ai-data-center-readiness-ranking-as-smaller-markets-advance)

### Silicon
**Xsight Labs Raises Over $300 Million for an 800 Gbps DPU and a 12.8 Tbps Switch: Semiconductor Engineering**
Semiconductor Engineering's Q3 2026 startup-funding review says Xsight Labs raised over $300 million, led by Fidelity Management & Research Company. The company designs programmable data-processing units and Ethernet switches for hyperscale, edge and AI data-centre networks. Its DPU uses 64 Arm Neoverse N2 cores sitting in the data path, runs native Linux, DPDK and SONiC, and is described as processing millions of connections per second at a full 800 Gbps line rate. The 12.8 Tbps Ethernet switch is specified at deterministic, sub-700-nanosecond latency and under 200 watts. The round is to accelerate the roadmap, add support for emerging Ultra Ethernet Consortium standards, hire and scale manufacturing. Source: [semiengineering.com](https://semiengineering.com/startup-funding-q3-2026/)

**Musk Sets a Ten-Design Test Goal at Terafab's Giga Texas Research Fab: Yahoo Finance**
Elon Musk says Tesla and SpaceX want to make and test about 10 chip designs a year at Terafab's Giga Texas research fab, a faster make-and-test cycle rather than 10 designs entering mass production each year. He compared that goal with the roughly two years he says a significant chip design typically takes to reach volume production. The proposed research fab would put fabrication, testing, packaging and mask production in one building so a failed test can lead to new lithography masks and another version without breaking the loop. Musk called the aim high-risk, high-payoff bets, said "I have no idea how to make chips, but we'll figure it out," and said reaching it might take quite a few years. ### Supply Chain & Policy
**Europe's Cyber Resilience Act Makes Chip Security a Sign-off: EE Times**
EE Times reports that on 11 September the first operational piece of the EU Cyber Resilience Act went live: a company selling a product with digital elements in Europe must flag actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe security incidents within 24 hours, and file a fuller report within 72 hours, through an ENISA platform. The duty covers products already on the market, including units shipped years ago. Most products sit in a default tier that can self-assess; microcontrollers and processors with security functions, tamper-resistant versions of those, and secure elements and smartcard chips sit in higher tiers, and the top tiers need an outside evaluator before the product can carry the CE mark. Japan has set rules for a supply-chain security rating, and the US is set to require its Cyber Trust Mark on connected consumer devices bought by federal agencies from January 2027; EE Times describes those as softer than the CRA, which is binding, carries fines and CE marking, and applies to anyone selling into Europe. Source: [eetimes.com](https://www.eetimes.com/security-becomes-a-sign-off-item/)

### The Teardown: Why the Spare Part Prints on the Same Clock as the Wafer
A scanner is bought once. The parts that keep an EUV or DUV tool printing are bought for as long as that tool runs. TradingKey, citing South Korean outlet The Elec, reports that ASML will apply a uniform 10 percent price increase to replacement parts for EUV and DUV lithography equipment supplied to the South Korean market. The list covers regularly purchased consumables and major components replaced because of faults or performance loss. The new prices apply to parts delivered starting January 2027. Procurement departments at Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix have accepted the increase.

That split is the mechanism. The scanner is capital equipment. Wear items are an operating purchase: consumables on a schedule, larger assemblies when they fail or drift. SK Hynix is expanding advanced DRAM and HBM capacity, so more tools on the floor mean more of those parts in the buy. The Elec's account, via TradingKey, ties the maintenance impact to how much equipment is installed, how many components are procured, and how service contracts are written. Depreciation, materials, labor and energy stay in the wafer-cost stack beside that parts line. A flat 10 percent does not replace those other lines. It raises the line that grows when utilization grows.

Second-quarter figures at SK Hynix show the margin this increase has to live inside. Revenue was 79.32 trillion won, up 257 percent year over year. Operating profit was 60.54 trillion won, up 557 percent year over year, for an operating margin of about 76 percent. That margin is what memory pricing cleared against the cost stack before January 2027 deliveries. Adding scanners while taking a uniform increase on every replacement part stops clearing its own cost when extra tool-hours push the parts invoice up faster than the memory price that produced that 76 percent operating margin.
