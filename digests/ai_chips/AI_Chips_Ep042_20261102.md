> **A 72-GPU Rubin rack now carries 20.7 terabytes of HBM4 for inference.**

**What You Need to Know:** ASRock Rack showed a Vera Rubin NVL72 at OCP with 20.7 terabytes of HBM4 across 72 GPUs. OpenAI left its Seoul meetings without a new memory contract, while TrendForce sees HBM's blended price more than doubling in 2027. DigiTimes reports Elon Musk is now discussing operational control and joint investment with TSMC on Terafab.

### Top Story
ServeTheHome reports that ASRock Rack showed the XGMRACK-VR/NVL72 at the OCP Global Summit in San Jose: a 48RU NVIDIA MGX rack with 72 Rubin GPUs, 20.7TB of HBM4, a 5000A busbar and a liquid manifold. Inside are 18 Vera Rubin compute trays and nine NVLink 6 switch trays, for 36 NVIDIA Vera CPUs with those GPUs. NVIDIA lists the rack at 3,600 PFLOPS of NVFP4 inference (sparse) and 2,520 PFLOPS of NVFP4 training (dense), and each Rubin GPU gets 288GB of HBM4. Power comes from four 3RU shelves rated at 110kW each, using six 18.3kW supplies plus bulk capacitors; cooling-distribution options are in-rack, in-row, rear-door heat exchanger, or a liquid-to-air sidecar. Each compute tray is liquid-cooled, with eight 800Gbps OSFP ports from ConnectX-9 SuperNICs and two 400Gbps QSFP112 ports from a BlueField-4 DPU. Source: [servethehome.com](https://www.servethehome.com/liquid-cooled-nvidia-servers-at-the-asrock-rack-ocp-2026-booth/)

### Silicon
**Xeon 6 leads a recorded agent-workload bake-off: Intel**
Intel says a Xeon 6 processor-based instance delivered 1.66 times the normalized agent-task throughput of a 5th Gen AMD EPYC instance and 4.5 times an Arm v9.2A instance on a healthcare Terminal-Bench trace. The comparison ran at 24-way concurrency for 60 minutes with identical code, inputs and agent trajectories. A record-and-replay method held the workflow fixed so the gap is attributed to the platform rather than to changing tool choices. The healthcare set included clinical documentation, evidence retrieval, fraud detection and laboratory diagnostics, among other tasks. The write-up is by Nithya Rao and Saumya Buliya of Intel. Source: [t.co](https://t.co/21eeu6lx9B)

**AI Reshapes Memory Architecture as Macronix Expands Flash Portfolio: EE Times**
Martin Lin, Macronix marketing director, told EE Times Taiwan that AI is changing memory architecture, not only memory demand. Macronix raised 2026 capital expenditure to NT$37.8 billion to expand 12-inch wafer capacity for 3D NOR, NAND, eMMC and related products. Lin said a previous-generation rack used about 600 to 800 NOR flash devices and the latest generation has more than 1,000, for boot, firmware, control and security as controller and switch counts rise. Market data in the piece put the memory market above $20 billion in 2025 and near $70 billion in 2026, with NOR flash, SLC NAND and low-capacity eMMC together around $24 billion in 2026. The article describes high-bandwidth flash, built on 3D NAND, as a higher-capacity tier for data that does not have to stay in HBM, and says equipment install, qualification and yield ramp can take two to three years. Source: [eetimes.com](https://www.eetimes.com/ai-reshapes-memory-architecture-as-macronix-expands-flash-portfolio/)

ET CIO, citing The Korea Herald, reports that OpenAI chief Sam Altman met about 50 Samsung executives in Yongin on Saturday and then SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won with SK hynix and SK Telecom executives, and that no new supply or investment agreements were announced. Altman, as carried by that report, said simply using AI will no longer be enough to secure a sustainable competitive advantage. International Business Times, citing The Wall Street Journal, adds that a hoped-for valuation fell to about $1.25 trillion from $1.4 trillion and that the raise was later cut to a target of as much as $20 billion. ### The Teardown: When the Old Well Still Pumps
A token is the barrel. Each GPU generation is a well. EE Times, walking through a Gartner forecast and an a16z rental chart, treats the cost of that barrel as mostly energy plus whether the chip can do the job at all. Easy wells — newer silicon that finishes more work per watt — stay open when the token price falls. Hard wells shut first. Rent sitting above a depreciation line does not make the well profitable: power, cooling, networking, the building and the cost of borrowing still come out.

Gartner's arithmetic, as EE Times reports it, starts with providers spending close to $1 trillion on AI infrastructure in 2026 and another $6 trillion from 2027 through 2030. To pay for that spend, cover borrowing, and still earn a 12 percent return, the owners of those data centers would need about $8.5 trillion in revenue through 2030. Gartner expects the AI revenue that actually reaches those data centers to be $3.6 trillion at most. The gap is what Gartner calls more than $3.5 trillion of spending that could be exposed to write-downs by 2030. EE Times is careful: that is a risk estimate under Gartner's assumptions, not a prediction that the full amount will be written off. On the same arithmetic, more than 80 percent of neoclouds would no longer exist as independent companies.

Set that against the price of the work. Epoch AI, as cited in the piece, estimates that the price of reaching a given benchmark score has fallen by roughly 13 times a year since 2023, on the benchmarks it tracks. Cheaper tokens can pull in new jobs — the Jevons pattern, where a lower cost per use raises total use — but nothing in the chart guarantees that demand grows faster than the price falls. If use only doubles while the price of a scored result drops thirteenfold, revenue still shrinks.

The rental chart is the field log. a16z, using Ornn data as of October 19, drew a straight line from each Nvidia generation's first observed rent down to zero over six years, about the period hyperscalers use to write the servers off. Every generation was renting above that line. The H200 sat 143 percent above it. EE Times notes that most of the gap opened after April, when token demand took off, and that for most of their lives these chips had rented below the line. A busy well can still lose money once the power bill is paid.

The oil picture breaks where tokens are not the same crude. Interactive and agent work wants low latency and the newest memory. Overnight document work, synthetic data and evaluations mostly want a low cost per token and barely notice delay. A chip whose capital is already written off, with power, cooling and upkeep left as the main costs, can be the cheapest barrel for that second market. There is not one token price. There are at least two.

Keeping every generation online stops being the right call when a newer generation is arriving in volume and the token price has fallen far enough that an unpaid-off older chip's energy cost exceeds what even the latency-blind jobs will pay. Until that crossover, the expensive well still pumps, because the alternative is turning away work the new wells cannot yet absorb. Source: [eetimes.com](https://www.eetimes.com/jevon-paradox-and-the-silicon-roadmap/)
