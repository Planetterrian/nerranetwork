A token is the barrel, and each G P U generation is a well. EE Times uses a Gartner forecast and an a16z rent chart to say the cost of that barrel is mostly energy, plus whether the chip can do the job at all.

Newer silicon that finishes more work per watt is the easy well. It stays open when the token price falls. The hard wells shut first.

Rent above a depreciation line is not profit. Power, cooling, networking, the building and the cost of borrowing still come out.

Gartner, as EE Times reports it, has providers spending close to one trillion dollars on A I infrastructure in twenty twenty-six and another six trillion from twenty twenty-seven through twenty thirty.

Interactive and agent work wants low latency and the newest memory. Overnight documents, synthetic data and evaluations mostly want a cheap token and barely notice delay.

A chip whose capital is already written off, with power, cooling and upkeep left as the main costs, can be the cheapest barrel for that second market. There is not one token price.
