# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **A two-billion-dollar pact puts US-made AI interposers into TSMC's CoWoS flow.**

**What You Need to Know:** GlobalFoundries has signed a $2 billion agreement to supply TSMC with silicon interposers from Malta, New York, for CoWoS packaging. Volume production there is scheduled for the first half of 2028 under an initial five-year contract. Elsewhere, a bankrupt Texas campus is being recast as a 73 MW research and AI compute plan, and a new coldplate claims a further cut in GPU hotspot temperature.

### Top Story
GlobalFoundries has signed a $2 billion agreement to supply TSMC with U.S.-made silicon interposers for Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate packaging, Industrial Info reports, and Electronics For You describes the same pact as a reached agreement with an initial five-year term. Capacity is to be added at GlobalFoundries' fab in Malta, New York, and the company will expand that campus. Electronics For You calls the setup the first domestic U.S. source of silicon interposers and says the parts integrate embedded deep trench capacitors to improve thermal and power efficiency in high-performance computing and AI accelerators. Volume production and the capacity ramp at the New York site are scheduled to begin in the first half of 2028, with provisions to scale further as hyperscale demand for chiplet packaging grows. Xsight Labs announced the E1L on October 8, 2026, as the newest member of its E-Series data processing unit portfolio, HPCwire reports. **Intel's Packaging Technology Gains Nvidia's Interest, Easing Pressure on TSMC's Backend Operations: BigGo Finance**
This is a reported evaluation, not a confirmed Nvidia award. BigGo Finance says GF Securities analyst Jeff Pu wrote that Nvidia could adopt Intel's Foveros packaging for backend work on select AI chips in the second half of 2028, alongside an expectation that Intel's 14A process enters mass production in that same half. The piece notes a January DigiTimes report that Nvidia might use Intel 18A or 14A for next-generation GPUs. It also says Intel's EMIB and EMIB-T packaging have reportedly drawn Google for TPU chips. Foveros, as described there, stacks compute dies or chiplets on a base die rather than placing them side by side as in EMIB, which the report says shrinks horizontal footprint and improves power efficiency and data transfer speed. Source: [finance.biggo.com](https://finance.biggo.com/news/a75adb64-9283-4678-9e40-abf34eb6e9fb)

### Data Centres & Power
**ThisWay Global to Convert Bankrupt RELLIS Site into 73 MW AI Hub: Data Center Knowledge**
ThisWay Global has announced a 73 MW Research and AI Compute Center in Bryan, Texas, on Texas A&M's RELLIS Campus. Data Center Knowledge reports that a private developer began a data center there in 2024 and filed for Chapter 11 in November 2025, leaving the project unfinished, after the Texas A&M University System had explored a data center on the campus since 2013. ThisWay, an Austin company, says it acquired the project out of bankruptcy and completed the transaction on September 30, 2026. The phased plan totals 73 MW on about 25 acres of university-system land, and chief executive Stephan Fabel said the first building will encompass about 25 percent of the campus's total compute power. Capital came with Texas-based Centaurus Capital and Skylar Capital Management, among other investors, and ThisWay says that capital was to purchase the project and make creditors whole. The company plans to adjust the existing design and expand capacity, including a redesign aimed at HPC density and liquid cooling, and it intends to use the site to demonstrate a Sovereign AI Blueprint it introduced in September 2026. Source: [datacenterknowledge.com](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/ai-data-centers/thisway-global-to-convert-bankrupt-rellis-site-into-73-mw-ai-hub)

**Frore Systems Announces New LiquidJet Diamond Coldplates for AI Factories: TechPowerUp**
Frore Systems announced LiquidJet Diamond, a liquid-cooling coldplate for AI factories with an integrated diamond spreader. The company says it boosts AI tokens per watt, and revenue, by 35 percent versus traditional skived coldplates, with that revenue increase flowing to profit. It builds on the previously announced LiquidJet coldplate, a 3D ultra-short-loop multi-stage design Frore says can be customized to each GPU power map, including extreme hotspots. Frore says LiquidJet alone cuts GPU die temperature by 12 degrees Celsius and raises tokens per watt by 25 percent. LiquidJet Diamond adds a spreader with integrated diamond wafers at the hotspot locations and, Frore says, cuts hotspot die temperature by a further 10 degrees Celsius, taking the tokens-per-watt gain to 35 percent. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/353544/frore-systems-announces-new-liquidjet-diamond-coldplates-for-ai-factories)

### The Teardown: The Quarter the Channel Already Bought
A memory-price hike empties the next quarter's order book before it empties the warehouse. Vendors and distributors who can see DRAM and NAND costs rising fill the channel while the old invoice still clears. Units that end customers would have taken later leave in the first half instead. The second half then inherits stock that is already on the floor and a thinner book of new builds.

TechPowerUp, citing preliminary IDC data, puts worldwide PC shipments at 62.7 million units in the third quarter of 2026, down 20.1 percent from 78.5 million a year earlier. Shipments also fell 9.1 percent from the second quarter, even though the third quarter is normally the larger one. IDC's account, as that report carries it, is that the first half stocked up ahead of memory-driven price hikes, supply stayed constrained, and prices stayed high, so the third quarter had little demand left to work with.

The mechanism is timing, not a sudden disappearance of uses for a PC. A machine already sitting at a distributor does not get ordered again when the next invoice jumps. The OEM cuts the build plan. The memory supplier can still read the earlier pull as demand. AI server builds bidding for high-capacity DRAM and NAND are what tightened that market; the client PC is where the squeeze shows up as a missing shipment rather than as a delayed training job. The module that went to a server DIMM or an enterprise SSD is not on the notebook line, and an empty channel does not create a second source of client parts.

That forward buy stops being the right move once the pre-bought stock has cleared and the next quarter's shipments are limited by what end buyers will pay at the new memory price, not by what the channel already absorbed. Until that clearance, a steep year-on-year drop measures a timing shift as much as it measures destroyed demand.
