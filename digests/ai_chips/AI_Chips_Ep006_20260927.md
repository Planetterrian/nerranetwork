**What You Need to Know:** SK hynix is discussing a Solidigm listing at up to $150 billion, a raise that would fund memory expansion, and the company has not confirmed it. ### Top Story
AD HOC NEWS, citing a Reuters report, says SK hynix held preliminary talks with investment banks on Friday about a multibillion-dollar IPO of US NAND subsidiary Solidigm. A valuation of up to $150 billion and a capital raise of roughly $15 billion are under discussion. The company has not officially confirmed the listing plans. In the second quarter it posted operating profit of 60.5426 trillion won on revenue of 79.3187 trillion won. Counterpoint said SK hynix held a 50 percent share of the global HBM market in that period. **Helios Rack Claim Lists 31 TB of HBM4: AMD**
AMD said Helios delivers over 18,000 CDNA 5 GPU compute units in a single rack. The same claim lists 4,600 Zen 6 CPU cores. Memory in the claim is 31 TB of HBM4. The post looks back at Advancing AI with Dr. Lisa Su, so this is a restatement of that event's rack claim, not a shipment notice. Source: [x.com](https://x.com/AMD/status/2103854831899140287)

**Qualcomm Announces Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6: ServeTheHome**
ServeTheHome reports Qualcomm announced the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 and the Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 at its Snapdragon Summit. New Oryon CPU cores reach 5 GHz for the first time. The parts add GPU matrix cores and LPDDR6, plus new Wi-Fi and cellular radios. Extreme is the fastest, most feature-rich SKU; the other Gen 6 chip has fewer features, a split by features rather than the Gen 5 V-series approach of a smaller GPU. The comparison lists 5G Advanced downlink at 12.5 Gbps on one configuration and 14.8 Gbps on the others, both with dual active SIM. Source: [servethehome.com](https://www.servethehome.com/qualcomm-unveils-snapdragon-8-elite-gen-6-and-elite-extreme-gen-6-next-gen-flagship-mobile-chips/)

### Supply Chain & Policy
**Xi-Trump Summit Left Chip Controls Off the Readout: UA.NEWS**
UA.NEWS, citing the South China Morning Post, reports US chip export controls were not a main topic at the Xi-Trump summit in Washington, and final statements from Beijing and Washington contain no direct mention of the restrictions. US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told CNBC that export-control issues related to national security were not considered as part of this week's trade negotiations. The administration has largely retained the October 2022 rules, so the quiet readout is not a lifting of the controls. A Taipei-based research company forecasts the share of Nvidia, AMD and other foreign suppliers will decline to 21 percent in 2026 from 34 percent last year. Nvidia wrote off $400 million in the first half on excess H200 inventory caused by approval delays. Paul Triolo of DGA-Albright Stonebridge Group warned the pause may be temporary and pointed to the Match Act, meant to push Japan and the Netherlands to coordinate limits on sales of advanced chipmaking equipment to China. **Guai Guai Strike Vote Follows ASE Site Purchase: Tom's Hardware**
Tom's Hardware, citing the Taipei Times, reports 94 percent of workers at Guai Guai's Zhongli factory voted for strike action after the company said it would relocate to Hsinchu. Workers were offered a 5 percent pay increase, which the union says merely tracks Taiwan's newly announced minimum-wage change. ASE bought the Zhongli site for NT$5.6 billion, or US$176.1 million, and the union says no share of that sum goes to workers. The union wants written severance guarantees and, for those who stay, a NT$10,000, or US$315, monthly increase. Workers are waiting on the company's response. ### The Teardown: Qualifying a Packaging Tool Beside the Vendor
An advanced packaging tool is not qualified when it is unpacked. It is qualified when bond alignment, bond force, and the heat steps around a logic die and a memory stack have been run on the customer's flow, with the customer's limits deciding pass or fail. A vendor's own demonstration line cannot finish that job, because the warpage, the stack, and the test limits belong to the customer. That is why a verification center is built as a shared floor, not as another brochure lab.

Taiwan Economic Daily News, reported by Asiae, says the groundbreaking for the Kaohsiung Baifu Industrial Park was held on September 21. TSMC, the Ministry of Economic Affairs, and the Kaohsiung city government are starting what the report calls Taiwan's first advanced semiconductor equipment and materials verification center. The plan puts TSMC's advanced packaging verification center, a talent development center, and exclusive factories for partner companies on one site. Asiae reports that more than 20 suppliers are already planning to move in, and that TSMC is committed to supporting that move-in.

The mechanism is proximity. A process tweak on a bonder can be tried, measured, and rejected beside the customer's verification team, instead of being discovered after the tool has been dropped into a hall that is already running volume. Scrap at that stage is not a blank wafer. It is a finished die joined to finished memory. Keeping the tool company and the packaging customer on one site is how that scrap is supposed to stay off the volume line.

Asiae says a standard factory is scheduled for completion in the second quarter of 2028, and that the verification laboratory and the talent development center are set to open in phases in the fourth quarter of the same year. The tradeoff flips if that shared lab arrives later than a tool slot on a line that already exists. Partners who cannot wait until 2028 will still qualify across separate sites. The September 21 groundbreaking starts construction of the loop.
