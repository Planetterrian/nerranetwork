Test insertions on an AI die take it apart as a chain of partial views, and Semiconductor Engineering counts five of them on silicon photonics alone. The old cut was something the process and the physics already defined, and the result was a pass or a fail you could count. The new problem is that the job the chip was drawn for can change before the package leaves the line.

Wafer sort and singulated-die probe can live at the fab, on that fab's program, with that fab's list of how parts fail. The next build — compute die, high-bandwidth memory stack, other chiplets, interposer — is often an OSAT, on another program, and those two records do not have to be the same conversation.

Advantest's Scott Segura is the one describing why the chain lengthens: more active silicon in the substrate, parts shoved closer, functional test sliding into what used to be a contact check, and a thermal hold at die probe. Known-good die, in that account, has to take 3 kilowatts while it is still a single die, not yet in the package.

The panel replacing the round wafer at that OSAT step is 310 by 310 millimetres, so the tools hold more dies. Set the calendar next to that chain. ChatGPT showed up in November 2022. Agentic systems in wide commercial use took a little more than two years, which is about how long it takes to design, verify, validate, ride a shuttle, package, and land a chip in a working box.

The design cycle people are trying to crush is still 18 to 24 months, and some large chipmakers have told their tool suppliers they want six. Hit six on the drawing board and the fab plus the package would still be chasing a model generation that turned over inside the same span. Segura's customers already tape out, run the model or look at the end use, and then do not ship that silicon.

They ship a derivative, or another version. The 3-kilowatt probe, the five photonics stops, and the panel test were written for a part the lab may already have dropped.

That slice stops earning its tester hour when the miss you still need only appears once compute, memory, interconnect, firmware, and software share one box, and the company holding the box never sees the wafer-sort mechanisms from the company that probed the die.
