# AI Chips & Data Centres Daily
> **Twenty thousand Rubin GPUs in a four-billion-dollar order would add seventy megawatts of Indian compute.**

**What You Need to Know:** AM Intelligence has placed a binding order for 20,000 Nvidia Rubin GPUs, about $4 billion, to stand up 70MW of capacity. Yotta’s Nvidia build has moved into deliveries, with the larger B300 phase now slipping past a September 30 target. A Utility Dive sponsored analysis puts more than 2 TW of generation and storage in U.S. interconnection queues by mid-2026.

### Top Story
TradingView, citing two people aware of the development, reports that Greenko founders-backed AM Intelligence has placed a binding order for 20,000 Nvidia Rubin GPUs in a deal valued at around $4 billion to set up a 70MW capacity, with an announcement expected shortly. Those GPUs are described as driving a 100MW expansion across India and Malaysia. The order follows an August deal to deploy 9,000 GPUs at AMI’s Hyderabad centre. The same account says that once this order is deployed, AMI will operate the largest operational compute capacity in India by the second quarter of 2027, on Nvidia Vera Rubin NVL72 systems it calls more power-efficient than Blackwell. A January memorandum of understanding with InvestUP, for a 1GW hub by 2030 and $25 billion, is not this binding GPU order. Separately, the people cited say AMI plans another 300MW online in the next 15 months, requiring $20 billion. **ASUS Announces New 64GB Configuration of the Ascent GX10:** TechPowerUp

**Extreme Environments Push Chips To Their Breaking Point:** Semiconductor Engineering
Semiconductor Engineering reports that chips are now being designed for combined stresses — radiation, thermal shock, vibration, pressure, noise and cryogenic temperatures — including orbital data centers. Dana Neustadter, senior director of product management at Synopsys, said the company has seen a major uptick in activity for rad-hard fab processes, particularly on newer process nodes, to support orbital data centers. He attributed a lot of that activity to Golden Dome, the multi-layered U.S. missile defense initiative. The piece says virtual models still have to be checked against physical lab behavior before an extreme-environment prediction is trusted. Source: [semiengineering.com](https://semiengineering.com/extreme-environments-push-chips-to-their-breaking-point/)

### Data Centres & Power
**Yotta’s Nvidia GPU push enters execution phase:** Communications Today
Communications Today reports that partner Gorilla Technology has begun deliveries under a program that could place more than 25,000 Nvidia GPUs in Yotta facilities. In February, Yotta said it would invest more than $2 billion to deploy over 20,000 Nvidia Blackwell Ultra GPUs, with Nvidia expected to use about half of that capacity for four years for DGX Cloud. Gorilla’s first phase is about 640 servers carrying about 5,110 Nvidia B200 GPUs; the second phase covers 20,736 Nvidia B300 GPU cards. In April, Gorilla put the B300 phase at about $2.8 billion and expected completion by September 30. That date has slipped: late September, Gorilla said the first of four B200 phases was done, with the rest due early or mid-October, and B300 deliveries starting in October in tranches of around 5,000 GPUs and finishing by year-end. Gorilla’s management said revenue recognition depends on equipment being delivered, configured, accepted and placed into billing, under project-level financing rather than Yotta carrying the full accelerator cost on its own balance sheet. Source: [communicationstoday.co.in](https://www.communicationstoday.co.in/yottas-nvidia-gpu-push-enters-execution-phase/)

**Vertical Data Centers Face Cost-Proximity Trade-Offs:** Data Center Knowledge
Announced only as a proposal still subject to approval, a 20-story data center tower would occupy about 0.23 acres in downtown Kansas City, Missouri. Skidmore, Owings & Merrill designed it for developer Revitalization Unlimited, and a related city development-plan ordinance is on hold until October 13. Steve Austin, the developer’s founder and CEO, told Data Center Knowledge that inference has to happen near the user because latency matters, and he pointed to nearby carrier infrastructure, planned diverse fiber entrances and a carrier-neutral design. Luis Fernandes of IDC said stock traders seek addresses closest to exchange servers to gain fractions of a second, and that the economics depend on the premium for building vertically. Michael Martin of JLL said clients do not come asking for a downtown data center; they come with a business problem. Source: [datacenterknowledge.com](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-site-selection/vertical-data-centers-face-cost-proximity-trade-offs)

**Hidden capacity: The overlooked opportunity in North America’s race for power:** Utility Dive
A Utility Dive sponsored analysis cites NERC projecting U.S. electricity consumption rising from 4,281 TWh in 2024 to 5,353 TWh by 2034. It says more than 2 TW of generation and storage was waiting in U.S. interconnection queues by mid-2026, a figure it calls equivalent to more than double current U.S. power capacity. ERCOT alone received nearly 200 GW of large-load applications in the first quarter of 2026, the piece says. Analysis it cites puts more than $100 billion in savings over the next decade on higher utilization of infrastructure that sits underused except at peak. Source: [utilitydive.com](https://www.utilitydive.com/spons/hidden-capacity-the-overlooked-opportunity-in-north-americas-race-for-pow/830945/)

### The Teardown: Four Supplies for Eight Slots
A six-U chassis that holds eight full accelerators is a power problem before it is a compute problem. INNO3D’s announced AGS-6220V2, in TechPowerUp’s item on the product notice, pairs dual Intel Xeon 5th Gen Scalable processors and up to 32 DDR5 RDIMMs with eight PCIe Gen 5 x16 slots for NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs. It feeds that stack from four 2700 W redundant Titanium power supplies.

The slot and the supply do different jobs. A PCIe Gen 5 x16 link is the attachment for data and for a limited amount of slot power. A server GPU at a sustained training or high-batch inference load does not live on that slot budget. Board power has to come from the chassis rails. Eight of those boards in six U put the rails, the bus bars and the converters in the same short column of air as the GPUs, so the supply count is as much a specification as the slot count.

Titanium, on the 80 Plus ladder, is the efficiency band in which the supply turns the least of each incoming watt into heat in its own magnetics and switches. Six U leaves no spare volume for a lazy converter. Redundant, on a four-supply set, means the nameplate is not the continuous budget: the chassis is built so one module can drop and the others still hold the rail. The notice lists four 2700 W supplies, eight GPU slots, two processors and up to 32 DIMMs, and it does not assign a wattage to each GPU. The nameplate is shared headroom and a spare, not a per-chip rating.

The tradeoff flips when the GPUs are not the load and the rack feed is already full. Extra Titanium modules then buy a hot-swap and a cleaner efficiency curve, not another accelerator. They also take airflow and rear cable space a denser tray would have given to another GPU. On a light inference or visualization node, the spare supply is insurance. On a fully loaded eight-GPU box sitting on a constrained rack circuit, those four modules are what let a supply be serviced without dropping the rail, and they are why a ninth card does not fit the power story. Source: [techpowerup.com](https://www.techpowerup.com/353391/inno3d-announces-6u-rtx-pro-6000bse-gpu-server)
