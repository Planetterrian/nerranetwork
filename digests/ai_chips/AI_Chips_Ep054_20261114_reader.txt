A training step on a consumer GPU comes apart at the copy, and infra.bea.ai times that copy against the arithmetic.

The guide rates an RTX 4090 at eighty-two point six teraflops of BF16, then names three gaps the flops do not close: twenty-four gigabytes of memory against eighty, a link of thirty-two gigabytes per second against nine hundred, and no error-correcting memory where training expects it. Weights, optimizer state and activations all have to fit in those twenty-four gigabytes.

If the model is split across cards, a slice of those numbers has to move after every layer. PCI Express is the only path. There is no NVLink, so a tensor-parallel all-reduce cannot hide on a dedicated GPU fabric.

Take the guide’s seven-billion-parameter case, sharded across eight of those cards. Micro-batch of one, sequence length two thousand forty-eight, full checkpointing: activations are about two gigabytes, the running total about eighteen, and the headroom six. That six gigabytes is the budget for a longer sequence.

At four thousand ninety-six tokens the same card needs gradient checkpointing and a micro-batch of one. Longer context is the memory left after weights and optimizer state have taken their share.

The wire is why more cards do not refill that budget. Tensor-parallel all-reduce has to finish after every layer. The guide times it at six hundred forty milliseconds on PCI Express and twenty-two milliseconds on NVLink. If the forward and backward pass takes five hundred milliseconds, the PCI Express copy more than doubles the step. Fully sharded data parallel does not dodge the bus.

An all-gather at the start of each layer and a reduce-scatter at the end add about nine hundred milliseconds on PCI Express to a seven-billion-parameter step that would spend about thirty-two milliseconds on the fast link. Eight cards buy eight small memories and a bus that spends longer synchronizing than the arithmetic spends computing.

The cluster stops being the right machine when either budget is spent. Once sequence length eats the six gigabytes of headroom, another card does not make one larger address space. Once the six-hundred-forty-millisecond all-reduce outlasts the five-hundred-millisecond compute step, another card joins the copy instead of shortening it. The eighty-two point six teraflops are still on the board.

They wait on thirty-two gigabytes per second, and on twenty-four gigabytes that cannot see the next twenty-four.
