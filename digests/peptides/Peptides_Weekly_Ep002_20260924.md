# Peptides Weekly
> **Nearly one in six children taking GLP-1 drugs show nutritional deficiencies in a new study.**

**What You Need to Know:** A retrospective analysis found nutritional deficiencies in almost 17 percent of children prescribed GLP-1 receptor agonists for weight loss or diabetes. The finding comes from claims data and highlights a measurable gap in monitoring for younger patients. Separate reports note rising adverse-event filings for semaglutide in Sweden and online platforms offering the medicines without physician review.

### The Week in Peptides
**GLP-1 weight loss and diabetes drugs linked to nutritional deficiencies in nearly 1 in 6 children: ScienceDaily**
An observational analysis of insurance claims data linked GLP-1 receptor agonists to documented nutritional deficiencies in nearly one in six children. The study examined real-world prescribing records rather than a controlled trial. Researchers tracked filled prescriptions and subsequent laboratory results for vitamin and mineral shortfalls. No causal mechanism was established in the dataset. The analysis covered multiple GLP-1 agents used for type 2 diabetes or weight management. Claims databases capture routine care but miss unrecorded supplements or dietary changes. Source: [sciencedaily.com](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/09/260923035926.htm)

**Websites ease GLP-1 access without doctor oversight, Yale study finds: Yale Daily News**
A Yale review of online platforms found multiple sites supplying GLP-1 medicines after only a questionnaire and without an in-person or video visit by a licensed clinician. The study documented the absence of required medical evaluation in the sales process. Researchers classified the model as direct-to-consumer access that bypasses standard oversight. Platforms required self-reported height, weight and medical history but did not verify details with a prescriber. The review covered several high-traffic sites operating in the United States. No regulatory action was described in the report. Source: [yaledailynews.com](https://yaledailynews.com/articles/websites-help-glp-1-access-without-doctor-oversight-yale-study-finds)

**Sweden sees rise in reported adverse reactions to semaglutide: euractiv.com**
Swedish pharmacovigilance records show an increase in adverse-reaction reports tied to semaglutide in the most recent reporting period. The data come from the national spontaneous reporting system and do not include confirmed causality assessments. Reports included gastrointestinal events and other side effects already known from clinical trials. The national agency collects voluntary submissions from healthcare professionals and patients. Source: [euractiv.com](https://www.euractiv.com/news/sweden-sees-rise-in-reported-adverse-reactions-to-semaglutide/)

**Peptides Promise Superhero Healing Powers. The Dangerous Reality Does Not Match the Hype.: Outside Magazine**
An investigative feature examined unregulated peptide products sold for recovery and performance. The reporting contrasted marketing claims with documented cases of contamination and adverse effects in users. No controlled human trials supported the promoted benefits for the products discussed. The article described products labeled for research use only that reached consumers through online vendors. Laboratory testing cited in the piece revealed inconsistent purity across batches. Cyclists and other athletes were among the users interviewed for the story. Source: [velo.outsideonline.com](https://velo.outsideonline.com/road/road-training/peptide-guide-for-cyclists/)

**IGF2BP3 drives macrophage M1 polarization to promote intracranial aneurysm rupture through m6A-mediated NPY1R stabilization: Peptides**
A laboratory study in cell and animal models identified IGF2BP3 as a regulator that stabilizes NPY1R mRNA and shifts macrophages toward an M1 phenotype linked to aneurysm rupture. The work used molecular assays and rodent aneurysm models. Human data were not reported. Researchers measured changes in m6A modification on the NPY1R transcript after IGF2BP3 knockdown. Macrophage polarization was assessed by surface markers and cytokine profiles. The pathway was tested in both in-vitro cultures and in-vivo aneurysm induction. Source: [sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0196978126000598?dgcid=rss_sd_all)

### Peptide Spotlight: Semaglutide and the GLP-1 Class
Semaglutide is a long-acting analogue of the gut hormone GLP-1. It binds the GLP-1 receptor on pancreatic beta cells to increase glucose-dependent insulin release and on alpha cells to suppress glucagon. The same receptor activation in the brain reduces appetite and slows gastric emptying.

In the SUSTAIN-6 trial, 3,297 adults with type 2 diabetes at high cardiovascular risk received once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide or placebo for 104 weeks. The primary composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction or nonfatal stroke occurred in 6.6 percent of the semaglutide group versus 8.9 percent of placebo, confirming noninferiority and suggesting superiority. In the SELECT trial, 17,604 adults with overweight or obesity but without diabetes received 2.4 mg weekly semaglutide or placebo for a mean 39.8 months. The same composite endpoint occurred in 6.5 percent versus 8.0 percent, establishing a cardiovascular benefit in this population.

The PIONEER program tested oral semaglutide in more than 8,000 adults across eight phase 3 trials lasting up to 78 weeks. Doses of 7 mg and 14 mg once daily lowered HbA1c and body weight more than placebo, sitagliptin, empagliflozin, liraglutide and dulaglutide. Gastrointestinal events were the most frequent side effects and were usually transient.

A post-hoc analysis of a 32-week randomized trial found that semaglutide increased the reactive hyperemic index from baseline but the change did not reach significance versus placebo; E-selectin fell significantly versus placebo while other cell-adhesion markers showed mixed responses.

Cost-effectiveness modeling using SURMOUNT-5 head-to-head data estimated that maximum-tolerated tirzepatide produced lower total costs and higher quality-adjusted life years than maximum-tolerated semaglutide in adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes.

What it is: Semaglutide is a synthetic GLP-1 receptor agonist peptide used to improve glycemic control and reduce cardiovascular risk.
Humans given it: More than 25,000 adults participated in the phase 3 trials summarized in the abstracts.
Regulatory status: Approved in multiple countries for type 2 diabetes and for chronic weight management in adults with obesity or overweight plus at least one weight-related condition.
Risks: Transient gastrointestinal effects are the most common; cardiovascular safety has been established in large outcome trials.
What it does not show: The abstracts do not establish long-term effects on nutritional status in children or comparative safety versus newer multi-agonists outside the modeled populations.

### Worth Knowing
- An observational analysis of insurance claims shows nutritional deficiencies appear in nearly one in six children prescribed GLP-1 receptor agonists, indicating a need for laboratory monitoring in this age group.
- Spontaneous reporting data from Sweden record a recent increase in adverse-event filings for semaglutide, underscoring the value of ongoing pharmacovigilance for approved GLP-1 medicines.
- Laboratory work on IGF2BP3 and aneurysm rupture illustrates how basic peptide signaling research can identify new molecular targets, though the findings remain at the cell and animal stage.
