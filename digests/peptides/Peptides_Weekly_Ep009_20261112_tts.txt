Lipoedema symptom scores are the subject of a Metabolism Open paper on forty-nine women treated in routine care at one specialist clinic from January twenty twenty-four to May twenty twenty-six. Tirzepatide, the subject of this week’s spotlight, is an acylated peptide built to switch on two receptors at once, GIP and GLP-1, and a twenty twenty-two review calls it the first dual agonist of that pair approved for type 2 diabetes in the United States, Europe, and the UAE. The sequence is thirty-nine amino acids, drawn from native GIP and altered so one molecule can engage both receptors.

Those receptors help drive insulin release, and they also sit in brain regions that govern food intake.

The reason anyone combined the two hormones is older human physiology, summarized in a twenty twenty-one review. GIP alone, pushed to very high infusion levels, had not looked able to release insulin in people with type 2 diabetes, so it was shelved as a glucose drug. Infusing GLP-1 and GIP together produced a larger insulin response and a stronger brake on glucagon than either hormone by itself.

That is experimental physiology in people, not a phase three outcomes trial, and it does not prove the GIP half is what adds benefit once both activities live in one weekly peptide.

Five SURPASS trials in people with type 2 diabetes are the strongest human evidence in these papers. HbA1c fell by one point two four to two point five eight percentage points, and body weight fell by five point four to eleven point seven kilograms, ranges that review calls unprecedented for one agent.

Between twenty-three and sixty-two point four percent of participants hit an HbA1c target the paper describes. Glucose and weight fell further than with semaglutide, a GLP-1-only peptide, and further than with titrated basal insulin. Insulin sensitivity and the insulin secretory response improved more than with semaglutide. Mealtime insulin and glucagon were lower.

Appetite fell by a similar amount, and weight fell more, which means the extra weight loss is not explained only by the appetite change those trials recorded.

A meta-analysis that searched through the twenty-seventh of October twenty twenty-one pooled seven randomized trials lasting at least twelve weeks, six thousand six hundred nine adults with type 2 diabetes. HbA1c superiority was dose-dependent against every comparator class. Versus placebo the mean gaps ran from one point six two to two point zero six percentage points.

Versus other GLP-1 receptor agonists they ran from zero point two nine to zero point nine two. Versus basal insulin they ran from zero point seven zero to one point zero nine. Extra weight loss versus GLP-1 drugs ran from one point six eight kilograms at the lowest dose in that pool to seven point one six at the highest.

When that review was written, tirzepatide was still under review for approval. Use the twenty twenty-two paper for status, and the meta-analysis for the pooled comparisons.

Heart data in the twenty twenty-two review are a safety check with few events. Adjudicators looked across the program. A four-part outcome — nonfatal heart attack, nonfatal stroke, cardiovascular death, and admission for angina — tended to be lower over as long as about two years.

In a program-wide meta-analysis, no cardiovascular outcome had a hazard ratio above one against pooled comparators, and the upper confidence bounds for major events sat under one point three, which met a standard definition of cardiovascular safety. A ratio that has not risen above one, on a small event count, does <emphasis>not</emphasis> show that heart attacks are prevented.

The harms look like GLP-1 harms. The twenty twenty-two review names nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and constipation, more often at higher doses. In the meta-analysis, low blood sugar was about as common as with placebo and less common than with basal insulin.

Against placebo, nausea was more frequent, especially at the highest dose, with an odds ratio of five point six zero, vomiting five point five zero, and diarrhoea three point three one. Gut effects were generally similar to other GLP-1 drugs, except diarrhoea at the middle dose, odds ratio one point five one.

The highest dose led to more people leaving the study drug because of side effects, whichever comparator was used. Serious adverse events and deaths were not increased in that pool.

What is still open is the claim people repeat most. The twenty twenty-two review says GIP cuts food intake and body weight in rodents, and that those effects have not been shown in humans. It also says nobody has yet shown that turning on the GIP receptor can improve insulin secretion in type 2 diabetes, a group earlier work found unresponsive to GIP.

A larger drop in glucose and weight than with a GLP-1-only drug is a human trial result. A proven GIP explanation for that gap is not. Cardiovascular follow-up in these sources runs up to about two years, with few events.

The papers do not show a longer horizon, do not show benefit outside type 2 diabetes, and do not convert a biomarker link or an uncontrolled clinic series into proof that the dual agonist caused those changes.

One thing worth knowing from the clinic records is that heart events clustered among people who had stopped, while illness or other reasons for stopping can still explain the gap. A required eye warning is a regulator deciding a rare optic-nerve event belongs on the label, which is not the same as a trial that counts the event.

In randomized trials in thousands of adults with type 2 diabetes, the dual peptide lowered glucose and weight more than GLP-1-only drugs and basal insulin, and the human share of that gap that belongs to GIP is still unproven.

In a small clinic series without a control group, lipoedema scores improved without tracking weight loss, and that improvement cannot be credited to the drug until a controlled study says so.
