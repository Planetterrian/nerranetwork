This week's spotlight looks at semaglutide and the broader GLP-1 receptor agonist class.

Semaglutide is a long-acting analogue of the gut hormone GLP-1.

It binds the GLP-1 receptor on pancreatic beta cells to increase glucose-dependent insulin release and on alpha cells to suppress glucagon.

The same receptor activation in the brain reduces appetite and slows gastric emptying.

In the SUSTAIN-6 trial, three thousand two hundred ninety-seven adults with type 2 diabetes at high cardiovascular risk received once-weekly subcutaneous semaglutide or placebo for 104 weeks.

That result confirmed noninferiority and suggested superiority.

In the SELECT trial, seventeen thousand six hundred four adults with overweight or obesity but without diabetes received 2.4 mg weekly semaglutide or placebo for a mean 39.8 months.

The same composite endpoint occurred in 6.5 percent versus 8.0 percent.

That established a cardiovascular benefit in this population.

The PIONEER program tested oral semaglutide in more than eight thousand adults across eight phase 3 trials lasting up to 78 weeks.

Doses of 7 mg and 14 mg once daily lowered HbA1c and body weight more than placebo, sitagliptin, empagliflozin, liraglutide and dulaglutide.

Gastrointestinal events were the most frequent side effects and were usually transient.

A post-hoc analysis of a 32-week randomized trial found that semaglutide increased the reactive hyperemic index from baseline but the change did not reach significance versus placebo.

E-selectin fell significantly versus placebo while other cell-adhesion markers showed mixed responses.

Cost-effectiveness modeling using SURMOUNT-5 head-to-head data estimated that maximum-tolerated tirzepatide produced lower total costs and higher quality-adjusted life years than maximum-tolerated semaglutide in adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes.
