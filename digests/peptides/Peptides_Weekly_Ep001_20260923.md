# Peptides Weekly
> **Phase 3 results for an investigational amylin peptide medicine in diabetes and obesity were released this week.**

**What You Need to Know:** Novo Nordisk reported new phase 3 data on an amylin-based candidate. A pair of randomized trials found oral semaglutide did not slow progression in mild Alzheimer’s disease. This episode introduces how the show evaluates peptide evidence from cells through human trials.

### What This Show Covers
A peptide is a short chain of amino acids. The term now appears in contexts that range from long-approved medicines such as insulin to vials sold online with “research use only” labels. The show explains what each compound is, how it is studied, and what the data actually show at each evidence level. Insulin serves as the first worked example because it is a peptide whose regulatory status, manufacturing history, and clinical record are documented in peer-reviewed sources. The posture is education and awareness only. The show never supplies doses, schedules, combinations, sources, or protocols and never instructs anyone to start, stop, or change a treatment. Every named compound carries the regulatory status stated in the source material. Peptides Weekly is part of the Nerra Network, an independent, ad-free network of shows at nerranetwork.com. Listeners are invited to follow the evidence as it is published rather than as it is marketed.

### The Week in Peptides
**Phase 3 Results for Next-Generation Diabetes and Obesity Medicine: Novo Nordisk**
A randomized phase 3 trial tested an investigational amylin peptide candidate in adults with obesity or diabetes. The company stated the results move the program closer to potential human use. The findings remain at the phase 3 randomized human trial level. Source: [novonordisk.com](https://www.novonordisk.com/news-and-media/news-and-ir-materials/news-details.html?id=916774)

**Oral semaglutide ineffective for mild Alzheimer's in EVOKE trials: The Lancet**
Two randomized human trials examined oral semaglutide, a GLP-1 peptide, in patients with mild Alzheimer’s disease. Neither trial showed slowing of disease progression. The result is a negative finding from phase 3 randomized trials. Source: [thelancet.com](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(26)00459-9/fulltext)

**Altered gastrointestinal function and neurotensin signaling in experimental type 2 diabetes: Peptides (journal)**
An animal study measured neurotensin signaling and gut function in a rodent model of type 2 diabetes. The work is at the experimental animal level. Source: [sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0196978126000458?dgcid=rss_sd_all)

**KR-36996, an orally active urotensin II receptor antagonist, attenuates hepatic inflammation and fibrosis in methionine- and choline-deficient diet-induced fibrosing steatohepatitis: Peptides (journal)**
A rodent study tested an oral urotensin II receptor antagonist in a dietary model of liver disease. The evidence is at the animal study level. Source: [sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0196978126000549?dgcid=rss_sd_all)

**Phoenixin-14 attenuates pancreatic injury by modulating inflammation, oxidative stress, and apoptosis in L-arginine-induced acute pancreatitis: Peptides (journal)**
Researchers gave phoenixin-14 to rodents with chemically induced pancreatitis and recorded changes in inflammation and cell death markers. The data come from an animal model. Source: [sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0196978126000501?dgcid=rss_sd_all)

### Peptide Spotlight: Insulin: The Peptide Everyone Already Trusts
Insulin is a peptide hormone whose signaling pathway is examined in multiple studies of metabolic disease. One longitudinal observational study followed 17 Pima Indians whose glucose tolerance deteriorated from normal to impaired to diabetic over an average of 5.1 years. Transition from normal to impaired glucose tolerance coincided with increased body weight and reduced insulin sensitivity, while insulin secretion remained relatively preserved until later stages. Endogenous glucose output rose as tolerance worsened. These changes were quantified through repeated measurements of insulin action, secretion, and glucose production during oral glucose tolerance tests.

Mitochondrial function was tracked in a separate time-course study using Otsuka Long-Evans Tokushima Fatty rats and their non-hyperphagic Long-Evans Tokushima Otsuka controls. At five weeks of age, hepatic mitochondrial respiration and enzyme activities were already reduced in the obese strain, before elevations in serum insulin, glucose, or hepatic triglyceride content appeared. Insulin resistance and steatosis developed only at later ages, indicating that mitochondrial abnormalities preceded measurable resistance in this model. The study sacrificed groups at 5, 8, 13, 20, and 40 weeks to map the sequence.

Insulin-like growth factor 1, a peptide in the same signaling family, was measured in a field study of 669 wild Soay sheep lambs sampled at approximately four months of age across nine summers. Higher circulating IGF-1 concentrations correlated with faster early growth rates and higher overwinter survival. The association held after accounting for cohort and environmental variation, showing that variation in this pathway tracks life-history traits under natural conditions.

Additional observational work examined insulin resistance patterns in women with prior miscarriage and in elderly adults stratified by obesity status. Postprandial insulin resistance showed stronger links to miscarriage history than fasting measures alone in one multicenter cohort. Sex differences in basal insulin secretion emerged more clearly in obese versus non-obese elderly groups. None of these studies establish causal mechanisms or test therapeutic interventions.

Insulin is an approved biologic medicine. The supplied sources do not establish regulatory status or long-term outcome data for any newer peptide that match the breadth of human observations accumulated for insulin. No source reports oral formulations reaching approval or documents specific manufacturing transitions.

### Evidence Ledger
- Transition from normal to impaired glucose tolerance was linked to increased body weight and reduced insulin sensitivity before insulin secretion declined, from a longitudinal human observational study of 17 Pima Indians followed 5.1 years on average: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/10491414
- Hepatic mitochondrial dysfunction was detected at five weeks of age in obese Otsuka Long-Evans Tokushima Fatty rats, before insulin resistance or steatosis appeared, from an animal time-course study: https://europepmc.org/article/MED/20347174
- Higher plasma IGF-1 concentrations predicted faster growth and survival in wild Soay sheep lambs, from an observational field study of 669 animals: https://europepmc.org/article/AGR/IND609489719

Insulin shows what documented evidence looks like for a peptide therapeutic.
