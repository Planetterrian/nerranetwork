Oral semaglutide is this week's spotlight, a GLP-1 peptide built so a fraction of it
can cross the stomach instead of going in through a needle.
Most peptide drugs are injected because a peptide is a short chain of amino acids, and the
gut is designed to cut chains like that apart.
An injection skips that digestion, getting a peptide into a tablet has to solve two problems
together, survive the gut, and cross the gut wall in an amount that matters.
A 2016 review looked at more than 250 intestinal permeation enhancers tested in intestinal
delivery models, mostly preclinical work, and stressed that enhancer and peptide have
to show up together at the small intestinal lining in a high enough concentration.
Some proprietary enhancer systems have reached clinical trials.
That paper is not a trial of one approved tablet.
