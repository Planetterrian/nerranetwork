Premier Lorne Doerkson signals $1,000 B.C. income tax cut in Spring 2027: Daily Hive Vancouver

Premier Lorne Doerkson says his newly sworn-in Conservative government will bring its promised $1,000 annual provincial income tax cut to the legislature in the spring 2027 session. He told reporters today, just after he and his cabinet were sworn in at Government House, "We're committed to doing that, and it will happen in certainly the spring session. My hope, don't hold me to this, my hope is that that will be dated back to Jan. 1." A fall sitting was ruled out this week. The Conservative platform would raise the basic personal amount from $13,216 to $32,000. The party estimated about $1,000 a year for someone who would otherwise pay at least that much in B.C. income tax, or about $2,000 for a couple if both qualify, and a smaller saving or none for someone who already pays little or no provincial income tax. The platform put the cost at $725 million before March 31, 2027, and $2.9 billion for each full year after. The former BC NDP September forecast put the 2026/2027 deficit at $13.8 billion; the Conservatives' own plan still projected $13.7 billion that year. Finance Minister Peter Milobar, sworn in today, accused former premier David Eby during the October 2026 campaign of breaking a grocery-rebate promise the NDP administration shelved in February 2025. Doerkson also named Carson Binda, formerly the B.C. director of the Canadian Taxpayers Federation, Minister of State for Red Tape Reduction, and first-time Coquitlam-Burke Mountain MLA Ryan Peterson as health minister, citing a ministry the previous government budgeted at $40.6 billion for 2026/2027. Vancouver Council Passes ‘the People’s Audit’: The Tyee

Council also directed staff to carry out a June report on ending traffic deaths and injuries, including recommendations for 30 or 40 km/h limits on major streets, after three pedestrians were killed and two injured since late October, among them an 87-year-old woman whose death Vancouver police announced in November. Council cancelled a robot food-delivery pilot that Serve Robotics' Vignesh Ram defended as an evidence trial for which the city had already hired local staff; the Canadian National Institute for the Blind and Vision Zero Vancouver said the machines threaten people who need clear, predictable sidewalks. Top Stories

Metro Vancouver's living wage has increased as everyday costs rise: Daily Hive

BC Policy Solutions, in its BC Living Wage 2026 report, calculates the hourly wage a full-time worker needs to cover essential expenses, avoid chronic financial stress, and take part in community life. In Metro Vancouver that figure is $28.55, a 2.5 per cent rise from 2025’s $27.85, and $10.30 above B.C.’s $18.25 minimum wage. The institute’s press release says the increase reflects wages not keeping up with the cost of living; it ties higher prices for food, fuel, clothing and footwear to the trade war with the U.S. and the war in Iran. It says 35 per cent of Metro Vancouver workers, more than half a million people, earn less than the living wage. Lead author Iglika Ivanova, co-executive director and senior economist, says the Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation reports rent cuts and vacancies concentrated in new high-end buildings, while the market stays tight for lower-income households, especially families needing two or more bedrooms. Food, the second-largest expense in the living-wage budget, rose 4.1 per cent from last year, and out-of-pocket childcare for the living-wage family of four rose by nearly $160 a month. The report says the wage would have been higher without the Canadian Dental Care Plan, the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit and the BC Rental Assistance Program, and that ending the BC Climate Action Tax Credit hurt low-wage earners without children. Co-author Navdeep Singh Chhina, managing director of Living Wage BC, called on the incoming provincial government to invest in affordable housing, public transit and childcare. Squamish’s living wage is $29.05, up 3.8 per cent, and Whistler’s is $30.50, up 3 per cent; the lowest in B.C. is Grand Forks at $22.15, and Port Alberni is $23.75. Source: dailyhive.com Source: biv.com
