Vancouver Daily News

A thirty-two-thousand-dollar basic exemption is the first bill Lorne Doerkson wants for B.C. taxpayers.

What You Need to Know: Premier-designate Lorne Doerkson says his first bill will raise the basic personal exemption to $32,000, so earnings up to that amount are not taxed. A day after the Conservative win, he also left a fall sitting open, named a transition chair, and set a day-one review of a deficit he put at nearly $14 billion. Elsewhere, more than 1,200 workers are off the job at Highland Valley Copper, and about 150 North Vancouver grads spent hours stuck on a party boat in Burrard Inlet.

Top Stories

B.C. premier-designate Lorne Doerkson hints at a fall legislative session: Vancouver Sun

A day after becoming premier-designate, Lorne Doerkson told reporters at the JW Marriott Parq Vancouver that he has not ruled out a fall legislative session, and that briefings with the public service begin in the coming days. He said that on day one the government will launch an independent review of the province’s finances before a budget, citing a deficit of nearly $14 billion and eight credit downgrades in three years, and that within the first 100 days it will stand up an expert commission on keeping emergency rooms open and cutting wait times. The Vancouver Sun reported that his first big bill will raise the basic personal exemption to $32,000, and that the change has to be made by January 1 if British Columbians are to see it in the 2027 tax year; Daily Hive reported the cut as about $1,000 a year for nearly every working British Columbian, and quoted him saying, “We said we will not raise your taxes, and we will not.” Daily Hive also reported NDP forecasts of a $13.8 billion deficit for 2026/2027, $12.7 billion for 2027/2028 and $12 billion for 2028/2029, with total provincial debt expected at $180.9 billion by the end of this fiscal year and almost $236 billion before the end of the decade, against about $89 billion when David Eby became premier in late 2022. He appointed Kim Henderson, former deputy minister to Christy Clark and former head of the public service, to chair the transition team; Daily Hive reported she will do it as a volunteer on unpaid leave from Trans Mountain, and that he has spoken with Lieutenant Governor Wendy Cocchia, with a swearing-in later next month. He said he will remain party leader and called the interim-leader rule an administrative detail; Daily Hive reported the party constitution does not let an interim leader run for the permanent job without a two-thirds vote, and that the board pledged during the campaign to pursue that change. He said he still intends to repeal the Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act, wants that as soon as possible and pointed to the spring 2027 session when asked about timing, while saying he will take a meeting with First Nations any day a partnership can grow. He said he wants a call with Prime Minister Mark Carney and Alberta Premier Danielle Smith on the proposed Pacific Link pipeline, called the U.S. trade conflict the province’s biggest economic threat, and said he wants to talk to Carney about expanding $10-a-day daycare, including spaces in private facilities. He reaffirmed a switch back to a 10-lane bridge to replace the George Massey Tunnel, said early site work should continue for now, and said the immersed-tunnel project cannot proceed in its current form; Daily Hive reported the updated tunnel cost at $8.5 billion, up from $4.15 billion, with no environmental assessment certificate and the bidding split into five contracts. He promised a Respect Local Democracy Act to ban provincial and municipal campaigns within 90 days of each other, said he has no plan yet for the Vancouver Whitecaps but wants a conversation, and said he will hold a business summit in Vancouver next summer. As of Sunday morning the Sun reported the Conservatives had won 59 seats and led in five more, the NDP had won 23 and led in four, and the Greens had won two, with 99.75 percent of ballot boxes reporting and a final count on November 6; Daily Hive later reported the Conservatives leading in 63 of 93 ridings, the NDP ahead in 28 and the Greens in two, with the final count scheduled for November 6 to 10. The Closer Look

BC NDP party faithful dismayed at impacts of Eby’s snap election: Island Social Trends

Island Social Trends reported from Victoria that the snap election has put the B.C. NDP back in Official Opposition after nine years leading the province, and that Sunday found party loyalists still sorting a campaign many had not expected. The next election had been scheduled for October 2028, with two years left in the 43rd Parliament. The provincial campaign also overlapped municipal campaigns already underway toward an October 17 civic vote, with the provincial election set for October 24.

Bowinn Ma, re-elected in North Vancouver-Lonsdale, defended the call on election night and repeated David Eby’s point from the trail: votes to pass legislation had to be cobbled together from MLAs who were in hospital, including for maternity or cancer. Adrian Dix, re-elected in Vancouver-Kingsway for a seventh time, told television the other way. “Obviously it was a mistake to call the election,” Dix said. He said the call overwhelmed the campaign, did not let the party’s issues break through, and hurt people’s assessment of the premier, and that the premier took responsibility that night.

Dix, who lost as NDP leader in 2013, said the burden on a leader who loses is enormous, for the candidates, the people and the issues. He recalled that the NDP won only two seats in 2001, nearly won in 2005, and then won in 2017, 2020 and 2024. “We’ll be back, you bet we’ll be back,” he said. He said the party’s job now is a strong Opposition that holds the Conservatives to account, and he described that victory as one “without substance, a clear platform or agenda.” He called the night immensely disappointing and named losses by incumbent Sunita Dhir, hopeful Richard Zussman, and Surrey candidates Amna Shah and Jesse Sunner, whom he called future stars, plus Jagrup Brar and Gary Begg. He said health care and education delivered over nine years are part of what was at stake.

Former Victoria-Swan Lake MLA Rob Fleming, who held the education file under John Horgan from 2005 to 2024, spoke Saturday night at the South Island after-party in downtown Victoria. “It was David Eby’s decision and it was his decision alone to call this snap election,” Fleming told Island Social Trends. He called it a spectacularly poor political calculus, seen as cynical and unwanted beside the municipal elections. He said Kerry-Lynne Findlay resigned on a Sunday and Eby called the election on a Tuesday anyway, after expecting to run against her. Fleming said good candidates and former ministers who lost would probably not send Eby a best-wishes card. He emceed for four who were re-elected: Diana Gibson, Nina Krieger, Grace Lore and Darlene Rotchford. He also said a Green vote produced Conservatives on the south Island, and that he believes Saanich South was lost for Richard Zussman because of a heavy Green vote. Source: islandsocialtrends.ca

Vancouver Daily News

Sixty-four seats give the Conservatives a majority, and David Eby is resigning as NDP leader.

What You Need to Know: Sixty-four seats give the Conservatives a majority, and David Eby is resigning as NDP leader after Saturday’s snap election. He conceded less than two hours after polls closed, keeps his own Vancouver seat on the projection, and stays premier only until the new government takes over. Elections BC’s final count runs November 6 to 10.

Top Stories

David Eby steps down as BC NDP leader after snap election defeat: Philippine Canadian Inquirer

Conservative interim leader Lorne Doerkson is premier-elect after Saturday’s vote, with the Conservatives leading in 64 of 93 districts to the NDP’s 27 and the Greens’ two. Island Social Trends, citing Elections BC, put the Conservative share at 48.05 per cent, the NDP at 37.38 per cent and the Greens at 10.97 per cent, with preliminary turnout at 48.06 per cent. Eby, who called the election on September 22 from a one-seat majority, nearly two years early, conceded less than two hours after polls closed at 8 p.m. and told supporters in Vancouver, “This is obviously something I do with a very heavy heart. But it is time for new leadership within our party.” He was projected to hold Vancouver-Point Grey, said he had called Doerkson to congratulate him, and remains premier during the transition. The Philippine Canadian Inquirer reported the result puts Doerkson on course to be the first Conservative premier since 1933; a majority needed 47 seats. The Vancouver Sun counted 1,770,371 votes, 47.97 per cent of registered voters, against more than two million and 58.74 per cent in 2024. Green leader Emily Lowan, 26, won Saanich North and the Islands, and Daily Hive quoted her saying the Greens are “the only party who is equipped to stand against the Conservatives and their existence.” Elections BC’s final count is November 6 to 10; Island Social Trends said about 70,000 mail and other ballots could move close races but not who formed government. Source: canadianinquirer.net Source: islandsocialtrends.ca Source: vancouversun.com Source: dailyhive.com

The Closer Look

ELECTION NIGHT LIVE: B.C. Conservatives capture all 10 seats as blue wave engulfs Surrey: Surrey Now-Leader

The Conservatives took all 10 Surrey ridings on Saturday, a clean sweep the Vancouver Sun also recorded, in a city where the party had won six of 10 in 2024. The Sun named longtime NDP MLAs who lost: Jagrup Brar in Surrey-Fleetwood, Amna Shah in Surrey-City Centre, Jessie Sunner in Surrey-Newton and Garry Begg in Surrey-Guildford. Surrey Now-Leader reported Conservative Purewal won Surrey-Fleetwood, ousting Brar.

The closest count was Surrey-City Centre. With all 72 ballot boxes in, Zeeshan Wahla had 5,696 votes, 47.5 per cent, to incumbent Amna Shah’s 5,305, 44.2 per cent. The lead changed hands through the night and, at one point, the two were a single vote apart. Shah had beaten Wahla in 2024 only after an Elections BC recount. Oliver Wildfong took 583 votes and Raman Sharma 418.

In Surrey-Newton, Harj Dhaliwal finished with 7,316 votes to Jessie Sunner’s 5,461, ending years of NDP control. Sunner, Minister of Post-Secondary Education and Future Skills, had won the seat in 2024 with 7,924 votes. In Surrey North, incumbent Mandeep Singh Dhaliwal had 7,378 to Sahib Thind’s 4,201 with all 65 boxes counted. In Surrey-Panorama, Bryan Tepper had 8,557 to Jinny Sims’s 4,886 with all 60 boxes in.

Surrey South went to Megan Knight, the former White Rock mayor, with 12,550 votes, 61.4 per cent, to Parik Tahlan’s 4,533 and CentreBC leader Elenore Sturko’s 2,074, after all 82 boxes. Knight told Peace Arch News she thought Eby “didn’t listen to the people,” and that a Conservative government is likely to look into housing edicts resisted in White Rock. Sturko congratulated Knight and said she hoped the new government serves British Columbia well. In Surrey-White Rock, Trevor Halford won again with 14,332 votes, 58.2 per cent, to Darryl Walker’s 7,123. Halford told supporters that some 20,000 B.C. workers had lost their jobs in recent weeks and said, “Tonight, David Eby lost his job.”

Garry Thind won Surrey-Guildford with 7,693 votes to Garry Begg’s 6,230, all 76 boxes counted. A week earlier he had placed second for Surrey school trustee with 43,900 votes, after the September 18 withdrawal deadline had passed and his name stayed on the October 17 civic ballot. He told Surrey Now-Leader he had not yet been sworn in as trustee and that staff were looking for a legal way to avoid a byelection. The Eligibility to Hold Public Office Act, as the paper cited it, disqualifies an MLA from local office and deems the person to have resigned that office on the date of the writ. Thind said people would be happy if taxpayers did not pay for another vote. The Sun said the same Conservative wave also swept Delta and Richmond.
