Melissa Granum elected mayor of Delta: Vancouver Sun

Melissa Granum won the Delta mayoral race with 49 percent of the vote, 14,988 ballots, in results the Vancouver Sun reported at 10 p.m. Dylan Kruger was second with 28 percent, 8,567 votes, and George Harvie third with 23 percent, 6,985. Doug McCallum unseats Brenda Locke in Surrey: Vancouver Sun

Former mayor Doug McCallum unseated incumbent Brenda Locke, taking 31.9 percent of the vote to Locke's 23 percent with all 164 polls reporting, 42,741 votes to 30,886. Locke conceded just before 10 p.m., telling a solemn crowd, "Surrey voters have definitely spoken and this was not the result we counted on," then congratulated McCallum and, asked if she would run again, said, "Never say never." McCallum said, "We will address affordability, we will address your safety, and we will address your infrastructure," and said he planned to appoint Mandeep Nagra as deputy mayor. Safe Surrey won a council majority; the only councillor not on that slate is incumbent Harry Bains, from Locke's team, who received the most votes of any candidate. Elford called the result surprising and said residents could expect the slate to carry out its promises, including a four-year property-tax freeze, purchasing a police helicopter and seeking funding for another Surrey hospital, "as best as we can." A Leger poll for Postmedia News, conducted Sept. 23 to 30, had suggested Locke would receive a second term. Advance voting was 52,600 ballots, about 15 percent of registered voters, up 47 percent from 35,701 advance votes in 2022. McCallum previously served as mayor from 1996 to 2005 and from 2018 until his loss to Locke in 2022; Locke won that race by less than 1,000 votes. The Sun reported the two became rivals over policing, with McCallum wanting a municipal force and Locke wanting to keep the RCMP. Mike Hurley wins third term as Burnaby mayor: Freshet News

Mike Hurley has won a third term as mayor of Burnaby, and the Burnaby Citizens Association kept a majority on city council. Freshet News reported unofficial results from Saturday's vote. Hurley won a six-way mayoral race with 28,946 votes, 58.43 percent of 50,326 ballots cast. Burnaby First candidate Heymann Yip was second with 9,250 votes, 18.67 percent.

He thanked residents for electing him to another four years and said, "It's an amazing journey, an opportunity to get out there and help people, which is something that I live to do."

The council ballot had 42 candidates and several new parties. The BCA majority held. Three BCA incumbents did not run: Sav Dhaliwal, Pietro Calendino and Alison Gu. Three new BCA candidates were elected in their place: Cindy Lee, Ryan Erwin and Kiran Singh. New Burnaby Party incumbent Richard Lee remains the only non-BCA member of council. Freshet News said it was unable to reach Lee for comment on election night.

Joe Keithley, first elected with the Burnaby Green Party and switched to the BCA in December, led the council count with 18,073 votes, 5.51 percent. Cindy Lee was next with 17,429, 5.32 percent, then Ryan Erwin with 16,476, 5.03 percent. Lee said she had not expected that finish and credited support from her own community and from running with the BCA team. She said many residents had read the candidates' profiles and recognized her while she was door-knocking, and that "in Burnaby, our residents are really responsible. That's why we have a great city."

Incumbent BCA councillors Maita Santiago and Daniel Tetrault each kept a seat for a second term, with 16,292 votes, 4.97 percent, and 15,288, 4.66 percent. Richard Lee also won a second term, with 13,257 votes, 4.04 percent. Kiran Singh took the last council seat, finishing 1,180 votes ahead of independent Allen Chen: Singh had 13,209 votes, 4.03 percent, and Chen 12,029, 3.67 percent.

Voter turnout in Burnaby rose to 29.23 percent of eligible voters, from 19.7 percent in 2022. Source: [freshetnews.ca](https://www.freshetnews.ca/mike-hurley-wins-third-term-as-burnaby-mayor/)

Chinese state-owned XCMG facing lawsuits alleging it defrauded Canadian equipment dealers: Global BC

At Vancouver's law courts on Friday, veteran heavy-equipment dealer Ryan Martin said a business that was good for years has wrecked his family. His company, Arctic Machinery, with dealerships in Prince George, Chilliwack and Kelowna, started selling machines from Chinese state-owned manufacturer XCMG in twenty seventeen. Martin said business was good until about twenty twenty-one. "It's destroyed our family," he told Global News. XCMG is one of the world's largest manufacturers of heavy equipment. Martin said the quality changed several years ago: "The quality of the equipment that time was not what the quality was in the past."

Civil lawsuits now allege that XCMG defrauded equipment dealers in Canada. Martin said other Canadian XCMG dealers hit similar problems and, in troubleshooting them, found what they describe as evidence of a process "of changing serial number plates and changing the years of the machines in order to maybe misrepresent to the end user what they were buying." His example was a machine built in twenty twenty and sold to the dealer as a twenty twenty-two. In July, a Saskatchewan dealer sued XCMG, alleging unlawful tampering with serial-number plates on equipment sold in Alberta this year. LTR Equipment, based in Quebec, filed a similar case. A lift company in Ontario has also filed, alleging serial numbers were tampered with.

XCMG Canada told Global News: "Arctic Machinery purchased approximately $18 million in XCMG-branded inventory, which it did not pay for." The company said it "is fully defending the claims in the civil actions." Martin said the family firm was forced into bankruptcy when XCMG called in its loans, which he said was retaliation for blowing the whistle on the alleged tampering. The people and companies in these suits are accusing and defending, not convicted of a crime. RCMP in Alberta's K Division confirmed that members of their auto theft unit carried out a search warrant at XCMG's headquarters in April and that the investigation is ongoing. For Arctic Machinery, the fight is already a bankruptcy. For buyers of those machines, the allegation is that a serial plate and a model year may not have matched the equipment they thought they were getting. Both Sides: Compensation when Crown land moves to First Nations

A leaked provincial briefing and a presentation dated September tenth outline a commitment to transfer four hundred parcels of Crown land to First Nations, and proposals to limit, through Forest Act changes, what timber-rights holders would be paid, citing continuing fiscal constraints. The presentation landed less than two weeks before Premier David Eby called a snap election. Kim Haakstad, chief executive of the Council of Forest Industries, said she first learned of the proposal in early August under non-disclosure agreements, and is speaking now because reporter Rob Shaw made the documents public on Northern Beat.

Speaking as NDP leader, Eby said the list comes from "very public, discussed agreements with treaty First Nations," some signed more than a decade ago under previous governments, and that the documents are proposals to reduce the cost of those transfers. His best reason is the taxpayer: "to drive down the cost of that, so that taxpayers aren’t bearing the cost of that, is absolutely crucial." He said even B.C. Conservative leader Lorne Doerkson agrees that agreements with First Nations are part of the path forward, and that some transfers clear the way for forestry where "court challenges to forest tenures, to cutting permits and other things" have left the industry stalled.

Haakstad said timber companies support reconciliation and understand that those agreements need to happen. Her objection is who pays. "We think this proposal should not go forward," she said. "We think it is not acceptable to have us pay for these costs, especially at this time when the sector is in a deepening crisis." B.C. Conservative candidate Scott McInnis called the behind-closed-doors proposal unethical and "absolutely abhorrent," a betrayal of an industry "begging this government for some action and some support." He said the party supports partnerships that advance quality of life for Indigenous people, and that the public is not willing to accept agreements made behind closed doors. Vancouver lawyer Wes Mussio said that where a transfer is meant to let forestry proceed, government would open itself to "quite a few legal disputes." He said the development industry has virtually ground to a halt, buyers are no longer lining up for pre-construction sales, and the investor class has gone quiet, with some shifting capital to Alberta and Washington State. Ryalls said many of Vancouver's unsold units are small and were built with short-term rentals or international students in mind. Those markets dried up when government policy changed. There is also uncertainty over mortgage rates. What is being decided, in a market report rather than a council vote, is how homes above $4 million in West Vancouver should be priced and shown this fall. Sotheby's International Realty Canada says the market is firmly in buyers' territory. Its best reason is the count: from Jan. 1 to Aug. 30, 57 properties sold for more than $4 million, a 25 percent decrease from 76 in the same period last year. Managing broker Kevin O'Toole says 195 residential properties were listed above $4 million as of Sept. 1, and that sellers need to be realistic with pricing expectations, conditions and timeframes to attract qualified buyers. The case for a wider net, including a public listing, is also Sotheby's. The firm says its review of land title and MLS records from January 2024 through August 2026 found that one in seven West Vancouver properties above $4 million sells without ever appearing on MLS, and it still says that in current conditions broader public marketing on MLS, with private outreach, is key to attracting qualified purchasers. Chief marketing officer Elaine Hung says that where supply exceeds demand, advisors are encouraging sellers to combine targeted outreach with exposure in premier media and on MLS. Daily Hive, using Zealty, says about 30 homes sold above $4 million in the last 90 days, and that only one of those was ultra-luxury, at $10 million or more. Both readings accept the same drop in sales and the same surplus of listings. The report puts the market in buyers' territory this fall and does not set a date when that would flip.
