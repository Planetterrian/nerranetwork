Fifty-eight teardowns later, the pattern should be familiar enough to
carry with you. Almost nothing you buy costs what its atoms cost. The
gap — the Idiot Index this book opened by defining — is where all the
interesting information lives: sometimes it measures genuine
difficulty, sometimes accumulated regulation, sometimes a patent or a
bottleneck or a billing department, and sometimes nothing but the fact
that no one has asked the question recently.

Three habits are worth keeping. First, always find the floor before
judging the price: the magic-wand estimate — what physics and commodity
markets would charge if everything else vanished — turns outrage into
arithmetic. Second, watch the denominator: half the errors this series
corrected in its own chapters came from comparing a delivered price
against the wrong floor, or a one-time cost against a recurring one.
Third, treat a high index as an invitation, not a verdict. The stories
that ended well — sunlight, containers, light itself — are the ones
where somebody treated the gap as an engineering target.

The teardowns continue: *First Principles Daily* publishes a new one
every day, and future collections will gather them as they accumulate.
The last page of this book tells you where to listen.
