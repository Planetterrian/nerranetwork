Seventy-three stories, eight kinds of terrain, and a handful of mechanisms that
kept reappearing no matter the century or the country. It is worth
naming them plainly on the way out.

Incentives are honest. People respond to what a system rewards, not to
what its designers hoped it would reward — the test-focused school
districts, the landlords converting rental units, and the drivers
adjusting to their mandated seatbelts were all behaving exactly as
the rules they were handed suggested they should. When a policy produces surprising behavior, the surprise
is usually in the policy, not the people.

Measurement changes the thing measured. A target that stands in for a
goal will be hit at the goal's expense — reading scores for education,
visitor counts for conservation, tar numbers for health. The corrective
is not to stop measuring but to keep asking what the measure has
quietly replaced.

Risk is more often moved than removed. Seatbelts, levees, filters,
flood insurance: intervention after intervention shifted danger to a
different place, a different population, or a different decade, and the
ledger only looked clean because nobody was reading the far column.

And finally: the fixes have consequences too. Several chapters end with
a correction that spawned its own chapter-worthy story. That is not a
counsel of despair — it is the strongest argument in this book for
humility, for monitoring after the ribbon-cutting, and for designing
policies that can be revised without being defended to the death.

These stories continue. New episodes of *Unintended Consequences* are
published daily, and the show's story tracker keeps the threads in this
book up to date as the record moves. The last page of this book tells
you where to find them.
