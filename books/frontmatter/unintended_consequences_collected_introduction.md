Every story in this book starts the same way: someone saw a problem,
designed a solution, and was right about almost everything — except what
happened next. A hydroelectric dam that tamed a river and starved a
delta. A drug-education program taught to millions of children that made
no measurable difference. A wonder vine planted to save the soil of the
American South that became, in the telling if not quite in the ground, a
monster.

That last one is where this collection begins, because the kudzu story
is a test the reader should watch this book pass. The version most
people know — the vine that ate the South, swallowing a state's worth of
land — is mostly myth, and the first chapter takes it apart with the
actual survey numbers. That is the standard for everything that follows:
these are not just-so stories about hubris. Where a claim carries a
source, the source was checked, and the Sources section at the back of
the book lists them.

The thirty stories here were chosen from more than eighty episodes of
*Unintended Consequences*, the Nerra Network's daily podcast, and they
were chosen for a specific reason: each one shows a distinct *mechanism*
by which good intentions go wrong. Perverse incentives. Shifted
baselines. Measurement that becomes the target. Risk moved rather than
removed. The five parts group them by terrain — nature, medicine,
infrastructure, rules, and the machines and systems we build — but the
mechanisms echo across all five, and noticing the echoes is most of the
value of reading the collection whole.

Two honest notes before you begin. First, several of these stories are
still moving: lawsuits settle, agencies revise numbers, ecosystems keep
changing. Each chapter reflects the record as it stood when the story
was verified for this edition. Second, the lesson of this book is not
that intervention is futile — several chapters are about interventions
that worked, at a cost nobody priced in. The lesson is narrower and more
useful: the consequences you cannot see from the design table are the
ones that decide how the story ends.
