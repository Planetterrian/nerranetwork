Ep 60: Two Russian missiles and a drone fell in Poland, testing NATO's line on collective defence.
Download Read transcript

Omni View Top World News

Two Russian missiles and a drone fell in Poland, testing NATO's line on collective defence.

What You Need to Know: Two Russian missiles and a drone fell on Polish territory during Russia's overnight attack on Ukraine, and Warsaw is treating the hits as a deliberate test of the alliance. Poland's defence minister says the weapons came down in the southeast on November 20, with no immediate injuries, while the prime minister calls it an intentional provocation. This edition also draws on the Omni View Omni View North America desk.

The Ten

Russian missiles hit Poland: What happened, and could NATO respond?: Al Jazeera

In Poland, a NATO member on Ukraine's western border, the government says Russian weapons fell on its territory during the night of November 20, while Russia was bombarding Ukraine and European troops were drilling in the southeast. Poland's Defence Minister, Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz, told a news conference in Warsaw that two missiles and a jet-powered drone came down in the country's southeast. He placed the missiles at Nowy Lipowiec and Rudnik nad Sanem, and the drone at Molodycz. Al Jazeera reports that security cameras captured the moment a suspected Russian missile struck Polish territory during that attack on Ukraine. The government says Poland may have been hit by two missiles and a jet-powered drone. No immediate injuries or damage were reported.

The geography is what turns debris into a political fact. One missile is believed to have crashed about 35 kilometres, or 22 miles, from where European troops were exercising. A Kh-101 cruise missile went down about 50 kilometres, or 31 miles, from the Ukrainian border. What officials believe was a jet-powered drone hit about 30 kilometres, or 20 miles, from that border. The incidents came as the United States pushes to revive talks on ending the war in Ukraine, and they have raised the question of whether the strike was deliberate. Al Jazeera's account notes that Warsaw accuses Moscow of intentional provocation, while the bar for triggering NATO's collective-defence clause remains high.

That same night, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia fired more than 400 drones and at least 110 missiles, including 27 ballistic missiles, some with cluster warheads. He said at least eight civilians were killed and more than two dozen were wounded. Al Jazeera describes the barrage as one of Russia's heaviest bombardments of Ukraine. The Polish impacts are therefore not a separate skirmish in the reporting. They are objects that left the attack on Ukraine and came down inside a treaty ally, close to a border and close to foreign troops who were already in the field.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk said it appeared "we are dealing with an intentional provocation" by Russia. He said the incident was likely timed to coincide with the European military exercises in Poland. Those exercises are described as unprecedented. The two-week drill began on November 9 in southeastern Poland. It is preparing forces for a possible post-war mission in Ukraine, to help stabilise the country and rebuild its military. Eleven countries are holding the exercises. One impact is believed to have landed about 35 kilometres from that training ground, so the drill and the debris share the same southeastern corner of the country.

Scott Lucas, professor of international politics at the Clinton Institute, University College Dublin, told Al Jazeera the incident "fits into a pattern going back for months" of Russia "testing how far they can go." He said that a few months ago the pattern was drones flown into airspace, and that a couple of times fighter jets were flown across the border into the Baltic states. "You just test how NATO responds," he said. This time, he said, Moscow had "gone a bit further." "You lob a couple of missiles across and a drone" near where 11 countries are holding exercises. His reading is a sequence, not a finding of intent by a court: drones, then jets, then missiles and a drone beside a multinational drill.

The Polish Air Force had already launched preventive air patrols on September 17, aimed at securing the eastern part of Polish airspace after repeated incursions in the previous weeks. That date sits two months before the November 20 impacts and three weeks before the November 9 start of the southeastern exercise. The week of the hits therefore has a longer runway in the same reporting: patrols in September, a two-week drill from November 9 aimed at a possible post-war mission, an American push to revive talks, and then weapons on the ground on November 20.

Harry Nedelcu, partner and senior director for geopolitics at Rasmussen Global, told Al Jazeera the testing has not been limited to Poland. "During the war, there's been repeated instances of Russia testing NATO allies – Poland, Romania and also non-NATO Moldova as well, which is experiencing – especially over the last few months – increasing numbers of drones and missiles flying over its territory and airspace," he said. He tied the incursions to a core Russian war aim: preventing Ukraine from being part of NATO. He said the full-scale invasion has lasted almost five years, and that Moscow's goal is to undermine the alliance. "What Russia is doing with these missiles, drones, that sometimes find their way into NATO territory, tests NATO just under the radar of Article 5 to see how far can they push things. How far can they take hybrid warfare?" he said. Article 5 is the collective-defence clause. Al Jazeera's framing is that the bar for triggering it remains high even as Warsaw accuses Moscow of intentional provocation.

What changes, and for whom, follows from those distances and from that framing. People in three southeastern Polish localities — Nowy Lipowiec, Rudnik nad Sanem, and Molodycz — now have a recorded impact, even with no immediate injuries or damage reported. The troops of 11 countries, already in a drill that began on November 9 and pointed at a possible post-war mission in Ukraine, were within about 35 kilometres of one impact. Ukrainians absorbed, on Zelenskyy's numbers, a night of more than 400 drones and at least 110 missiles, among them 27 ballistic missiles, some with cluster warheads, with at least eight civilians killed and more than two dozen wounded. NATO governments are left with a Polish prime minister calling the episode an intentional provocation, a defence minister naming two missiles and a drone, and analysts describing a months-long test kept, in Nedelcu's words, just under Article 5. The United States, in the same reporting, is pushing to revive talks on ending the war. A strike that Polish leaders call deliberate, landing while those talks are being pushed and while European forces rehearse a mission inside Ukraine, is the decision point the day actually contains: not a declared alliance war, and not a confirmed Russian admission in this account, but a named allied government's account of weapons on its soil and an open question, which Al Jazeera puts in the headline, of whether NATO responds.

The distinction the reporting keeps is between a claim and a finding. Kosiniak-Kamysz stated the impacts as fact at a Warsaw news conference: two missiles, one jet-powered drone, three named places, the night of November 20. Tusk's sentence is a judgement of purpose: an intentional provocation, likely timed to the exercises. Lucas and Nedelcu supply the pattern they say the night fits — months of drones and jets, tests of Poland, Romania, and Moldova, a war aim of keeping Ukraine out of NATO. Cameras, Al Jazeera says, captured a suspected Russian missile striking Polish territory. Officials believe one object was a Kh-101 and another a jet-powered drone. Those labels are official belief and ministerial statement, not a published forensic report in the articles. No immediate injuries or damage were reported, which is why the consequence is political and military rather than a casualty count inside Poland. The people it changes something for, and soon, are the residents of those three places, the Ukrainian civilians under that night's barrage, the soldiers of 11 countries whose drill sat about 35 kilometres from one impact, and the governments that must decide whether a high bar for Article 5 still holds after a prime minister has called the night deliberate.

Ranks: People in southeastern Poland, Ukrainian civilians under that night's barrage, and the troops of 11 exercising countries · a NATO member records impacts and calls them provocation, with no immediate Polish injuries reported · immediate, response unstated Source: aljazeera.com Source: aljazeera.com

Both Sides: Should the EU cut Russian travel after Denmark's allegation?

Denmark is asking European Union governments to answer a plot it says Russian intelligence planned and started, and that Danish security services say they stopped. The person named by Danish media is the rapper Kyivstoner, whose real name is Albert Vasiliev, a Ukrainian-American. Denmark's security and intelligence agency, PET, said it stopped individuals who were intending to kill him at a concert in September. Three individuals were stopped in a car near the venue on 25 September. Firearms and knives were found in the vehicle. Two people have been charged with attempted murder and with assisting a foreign intelligence service, and both pleaded not guilty. PET said the individuals were associated with a banned Danish gang, Loyal to Familia, which the agency describes as known for extensive, serious and violent criminal activity. PET worked with police on the case. Denmark's foreign minister said Russia's defence attaché will now be expelled and must leave within seven days.

The case for tighter travel rules is Denmark's, joined by the European Commission president. Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen called the alleged attempt Russian intimidation meant to make Denmark waver in its support for Ukraine. In a message to EU governments, confirmed by two diplomats and seen by Reuters, Denmark urged "concrete actions" demonstrating European strength against Moscow, including further restrictions on Russian nationals' access to the Schengen zone, a halt to tourist visas for Russian citizens, and a Schengen-wide policy of not recognising Russian non-biometric passports. Rasmussen said Copenhagen was already very reluctant to issue visas, but Russians can still obtain a Schengen visa in another country and travel to Denmark. He wants such visas to apply only in the issuing country. Denmark would present the proposals to EU ambassadors in Brussels on Monday. France 24 reports that Danish authorities have ordered the expulsion of a Russian diplomat and announced tighter restrictions on visas for Russian tourists. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said on Saturday she strongly condemns an assassination attempt against a US-Ukrainian citizen in Denmark, orchestrated, she said, by Russian intelligence services. "This was an attack on EU soil. And it is unacceptable," she said, adding that the EU stands in full solidarity with Denmark and with all member states targeted by Russian hybrid attacks. She said Russia's hybrid threats are becoming less and less hybrid, and more and more real, and that the EU will soon propose a new European Security Strategy to reinforce preparedness and deterrence.

The case against is the Russian embassy's in Copenhagen, and Moscow's stated intention to answer the expulsion. The embassy said on Friday the accusations were not backed with proof. It called the allegations unsubstantiated and said Copenhagen failed to provide evidence of Moscow's involvement. It described a "Danish model" in which a "Russian trace" is discovered and "exploited for a propaganda spectacle," and accused Denmark of provocations meant to frighten the Danish population and justify a prolonged confrontation with Russia. Russian state media cite the foreign ministry in Moscow as saying it will respond to the expulsion of its official "accordingly." Separately, Russia's FSB security service accused Vasiliev and the Ukrainian authorities in July of organising a "terrorist attack" on military facilities and personnel in Russia. The rapper denied that allegation on social media. The two people charged pleaded not guilty. Those pleas are not findings. What happens next is dated: Denmark takes the visa and passport proposals to EU ambassadors on Monday, the defence attaché has seven days to leave, and von der Leyen says a new European Security Strategy will be proposed soon.

Progress Watch

Ukrainian forces have liberated 14 settlements and reclaimed nearly 200 square kilometres, about 77 square miles, in the Kharkiv region in a counteroffensive codenamed Bear Trap, according to BBC News Ukraine on November 20, as reported by UNITED24 Media. The operation disrupted a Russian offensive, codenamed Bear Corner, that began in late June 2026 and aimed at encircling Ukrainian troops near Prykolotne and capturing about 500 square kilometres. BBC News Ukraine said Russian troops had established positions near Vilkhuvatka and threatened Ukrainian supply routes, and that Ukrainian commanders deployed additional forces to the sector in September. The counteroffensive began on September 20. Rather than hitting Russian positions head-on, Ukrainian troops used armoured assaults to draw reinforcements toward Vilkhuvatka, then struck the concentrated forces with 18 Fire Point fixed-wing attack drones, advanced from two flanks, and by October 10 had reportedly completed the encirclement. The 475th Separate Assault Regiment CODE 9.2 was a key assault unit, with the multidomain centre Phalanx coordinating planning. CODE 9.2 released footage of M1 Abrams and T-80 tanks, Bradley infantry fighting vehicles, attack drones, and assault teams near Vilkhuvatka. Its figures of about 911 Russian personnel losses and nearly 400 pieces of equipment damaged or destroyed have not been independently verified. Ukrainian forces also used drones to mine Russian logistics routes up to 30 kilometres deep, on a mesh network that worked without Starlink, and used StarGate heavy-lift drones for seven evacuations of wounded soldiers over several weeks. Lieutenant Colonel Oleksandr Nastenko, callsign Flint, commander of CODE 9.2, told BBC News Ukraine the operation remains ongoing, with troops still clearing settlements near the Russian border. The Ukrainian military says the counteroffensive has stabilised the sector, despite Russian Chief of the General Staff Valery Gerasimov reporting to Vladimir Putin on October 16 that Moscow's encirclement operation had been completed. In October, Ukraine's 3rd Army Corps completed Operation Vivaldi in the Donetsk region, reporting the recapture of 16 settlements and 277 square kilometres. Source: united24media.com

The Ten

Three Kyiv bridges hit during major Russian missile attack: BBC World

In Ukraine, Russia hit three bridges in Kyiv overnight, the BBC reports, in a nationwide assault that also struck railway stations days after Ukraine damaged the Crimean Bridge. President Volodymyr Zelensky called it one of the most massive strikes of the war and said it left at least eight dead; the BBC reported five people killed. Ukraine's air force said Russia launched almost 100 missiles and around 400 drones, and air force spokesman Yuriy Ignat called the attack one of the most powerful yet. The Podilsko-Voskresensky, Darnytskyi and Southern bridges were closed, and Mayor Vitali Klitschko urged people to avoid driving into the city unless necessary, saying a critical facility was damaged and seven medical personnel were injured. Prime Minister Serhiy Koretsky said 28 people were injured across nine regions, including an ambulance hospital in Kharkiv. In Lviv, Mayor Andriy Sadovyi said two people were killed at the Sknyliv railway station and ten were injured, with rescue work still going on. Polish Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz said a Russian cruise missile landed about 50 kilometres, or 31 miles, from Poland's border with Ukraine, and that two further projectiles fell in nearby villages and harmed no one; Polish and Swedish jets scrambled and were later stood down. President Vladimir Putin had said retaliation for the Crimean Bridge strike would be far more painful, after Zelensky said Ukraine's operation had been weeks in the making. The barrage began hours after UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham, French President Emmanuel Macron and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said they were ready for talks with Moscow, as the Kremlin pushed back on a US claim that Russia could accept Western-backed security guarantees for Ukraine. US envoy Jared Kushner said the United States wants to make a deal with Europe by his side.

Trump, Xi and Putin release ‘peace declaration’ from unprecedented summit: Hawaii Tribune-Herald

In China, the United States, China and Russia agreed a symbolic peace declaration on Thursday at a three-way summit in Shenzhen, the Hawaii Tribune-Herald reports, and reaffirmed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought. The leaders pledged United Nations reforms, larger supplies of fuel products for all countries, and the immunity of state officials in international courts, and said they would discuss cutting nuclear risks, with no date set. President Vladimir Putin said the declaration set no ambitious goals and that nobody would be forced to comply with it. Trump greeted Putin as his friend, was hugged by President Xi Jinping, told NBC the meeting rated a 12 on a scale of zero to 10, and told Reuters the summit was bigger than Yalta. He said it is better to have people with 4,000 nuclear weapons as a friend than as an enemy, and that giving those weapons up is not easy. Foreign Minister Wang Yi said the three would keep coordinating at working level, and Trump said he would hold a news conference in Washington at noon today. The Reuters account said the three-day Asia trip yielded few tangible victories despite Trump's claims of making history.

U.K. and EU hold rare summit as Burnham seeks to mend Brexit damage: The Globe and Mail

In Belgium, Prime Minister Andy Burnham met European Council President António Costa and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen in Brussels on Friday for only the second UK-EU summit since Britain left the bloc, The Globe and Mail reports. The package on the table would let up to 100,000 young adults from each side live and work in the other for up to three years, link emissions trading, and drop checks on food and farm goods. Burnham said the deals could add 0.5 percent to Britain's economy, up from an earlier 0.4 percent, and he cited Treasury analysis putting the gain at up to 0.5 percent, calling it the largest growth step of this Parliament. An EU official, speaking on condition of not being named, said nothing takes effect unless the two sides agree university tuition fees within six months; EU negotiators want European students in Britain to pay domestic rather than foreign fees, which the UK has resisted. EU sources told The Guardian that tying the food deal to fees is meant to offset leverage London would gain from a six-month delay on tuition while Britain seeks a place in Made in Europe rules aimed at supply chains and China. France has insisted the UK cannot have a vote on food and drink legislation, and talks continue on access to those manufacturing rules. The deals require Britain to accept some EU standards it does not help write, and Burnham has said rejoining is one option, not a near one, and would need British voters plus every EU government's support. Von der Leyen said she wanted more chances for young people, easier agrifood trade and closer economic-security cooperation; Burnham pitched the package as a way to cut supermarket prices.

Liberals’ 2026 budget passes in House of Commons: Global News

In Canada, the House of Commons passed the 2026 federal budget by 162 votes to 151, Global News reports, after it was tabled on November 5. The plan sets out billions of dollars in new borrowing and tax measures aimed mainly at infrastructure and other capital projects, including a $36-billion productivity deduction meant to push firms to buy machinery. Liberals now hold a majority after byelections and floor-crossings, unlike last year, when Green leader Elizabeth May cast the deciding vote and two New Democrat and two Conservative MPs abstained or were absent. Conservative Leader Pierre Poilievre has argued the borrowing hands costs to Canadians and does not make life more affordable. Parliamentary budget officer Annette Ryan warned about the scale of borrowing but found the government narrowly on track to keep its fiscal anchors, with higher oil prices and stronger revenues improving the books since spring. Finance Minister François-Philippe Champagne said confident countries invest, and that Canada is one of the few G7 countries able to fund growth and affordability measures at the same time. New Democrat Leader Avi Lewis said his MPs would vote against a plan he argues serves big business rather than groceries, phones, housing and health care, and Bloc Québécois Leader Yves-François Blanchet criticized the title Maîtres Chez Nous as failing to give Quebec more control of its affairs. Implementation bills can still be amended in committee, and the second implementation act for the 2025 budget, tabled in May, is still before Parliament.

Britain is deciding whether a reset with the European Union should bind it to standards it does not write, and to a later bargain on what EU students pay to study in the UK, before easier food trade and youth movement can take effect. Omni View Top World News

10,000 Rohingya were killed, the world court says, in the first ruling that a state committed genocide.

What You Need to Know: The International Court of Justice has ruled that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, the first time that court has found a state responsible for the crime, and Al Jazeera reports the court's president said evidence shows forces killed some 10,000 Rohingya. The same day, U.S., Chinese and Russian leaders closed a first three-way meeting by agreeing a nuclear war must never be fought, and Israeli forces finished a rare push beyond Gaza's Yellow Line. This edition also draws on the Omni View Omni View North America desk.

The Ten

Myanmar committed acts of genocide against Rohingya minority, world’s top court rules: The Guardian

Myanmar was found by the International Court of Justice in The Hague, on Thursday, to have committed acts of genocide against the Rohingya during military “clearance operations” in 2016 and 2017, and The Guardian reports the court has never previously found a state responsible for genocide. Court president Yuji Iwasawa said the court was fully convinced that Myanmar committed acts under the Genocide Convention with intent to destroy a substantial part of the Rohingya population in Myanmar. Al Jazeera reports Iwasawa said evidence shows Myanmar’s forces killed some 10,000 Rohingya, and that security forces killed thousands of civilians, perpetrated widespread sexual violence and extreme violence against children, and attacked people as they fled toward Bangladesh. The Guardian reports the 2016–17 campaign forced more than 700,000 mostly Muslim Rohingya into Bangladesh; Al Jazeera puts that flight at more than 730,000, and says more than 1.3 million Rohingya remain displaced, including 1.2 million in camps in Cox’s Bazar. The Guardian says more than 1 million still live in those Bangladesh camps and that fewer than 600,000 are estimated to remain in Myanmar. The Gambia filed the case in 2019 for survivors, resting on genocidal statements and patterns of conduct in Rakhine state, including shooting people as they fled and blocking their return. Myanmar denied a genocide and said it was answering attacks by the Arakan Rohingya Salvation Army; the court said the only reasonable inference from the atrocities was intent to destroy a substantial part of the group, and that Myanmar violated its obligation to prevent genocide. The Guardian reports the court cannot enforce its rulings, though they are considered binding and can shape sanctions, and that governments watched in part because the judgment is a precedent for other genocide claims, including a case against Israel. Min Aung Hlaing, who headed the military during the attacks, has a pending International Criminal Court arrest warrant on allegations of crimes against humanity; he overthrew Aung San Suu Kyi’s elected government in a 2021 coup and was installed as civilian president in April after elections The Guardian describes as widely discredited.

Ranks: Rohingya survivors and more than a million refugees · a binding genocide finding, with enforcement still diplomatic · the judgment is in now Source: theguardian.com Source: aljazeera.com

Trump, Xi and Putin sit down for a rare three-way meeting in China: CBS News

In China, Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin agreed that a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought, ending their first trilateral talks after about an hour and a half on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen. A Kremlin readout said the three exchanged views on the international situation, reaffirmed strategic stability and international security, and, as permanent members of the Security Council, recommitted to reforming the United Nations so it can meet global challenges. That readout said they committed to increasing oil and gas production for the benefit of all countries, stressed state consent and respect for the immunities of state officials, and said the International Criminal Court and similar bodies should not abuse their powers. Trump opened by saying, “We are really making history,” and that no peace was ever forged by declining opportunities for engagement. Putin said the three had numerous shared interests in settling regional conflicts, including the conflict in Ukraine. Xi did not refer to Ukraine directly; he said that when major powers live in harmony the world is at peace and when they clash the world falls into turmoil, and Xinhua, cited by Al-Monitor, quoted him saying nuclear weapons should be prohibited and eliminated toward a world free of them.

Ranks: the three nuclear powers and countries touched by the Ukraine and Iran wars · a shared pledge, not a new disarmament treaty · the statement is out, no next date named Source: cbsnews.com Source: al-monitor.com
